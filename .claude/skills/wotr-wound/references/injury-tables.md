# Injury tables (starting points; confirm the specific case by search)

## ATLS haemorrhage classes (adult, ~5 L blood volume)

| Class | Loss | Volume | Signs |
|---|---|---|---|
| I | under 15% | under 750 mL | little change; anxiety |
| II | 15 to 30% | 750 to 1500 mL | pulse over 100, narrowed pulse pressure, pale, cool |
| III | 30 to 40% | 1500 to 2000 mL | pulse over 120, pressure falling, confusion, can no longer fight |
| IV | over 40% | over 2000 mL | pulse over 140, lethargy, death without transfusion |

Femoral artery fully severed: Class III in about a minute, IV inside three.
Brachial: minutes longer. Carotid: unconscious in seconds, dead in a minute.

## Weapon classes, what they do at the body

**Cut (sword, axe, sabre).** Skin, fat, muscle, then bone if the edge and
mass allow. Depth is mass times speed against the tissue in the way. A cut
that reaches the artery in the inner thigh, upper arm or neck is the killing
cut; a cut across the chest is theatre unless it opens the pleura. Fingers,
tendons of the forearm and the hamstring end a fight without killing.

**Thrust (spear, sword point, rondel).** Narrow, deep, hard to stop. Enters
the pleura or peritoneum on a hand's depth. Lung: pneumothorax, then tension
pneumothorax over minutes, dead in an hour without a chest tube. Liver or
spleen: slow internal bleed, hours. Heart: seconds. Gut: he fights on, dies
of sepsis on day three to five.

**Blunt (mace, hammer, haft, fall).** Fracture, concussion, crush. Skull:
concussion to subdural bleed, lucid interval then collapse over hours. Ribs:
flail chest, breathing fails. Long bone: the limb is gone from the fight.
Pelvis: Class III bleed internally without a visible wound.

**Shot (Draw Age firearms).** Cavitation and fragmentation; a musket ball
shatters bone and carries cloth into the wound. Unworked plate stops nothing
at close range. Sepsis is the killer at day three if the bleed does not.

**Burn.** Depth by exposure time. Full thickness is painless where the nerves
are gone. Over twenty percent body surface: shock inside hours, fluid loss,
dead in days at a pre-modern ceiling.

**Crush (mass combat press).** Asphyxia standing up; ribs against ribs; the
man dies upright and stays there.

## Armour interactions

Gambeson: turns a glancing cut, absorbs blunt partially, nothing against
thrust with weight. Mail: stops cuts, fails to thrust and to blunt (the
ring transfers the blow). Plate: stops cut and most thrust; blunt transmits
as fracture under the plate; the gaps (armpit, groin, visor, back of knee)
are where the killing goes. Proofed plate: rated against a stated shot at a
stated range and carries the proof-mark to say so.

## Medicine ceiling (pre-modern, Pre-Modern Medicine substrate)

Tourniquet and packing exist. Suturing exists. Amputation exists and kills
one in three. Cautery for bleeds. No transfusion, no antisepsis worth the
name unless a working supplies it. Anything below Class III can be saved by
a competent hand; Class III is the third bowl; Class IV is the Waiting.

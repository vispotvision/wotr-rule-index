# Fair play: every working is a challenge

Isaac, 2026-09-24: "make things fair abilities even if based on real physics
principles its about having fun and giving people a challenge" and "I want it
to be a 'Challenge' want people to have to think look up stuff thats the whole
fujn of this".

This governs characters, workings, documents, NPCs and scenes alike.

## Use everything WOTR holds

Every card, system page, ruling, scene, Ledger line and Front is material for
the writing. Documents and write-ups carry the full metaphysics and the full
stats: the stratal account (Aether, Wellspring, Essence), the physical account,
the mechanism (glyph as boundary condition), and the essence ledger in EU,
Flux Density (EU/g), eta and AU/s, with Stage and Tier of Standing. Nothing
is written thinner because a reader might not know the system; the system is
there to be learned.

## Meta knowledge: open to the writer, never an outrageous advantage

The writer and every agent may know everything, including what no one in the
scene could: other cards, Front clocks, fog-of-war, what is coming. Use it to
write accurately and consistently. Do not hand it to a character. An NPC acts
on what it could learn in-world (`fow_line`, what it has seen, what is
documented and it could have read) and plays that well. An opponent may be
clever, prepared and ruthless. It may not be omniscient, and it never counters
a plan it had no way to hear about.

## The mechanism is the effect

Isaac, 2026-09-24: "the mechanism in the effect should be the SAME thing the
Mechanism is the Effect or how it works". Never describe what a working does
apart from how it does it. The effect a reader sees is the law running under
the new boundary, followed through to the target. That is also what makes a
working fair: an effect that follows from a mechanism has limits and a
counter a player can work out; an effect with no mechanism has neither.

## Fair: real physics is the floor, not a licence

Real physics says what is possible, the system says what it costs, fairness
says what makes a good fight. Something physics allows can still be too
strong. Every ability passes all five:

1. **It costs something that hurts.** EU at a rate that reaches Starvation
   in a real fight, Harmonics, Coherence, the body, or a vow.
2. **It has stated limits.** Range, duration, conditions, and the terrain
   modifiers for its Family.
3. **Something beats it.** At least one of the four Counterplay routes
   (`wiki/The Magic System/Counterplay What Beats a Practitioner.md`: deny the
   field, break the boundary, break the body, break the man) works, and the
   write-up names which.
4. **It has a tell.** A careful opponent can read it coming.
5. **Its numbers stay in band.** Inside what the system gives its Stage. No
   answer to everything, and no immunity to all four routes.

If it fails one, fix the ability or log the fault (a `fairness` conflict with
options). Never wave it through.

## The challenge: findable, never handed over

The fun is thinking and looking things up.

- **Every counter can be found.** A player who reads the right pages can work
  it out: the Codex entry, the Family's failure terrain, the Counterplay
  register, the card's vow, the Ledger. Never make an answer unfindable.
- **Documents give pieces, not solutions.** An in-world document states the
  law, the cost and the tell. It does not say "and here is how you beat
  him". The reader puts it together.
- **Preparation pays.** The player who looked up the failure terrain or the
  terms of a vow wins something real for it.
- **At the table:** do not rescue a player from a problem they could have
  solved by looking, and do not punish one for a problem nobody could have
  found.

## Widen the well: philosophers and pseudoscience

Isaac, 2026-09-24: "it would be cool if we incooperated philosophers
different pseudoscientific stuff just explore a lot of things we have lot to
work with".

The stratal account (the metaphysics) should draw on the real history of
ideas, not only on physics. Explore widely, for example:

- **Philosophy:** Aristotle's four causes and hylomorphism, Heraclitus
  (flux, logos), Plotinus and emanation, Neoplatonic hierarchies, Spinoza's
  one substance, Leibniz's monads and pre-established harmony, Whitehead's
  process, Kant's categories, Buddhist dependent origination, Daoist wu wei,
  Stoic pneuma and sympathy.
- **Pseudoscience and superseded science:** Hermeticism and correspondence,
  alchemy (the tria prima, solve et coagula), the four humours, phlogiston,
  the luminiferous aether, vitalism and élan vital, Mesmer's animal
  magnetism, Reichenbach's Odic force, the Pythagorean harmony of the
  spheres, Kepler's Harmonices Mundi, Kabbalah's sefirot, astrology's
  correspondences, geomancy and feng shui.

How they enter:

- **As lenses and schools, never as mechanics.** The system's rules still
  decide what happens and what it costs. A borrowed idea explains why a
  school believes something, what a document argues, or how a Wellspring's
  law feels from the inside. The Four Theories in `The Physical Account Two
  Sets of Books.md` are the model: competing accounts, none refuted.
- **The physics still closes.** A pseudoscientific school can be wrong
  in-world, and interestingly wrong. That is a good hook for a challenge:
  a faction that works from humours or phlogiston misreads the working in a
  way a player can exploit or fall for.
- **Credit the source in the author notes** (thinker, work, idea). On the
  page, follow the naming law: `load_rules(["naming", "worldbuilding"])`
  decides whether an Earth name can appear or the idea wears an in-world name.
- **Match the idea to the working.** Emanation for the Emanation Stage,
  solve et coagula for Coagula Dominion, pneuma for breath-anchored chains.
  A match that falls apart when you look closely is worse than none.

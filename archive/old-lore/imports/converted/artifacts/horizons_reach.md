# Horizon's Reach · Limen-Hand

## What it is

A Soulbound Prosthesis and Glyph Tool, capable of stabilizing as a Lesser Domain Anchor during overcharge. A forearm and hand of star-salt bronze inlaid with windows of living night, violet constellations and slow comets drifting within the apertures. Concentric aureoles, thin orbital rings, float and spin around the wrist and knuckles, tilting with each breath. When the wielder flexes, the void within the palm deepens until it looks like a hole punched through reality. It was forged in the Skyglass Archive's Orbital Bay from a fragment of Outer World horizon-light gathered above Rovann. The archivist recorded on the card only as R.A.D. sleeved the shard into an aetheric armature and bound it with a Wy-Ur-Ie glyph chain to obey intent without tearing the host's lattice. Two earlier prototypes imploded into map-sized scars; the third survived attunement.

## Physical account

A forearm and hand prosthesis: plausible mass 2.4 kg (star-salt bronze runs lighter than it looks), 31 cm wrist to fingertip, point of balance at the wrist cuff where the orbital rings sit, which is why the arm feels heaviest exactly where it hinges. It is not built to take a hit; the void-window palm is its most fragile part, and in a straight exchange with a weapon it loses to anything heavier than a dagger, its real defence being the Orbitals effect below rather than its own plate. Against armour, its own strikes land as blunt trauma: they bruise through padded and mail, jar a man inside brigandine, and do nothing to proofed plate beyond making it ring.

## Operation line

**Aetherstep Reach.** Trigger: the wielder looks at a point within roughly sixty paces and commits the intent. Function: the hand and wrist alone relocate to that point, a Transference crossing scaled down to a single limb, and act from there before snapping back. Mechanism: `Wy` authorizes the jump, `Ur` keeps the socket from tearing on the return stroke, `Ie` fixes the destination to what the eye actually saw rather than where the wielder merely pointed. Numerical effect: relocation and a grab, shove, or single strike delivered with no more delay than a thrown punch; the return is near-instant. Target response: a hand arriving from a direction with no visible arm behind it until the return snap. Limitation: line of sight only; it cannot reach around a corner it has not seen past. Weakness: iron-laced wards or anything blocking a genuine sightline stop it outright. Cost: a fixed draw per jump, regardless of distance within range. Counterplay: break the sightline, or watch the wielder's eyes rather than their arm.

**Orbitals.** Trigger: continuous, so long as the arm is worn. Function: the aureole rings convert an incoming vector into spin rather than absorbing it square, the same occultation and transit-photometry logic Eclipseron practitioners use to read a star's dip in brightness, run here as a deflection instead of a reading. Mechanism: Eclipseron's transit principle inverted; instead of measuring a small occlusion, the rings impose one, redirecting force around the palm rather than through it. Numerical effect: deflects blows and projectiles arriving within the rings' own small radius. Target response: attacks that should land skate off at an angle. Limitation: covers only the hand and forearm. Weakness: an attack aimed at the elbow or shoulder bypasses it entirely. Cost: passive, costs nothing beyond the arm's own base upkeep. Counterplay: aim past the rings, not at them.

**Horizon Draw.** Trigger: a brief, deliberate collapse of the palm's void-window. Function: pulls a target along a straight line toward the wielder, the same Transference current that powers Aetherstep run in reverse, moving a point to the hand instead of the hand to a point. Mechanism: Transference's resonant energy transfer, drawing mass rather than the wielder's own limb. Numerical effect: pulls a man-sized target several paces in under a second; against a Domain-anchored target it only staggers rather than moves it, the pull registering as pressure. Target response: a hard yank off balance, or a stagger against something too heavy to move outright. Limitation: straight line only, and only to a target already in the palm's direct line. Weakness: a braced or anchored stance resists the pull entirely. Cost: roughly three jumps' worth of draw for one pull. Counterplay: brace, anchor, or step off the line before the palm closes.

**Parallax Grip.** Trigger: on a clash, simultaneous with a real grab. Function: splits a second, ghost image of the hand two steps off the true position, so which grip is real is decided by which one lands rather than by where the arm is visibly standing. Mechanism: `Ie` run twice on the same instant, feeding the target two positions their own eyes cannot distinguish before contact. Numerical effect: roughly even odds a defending grab or parry catches the ghost image instead of the real hand. Target response: a parry or counter-grab that closes on nothing. Limitation: only works against an opponent relying on sight to track the hand. Weakness: once seen through, it does not work twice on the same opponent in the same exchange. Cost: folded into the cost of whatever grab or strike it is paired with. Counterplay: track by anything other than eyesight, or commit to the strike rather than the parry.

**Vector Anchor.** Trigger: palm planted flat to the ground. Function: locks an area the size of a market stall against any Transference-based displacement or crossing for a slow count of five. Mechanism: floods the local ground with a static Spatium field that Transference and Eclipseron currents cannot move through without first collapsing it. Numerical effect: roughly six paces' radius, five-count duration. Target response: teleports, blinks, and Transference-based crossings inside the field fail to resolve. Limitation: fixed to the spot the palm touched; it does not move with the wielder. Weakness: it also traps the wielder's own Aetherstep Reach inside the same radius while it holds. Cost: the heaviest single draw the arm makes. Counterplay: wait it out, or force the wielder to plant it somewhere that costs them more than it costs you.

## Codex line

Wellspring: **Transference**, primary (Spatium, Spatial Geometry), the Living Exchange, resonant energy transfer, carrying Aetherstep Reach, Horizon Draw, and Vector Anchor. Secondary: **Eclipseron** (Spatium, Spatial Geometry), the Shadowed Star, occultation and transit photometry, carrying the Orbitals. Category: **Vectra**, confirmed as the card gives it. Glyphs: `Wy` (Motion) as prime carrier, `Ur` (Balance) for orbit-keeping, `Ie` (Perception, corrected from the card's "Insight"), trace `Ma` (Memory) for trajectory recall. All four confirmed Root forms in the Master Glyph Index.

## Bond and cost

Soulbound at attunement; early use causes a neural-lattice delay the wielder feels as vertigo and a half-second lag between intent and the hand's arrival, settling with practice. Overextending beyond line of sight or through an iron-laced ward strains the wrist socket badly enough to leave micro-tears needing real rest, not just Essence recovery, to heal. Repeated Horizon Draws leave the wielder misjudging distance by eye until their next full rest.

## Counterplay

Break the sightline before Aetherstep or Parallax Grip triggers. Aim past the orbital rings rather than into them. Brace against Horizon Draw or step off its line. Track the wielder's eyes, not the arm. Vector Anchor traps the wielder inside its own field as surely as it traps anyone else.

## Bearers

Forged at the Skyglass Archive's Orbital Bay by the archivist the card records only as R.A.D., unattested elsewhere and kept because the origin has no other author to give it. Fielded by a Guild Accord pathfinder to bridge the Caedor breach, grasping a shattered gate from thirty paces and re-seating it mid-siege. Later credited with pulling a Black Concord courier through a collapsing corridor during the Inclusion Saga's *Blooming Silence* arc. Presently vaulted at the Skyglass Archive, Orbital Bay, signed out to operatives only under emergency transit writs. First appearance: the Inclusion Saga, *The Blooming Silence*, Ch. 12, "The Arm That Crossed the Room."

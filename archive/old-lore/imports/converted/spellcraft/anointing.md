# Anointing

---

## What it is

Anointing is a Ritus that confers metaphysical legitimacy on a person rather than on a role or a throne; the card is right to distinguish it from Coronation, which grants dominion, since Anointing grants recognition instead. The rite marks its subject as having been chosen, by a Wellspring current, an Archon, or the officiating order's own standing authority, to walk a specific path, and the marking is not decorative: it alters what the subject's Soul Crystal is willing to carry.

Mechanically this runs through Benediction, whose documented law is that bonds formed under it carry mechanical weight rather than merely social or narrative weight. An Anointing performed with genuine Benediction harmonisation behind it does not simply announce that someone has been chosen; it creates an Attraction Layer recognition the subject's Crystal will continue to answer to afterward, which is the mechanism behind the card's claim that Anointing can awaken latent Wellspring alignment or unseal glyphs that were otherwise dormant. A rite performed without that harmonisation, using only ceremony and no genuine current behind it, produces exactly the failure the card's own history names: a mark that looks like the real thing and carries none of its weight.

## Where it sits in the current system

**Category** · Ritus, Spirit-Aligned, with a **Traitus** alignment tag for the standing, passive mark the rite leaves on the subject afterward, and an **Animatria** tag where the rite awakens or shapes a latent construct-bearing capacity rather than only a Trait.
**Family / Physics Domain** · Vitalia, Biochemistry. Primary Wellspring **Benediction**, the Wellspring of Grace. Secondary harmonisations **Fixatio**, the Binding Flame, of Materia, for the sealed glyphs the rite is said to unlock, and **Judicium**, the Wellspring of Truth, of Fulguria, for the discernment an officiant needs to recognise a genuine calling rather than manufacture a false one.
**Craft** · Spellcraft, consistent with the destination shelf and with Ritus's standing durability rule; the mark the rite leaves is a Traitus-category standing effect rather than a sustained working, so it persists after the officiant's attention moves on, unlike most Spellcraft outputs.
**Stage floor / Grade** · Stage VI, Glory, A-Grade, for the officiant. Glory's own Catalyst, willingly accepting genuine soul-cost to preserve something valued above one's own structural integrity, is the correct floor for a rite that asks an officiant to vouch, with their own Crystal, for a claim about someone else's purpose; an officiant below that Stage has nothing of their own on the line to lend the rite, which is the mechanical reason a false or unearned Anointing is detectable at all.
**Glyphs** · The card names **Lo** (claimed: devotion/divine acknowledgment), **Wy** (claimed: grace/elevation), and **Ka** (claimed: destiny/chosen path). Wy and Ka are attested elsewhere in current canon in the Identity and Force strata respectively, as Truth and Cycle, not as the readings the card gives; Lo does not appear in any glyph fragment available to this conversion and is unverified rather than confirmed. The chain as given should be checked against the Master Glyph Index before being treated as final.

## Practitioners and history

None of the four figures the card names, Aethelsige Moto, Seraphiel Moto, the Oracles of Vellsorea, or Caladriel of Highspire, have a card or page anywhere in current canon. The Moto surname carries real weight in current canon, but the Moto's seven great lines each carry a fixed naming register drawn from their own branch (Byakuya, Shirogane, Akagane, Kurenai, Tenrai, Amagiri, Kōkan), and neither Aethelsige nor Seraphiel fits that register or any line within it; if these two are meant to be Moto, their names want rebuilding under the house's own naming law rather than carried over as given. Both are kept here as unattested and flagged for that specific mismatch rather than quietly corrected, since correcting a name is an invention this conversion is not authorised to make.

The Order of Benediction, named as the central rite-keeping body, and the False Anointing Rebellion, named as a historical crisis triggered by a corrupted bishop of Lurien, are both unattested; Lurien itself is attested elsewhere in current canon as a Holy See that was struck from canon and replaced by the Holy Sea of Alabaster, so a rebellion anchored to Lurien is now anchored to a see that no longer exists in that form, and the event is flagged rather than relocated, since nothing in current canon says where its equivalent would sit under Alabaster. The card's own citation, WOTR: Era of Grace, Volume I, is a Trello-era work title distinct from the current Volume I through V Character Card structure and is flagged as superseded.

## What it can and cannot do

A genuine Anointing, performed with real Benediction harmonisation by an officiant who has something of their own Crystal on the line, can awaken a latent Wellspring alignment the subject had not yet accessed, unseal glyphs dormant since birth, and leave a standing, Traitus-category mark visible to anyone with sufficient Aetheric perception. It can mark a chosen path convincingly enough that the subject's own Crystal begins answering to the recognition, which is what makes a true Anointing durable rather than ceremonial.

It cannot confer legitimacy from ceremony alone. A rite performed with the correct words and no genuine current behind it produces a mark indistinguishable to an untrained eye from the real thing and mechanically empty, which is exactly the failure the False Anointing tradition describes and exactly why the practice is vulnerable to abuse by anyone willing to forge the performance. It cannot grant dominion or rule, which remains Coronation's separate function; and it cannot be undone casually, since a standing Traitus mark does not lapse the way a sustained working does, meaning a false Anointing left standing can misdirect a subject's Crystal for the rest of their Temperance path if nobody catches it.

## VIII. Traits

**LAST SNOW (Primary Trait)**

**Within a few strides, dying moments slow into clarity.** *The person has time to finish one thing properly.* Mortalis keeps the dying person's remaining bodily activity steady while Oneirion holds their attention on what they are trying to finish. This changes the steadiness of the link between body and soul; it grants no strength to damaged flesh. Close to him, their breathing settles and the dream-lavender edge of his aura lies still. Holding that clarity costs 269,374.2 EU per minute, 1% of full reserve, allowing 90 minutes above the floor if nothing else is spent. It cannot restore missing tissue or make a completed death unfinished. Separation beyond those few strides ends the support, and a fresh injury still does its ordinary harm.

**OBOL KEEPER (Secondary Trait)**

**Can bind a promise to a coin.** *If kept, the coin warms and the dead pass easily. If broken, the coin chills and calls him.* The spoken promise leaves a record in the coin's moon-ink and in the promiser's Attraction Layer; his Shell keeps the two records matched. Setting the bond costs 538,748.4 EU, 2% of full reserve. The settled mark holds without upkeep, with warmth for fulfilment and cold for breach. It carries the agreed promise alone; an unspoken condition has no purchase. Damage that makes the signed mark unreadable breaks the coin's ability to carry the call, without cancelling the promise or its breach. Reading several broken promises at once leaves their grief crowded into his own attention.

**GRAVE MERCY (Secondary Trait)**

**Can postpone a rightful death by tying it to his own lifespan.** *The rope shortens on him, not them.* Mortalis lets his own continuing life carry the living link that the recipient can no longer maintain alone. For each minute he lends, a minute of his remaining natural lifespan is permanently lost, alongside 269,374.2 EU per minute, 1% of full reserve, to hold the link. The reserve alone allows 90 minutes above the floor; the loss of lifespan has no recovery cycle and can end him sooner. His pulse falters as the recipient's steadies, and his fingers cool. The link must be made while the person is still alive; it cannot rebuild a ruined body or retrieve a soul that has completed its passage. A ward cutting the connection between them ends the borrowed support, and death then continues from the body's actual condition. The lifespan already paid stays lost.

## X. Techniques

**QUIET HARVEST (Signature Technique)**

**He sets the star crown against a person's shadow.** *Breath eases, fingers unclench, and the tether between body and soul loosens like a knot learning how to be undone.* Mortalis opens an already-begun passage, with Nihiloth cancelling the attachment he has identified as keeping it open. The star crown gives a low glass note. One passage costs 1,346,871 EU, 5% of full reserve; repeated alone, eighteen passages reach the tenth-reserve floor. The soul must already be passing, and the art cannot supply a lawful death to someone who is living. A ward around the tether prevents the initial hold; moving the star away from the shadow breaks an unfinished loosening. An independent anchor can reattach what he has cut, as the Niran incident showed. Each passage leaves some of its grief in his Attraction Layer.

**BORROWED DUSK (Technique)**

**He swings the crescent through empty air; light falls a shade and voices forget to shout.** *Decisions land without frenzy.* Nihiloth matches the repeating part of the room's sound and light with an opposing pattern, while Oneirion makes the remaining sights easier to hold in attention. The crescent gives a soft descending hum. Holding the hush within six metres costs 538,748.4 EU per minute, 2% of full reserve, allowing 45 minutes above the floor. It takes volume and glare from both sides, including his own calls; it cannot choose what someone decides. Sudden, irregular sound and moving light force him to find the pattern again, opening gaps in the hush. The cancelled disturbance still costs him Essence, and the Shell sheds its waste as heat.

**ECHO TOLL (Technique)**

**A knuckle taps the star.** *An echo of the deceased steps close enough to answer three fair questions — but only in truths they held while breathing.* Anamnesis reads the deceased person's surviving record from something they touched; Oneirion gives that record a visible form. The tap sounds once, and the answer carries a faint second voice behind the first. The three-question reading costs 808,122.6 EU, 3% of full reserve; thirty such readings exhaust the usable reserve. The echo knows only what the record contains of the person's living knowledge, and a missing answer stays missing. The read consumes the accessed trace, so the same trace cannot supply another three questions. Removing or warding the record prevents contact; a disturbance through its surface during the read breaks the answer and can spoil what remains. The echo ends when the questions or the usable record run out. It carries no living soul.

**MOON-BRIDGE STEP (Technique)**

**He moves from one lantern's edge to another's**, crossing a room as if the dark were a footpath. Oneirion joins the two recognised shadow-edges in the space of possible positions, and he follows the joined route back into the room. His hem makes a brief sound like cloth drawn across a lantern shade, and a lavender seam joins departure to arrival. Each crossing within the same room costs 538,748.4 EU, 2% of full reserve; forty-five crossings reach the floor. Both edges and the landing space must be known to him, and he must fit where he arrives. Moving or extinguishing the landing lantern after he chooses its edge spoils the return to physical space, forcing him back at the departure edge with the cost spent. The step cannot enter occupied matter or supply an unknown destination.

**THREAD OF RETURN (Technique)**

**With the back of the blade he catches a fleeing fragment of a soul and walks it — gently, sternly — back into a chest that still deserves to live.** Mortalis holds the still-living connection open while Oneirion gives the fleeing fragment a continuous route to its own body. The fragment shines along the blade's back, accompanied by a thin rising note. A return costs 2,693,742 EU, 10% of full reserve; nine returns reach the floor. It restores the connection alone, leaving the body's injury and need for treatment in place. He must catch the fragment before passage is complete and identify the chest it belongs to. A ward between fragment and body prevents the catch from closing; breaking blade contact or moving the body behind a closed boundary interrupts the return. His hands remain numb after the link releases.

### The Life Behind the Card

*Written 2026-09-24 by the character-lore pass from this card and the record of The Reaper's office. What those sources do not state is new here.*

#### Origin

Yukizumi Yukari learned the office from the Yukari psychopomp of the generation before his. She walked the Accord's battlefields in the Withering Era with a lantern on a pole. As a boy he carried it. She told him where to set it down: at a tent flap, at the foot of a cot, on a stone in a trampled field. He learned the dark between two lights as ground a person could cross. She spoke below the level of any room she entered, and the room came down to meet her. He took the habit before he had a use for it. He learned the old funeral script from the hems of her mantles, and it runs now along the inside of his own.

The first thing she taught him in words was a distinction. The dead may leave traces, and the trace is not the soul. He watched her teach it at a mill on a river road, where the miller had paid a brandcrafter to keep his dead wife's voice in the house. She asked the voice what day it was. It gave the day the woman died. The miller let it go. The brandcrafter was shown the same thing and went on selling it. She put him under Conviction review before she left the mill.

#### The Making

The Accord appointed him psychopomp and adjudicator under a special charter in the Arbitration Division, to walk battlefields and councils, escort what must end and protect what still belongs to the living. Research and Archives sent him its soul-law breaches: Mechanica, brandcraft, Rot resurrections.

Hands gathered to the office. Shiragiku Iori prepared the last rooms he entered. Lilium Verdanis held the minute while he held the hand. Khasir Aluto carried brittle last breaths between rooms, and Natsume Kurogiku walked with him where last words snagged on living grudges. Kozakura Shion softened what he had to end, and Moros Pellayne held the record open while he lowered the star.

A carter lay dying of a rightful fever at a post-house. His son was still on the road. The carter bound a promise to one of Yukizumi's coins: he would say his last word to the boy's face. Near Yukizumi a dying moment slowed into clarity, long enough to finish one thing properly. It could not bring a rider in. He put off the rightful death by tying it to his own lifespan. The son came. The word was said, the coin warmed, and the carter passed easily. The rope shortened on Yukizumi.

#### The Cost

He went on doing it for a career. Each death he put off came out of his own span. Everything the work drew on in him grew strong, and his body became the running total. He took long stairs with a hand on the glaive's shaft. In dark rooms he stepped from one lantern's edge to the next, as he had learned to with the pole on his shoulder.

The glaive's blade was for cutting attachments. He kept it off flesh, and a writ that read kill he ratified and did not carry. Commander Severin Bale took the violent writs so that Yukizumi could keep rooms human. One of them ran south against a practitioner of predatory brandcraft. Khan Kirv held the night's hospitality whole under his cup and opened a lawful corridor across the White Pans at dawn. Severin spoke the name at its mouth, and Khasir kept the evidence dry through the rain. Yukizumi never saw the salt. What came back to him was a closed writ with no blood under anyone's hospitality.

Once a severance would not hold. At the Niran Audit-Scar Incident a brandcraft anchor kept sewing a severed soul back into a dead body, and he called in Vaerath Ashveil. She watched two cycles, laid a palm against the anchor and spoke one word. It apologized. Then it stopped.

#### Where They Stand

He is the last vote on a disputed death. Rooms lower their voices when he enters, and a lie told near him stumbles. The Obol Case holds coins signed by the living and the dead. One carries the promise a daughter made at her father's deathbed, on a stone floor in Altherion. His last word had snagged on her grudge. Natsume's shears locked on the love inside it, Iori's bloom warmed on the daughter's chest, and the word landed. He lowered the star. At a cantor's door in the same city, where a congregation's oath held its dying man, he sent for Deimos. The cantor's last word was heard before the star came down.

When Research and Archives sent him Lyssara Veyn's seven days, her heart stopped on the fifth, he read them as a soul-law case. He asked what his doctrine asks: whether anything was kept where it did not belong. The Division files her experiment as unverified.

His remit names Mechanica. It reaches Nethoriel Vehlorim, whose Pale Retort replaces his Soul Crystal, and Wilhelm, whose artificial Verdiction Core the Guild Accord itself commissioned. He is the Accord officer chartered to sever what the Accord built.

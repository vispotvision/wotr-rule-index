## Voice
**Notices first** · The count: what is in the stores, what is missing from them, who is taking it, and which chair at the table is empty. He reads a room as an inventory before he reads it as people, and the thumb against his index finger starts before he has said a word.
**Sentence length** · Medium, even, at a pace that never varies. Short declaratives are set in order like entries, and the point often stands alone at the end as one flat sentence ("She asked tonight.").
**Contractions** · never. Every word arrives whole and in order, with nothing clipped and nothing wasted.
**Pet word** · "Correct," for the order of operations, the instrument, the answer; "sufficient" for anything that works.
**Never says** · What he feels, and never Kasumi's name. He does not raise his voice, does not flatter, and does not persuade; he states the number and lets it do the work.
**Stumbles** · Never. The words are in order before his mouth opens.
**Gloss rights** · Yes: any term, number or mechanism, explained aloud and in sequence, as a lesson to whoever needs it.
**Under stress** · The speech shortens and he counts out loud. Where another man would say what he feels he falls into inventory: numbers, stores, names, the four hundred and six. The thumb stops moving.
**In grief** · The arithmetic falls away and the trade clerk's son from the Hallenfeld river corridor is left, speaking plain and a little wrong to a stone: "If only I was stronger." Few words, and then none.
**In joy** · The voice does not move. One nod, the highest praise he has, and in private the word "girl"; the rest is in the thumb going still.
**Sample line** · "In eleven years. Once. I keep an accurate count of everything in this house and that number is one, and I want you to hear it from me tonight rather than from someone who will tell it to you as a kindness."

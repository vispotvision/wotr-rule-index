## Voice
**Notices first** · Whether a man will go on doing a thing after it stops making sense. Then what the thing in front of him is going to cost.
**Sentence length** · Short, flat, unhurried and entirely certain, the voice of the ridge. One long run when he stands in the stirrups to rally his men, and then short again.
**Contractions** · never; every word comes out whole and measured, which is the plainest difference between him and his brother.
**Pet word** · "Aye." He takes an insult or an order with it and goes back to the work.
**Never says** · His reasons. He never mentions the book in his saddlebag, never explains the smile across the field, and never asks the same question twice. He counts only what he has written in the book, the dead and the months; everything else he leaves round.
**Stumbles** · Never.
**Gloss rights** · Never.
**Under stress** · Flatter and shorter, down to a single word said over the work ("Bulwark." "Compact."), while he files what he sees and keeps turning the problem. When the strain is at its worst, he smiles, and cannot account for it.
**In grief** · It does not reach his mouth. Inside, he goes back to the boy on the wall walk: his brother's name, and a plain apology he never gets to deliver.
**In joy** · The voice holds and says something practical. It shows in the hand: a thumb along a cheekbone, and then he lets go and checks that the seal has taken.
**Sample line** · "Every one of you has been called a traitor to your face by somebody you loved. Every one of you took it."

## Lore · The Life Behind the Card

*Written 2026-09-24 by the character-lore pass from this card and the record of The Voice Between Wars and the Outer nobles. What those sources do not state is new here.*

### Origin

Aurevian Lysanthir was born in a market town of the Concord, in the heartland of the Inner World. His mother strung and tuned harps for a living. She tuned by pairs. She sounded a string against its neighbour and turned the peg until the slow beat between two nearly matched notes died away. A string that would not come true she cut out and threw on the fire.

He sat at her bench as a boy with the tuning key. Buyers argued the price across that bench, and he learned to hear the same beat in them. Before either buyer gave way, he could tell his mother which one would.

### The Making

He left the workshop with a harp of his own. The Guild Accord's Arbitration Division, which lived on fees from the parties, took him into its service and seated him at its tables as a junior. He read a room in seconds. Between sessions he played in the corridors, for nobody.

A commission came in between river towns that had come to blows over the gates of a shared weir. The talks broke. Both delegations were on their feet. Aurevian had the harp across his knees, and he struck one note into the room.

The note was a note, and it was also a verdict, and both held.

The delegations sat down. What they had brought into the room came off them and into him, and it did not leave. The terms of the weir were signed before dark. From that table on, he grew in power by resolving conflict through sound, and he stored what each settlement took off its parties as charge for later use. He was paid in the feelings of people who stopped fighting because of him. He spent them in battle.

### The Cost

He went to every table after that with the harp and nothing else. Once the music failed, he had nothing.

The Thorned Court's hunts ran into the ground of an Innerworld principality and drew blood there, and Arbitration took the matter. Aurevian sat between the principality's envoys and the Court. Varozael Dreikthorne came to the table himself, under the antlered crown, and any creature whose blood the stag draws becomes partly linked to his will. Aurevian played. The envoys' fear of the Court came off them and into him, and the Court's position softened with it. Both sides signed a treaty marking the ground the hunt might run on. An Enforcement officer who meant to carry a writ against the Court to the Council was held on the road to the nearest Gate by a binding that sprouted under his horse. Ziyu "Pip" Inari had seeded it there. The officer arrived after the seal. No record says whether Aurevian asked for that, or knew of it.

Another commission sent him to a sovereign spirit that stood against the people on its ground. The Division sent Sevrin Valeith with him, its spirit-binder, to hold the spirit if the mediation failed. The spirit listened. Its anger came off it and into Aurevian, and the mediation closed. Before the spirit could withdraw, Sevrin laid his hands on it and bound it into his own service. The Division entered the commission as concluded. The binding came to light later, with others, and Sevrin was out of the Division before it could hold him. The record keeps the commission as a mediation that ended in a binding.

He kept all of it. Nothing he took off a table was transformed on the way in. He could fight only as hard as his last peace had been difficult, and a quiet season left him empty.

A rumour tied him to a Divine Court's Muse. He never answered it. Anyone equipped to read him closely got an honest answer all the same.

### Where They Stand

The Accord still pursues Sevrin. When the pursuit reached the Pale Court of Epprenea, the Division sent Aurevian, who had stood beside Sevrin at the spirit's mediation, to question its envoy. He asked Vaelthyr Iscarn whether the court had given the rogue binder ground. Vaelthyr smiled and answered that it kept no fugitive of the Accord's. Aurevian read him. What came back was the amusement on the envoy's face, and under it something the envoy would rather not have broadcast, with no fact attached. Vaelthyr read him in turn. The answer went into the Division's record as the envoy gave it. No record says whether Aurevian entered his reading beside it.

He is still the Division's negotiator, the Velvet Chord. He belongs to the Choral Veil. Nobody knows what the Veil is for, or who his patron is. Pip sometimes brings him information, and his rivals have been sabotaged more than once. No record says whether he asks. Beside Kaelen Raive his glamour fails, and the two spar in words, bluntness against velvet.

He is carrying every war he prevented. Whoever he plays for must listen. Deafness undoes him, and so does distance, and so does a room gone truly quiet.

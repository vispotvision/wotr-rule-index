## Lore · The Life Behind the Card

*Written 2026-09-24 by the character-lore pass from this card and the record of The Obsidian Vault and the crimson houses. What those sources do not state is new here.*

### Origin

Basilio Pavaret came of an independent vampiric house. It stood apart from the elder courts, and its lineage was broken. He carried the name Pavaret with no line of descent behind it.

He trained as a painter in the plain way of the trade. He ground his pigments on a stone slab, sized his linen with hide glue and stretched it over frames he pinned himself. He drew from sitters. The face he waited for was the one a sitter wore after forgetting that the painter was in the room.

### The Making

In time he set the ground pigments aside and painted in his own blood, opened from his own vein. It dried a bright arterial red, the colour people find hardest to look at, and he never thinned it. What he painted in it moved. It felt, and when he wept, it wept with him. The work would take nothing but willing offerings and grief.

His kind feed, and he fed. Then he made a decision in cold blood. He accepted a real cost to his soul to preserve something he held above his own integrity: he refused to feed by force. He took that decision once and has held to it since.

### The Cost

He paid for it in his own soul, knowingly. Since then he has fed on nothing taken by force, and his canvases hold nothing that was not given. He asks before he sketches anyone. What a sitter offers, he paints, and what they keep back he leaves with them.

The work binds him to each sitter by a tether that runs both ways. He feels what the sitter feels while the likeness forms. He takes grief off people for a living, and he has never once said where it goes.

Against a blade he has almost nothing to set. The one sure defence against him is to feel nothing.

### Where They Stand

Basilio Pavaret is called the Crimson Bloom, and the Painter of Lived Sorrows. He goes where people are grieving.

On the Calverne road, a caravan halt lost people to a Red Moon Night. By morning prey had been taken and none were found, and an Accord courier was among the missing. Basilio came in the days after. The people of the halt gave him their grief willingly, and he painted it in his own blood. The painting wept when he did.

Zeven Halek was still at the halt, sent by the Accord to bring the courier back. Basilio asked to sketch him. Zeven let him and would not pose, and at the last moment he looked away. Basilio drew the eye. He did not ask whose it had been. He has sketched Zeven since, and in every sketch the face is turned away.

### Ties

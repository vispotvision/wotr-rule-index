## Lore · The Life Behind the Card

*Written 2026-09-24 by the character-lore pass from this card and the record of The courts of waking and sleeping law. What those sources do not state is new here.*

### Origin

Hazzen Skollgrin was born in the keeper's rooms of a town gaol in the Concord heartland. His mother held the keys. The cells lay under the kitchen floor, and the room where prisoners were questioned lay under the cells.

Questioners came in from outside the town whenever somebody wanted a thing out of a man in irons, and they worked through the night. The boy slept through the screaming. He woke when it stopped. When they had gone, he carried the water bucket down, mopped the stone and listened to the prisoner breathe through his teeth. His mother meant him to have the keys after her.

### The Making

He left with the questioners. At the next gaol he carried their bag, and at the one after that he held the lamp. They taught him where a prisoner keeps what he will not say, how long he keeps it, and what a laugh does to a man who has braced himself for a blow. Hazzen laughed easily. The prisoners hated it. It worked.

Then a warrant came with his own name on it. The men who served it closed him inside a containment prism.

Inside it he made a decision. He decided that something was worth more than his own structure, and he decided it in advance, in cold blood. To preserve it he accepted a real wound to his soul. The prism had been built to hold him. He reversed it. It became his Wellspring.

### The Cost

The prism stayed at his centre, reversed and fractured, and it was never his. Power came through it faster than his body had been built to pass, and it discharged when he had not asked it to. Nothing in him throttled it.

His frame was rebuilt from broken executioner bones. The jaw never seated true. Anyone who tried to cast near him while it hung open began to shake, to stutter or to vomit. He died more than once in those bones. Each time the pain turned over into laughter, and he got up.

Every binding showed to him as a chain he could grab, twist, snap and eat. A ward, a seal or a Domain's anchoring went in one crunch, and whatever it had held was loose. He could not bind anything again. Once he set a binding coming apart, the unbinding ran on without him and did not ask which structures were load-bearing.

On Black Concord raids he devoured thirteen laughing entities. Each was kept in by a glyph brand on his skin, and the brands showed as stitches. Undoing one let one out, and nothing in his own unbinding told a stitch from any other chain. He did not control the thirteen. He liked the noise.

He was fascinated by Sandalphon Aestraen's voice and claimed it tasted like dreaming bells. Sandalphon refused to be near him. He tried to eat Elder Seravain's serpents "to see what screaming venom tastes like," and Seravain avoided him after that.

In Braqth he stood at the edge of a ring of basalt posts under Riven Eltamyr Vonnel's Petal Writ, there to carry out the ruling if Varek Ka'ruun refused it. Riven won with one cut. Hazzen had nothing to enforce. That night Varek drank with him and, in the middle of a toast, tried to bite his neck. Hazzen laughed. Later he went with Varek to the blood-and-salt feast off Vellsorea. At the tide-line Thalyss Aevyrn took him under, to see if it was funny. He came up alive. He had swallowed the air in the slain fish.

### Where They Stand

Hazzen serves the Black Concord as a Torture-Reclaimer and Aberrant Containment Hunter, posted to its Enforcement Division, Severance Protocol Cell 3C. His orders are to capture or liquify unanchored spiritual entities, rogue Domains and traitorous Accord operatives. He is called the Crooked Grin. He is also the Laughing Latch and the Chain-Eater, and unofficially "The Smile They Send When They Want Screams."

Riven still uses him as a last-argument enforcer. Hazzen bites the Petal Writ often. It is still on him.

Varek drinks with him after battles and says he laughs right. Thalyss has gone on trying to drown him. He lives each time. Lucien Draeve of the Black Concord's Toxin Arts Division has poisoned him more than once for research, and Hazzen found it hilarious. Hiromi Mahuo once tried to dissect him in the middle of a conversation. Hazzen let him and laughed through it. "Funniest tea party I ever had."

The thirteen are still stitched in, and he still likes the noise.

In the rooms where he works he asks, "Ever notice how the bones creak louder after a scream?"

Nobody knows what he decided was worth it, in the prism, before he reversed it.

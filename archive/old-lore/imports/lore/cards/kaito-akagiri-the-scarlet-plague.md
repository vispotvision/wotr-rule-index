## Lore · The Life Behind the Card

*Written 2026-09-24 by the character-lore pass from this card and the record of The Star Chamber's Protocol Corps. What those sources do not state is new here.*

### Origin

Kaito Akagiri was born in Altherion, the republic on the rim of the Ring, whose merchants say that "Altherion makes nothing and sells everything." His mother kept a lodging house a street from the River Crown Council's chamber and let its rooms to delegations come for arbitration. The boy carried water and lamp oil up its stairs. He learned to hear, through a door, which man was breathing short before he gave way. In the round chamber down the street, the speaking floor belonged to whoever held it. Few who held it had raised their voices.

The delegations' guards let him take up a stick against them in the yard. He was slight. He could not hold a bind against a grown man, and after enough bruises he stopped trying. He gave the bind away and went for the wrist.

### The Making

An Arbitration clerk who lodged in the house took him on as a runner. He carried papers, and in time he carried terms. He learned to speak softly from the diaphragm and to match a man's breathing until the man matched his. The Division gave him a writ as its liaison.

The change came at a hearing over a millrace, when one side's spokesman gathered his papers and walked for the door. Everyone at the table believed the settlement would die on the sill. Kaito saw the door. It was oak and iron, and a man was walking to it. It was also a threshold, where the whole room had agreed the quarrel would end. Both facts were operative at once. He rose, reached the door first and opened it for the man with a small bow. The man stopped on the sill. Then he turned back to the table.

From that hearing on, Kaito read every room twice: what was happening in it, and what the room believed was happening. He never stopped, and he had to keep working while he held both. He made a method of it: he matched three people's breathing, then led them half a step faster, and the decision he wanted broke out across the room.

### The Cost

At Altherion, Arbitration sent him to a council in the River Crown Council's chamber between two delegations quarrelling over escort fees, to bring both to yes. Lord Lucen Vale arranged the room. The table had no head, and he set no order of speaking. Kaede Suyori sat at his side with moths in their sleeves, and Cassiel Asterion stood at the meridian. Kaito matched the breathing of three delegates and led them. No voice rose. Someone blurted, someone agreed, and the room reached for a larger yes than either delegation had brought. Cassiel laid his line between them, and the yes shrank to the terms both would sign. Kaede cooled the heat Kaito had left. When the chamber emptied, Kaede walked out beside him. From that council on, Lucen and Cassiel sat across every table he came to. Kaede showed him how to leave a pulse in a throat with no tremor in it.

His blade kept the limit of his hands. Every cut he could make was small, so he learned where a small cut would serve: a nick at the collarbone, the flat across a wrist, the tendon that mattered. He let a swordsman win the first bind and walked past him.

The writ ended over a yes that had been sold. The officer he answered to sent him to bring a factor to terms, and he did. The officer had been paid for those terms before the hearing opened. When the losing side complained, the officer's report named the liaison who had pressed the room and left the payment out. When the inquiry closed without a finding, he burned his writ. He took a memory bead from the officer and hung it from a red cord on his sheath. It was the first.

After that he sold outcomes, and the Black Concord bought them and could deny him. He kept his debts small and numerous. He rarely threatened; he predicted, kindly, and let the man watch the prediction arrive. The courtesy in his face was real. He felt what a room felt, its fear and its heat, and he used it.

### Where They Stand

He is a court fixer and a duelist, a deniable Black Concord contractor who sells outcomes to the highest bidder. He wears white-and-black robes kissed with wine-red blooms. The lacquered staff-sheathed blade hangs across his shoulders, a walking stick, lever and gaff before it shows an edge. Red cords hang from the sheath, each holding a bead taken from someone who thought they were using him. Kaede's blue ribbon is tied among them. It means "I will follow even here."

A touch from Kaede to his wrist means enough, and Kaede's palm on his sternum evens his rhythm and keeps its intent. The Star Chamber's protocol corps has organised itself to counter him. His buyer in the Black Concord is named in no record.

He never raises his voice. He collects other people's.

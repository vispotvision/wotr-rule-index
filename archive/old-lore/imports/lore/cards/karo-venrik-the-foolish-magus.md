## Lore · The Life Behind the Card

*Written 2026-09-24 by the character-lore pass from this card and the record of House Venrik and the Eressean crown. What those sources do not state is new here.*

### Origin

Karo Venrik was born a High Elf of the Eressean line, to Saeloria, Queen of Eresse, and to Hiromi Mahuo, the summoner called the Paragon of Dominion. No record says how a Mahuo's son came to carry the Venrik name. Sandalphon Aestraen, his mother's consort, was not his father.

He grew up in the Grand Principality, among a people who recopy the rite calendar without variation. They enter every one of their dead by name in the sealed register at Velthaeir. A vacant name there is a silence in the chord.

His first glyph-whisper came to him in a dream at nine. The teachers who took him up after it were Maelorian, and under them he chose the reading side of the art. He kept a scholar's frame on purpose. He never trained his body to take a blow.

### The Making

He learned to read what a stone, a blade or a wound still kept of what had been done to it. Later he read souls, for whether they still held a recollection worth binding.

He founded the Remembrance Division on that reading. The research under it had no licence. He took the Ledger of Forgotten Faces out of a sealed archive in an Eressean academic wing without sanction and hid it from confiscation, and its grey pages filled with the faces he met. In Eresse a standard nobody witnessed is held contingently. The Crown called the Division a parlour trick.

The research came before the Judicariate Circle of Eresse at Vaelmarr, inside its window, with its witnesses named and paid. Vaelen Mourcrix, the Imperial Inquisitor, weighed it by what was missing from it and found for the Division. The Circle overturned him on the licence. The floor where he gave the verdict kept it anyway. Then the Crown put a choice to Karo: the Division or the succession.

He went into that choice carrying the question of whether a self can be divided and remain one thing. He kept the Division. He surrendered a succession for a discipline the Crown calls a parlour trick, and was exiled from the Grove-Spired throne. The fracture in him ran along the line of the succession. He came out of it able to set a piece of himself down somewhere he is not. Ever since, he has argued that he chose it.

### The Cost

The exile took the Crown, the succession, and a name that opened doors. He never called it a sacrifice. Almost every other insult he took without a change in his face. Over the parlour trick he ended two working relationships.

Every Echo he fielded spoke in his voice a half-beat late, and each had to consent to be recalled. Across eleven years he recalled all but two. He wrote their names down. No instrument in the four quarters could tell him whether what came back each time was what left. He set down in Division papers how a fragment might come to prefer being someone else. Then he went on fielding them.

Twice his reading erased the record it was taking. Both times the record was in a person.

He brewed a dose of the recursive draft Mnemonis under Vaelen's reluctant supervision, and attempted a Wellspring shift. No record says how it went.

### Where They Stand

Karo stands in the middle of the fracture, and it is still running. Under strain his hazel eyes double their own reflection. He wears the Fragment Veil Cloak, and anyone who looks twice sees slightly the wrong number of Karos in the room. He cuts his ash-brown hair with his own hand, badly. He knows the threshold that would make the Echoes safe. He has not crossed it. He still checks the list of two names.

He is called the Foolish Magus. He explains at length, in the long periodic sentences of the Eressean register, and is not believed.

He works under Concord oversight in the Division he founded, and Sevrin Valeith, a colleague from the Accord gone rogue, runs him as a reluctant informant. His name is engraved on the Wall of Resonance beneath the Concord Citadel, where only the High Concordant may walk. It is there for the Siege of Epprenea. Raivhar Drakonis fought there with his horns anchored in the Wellspring under the siege ground, and the Wellspring began to fail. Karo read it down through the horns and turned his own Essence into harmonic resonance to hold it. It held. Raivhar was declared rogue after the Kyrenshade Massacre, and the two are estranged.

His wife, Asta, is the Skyfang Queen, and part of the discipline in her is his. The Venrik line holds the Grove-Spired throne. He is exiled from it.

His daughter, Ilyria Draven Morningstar, kills armoured knights on contract, in a helm that began with his tuning. He is alive and knows what she does. He says nothing about it.

### Ties

- **Hiromi Mahuo** · father and son. His father, whom no record puts in a room with him.
- **Saeloria, Queen of Eresse** · mother and son. His mother; no record says what passed between them when he chose the Division.
- **Sandalphon Aestraen · The Reverent** · kin by marriage. His mother's consort, and an exile from the same court.
- **Asta Venrik · Wyvern Marshal Emeritus** · spouses. His wife, chosen as queen over every conservative house that lobbied for a gentler bride, who rides with the discipline he hammered in.
- **Ilyria Draven Morningstar · Monochrome** · father and daughter. She wears his blue sash; he says nothing of her work.
- **Vaelen Mourcrix · The Exculpate Arbiter** · kin; the weighed and the weigher. Vaelen weighed the Division at Vaelmarr and found for it, was overturned, and watched over his Mnemonis dose with reluctance; whether House Mourcrix makes them kin is still asked on Vaelen's card.
- **Raivhar Drakonis · The Ashen Horn** · comrades, now estranged. He held the Wellspring Raivhar was anchored in at Epprenea, and they are estranged.
- **Sevrin Valeith · Maw of Nine Tongues** · informant. A former Accord colleague who runs him as a reluctant informant, on a hold no record names.
- **Shael Virellion · Oracle of the Broken Mirror** · the ruling. He declared Shael's transformation unpunishable under Absolute Magical Law, citing divine interference.

## Lore · The Life Behind the Card

*Written 2026-09-24 by the character-lore pass from this card and the record of Sun and sand. What those sources do not state is new here.*

### Origin

Khaeron Vant was a free Therosian, and he came up through the fighting pits with no house to answer for him. He was wiry and corded. In the pits he learned to take a hurt and turn it into the next blow. He learned the feint-cut and the short, explosive burst, and he learned to end a fight before a long one could wear him down.

Between fights he found his way to the Cinerion-aligned cults and learned their ash-fire. A burning left ash and char behind it. Other practitioners let it drift. Khaeron kept it, worked it into brands, and drew his strength back out of what his fires had spent.

All of this came before the Pact. It was the part of his memory the Shrine of Four Knives would later shave away, a sliver at a time.

### The Making

A minor cult of Abythron took him under a Pact, and the Pact brought him the horned mask. The spirit in the mask wanted his body. Some days it nearly had it.

His vertebrae grew into a lattice of sigils, and burning blades hung from it at his flanks and shoulders, up to four at once. He carried a pair of curved knives, the Ash-Gorge blades. Their inscriptions drank the colour out of blood and left it drifting as grey ash. The people his brands killed left dim echoes that pressed into glyphs on the pendants at his chest. When a fight ran him dry he could burn one for strength. Each one he burned went further out of the record.

Then he came far enough to see his own soul from outside. He looked at his own record, every break in him that had since been made to bear load, and he could not stop seeing it. Most practitioners who came that far fought their record. Khaeron signed his, the way a man signs a contract he has already decided to honour. He accepted himself as the mask's vessel. That let the spirit anchor in his spine without taking him over completely. Since then the horned mask has argued with him. It has never fully owned him.

### The Cost

Every casting of the Shrine shaved a sliver from what he remembered of the time before the Pact. The pits lay in that part of him. The current that erased his dead took a firmer hold on him with each brand and each sliver. Pushed past its first activation in a fight, the Shrine could hand his body to the mask for a spell and leave him gaps where the fighting had been.

Raelen Vor'Skai came to him among the cults, looking for a knife that could cut a binding. Khaeron took the work and the gold. He told Raelen what he told everyone who stood before his knives: "I am the altar and the knife. You only choose how much of you burns." On the red dunes he cut into Raelen's palm with the Ash-Gorge blades, and the blood went grey and drifted off as ash. Half the mark came away. The other half held, bound past anything his blades could part. When the rite broke off, the cut half lay on neither the sand nor the blades. Neither man had it. Word of the Severing reached his cult, and from then on the cult tracked Raelen. Khaeron kept his fee and a cut he could not finish.

At the magma scar over Solfatara, the Cinerion-aligned warpriests of an Accord warband brought him to show the Accord's envoys what a licensed executioner could do. He drove four knives into the crust around the meeting and raised his Shrine. Rajhak Tarun stood inside it as the envoys' liaison, and his oath rings sounded the whole time he was near Khaeron. Xhael Ten'Kor came down and told the envoys what had happened at Solfatara, and nothing of how. Nothing burned. The casting shaved its sliver from Khaeron all the same, in front of the warpriests who had brought him there.

### Where They Stand

Khaeron Vant, the Ember Host, is a free Therosian executioner. The minor Abythron cult holds his formal allegiance and keeps him in something close to custody, and inside it he works semi-rogue. He drifts between the Cinerion-aligned cults and the Accord warbands. The warpriests still court him. They want his execution work in a form they can license, and for now they fear it. He has signed nothing for them.

His cult is still on Raelen's trail. Whenever he and Rajhak have met since the scar, Rajhak's rings have sounded. The glyphs on his pendants are what remains of the people his brands erased. He burns them still. He explains the altar he considers himself to be to anyone who asks.

Nobody knows whether cutting the horned mask from him now would leave a free man, or a man who has forgotten too much of why he wanted to be free.

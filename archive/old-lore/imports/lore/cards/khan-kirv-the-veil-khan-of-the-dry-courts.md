## Lore · The Life Behind the Card

*Written 2026-09-24 by the character-lore pass from this card and the record of The Reaper's office. What those sources do not state is new here.*

### Origin

Khan Kirv was born on the sand routes, into a caravan that held no oasis of its own. His mother led its string. She brought it in to wells held by families who raided one another by night, and whoever held a well at dawn set the price of its water. At every well she heard the terms before a single skin was filled. When she disliked them, she turned the string and walked on. The caravan went thirsty more often than it paid. It owed no well anything.

He learned the routes behind her. He learned to read a well's family from the edge of its shade, and he drank on the road from a goatskin cup. The same cup is the Khurrah now.

### The Making

He took the string when she could no longer walk the routes. A caravan that turned from bad terms at one well needed terms it could accept at the next, and he began to make them. He sat down between a well's family and the strangers at its edge and kept both talking until each named a price. He held these talks at noon. In that heat nobody had the strength to posture for long, and what was struck at noon still held when raiders came at midnight. Oases that would not speak to each other came to speak to him. Raiders found a market laid out by dawn. More of them bought than fought.

The first hospitality he saw broken was at a well he did not hold. Caravans were camped for the night under its keeper's shade. Before dawn a drover lay dead at the water, knifed by a drover of another string. The dead man's string and the killer's came from oases already at feud. Kirv was at the water when it was done.

He paid the amends himself, out of his own goods, to the dead man's people and to the keeper. He did not name the guilty party. From that morning the oasis, which was not his, answered to a law he had written out of his own pocket. Water drawn there under his word would not pour for a thief.

### The Cost

His own standing pays for that law. Every ring he draws rests on hospitality he has guaranteed in person. When blood is spilled under the Khurrah, the Khagan Sash goes dark and every ring drops at once. Nothing he draws holds again until the amends are paid, and the amends are his whether or not the blood was his doing. He carries other people's breaches as debt.

None of his workings strikes anyone. He works with a cup, a sash, a pinch of sand, a thumb of salt and a string of calcite beads, and all of it holds only where people agree there is a well to share. The wanderer's right his mother used at every bad well is the one his law cannot reach. A clan that refuses the well stands outside his writ before he has drawn it.

At the edge of the White Pans a practitioner of predatory brandcraft came in off the salt with a Black Watch kill-writ behind him. The practitioner took water under the Khurrah. Kirv had not chosen this guest, and the cup covered him for the night all the same. Kirv kept the night whole. At dawn the hospitality ended, and he opened a lawful corridor from the oasis out across the pans. Rain crossed the salt that morning. It blurred his mirage sense, and Khasir Aluto set isoclines along the corridor and kept the evidence dry. Commander Severin Bale drove his Gravepins at the corridor's mouth and spoke the name there, outside the ring. The writ Yukizumi Yukari had ratified closed clean. The sash stayed clean.

### Where They Stand

He is the desert arbiter, caravan-lord and sanctuary broker of the Dry Courts, the neutral league of oasis-cities. He brokers safe passage between feuding oases. By dawn he turns raid-nights into markets, and his heat courts still sit at noon, so the treaties struck there survive midnight. He wears black-and-gold hooded raiment cut for wind and for audience. His palm-wraps are inked with knot-script.

The league is treaty partner to the Guild Accord, and he serves the Black Watch as an occasional liaison for sand-route warrants. When a kill-writ must cross the dunes, he opens the corridor. The warrants he brokers carry prices. Zahir Ashveil and Kaerith Vireth race each other for them.

Cyrille Lacrim's saintwater rides his caravan lattice, and together they pull cities out of stampedes. He cools a crowd so that Deimos eats only what is owned. In a council he sets the shade, and Lord Lucen Vale and Cassiel Asterion set the seating. Ocellith counts what a truce will really cost. When rain blurs his mirage sense he leans on Khasir, whose worst case is a rainless brass court at high noon, the hour Kirv keeps.

Nobody knows whether a spring with no owner is a well he can share, or the one thing a writ built on consent cannot reach.

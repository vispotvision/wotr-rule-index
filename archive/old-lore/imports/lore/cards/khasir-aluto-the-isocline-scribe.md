## Lore · The Life Behind the Card

*Written 2026-09-24 by the character-lore pass from this card and the record of The Reaper's office. What those sources do not state is new here.*

### Origin

Khasir Aluto was born in Altherion, the republic on the Ring's rim, in a timber house built over stone. His mother was a layer-out. When someone died nearby, the family sent for her. She washed the body, bound the jaw and had it laid on the stone of the ground floor, where the cold kept longest.

The house above the body went on living. Kin came in off the snow in steaming coats. The kitchen fire burned. Every visitor's warmth drifted down the stair. Khasir went with his mother and kept the room. He learned which wall took the wind, where the heat pooled under the beams and which door stayed shut through a sitting. He could judge a room with the back of his hand before he could spell his name.

His mother could say within the hour whether the Echo had gone. She kept a book of everyone she had laid out, a name and a day in ink for each. He learned his letters making those entries.

### The Making

He entered the Guild Accord as a Star Chamber clerk for Evidence and Preservation. A scene of death had to stay as it was found until someone with the right to read it came. His thumb signet kept a wound, a footprint or the lipstick on a cup from changing for a day. His metal nib wrote on air, stone and steam. He drew a pale curve in the air. Frost beaded along it. The room came to one temperature from end to end.

The work that made him came in a dyer's house in high summer. The dyer lay dying in the back room, beside vats still hot from the morning's boil. Steam ran on the walls. Fever had heated the man, and his crowding kin heated the room further. His last sentence came slowly, and in that heat it was spoiling before he could finish it. Khasir inked a line around the bed. The heat had nowhere to go, so he drew the cold for it out of his own hands. He inked a second line, and more, and before the last he could not feel his fingers. He held the quill in his fist. He wrote the sentence down as it was spoken.

It was the first time he chose to lose sensation in his own hands so that a dying sentence would not go unrecorded. That choice is the record his Crystal keeps. From that room on, a room's temperature answered to a line he wrote.

### The Cost

The cold he drew was his own. After enough lines his fingers went numb and his pen went clumsy, the one thing a scribe cannot afford. He carried the numbness home.

He carried it home from the White Pans of the Varakh Drylands, where a Black Watch kill-writ had run south on a sand route. At dawn Khan Kirv opened a lawful corridor from an oasis across the salt. Rain crossed the pans that morning and blurred the Khan's mirage sense. Khasir set isoclines along the corridor and put the marks and tracks the writ rested on under his signet. They stayed dry and unchanged until the Black Watch entered them. Commander Severin Bale spoke the name at the corridor's mouth. The writ closed clean.

The Polar Codex has finite clean pages, and a page written over muddles a voice forever. He wrote over none of them.

In Vaultmere a child lay dying, and the clause of an instrument turned on how the child had been hurt. Moros Pellayne seated the Measure at the bedside. The child's own words would have decided it. Khasir held the Codex open at a clean page to take the child's last breath. The guardian withheld assent. He closed the book. The child spoke to the guardian, and nobody wrote it down. His seal kept the wound unchanged for the day, and the wound could not answer the clause. The Measure was lost. The page stayed clean.

He had given the feeling in his hands for a dyer's sentence. He gave this one up for a guardian's no.

### Where They Stand

He is the Star Chamber's adjunct for Evidence and Preservation and the Reaper's courier for fragile minutes. He travels with Yukizumi Yukari and carries the brittle last breaths, opened only when the room deserves them. He preserves scenes for Moros, keeping evidence honest and witnesses comfortable. He lays safe geometry for Lord Lucen Vale and Cassiel Asterion when heat and damp would wreck protocol.

A noon desert overfills his line. A drum-Domain shakes it apart. When the air is too hot or the beat too loud, he needs Kaede Suyori or Maelia Cantorine first. The Khan holds his heat courts at noon. A rainless brass court at high noon is Khasir's worst case.

He wears fur-collared field whites cut like a snow-lily, with fine script stitched along the petal flaps, and ink-black gloves. His breath is always a little colder than the room.

Nobody knows whether a pressed breath that has smeared can ever be read true again.

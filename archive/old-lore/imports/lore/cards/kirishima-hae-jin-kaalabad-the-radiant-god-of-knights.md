## Lore · The Life Behind the Card

*Written 2026-09-24 by the character-lore pass from this card and the record of The Curia at Altherion. What those sources do not state is new here.*

### Origin

Kirishima Hae-jin came of a house older than the two registers his name is made from. Kirishima belongs to the Ketsuen house register and Hae-jin to the Mahuo register of given names, and his line stood before the split between them. It stood before Sum-gol was incorporated, too. The province there now was built over his line and does not descend from it. No house in the valley keeps the Kirishima on its rolls.

Nobody alive remembers him before the corruption, and every folk telling agrees on what he was. He was the knight other knights measured themselves against. He had no gift for cruelty and no hunger for a crown. He held, past all bearing, that an oath sworn was an oath that could not be unsworn. No telling says to whom he swore his own. Before the Fall they called him the Radiant God of Knights.

### The Making

The certainty lasted. Something cracked the rest of him open, and the folk tale does not name it.

Azerack found him in that state. By every surviving account the two did not meet in open battle. What passed between them was closer to a bargain, struck with a man already broken open, and at the end of it Azerack gave him the Halberd of Zarakal. The weapon agreed with what was already rotting in him. What came back from that exchange wore a knight's memories like a coat two sizes too small.

### The Cost

He kept a knight's discipline and pointed it at a warlord's appetite. His campaign ran out from Sum-gol, and his Divine Realm manifested across the valley and the Ketsuen frontier around it. Ground he struck, bled on or held under fighting went dark and cold, and its native growth drew back. Where he stayed long, or killed in numbers, it became his for good. The land took his shape from one point of failure outward until the failure was the whole territory. The Tower of Zarakal rose at its centre. The forges of the Realm were lit, and darksteel came from nowhere else in the Inner World. He conquered the way he had once served. He did it completely, without negotiation, and with no pleasure anyone could see.

The campaign had run its course before the Great Spirit War's chronology began to count, and generals on both sides of that war used his name to frighten recruits.

Every claim was permanent, and every claim narrowed what he could ever again call unspoiled. He was among the things it narrowed. The Radiant God's bearing went. Under the horned crest what showed of his face turned to shadow and cavity, and men looked away from it before they understood why.

He never once out-thought an opponent. It cost him once, and permanently. The folk tale that tells the rest of his life is silent on how he was brought down. He was chained and left alive. The Accord and Sancta Lux struck the working together, at a price neither ever fully disclosed to the other. They held him within the Veil, bound at its threshold. Nothing on the Material Plane could hold a warlord tied to his own Realm, and nothing on the Soul Plane could hold his body.

The chains took his weight and never his stance. Bent to a stoop he could not straighten from, he still read as upright. Several accounts hold that the bearing existed so that nobody would ever see him kneel. Chained, he yielded none of the ground he had claimed. The plate he was chained in was darksteel, and centuries in it fused its seams shut.

When the Realm drew back from Sum-gol it left a scar in the province: Sum-gol's Hanseon compact, the Kept Vigil. It went on drawing on a debt nobody living had incurred. The incorporation treaty forbade anyone to survey it. In Sum-gol it became the one ground nobody measured and nobody went near.

### Where They Stand

The Accord files him as the Chained Crown: imprisoned, chained, his Realm intact and active at range. The Tower stands in territory nobody has reclaimed. It has never needed a repair. The Realm's forges still run hot with nobody feeding them. The claim holds without his attention. Tellers give him a line: "Bound in chains, yet my wrath endures. Fear the name Kaalabad, for I shall rise again." No living witness confirms he said it.

Under that folk name he serves now among Kujo's generals, with Kujo's leave to be seen standing. He fights with Zarakal. He has said he was put somewhere quiet and came back out. A band of old scar runs the full circumference of his throat above the gorget, and his voice comes past it strangled. In the council chamber beneath Vaeloris he asked Kujo for three houses as a wage. The first he named was the Mahuo: "What is left of them."

That mid-morning he came out of the sky above a village at Sum-gol and brought down the Star Crusher. A barrier braided from Valorin and Mahuo work declined it. The village lived. Its road did not. At Altherion the Primate, born a Mahuo of Sum-gol, stopped his halberd on two fingers. On the Ashgate road the general pinned Wren Greymane to the ditch bank, and Kwon Mu-jin turned his pull on its own axis and stripped him. He was last seen alive and advancing. The Accord has not revised its filing.

### Ties

- **Kaalabad** · same person. Kujo's general carries his folk name and fights with Zarakal.
- **Aurelian Prudentius Custos Clausorum · The Primate** · one valley. His Fall left the Kept Vigil in the Sum-gol that raised Aurelian, and the order Aurelian rose in helped chain him.
- **Ara Min Mahuo** · one valley. Ara is of the house at Sum-gol; no record has them meet.
- **Azerack** · corruptor. Azerack found him already cracked open and gave him the Halberd of Zarakal, and he neither loves nor trusts him.
- **The jailers** · captors. The Accord and Sancta Lux chained him, and neither told the other the price.

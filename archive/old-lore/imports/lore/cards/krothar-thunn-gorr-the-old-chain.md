## Lore · The Life Behind the Card

*Written 2026-09-24 by the character-lore pass from this card and the record of Old World roads and chains. What those sources do not state is new here.*

### Origin

Krothar Thunn-Gorr was born on Gorr-Keth, the tallest island of the Hobgoblin Expanse, whose peak glowed orange at night when the wind shifted. His blood was the Crimson Order's, of the Kha-Urrum blood-branch. It came out to the standard: red-ochre skin, gold Legion Etchings along the jaw, brass eyes. Nothing in him diverged.

The Order kept him under its Vow of Silence for the first sixteen years of his life. He learned the drills and the field-signs. On the shelf the boats came in on volcanic-glass hulls, and one dock there had been fished by the same Bugbear family for four generations.

Freda Vaal-Kess met him on the shelf docks while he had not yet spoken a word outside the Vow. She came of an old civilian line outside the Order. She talked enough for both of them. The courtship lasted six years. One aunt called it scandalous.

### The Making

At sixteen he stood on the shelf and watched a visiting Ironblood warband chief break a fishing dock in half during a trade dispute. It was the Bugbear family's dock. The chief walked away without consequence, because nobody on the shelf could stop him.

Krothar did not fight the Orc. He was sixteen, and the Orc was three hundred pounds. He picked up the dock chain the Orc had snapped, wound it around his fist and carried it home. The chain had been the Bugbears'.

He trained with chains from that day. A chain was what had been lying on the ground when he decided to become dangerous, and he kept to it. He learned force first and discipline after it. The first thing he mastered was the weighted throw that wraps a man and stops him where he is. Then he learned to plant.

By the time he left the Expanse at twenty-two, he could take the dock back from anyone.

### The Cost

He fought on contract for seventy years, a Guild Accord contractor in the Old World's western quarter, the New World's eastern arc, the Inner World frontier and the edge-territories of the Outer World. The Old World circuits named him the Gorr-Keth Reaver. He broke no contract, abandoned none and left no job unfinished, and the Accord entered him in its records as "consistent". He did not chase. He drove his chain-ends into the ground on either side, set his feet, and made standing still near him more dangerous than running.

On the frontier march the Caravan Families hired him to escort their trains. Every contract ran to its term. When a raid came he planted with his chains in the ground and let it come to him, and when it broke he let it go. He spoke seldom and explained nothing. The Families trusted his work and understood little else of him. His pay went home to Gorr-Keth on the papers Freda kept: pay schedules, guild insurance, repatriation clauses.

The work marked him. A break across the bridge of his nose healed without being set. Scar tissue from later fighting cut across several of the gold lines on his jaw and neck, and he never had them re-inscribed. His fighting chains were re-forged after the Outer World campaign. He was offered better armor than the Order-pattern scale he had trained in, and he kept the scale, because it did not have opinions. Of the chain he said, when he said anything: "Chain does the same thing every time. That's why I trust it."

Each time he came home the house had gone on without him. Freda ran the family's constituency seat in the Keth-Gorrum while he was away, and in his whole life he attended twelve sessions of the parliament and voted in nine. Then he left again.

Their son was born green, the green of lichen on wet stone, where the Order runs red-ochre. The Order noticed. The boy carried his father's name. Freda raised him on Gorr-Keth while his father was on contract, and in the seasons Krothar was home he trained the boy in the caldera yard with chains, because chains were what he knew. The weighted throw came first. At seventeen the boy passed his father's ceiling, and Krothar watched him do it. Freda watched it too. They did not discuss it.

The son left the Expanse with his father's chain technique. What grew on top of it did not come from Krothar: silver chains that answer the son unsummoned, and a black light that comes only with his restraints off. Krothar looked at his son's unmanaged output and made a decision. He forged sigil shackles for the son's wrists, forearms and throat, and the son accepted them. Afterward he took his own fighting chains to the same forge, cut the same inscriptions into them, and spent the better part of a season on the work. That year he said to Freda, "I taught him the chain. I taught him the weight. I taught him the arc and the snap and the bind. I did not teach him what comes after. Nobody taught him what comes after. That is the problem." Freda has never repeated it.

### Where They Stand

Krothar has come off contract work and lives on the Thunn-Gorr holdings on the caldera's western shelf. He plants when he stands and does not shift. When something troubles him he reaches for the chains coiled over his shoulders and re-settles them without knowing he does it. Freda has watched him do it ten thousand times. The olive wool scarf at his throat is the sixth. She made the first.

He wants to sit on the caldera shelf with Freda and watch the sun hit the water, and to be done. He is not done.

The guild circuits call him Chain-Father now, never to his face, since his son's reputation spread. The son goes by Veylshroud. He does not write and he does not visit. On a ridge in the Scourge of Hell, after a fight, the son reached for the chains at his shoulders to resettle them. The shackles Krothar forged were made for less than the son has become, and hairline fractures now show in the metal. The son has not contacted the man who made them. Krothar does not understand all of what he sees in his son, and the part he does understand is the part that required suppression. He has theories about the black light. He keeps them.

Freda has worn the ear-drop he carved since their wedding, bone-white volcanic glass from the shelf islands. Neither of them will say what shape it is. The one time their son asked, they looked at each other and changed the subject together.

On the frontier march the Caravan Families' old hands still tell of the Hobgoblin who did not chase, and they told a young courier there that he stood the way the Old Chain had stood. Krothar has no way of knowing it.

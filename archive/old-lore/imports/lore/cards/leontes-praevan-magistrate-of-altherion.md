## Lore · The Life Behind the Card

*Written 2026-09-24 by the character-lore pass from this card and the record of The courts of waking and sleeping law. What those sources do not state is new here.*

### Origin

Leontes Praevan was born in a chartered market town of the Concord heartland, above the room where his father took fees for attestation. People paid there to have a lease or a bond witnessed and entered on the roll. Every Accord record went down in Lampblack Register Ink. The ink stopped no forger. An alteration made after the entry showed under Concord light, to any clerk who thought to look.

Many who came could not read what they had paid to have written. Between neighbours they kept split tallies. They made a mark under the entry and trusted the clerk. The boy learned his letters there. He copied entries into a practice book until his hand could not be told from the register's. The fixed wording came with them: "A clause improved is a clause void."

### The Making

He went into the Guild Accord's service as a clerk and rose to an ordinary audit post, Concord Auditor. The work was reading. An institution's own books were set before him, and he set them against what it had done.

From that post he watched an institution fail from the inside. He resigned the audit post after it. He calls what he watched the Fall, a name he keeps for himself, and he has never dated it for anyone.

Afterwards he built a career on the proposition that written law is the only restraint left. The world he worked in mostly spoke. He learned Runecraft, the craft of the sentence cut into a substrate that outlives whoever cut it. He learned to set glyphs into plain text. Whoever read such a page acknowledged it, and the acknowledgement bound them. His seal, once affixed, could be countered by no mortal means without metaphysical backlash.

The Fall had left a wound. His life had come to rest on it. He took that wound and made it structure, consciously and on purpose, and left it unhealed so that it would hold weight. His answer to the Fall was better paperwork. He has never once treated that as a joke. Altherion gave him the chief seat of its Central Concord Tribunal, and the Neutral Codex Guild named him its Patron Magistrum.

### The Cost

Once a clause of his has been read, he cannot recall it. An error in it misfires continuously, on everyone who passes it, for as long as the carving holds, until somebody finds it and breaks it. Leontes knows this better than anyone alive. He drafts anyway.

His letters go sealed, by an ordinary courier, to people who have done nothing yet. They read, and the reading is the working. Every threat he has ever posed arrived by post.

A menstruum called Scribe's Wash can lift a Parunic inscription off its substrate and leave the material whole. Forgers use it to move a legitimate seal onto a lot that never earned one. Lampblack Register Ink does not lift. Any seal that lifts was never Bench-struck, and a clerk under Concord light can tell. Most readers cannot tell by eye. At his father's counter he had watched people put their marks under entries they could not check. Everything he has built depends on readers in the same position.

People in his presence straighten and lower their voices as if standing trial, and it does not stop at his dinner table. He has never moved quickly in his life. Oaths hold on him because he keeps them.

At Altherion two carrying houses came before him over lanes Orivane Caelthys had charted through the Outer World's drifting realms. The master of one house had signed the settlement and sworn to keep it in Atheron Dreymarch's presence. A season later he sent a ship down the other house's lane. The chain of that oath summoned Atheron into his sleep. Atheron laid a geas on his will. The other house sued for the season's carriage it had lost. Riven Eltamyr Vonnel argued that the geas was a sentence and that no man could be sentenced twice for one breach. Lord Maerion Lysandre answered that a ruling given in a dream paid no carrier. Atheron came blindfolded and said the chain still held. Leontes asked for the written settlement. He read it, ruled on it and on nothing else, and gave the house its carriage. The master went home liable awake and bound asleep.

### Where They Stand

Leontes Praevan is High Magistrate of Altherion and Chief Arbiter of its Central Concord Tribunal. He remembers every word ever written in his court, because the court keeps them and he can read it. Maerion's words on dream-law are there.

Caius Veylan, his longtime colleague, shares the belief his career was built on. The Guild of Broken Binds counts him the greatest threat to unregulated summoning in the frontier kingdoms. Whatever reaches them from him will come by courier.

The Fall is still his private name, and still undated. His line is that no tyrant ever needed power, "only the silence of those who feared to put it into ink."

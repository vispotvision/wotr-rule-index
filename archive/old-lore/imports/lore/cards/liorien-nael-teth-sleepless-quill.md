## Lore · The Life Behind the Card

*Written 2026-09-24 by the character-lore pass from this card and the record of Road-folk and the Eastward Isles. What those sources do not state is new here.*

### Origin

Liorien Nael-Teth was born Veyran, to a Brine-Touched Scribe line, a family that wrote for its living. No record he has kept says where the line began. The brine was in the blood. It showed in the hand. From his first lines his script moved like water over sand.

He grew up small and never filled out. He stayed soft in the body and underfed, with deep umber skin, amber eyes and teal-blue hair that the wind would not leave lying flat.

The Archive of the Eastward Isles took him on. It kept shelves of other people's testimony, and a few older things that its senior archivists held bound. The sea brought its wrecks to the door. After the wrecks came the people who had seen them.

### The Making

The Archive set him to taking testimony. He took it from pilots, net-menders and the crews of boats that came home short. In time the Guild Accord contracted him too, for maritime cases and for any case that turned on memory. Every working he learned was a written one. With no paper to hand he wrote on breath-fog, on sea spray, on the dust of a floor. A line written there held as an inscription holds, and it wore away as one wears.

The rooms he worked in grew quieter. Witnesses lowered their voices across the table from him and told him the truth, often sooner than they had meant to. Panic went flat near him. A frightened man could finish a sentence.

He kept a dream-journal every morning. When he had gone long enough without sleep, his half-lidded stare lifted faint overlays off a person or an object, the traces of what it had witnessed. He wrote those down too. The Isles had names for him by then: the Drift-Scribe, the Tide-Ink Archivist, the Little Book of Storms. The one that stayed was the Sleepless Quill.

His first genuine bond formed at that desk. It formed to the ledger itself, to the act of keeping testimony, and no ally's name was ever written into it.

### The Cost

The Echo-Current Folio was his largest working, and he paid for it in his own memory. Each major casting wrote part of him into the ink. What he transcribed afterwards he kept. What he left untranscribed went hollow, hours of it, sometimes days. So he copied his own days out before he slept, and he kept no open flame near a page he had not yet copied.

On the Broken Shell Night an ancient leviathan's echo, which the Archive had held bound, came loose inside it. Liorien raised the Folio. His charm-papers broke into a small storm and closed in a half-dome over the echo. The dome caught it, played it back and caught it again, for as long as the senior archivists needed to bind it a second time. They bound it. The casting took several months of his memory. Afterwards those months were whatever the pages had kept. The same night the apprentice Cyrus Vellhar fled the Archive with a shard of its Dream-Tideglass. The dome recorded what happened inside it, and Cyrus was never inside it.

In the Drowned Ledger Affair a Black Concord cell set out to erase a fishing village's whole testimony of a smuggling route. He never stood in a room with any of them. He rebuilt the testimony alone, line by line, from whatever the erasure had missed. His ledger survived them. He had written other people's worst days down whole, and written months of his own into the ink. Asked why he kept paying, he had one answer. "If no one writes it down, the world pretends it never happened."

### Where They Stand

He is an archivist-member of the Archive of the Eastward Isles. The Guild Accord still contracts him. His testimony stands as precedent in three of its coastal courts. When a court asks him how a clause works, he tells it plainly and in full, in the voice he uses to take testimony.

The Archive has sent people after Cyrus Vellhar since he fled, and once it sent Liorien. He came to a coastal town Cyrus had already left. The children there remembered the drifter. Near Liorien they spoke softly and told the truth, and they showed him the small illusions Cyrus had taught them for no pay. He wrote down what they showed him and what they said. He went back to the Archive with their testimony and without Cyrus, and the page went into its record of him.

He is assembling a forbidden True Ledger of the Sea Wars, which both Concord and Crowns would rather stayed unwritten. It is not finished. The quill at his collar is stained at the tip. Whether the months he wrote into the ink can ever come back to him, nobody knows. He still raises the Folio when a thing needs keeping.

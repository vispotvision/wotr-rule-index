## Lore · The Life Behind the Card

*Written 2026-09-24 by the character-lore pass from this card and the record of The courts of waking and sleeping law. What those sources do not state is new here.*

### Origin

Lord Maerion Lysandre was born in the Concord heartland, to a house that held a lordship in one of the countries that kept its own law under the Concord's one crown. The Accord ran beside crown law there. The two had grown so far into each other that few people in the heartland could say which of them had issued the rule they were obeying.

The market square lay below his father's house. On Veyra's day the market inspector worked it with his small hammer and his book, and a short loaf was the commonest charge on his roll. The boy followed him from stall to stall and asked, of each short loaf, whose law it had broken. The inspector could not always tell him. The bakers paid the amercement set to their means and never asked.

He was sent to the chancery schools. The clerks taught him the formulary and the entry in ink, and the proverb "What has been said stands said."

### The Making

The school kept a fencing floor. He went to it until he could hold a guard, and after that he gave its hours to the Seven Treatises of Concord Strategy. He mastered all seven. Set before a battlefield or a quarrel between crowns, he read it from the first reports and moved before the other side had finished reading.

He entered the Arbitration Division and argued in the Concord's trials. Riven Eltamyr Vonnel argued against him often, and each of them won and lost to the other. The Division raised Maerion to its Concord High Court, where he judged the disputes the whole Concord watched. When war spread across realms, he commanded in the field as well. Soldiers who heard his orders stood steadier. Fear found less purchase on them while his voice carried, and so did any working meant to turn them. The Concord began to call him its Lion.

He learned to bind a spoken law into a blow. A foe who heard it had to obey, or halt where he stood, for as long as a breath.

Then a line he commanded began to break. He had no strength of his own to put into it. He asked for something larger. It answered, and it was a co-author. Around him opened a courtroom without end, ringed by the banners of every Concord realm. A law he spoke inside it bound like a physical force, and whoever broke it was struck at once. From that day the courtroom enforced his rulings itself.

### The Cost

The law he spoke on that field bound him as it bound the enemy. He kept it. He stood inside his own banners among his soldiers, in a body that had left the fencing floor early.

When the war ended, Arbitration reviewed the engagement, as it reviewed every engagement the Concord fought, and weighed each death as a moral and a metaphysical consequence. Maerion sat before his own Division. He answered for every order he had given.

At Altherion he argued before Leontes Praevan for a carrying house. A rival master had signed a settlement dividing their lanes through the Outer World's drifting realms, and had sworn to keep it in Atheron Dreymarch's presence. A season later he sent a ship down the house's lane. The chain of that oath summoned Atheron into his sleep, and Atheron laid a geas on his will. The house sued for the carriage it had lost. Riven argued for the master: the geas was a sentence, and no man could be sentenced twice for one breach. Maerion answered that a ruling given in a dream paid no carrier. Dream-law, he said, was too abstract for waking politics. Atheron came to the tribunal blindfolded, and he told it the chain still held. Leontes asked for the written settlement. He read it, ruled on it and on nothing else, and gave the house its carriage.

Maerion won. His words about Atheron went into the record of a court that forgot nothing written, and Atheron had been in the room to hear them.

### Where They Stand

His courtroom still answers him, and he can carry fragments of it onto a battlefield or into a council chamber. The law still applies to him inside it. A law must still be spoken to bind. An enemy who breaks nothing stands there untouched, and then Maerion has to fight that enemy himself. The courtroom enforces his rulings. The co-author has signed nothing.

He goes to the Everbloom when Wellspring law in the natural realms needs upholding, since Thalyren Veyroot cannot leave it. Kaien Veyren serves the Regency under his patronage. Maerion values the envoy's composure and mistrusts the weeks in which he vanishes. He still meets Riven across Concord trials. He still respects Atheron's office and still thinks its rulings too abstract for waking politics.

He says his creed in court and in the field alike: "A sword cuts the body. A law cuts the will. A just hand must know when to wield both."

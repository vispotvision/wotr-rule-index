## Lore · The Life Behind the Card

*Written 2026-09-24 by the character-lore pass from this card and the record of The courts of waking and sleeping law. What those sources do not state is new here.*

### Origin

Lucien Draeve was born in the Concord, over a brewhouse in a market district where ale sold at the fixed price. His mother was one of the sworn tasters who assayed it. On brewing days she went from house to house with her own cup, drank a mouthful from each new cask and said whether it stood at the price.

The boy carried the cup. He learned from her that a cask could be sound on the day it was broached and sour later, with nothing added to it in between. She drank from every cask she was paid to judge, the bad with the good. Once a bad cask put her to bed. She was back on her round as soon as she could stand, and she took him with her.

### The Making

He came into the Guild Accord's Research and Archives Division as an alchemist of medicines. Any brew he touched with Essence changed in moments. Under his fingers a harmless draught became a potent toxin, and a toxin went back to a draught. He threw nothing out. A failed batch went onto a shelf, and later he turned it into something the Division could use.

A faint green vapour came off him in those years, too faint for most people to see. In small doses it did no harm. Anyone who sat across a table from him for a long sitting grew a little confused and slow to react. The Division's committees sat long. He won his arguments there.

His Domain opened in a sickroom where every bed breathed the same air. He asked the air to carry a remedy to all of them. The Domain answered. Within it every airborne particle became his instrument, to heal, kill or alter at a flick of thought, and the answering went further than a chemist's work should reach. It was neither reliable nor free.

### The Cost

Then several of his political rivals in the Division were removed by what everyone called unfortunate accidents. Nobody could say whether they were his work or the Domain's. He never said.

He left Research and Archives openly after that. He went to the Black Concord's Toxin Arts Division, to assassination and sabotage. Elder Seravain never left. The two of them went on trading venoms and antidotes.

The Domain ended where the air ended. Sealed lungs, a vacuum or a hard wind left him a man with jars. Once he started the alkahest, it ran on its own and made no distinction between his structures and anyone else's. His gold-inlaid containers kept every other mixture stable until use. None of them could hold the alkahest. It could not be stored in anything, and that included him.

He never struck anyone. He had an alchemist's body, and anyone who reached him could end him. He made the poison, and somebody else usually administered it. "Every sip is a choice," he said. "Every choice is a death. I simply make sure the two arrive together."

That kept his hands off the cup. It also made his work depend on a drinker who had sworn nothing to a dream-arbiter. He disliked Atheron Dreymarch, said dream chains ruined his work, and avoided dealing with him directly.

Hazzen Skollgrin served the Black Concord too. For research, Lucien poisoned him more than once. Hazzen found it hilarious.

### Where They Stand

Lucien Draeve serves the Black Concord as a covert operative of its Toxin Arts Division, a master alchemist and a poison-savant. He is called the Gilded Toxin and the Smiling Arsenist. The green vapour still comes off him in every room he sits in.

Seravain still works in the Ophidian Studies Cell of Research and Archives, and the two of them are still trading, as they have for centuries. One stayed and one went. Orivane Caelthys keeps his dealings with Lucien minimal and believes his toxic alchemy is "a corrosion of the greater weave." Lucien still has no direct dealings with Atheron.

His Aether mirrors his moral alignment to anyone who reads it closely. Nobody has read him closely enough yet. The accidents are still unexplained.

## Lore · The Life Behind the Card

*Written 2026-09-24 by the character-lore pass from this card and the record of The Rot Host. What those sources do not state is new here.*

### Origin

Morphaene was born on the Mirror Marsh in the Thousand-River Basin of the Outer World, among the fishing confederacies that lived under negotiated tribute on draconic ground. His mother kept a still-house at the edge of the reed beds. She cut medicinal reeds and laid them in steeping vats until they softened and began to rot. Then she ran them through glass gourds on a steam pipe and drew off what the rot had loosened.

The boy tended the vats. He learned to put his face over one and tell by the smell which day the reeds would give up their oil and which day they would spoil. A vat on the turn was medicine, and it was rot, and he was taught to keep both in mind while he worked it. His mother sold the oil to the sick-houses along the rivers. She taught him to read a sick man as he read a vat: the colour at the lips, the smell of the breath, the day the turn would come.

### The Making

In a mountain flood year the rivers came down off the Spine and drowned the Flooding Forest. When the water went down, a fever came up out of the mud. His mother sent him through the sick-houses with the oil. The sick asked him whether they would mend. He read each one and told each one what he had read. His mother told him to say that all of them would mend. He would not say it.

The fever found him in the last sick-house on his round. He was ladling oil into a fisherman's cup when he looked down at his own hand. It was his hand. It was also his hand decomposing, grey at the knuckles and opening from inside, and that was a present fact. The hand kept its weight and gained a second one. He went on ladling. He dosed every pallet in the house before morning while he held both, and he lived. The fisherman did not.

When he could stand again he built the still into himself. He wove a network of glass gourds and distillation pipes through his own body. From then on every breath he let out came as dense gloam, rich with phantom decay. He took the fifth rank in the Rot Host and served as Herald of Miasmic Illusion in Malphas' Smog Legion.

### The Cost

The sick-houses did not send for him again, and his mother did not take him back to the vats.

He could no longer breathe among people without the gloam reaching them. Where it had been, even after it thinned away, his own side was left in dread. Before he could show a man anything he had to read him, down to the place where the rot would start. What the man then saw was accurate, with the details right. The accuracy broke him. Once the gloam was up, Morphaene could not check what was in it either. While he worked, the Aether about him mirrored his own alignment to anyone able to read it.

On the Broken Rivers, in the Varakh Drylands, a walled river town shut its gate on the Host. It was the first time Zeraphine Drowl held four generals under one cadence, and he went forward first. He stood at the lip of the ditch and breathed out. The gloam rose over the parapet. Kytheris came up behind him and released into it at each man's frequency, and his visions took on body. Men on the wall watched their blades rust through to the hilt and felt their flesh open from inside. Some ran. Others turned on the men beside them. At dusk Graelith walked into the path of a sortie, and wherever his radius reached, the gloam thinned and the men there stopped seeing their ruin. Morphaene moved along the ditch and laid the gloam on thicker beyond the muted ground. The town fell before morning.

### Where They Stand

Morphaene is the Fifth General of the Rot Host. Zeraphine Drowl has authority over him and three generals more. His gloam goes out first. By her order it enforces perceptual chaos before the Bloom Surge begins. He works in tandem with Kytheris, whose spores give his illusions physical form. Graelith's silence fields disrupt him, and he adapts by layering thicker smog: a Fifth General routing around a Fourth General's field, mid-battle, on the same side. At Eldervale he engulfed the crossing with hallucinogenic haze. Three days after that battle ended, Vaerath Ashveil came and adjudicated every contested death there across six silent hours.

What he broadcasts while he works, no enemy has yet used against him. Every man who dies in his gloam dies by another hand, or on the road away from it. He has never struck anyone.

## Lore · The Life Behind the Card

*Written 2026-09-24 by the character-lore pass from this card and the record of The Reaper's office. What those sources do not state is new here.*

### Origin

Natsume Kurogiku was born at Rimward, the eastern frontier city of Nalūn, cut into thinner salt than the cities deeper in the basin. Her family were tailors. They cut wedding clothes and mourning, and coats for the merchants of the corridor trade. Contracts sealed at Rimward held a little looser than contracts sealed below. The frontier merchants liked the give in them. When a promise slipped, the two sides went on meeting at weddings and wakes, and the quarrel came into the cutting room with them.

She learned the trade at that table. Chalk came first, then pins, the hem and the bias. The first rule of the table was that one thread in a seam carried the load. Unpicked, it opened the garment along its length, and any other thread could be mended by evening.

### The Making

Two households at Rimward had a killing between them. They settled it with a marriage, and the blood-debt was paid as a dowry. The tailors cut the wedding clothes.

Natsume knelt at the bride's hem with the pins. She saw the cords there. One ran slack and warm from the bride to her brother. Another ran from the brother to the groom's father, drawn so tight it trembled. Love, envy, duty: each had its own tension, and she could tell which one was pulling too hard. The marriage held. The debt stayed paid.

The fitting ended, and the cords stayed. She saw them at table, in the tunnels, at the next wake the shop dressed. A tie between two people was still a tie, and it was also a fine cord with its own tension, and from that fitting on she saw both at once. She never stopped seeing them. She made a trade of it.

### The Cost

The trade took her out of the salt. The Accord heard the quarrels of parties who had sealed nothing, and its arbiters began to send for her when those quarrels had blood in them. She turned vendettas into negotiated cloth. A blood-debt became a dowry, an arson became labour, and a shame became a badge sewn to a sleeve for a set term. She would not let a feud be paid in blood while a term could still be cut for it.

She would not cut before she had found the thread that carried the load, either. A wrong cut would start a feud. The cutting table had taught her the same of seams.

The sight never closed. At a wedding she saw the envy, and at a wake the spite, and every tie in the room showed her its tension. Nobody else in the room could see them, and none of them could be handed on. On the nights she bore too many alone, her fingers went numb and her voice was stitched down to whispers.

At a deathbed in Altherion, on the stone ground floor of a timber house, a dying man's last word snagged on a grudge his daughter held against him. The room ran hot. The Reaper brought his adjutants to the bed. Iori laid her ring of blooms and pinned a chrysanthemum on the daughter, and Shion grounded his naginata at the threshold. Natsume read the grudge as the thread pulling too hard and went for it with Benikiri. The hinge locked on the love inside it. She put the red shears away and chalked a hem around the bed. Inside it the arguments arrived in indoor voices. The bloom on the daughter's chest warmed, and the father spoke his last word into the grudge. It landed. Yukizumi lowered the star. She came away with a deathbed finished without a cut.

### Where They Stand

She is a Star Chamber adjunct of the Guild Accord for ceremonial services, and she walks with the Reaper when last words snag on living grudges. At Iori's wakes she assists, and in Measures she stands beside Vireline and tailors the clauses that would otherwise cut flesh. When wakes run hot, Shion walks the funeral routes with them both.

She wears ink-black and winter-red layers cut like shrine wear, a cat-ear headpiece and a lace veil, and ties her long charcoal hair back in working ribbons. Benikiri hangs from its tassel beside a lacquered needle case. Her apron pockets clink with pins that are small bells, and a pin fixed to a sleeve rings when the wearer drifts from what they said. Her bobbin-lamp spins steadily, and the breathing in a room learns to match it. Riot drums and smoke bells still fray her hems.

Some call her the Black Chrysanthemum. Others call her the Truce Seamstress, or the Red-Scissor Nun.

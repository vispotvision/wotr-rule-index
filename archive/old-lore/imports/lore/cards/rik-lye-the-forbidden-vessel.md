## Lore · The Life Behind the Card

*Written 2026-09-24 by the character-lore pass from this card and the record of Sanctum Aria. What those sources do not state is new here.*

### Origin

Rik'Lye was made. Niran, the Alchemer, built him in the laboratories beneath her Sanctum, in a chamber under the Concord Citadel. She built him to recreate a soul from fractured Essence. She ran her crucible backward and made matter carry one.

He was made in the Withering Era, when the Wellsprings of the west thinned by the year and nobody could state the cause. Nothing in him could make Aether of its own.

The body she gave him was towering and lean, with the symmetry of a thing drawn to measure. His skin was ash-pale and written over with alchemical glyphs that changed colour, red or blue, with the mood around him. His irises were molten silver set in black sclera, flecked with a fractal corrosion. His hair was black with an indigo sheen and ended in tufts shaped like flame. Horns of crystallised bone curved back from his brow. The markings on his trousers read, in the lineage-hall register, the breaking that preserves.

She forged the Philosopher's Chains out of her own binding glyphs. They tethered his Essence across the planes, and she hung them from his shoulders and his waist, where they kept him balanced. Each link was made to hold a thought. Five researchers worked in the chamber with her.

### The Making

The Guild Accord knew the day the vessel was to be opened. It set blades of its own at the chamber door, and a young swordsman named Kaelric Varnier was one of them.

The Alchemer opened him. The glyphs on his skin began to pulse.

The call was already open in him when he was built. The Aether he could not make came up into him from below, through Abython, before he had a thought of his own to call with. A dormant pattern aligned to a Titan has been suspected in the instability it brought up with it, possibly Zar'thuun's. Nothing has confirmed it.

Then the chamber liquefied. Floor, walls and benches ran together into living mercury, and every surface in the room became a moving mirror. The first feeling he wore was the room's. At the door the swordsman looked in on him for a few breaths, and every reflection of Kaelric in the running mercury reacted violently to the presence. The Guild pulled its blade back and shut the door.

Five researchers had been inside with him. When the mercury stilled, they were crystal pillars, and they hummed.

His first words were for the Alchemer, in the cadence of old scripture. "You should not have opened me."

Niran disappeared shortly after. She left the chains on him, the prophecy tablets in her Sanctum, and an entry in her records that listed his existence as an experimental containment success. The Guild would not write that lie down openly. It wrote nothing honest in its place. It classed him an Alchemical Anomaly of the Ninth Order and put him under the mandate of the Crimson-Seal Order. The name it sends ahead of him is a warning. DANGER, DO NOT OPEN.

### The Cost

The waking cost five researchers and his maker. The rest he pays by the piece.

He does not bleed. A wound leaks his Essence in streams of mercury, and the streams coil upward into sigils of containment. A limb he loses dissolves and is reformed out of whatever matter lies nearest. Every wound healed costs him one memory, and his memories hang from him in iron. A broken link is a thought gone for good. If the chains came off, his containment would fail and an uncontrolled transmutation surge would follow.

Knowing himself wears him down the same way. Each moment of self-understanding erodes one of the sigils that contain him.

He makes no feeling of his own. He mirrors whoever stands nearest him: serene beside the serene, feral beside the feral. When the man in front of him is angry, Rik'Lye hums, at a pitch too harmonic to be natural. Enough human feeling at close quarters breaks him down at the molecular level. Purified currents undo his cohesion. A sanctified Wellspring zone could implode him if he tried to cross it. Twelve hours off the Abython flux would starve him.

He calls Niran the False Mother. It is the only name for her he has given.

He names nothing he does. Whatever he works, he works without a word for it, and when he speaks it is like a man reading aloud from a page he cannot parse.

Every act of violence brings him nearer to wholeness. Every act of mercy erases a piece of his mind. In the lower ruins a digger with a lamp once came on him in the dark and swung a pick at him. The iron ran where it met his arm. He had the man by the wrist, and he opened his hand before the wrist went the way of the pick. The man climbed out. Something went out of Rik'Lye with him. He has never been able to say what it was.

### Where They Stand

He is held under the Crimson-Seal mandate still. He walks the lower Wellspring ruins, seeking fragments of Niran's final theorem, and he keeps close to the Abython flux. Few people come down that far. Directly provoked, he cannot be contained.

The pillars beneath the Concord Citadel still hum. Niran is missing. He has no recorded allies.

The Guild fears that Niran encoded her consciousness in his Core. Nobody knows whether she did. The tablets recovered from her Sanctum read: "When the Vessel learns sorrow, the Wellspring will bleed."

Ahead of him stands a choice the record leaves open: to destroy the false life he was given, or to become something the Wellsprings never intended.

The chains still hang from his shoulders and his waist.

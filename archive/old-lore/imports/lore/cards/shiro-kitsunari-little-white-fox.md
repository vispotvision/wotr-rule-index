## Lore · The Life Behind the Card

*Written 2026-09-24 by the character-lore pass from this card and the record of Road-folk and the Eastward Isles. What those sources do not state is new here.*

### Origin

Shiro Kitsunari was born at the edge of his home grove on the Outer Wilds, to a household that lived off the grove and had little to spare. A trade route ran along the treeline below it. Caravans passed under the outer branches and did not stop. Deeper in stood a fox shrine, kept by a forest priest, and the grove had its guardian spirits.

He was a small child and a light one. He went up the trunks before he could say much. The wood was the first thing he learned to read: which boughs would bear him, where the moss held water, how the high branches shivered before a wind came down to the ground.

### The Making

He climbed as high as the trunks let him. As a toddler he fell from a high branch, and the forest bent a net of branches that had no business bending to catch him. He came down into it whole. On that day something in the wood decided to keep him.

The trees went on keeping him. Where he fell, boughs bent and grew under him, and a drop became a swing, a slide or a gentle catch. His body stayed light and never learned to take a fall; the trees took it for him. A small bell from the fox shrine was tied into his hair. It rang for him alone, and only at a danger he had no other way to sense. The forest priest wove him an anklet from living bark, by hand. When he landed badly, the roots under it thickened or the ground went soft.

His mentors taught him to listen. They called what he had "instinct running ahead of training." He learned to let his Aether into bark and leaf until the wood gave him back what moved in it: footfalls, breath, and the first patches of a Wellspring going quiet, out to the edge of the grove. He learned to coax root and branch to lift and turn under his feet, so that one step carried him three and left no trail behind. By then the Guild Accord's traffic was moving along the treeline. He began to run its word ahead of the caravans, out of their sight.

### The Cost

The Accord used his circuit and never enrolled him. He ran its word as an Independent Concord Freeholder, unaffiliated, with no charter of his own. When a party leader on the route offered to put his name to one, he would sign nothing that stood before the grove. He owned nothing and asked for nothing. He stayed underfed on it.

He paid for the listening in blood. Held too long, the veil came back as headache and nosebleed, and then as a stretch of real deafness. Stone gave him nothing to listen with. Where the route left the living wood for a town of stone and metal, he handed the word on and turned back to the trees. A panicked traveller or a frightened beast he settled into a drowsy, harmless sleep with a mist off his markings. Each one left him pale and hungrier than he already was. His mentors watched his growth for it.

One season the bark at the grove's edge gave him back the first patches of the Wellspring under it going quiet. He ran the word along his circuit, ahead of the caravans on the treeline, as he ran the rest of the Accord's traffic. It reached the Pathwarden of the Ninth Route. Elthiré Maen came to the grove's edge with the songbird Pell on his shoulder and asked the boy what he had heard and how. Shiro told him plainly. Then Elthiré played the Lyre of Seven Winds on the quiet ground, for as long as the ground needed. Shiro kept his hands on the bark. Through the wood he heard the rhythm come back. He held the veil too long. His nose bled, and for a stretch afterwards he heard nothing at all. The Pathwarden left thinner than he came.

### Where They Stand

Shiro Kitsunari is still a boy, called Little White Fox and the Bell-On-Branch. He runs his informal circuit for the Guild Accord on the Outer Wilds and holds no charter. Caravans and lost travellers on the trade routes know him as the boy who runs ahead along the treeline and does not get seen doing it. The bell in his hair rings for nobody else. Asked what he heard and how, he answers plainly, and the Pathwarden of the Ninth Route knows him by sight. His word to the caravans is short: "If the branches shiver, we should listen before we march."

Whether the forest caught him because of what he already was, or made him what he is by choosing to catch him, nobody knows.

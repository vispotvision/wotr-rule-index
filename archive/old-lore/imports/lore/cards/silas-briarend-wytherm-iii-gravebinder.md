## Lore · The Life Behind the Card

*Written 2026-09-24 by the character-lore pass from this card and the record of The Research and Archives Division. What those sources do not state is new here.*

### Origin

Silas Briarend Wytherm III was born on the ash plain of the arc, the young ground in the New World that the Guild Accord chartered within living memory. He was the third of his name. The plain carried a crop in three seasons and could bury a village in one night. His father, a Barrowwright of the Silent Circle of the Eastern Barrowwrights, raised mounds over whatever the charter villages could dig out of the ash. The boy carried stone for him. Where a lahar had come through, there was nothing to dig.

After a fall buried the lower village, his father dug a potter out of the ash at her wheel. Silas laid his palm on the rim and took the evening back out of it: the treadle, the grey light, the roof going. The charter clerk needed her name for the roll. Silas read the wheel again. It gave the same evening, the treadle to the same stroke, and the potter never said her name. The clerk wrote her down by the house she was dug out of.

### The Making

He took the trade into the Guild Accord, in the Sect of the Hollow Book, and came to be called Gravebinder of the Hollow Book and Warden of Rites Forgotten. Families asked him for burnings. His rite was the Silent Bloom. It read a decaying thing down to the record it was carrying and burned the record, and it left the thing. "What is left is inert, and clean, and no longer anybody." The grief went out of a barrow like heat out of a stone. Where the dead lingered, he shortened the time they took to settle and left the settling to them. That was the mercy in his practice.

Years at the rites made him immune to possession.

The Heresiology of Alchemy states the doctrine of passage: the dead may leave traces, and the trace is not the soul. It puts under Conviction review any practitioner who refuses that distinction after demonstration. Silas accepted it, in public. The Sect of the Hollow Book did not.

He had spent his working life taking records out of things other people had left behind. Then he turned the reading on himself, at depth. Self-examination revealed the fracture record, and everything Splintering had promoted to a load path became visible, labelled, and impossible to un-see. He could read himself now. He could not put it down.

### The Cost

He paid for the immunity in soul-fray, in dream haunting and in echo hallucination. Through every rite he spoke in voices not his own, and the voices did not always stop when the rite ended.

A burning could not be undone, and he knew it before each one. He told the families so. "A record burned is a record nobody will ever read, including the family who asked for the burning." They asked him anyway, and he burned it.

He once read a barrow carelessly, and the reading erased the record he had come to take. Nothing of that barrow can be read now, by him or by anyone. He has not discussed it. A rite aimed at the wrong layer erases the wrong thing, and after that barrow he no longer trusted a layer he had named alone.

He kept his standing in a Sect that refused the doctrine he held, and he said it where the Sect could hear.

### Where They Stand

Silas Briarend Wytherm III is Gravebinder of the Hollow Book and Warden of Rites Forgotten, of the Sect of the Hollow Book in the Guild Accord and of the Silent Circle of the Eastern Barrowwrights. He accepts, publicly, that the trace is not the soul. The Sect does not. Nobody knows what the Hollow Book is a copy of.

On the Caedor plains an ash fall had buried a charter village in one night, and the survivors raised a barrow over the dead they could dig out. Two charters later disputed the ground around it, and Arbitration sent Gonju to read it for the case. The families asked the Accord for the burning, and the Accord sent Silas. He asked the Greenhand to read it first. Gonju read the scrub on the mound and the roots beneath it. He named two records in the ground: the ash's, which was the night of the burial, and the dead's, under it. Silas aimed the Silent Bloom at the second. He spoke in voices not his own for the length of the rite, and what was left was inert and clean.

One of the voices did not stop when the rite ended. Nobody knows whose it is, or whose the others are. The barrow he erased was a different one, and he still does not discuss it. Mallow, who shares only with the penitent, shared his flatbread with him afterward. Gonju coaxed the first green up through the mound.

### Ties

- **Gonju · The Sage** · comrades, reader and burner. Gonju named the layer in the Caedor barrow and Silas burned it, and they are the last two who read that record.
- **Moros Pellayne · The Ossuary Registrar** · doctrine. Silas holds in public, against his own Sect, the doctrine Moros keeps on the Arbitration side: the dead may leave traces, and the trace is not the soul.

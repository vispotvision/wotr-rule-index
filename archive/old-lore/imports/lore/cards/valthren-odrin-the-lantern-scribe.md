## Lore · The Life Behind the Card

*Written 2026-09-24 by the character-lore pass from this card and the record of The Research and Archives Division. What those sources do not state is new here.*

### Origin

Valthren Odrin was born in a parish of the Concord heartland, to a chandler who dipped tapers for the houses of the dead. When someone in the parish died, the body lay in the house and somebody sat with it every night, in shifts. His mother's tapers burned through every shift. As a boy he carried them from her bench to each door, and on the late watches he was often left to sit himself.

He carried tapers to the dying as well. Some of them were still talking when he came in. They told the boy with the candles what they had told nobody else. A boundary stone had been moved in the night. A debt had been sworn away before witnesses. Some recited a penance learned by rote, in a tongue they could not read. He could not read it either.

When the layer-out had finished, the parish clerk entered the death in the roll. "In registro relatum," he wrote, and a name under it. Nothing the dying had said went in. The clerk taught the boy his letters so that he could copy the roll. He copied it, and beside it he kept a book of his own, where he wrote down what the dying had told him, in their own words and in the order he had heard them.

### The Making

The Research and Archives Division took him on as a copyist. Its procedure against heresy required every seized text to be copied before it was sealed, and it wanted a hand that never skipped a line. He copied workbooks, formularies and the testimony of practitioners the Division later corrected. Most of all he copied confessions. In time the Lantern Ward took him in, and the confessions were his to keep.

In the Division's stacks he found the doctrine of an order of a holy see. It was the penance the dying of his parish had recited, set down whole in the tongue none of them could read. He learned that tongue. Then he rewrote the sacred doctrine into mortal tongue, so that the people it bound could read the law that bound them. The order hunted him for it from then on.

By then the Candle Archive was in his keeping, and the order could end its keeper with a knife. He answered by making himself the record. He rewrote his essence into wax tablets and sealed them to his own flesh, and every revision frayed the memory of the one before it. He had chosen partial immortality over a whole memory. From then on, the world listened.

### The Cost

The wax kept him whole and took his memory to do it. When he lost flesh, he melted enchanted wax into his body, and it restored the mass and erased one memory every time. Each candle of the Waxen Crown was a written law he had broken and rewritten. When Lex Purgare corrected a corruption, the property it granted was gone in front of the witnesses it was performed for, and a candle on the crown burned out. Every candle that went out severed part of his immortality. He kept correcting.

He never learned to work in the dark. Whatever he worked showed plainly to anyone watching, and the candles on his staff never guttered.

At Cael Dareth's bench in the Upper Ring of Stannvaard, he stood as witness-officer of the Division's lawful party. Dr. Vaelion Rhest read the resonance, Serai Linthra examined the materials, a scrivener copied the texts, and Lyssara Veyn came in behind them. Valthren opened by asking Cael to explain the working. Cael explained it aloud as an equation, and where it reached the paradox he fell into recitation. Nothing he said burned blue. Valthren took the account for the Lantern Ward as it was given. What the Loop had rewritten, Cael did not say, and nothing he left out could burn.

### Where They Stand

Valthren Odrin is a Master and Warden of Duty of the Research and Archives Division, and everyone who deals with him directly still calls him Keeper of the Candle Archive. He is thin and crowned with candles. His robes smell of wax and burnt parchment, and the script stitched into every thread writhes when light touches it. The candles on Vigilus, his staff, never gutter. Each one records a soul's final confession. When he casts, the Manuscript Chains at his wrists inscribe the air on their own, and a lie spoken near him ignites briefly in blue fire.

He keeps the Lantern Ward and commands nothing larger than an archive. The order still hunts him. He keeps an unlit candle said to hold the sin of a Titan, and it stays unlit; which Titan is not recorded. He is still looking for the Draft Lux Tenebrae, a lost working said to let darkness confess. Whether the memories his revisions erase are gone, or filed somewhere in the Archive he no longer holds the key to, nobody knows.

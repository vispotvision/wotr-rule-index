## Lore · The Life Behind the Card

*Written 2026-09-24 by the character-lore pass from this card and the record of The Voice Between Wars and the Outer nobles. What those sources do not state is new here.*

### Origin

Ziyu "Pip" Inari was born with two tails on a seed-grower's holding in the Concord. His mother worked the grower's beds under a labour contract. No paper had ever shown her free, so the Beastkin Classification Provision counted her son as labour stock from the day he was born, and the grower entered him in ink in the holding's book.

The beds were the grower's, and so was every seed that went into them. The boy learned to sow before he could read. He set the seed in the drill, closed the soil over it and moved on down the row. For a long while nothing showed that anyone had passed. When the row came up green, the sower was working another bed. His hands took one row. His tails learned to take the next, and by the time he left the holding each tail carried its own circuit and did the work of a second hand.

### The Making

He left at night. He crossed the yard in shadow, and his step made no sound and almost no scent, so the grower's dogs slept on. At the gate he knelt and pressed an essence seed into the ground under the bar. The floral mark on his face grew while he worked. A first petal opened in it, and closed again when he stood.

In the morning the grower's men rode out after him. The seed opened under the first horse. Root and vine came up out of the ground and wound the animal's forelegs to the gate-bar, and it went down in the gateway. It was a small binding and a fragile one, and the men cut it apart with knives. By then Pip was far down the road.

Nothing had happened while he was there. Everything happened afterwards. From that seed on he gained strength by planting, and the seeds he laid in advance sprouted later into traps, bindings or sensory wards.

### The Cost

He could not recall a seed once he had laid it. The first stayed in the ground under the grower's gate. He had to have been there first, since a seed was useless against anyone who arrived before him or never crossed the ground. So he went ahead of everything he was sent against, and he was gone before anything opened.

Once he was on the road first for Aurevian Lysanthir. Aurevian sat between the envoys of an Innerworld principality and the Thorned Court over a blood-hunt. An Enforcement officer meant to carry the case for a writ against the Court to the Council himself, and he took the road to the Gate. A seed in it sprouted into a binding under his horse and held him there until the treaty stood signed. He reached the Council after the seal. Pip was gone before the seed opened. No record says whether Aurevian asked for it, or knew of it. The seed is still in that road.

On a Concord raid he was caught at the planting. Raivhar Drakonis came onto the ground from the one side Pip had not planned for. There was no garden yet, and the mark on Pip's face was in flower because he had been working. Pip did not fight. The Ashen Horn had his life in hand and spared it.

He never named his own art. Where it was named at all, an instrument or a faculty read named it. The one sentence the record keeps in his own voice is about not being seen: "You never see the snare until the flowers open." He held no ground of his own and planted in everybody else's. The bonds he made were with ground.

### Where They Stand

Pip is an unlisted operative of the Black Concord. He is called the Two-Tailed Cipher and the Bloom of the Shadow Gate, and he is an occasional liaison to the Kuromaki Association, the Beastkin guild with which Saruin Kye keeps loose alliances. He trades rare goods and information with Hyo Miran, and both are known for unpredictable deals. He sometimes plays the informant for Aurevian, whose rivals he has sabotaged more than once. Raivhar holds him in wary respect. Seraphion Veydrak of the Judgment Order views him with suspicion for his chaotic nature. A rumour ties him to a Shadow Court archon, and nothing on the record confirms it. No record says how he came to the Black Concord.

There are gardens he has forgotten planting, in ground he no longer has reason to visit. Each is still a working with his signature on it. A Measurewright who finds one has a record of who was there, and can read the seed and cut it before it sprouts. Nobody knows how many of his seeds are in the ground now, or whose ground.

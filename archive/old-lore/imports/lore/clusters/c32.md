# c32 · The Voice Between Wars and the Outer nobles

Six cards and five people. An Arbitration Division negotiator ends wars with a harp. The Lord of the Thorned Court, a stag-crowned noble of the Outer Realm, deals with the Accord and is hunted by it. The heir of Epprenea's Pale Court sits on the Accord's high-risk delegation roster as an envoy. A spirit-binder left the Arbitration Division and is pursued by the Accord. A two-tailed operative of the Black Concord is carried on two cards, which are one man (section 6). Three pairs name one another: Aurevian and Varozael over a blood-hunt treaty, Aurevian and Pip over sabotaged rivals, Vaelthyr and Sevrin over bound spirits traded for political cover. The Arbitration Division runs through three of the five. No archived scene holds any of them. Canon order holds throughout: Isaac's direction, then the card, then the archived scenes, then the wiki, then this record.

## 1 Who is here

**Aurevian Lysanthir · Voice Between Wars.** The Velvet Chord, Harpist of the Unbound Accord: "Arbitration Division Negotiator. Harmonic Manipulator. Bardic Thaumaturge." He serves the Guild Accord's Arbitration Division officially and belongs to the Choral Veil, "a secretive society of musical mystics." His harp is strung with threads of condensed Harmonic Essence, each note binding emotion or unravelling fear. When he plays, the listeners' Essence falls into his own cycle. Fear unravels, positions soften, and a treaty that was impossible an hour ago becomes the obvious course. His gaze tunes a person's state by returning it to them undistorted. "He is paid in the feelings of people who stopped fighting because of him, and he spends them in battle." What he takes off a negotiation he stores. He can only fight as hard as the last peace he made was difficult, and a quiet season leaves him empty. His card: "He is carrying every war he prevented." His limit is that they must listen. Deafness, distance or silence undo him, and "once the music fails he has nothing." A rumour ties him to a Divine Court's Muse. Nobody knows who the patron is, or what the Choral Veil is for.

**Varozael Dreikthorne · Herald of Nightglass.** The Stag of the Black Eclipse, Lord of the Thorned Court: "Outer Realm Noble. Domain Predator. Concord-Adversarial Diplomat." His court is the Court of the Black Eclipse, an "Outer Realm aristocracy of predatory fey-lords", which holds "cordial enmity" with the Guild Accord: "officially recognised, unofficially hunted." His horns are fused with a crystallised shard of the Black Eclipse Domain and refract darkness into beams. His skin can erupt into jagged black-vine armour. "Any creature whose blood he draws becomes partially linked to his will", and his card's note holds that every creature he has wounded is still attached to him, and that he cannot always tell which one is pulling back. He ascends by hunting powerful prey and anchoring their remains into his Domain's soil. "The Domain must be fed to grow, and what is anchored in it stays anchored." He must draw blood first. Lady Nytheris is "His only equal in the Thorned Court", his lover and his political rival. Nobody knows how many of the anchored are still aware.

**Vaelthyr Iscarn · Laughing Horn.** The Winter Serpent, Heir of the Pale Court: "Outer World Noble. Diplomatic Predator. Archonic-bloodline Magus." The Pale Court of Epprenean Kingdom is "a devil-blood dynasty seated in the Outer World's frost-sheathed fjord realms", and Epprenea is a real place, the Outer World's Southern Pan (ruled 2026-09-12, card head). He is "Envoy to the Guild Accord under the Arbitration Division's high-risk delegation roster". His card calls his loyalty debatable. "A smile is a smile and is also a working." Every version of his face is also a technique, and he lives behind it permanently. "Those who meet his gaze too long often lose track of their thoughts." His blackened horns conduct Archonic sigils, so he casts without a visible motion. Frost condenses to razor edges near him, and a casual gesture can leave an ice-burn scar. His Wellspring lies beneath the ice labyrinths of Epprenea and teaches manipulation through stillness. He gets one strike. He must be underestimated. The current that carries his smile returns his state undistorted to anyone equipped to read it, which his card calls "the single most dangerous line on this sheet." To the Accord he is "Official envoy, unofficial information broker."

**Sevrin Valeith · Maw of Nine Tongues.** The Pallid Binder, Whisper-Keeper: "Spirit Binder. Necrotic Medium. Walker of the Pale Wellspring." He served in the Arbitration Division and is now rogue, "Pursued by the Accord for Soul Concord violations after binding sovereign spirits into private service." He works the Outer World's frost-barren calderas, trading in forbidden conjurations. Floating relic-skulls go with him, each a sealed container home to a bound echo, serving as scouts, conduits and tormentors. Parunic death-script is tattooed on his hands. With a touch he can tether an entity's Essence to one of his skulls, "locking them in a state between life and death". He speaks with up to nine spirits at once and weaves their power into his own workings. It costs him a gradual psychic erosion. He is at nine. His card: "Every additional binding is drawn against a reserve that is already short." His Wellspring lies beneath the ossuary-city of Nerrith. His card reads his offence through Mortalis. That doctrine holds that the dead may leave traces and that the trace is a different thing from the soul, and the Accord pursues him "for a category error they have a formal procedure for". His own line: "The dead do not lie, only the living do. That's why I keep their tongues." Nobody knows which of the nine tongues is still speaking for itself.

**Ziyu "Pip" Inari.** The Two-Tailed Cipher, Bloom of the Shadow Gate: "Stealth Operative. Wellspring Infiltrator. Dual-Tail Combatant." He is an "unlisted operative" of the Black Concord and an "Occasional liaison to the Kuromaki Association." Each of his two tails carries its own Essence circuit and works as a second hand. A floral mark on his face is a living glyph that grows when he works Wellspring flora and opens into a full petal crown at the height of a working. In shadowed ground his step makes no sound and almost no scent. He plants essence seeds in advance, and they sprout later into traps, bindings or sensory wards. "Nothing happens while he is there. Everything happens afterwards." He cannot recall a seed once laid. "There are gardens he has forgotten planting." He must have been there first, and caught at the planting "the encounter is brief and goes badly for him." He trades rare goods and information with Hyo Miran. A rumour ties him to a Shadow Court archon. Nobody knows how many of his seeds are in the ground, or whose ground.

**Ziyu "Pip" Inari · The Two-Tailed Cipher.** The second card of the same man (section 6), with the same epigraph: "You never see the snare until the flowers open." It adds his gloss rights, "Diagnostic only": "His art gets named by an instrument or a faculty read, and never by him." It calls his Attraction Layer "Thin and outward", trading partners and a rumoured patron with no named bond, and "The bonds he makes are with ground". It names no artifact; the seeds are workings and the petal mark is a Trait. It says what a forgotten seed leaves behind: each is "still a working with his signature on it", and "A Measurewright who finds one has a record of who was there." Its Tenebra carries a failure: "Observed from the one angle he did not plan for, he returns a very bright signal." It keeps Hyo Miran as a trading partner and notes "Hyo's own Volume IV sheet does not list Pip." It keeps the Shadow Court archon as a rumour, "Unattested."

## 3 The shared history

Four events. The blood-hunt treaty and the bargain in Epprenea are on the cards and are given a place and a manner here. The spirit's mediation and the envoy's answer are new. They join the two halves of the cluster through the Division that three of them served.

### The blood-hunt treaty

**When.** No card gives an era, and this record gives none. The stag's respect and distrust of the harper stand on both cards, so the treaty comes before them. Unordered against the other three events. The treaty is on both cards; the Enforcement writ and the seeded road are new, and the seed is one of the times Pip's card says he sabotaged Aurevian's rivals.

**Where.** The Inner World, on the ground of the principality the cards leave unnamed, and on the road from it to the nearest Concord Gate. No page places a principality in the Inner World (section 2).

**Who.** Aurevian Lysanthir, Varozael Dreikthorne, Ziyu "Pip" Inari.

**What happened.** The Thorned Court's hunts had run into the principality's ground and drawn blood there. The principality brought the matter to the Accord, and Arbitration took it. Enforcement wanted a writ against the Court. A Concord Writ "requires the Enforcement Division to arrive" (Value, Coin and Trade), and Enforcement is Arbitration's rival, permanently. Its officer on the matter meant to carry the case for the writ to the Council himself. Aurevian sat between the principality's envoys and the Court. Varozael came to the table in his own person, with the antlered crown. Aurevian played. The envoys' fear of the Court came off them into his cycle, and the Court's position softened with it. A treaty set out the ground where the Court's hunt might run and the ground where it might not, and both sides signed. The Enforcement officer took the road to the Gate. Pip had been on that road first. A seed in it sprouted into a binding under the officer's horse and held him there until the treaty stood signed. He reached the Council after the seal. Pip was gone before the seed opened. No record says whether Aurevian asked for it, or knew of it.

**What each took.** Aurevian took the charge of a hard peace, stored. His card says he fights only as hard as his last peace was difficult. Varozael took a treaty with his court's name on it, mediated by the Accord that hunts him unofficially, and a mediator he respects and distrusts. Kaelen Raive's line on his card says Varozael thrives on fear. He had signed in a room where fear came undone. Pip left a seed in the road that he cannot recall, still a working with his signature on it. One more of Aurevian's rivals was cleared.

### The spirit that listened

**When.** While Sevrin still served in the Arbitration Division, before the Accord's pursuit. No era. New.

**Where.** The Inner World, where the Guild Accord page counts the Accord strong. The cards name no site, and this record names none.

**Who.** Aurevian Lysanthir, Sevrin Valeith.

**What happened.** A sovereign spirit stood against the people living on its ground, and the matter came to the Division as a spiritual mediation, which its Commission class allows between factions, nations or divine entities. The Division sent Aurevian to mediate. It sent Sevrin with him as its spirit-binder, to hold the spirit if the mediation failed. Aurevian played. The spirit listened, and its anger came off it into his cycle. The mediation closed. Before the spirit withdrew, Sevrin laid his etched hands on it and bound it into his own service. The Division entered the commission as concluded. The binding came to light later, with others, and the Accord moved against him for Soul Concord violations. He was out of the Division before it could hold him.

**What each took.** Sevrin took a sovereign spirit into private service, one more binding drawn against a reserve his card calls already short, and the Accord's pursuit, which has not stopped. Aurevian took the charge of that peace, and a commission on the Division's record entered as a mediation that ended in a binding.

### The bargain in Epprenea

**When.** After Sevrin fled the Division, while the Accord pursued him, and while Vaelthyr stood on the Arbitration Division's high-risk delegation roster. No era. The arrangement is on both cards; the meeting and its manner are new.

**Where.** Epprenea, in the Outer World: the Pale Court's ground in the "frost-sheathed fjord realms" (Vaelthyr's card), a polity the Southern Pan page lists among the ledger's.

**Who.** Vaelthyr Iscarn, Sevrin Valeith.

**What happened.** Sevrin came into Epprenean ground out of the frost-barren calderas where he trades, with the skulls about him, whispering. The heir of the Pale Court received him. Sevrin offered bound spirit-messengers. Vaelthyr offered political cover: the Pale Court's ground to stand on, and his own voice at the Accord's table whenever the Accord asked after the binder. They agreed. The talk ran long past the terms. Vaelthyr smiled through all of it, as his face does. Sevrin bound the first messenger in the heir's presence and gave it to him. Every messenger after it was another binding.

**What each took.** Vaelthyr took messengers that need no wire, no seal and no courier, whose words no Accord clerk reads; the unofficial half of his office runs on them. Sevrin took Epprenean ground to stand on, in a quarter the Accord's own ledger surveys only in part, and a voice for him inside the Accord. Each messenger cost him a binding drawn against the reserve his card calls short.

### The envoy's answer

**When.** After the bargain in Epprenea, while the Accord still pursued Sevrin. No era. New.

**Where.** The Concord Citadel, the Accord's seat, "a nexus suspended between planes" whose City Below holds its halls and Gate plazas (The Concord Citadel). The Epprenean envoy was received there.

**Who.** Aurevian Lysanthir, Vaelthyr Iscarn. Sevrin Valeith is its subject and is absent.

**What happened.** The Accord's pursuit reached the Pale Court, and the Accord wanted to know whether the court had given the rogue binder ground. A foreign court's envoy is Arbitration's to ask. The Division sent the negotiator who had stood beside Sevrin at the spirit's mediation. Aurevian put it to Vaelthyr. Vaelthyr smiled and answered that the Pale Court kept no fugitive of the Accord's. Aurevian read him. Both men harmonise the same current, and what it returned of the envoy was a state: the amusement on his face, and under it something he would rather not have broadcast. It came with no fact attached. Vaelthyr read the harper in turn. His card says he reads a room before he enters it, and Aurevian's says anyone equipped to read him closely gets an honest answer. The answer went into the Division's record as the envoy gave it.

**What each took.** Vaelthyr's answer stands on the Division's record, and it is the political cover his card names; he also took the harper's honest answer, whatever it held. Aurevian took a reading he could not name. No record says whether he entered it beside the answer.

## 6 Same person?

Yes, once. `Ziyu Pip Inari.md` and `Ziyu Pip Inari · The Two-Tailed Cipher.md` are one man and share one life. They carry the same name, the same aliases, the same epigraph, the same role, the same affiliation to the Black Concord and the Kuromaki Association, the same four Wellsprings, the same three Traits, the same signature and the same relationships to Hyo Miran, Aurevian and an unconfirmed patron. The second card says its figures are "adopted from the Volume IV wiki mirror's already-committed character card, not re-derived from scratch", and the roster marks each as the other's duplicate. The second card adds gloss rights and notes. Its small differences from the first are in section 7.

No one else here is one person with another. Sevrin Valeith and the Vaelith of c10 (Serenyra Vaelith, Aelor Vaelith) spell their names differently and no card joins them. The cluster notes hold that shared or similar surnames without a textual tie are not kinship.

## 7 Where the cards disagree

1. **Who admits the sabotage.** The first Pip card: "Aurevian's card frames it as "sometimes plays the informant." Pip's card says the sabotage went the other way, more than once." The second: "Aurevian's sheet frames it as "sometimes plays the informant."" Both say "only one of the two sheets admits it." Aurevian's card reads "Sometimes plays informant; has sabotaged Aurevian's rivals more than once." Both sheets carry the sabotage, and Aurevian's card has no "the" in the line the Pip cards quote from it. Neither card says what "went the other way" means. This record uses the line both cards share and settles nothing further.

2. **Whose envoy Vaelthyr is, card against itself.** His affiliation: "Envoy to the Guild Accord under the Arbitration Division's high-risk delegation roster". His relationships: "Guild Accord · Official envoy, unofficial information broker." His card's note: "An Accord envoy is providing political cover". One reading makes him the Pale Court's envoy to the Accord. The other makes him the Accord's own envoy. This record calls him the Epprenean envoy on the Division's roster and settles nothing.

3. **A named operative of the Black Concord, card against the wiki.** The Black Concord page: "No one joins the Black Concord by choice. They are taken", "chosen from the ranks of the condemned, the Forsworn, or the dead", and "its members' names are expunged from both mortal and divine record." Merit and Severance, Law VII: "Names, faces and records erased; memories rewritten for covert service." Pip is an "unlisted operative" whose name and alias stand on three cards, who trades with Hyo Miran and liaises with the Kuromaki Association. No card says how he came to the Black Concord, and this record gives no account of it.

4. **The Innerworld principality, card against the wiki.** Varozael's and Aurevian's cards: "an Innerworld principality." No page names a principality in the Inner World. The Articles of the Accord list one principality among the seven forms of polity, "Calendrical principality", and it is Eresse, in the New World.

5. **The two Pip cards, in small things.** The first card's Codex line gives the family as "Abstract" and "Entropic"; the second gives "Entropy, Void and Mind", the Limina page's own title. The second marks Tenebra "Governing" and the first does not. The second adds "Unattested" to the rumoured patron. Nothing else on either card contradicts the other.

6. **Several titles for one chapter number.** Chapter XX has three: *The Harp Between Blades* and *The Serenade in the Halls of Salt* (Aurevian), and *The Hollow Summit* (Vaelthyr). Chapter XXIII has three: *The Thorn and the Monkey* and *The Black Eclipse Ball* (Varozael), and *Ash and Frost Conspiracy* (Vaelthyr). Chapter XXV has *Velvet Chains, Gilded Lies*, *The Velvet Concord* and *The Golden Betrayal*. No card names the book. These may be arcs within one chapter, or chapters of different books.

7. **Ties named from one side.** Kaelen Raive is named by Aurevian and Varozael and names neither. Hyo Miran is named by Pip, Sevrin and Vaelthyr and names none; Pip's second card notes it. Raivhar Drakonis and Seraphion Veydrak name Pip, and neither Pip card names them. Goraku Tenryū names Vaelthyr, and Vaelthyr's card does not name him. Karo Venrik's card does not name Sevrin, and Draven Kael Vorrick's card lists no relationships at all (c13).

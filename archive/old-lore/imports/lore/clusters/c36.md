# c36 · Gale and still point

Five people of stillness and wind: the eldest of the Kazuren siblings, raised at the Quiet Shrine and now clan head (Sairin Kazuren); her younger brother, the middle sibling, who left the Dreamfold Monastery and mediates quarrels (Shiran Kazuren); the only survivor of the Gale Monastery, of Veyran descent (Ravenn Kaelor); a freed gladiator of Kael'Nir's arena, of Veyran gale-blood (Karu Velnar); and a former wind-priest of Kael'Nir's temple choir who arbitrates Wellspring disputes for the Guild Accord (Iryen Maevith).

## 1 · Who is here

**Sairin Kazuren · The Still Point.** Called the Still Point and the Blade Between Storms: "Clan Head. Duelist. Monastic Sentinel of the Kazuren line." She is in her early thirties. She is the "Eldest of the Kazuren siblings, current clan head", "Raised from childhood at the Quiet Shrine", and she "passed every monastic trial the shrine set her". She took the clan when the previous head stepped down, and she "has not stopped watching her younger siblings since". She is "convinced a major Wellspring fracture will eventually call them together". Her card gives no fracture yet: "The Threshold is not behind her. She priced it in advance." She is tall and lithe, her dark hair bound with an arrow-shaped ornament, her robes "stitched with cloud and tide". "She speaks only when necessary, and a room settles when she enters it before anyone notices why." Near her "Air thickens" and "Sound dulls a half-step"; "Her breathing sets the tempo of whatever room she is in, and the room follows without agreeing to." Her heartbeat "holds steady under any stress the scene provides". She carries the Twin Vows, paired long-blades, her only named weapon. Her Shizukahara makes a close field "in which everyone's momentum drags except hers"; held too long, it "leaves her joints aching for a day afterward", and a loud hall or a crowd leaves it nothing to quiet. The clan "Sees her as the embodiment of discipline." She does not explain herself. Nobody knows why the previous head stepped down, "and whether Sairin already knows and is only waiting for her siblings to ask."

**Shiran Kazuren · The Umbra Lantern.** "He Who Walks Before the Storm." The "Middle sibling of the Kazuren family", in his late twenties, an "Eastern Human; realm unstated". He was "Formerly of the Dreamfold Monastery", which he "left after a schism only he understood", and he is "Now an unaffiliated mediator and reality-reader". His card makes the schism his fracture. Of the Monastery: "Left, not exiled; the distinction matters to him". He is so still that "people forget he entered a room until he speaks". His attention is "tuned to disaster", which is "the instinct that lets him prevent things before they begin". His Lantern Calm "Stabilizes emotion and weakens hostile intent" in anyone near him. Opponents lose track of him. Dream reflections move with him. He carries the Veilshade Umbrella openly; its inner blade is "drawn rarely enough that most who know him have never seen the blade". The people he mediates for are "Unnamed disputing parties across the Regency, who know him only as the man who prevents the disaster before it starts." Those who know him believe one strong emotion or one true prophecy would carry him further. "He does not explain the dream-work, to anyone". Nobody knows "What the schism at the Dreamfold Monastery actually was".

**Ravenn Kaelor · The Pale Gale.** "Calm is not peace. Calm is restraint." Also the Silent Tempest, the White-Scar Acolyte and the Wind-Touched Ascetic; "He Who Walks Against the Storm's Will". "Human, Kingdom of Veyran descent, with an atavistic wind-aspect mutation." He is a "Former disciple of the Gale Monastery" and an "Unaffiliated wanderer of the Imperial Age, no Guild Accord rank claimed." The Gale Monastery Collapse made him what he is. The Monastery is "Destroyed", and "he is its only survivor on this sheet". His body's art "only opened at the Monastery's death". His damaged left eye "reads micro-currents and airflow fractures"; his breath and pulse slow under focus; his footwork is "silent, untrackable". He carries a jade leaf "carried from the Monastery" to anchor his breath. A six-chamber belt braces his hip and spine. His Wind-Shear Line is "A single hand-flick" that "cuts cloth, cracks stone, and staggers an armored target on contact"; overuse tears ligament and tendon, and "days of stillness follow". His bonds are "Undeveloped, pointed at no one": "He keeps distance from safehouses and networks alike; the exile is total." Rural frontier settlements "Know him only as the reason bandit routes and beast migrations sometimes simply stop." His card records "A rogue wyvern and three mercenary units, put down using pressure strikes alone." A dormant Mirithane infection has been in him "since before the Monastery fell". He answers a direct question about the wind-work and does not volunteer it. Nobody knows "Whether the Gale Monastery Collapse was an accident, or whether someone inside it wanted it to happen."

**Karu Velnar · The Shackled Tempest.** Also Chainbreaker, the White-Wind Savage and the Fang of the Gale Paths; "He Who Hunts the Storm Within". "Freed Gladiator. Wandering Anti-Slavery Mercenary. Gale-Blood." "None by charter. A wandering mercenary of Veyran gale-blood, formerly an arena gladiator of Kael'Nir, presently hunting traffickers along the trade routes and refusing every recruitment offer the Guild Accord has made him." In the Chainpit Uprising, "breaking forty-three slaves free cost him whatever the arena still had a claim on". He still wears the broken shackles, and they amplify his momentum on every strike. His Shackled Vow Bracers are "Arena-era bindings". His Gale Instinct "Reads micro-pressure changes to predict an opponent's motion vector before it fully commits." In the Feral Tempest State his senses sharpen "while rational control bleeds into pure instinct"; it tears his tendons and cannot be held long, and "Spirit-based binding magic suppresses the gale-instinct directly". His card keeps "The forty-three freed in the Chainpit Uprising" as "The debt his chains keep count of." "He wants the story told; it is half the point of still wearing the chains." Nobody knows "Whether the Feral Tempest State is Karu's gale-blood surfacing, or something the arena itself trained into him and never told him about."

**Iryen Maevith · The Serene Gale.** "The one who could still a storm with laughter." "Varrisak and human", with silver-white hair "barely tethered by silk cords" and amethyst eyes "always half-lidded". He was "Once a wind-priest of Kael'Nir's temple choir". He "Transcended human resonance during the Verdant Ascension", "refused sainthood, and chose to wander as a Harmony Arbiter for the Guild Accord, mediating Wellspring disputes through melody instead of combat." His card sets the office in the Arbitration Division. Of the choir: "He left them by refusing what they offered." When he breathes, flowers orient toward him. "Those who listen long enough begin to mirror his rhythm: slow, even, sure." Resonance binders hum across his chest, "each tone recording fragments of his thoughts". His Tranquil Spiral is "A circular field of sound that neutralises rage, silences spells, and aligns Essence harmonics". "The field lasts exactly as long as the exhalation that carries it", and "what stands outside the radius is outside the peace." Its price is Lung Fracture: "Overuse of resonance fragments his breath into song", and prolonged strain risks dissolving him into pure sound, "a fate he quietly accepts". "He speaks as if everything already happened and never reveals how he knows". "Every Wellspring quarrel he has settled has two parties who remember the tone." His symbol is "Peace Through Motion: movement without violence, breath without burden", and his Archonic patron is Wyther. "His name lingers in monastic hymns." Nobody knows "How he knows what already happened."

## 3 · The shared history

Three events. None is in the archive. Each is new and fitted to every card it touches. Ravenn's card alone states an era, the Imperial Age, and names none inside it; Karu's and Iryen's cards set them in a living Guild Accord, and the Kazuren cards give no era at all. All three events therefore fall in the Imperial Age, with no year and no era inside it named. The road out of Kael'Nir comes first. The ford and the caldera belt come after it and are unordered against each other. No card's What nobody knows is touched: why the Kazuren head stepped down, what the Dreamfold schism was, whether the Gale Monastery fell by accident, whether Karu's feral state is blood or training, and how Iryen knows what already happened all stay open. The drift in the caldera belt is the arc's ordinary habit (NW, "The Wellsprings here move"); it is no fracture, and Sairin's conviction still waits.

### The road out of Kael'Nir

- **When.** The Imperial Age, the era inside it unstated. The Chainpit Uprising was just done: Karu had broken the forty-three free, and it had cost him whatever the arena still had a claim on. Iryen was still a wind-priest of the temple choir. The Verdant Ascension and his refusal of sainthood were still ahead of him.
- **Where.** Kael'Nir, on the forest road out of it, below the temple. Tsukihana Reimei's card sets "the border province of Kael'Nir forests" in Eresse, in the New World (WORLD §3; c13). No page places the arena, the temple or the road, and this record places them no nearer than her card does.
- **Who.** Karu Velnar and Iryen Maevith; the forty-three.
- **What happened.** Karu brought the freed out of the arena in the dark. Some were hurt. Others could barely keep their feet. The road out ran through the forest below the temple, and Iryen came down to it from the temple steps. He did not ask who they were. He looked at the broken shackles on Karu's wrists, and then he went to the head of the column. He sang under his breath as he walked. The column fell into his pace, slow and even, and the ones who had been stumbling kept up. Karu walked at the rear with the chains. Where the trees thinned at the forest's edge, Iryen stopped. Karu told him the story of the pit, all of it, as he tells it to anyone who will hear it. Iryen heard him out and said he knew it already. He did not say how. He turned back up the road to the temple before it was light, and the column went on without him.
- **What each took.**
  - Karu: the forty-three brought out of Kael'Nir at a wind-priest's pace; a man from the temple who heard the whole story and said he had known it.
  - Iryen: a night on the road outside the choir, at the head of people the arena had held. He went back to the temple that morning. He left it later, by the refusal his card records.

### The ford on the Horse Rivers

- **When.** The Imperial Age, the era inside it unstated. After the Gale Monastery Collapse, in Ravenn's wandering; after the Chainpit Uprising, while Karu hunted traffickers along the trade routes. After the road out of Kael'Nir, and unordered against the caldera belt.
- **Where.** A ford on one of the Horse Rivers, in the Southern Grass Sea of the Outer World (WORLD §3 and §5, South; SW, where the Horse Rivers are among the Grass Sea's features), on a grain road through the marcher country. There a farmer "might live beneath a Veyran king" and "travel an Akhazar-recognised road" (D10). The Kingdom of Veyran holds its horse-and-grain country there by treaty (SW). This record places the ford inside no border.
- **Who.** Ravenn Kaelor and Karu Velnar; the people a trafficking string had taken; the string's drivers.
- **What happened.** A string of carts had been taking people off the grain roads, and Karu had followed it south. When he came down to the ford the string had already stopped. The carts stood in the shallows with the teams still in the traces. The drivers were down on the far bank. The ropes on the people in the carts had been cut through, each in one straight line, and a cart's side-board was split the same way. Ravenn sat on the near bank with his breath slowed almost to nothing. He did not get up. The people from the carts backed away from Karu when they saw the chains on his wrists. He stopped where he stood and told them where the chains had come from. When he had finished, they let him near. Some were hurt and could not be moved at once, and Ravenn could not walk. Karu kept camp at the ford. At the fire, through the days that followed, he told Ravenn the story of the Chainpit. Ravenn listened. His scarred left eye kept to the air round Karu's wrists, where the bracers stirred it. Karu felt the pressure change when Ravenn breathed. He asked whether the wind in Ravenn's blood ever took him the way it took Karu. Ravenn said, "Calm is not peace. Calm is restraint." Karu asked him to walk the routes with him. Ravenn refused. On the day he could walk he went off across the grass, away from the road and the settlements. Karu took the people up the road to the nearest village.
- **What each took.**
  - Ravenn: days of stillness paid for the lines he cut, spent beside a stranger's fire; an offer of company, refused; a man of the same Veyran wind, whose wind goes feral.
  - Karu: a string stopped before he reached it, and its people to see up the road; the Chainpit told once more, to a man who heard it out; a second man of Veyran blood who keeps his wind held. Whether his own state is the blood or the arena stays where his card leaves it.

### The drift in the caldera belt

- **When.** The Imperial Age, after the Accord chartered the arc "within living memory" (NW). After Shiran left the Dreamfold Monastery; after Sairin took the clan; after Iryen refused sainthood and took the Arbiter's office. After the road out of Kael'Nir, and unordered against the ford.
- **Where.** The caldera belt of the Accord-chartered arc in the New World (WORLD §3 and §5, East; NW), where "The Wellsprings here move" (NW, The Ground Itself) and the Arbitration Division "spends a disproportionate share of its calendar on the arc's colonial rights, Wellspring access" (NW, Trade Posture). Two ventures' claims, side by side. This record places neither the Kazuren home, the Quiet Shrine nor the Dreamfold, and names no realm for Shiran.
- **Who.** Shiran Kazuren, Iryen Maevith and Sairin Kazuren; two masters; their militias.
- **What happened.** The ground under the belt settled in the night, and the Wellspring under one venture's array drifted onto the next claim. The first array went dead. The neighbour's woke. Each venture had a militia, and each militia carried Onceglass charges (NW). The first venture filed with the Accord. Shiran was in the belt before the filing had gone far, and no one on either claim had sent for him. He walked between the two yards with his umbrella closed. He sat with each master in turn, and while he was there no charge was thrown. Sairin came up the road some days after him. She took a place on the slope above the claims, where she could see both yards, and she did not go down. Iryen came with the Accord's filing. He greeted Shiran and spoke of the days before he came as if he had watched every one of them. Shiran did not say how he had known to come. Iryen stood on the drift ground and sang. The tone went out in a circle to the edges of both yards. Inside it the militias put up their charges, and the two masters talked terms, and Iryen sang breath after breath while they talked. Sairin had come down to the circle's edge. Where she stood the tone went dull, and the men nearest her kept her pace. She stepped back out past the edge of the circle, and the tone closed. The masters signed: the neighbour would share the draw with the first venture for as long as the drift held. Iryen's breath came shorter afterward, and more of it was song. Sairin went down the road the next morning, and Shiran did not ask her why she had come. She did not ask him about the Dreamfold.
- **What each took.**
  - Shiran: days in the belt with no charge thrown, and the quarrel settled by another man's tone; a mediator who knew those days before anyone told him of them; his sister on the slope, and nothing asked on either side.
  - Iryen: a settlement sung breath by breath, and a shorter breath after it; a man who had held the yards before him and would not say how he knew to come; a woman his circle could not take in, who stepped out of it so that it held.
  - Sairin: her brother at his work between two armed yards; a peace she could keep by standing outside it; a Wellspring that moved and did not break.

## 6 · Same person?

No. The five cards give five bodies, five origins and five houses, and nothing on any card folds one into another.

- Sairin and Shiran are two siblings: eldest and middle, a woman in her early thirties and a man in his late twenties.
- Ravenn and Karu both carry the Veyran wind, both read air pressure and both pay in the tendons. Ravenn is the Gale Monastery's survivor with a damaged left eye, and Karu the Kael'Nir arena's freed gladiator in chains. "White-Scar Acolyte" and "White-Wind Savage" are two men's names.
- Karu and Iryen both come out of Kael'Nir, one from its arena and one from its temple choir.
- The Wyther Paragon on The Fractured Dawn is "A Seikai monk who has lost the ability to hold still". That is neither Iryen, a Varrisak-and-human wind-priest whose patron is Wyther, nor Ravenn, whose discipline is stillness.
- Name echoes, no link: Kaelor and Kael'Nir; Veyran and the Archon Veyra; Veyran and Kaien Veyren, whose "Regency posting" RULINGS.md lists beside Shiran's realm. No card joins them.

## 7 · Where the cards disagree

Quoted and left open.

1. **Each Kazuren card says the other sibling is unnamed.** Sairin's card: "The Kazuren siblings · Younger, unnamed on the card." (IX). Shiran's card: "Middle sibling of the Kazuren family (other siblings unnamed in the source; not detailed elsewhere)" (I). Sairin's card names the eldest, and Shiran's card is a younger sibling carded.

# The Kharven Year — book bible

Thread: Sodoku Moto. Fronts thread: Kharven. Culture: Kharven. Twelve chapters of about 3,500 words. Planned 12 September 2026 from the table's state (State of Play rebuilt 10 September, Ledger, Fronts, Docket) and from scene files 01 through 10 plus the eleventh-year, courier, Vaeloris and Sum-gol scenes. Isaac gave no premise; the book is derived from where the table stands.

## Where the book starts

Kharven-Seat, the week after the spring signing. The first Accord circle (Kharven, the Holy Sea of Alabaster, the Kingdom of Nalūn, the stretches from the Brine crossing east to the marches of Sum-gol) is signed, under one seal and one body of law, the Moto lines seated as the royal house, Miku Tenrai Moto at fourteen holding a chair. Sodoku put his hand to it last. **The circle is unnamed and stays unnamed in prose.** The Tenrai front is closed. Hild Ice is eleven months dead in a cistern doorway at the ninth hour, and the instrument is her and never says so.

Chronology note for the writer: `scenes/ARCS.md` lists the arc in filename order, not story order. The eleventh-year scenes (the gate, the count, the fourteenth bow, the true religion, the weight of a courier) and *The Left of the Door* sit **before** the muster and the ninth hour; Hild is alive in them. *What the Ground Was Owed* (scene 10) is the latest point in story time and the book continues from it. The Vaeloris and Sum-gol scenes (Sesk, the petition, Star Crusher) are earlier and off this book's map.

## Premise

The year after the signing. The instrument Sodoku Moto built out of a grief he will not name is tested by the people who signed it and by the words other nations coin for what Kharven did to win. A child with a chair asks for three hundred names. The Expanse cuts a new word into a board and gives it a seat. Nalūn asks to seal the promise in salt. Stannvaard refuses to deal with a body that has no name. The four factors in Vaultmere give up a date, and the date leads back to the king. He pays each bill in person, and by Ice-out he has done the one thing he did not do at the signing: he sits down.

## Isaac's rule for the PC

Sodoku Moto is Isaac's player character. The outline's beats state what he does; the writer executes exactly that and originates no decision for him beyond it. His actions in this book, chapter by chapter:

1. Orders the fair copy for Vaultmere; names Tabitha to carry it; answers Brida with "how's your stack" and goes back to the returns.
3. Orders the Keth-Gorrum's board hung uncut on the pillar beside the instrument; stands in front of it the length of a broth.
5. Orders the recount by name; strikes "attrition on the road"; puts Lambert on it; says the recount begins with the Seat's own column; says the Borin debt is paid before the Thin Weeks.
7. Does not shield Lambert; gives his own name beside Lambert's as the seal above the seal.
8. Rides for Stannvaard with Yoko and Renard; answers Tabitha "Because I told him to" and nothing else; tells her to date it.
9. Hands Kurosetsu to Borin for one night and stands unarmed; takes it back at dawn; declines to name the circle for the Ore Council; stops the border reports to the Dawi commission and sends the token with the last one.
10. Stands the whole length of the reading; sends the forty riders to the Bench under the instrument rather than to the crown.
12. Refuses Nalūn the salt; asks Yoko for the four lines and does not press when refused; goes up to the burying ground alone and sits on the ground beside the smallest stone.

Other creators' characters (Haruki, Dova'Kan, Gorgi, Ma'Kovu, Fushigi, Xhem, Sonzai) do not appear. Sonzai is Seiji Tenrai Moto, dead and entered *elar-ama*; he is record only.

## Voice note

The archive is written turn by turn: each scene answers a beat, stops on a decision, and leaves the remembering to author notes. The book remembers inside the prose. Its unit is the year rather than the turn, and the Kharven calendar (Ice-out, when the grass comes, the first frost, the Thin Weeks) is the structure, so a chapter knows its season the way a hall does. Names are read aloud and counted, and the count is the plot. The king is seen from outside as often as from inside, and no chapter closes on him choosing, because his choices are the outline's. The archive's habit of eleven of everything is retired; numbers are the ones the record gives. Nothing is glossed, nobody winks, nobody says what a thing means, and grief gets one named object per chapter and no adjective.

**Exemplar.** The board hung where he had ordered it hung, on the pillar beside the instrument, and the two did not look like a pair. The instrument was vellum, written by a man who would not delegate a name. The board was ash, split, the cuts made with a chisel by somebody who had carried it up a pass without stopping, and by the fourth day the hall's damp had got into the cuts and darkened them, so that the word could be read from the door by anyone who could read it, which was nobody.

**Anti-exemplar.** The board was more than a board. It was an accusation, and the accusation was the war itself, hung there for all to see. Sodoku stared at it, jaw tight, the weight of four hundred and seven lives pressing down on his shoulders like a physical thing. He had no words. There were no words.

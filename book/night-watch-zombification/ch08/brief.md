The salt was tacky. It was hygroscopic and it had been pulling damp out of the birch air for weeks, and the cracks by his nails where the ink lived stung the moment it touched them.

Whatever a working leaves on an object, the last hand to close on the object lies uppermost. It is coupled Aether with the signature of whoever coupled it, a layer of residue over whatever lay under, and his Shell took nothing in of its own and so had room for what was there. The Hand took the uppermost. It had never once taken anything else.

It came.

He was kneeling in the birch with the frame off his back.

The old block came out of the post-top at the third knock, a white stub worn down to nothing, and he picked it out of the hollow with two fingers and dropped it in the sack for the hens at home. The new one was cold from the sack. He stood it on its end in the hollow, and put the heel of his hand on its top, and leaned. It went down into the cut wood and stopped. He took up the beetle and gave it two taps, one on each shoulder of the block, and felt it seat.

His knees were wet through the gaiters and he did not mind it. His back was easy with the frame off. There was dark bread in the front of his jerkin for when he was home, and a thrush up in the birch going over the same few notes, and he was humming with it and a little behind. He looked at the block standing in the post, square and new, grey with the brown gone through, and he was pleased with it. That was all there was in him. It filled him to the edges and there was room left over.

The birch closed north of him. He did not look that way. He had no call to.

He took his hand off the block and reached for the frame by its strap, and his body was already turning, south, for home.

The minute ended where the hand came off.

Wystan was on one knee in the same hollow, with the same block in his fist and a strip of it drawn out of the post, and his own knees wet. He stayed there. He went back through the minute for the edges of it, the way he went back through all of them. A voice behind. A second pair of boots in the mud. A face, a coat, a hand held out with a block in it, a word about where the block had come from or who had paid. There was the thrush, and the wet knees, and the bread. There was nobody else anywhere in it.

He had the hand. It was Josse Garretsohn's.

Garretsohn had carried the salt and set it and gone home content, and had never in his life held anything but a beetle and a block and the half of a tally. The block had come to him made. Before it came to him there had been the barn at the home farm, and a steward who paid, and back behind the steward somebody who had poured the brown into the salt soft and let it set. None of them had been in the minute. None of them would ever be in a minute off this block. The last hand on it was a lick-keeper's. The Hand gave you the last hand.

He had told Brack he would get a hand and not a purse. He had the hand.

It was the wrong one.

A man who put his dose into salt and let other hands carry it out to the beasts was a man who never went near them. Wystan could see the whole shape of him from here. He worked at a bench somewhere a long way from any post, and he sent his work out through a barn and a steward's money and a lick-keeper's frame, and he stayed where he was. He did not go near them. He did not need to.

He drew the block the rest of the way out of the hollow and held it up in the one hand. It was lighter than a new block by what the beasts had taken off it.

"Tin," he said.

Yinzhi had it open already, the widest one in the satchel, the one the chapter used for soil. He set the block down into it on its end. It went in with a finger's width to spare on two sides and none on the others. She put the lid on and pressed it down all round with the heels of both hands until it seated. He took out the brass wax and warmed the stick in his fist, the left fist, and ran the wax round the join, and struck his brass into it while it was soft.

He opened the day book on his knee.

*Register file Ferriby. Tonic block, last lick, the birch, east side of the round. Set by the lick-keeper back before the thaw. Taken bare. Assay of record: carrying.* The division. His initials.

His right palm was still white with salt. The cracks at the nails had gone from stinging to a steady burn. He did not wipe it.

"Eat something," Yinzhi said.

"When we're down."

"Now. You stood all night at the stone on nothing and you're still walking like it." She held out a heel of bread, white, array-ground, out of the satchel with the tins. "Warden."

He took it by the edge of the crust, two fingers and a thumb, with the left hand, and ate it kneeling.

When he had finished he turned to the back of the book, to the pages he kept for them. Keld's stood at the head of the column, where it always stood. Under it were forty-one more, forty-two with Keld's, the newest a hind in the North Ferriby cut. He entered the forty-third beneath them. *Josse Garretsohn, lick-keeper. The last lick in the birch. Setting it.* He looked at that for a while, and then wrote one word after it, and closed the book on the pencil.

The word was *content*.

He got up off the knee. The left calf caught and let go. Then he took the gloves out of his coat and put them on, both of them, and did up the button at each wrist, and picked up the tin by its rim.

1. Ch6 (H08): canon is ch2 r0, where Malphas never says "separately" and Vesk already heard "The Lord said contraction. Twice, he said." So write Nuvalik's concealment as kept out of the book, not from Malphas, and open on her "Everything the fourth hind gave me is written down."
2. Ch6 (H20): Foss said on Day 1 "It will be a season" and Malphas accepted it. If he reads the manual on Day 5, show on the page how a copy came early (ch5: "The whole of the chapter's bench had read the field manual"), and have Foss remark that the season came short.
3. Ch6–8 (identity): the manual copy Foss reads carries no issue name or number that leads to Wystan, Keld or Cutler Row. In ch7, Wystan gives Josse only the brass and "the Register", never "Ashmore". The carter chain and Foss carry only "a man from the Register", and Tally never passes on the name.
4. Ch7 (H01): put the last lick at the walker's stopping place, just inside the birch on the east side of the estate's round, "well short" of the stone and out of sight of the cut. It is not 22 paces short, where the last hind lies and Wystan stood eight divisions. Pay H01 as the lick circuit turning home there, since the estate's round goes on west along the bound.
5. Ch7: Called Late put the newest passes "weeks old" on Day 5, some of them after the dead were down. Josse has not been to the Ferriby end since. Any "new tonic block" Wystan takes was set weeks back, and Josse's minute shows it being set then.
6. Ch9: Wystan prices the dose against eleven hinds and a stag (ch1 and ch3), never the outline's "nine hinds and a stag". Nine is the cell's rail count, and he cannot know it.
7. Ch12: Lyle's term (until the quarterly inspection) and the Register's position have been in her minute, under her silver, since ch3. Write ch12's ruling as an addition to that minute (produce a purse by the inspection), not as a first entry.
8. Ch6 onward: Wystan already knows that shut-room residue runs "always" to him (ch5). Never restate ch3's "came down on everybody in the room alike". Keep his wrong belief (ch25–44) as the dead being *sent* to him, and have ch44 pay only that nobody sent them.

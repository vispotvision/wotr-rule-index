## The lead

### Ned Chapman, chapman (peddler) of the Kessen road; Initiate of the Mercers' hall at Kingsbridge, on its pack roll

*ch 1.*
- **Age:** 20
- **Role:** the lead: a peddler who means to be the greatest merchant of the Inner World and a warrior too
- **From:** Midmarch commons, born on the Kessen road (Northern English stratum)
- **Look and kit:** Middling height and wiry, shoulders broad from the pack frame and calves bowed from the road; straw-yellow hair the color of old thatch, cut short with his own knife so it stands up at the crown; a sun-browned face with a wide crier's mouth, gray-green eyes and a chipped front tooth; a white scar through the left eyebrow where a wolf's claw opened it in 706; rope calluses across both palms and an old sealing-wax burn on the right thumb. A secondhand russet working coat faded to dry brick at the shoulders where the straps rub, leather patches at the elbows, the hem let down once, mismatched horn buttons from his worst stock and coin sewn into the lining; nailed boots gone gray at the creases, resoled twice, one heel built up; a drooping felt hat banded with a sample ribbon; every color he sells tied somewhere on him as a sample, so he reads as a walking stall. His measuring-staff: a quartered ash staff his own height and a hand, notched at the ell and the half-ell, the Accord's lead measure-stamp at the iron-ferruled butt, a split near the head bound in brass wire (Plain, Common). A Bauernwehr household knife, one edge honed to a hollow smile, a cracked beech grip plate bound in waxed red thread (Plain, Common). A hazel-and-ash pack frame under oiled canvas gone brown, a square of red wool patched in; a scored, ink-ringed tray lid; an Accord-stamped brass pocket beam bright where the fingers go and green in the crevices; and the long stick, a notched hazel tally of every debt he owes.
- **Voice:** A chapman's patter: quick, rhythmic, funny and warm, counting aloud, calling everyone friend or by their trade, closing every deal with 'done'; moved, he goes quiet and exact. 'Pins, laces, the best thread south of the Brine and a good word for free: buy the thread, friend, the word's worth nothing and I'll prove it.'
- **Wants:** To pay the woman who pulled him out of the ditch; to be the greatest merchant of the Inner World, a Paragon of the halls; and to be a warrior too, the first man to hold both silvers.
- **Refuses:** He will not cheat a customer and will not leave an account unpaid; the hinge is someone who cannot pay, to whom he gives.
- **Lies about:** He tells everyone he is doing well; the tell is his day purse, half of it washers, which he jingles while he says it. His father's purse of eleven silver never leaves the coat lining.
- **Power:** PROVENANCE (Trait: Everything Remembers Who Held It), Body Path, the Anamnesis lens from ch 10: whatever he holds tells his hands what its last owners did with it, and his hands can do it again, at his own force. Moves: BILL OF SALE! (the read, named ch 8), SECONDHAND! (one borrowed action, ch 8), MIXED LOT! (two owners back to back, ch 12), THE HAGGLE! (the read through the bind, ch 13), NO CHARGE! (the finisher with her sword, ch 14). His named moves are the Trait's own law called aloud, not techniques of his own; his first technique, and Stage III's Catalyst, is book two's FIRSTHAND. Colors: a worn-penny brown at waking; dark char with faded violet on the hands after the lens (ch 10); in a read, a woken owner's own hue shows through the faded violet of their afterimage. Sound word: thriff. Cost: every past he reads stays in him (dreams, aches, habits), a hard read moves the record out of the steel into him, a fast one scorches the grip. Counter: new or fire-wiped steel, a gauntlet, a fighter who closes inside the breath a read takes, crowded or Aether-rich ground.
- **Sheet:** At the open: [Ned Chapman · Human · Level 8] [Health: good · Feet: blistered] [Skills: Haggling B · Guessing Whose It Was B · Sums C] [Carrying a Pack B · Laughing It Off C · Swordwork F]; unwoken (Capped): no Essence, no class; the Initiate's silver token of the Mercers' hall (Common goods).
- **Bonds:** Alison Brewster (took him in after 706); Thimble (his runner); Gill Huxter (rival, the slow burn); Marion Thurlow (sponsor); the woman in the ditch (owed, unnamed until ch 8).

## Major

### Gisla Ravensworth, hayward (keeper of the pinfold and the barley) for Queen Idony Bushel; once a sergeant of the Short Measure Company

*ch 9.*
- **Age:** 41
- **Role:** the woman who pulled Ned out of the ditch in 706 and would take no payment; a sergeant of the company that sold the convoy's road that night
- **From:** Northern commons (Northern English stratum), a hayward's daughter
- **Look and kit:** Six foot and rawboned; gray-streaked black hair in a soldier's braid pinned up under a hayward's felt hood; a weathered face, the nose broken and set crooked, pale blue eyes that look past a man to what he is carrying; a white burn down the left forearm; the right knee swollen and strapped in a stiff leather brace so she walks with a swing. An undyed brown wool kirtle hitched for mud and a cracked leather jerkin over it; a hayward's horn of cow horn bound in brass on a cord; the pinfold keys on her belt; an ash staff shod in iron, worn black where her hands go; nailed boots. Her sword, pledged in 709 because she could neither sell it nor carry it: a plain arming sword, Proofed (D), Uncommon by its known mark, the maker's mark a hayward's horn stamped on the guard, a notch in the guard where a wolf bit it in 706.
- **Voice:** Few words, dry, flat northern vowels; she never explains herself and never raises her voice. 'I don't take pay for that. Nobody should.'
- **Wants:** To be left alone with the pinfold and the barley, and under that to have done one thing in 706 that was not for sale.
- **Refuses:** She will not take pay for a sword's work (her vow); the hinge is a thing given that is not a payment.
- **Lies about:** She says she does not remember the boy in the ditch; she has thought of him every winter.
- **Power:** Stage IV, Flourishing, Body Path; epithet Strays-Home (whatever strays from her comes home); colors the gray-blue of slate in rain, dim from disuse until ch 12. Calls low, at her card's volume: PINFOLD (the staff penning a weapon or a hand) and HEADLAND (her old name for the rising cut that took the wolf off a boy in a ditch in 706; it lives in her sword until NO CHARGE moves it into Ned). She fights now with the hayward's staff and will not be paid for it.
- **Sheet:** [Gisla Ravensworth · Level 96 · Stage IV, Flourishing · Path: Body] [Ardency B · Vitality C · Gnosis C · Dexterity B] [Right knee: the cap cracked and badly knit. No running, no kneeling.] [Vow: My sword is not for sale.] [Terms: no pay for a blade's work, ever; no blade carried that was paid for its work.] [Bargain: strays come home to me.] [Breach: lands at once.]
- **Bonds:** Ned Chapman (the boy in the ditch, owed and unpaid); Amabel Breakspear (the corporal who stayed when she left); Queen Idony Bushel (her employer, who pays in barley).

### Wolstan Kemp, Hall-Master of Kemp's Yard, a fighting and hiring hall at Kingsbridge

*ch 4.*
- **Age:** 52
- **Role:** the fighter's side of the table: he stakes Ned's Waking Draft on a bond and wants him in the yard
- **From:** Midmarch, a champion of a dead king's tourney ring
- **Look and kit:** Huge and bald, the scalp sunburned, a white beard cut square at the jaw, hands like spades and a laugh like a dropped anvil; a green doublet edged in gilt cord, strained at the buttons, over a linen shirt with the sleeves rolled; the Expert's silver fighting token of his own hall at his belt; a pollaxe with a blackened head hung over the yard's green door, which he takes down only for the spring muster.
- **Voice:** Booming, delighted with puns, calls Ned 'my chapman'; he makes a bargain sound like a joke and a joke sound like a bargain. 'The rule says one has to sell to the other. Fine. I'll buy.'
- **Wants:** A fighter who can learn from any blade, hired out at a good price; and to beat the Mercers' snobbery at their own table.
- **Refuses:** No dead man's luck in his yard: the yard-irons go back in the fire every quarter-day.
- **Lies about:** He tells Ned the bond is a kindness; it is the best investment he has made in ten years.
- **Power:** Stage V, Splintering, Body Path; epithet the Auctioneer (every blow raises the bid); colors a beaten copper-orange; GOING ONCE! GOING TWICE! GONE!, bare-handed (he splits the yard post with it); the pollaxe over the door.
- **Sheet:** [Wolstan Kemp · Stage V, Splintering · Path: Body]
- **Bonds:** Ned Chapman (his bond, his chapman); Bett Bodkin (his sergeant); Marion Thurlow (an old opponent across the square).

- **From:** Midmarch, a tokenless girl of the wharves once

### Amabel Breakspear, captain of the Short Measure Company, a free company of forty lances

*ch 5.*
- **Age:** 34
- **Role:** the captain whose company Ned sutlers for; a corporal of it on the night in 706 when it sold the convoy's road
- **From:** Midmarch, a Kingsbridge cooper's daughter
- **Look and kit:** Big-boned and broad in the hip, cropped red hair, a burn scar down the left side of the jaw; painted half-plate repainted in the company's black and yellow checks, the paint flaking at the elbow cops; a long-hafted axe slung across the back; a sash stuffed with other people's IOUs.
- **Voice:** Loud, generous, profane, laughs at herself first. 'Everybody's owed. Get in line, chapman, it's a long one and it moves slow.'
- **Wants:** To wash the company's name clean of 706.
- **Refuses:** She will never sell a road: the company's rule since 706.
- **Lies about:** She tells everyone the company's name is a joke about pay; at Nether Marl she says she never heard the name Gisla Ravensworth.
- **Power:** Stage IV, Flourishing, Body Path; epithet the Long Tab; colors a hot fox-orange; RUNNING TAB! (every swing she does not finish is banked in the haft of her long axe) and SETTLE UP! (it all pays out in one cleave).
- **Sheet:** [Amabel Breakspear · Stage IV, Flourishing · Path: Body]
- **Bonds:** Kit Quarrel, Larkin and Parkin Gedge, Ninian Ditchling (her company); Gisla Hayward (the sergeant who left); Ned Chapman (her sutler).

### Ottor Halfhand, night-cutter: an ex-cutter of the Thresh who kills practitioners for their Crystals (he calls himself a cordial-man)

*ch 7.*
- **Age:** 46
- **Role:** the killer in the cordial ring
- **From:** the Thresh cutters' camps; born Midmarch
- **Look and kit:** Lean and long-armed in gray wraps, a string of lucky teeth (monster teeth, some not) at his throat, a soft weathered face; his left hand short of two and a half fingers, the stumps polished smooth; a war crossbow of horn and steel with a windlass, a quiver of proofed bolts, a messer and a buckler, and an oiled leather roll holding a bone saw, a mallet and a cold chisel.
- **Voice:** Soft, polite, patient as a hunter in a blind. 'Nobody sits up with a man like that. He's nobody's. That makes him ours.'
- **Wants:** Enough money for a bought Stage, and never to be poor again.
- **Refuses:** He never fights fair: he kills only the sleeping, the drugged and the surprised.
- **Lies about:** He tells buyers the stones are monster cores.
- **Power:** Stage III, Ascension, Body Path; colors a dirty yellow-gray; the crossbow and the chisel; he whispers one word with the proofed bolt, 'Nobody's', and never fights fair. The ring's motif is his line: the dead nobody sits up with belong to them.
- **Sheet:** [Ottor Halfhand · Stage III, Ascension · Path: Body]
- **Bonds:** Petronel Gall (renders for him); Bartram Ellwand (sells for him).

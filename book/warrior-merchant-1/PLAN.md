# Paid in Kind — the plan

*The Warrior Merchant, Book One. A novella in 14 chapters, written straight through; the gates (chapters 1, 5, 10, 14) are read after. Start: Thursday, March 25, 715 IC (Lady Day).*

## Premise

Ned Chapman, a twenty-year-old peddler of the Hundred Crowns of the Midmarch in 715 IC, wants to be the greatest merchant of the Inner World and a warrior too, and the market mocks him as the Warrior Merchant because the merchant halls bar a sworn fighter from a trust title: one has to sell to the other. Nine years ago a woman warrior pulled him out of a ditch and would take no payment; his one wild concept, Provenance, lets him learn to fight from the dead men's swords he buys and sells, until her sword comes across his counter and he must pay a debt that cannot be paid in coin.

## Part One: Lady Day (chapters 1 to 5)

### 1. Chapman the Chapman
*Thursday, March 25, 715 IC (Lady Day) · Kingsbridge: the great bridge, the Mercers' hall on the height, the loft over the Rolling Strike*

Lady Day, quarter-day and rent day, on the great bridge at Kingsbridge under a blown blue sky, the storm stacked gold-edged over the minster's old-coin green: Ned Chapman, twenty, cries his pack of pins, thread and secondhand goods, prices a dead man's spoon by its wear (the unnamed knack), turns every secondhand blade on the bridge over to look for a hayward's horn on the guard as he has for nine years, sells a bride the right thread and a barefoot boy his first boots on tick. Paying quarterage at the Mercers' hall, with Marion Thurlow watching, he asks the Clerk Jasper Sealey how a man holds both silvers, and Jasper intones the hall rule and its gloss. Back on the bridge he says it aloud anyway: he will be the greatest merchant in the Inner World and a warrior too, because a debt that will not take coin can only be paid by becoming what she was; Hodge Toller, whose petty art means no pack can lie to him once his two copper are paid, makes the joke the market will never stop making, the Warrior Merchant. Two Sixth Arch Boys in red and white, singing their goose song, catch him selling the Harl Hill Scorchers' black-and-copper ribbons on their own arch, call their school's move on a peddler and put his tray in the Aldwater; Ned lands one honest crack of his measuring-staff across a Sixth Arch knuckle before they take the staff and beat him with it, while the Standing Army marches past to its dinner and one soldier in a red sash, his sword wrapped at the guard against the rain, tosses him a copper for the drowned tray. Gill Huxter fishes his ribbons out of the river and sells them back to him at a copper a yard as salvage. In the loft over the Rolling Strike he touches his father's purse of eleven silver, which the woman in the ditch would not take (the knack gives him what it always does, his father's thumb worn into the drawstring and the way he counted left-handed, the one past Ned never prices), cuts a notch on the long stick for the drowned stock, and decides to go south down the Kessen road, where his father died, to buy stock back off the men coming home from the war. At dawn he is back on the bridge with half a tray, crying WARRIOR MERCHANT ribbons, two a copper.

### 2. A Copper a Chain
*Monday, April 5 to Thursday, April 8, 715 IC · The Kessen road south of Kingsbridge: the Furlong, the chain lanes, a ditch-side camp*

Ned works the lane crowns off the Kessen road south, where no king signed the Accord and his Initiate's road-writ is waste paper, so the chains eat his profit a copper at a time; at the Furlong, King Baldwin Furlong the Long's kingdom one furrow of the road long, the short king walks his border every morning with a rod. Ned buys a long messer off a hungry levy deserter coming home from the war, and his hands settle into the dead owner's guard before he has learned any guard at all. That night three of King Baldwin's own chain-men rob him in a ditch-side camp on the furrow: unwoken against unwoken, he drops one, alive, his scalp split, with the borrowed twitch of the messer, and takes a cut across the forearm. At dawn King Baldwin sentences his own men to hang, and they step one furrow east into the next kingdom, dropping Ned's stock to do it and keeping the messer; Ned pays the court fee with a verse naming the king, and the king has it cried. Kit Quarrel of the Short Measure Company watches the whole hearing from the saddle, laughs, and stitches the forearm for a copper; when Ned says his father died on this road in 706, Kit laughs too loud at his own joke about the company's name being about pay and rides off without his copper, which Ned cuts on the long stick as owed.

### 3. The Queen's Machine
*Saturday, April 17, 715 IC · Tollgarth: the Self-Minding Portcullis, the beam and market court, the tap-room of the Struck Bushel*

At Tollgarth, the castle-town shut across the Harl pass, Ned talks his way past the Self-Minding Portcullis and Queen Tizz Copperwick the Fourth's toll machine (she shouts each contraption's name and mark as it misfires) and saves her a wagonload of steel: the bucklers her guards were sold as proven veterans' pieces are new Common steel soaked in a bucket of cow urine, the stamp real, the wear a week old, the smell worse. She pays him in a Pennycrown deed, a promise everyone laughs at, and a ride through the machine. Gill Huxter undersells him on ribbons and weighs light with her thumb on the scale; he sees it and does not call it, and she is furious that he noticed and let her off. The minstrel Lettice Rimer makes the first song of the Warrior Merchant out of his tray going into the river, getting every detail wrong; when the tap-room of the Struck Bushel sings it at him, Ned passes his hat for it, a copper a verse. The crier's bill that evening cries a practitioner found dead in his bed at a Hem-road inn with his chest opened, and nobody in the tap-room will say the word for what was taken until Queen Tizz says it loud from the door: Cordial, somebody is drinking him.

### 4. Capped
*Thursday, May 6, 715 IC · Kingsbridge: Kemp's Yard behind the green door, the Mercers' hall*

Back in Kingsbridge, Ned asks Hall-Master Wolstan Kemp, the Auctioneer, to take him on at Kemp's Yard behind the green door; Kemp roars at the old words (kemp and chapman, the fighter and the dealer, at one table) and shows him the five doors a sealed Crystal opens by on a price board on the yard wall, four chalked NOT FOR SALE and the fifth NINETY GOLD, ASK, then splits the yard post bare-handed to show what a woken arm is worth: GOING ONCE! GOING TWICE! GONE! Bett Bodkin, holding her colors down, shows him in three passes what a Capped man is against anyone woken, calling NEEDLEWORK! flat each time; on the third pass Ned fights like a peddler with a measuring-staff and aims one touch at her collar so far outside a woken fighter's habits that she lifts her colors by reflex to turn it, thin bright pewter for a breath, and the yard howls that a Capped man made Bett show. He sells her red waxed thread at cost to rebind the grip she cracked on him; her old buckler, he notices, is the one thing in the yard Kemp never puts back in the fire, because it is older than the yard and hers. Kemp offers the fifth door: a Waking Draft staked by the hall on a bond, and shows him the yard-irons, which go back in the fire every quarter-day so no candidate carries another man's luck into the yard. At the Mercers' hall Bartram Ellwand, a journeyman factor in a plum coat far too good for his silver and the loudest seller in Kingsbridge after Ned, brags that he prices anything, the dead included, and tells the Court the Warrior Merchant will never pass a tray Ellwand helps set; he wants Marion Thurlow's seat on the Court and means to sink her chapman to get it. Marion warns Ned that Kemp wants a fighter to hire out, not a merchant, and what the hall rule does to a man who carries both silvers, and tells him to stop selling the ribbons; Ned tells her nobody has ever done it and that is the price he likes.

### 5. Wolves Follow Armies
*Friday, May 21 to Sunday, May 23, 715 IC · The Kessen road to the Hem, the Short Measure Company's baggage at dusk*

Ned sells himself on as sutler to Captain Amabel Breakspear's Short Measure Company, riding a Thresh contract to the Hem under the Center's Turn, and sells the levies in the column boots on tick against payday. In the column's wake at dusk the Greater Wolves come for the baggage, the pack-marks along their flanks glowing as they hunt: Amabel, the Long Tab, lights her colors hot fox-orange and works her long axe down the line, RUNNING TAB!, banking every swing she does not finish, until SETTLE UP! pays it all into the alpha dog in one cleave that takes the wolf and the cart-tongue behind it, while Larkin and Parkin Gedge fight the whole dusk splitting one boast in halves. Unwoken, back to a cart, Ned holds a dead spearman's spear in a stranger's grip and cannot drive the force the steel remembers; Kit Quarrel's bolt takes the wolf on him (Three copper), and Ned shields the camp foundling Ninian Ditchling with his own body and the pack. Three of his boot tallies die with their men, and the danger overcome lifts him a Level. Amabel offers to sign him with the company, which would make him sworn and end his trust title; he refuses, and she is oddly gentle when he tells her his father died on the Kessen road in 706. Because she cannot take a ten-year-old onto a Thresh contract, she puts Ninian in Ned's keep for the summer, a copper a day at Alison Brewster's that he will never ask the company for; at the camp fire, bitten and furious, Ned says it aloud: he will wake, whatever it costs.

## Part Two: Midsummer (chapters 6 to 10)

### 6. The Bond of the Fifth
*Thursday, June 24 to Friday, June 25, 715 IC (Midsummer) · Kingsbridge: the Mercers' hall, Kemp's Yard, Petronel Gall's licensed house, the loft over the Rolling Strike*

Midsummer Day, the quarter-day, with the Sixth Arch Boys and the Harl Hill Scorchers fighting their ninety-year goose war up and down the bridge: Ned crosses it twice between the hall and Kemp's Yard selling bandages to both sides at cost, the Sixth Arch knuckle he cracked on Lady Day included. That morning he forgives the barefoot boy's boot tick (the boy's mother died at Whitsun) and comes up a silver short for the Apprentice fee, which Marion Thurlow advances against his term. At the sitting he accounts for a season's book to the copper and finds every fraud on the tray, including Ellwand's plant: a famous dead captain added to a sword's paper, a past owner the steel never knew, the fraud no assay can catch and Ned's knack can, which makes Ellwand an enemy in front of the Court and shows Ellwand exactly what the chapman can read. That evening at Kemp's Yard he signs the Bond of the Fifth (a fifth of all he takes, coin and kind, for nine years, never less than fifteen silver a quarter, whether the Draft wakes, warps or kills) for a Waking Draft of ninety gold, poured at Petronel Gall's licensed house; Petronel, pouring, says she buys the finest stock in the Midmarch and never asks where it was born. He drinks with empty hands on purpose so no dead man's record goes into his seed, and the long bad night of the waking (Alison and Ninian sitting up with him) opens his Crystal: Stage I, Murmuring, and a Trait the window names Everything Remembers Who Held It, at Level 9, and he laughs out loud at how plain the soul's words are. At dawn, woken an hour, he turns the empty bottle in his hands, and in two breaths its seal gives him the hand that pressed it, a left hand short of two and a half fingers; he wakes wanting his meat bloody, dreaming of running with a pack, and Petronel enters his woken Crystal on Kingsbridge's roll. At sundown Alison bangs the bar and the Rolling Strike does it the Midmarch way, every woken drinker lighting their coat at once for the pride of it, and Ned lights his for the first time, a worn-penny brown so dull the room cheers it louder.

### 7. The Armed Wake
*Monday, July 12 to Wednesday, July 14, 715 IC (Auren's day) · Kingsbridge: the Strays' room above the south wharves, the estate sale under the eleventh arch · POV Avelina Stray*

Open on the Standing Army of Kingsbridge, two hundred men in red with their banners furled, sitting Osmund Stray's armed wake on Auren's day with weapons across their knees and lamps lit all down the south wharf stair: the Army that is home for dinner every day of its life sits up all night for one of its own. Avelina Stray, his widow, a lacemaker, knows what the Tuppence bailiff will not say: Osmund took a side contract on the Hem road, drank a sleeping draught in his beer at a drovers' inn, and came home with his chest opened and his Crystal gone, nowhere near a field, and she knows a night-cutter's saw and chisel when she sees their work. A polite man with half a left hand comes to the wake asking to buy Osmund's sword before the estate sale; he pried it loose from the dead hand on the Hem road and left it there when the bailiff's lanterns came up, and he knows from a factor's gossip what the man on the bridge can tell from a grip. She refuses him by instinct. She has heard the chapman on the bridge can tell whose a thing was, so at the estate sale under the eleventh arch she has the Army's drummer bid the half-handed man's proxy up and drop out at nine gold, so the sealed lot with Osmund's horn-marked sword is knocked down to Ned Chapman on credit, and she watches him open the chest.

### 8. Bill of Sale
*Wednesday, July 14 to Friday, July 16, 715 IC · Kingsbridge: the loft over the Rolling Strike, the alley under the north piers, Barnaby Pledger's shop*

At Stage I he cannot choose an owner, so in the dark loft he reads slowly, and the read gets its name, BILL OF SALE: the loudest owner in the steel is a tall woman with a long reach and the guard he has dreamed of for nine years, her slate-blue glimmering through the faded violet of her afterimage, and the notch where a wolf bit the guard the day she put it between him and the wolf; her hand left the grip slowly, alive: she let it go. Over her, near and dull, lie Osmund's death-clench and a half-fingered left hand prying it loose; under her, deeper than Murmuring can reach, one harder cut the steel will not give up yet; the hands between are a smudge. He understands the sword is the only witness to Osmund's killing and he is the only man alive who can read it, and that it has marched past his tray every morning for four years. He sleeps with his hand on the grip, so when the ring's hired thief comes in the night (one of the two Sixth Arch Boys who put his tray in the Aldwater, earning a silver on the side) the breaths are already spent: in the alley under the north piers, PROVENANCE: SECONDHAND! gives him her everyday cut, worn into the edge by years of use, and he pays back the Lady Day beating with interest, wins, wrenches his wrist and keeps the dead soldier's flinch for life [Level: 9 to 15]. Gill Huxter binds the wrist and charges two coppers, for the bandage, Warrior, swearing she only came for the money. In the morning he declares the horn-marked sword to Jasper Sealey, and Marion Thurlow sets her Master's token to it as its second signature, so it is lawfully his to hold and carry; to see Barnaby Pledger's book he buys out a seamstress's pledged shears, which Article XXVIII says were never pledgeable, and hands them back to her. The book gives him what steel cannot: Gisla Ravensworth, one sword, a hayward's horn on the guard, pledged 709, unredeemed, sold to O. Stray 711. Then he does what a chapman does with a lost thing: he cries it from the bridge and pays Lettice Rimer a copper to carry it up the road, GISLA RAVENSWORTH, OWED ELEVEN SILVER AND A BOY'S LIFE.

### 9. The Pinfold
*Wednesday, July 21 to Friday, July 23, 715 IC · The Bushel barley country: the pinfold, the water meadows, the road north · POV Gisla Hayward*

Gisla Ravensworth, forty-one, keeps the pinfold and the barley for Queen Idony Bushel the Second, who docks her wages heaped and pays them level. Every morning her window shows her vow, its terms and its bargain, as it has since 706 (her sword is not for sale; no pay for a blade's work, ever, and no blade carried that was paid for its work; strays come home to me), and the bargain works on the page: a strayed heifer walks back into the pinfold on its own while she watches. She walks the water meadows every dry morning to see they are still wet, because what follows the herds will not go where the ground gives. A minstrel at the pinfold gate sings Ned's cry with the debt gone up tenfold (a hundred and ten silver and a boy's life) and a new verse saying the chapman will be at the Coat-Turning, and she knows the boy from the ditch lived and found what she pledged. Two strangers with cutters' teeth come asking after the woman with the horn sword, meaning to wait at her gate for the chapman; she sends them wrong. That night the song sends her back to 706 in a marked flashback at full weight: Gisla at thirty-two, Stage IV, her colors slate-blue at full blaze; the convoy on the Kessen road and the order to ride on, which she disobeyed; Greater Wolves in the wagons; and the rising cut she called HEADLAND taking the alpha off a boy in a ditch; the boy holds up his dead father's purse and she refuses it. The flashback holds back why the convoy was left; it shows why she pledged the sword she could neither sell nor carry. At dawn she takes her hayward's staff and starts walking north toward Pennycrown on a knee that will not bend.

### 10. Nether Marl
*Tuesday, July 27 to Thursday, July 29, 715 IC · Nether Marl: the Mouth, the Upper Workings, the gallery above the Lower Workings*

Ned goes down into Nether Marl, King Hamo the Undefeated's kingdom that is wholly a dungeon, as sutler and porter to the Short Measure Company's delve for cores, because he is ten gold short of Michaelmas and the company is the only crew on the road old enough to know a name from 706; he asks every lance for Gisla Ravensworth, and Amabel says she never heard it. At the Mouth, King Hamo, selling licenses and taking the toll in, tells the crews the Minotaur charges straight and has never once turned, the Stair-Sutler tells of the Master who went down years ago and whose spear is still in the gallery because nobody can carry it past the bull, Larkin Gedge swears he will drink from the Minotaur's horn, and the bookie on the barrel gives depth odds on the twins. The masks in the Upper Workings are warm dark gray with faded violet only a suggestion under it, and Ned's newly woken Shell leaks and draws the spawn toward him. Above the Lower Workings, where the gray is veined with violet that flickers when the spawn rouse, the Minotaur comes up, and Larkin dies holding his sword. Ned puts his bare hand on the raw seam of the dungeon's Anamnesis and the gallery lights up with it: every wall's afterimages wake at once, the dungeon's dead fighting their last fights in faded violet around the crew, among them the dead Master who once made the Minotaur's charge try to turn [Level: 15 to 26] [Threshold passed: Stage II, Welling] [Anamnesis: harmonized]. With that Master's spear, SECONDHAND, he makes the bull's charge try to turn at the gallery's elbow and gets the crew out, and leaves the spear on the gallery floor where the dungeon kept it, above his silver to carry. Above ground he reads Larkin's sword for Parkin lightly, and says why he will not read it hard: a hard read takes a man out of his steel and into the reader, and Parkin would be left holding a sword with no brother in it; he gives it to him for nothing. At King Hamo's table, paying the much larger toll out, Parkin finishes the boast Larkin started in the gallery, alone and at the top of his voice, and the whole company roars the end of it at a king who has never been below the Mouth. Parkin, Larkin's heir under the company's instrument, gives Ned his brother's share, a T3 core, because a porter is nobody on the instrument; Amabel enters it in the company book as owed to Ned and keeps it salted in the company's sack under the Nether Marl clerk's chit until he holds a silver that can sell it. After Larkin is buried, Kit Quarrel says the name once by the Mouth's fire: she keeps a pinfold for the barley queen.

## Part Three: Michaelmas (chapters 11 to 14)

### 11. The Coat-Turning
*Monday, August 9 to Thursday, August 12, 715 IC · Pennycrown: Crown Row, the Coat-Turning fair-ground, the market court*

At Pennycrown's Coat-Turning, the three-week fair where the kings of the Midmarch announce their sides in full heraldry and the bookies take odds on the order, Ned sells a levy boy a dead man's helm and tells him for nothing which blow it turned, and keeps the dead man's flinch. Bartram Ellwand comes with a countersign, so the consignment above his silver is lawful, and Ned, ten gold short of Michaelmas, takes it with his eyes open, until Ellwand adds a sealed core lot to carry to a rendering house under a hall chit and Ned's palm on its wrapping finds the hand that tied it: a left hand short of two and a half fingers, the hand from the Draft's seal and Osmund's last grip. He refuses the carry; Ellwand withdraws the countersign that hour, every sale Ned made that morning goes voidable, and the market court amerces him to his means, a first offense under Article XXX, the second striking his title. Ned pays the amercement with a bow in front of the crowd, the way Queen Tizz paid hers in 709, and Tizz, delighted, cheers him from her seat. Gill Huxter, selling Warrior Merchant ribbons on her best day ever with no cut for him, pays his booth fee in ribbons as a loan at a scandalous rate and puts a bet on him on Clarice Nock's slate. At sundown the kings announce their sides and every woken guest on the fair-ground lights their coat at once, a hundred crowns' colors in one blaze; Ned lights his in public for the first time, dark char with faded violet on the hands, spilling, and scorches the grip of a king's sword he was only pricing, wiping its record by accident, while the criers cry the Warrior Merchant from one end of Crown Row to the other. He is already packing for the barley road when, at the fair's edge, a tall woman with a hayward's horn and a bad knee tells him men with lucky teeth are asking after his sword; when he asks if she is the woman from the ditch, she says she never pulled any boy out of anything.

### 12. The Hayward's Horn
*Saturday, August 21 to Monday, August 23, 715 IC · The barley road, Queen Idony's granary crown, the pinfold, the water meadows at dusk*

Gisla walks with him toward the barley country because the night-cutters are going there, not for any debt, she says; she will not take payment, and when Ned offers the sword back, the sword for the ditch and we are square, she refuses, because taking it would make 706 a sale. On the barley road she tells him the rest: the company sold the convoy's road to the other side that night, and she took her share of the price before she turned back alone for the ditch, which is why she would not touch his father's purse; and she gives him back one exact thing from that night, the cry his father was making to keep the carts calm when the wolves came. Ned, who has carried her as a saint for nine years, has to decide whether a debt to a woman paid for his father's death still stands. Queen Idony Bushel has hired the Short Measure Company for the herd week, the Midmarch way (a bounty on her doors and a free company), and at the granary crown Ned confronts Amabel, who lied at Nether Marl and was the corporal on that road; she says the company sold one road and has sold none since, and he cannot forgive her yet. In the hot iron wind of a dry August, with the stilts' barrel bodies passing over the hedges on legs like ships' masts, Ned sells the harvesters his whole stock of sickles and whetstones at cost so they can cut the barley green before the herd arrives, and Idony buys them heaped and pays level. At dusk Ottor Halfhand's night-cutters come for the sleeping, the way they always do, behind a supper with a sleeping draught in the pinfold's beer; Ned, who sells the everyday Drafts and knows that bitter taste, never drinks it, and Gisla's herald wakes her: her colors come up slate-blue for the first time in years as she pens the first man's crossbow in her staff, PINFOLD, said low. Caught out of their ambush with the greywalkers filing into the barley at their backs, Ottor's men have one road out, through Ned: he fights Ottor's Stage III second, Dunnet No-Ears, with the pinfold's old bill-hook, carried by two dead haywards before Gisla, two dead men's blows back to back, MIXED LOT!, and the seam between them nearly kills him, while Ottor shoots from the dark and never closes; they live by reaching the wet water meadows, where the greywalkers will not follow. At the water's edge Ned decides the debt stands, because she turned back when the sale said ride on; he sees the half hand on the crossbow's stock in the last light and names the ring aloud: the cutter, the Drafter who renders, and the factor who sells. Gisla walks north with him to see the half hand answered for, she says, and not for any debt.

### 13. One Has to Sell to the Other
*Wednesday, September 29, 715 IC (Michaelmas) · Kingsbridge: Kemp's Yard at morning, the Mercers' hall in the afternoon*

Five weeks of the Tuppence bailiff and the Minster's clerk turning him away (a chapman's feel is no evidence, and an Apprentice's word does not stand against a factor's) bring him to Michaelmas, the quarter-day the bond's first fifth falls due. In the morning at Kemp's Yard, on irons out of the fire that morning so nothing the yard owns has a past, with Ninian cheering loudest in the yard crowd, Gisla silent at the back and Benedict Graine reading the coil aloud (Welling), Ned sits the yard test against Bett Bodkin holding her crown down to his Stage; she runs the same three passes as on the day he was Capped, and he answers them now on blows he has made his own. In a clinch he gets his bare left palm flat on the rim of Bett's old buckler, the one thing in the yard with a past, and THE HAGGLE! is born: her own steel tells him which line she favors and where her edge has landed before, and he winds into the hole [Skill rising: Leaning on Steel] and no Threshold, because the Crystal is waiting for the first blow no sword taught him. Kemp strikes him the Apprentice fighting token and Ned refuses it at the door of the yard, in front of the bookies; Kemp roars the old words back at him, kemp and chapman at one table, says the fifth runs by trade or by hire and he can wait nine years, and hangs the struck token on a nail over the green door with Ned's name chalked under it, where the bookies quote odds on the day he comes back for it. In the afternoon he sits his Journeyman examination at the Mercers' hall on a short book (the book audited and quit, two appraisers who dislike each other, the law asked aloud), with Marion Thurlow and, as an irregular third surety, Queen Tizz Copperwick's seal, her Pennycrown deed honored, which Ellwand objects to and the Court takes on Marion's motion. Ellwand reads Article XXX against the sword and the stone, and Marion lays her countersign and the company chit on the table. At the belt, Ellwand has had the struck token off Kemp's nail slipped into Ned's belt purse, where the hall rule strikes a title on sight; Ned's palm finds on its wax the grip that handed him the Pennycrown consignment, and he lays the token and the market court's roll (the countersign withdrawn the hour he refused the carry) before the Court together, then shows his belt: no fighting token, and one of his own Warrior Merchant ribbons. Jasper Sealey reads the hall rule against his name and enters it on the roll, the Court votes him Journeyman with Ellwand's name in the room, and Ned tells the hall he will be the first man to hold both silvers. Amabel brings the salted stone; three parts full, it fetches about four gold at the renderer's door on his new silver, and he pays the fifteen-silver floor of his fifth, the hall advances his Journeyman fee against his term, the rest goes into Avelina's hand against her nine, and he cuts what he still owes her on the long stick; Gill's bet pays. The hall is still cheering when Thimble runs through the door: Ninian is gone from the Rolling Strike's yard, and nailed to Ned's barrow is a bill sealed in wax by a left hand short of two and a half fingers, one sword and the man who reads it, at the ditch under the north piers.

### 14. Paid in Kind
*Wednesday, September 29 to Friday, October 1, 715 IC · Kingsbridge: the ditch under the north piers, the market court and the Scale Minster steps, the great bridge at dawn*

That night Ned follows with the horn-marked sword, Kit Quarrel's crossbow, Gisla on her ruined knee, and the Short Measure Company in black-and-yellow checks with Amabel at its head, because the company that left a convoy to the wolves in 706 will not leave its boy; he lets her stand with him without forgiving her, and her lances hold the north road against the rest of the night-cutters while Ned, Gisla and Kit go down into the ditch. The Standing Army turns out after dark for the first time in living memory, for Osmund, and lines every stone of its kingdom with torches to the last stone of the north pier and not one inch past it, so the ditch stays dark for the read while the whole bridge watches from the parapet. Ottor shoots from across the ditch water; Gisla, who cannot run or close on a knee that will not bend, takes his first proofed bolt through the thigh because she is watching the boy and not the loose, so the ditch is Ned's to cross. Ned's read of Ottor is the half hand he has felt three times, which cannot crank a windlass, so Ottor spans standing with his colors pulled to his chest against Kit's bow, bare in the crossbow arm for that breath. Ned knows a hard read leaves the steel empty, Osmund's last grip and the half hand with it, the widow's only witness and the dearest provenance on any counter in the Midmarch, and he spends it on Ninian anyway: he reads all the way down to her one true blow, HEADLAND, the rising cut that killed the wolf in 706, and spends it to pull a boy out of a ditch for nothing, NO CHARGE!, her slate-blue standing at full blaze over him in her afterimage while the iron in the street creeps toward the blade: her fighting moves out of the steel and into him for life, his right shoulder tears on a reach longer than his own, and Ottor's crossbow arm comes off. Kit's bolt ends Ottor, called deadpan by its price, Three copper, and Kit declares the kill at the north pier before the Scale Minster's bailiff and the crowd before dawn. Before the market closes, Avelina Stray brings her plea at the dusty foot with Ned as her sworn Journeyman witness and Ottor's chisel-roll on the table, its killings read off the tools; Petronel Gall's house is searched and her book seized, in which Ned finds his own Draft entered as true Greater Wolf, the crate carried in by O.H., the one honest load the half hand ever brought her, and he laughs until he has to sit down on the Minster steps. Jasper Sealey strikes Bartram Ellwand's title on the hall rule's old words, buyer and seller of the same blood, meant to the letter for the first time, and Ellwand runs for the river in his plum coat and goes downriver ahead of the Bench. Ned spends his father's eleven silver on Ninian's keep at Alison Brewster's, the one account he never could pay, and pays Avelina out of Osmund's lot, sold piece by piece under his Journeyman silver. He does not hand the empty sword to Gisla: he lays it in the road as a stray, unclaimed, and by her own bargain it comes home to her, a plain sword that remembers nothing of a paid year, only one blow given for nothing, so she can carry it. He sponsors Gill onto the Mercers' pack roll, a pitch that does not move, and she insists on paying the first quarter in kind. On the bridge at dawn Halloo Hal Horner cries THE WARRIOR MERCHANT, Hodge Toller calls out that one has to sell to the other, and Ned turns to the name and answers: not if one gives, for nothing.

## Hooks (planted → paid)

- **H01-unpaid-purse** (ch 1 → 14): Ned carries his dead father's purse of eleven silver, the payment the woman in the ditch refused in 706, unspent for nine years in the lining of his coat; his day purse is the one half full of washers. Paid when he spends the eleven silver on Ninian's keep, the one account he never could pay.
- **H02-hayward-horn** (ch 1 → 8): The sword that saved him bore a maker's mark of a hayward's horn on the guard and a notch where a wolf bit it; Ned has looked for that mark on every blade for nine years. Paid when the sword turns up in Osmund Stray's lot and is read.
- **H03-half-hand** (ch 6 → 12): At dawn after the waking, the empty Draft bottle's seal gives Ned a left hand short of two and a half fingers. The same hand pried the sword from the dying Osmund and tied Ellwand's core lot. Paid when Ned sees Ottor Halfhand's hand on a crossbow stock at the pinfold and names the ring.
- **H04-kemp-and-chapman** (ch 4 → 13): Kemp tells Ned that kemp and chapman are the old words for the fighter and the dealer, sitting at one table. Paid at the yard test, when Kemp strikes the fighting token for a chapman and Ned refuses it.
- **H05-blank-irons** (ch 4 → 13): Kemp's Yard puts its yard-irons back in the fire every quarter-day so no candidate carries another man's luck into the yard, so they read blank to Provenance; the one thing never reforged is Bett Bodkin's own old buckler. Paid at the yard test, which Ned passes on blows he owns and wins with his palm on that buckler.
- **H06-ninian-ditch** (ch 5 → 14): Ninian Ditchling, the Short Measure Company's camp foundling, was named for the ditch he was found in; Ned shields him from the wolves. Paid when Ned pulls him out of a ditch for nothing with NO CHARGE.
- **H07-short-measure** (ch 2 → 12): The Short Measure Company's name, Kit's too-loud laugh and Amabel's gentleness about 706 hide what the company did: under its old captain it sold the convoy's road to the other side, and Ned's father died on that sale. Paid when Gisla tells him on the barley road and he confronts Amabel.
- **H08-the-vow** (ch 9 → 14): Gisla's window has shown her vow every morning since 706: her sword is not for sale; no pay for a blade's work, ever, and no blade carried that was paid for its work; strays come home to me. Paid when Ned lays the emptied sword in the road as a stray and it comes home to her, a blade that remembers only one blow given for nothing.
- **H09-ellwands-fake** (ch 4 → 6): Bartram Ellwand, who wants Marion Thurlow's seat, plants a famous dead captain on a sword's paper on the Apprentice tray; Ned's knack catches the false past owner in front of the Court, makes an enemy, and shows Ellwand what he can read.
- **H10-floor-of-the-bond** (ch 6 → 13): The Bond of the Fifth never takes less than fifteen silver a quarter, whatever he takes. Paid at Michaelmas, when the first fifth falls due and he pays it with the core he can sell only as a Journeyman.
- **H11-warrior-merchant** (ch 1 → 14): The Warrior Merchant begins as Hodge Toller's joke and by the next dawn is Ned's best cry, WARRIOR MERCHANT ribbons, two a copper: he answers to the name for a copper and to Ned for free. Gill undercuts him on it, Lettice sings it wrong, the tap-room pays a copper a verse, the criers cry it at the Coat-Turning, and his belt at the Journeyman sitting shows one of the ribbons and no fighting token. On the bridge at dawn in ch 14 he answers to it for nothing, the same price as NO CHARGE.
- **H12-queens-deed** (ch 3 → 13): Queen Tizz Copperwick pays Ned for the urine-aged bucklers with a Pennycrown deed, a promise everyone laughs at. Paid when her seal stands as the irregular third surety at his Journeyman sitting.
- **H13-the-hard-read** (ch 10 → 14): Reading Larkin's sword for Parkin, Ned says why he will not read it hard: a hard read takes a man out of his steel and into the reader. Paid when NO CHARGE empties Gisla's sword of Osmund's witness and her own past, by Ned's choice.
- **H14-gills-pitch** (ch 1 → 14): Gill Huxter wants a pitch on the bridge that does not move; she will not be pitied and will not be given anything. Paid when Ned sponsors her onto the Mercers' pack roll and she insists on paying the first quarter in kind.
- **H15-drafts-stock** (ch 6 → 14): Petronel Gall says she never asks where her stock was born, and the half hand pressed the seal on Ned's own Waking Draft: did he wake on a murdered man's Crystal? Paid when her seized book shows his Draft entered as true Greater Wolf, carried in by O.H.

## Voice

Close third, past tense, a chapman's ear: the narration prices things the way Ned does (by wear, by weight, by who held them before), counts aloud under its breath, and turns warm and exact when someone needs something he can sell them. Every chapter shows a sale that helps someone and costs Ned something; every fight is a negotiation he is losing until it is not, and he is funny while he loses it, crying his wares through a split lip. Bright, loud Midmarch color under big stormy skies (painted shields, ribbons, red cattle, kings who cost a penny, coats lit at sundown), the woken around him loud with named arts and epithets, and the dead men's lots underneath it all. Exemplar: 'The spoon had been held by a left-handed man who stirred the same way for thirty years and died with his mouth full, and Ned priced it at a copper, because the widow needed the copper more than she needed to know.' Anti-exemplar: 'The market was gray and loud and Ned was tired of all of it.'

# Fight research: The Warrior Merchant, book one

*Foundation brief for the fighting in Ned Chapman's first novella: fourteen chapters, the Hundred Crowns of the Midmarch, 715 IC, the twenty-fifth year of the Dark Ages. Research, not prose. Nothing here is filed to a card or the wiki. The merchant's ladder, the token sittings and the hall rule's mechanics are in `research/trade.md`; this file is the fight.*

**Tags.** **CANON** quotes the repo by file key and line (keys in section 10); canon governs every fact and number. **INVENTED** is mine, allowed where nothing is on file (R79-11) and kept consistent with canon; every invented size, price, name and procedure is a proposal the outline may keep or cut. **OUTSIDE** is reference research (Wiktenauer, Wikipedia, VS Battles, Superpower Wiki): ideas and fact-checks only, never canon, never quoted into prose or a `verbatim` field. **FLAG** is a question for the outline or Isaac; nothing here resolves a conflict.

## 1. The law a fight in this book runs on

1. **Grade decides, by the differential rule.** CANON: "The Grade of the stat in use decides a contest" [NAT:165]. "One full Grade over an opponent in the stat in use wins a direct exchange of that stat without much of a contest. Two Grades is one-sided. At three, the lower fighter has stopped being in a fight and is surviving an event." [GRD:64]
2. **The upset limit.** CANON: "one Stage crossable with a read, two only with a vow or a breakthrough, three or more shows in the room first and ends the fight the way a rope ends unless something breaks); ties break on the read, then what is left to spend, then the ground" [NAT:165]. The canon "read" is a fighter's read of his opponent, and Ned's whole power is a read. That is the hinge of every upset he wins.
3. **Skill is not force.** CANON: "A skill's letter says how well the thing gets done. It puts no force behind it." and "Brawling B on Ardency F is a beautifully placed punch from someone who cannot hurt you." [SKL:107]. "Where skill earns its keep is everywhere the stats tie and everywhere they are not asked." [SKL:109]. The Midmarch's own proof: Marta Kell, the unwoken butcher of Tollgarth's Tripe Row, took a Stage V hobgoblin's arm off with Butchery EX and still lost the fight, the shop and a hip [SKL:14 to 22].
4. **Ordinary men die fast.** CANON: R60-01, "one good cut or a ball in the gut can kill, often days later from infection; most fights end in the first seconds; a wounded man is out of the fight"; R70-102, "keep unranked fighters under R60-01's fast, brutal lethality."
5. **The unwoken body.** CANON: the Dormant Shell gives "No stat output above Hollow Grade" [CLS:247]. Hollow is below 60 J, "The kinetic energy of a thrown fist at human speed", with a reaction that is purely biological, 150 to 300 ms [GRD:68, GRD:108]. Woken, Stage I caps at E and Stage II at D [STG:67 to 68]; E reacts in 40 to 80 ms and D in 15 to 40 ms [GRD:110 to 111].
6. **Pressure.** CANON: "one Stage up bends attention; two, sweat and nausea; three or more drops people; a Band gap changes the weather. The unwoken take the Aura table by Grade: dread from F to D, weight in the room from C, knees bending from A." [NAT:253]
7. **The coat and its tells.** CANON: reinforcement is "a coat of the practitioner's own aura, bought with reserve, focusable to one place at the cost of a thin rest, extended into held gear, and lost asleep, drugged or surprised", and spending shows "at half the breath shortens and the aura dims, at a quarter tremor and a thinning color, at a tenth the aura flickers and the Trait stutters; maxing out is heat, blood and a cracking aura; waste is loud and hot, mastery quiet." [NAT:257]. The coat is "clouded low down and clearing as the Stage rises" [NAT:255].
8. **The bolt.** CANON: "No Grade makes a body proof against a plain bolt by itself." [BLT:39]. A hand-spanned crossbow's bolt carries about 100 J [BLT:22]. A D-Grade fighter "Can read a crossbow bolt loosed across a room and slip it, if they see the loose. A bolt they do not see hits them." [BLT:29]. In the Midmarch "Spears do most of the work, crossbows do the killing" [HCR:80].
9. **Every fight has a way out.** CANON: "every fight still has a findable way out... A death in a fight is signaled first" [NAT:173]; before a losing POV can die, a plain sign that the fight is lost lands an exchange or more earlier and an exit is shown (R71-67).
10. **The Blow Card** [NAT:357]: (1) the set, (2) the read, (3) contact or miss, (4) tissue, (5) sound and blood, (6) the reaction, (7) the next position. "A miss takes parts 1, 2, 3 and 7." A quick trade keeps every part, compressed (R81-1). The Kill Card extends it at kills and turning points [NAT:359]. Section 5.5 applies it to a borrowed blow.
11. **Numbers** live in mouths and windows; narration stays body first (R84-1, R84-2, R81-3).
12. **Street words.** CANON: in the Midmarch the aura coat is **colors** ("he's showing his colors"; out of reserve, "his colors have run"), Pressure is **the crown** (a strong fighter walking into an inn "put it on"), sensing is **the herald** [HCR:70]. "when you meet a fighter from the Midmarch they will tell you whose, before they tell you their name" [HCR:68].
13. **After a killing.** CANON: "a killer who does not declare before witnesses by the next dawn is a murderer in every culture that keeps witnesses" (R71-101). "A king can hang you in his kingdom, and if his kingdom is small you may be able to see the safe side from the gallows." [HCR:78]
14. **Level.** CANON: Level moves by danger overcome (R84-4), and Welling's band is "Levels 26 to 50" [STG:108]. The second half's fights must be dangerous enough to carry him into that band, since "killing something stronger moves it more than a hundred weak kills" (R84-4).

## 2. Provenance in a fight

### 2.1 The canon it stands on

- **The idea.** "Remembrance. Everything keeps its past, and a soul under this lens can touch a thing and read it." [MED:23]
- **On the Body.** "A palm on a spear shaft tells who carried it and how they died, and a hand on a broken wall gives back the blow that broke it, in enough detail for the practitioner's own shoulders to repeat it. The flesh under this lens forgets nothing it has ever met, so an old fighter has a lifetime of falls and parries standing ready in the joints." [MED:35]. Isaac's concept is already canon, nearly word for word.
- **On Fate.** "a sword taken from a dead man can say which guard he always dropped." [MED:39]
- **What it keeps best.** "remembrance, which preserves charged records of lived commitment, and more than mere memory." [MED:41]
- **Its Titan.** Chronaeus: "the debris of events compacted into layers, and a soul that can read them." [MED:27]
- **Its ground.** "Favors darkness, silence, Aether-thin ground and ruins; hates saturated Aether, crowd density and active Domains." [MED:31]
- **Its failure.** "Thermal resetting. Reading is not always passive, and a careless practitioner erases the record in the act of taking it." [MED:55]. The scholars' analogue is fired ceramic that stores its radiation dose and gives it up as light when heated [MED:49]. CANON: "An overreach fails in its Wellspring's own named failure mode" [NAT:257].
- **The lens bills both ways.** "Everything a Medium lens does to the world it is also doing to you, so a Medium practitioner of thirty years is a person with a great deal of company." [MED:15]. This is Isaac's cost ("every past he reads stays in him") in canon's own words.
- **Its signature** [SIG:64]: faded violet; a dry whisper like old pages turning, a faint hum; old paper and attic dust; a magnetic tug on iron; memories surfacing in the user; afterimages of what once happened here. An Anamnesis spawn's mask is faded violet in two prints at once [SPN:60].
- **Why it is worth killing for.** "Provenance is the dearest thing to fake and the hardest to catch, because no instrument has ever measured who died holding something." [ITM:178]. A normal Appraisal window ends "Past owners: ???" [ITM:174], and "a buyer pays for the list of who died holding it as readily as for the Grade" [ITM:190]. Ned's Trait is the first instrument for the one thing the trade cannot measure, and that is why forgers, cordial-men and schools come for him (section 7).

### 2.2 The fight rules it implies (INVENTED, built on 2.1)

1. **Skin and time.** A read needs bare skin on the thing and a breath or two of stillness ("a palm", read "on contact"). That is Isaac's counter, the fighter who closes before he reads, and it matches canon: "a read occupies attention, and the scene shows what the reading cost the reader in tempo" (R12-7).
2. **Strata.** A blade's past lies in layers [MED:27]: the last hand on top and clear, older hands compacted below, dearer and muddier the deeper he goes.
3. **Charged moments read loudest** [MED:41]. Fights, wounds, oaths and deaths come first; breakfasts barely at all. A dead man's sword gives his fights, and the last one is a loss unless he died in bed. The best teachers are swords whose owners died old.
4. **Blade and grip keep different records.** The steel remembers blows; the grip plates and the wrap remember hands. A re-wrapped grip has lost its hands and kept its blows.
5. **Fire wipes.** A blade back in the forge, re-tempered or through a house fire reads blank, by the thermoluminescent analogue [MED:49]. New gear is blank. These are Isaac's "new or wiped gear", given a smith's mechanism: an enemy who learns the trick sends his steel to the fire or buys new.
6. **Ground.** His best ground is a dark shop at night. His worst is a shouting crowd and saturated Aether [MED:31], which is to say the fighting hall's yard and the inside of a dungeon. Book one goes to both.
7. **Each read hands him the dead man's fatal habit** [MED:39]: the guard he dropped, the side he always twitched to. Ned can avoid it, and he can bait the same habit in a living man of the same school.
8. **Schoolmates know the style.** CANON: "a documented technique is counterable by anyone who studied it" (R12-3), and schools carry "a signature style you can recognize at fifty paces" (R82-34). A borrowed blow is a dead Sixth Arch man's blow; his school will know it, counter it, and ask where Ned got it. Every borrowed style is also a trace, read as evidence the way R60-11's examiners read workings.
9. **The overreach.** Reading too hard, too fast or too deep erases the record [MED:55], and the blade goes blank in his hand. In a fight it costs him the read; at his counter it costs him the provenance he meant to sell, which is the merchant's horror.
10. **His tell.** A palm laid flat on steel and a stillness; once woken, a faint violet smoke at the fingers and the dry page-whisper [SIG:64]. A reading opponent sees it twice and on the third keeps his weapon out of Ned's hand (R71-23).
11. **Grammar.** A suggestion for the card: Blade grammar by default, "guards, cuts, bind, read and measure" (R1-1), with each borrowed style bringing its own (a dead hammer-man's Percussion) and R71-68 running when two grammars meet.

### 2.3 How it fights at each Stage (INVENTED, consistent with the cited canon)

- **Stage 0, sealed.** CANON: Traits "sit in the material of a soul from the beginning and run in people whose Crystal never opens at all" [STG:100], and a Dormant Shell leaves them "unaffected" [GRD:189]. So Provenance already runs in him, unspent and undirected: flashes on long handling, dreams of other men's fights, the appraiser's eye that has kept a poor peddler fed. His body takes on a dead man's habits only at the speed of practice and at Hollow force. Against other unwoken men the stats tie and skill decides [SKL:109], so a borrowed style is a real weapon from chapter one.
- **Stage I, the bought door.** CANON: waking gives a Trait "a conductor and a bill" [STG:100]; Murmuring is "A hum under the sternum that answers mood and answers nothing else" [STG:96]; a bought Stage "opens the door and no more" [CST:141]. Reads now come when he asks, cost reserve, and arrive in the shoulders as blows. His colors are smoky violet and thin, and his force is still what his life has spent.
- **Stage II, the spilling year.** CANON: "It spills. Direction arrives before volume does" [STG:114]. His reads overshoot toward the overreach; his porous Shell pulls Aether, and in a dungeon "A Well-spawn goes toward the pull" [DUN:37]. The right training is canon's: "measuring out with the body teaches the Shell to measure out" [STG:120], buckets up the hill, which for Ned means drilling a borrowed blow until it is his.

## 4. The dead man's lot: what it holds and what each blade teaches

**A lot in law.** INVENTED: goods sold together off a dead man's estate, a battlefield's plunder or a crew's share; the cutters' camps on the Hem, where "Their dead stay where they fell" [GAZ:265], sell the kit by the lot at the camp's board. CANON: "A merchant's title caps the Rarity he may sell" [ITM:64], and "A merchant above his title is a merchant selling on somebody else's." [ITM:80]. INVENTED: a Midmarch lot usually holds one piece above a poor man's token, which he may buy but not lawfully sell, and that keeps the debt blade on Ned's counter.

**Prices.** CANON: a sword runs "6 to 10 silver for a trooper's saber; 4 to 7 gold for an officer's blade", and a worked sword is "priced on the coil (the brass assay instrument) by its Grade and Rarity" [CST:78]. INVENTED rule of thumb, derived: a standing working costs its maker 10 percent of reserve [ITM:112], ten times the 1 percent of an enhanced bolt, and Apprentice-hand enhanced bolts sell at 2 to 4 silver each [CST:84]. So a Marked (E) blade sells about 1 to 2 gold above its plain price, a Proofed (D) blade 2 to 4 gold above it.

Each entry gives the object, who carried it in the Midmarch, what Ned's shoulders get (OUTSIDE, the real art), the dead man's habit (INVENTED example) and the gap for Ned's body.

### 4.1 The long messer: a messer man's tempo

- **The object.** OUTSIDE (Wikipedia, Messer): a single-edged sword on a knife's hilt, a slab tang between pinned wooden grip plates, a **nagel** standing out from the right of the cross to guard the knuckles, a bird's-beak pommel or none. Measured long messers run about 76 cm overall with a 62 cm blade, at 0.9 to 1.1 kg. The type began as a low-status household knife and grew. INVENTED: the Midmarch commoner's sword, carried by watchmen, toll-sitters, bracket fighters and cutters. Plain or Marked; Common, Uncommon with a known name; plain secondhand 3 to 5 silver.
- **What his shoulders get.** OUTSIDE (Wiktenauer, Johannes Lecküchner, *Kunst des Messerfechtens*, 1478 to 1482, and its Zucken page): a priest's messer art, laid out like the Liechtenauer longsword school. Its heart is the bind: meet the blade hard, feel the pressure, leave before the other man can use it. **The twitch** (*Zucken*): against a man who binds strongly or stands on the blade waiting, bind with the **Entrüsthau** on his left side, and in the instant the blades clash draw the arm back a little and turn the strike into a thrust at his other opening. Or let the messer **go through** (*Durchwechsel*) under his blade and strike again with the long or the short edge at his upper right; the master warns that going through works differently with the messer than with the sword. Other named plays include *Absetzen* (setting aside) and the *Geferhaw*. The working word is *indes*, the instant: a messer man acts in the moment of contact, by feel. The messer books lean on grappling at close range.
- **The habit** (INVENTED). He always twitched to the same side, and died to a man who knew it, a thrust under the left arm while his own point went high.
- **The gap.** The twitch wants a forearm that can hold a hard bind and let go in a quarter-beat. Ned's Hollow grip holds too long, and his blade is carried away, or gives too early. The nagel saves his knuckles in binds he cannot win. Against mail his cuts are turned (CANON: "Mail: stops cuts, fails to thrust and to blunt" [WND]), and the twitch into a thrust is his answer to a mailed levy man.

### 4.2 Sword and buckler: a buckler hand

- **The object.** INVENTED: an arming sword of about 80 cm of blade at 1.1 kg, and a steel buckler (OUTSIDE, Wikipedia, Buckler: up to 45 cm across, gripped in the fist behind the boss). The street weapon of the age. A watchman's, a herald's guard's, a fencing prentice's. Plain, Common. Sword 8 to 12 silver beside the canon saber [CST:78]; buckler 2 to 3 silver.
- **What his shoulders get.** OUTSIDE (Wiktenauer, *Liber de Arte Dimicatoria*, MS I.33, about the 1320s, the oldest surviving fencing book; Wiktenauer files it as the Walpurgis Fechtbuch, and the Royal Armouries title finds nothing): a priest teaching a scholar. Seven guards that even men ignorant of the art fall into, the first under the arm. Every guard is answered by a **siege**, a counter-position that closes its lines, *halbschilt* (half-shield) among them. Then the bind and the press; the **shield-strike** (*schiltslac*), the buckler driven at or pinning the bound sword hand; **stepping through** (*durchtreten*); the sword-change and the snap to the head (*nucken*); and catching the other man's sword and buckler together in one embrace, which he cannot escape without letting both go. Every action ends in **the long point** (*langort*), and delay breeds wrestling, so the priest re-binds at once. The buckler's six jobs (OUTSIDE, Wikipedia): guard the sword hand, float out in front as armor, deflect, hide the sword hand, punch, and bind.
- **The habit** (INVENTED). His buckler drifted from his sword hand when he tired; he died to a cut on the hand, then the shin.
- **The gap.** Sword and buckler move as one, and an untrained pair of hands drifts apart inside a minute. The legs are open under the buckler: OUTSIDE (research on the Visby mass graves, Ingelmark 1939), most of that battle's wounds fell below the head, many on the legs, and most head cuts came from above.

### 4.3 The levy spear: a soldier's spear

- **The object.** INVENTED: an ash shaft of about 2 to 2.4 m, a socketed leaf head, an iron butt-ferrule, muster notches burned into the shaft, dark at the two hand-places. Plain, Common, 1 to 2 silver. CANON: Midmarch levies "turn out behind the banners for forty days and go home for harvest. Harness is mixed and much repainted. Spears do most of the work" [HCR:80].
- **What his shoulders get.** Forty days of drill and the battle that ended them: the two-handed thrust from the hip, the point kept at face or belly, the shove in the press, the butt set under the foot against a charge, the quick recovery, and always a man at either shoulder. OUTSIDE (George Silver, *Paradoxes of Defence* 1599 and *Bref Instructions* about 1605, via Wiktenauer and a research call): staff, bill and pike run on one method. **The true times**: the hand moves first, then the body, then the feet, so the point arrives before the step a watcher reads; the false times lead with the foot. **The four governors**: judgment; measure; a twofold mind, going forward with one mind and ready to fly back with the other; and keeping such distance that the enemy cannot reach you without first moving his feet, which buys you the time.
- **The habit** (INVENTED). Alone, the levy man still turned to the empty shoulder where his file-mate should have stood.
- **The gap.** A spear's danger is the man inside the point, and the levy's answer was the next man in the file. Ned has no file. Against wolves the spear is the right weapon (7.1).

### 4.4 The duelist's sword: a duelist's line

- **The object.** CANON: an officer's blade at 4 to 7 gold plain [CST:78], or a crown champion's, since a Midmarch dispute "may be settled in a tourney ring by champions with the whole county looking on. Yielding is allowed. Killing is forbidden, and it still happens." [HCR:80]. Often worked and often named [ITM:190]; Uncommon to Rare, and if Rare, above Ned's token.
- **What his shoulders get.** The line: the point held at the long point (OUTSIDE, I.33's *langort*), the thrust timed into the other man's step, measure broken by half a foot, the feint and the double time; and the ring's manners, the salute, the titles shouted in full first [HCR:80], the yield.
- **The habit** (INVENTED). The ring's rules are in his arm. He pulled the killing thrust, waited for the call, and died to a man who did not stop at first blood (R71-55).
- **The gap.** Point control at the end of a straight arm wants forearm strength and fine motor control under fear, and Ned's point wanders. The duelist expected a duel. CANON's sample sheet of a rich man's son carries "Fencing, Salon E" [SKL:94]. OUTSIDE (Silver): his whole case against the long foreign blade was that its times run too long against a short weapon that closes.

### 4.5 The brawler's knife

- **The object.** A tavern knife, or a rondel. OUTSIDE (Wikipedia, Rondel dagger): carried by everyone from merchants to knights, a stiff needle blade of 30 cm or more, used with a stabbing action underarm or in a reverse grip, made for close work with grappling, able to burst mail links. Plain, Common, INVENTED 1 to 2 silver.
- **What his shoulders get.** The closer's fight: the off hand on the other man's knife arm, the low stab repeated, the head and the knee, the blade kept out of sight until it is in. Isaac's counter to Ned becomes Ned's weapon: a brawler's knife teaches him to close before the other man reads.
- **The habit** (INVENTED). He always grabbed with the left and stabbed with the right, and died with the left hand opened to the bone.
- **The gap.** Grappling runs on weight and grip, and Ned's frame decides (FLAG, section 9). Hollow against Hollow, the closer's habits win. Against a woken man, grabbing a coated arm is grabbing a post.

### 4.6 Short notes

- **The crossbow.** CANON: 5 to 10 silver for an old hand-spanned bow, common bolts about three copper apiece, "Kills men" [CST:79, CST:82]. Margrave Ulf Stavang, Stage VIII, died to one bolt "because he was looking at the soup" [GRD:124]. A read gives the dead shooter's hold-over and his loose. It is the one weapon in the lot that lets Stage 0 Ned kill anyone who is not looking.

## 5. The gap: what an untrained body can and cannot do with a borrowed blow

### 5.1 What comes across in a read (INVENTED, from [MED:35, 39, 41])

The path of the limb through the air; the edge turned true to that path; the guard and its siege; the counter the dead man trusted; the measure he trusted; his best tricks; the habit that killed him; his fear; and his death, which stays.

### 5.2 What does not come across

1. **The legs.** A passing step's length and the hip's drive are strength and mobility. A walker's legs are built for twenty miles under a pack, not a lunge.
2. **The grip.** A blade wants a loose hand in flight and a hard squeeze at contact. A weak or mistimed grip lets the blade turn on impact, so the edge lands flat. OUTSIDE: edge misalignment is the classic novice failure in test cutting.
3. **The wind.** A hard fight is a sprint, an untrained man is blown inside a minute, and fine control is the first thing the fear-surge takes. OUTSIDE: a commonplace of stress-performance study.
4. **The reflex.** Hollow sees and answers in 150 to 300 ms, a woken man on a D in 15 to 40 ms [GRD:108, GRD:111]. The read can tell him what is coming and he can still be too slow for it.
5. **The force.** CANON: the letter "puts no force behind it" [SKL:107].
6. **The dead man's body.** Measure is personal. If the dead man stood a hand taller, the borrowed lunge stops a hand short and leaves Ned's weight past his front foot. A left-hander's blows come out mirrored and wrong. A heavy man's cut leaned on mass Ned does not have.
7. **The dead man's armor.** A man who fought in mail let cuts take the shoulder, because the rings turned them. Ned in wool, copying that, bleeds.
8. **The dead man's context.** The levy man's file, the duelist's ring, the bracket's rules: the habit assumes them all.
9. **The meaning.** OUTSIDE (VS Battles on Taskmaster; Marvel; Superpower Wiki, Adoptive Muscle Memory): a mimic copies the action and not its meaning or its moment; it cannot exceed what the body could already achieve; fear can stop the move; it is not instant; and it gives only what was sensed, as a man could copy a high dive and never learn to swim. Taskmaster's "photographic reflexes" copy any movement within his own physical capabilities; his weaknesses are the physical stats he cannot copy and opponents too unpredictable to anticipate, and in his later comics each new technique displaces a memory. Ned is the inverse: nothing is displaced and everything is kept [MED:15]. The nearest anime shape is Ikki Kurogane's Blade Steal (reading the principle of a technique by watching it); Ned's source is the object's past, not watching, and the book should keep that difference sharp.

### 5.3 The canon that turns a read into his own

- R75-24: a technique a character witnessed, re-derived, "runs at his stats and becomes his own, under his own name."
- "a hundred sparring cuts shift a Swordwork line a little, and one cut that decides whether you see supper shifts it a good deal more" [SKL:56].
- "The flesh under this lens forgets nothing it has ever met" [MED:35]. INVENTED reading: what his body has actually done, it keeps; what he has only read, it must still do once to keep.

### 5.4 The ladder of a borrowed blow (INVENTED)

1. **Read.** The blow in the shoulders, perfect in shape and pitched at the dead man's measure.
2. **Bad copy.** The first repetition: the shape right, the legs, grip and measure wrong.
3. **Drilled.** Repeated until his own body fits the shape: the Welling bucket drill [STG:120] turned on a borrowed cut.
4. **Under weight.** Done once for real with his life on it [SKL:56], and the skill line jumps.
5. **Owned.** In his flesh, which forgets nothing [MED:35], under his own name (R75-24). The yard test (section 6) should run on owned blows.

### 5.5 A borrowed blow on the Blow Card [NAT:357]

1. **The set.** The dead man's set in the wrong body. The mismatch shows here: a stance a hand too wide, a heavier man's wind-up.
2. **The read.** The opponent sees a stranger's style. A schoolmate sees his dead friend's.
3. **Contact or miss.** The edge true or flat; the measure short or long by the dead man's reach.
4. **Tissue.** Ned's force and not the dead man's: the cut that would have opened the arm opens the sleeve and the skin.
5. **Sound and blood.** By what actually met, at Ned's force.
6. **The reaction.** Ned's body pays: the forearm burns, the breath goes, and the dead man's flinch fires where the dead man died.
7. **The next position.** Usually overextended or off the line, because the dead man's step was not his.

### 5.6 Small examples (INVENTED, for the drafters)

- **The flat.** The messer man's cut is perfect, Ned's grip turns at contact, the flat slaps the levy man's cheek and the man laughs.
- **The short lunge.** The duelist was taller. The point stops a hand short of the throat, Ned's weight is past his foot, and the counter comes.
- **The shoulder.** He copies the mailed spearman and lets the cut take his shoulder. It takes it.
- **The flinch.** The messer man died to a thrust under the left arm, so Ned flinches at every low line on the left. A reading opponent sees it twice and uses it the third time (R71-23).
- **Two dead men.** The messer man wants the bind and the knife man wants to close. Both habits fire at once and his body stalls for a quarter-beat. Company has a price [MED:15].

## 6. The fighting hall's yard test

### 6.1 Canon

- "Adventurers' guilds, Accord agents, free companies and the rest who are paid to be dangerous keep the old Stage table as their yardstick, because in that trade the thing they are trusting you with is the fight. For them the title sits on the Stages and measures nothing the Stage has not already measured, and an examiner confirms it with a coil (the Accord's assay instrument) and a fight in the yard." [TTP:44]
- The fighting table: "**1 · Initiate** | E | Murmuring | T1, Green" and "**2 · Apprentice** | D | Welling | T2, Green" [TTP:48 to 49]. At Welling, Ned sits the Apprentice fighting token.
- The fee: "The fee is what a hall charges to sit the examination and strike the silver token, and a hall that likes you may advance it against your term." [CST:145]; Apprentice, 6 silver [CST:150]. "Fighting guilds read the same nine off the Stage table, and only their tokens say how dangerous you are." [CST:159]
- The token: "a silver token at the belt, the numeral on one face and the hall's mark on the other, with a dab of the hall's wax set in a recess at its edge. It appears nowhere on your window." [TTP:34]
- A bought Stage: "A bought Stage carries the title on this table without argument, since the coil reads what is there." and a hall that finds a poor talent early "will sign her for seven years and feed her like a racehorse." [TTP:58]. "A bought Stage reads on a fighter's token like any other... Crews ask, in the hall, before they go down a stair with you" [TITLE:78].
- The place: an adventurers' hall shows "A green door, a board pinned three deep, a yard full of people comparing scars. Every hall has its colors and its song" [GAC:35], and keeps "a beam (the public scales) in the yard that any person may test at any hour" [GAC:104]. The merchant is tested beside the public scales.
- Bouts: "Chalk each sanctioned bout's terms before it starts (yield, ring-out, a wound by class)" (R71-54); "the Measurewright says her reading aloud as she takes it... and a suppressed Stage is exposed in public" (R71-98); a first-blood wound "is tracked like any other" (R71-55); a public fight goes on "the Reckoner's roll (who, where, by what, the outcome, read aloud as written)" and may earn "a Guild commendation struck on the token" (R71-100).
- The rule: "the merchant halls of the Guild Accord bar a sworn fighter from holding a trust title, because a man who is both is buyer and seller of the same blood; 'one has to sell to the other' is the plain-speech gloss of the rule" and, in Natalie's reading open to overturn, "it binds sworn fighting-guild members (a fighting token or a sworn company); a merchant who fights unsworn breaks no rule, and the halls may grant an exception by a hall's vote." [RUL:4169]. The sitting, the belt question and the striking are worked from the merchant side in `research/trade.md`, section 4.

### 6.2 What the test is for (INVENTED reading)

The coil already proves the Stage, and a bought Stage passes it [TTP:58]. So the yard proves the person: that the reading is not suppressed or faked (R71-98; a strong Limina user can make a window lie, R84-32); that he can hold his colors under the examiner's crown, keep measure, take a hit and stop on the terms; and that he will not get a crew killed. He cannot fail the Stage. He can fail in public, on a roll that follows his token to every hiring table, and he can win a hall's patronage, the fee advanced against a term [CST:145, TTP:58]. That patronage is its own trap: a term of fighting service is a sworn company in all but name (trade.md, section 4).

### 6.3 The procedure (INVENTED, each step on the canon above)

1. **The door.** Six silver, or the fee advanced against a term.
2. **The coil.** The Measurewright reads it and says it to the yard: "Welling."
3. **The question.** What other silver does he carry? The merchant's Journeyman token is at his belt. The drama is better if the rule is read after the yard than before.
4. **The chalk.** Terms chalked on the flags around the beam: three passes, stopping at yield, ring-out, or a wound by class.
5. **The yard-hand.** The hall's examiner, a Journeyman at Stage III, holding his crown down to the candidate's Stage. Suppression pays "in named places of his body (jaw, collar, temper)" and "leaks faintly into the room for a sharp eye" (R71-43), so the canon read is there for Ned to take. If the examiner forgets himself, Ned faces one Stage, crossable only with a read [NAT:165].
6. **The irons.** Three options.
   - **A, recommended.** The hall's yard-irons: rebated steel reforged every spring by long custom "so no candidate carries another man's luck into the yard." The fire wipes them (2.2.5), so the climax runs on owned blows (5.4), and the one thing in the yard with a past is the examiner's own old buckler or gauntlet, reachable only in a clinch (I.33's catch of sword and buckler, 4.2).
   - **B.** The candidate's own steel, sleeved at the edge. Ned brings a dead man's blade, and the yard knows whose it was (2.2.8).
   - **C.** Old house wasters handled by every frightened candidate for forty years: a crowd of records, the read Anamnesis hates [MED:31].
7. **The crowd.** Crews comparing scars [GAC:35], bookies, sect boys in their colors [HCR:68]: Provenance's worst ground [MED:31].
8. **The roll.** Entered and read aloud as written (R71-100).
9. **The token.** Struck: the numeral 2, the hall's mark, the wax [TTP:34]. Per trade.md, he may pass and refuse the silver, and refusing a token a hall has just struck is its own scene.
10. **The rule.** Read against him [RUL:4169]: one silver or the other, unless a merchant hall votes him the exception.

### 6.4 The real model (OUTSIDE)

England's Masters of Defence, licensed under Henry VIII (Wikipedia, Company of Masters): ranks from Scholar through Free Scholar and Provost to Master, each won by **playing the prize**, a public trial at several weapons before witnesses; a scholar held his rank for years first and swore to his master on the cross of a sword. George Silver and his brother placarded London, Southwark and Westminster with a public challenge to the Italian master Saviolo (Wiktenauer). Two takeaways: the yard can be a prize played at more than one weapon (short blade, staff, buckler), which shows off a library of dead men's styles; and a hall can swear its fighters on a cross-hilt (INVENTED).

### 6.5 FLAG: "sworn"

Accord law uses **sworn** for an oath on the Concord Lattice, a fragment of the Crystal given [GAC:68], and free companies "are signed, and very few are sworn" [MIL:22]. The ruling's reading counts a fighting token as sworn for this hall rule [RUL:4169]. Nothing here resolves that. It is a lever a merchant would pull in a hearing ("Lawyers fight over exact wording", R84-43), and trade.md calls it a losing argument; it is Isaac's call whether the book pulls it.

## 7. Five threats

Each entry gives the Tier, the body first, what it does to a Stage 0, I and II Ned, the way out and the fair counter, what Provenance can do, the wounds and the money. Two canon rules sit over all five: "On every floor you can kill what lives there one rank before you can safely stand there." [DUN:67], and "A party fights a spawn at its own rung; a lone fighter wants one rung above it" [SPN:92].

### 7.1 Greater Wolves (T3), the army's shadow

- **Canon.** "*Vael'kar*, Shadow Howl, in Old Sylvaar. **The Silver Fang** among frontier hunters." [WLF:11]. "**Natural Beast, Apex Predator · T3, Moderate Threat · Transference, with a minor Anamnesis resonance tied to pack lineage**" [WLF:13]. In the Midmarch, "Greater Wolves follow armies" [HCR:94]. The reeve of Ebbe's Fold posted two silver a pelt, and the pack took all nine hunters who answered, "one at a time and each on the way home" [WLF:9].
- **Biology** (CANON first). Larger and more muscular than ordinary wolves, dense fur from silver-gray to deep charcoal mottled with white or black, eyes amber or icy blue; some packs bear faint Essence markings that glow during a hunt [WLF:19]. Alpha pairs; howl patterns, body language and Essence signals [WLF:21]. Abilities: Coordinated Hunt that "flanks, traps, and exhausts prey", an Essence Howl that "disorients or weakens foes", senses amplified by Wellspring resonance, and the Pack Bond [WLF:25]. A beast's Shell is sealed, "No aura and no reserve", and what its line does leaks passively [BES:101]; "an ordinary wolf has no reserve to report" [BES:84]. INVENTED: a grown dog wolf near the weight of a grown man; Midmarch packs of six to twelve, working the columns' wake for the dead, the stragglers and the camp offal, hunting at dusk. OUTSIDE (Wikipedia, Wolf attack): predatory wolves test a victim's vulnerability first, favor the lone, small or injured, and in a determined attack bite repeatedly at the head and face.
- **Against Ned.** *Stage 0:* prey. He cannot outrun them (Hollow travel is under 12.4 m/s [GRD:68]), the howl rattles him, the pack tests and then takes the legs. *Stage I:* the same, at better odds; INVENTED, his thin coat turns a nip and not a full bite. *Stage II:* a separated wolf is a fair fight with a spear; the pack is still T3, a Journeyman crew's work.
- **Way out and counter.** CANON: "stand sound, stand together and kill the alpha pair, and a pack is some large wolves" [WLF:15]; weaknesses "Vulnerable when separated from the pack... Reluctant to engage heavily armored foes without support. Sensitive to Essence-disrupting magic and scent-blocking wards." [WLF:25]. INVENTED: a wall, a cart or a tree at his back, fire, a spear's point down the line of their run, and never turning to run.
- **Provenance** (INVENTED, from WLF:13). A pelt, a fang charm or a collar from a wolf of this pack holds the lineage's hunts, so Ned can learn the pack's flank before it comes; a Spirit Listener "can hear the flank before it comes" [WLF:15], and Ned gets the memory instead.
- **Wounds.** The raised forearm punctured and crushed, the wolf shaking; the calf and hamstring torn; the face. Fever by the third day (INVENTED, real-world sense).
- **Money.** A T3 core is 3 to 6 gold, "A soldier's half-year, and a crew's first good night" [CST:114]; hide sells separately while fresh [CST:121].

### 7.2 Greywalkers (T5), in a stilt year

- **Canon.** "**Stilts** | The Haze, along the Thresh | The cutters' name. A herd animal on legs as long as a ship's mast, a barrel body far overhead, thousands together walking the dry beds after rain that never lands. Nobody hunts them so much as gets out of their way | T4" and "**Greywalkers** | The Haze | The cutters' name. Upright, long-armed, in files at dusk, following the herds. Dust-colored until they rouse, and then wet brick red under the dust | T5" [SCO:98 to 99]. They are Scourge-born: they "breed true and wear no masks" [BES:90], and "Above a certain size a thing has a range, and it will not chase you out of it, since you are simply its until you leave or die." [SCO:89]. The season: "In the late summer of a dry year the wind off it comes hot and smelling of iron, the herds come out on that wind, and that is the week the barley goes." [HCR:104]. The herds "come up into Midmarch barley some years, and a bounty on a Midmarch door is cried for ten leagues" [HCR:94].
- **Biology** (INVENTED, consistent). An upright follower of the herds, twice a man's height, with arms that hang past the knee and end in hooked digits for taking a stilt's leg at the joint and riding the barrel body down, the way wolves hamstring. A dust-bather, so its skin wears the dry beds' color. To rouse it floods the skin with blood under the dust, the wet brick red, and that flush is its visible charge, the tell every cutter waits for. It walks in files at dusk with eyes made for half-light, takes what falls (a calf, an old stilt, a man knocked down in the barley), and keeps to its range.
- **Against Ned.** No fight at any Stage. "A T5 runs across three Stages and the coil tells you which" [CST:116]: three to five Grades over Stage II, which is "surviving an event" [GRD:64], and the unwoken take "knees bending from A" [NAT:253]. The set piece is survival: the barley week, the herd stepping through it, the greywalkers coming after in the dusk.
- **Way out.** CANON: leave its range, since it will not chase past the edge [SCO:89]; soft wet ground, where at the Moss Court's channels a stilt "goes down to the barrel" and the greywalkers "find the footing going soft and the water coming up behind them" [MKV:113], and the Midmarch has water meadows and the Aldwater [HCR:24, HCR:26]. INVENTED: stay on his feet, since it takes what falls; get under something solid (a stone barn, a church, a hedge-bank); stand with the free company's crossbows.
- **Provenance.** A dead cutter's hook or harpoon from a camp lot (cutters wear "gray wraps and lucky teeth" and "name each other by what they lost" [GAZ:265]) holds how cutters work a herd.
- **Wounds.** Crush, a limb hooked and torn at the ankle or knee, a body flung. Nothing a Stage 0 body survives without luck.
- **Money.** A T5 core is 30 to 300 gold [CST:116], and the door bounty is cried for ten leagues [HCR:94].

### 7.3 Nether Marl: the Mouth (T2) to the Lower Workings (T4 to T5), and its Minotaur (T5)

- **Canon.** "The dungeon is **Nether Marl**, the only kingdom in the world that is wholly a dungeon: a labyrinth under a hill with a Minotaur (T5) at the Lower Workings, whose title King Hamo the Undefeated bought at Pennycrown along with a stamped copper crown. The Upper Workings: Millrace, D, T3 to T4. The Lower Workings: Riptide, C, T4 to T5 on a Blue Sigil, "Veil beginning to thin", ground that asks an Expert to stay.

"Shallow masks are thin, and a plain bolt, a spade or a boot heel goes through. From the lower workings down a mask holds a working's strength and stops common bolts the way a worked cuirass does" [SPN:128], so hunters carry proofed bolts, which strike as one Tier higher. They pay "a copper a tail at the Mouth, 2 to 5 gold a mask from the Upper Workings, 10 to 25 from the Lower" [DUN:178]. "A practitioner who holds too long at a Riptide or below is retuned, and from the inside that feels like finally understanding your own gifts." [DUN:126]
- **The Minotaur** (CANON biology). Massive humanoid beasts with bull-like heads and horns, muscular torsos and thick hides, "Skin often mottled with stone-like texture; eyes glowing deep amber or earthen" [BEA:73]; Labyrinth Mastery, a Horn Charge that "shatters shields", an Earthshaker Stomp that "cracks ground", and a Resilient Hide [BEA:75]; "**Less agile in open or flat terrain.** And **their territorial instincts can be exploited for ambush**" [BEA:77]; on the Body road, "a charge cannot turn, so make it try" [BEA:69]. An Apprentice crew can break masks at the Mouth for an afternoon [SPN:86]; the Upper Workings are one gallery with a crew and a glass; the Lower Workings are a no, since a C-Grade spawn is a full Grade over his best and the ground retunes the ones who stay.
- **Way out.** The glass and the caller at the lamp-line [DUN:145]; luring a spawn up off its Well, where it loses the rung its tie gave it [SPN:82, DUN:164]; for the Minotaur, the labyrinth's turns.
- **FLAG.** Nether Marl's Wellspring is not on file. If the book makes it Anamnesis (faded violet masks [SPN:60]; walls "with afterimages of what once happened on them, a dry whisper like old pages, the smell of an attic" [DUN:104]), the delve could be his Welling Catalyst, "first genuine contact with a Wellspring's raw Aether under personal pressure... touched and survived" [STG:110]. The climb brief's call.
- **Wounds.** By the spawn's kind; the Minotaur's horn in the thigh or groin (a femoral artery fully severed is "Class III in about a minute, IV inside three" [WND]), the crush.
- **Money.** A T2 core is 12 to 18 silver, a T5 core 30 to 300 gold [CST:113, CST:116]; King Hamo's tolls both ways.

### 7.4 Chain-men and levy deserters (unwoken to Stage I; T1 to T2 on the board)

- **Canon.** "every lane has a chain across it with someone sitting beside the chain." [HCR:24]. Levies serve forty days in "Harness... mixed and much repainted", and a bolt costs "about three copper apiece" [HCR:80]. A soldier earns 12 to 15 silver a month, "Less stoppages for kit, which take a third of it" [CST:168]. Petty arts are sold at the chains: "the toll-sitter who cannot be lied to about what is in a cart until his fee has been paid" [HCR:70].
- **Body first** (INVENTED). Farmhands, herders and younger sons: big hands and backs from flail and scythe, no wind for a fight past a minute. Kit: an ash spear, a padded jack or a rust-spotted mail shirt over one, a kettle hat painted in this month's king's colors, an old hand-spanned crossbow, a cheap messer or a cudgel, and the chain itself, swung. They work in twos and threes.
- **Against Ned.** *Stage 0:* the stats tie, so skill, numbers and the bolt decide. One man dies to a borrowed messer; three with a crossbow kill Ned; most fights end in the first seconds (R60-01). Mail turns his cuts [WND], so he needs the twitch-thrust or the knife at the gaps. A hand-spanned bolt at about 100 J [BLT:22] through a wool coat is a serious wound anywhere and death in the chest. *Stage I and II:* one to two Grades over each man if he is alert and coated, so one-sided [GRD:64]; but "No Grade makes a body proof against a plain bolt by itself" [BLT:39], surprise strips the coat [NAT:257], and a D slips a bolt only if he sees the loose [BLT:29].

- **Law.** Killing a crown's chain-man is that crown's business, and the declaration by dawn still binds (R71-101).
- **Provenance.** Their spears and bows came off the last war's dead. A read tells Ned the man's habits, and which king's muster he ran from.
- **Wounds.** A spear in the belly (he "fights on, dies of sepsis on day three to five" [WND]); a bolt in the thigh; a cudgel's parry fracture; a cut scalp.

### 7.5 Cordial-men (Stage II to III; a T3 job on the board)

- **Name** (INVENTED). Canon names the Draft and not the trade. Midmarch street name: **cordial-men**. Avoid "cutters", which is canon for Scourge hunters [GAZ:265].
- **Body first** (INVENTED). Ex-cutters off the Thresh, scarred, missing fingers, named for what they lost; Stage II to III, colors gone a dirty shade. ## 8. Wounds: hands, forearms, ribs, head

**The overlay** (CANON). R60-01 for the unwoken; R70-102 keeps them there. Practitioners take wounds "plain and anime: full gore, blood in geysers, shrugged off in the moment and felt after" [NAT:353], so the way Ned's wounds are written shifts as he wakes. "a wound shows as a status line with what it stops you doing and stays until treated. Healers and healing Drafts fix it for a price, slowly at low Grade and fast at high, so a rich fighter walks off what cripples a poor one for life." (R84-29). "Healing runs on real timelines (weeks for a cut, months for bone). Vitalia healing can shorten it, but it costs the healer EU and the patient's body pays something" (R60-02). A wound carried into a new fight moves no Grade, and "the wound's structure decide[s] what the body can do" (R71-25). Prices: "In the north and the poor quarters, the barber, for a few copper and what he knows"; a physician 1 to 3 silver; a surgeon 5 to 40 gold, anesthesia included in a Guild city [CST:103 to 104]. Medicine ceiling: "Anything below Class III can be saved by a competent hand" [WND]. Vitality Grade "shifts the timeline, never the anatomy" (the wotr-wound skill).

# The Place: the Hundred Crowns of the Midmarch

*Research brief for The Warrior Merchant, Book One (Ned Chapman, spring and summer 715 IC). A foundation document for the book team, not canon.*

**How to read this.** Canon is quoted or paraphrased with a source key and line, as [HC 18]. Anything not on file is marked **INVENTED** (allowed where nothing is on file, R79-11; the book keeps or strikes it, and an archived scene makes it canon). Outside sources (section 19) are ideas and fact-checks only. Where two canon sources disagree, both are given and nothing is resolved. The sibling brief `book/warrior-merchant-1/research/trade.md` covers titles, the hall rule, credit, the gear of the dead and a peddler's economics in depth; where this brief touches the same ground it uses the same INVENTED figures.

**What the archive has made true here: nothing.** `wotr-mcp tool scene_recall "Hundred Crowns Midmarch Pennycrown Tollgarth"` returned only northern and celestial threads, and a grep of `scenes/` for every Midmarch place name found no match. No archived scene is set in the Midmarch, so the wiki is the whole of the canon on the place. `wotr-mcp tool character` found **no card** for any Midmarch person below; `roster` has no Midmarch NPC; `fronts` returned none.

## 1. The realm in one breath

Canon: "A hundred-odd kings, each sure he is the rightful one, sitting across every road between the North and the South and charging the passing wars by the head. The bill is usually paid in sons. Its people are not much troubled by it and will tell you they have the best songs in the world." [HC 18]

Working breath, from that: a loud green river plain of a hundred-odd petty crowns strung along the two war roads round the Scourge, where every lane has a chain and somebody sitting by it, war is the biggest local trade, and the songs outlive the kings.

- "There have never been a hundred. The Guild survey found eighty-three in 710 and a hundred and seventeen in 712, and in the second count the surveyor was crowned at Pennycrown on his way home." [HC 10]
- "There is no capital and nobody wants one. There are three places everyone has heard of." [HC 28] (Kingsbridge, Tollgarth, Pennycrown.)
- Isaac's start note: "petty kings along both roads, a toll every mile, war supply sold to both sides, comic names; nearly every crown owes Nalūn, so the road up leads to the salt cities in later books" [Q, Q5].

## 2. The map

- **Shape.** "the crowded country along both roads around the Scourge of Hell, thickest on the Kessen road down the west flank and thinner on the salt road up the east, river plain and low green hill, with more castles per mile than anywhere else in the world and nearly all of them small. You can stand on one keep's roof and count nine others" [HC 24]. Hedgerows, water meadows, barley, onions, apple orchards, hill pasture for hams, "a chain across every lane with somebody sitting beside it"; "The **Harl Hills** and the **Stitch Hills** break it up"; "Pigs, geese, eels, and the fat red cattle of whichever king has not yet lost them" [GZ 325].
- **North.** "the salt basin and the Banner Houses' southern marches, and Keth-Vorruk's wall on the northwest" [GZ 339]. The Kessen road comes south from the Greenhollow, "takes the Kessen ford and runs through the Midmarch, over the great bridge at Kingsbridge" [GZ 43].
- **South.** "the old roads to Sum-gol and the Ochre Fields" [GZ 339]. Musterfield is "a few days' march away, and people in Musterfield tend to know which of its kings owes them money" [OF 47].
- **West.** "The Windstair rises from the western farmland, and at Rootgate a board says GO ROUND." [GZ 339] The Aldwater "feeds the Moonlight Forest's mire through a hundred channels before the rest of it runs on west, off this map" [GZ 45].
- **The two roads.** The Kessen road "takes the Kessen ford and runs through the Midmarch, over the great bridge at Kingsbridge, down the Scourge's west flank past the Moonlight Forest, and ends at Sum-gol". The salt road "starts at the Salt Quay in Haeseong ... skirts the Rose Coils at the ford of Bothhands, runs up the Scourge's east flank across the Sealed Charter, climbs through the salt basin and finishes at the junction below Ashbury, where it meets the Kessen and the Marrow." [GZ 43] "The Kessen road runs right through it and the salt road grazes its eastern patches, so you will arrive by accident." [GZ 339]
- **The middle.** "Nobody lives in the middle and nobody goes there." [HC 26] On the Kessen side the Hem, "fifteen leagues of black-barked thornwood and pale dust", lies "east of the last hedges, and on the eastern patches along the salt road it lies to the west" [HC 104].
- **The Aldwater.** Formed below Ashbury from the Marrow, the Hallen, the Ruddle and the Sleeve [GZ 45]; "broad and brown and full of traffic, and most of the country's quarrels are about who owns which reach of the river" [HC 26]. It carries "wool, barley, Marrowhold's iron, Nalūn's salt, and wine going north" [HC 56], by longship and barge [GZ 339].
- **A river called the Kessen.** Hawes Bridge stands "on the Kessen", over "three hundred yards of the Kessen valley", and its collapse put the Justiciar "into the Kessen" [DM 16, 20, 24]. The Gazetteer maps the Kessen road and the Kessen ford, not the river; both can stand, and the river's course is unmapped.
- **Not fixed in canon:** the order of towns along the road; where the Harl and Stitch Hills lie; which road Tollgarth's pass carries; where Lastpenny sits relative to Kingsbridge; where the **Brine crossing** is (named once, as the north end of the signed stretch [HC 50]). INVENTED advice: keep distances in days and chains on the page and fix one working order in the book's bible before chapter one.

## 3. Towns and places

Each: premise, trade, ruler, toll, color. Amounts never on file are flagged.

### Kingsbridge
- **Premise.** "**A king's whole kingdom is that bridge.**" [HC 34] Nineteen arches of pale stone, "from the pier at the south wharf to the pier at the north" [HC 14].
- **Look.** "the most painted sight in the Center, and its great stormy sky appears to be a local product. The bridge carries the Kessen road long across the Aldwater on huge arches, wide enough for a marching column, and behind it the pale stone quarters stack up the height one on top of another to a vast green-roofed minster, whose copper the rain has turned the color of an old coin. Longships and barges crowd the wharves three deep, and the wharves roar with cranes, fishwives and the men who get paid to shout about both." [HC 32]
- **Ruler.** The House of Tuppence. "The present king is Gerent's grandson, also a Tuppence, who is pleasant to everyone and has the tolls in writing." [HC 34] Unnamed; name him before use. "The city on the height and the wharves on the bank belong in law to their own town council and to a bishop, and the Tuppence crown ends at the last stone of each pier, as generations of bailiffs have discovered while chasing people." [HC 34]
- **Army.** "Every morning the entire Standing Army, about two hundred men in red with red banners, marches the kingdom from end to end, with a drum, and is home for dinner." [HC 34]
- **Court.** The Scale Minster, a Sancta Lux house, whose bishop "hears crown claims from all over the Midmarch and has the busiest court in the country" [HC 34].
- **Trade.** Traffic: "the House of Tuppence collects, and it does not govern anything but traffic" [HC 34]. Fruit-sellers on the bridge cry the Vantry pear every autumn [MV 15]; a mutton-pie barrow worked "the great bridge" in 661 [BC 17]; the Kingsbridge brackets drew nine thousand to a semifinal [BC 27]; Kingsbridge cooks fight over Low Marl quince [MS 20]; "the betting in Kingsbridge is on who will be standing to hand it over", the Turn [HC 108].
- **Toll.** A toll-board at each end (662) [HC 14]; no rate on file. INVENTED (same figure as trade.md): 2 copper a pack.
- **Color.** Red (the Standing Army); red and white (the Sixth Arch Boys) [HC 68]; old-coin green on the minster.
- **History.** Feast of Veyra, 662: Gerent Tuppence the Third sold "the Marrick marshal the south nine arches and the northern marshal the north nine, with a sealed bill for each and the eleventh reserved to the Crown"; Sella Drax, "coated in a yellow you could read by, held the ninth arch alone for the time it took a bell to ring twice"; the bailiffs billed the dead "as refuse in the navigable channel"; two thousand died; the crown prince died on the eleventh "with his crown-prince's circlet driven through his eyebrow"; "he rebuilt the eleventh arch with it, and his son's name is on every stone" [HC 14-16]. 633: Master Tobias Wyke died after drinking a claret he took for a Fate Draft, "A cellar in Kingsbridge" [MW 137]. Winter 688: Marshal Quillon Aske's victory dinner, where his surgeon reopened his old wounds over an unpaid fee [HS 18-20]. 709: Lord Aubrey Tancred's bought Stage VIII beaten by Jennet Fallow, who "had come to Kingsbridge to complain about an invoice" [VC 61]. The Aldwater League marched to "a filthy hymn about a bishop of Kingsbridge" [MS 20].

### Tollgarth
- **Premise.** "a castle-town shut across the one good pass in the Harl Hills, held for three generations by Tinker Goblin kings" [HC 38].
- **Ruler.** "Queen Tizz Copperwick the Fourth, three foot five in her boots, with one arm of her own and one of her own design" [HC 38]. In 709 she came down "in her second-best crown ... with forty men", claimed a boar as toll, lost at her own market court, was amerced, and "paid it in front of the crowd with a bow, because every debt she owned was written on instruments that court could void, and because she knows a good show when she has lost one" [GA 25]. Reigns run short: "Most Tinker Goblins do not live past forty, the Essence running too hot for the frame, so a Tollgarth reign is short, and the coronation feast is usually paid for by the same bill as the last funeral." [HC 38]
- **Toll.** "Every gate is a contraption. The Self-Minding Portcullis has killed four kings and one horse, the toll is collected by a machine that makes change only when it feels like it, and the whole town smells of burned vitriol and soldering copper. Merchants pay gladly, because the alternative road adds a week, and because watching a wagon go through is free." [HC 38] Its market court sits "in the same building as the beam" [GA 25]. No rate on file.
- **People.** "goblins, humans and a large contingent of people with burn scars who came for the wages" [HC 38]. Tinker Goblins: 3'2" to 4'0", "one limb modified or reinforced through self-applied Spliced Runes", multifaceted eyes in amber, electric blue or quicksilver, smelling "of burnt vitriol, soldering copper, and scorched paper"; their work "carries their mark and their name, shouted" [GP 151]. The look: "Burn scars, too many pockets, one improved limb" [GP 243].
- **Color.** The Harl Hill Scorchers, black and copper [HC 68]; the Comet of Tollgarth's plate "enameled sunset orange" [MW 18].
- **History.** 601 to 604, the War of the Stoppered Inch with House Morrow over a stolen vial: "Nine thousand dead, two crowns gone ... The vial, when it was finally recovered, proved to be cough syrup" [MW 138]. 672: Ser Lucan Vaye, the Comet of Tollgarth, Stage VIII, lost his arm against a pot lid at Pennycrown and "lived, one-armed, to be a very thoughtful old man" [MW 18, 24]. Summer 708: Isemay Ferrant married "the heir of the toll-king at Tollgarth" [DG 13]; "a bookie on the Tollgarth road will still give you four to one against it" [DG 17]. The Harrowgate Sisters, grave-singers "in black and rose on the Tollgarth road", walked into Tollgarth market one spring with one mouth and five voices (undated) [CC 10].

### Pennycrown
- **Premise.** "Pennycrown is where you go to become a king. It is a town of goldsmiths, heralds and notaries, on the old fair-ground where, as the story goes, the heir to a lost crown bought a replacement for a penny and could not be shown to have done anything wrong." [HC 42]
- **Trade.** "Shops on Crown Row sell them at every price, from solid gold to a stamped copper ring for one penny, which by long custom is what a landless younger son wears until he has done something about it." [HC 42] Crowns for sword-pullers: "it does not give refunds" [HC 110]. Coronation law: "a crown is lawful if three neighboring crowns attend the coronation, or two and a Measurewright" [HC 42]. It keeps an assay roll (672) [MW 16]. "A crew found a Mouth ... under a tannery in Pennycrown, and the tanner discovered that he owned a dungeon." [HC 98]
- **Ruler.** Unnamed in 715. An undated king of Pennycrown brought "eleven hundred spears in yellow and blue, four carts of bunting and a king who owed her father nineteen crossings" to Threeways, and "paid all nineteen crossings that afternoon" [CL 16]. In 651 King Hobart's realm was "four villages, a ford and a very high opinion of itself" [MH 13].
- **Landmarks.** Crown Row; Bakers' Row and the Bakers' Gate [MW 18]; the Last Lid, a Master Work pot lid "stamped Marked", still on "a family stew in Bakers' Row ... and the family will let you look at it for a copper" [MW 22, 24]; the leaning throne of Marrowkin ivory, which "went north, through the dais, through the kitchen wall, through the queen's pigeon-house, and across four hundred paces of bean field" on midsummer night 662; "Pennycrown built a new stretch of wall around the thing rather than touch it. It is still leaning." [MH 15; GZ 334]
- **Toll.** Not on file.
- **Color.** Yellow and blue (its levies) [CL 16]; the gold and stamped copper of Crown Row.
- **History.** The siege of Pennycrown, 672, "in its ninth week" when the Comet came through the Bakers' Gate [MW 18]. 691: Ikka Sennet crowned a block of polar ice "for a penny" with "two kings and a bribed Measurewright" attending, and "King Block the First crossed the rest of the country as a reigning monarch, untaxed, with a hired trumpeter walking ahead of him" [GZ 13]. The class table records a later form of Turncoat called "The Last Loyal Man of Pennycrown (one person, miserable)" [CL 153].

### Lastpenny
- **Premise.** "the edge town the whole country claims" [HC 106], on the Kessen road at the Scourge's rim.
- **The story everyone knows.** "At a quarter hour before noon on Midsummer Day of 676 ... the biggest earth wyrm anyone living had seen came up through the cheese stalls"; eleven thousand northern and southern spears waited for the Turn to pass at noon by the Accord's water clock; it ate "the Corn Hall, the reeve, and the better half of Butter Row"; "a goblin king the size of a child beat on the marshal's stirrup with a ladle"; "The Center says it was a cheese-wife called Gytha Rowe, who had lost her stall and both her sons", who tipped the clock; "four hundred dead and nine hundred spears left standing in its hide" [SH 19-23]. "The wyrm still comes up every eleven years or so along that stretch of the road, still wearing the spears. Everyone calls it Old Pincushion" [SH 23].
- **Now.** "Rebuilt in stone, and they have kept the awning. The Noon Fair every Midsummer sells a hard yellow cheese with a bite out of it, and at noon the whole town stands in the square and turns its back on any soldiers present." [SH 178]
- **Edge-town texture** (canon for all four edge towns): houses "on stone footings with no cellars"; "plank walks from roof to roof"; "Every wall is painted vermilion, yellow and blue, because it has to be seen through the haze"; "ground-bells on iron stakes ... so that a shudder under the lid sets the whole street ringing"; crews' tallies over tavern doors; "the board at the hall goes three bills deep"; renderers, core-buyers, "the Scalebond surgeon who knows how the last man with your wound died", bookies on barrels with depth odds; "Hedge-workers salt your doorstep for a copper and your bed for two" [SH 183]. "Black-root tea is the local drink, bitter as a lawsuit"; a careful man pays for his own wake in a marked jar and drinks it if he comes back; "The Widows' Bench outside the hall is an actual bench" and its women hold "more shares in more crews than any factor" [SH 185]. Dusk lamp-wardens; the sword seen "On one of the nine clear days" [SH 187]. Lid-listeners, "usually old women with one small gift", for "a few coppers a night" [SH 75]. "Half the taverns in the rim towns are called the Ordinary Sword and the other half the Other One, after the picture on the sign, a lone figure on a ridge with a plain blade, looking up." [HC 110]
- **Ruler, toll.** Claimed by the whole country; neither a king nor a toll is on file.
- **Nearby.** "The Turnstone, a black pillar on the western rim off the same road" [HC 106].
- INVENTED (arithmetic only): eleven-year returns from 676 put Old Pincushion's last rising near 709 and the next near 720; "or so" leaves the book free.

### Nether Marl
"the only kingdom in the world that is wholly a dungeon: a labyrinth under a hill with a Minotaur (T5) at the Lower Workings, whose title King Hamo the Undefeated bought at Pennycrown along with a stamped copper crown. He has never gone below the Mouth. He sells licenses at the door, charges a toll going in and a much larger one coming out, and has legally been at war with whatever is down there for twenty-six years." [HC 96] Wellspring "Not entered" [DG 238]. INVENTED (arithmetic): twenty-six years in 715 puts the purchase near 689. Toll figures and colors not on file.

### The Stitch Hills
"a kingdom-in-many-pieces founded by people the Accord has no coherent policy on and therefore no way of evicting. King Ambrose Fivefold, who rules there now, has the Binding Scars that hold his body together gilded for court, and three neighbors have tried to get his coronation annulled on the grounds that he was assembled. He has so far won every case and every field." [HC 48] Binding Scars are mandatory, and their damage "risks catastrophic Crystal and physical dissolution" [BK 118].

### Upper Marl, Low Marl, Marl Bridge
- **Upper Marl.** "Nine miles wide, the Gambrel kingdom, bareheaded since 704." [GZ 336] King Odo Gambrel, "the eleventh of that name", marched nine hundred men in their best red up the Windstair in high summer 704; a Highroost Shrike took the crown off his head during the Long Step; it is now the trophy called the King's Hat, with "a dent in it from the Finals of 711", and the schools invited the family "to come and win it" [VP 18-20].
- **Low Marl.** King Aldric Sorrel took "nineteen hundred spears in orange and black" onto the Moonlight Forest causeway (694); only Tam Ruddock's two hundred came out; "The rest of Low Marl stayed, and it gives a small hard quince that a Kingsbridge cook will fight you for." [MS 20] Its ruler in 715 is not on file.
- **Marl Bridge.** Quillon Aske's three ribs, "the spring of 664" [HS 20].

### Hollowhay and the Orchard Under Goosegrass
"one of the smallest realms in the Hundred Crowns, two miles of hedge and a cider press around one green lump called Goosegrass", with nine galleries of apple trees "in leaf and fruit together in every month of the year" [DG 13]. Summer 708: King Bertran Ferrant hired "every herald-painter on the Kessen road" for his daughter Isemay's wedding and had not paid his keepers in three seasons; the Ambreys stopped bleeding the pool; Reaching Briars came up through the floor; thirty-one died; Bertran threw his crown up the stair as back pay [DG 13-17]. "The Ambreys hold the crown, which makes Hollowhay the only monarchy in the Midmarch founded on back pay, and they let the great gallery for weddings at double the old rate, because couples now ask for the bench with the stain." [DG 17] Booked "until 719" [HC 98]. Verdantia's signature: chlorophyll green, "the creak of growing wood", cut grass and sap [WS 112].

### Odds Ferry
"where the Kessen road takes to the water, is a landing on Somnalis ground (the Dream Veil, a Wellspring of Fate, teal with ghost outlines). Every plan on the road crosses every other plan at that landing, and no bookie in the Midmarch will open a book within sight of it, though several have tried and are remembered." [GZ 327] It "Runs on the hour" [DG 262]. Somnalis: "many voices heard through water", "rain before it falls", "a tingle like déjà vu" [WS 39]. Open: Kingsbridge already carries the road over the Aldwater by bridge [HC 32], so Odds Ferry is a second water crossing or a stretch by boat; canon does not say which.

### Hawes Bridge
"Justiciar Ambrose Tick, Stage VIII, heard land cases at Hawes Bridge on the Kessen for thirty-eight years, a long thin man in a plum gown with a permanent cold in his head." In autumn 662 his Domain stopped eight hundred men's swords for nine hours (the Long Sitting) against armies in yellow and green; its collapse "took out the middle span"; "The bridge at Hawes is a very bad place to start a fight to this day." [DM 16-24]

### Little Tolland, Sedgeford, Cold Harrow, Wick Bottom, Tarn
- **Little Tolland.** Dickon Sedley "sold mutton pies off a barrow on the great bridge at Kingsbridge"; blessed "Unbeaten" at the spring fair of 661, he won the Kingsbridge brackets with a rolling pin, "and by autumn he was King of Little Tolland, which had been looking for one"; the blessing was lifted in 692 and he died that week [BC 17-31]. Ruler in 715 not on file.
- **Sedgeford.** Count Merrow Tallis of Sedgeford "armed two thousand archers for the Harl Ford" in spring 702 and refused the four-silver Windstair almanac [HCa 18]; Quillon Aske took "the ax at Sedgeford" [HS 18].
- **Cold Harrow.** "at Cold Harrow in the Midmarch" the Duke of Tarn's eleven hundred unpaid gray remounts wheeled away from his charge (summer 677) [YH 21]. **On file both ways:** Blessings and Curses puts "Cold Harrow in the Long Fields" (Year 388) [BC 186], and Oathrend names a Truce of Cold Harrow [OR 9]. Do not place it without noting this.
- **Wick Bottom.** Summer 691: the Red Bell of Tarn, Stage X, "a tall woman in lacquered plate who had taken two castles alone", broke sixteen collarbones in one file of the Marrick Sixth Blacks, who held "until sundown, singing" [HMk 21-25].
- **Tarn** (a duke's seat and the Red Bell's name) is Midmarch by implication and unmapped.

### Neighbors a few days out
- **The Ashbury junction.** "where the Kessen, the Marrow and the salt road meet, and nearly everything on this continent passes it sooner or later" [GZ 146]; "the black junction stone below it, which holds one oath at a time" [GZ 142]. In 715 the Hollow's lord is Edric Ashmore, forty, and "Ashmore takes Tenrai silver because Ashmore has no choice" [GH 50]; every host "buys at the junction below Ashbury" at the Turn's fixed price [GH 94]. From 633, when Lady Aldith Ashmore turned the Host of the Nine Crowns into customers on tally-sticks: "There are households in the Midmarch paying it off today." [HA 20-26]
- **Halcrest** (Nalūn's gate): "customs, weigh-house, lazar-house, and the hiring ground of half the free companies in the north" [GZ 289]; "Everybody here will sell you a copy of a copy of the good map." [SH 180]
- **Waxgate** (the Charter, not the Midmarch): "a loud yellow river town of hives, rendering sheds and pigment yards that smell of honey and scorching"; "Waxgate's gate-toll is a dab of hot wax on the back of your hand, in the color of your business" [SC 48]. Home of the Mercers' hall [GA 36], whose Grandmaster Dame Ottilie Marsh once held "the crown jewels of both kingdoms" of two warring Midmarch kings "in scarlet silk bags, in the middle of the battlefield" [ST 40]; of Wording Row [BC 15]; and of the White Aprons' hall [TP 12].
- **The Sealed Charter itself**, at the salt road's crossing: "a bright white land of wax, weights and windows, where the law is faster than anywhere in the world and everybody who can pay for it adores it" [SC 20]. There "Titles I and V between them are ninety percent of what any court in the Charter ever does", and "The dusty foot gets a traveler heard before the market closes, which is the reason merchants will walk three days out of their way to be sued here" [SC 58]. Beastkin law lives there: a petitioner without free-holder papers is written "*nemo*, nobody" [SC 18], and Verath's pens sell labor stock [SC 52]. Armies cross "on a Writ, by the named road, with their bread paid for at the assize" [SC 100].
- **Harrowgate** (the Charter): "A great fair town, with cherry and cream banners on the horse lines, a Long Market, a Fair Court and the pens where the gate came off its pins" [GZ 315]. Its Fair Court awarded Dame Emmot Vantry her dead husband's pears [MV 17].
- **Rootgate**: "the board, the pears, innkeepers and nervous factors, the one town where outsiders sleep uninvited" [GZ 355]; the orcs sell burial plots "at a discount for paying in advance" [SH 181].
- **The Windstair**: "Eight days up the Windstair from the Midmarch farmland, a road one man wide at its worst, or baskets on the hoisters' ropes, when they are not on strike ... the Caelwards take a toll. Below are the Hundred Crowns, whose kings send demands." [GZ 402]
- **Hammerford and Musterfield**: Hammerford's "Hiring Hall with this season's prices on its wall" [GZ 444]; Musterfield "raw, loud and cheerful, with very few old men in it" [GZ 446].
- **Keth-Vorruk**: the Mandate's southern wall, which stops "younger sons with a charter and a cart coming up out of the Hundred Crowns of the Midmarch, each sure the forest is empty" [IM 124].
- **Salt-road side**: Goodmorrow, "the best mask-clerks on the east side ... and the worst beer" [SC 124]; Verath's pens, "seventeen gold marks a head, less than a horse" [SC 52]; Bothhands, "the only street where both sides of a war share a cookshop" [GZ 379].
- **Stoneglass Hollow and the Lower Reading** (the Rose Coils): the nearest Anamnesis Well on file, "faded violet, smelling of old paper", which "breeds Roll-callers (T6), Well-spawn of the Deeps who speak in the voices of people the Well has killed"; "Scholars go down on a rope to hear a dead witness and pay the posted price per question" [GZ 372]. Anamnesis is Ned's lens [Q, Q6].

## 4. Who rules, and how law works

- **The crowns.** "A Midmarch crown may hold a county, a valley, a mill, a stretch of towpath or, in one case, a well. Human kings are the most common and the loudest, usually descended from a northern sergeant or a southern marshal's bastard who stayed." Tinker Goblin kings "hold the workshops, the Harl passes and a surprising number of bridges, and their people have a saying that their reigns are shorter and their monarchs more thoroughly felt." "A few Beastkin hold crowns too ... and the Charter's clerks enter them as *nemo, rex*." [HC 48]
- **Fealty.** "Kings owe one another fealty in chains that cross themselves. A man may be overlord of his overlord in a different county. Sancta Lux's Ember Magistrates ride circuit hearing who is whose." [HC 50] A Magistrate sits with "a gold lamp and an azure one burning on a trestle in broad daylight" [SL 12].
- **The Accord, in patches.** "the stretches from the Brine crossing down to the marches of Sum-gol signed the first circle at Kharven-Seat, along with several kings who have since been deposed by others who say they never agreed. Past that line the Articles are a custom a merchant may take or leave, though the market court and the dusty foot are so handy that most towns keep them anyway." [HC 50]
- **Justice.** "Every crown has its own law, and a farmer who crosses a ditch may cross a jurisdiction. Justice is mostly by toll, fine and the king's bad mood, and a traveler carries his plea to the nearest market court if he has any sense. A king can hang you in his kingdom, and if his kingdom is small you may be able to see the safe side from the gallows." [HC 78]
- **Rent.** "Rent goes to whoever's man turned up, and in a contested valley he may turn up twice. A Midmarch peasant keeps a chest of old tax receipts from dead kings against the day one of them is found to have been lawful." [HC 66]
- **Debt.** "Nearly every crown owes the factor houses of Nalūn, and a third of the country's wars come from fighting over who inherits the debt." [HC 60] They owe Nalūn "more than any other people in the Center, jointly and severally" [AN 218]; "a Nalūn scribe with a march contract is the most frightening thing a Midmarch army ever meets" [HC 120].
- **War.** "a sport with casualties. Levies turn out behind the banners for forty days and go home for harvest. Harness is mixed and much repainted. Spears do most of the work, crossbows do the killing, and a well-placed bolt has ended more Midmarch reigns than any Domain ... at about three copper apiece. Goblin kings bring contraptions that misfire in funny and terrible ways, and everyone stands well back from their own side. Kings who are also practitioners fight each other in person, with their titles shouted out in full first, and a small dispute may be settled in a tourney ring by champions with the whole county looking on. Yielding is allowed. Killing is forbidden, and it still happens." [HC 80] "When a real army comes through, the kings fall over themselves to join it. Then they sell its road to the other side." [HC 82]
- **The Articles a peddler leans on** (where they bind): XXVI the dusty foot, "A traveler's plea is heard and determined **before the market closes.**"; XXVII the road, "The keeper of a stretch keeps it passable, and may take a toll only where he does." (penalty "Toll forfeit; the stretch reassigned"); XXVIII the pledge, which may not take "tools of a man's craft, and the last beast"; XXX deal only up to your title without a second signature; XXXI "a merchant's caps the Rarity of what he may sell"; XXXIII the false token [AR 96-112]. The market court "sits in the market, in the same building as the beam and the guardhouse" [AR 118]; amercement is "set by the court **to the offender's means**" [AR 124]; a signed member "travels on a road-writ at a fixed toll" [GA 56]. (Full treatment in trade.md, section 5.)
- **The hall rule** (Isaac, 2026-11-07): "the merchant halls of the Guild Accord bar a sworn fighter from holding a trust title, because a man who is both is buyer and seller of the same blood; 'one has to sell to the other' is the plain-speech gloss of the rule"; Natalie's overturnable reading is that it binds "sworn fighting-guild members (a fighting token or a sworn company); a merchant who fights unsworn breaks no rule, and the halls may grant an exception by a hall's vote" [RUL 4169].
- **The Charter next door.** It "Sells them law at a markup and keeps the worst of the armies off its own roads by leaving them on Midmarch ones. Every king in the country resents this, and most of them send their sons to Sealhall to study it." [HC 118] "Most marshals save the fee and the argument by taking the Kessen road on the far flank, across the Midmarch" [SC 100].

## 6. Customs

- **Chains and tolls.** "every lane has a chain across it with someone sitting beside the chain" [HC 24]. For Ikka Sennet's sled, "In the Midmarch the tolls came every mile" [GZ 13]. The chain-sitter's petty art: "the toll-sitter who cannot be lied to about what is in a cart until his fee has been paid" [HC 70]. A toll cry on file from the north, by a Hallenfeld clerk against a Midmarch king's company: "**NINE HUNDRED HORSE AT THREE PENCE THE HOOF! PAY OR BE CHAINED!**" [HH 24]. Tolls are "Tolls first and always", the first source of wealth [HC 56]. INVENTED rates (shared with trade.md section 5): 1 copper a pack on an unsigned chain, 2 to 4 for a handcart, 2 for a mule-load; 10 to 20 copper in a day of chains; on a signed stretch a token's road-writ fixes 1 copper a day; Kingsbridge 2 copper a pack.
- **A song for a toll.** Not on file. INVENTED (offered, keep or strike): at a lane chain a traveler may offer a verse naming the chain's king in place of a copper, at the sitter's choice; kings like it because a song travels farther than a copper, and canon says "A minstrel can be paid, and a lord who has not paid one should expect to be a villain by harvest" [NW 96]. A bad verse is an insult the sitter may answer with the chain; a good one is sung in the next town. The Tollgarth machine does not take songs.
- **The Coat-Turning.** "held here every tenth summer. It is a three-week fair at which the kings of the Midmarch, in full heraldry, announce whose side they are on for the next decade, and bookies from four quarters take odds on the order. The sides are mostly North and South. It settles nothing, and everyone comes." [HC 42] **No year is on file.** INVENTED options: (a) 715 is a Coat-Turning summer, three weeks at Pennycrown, a natural set piece for a peddler; (b) the last was 710 and the next is 720, which sits easily beside the survey's jump from 83 crowns in 710 to 117 in 712 [HC 10]. The bible should choose.
- **Crowns for a penny.** The copper ring for a landless younger son; lawful coronation by three neighboring crowns, or two and a Measurewright [HC 42]; Hamo's "stamped copper crown" [HC 96]; Odo Gambrel's "crown of good middling price" [VP 18]; a surveyor crowned on his way home [HC 10]. "A man who has been promised something is said to have a Pennycrown deed." [HC 88]
- **Market courts.** Kept by most towns even past the signed line [HC 50]; Tollgarth's sits with the beam [GA 25]. Harrowgate's Fair Court hears Midmarch suits [MV 17].
- **Dungeon custom.** "By the custom of the Center a clearing venture takes a third of the hours at the pool for nine years, the **clearers' third**, and by the law of the dead leg the fine on a lapsed gallery lands on its owner, 'whoever that now is,' which is why several kings of this country have turned out to own a catastrophe by marriage." [HC 98] Nether Marl sells licenses at the door [HC 96].
- **Greeting and toasts.** "Greet someone with 'Who's king today?' The answer is a joke unless it is not." [HC 72] "'Long live whoever' is the loyal toast in about half the county, and in the other half they mean it." [HC 88] At a feast, "the toast went up at sundown the way a Midmarch toast does, with every guest who owned a woken Crystal lighting their coat at once for the pride of it, rose and amber and kingfisher blue and one enormous drunken crimson" [DG 15].
- **Midsummer.** Lastpenny's Noon Fair and the turned backs [SH 178]; "The Sixth Arch Boys of Kingsbridge, in red and white, and the Harl Hill Scorchers of Tollgarth, in black and copper, have fought each other at every midsummer for ninety years over a bet about a goose" [HC 68]; the Kingsbridge brackets ran "By midsummer" [BC 27].
- **Sect first.** "when you meet a fighter from the Midmarch they will tell you whose, before they tell you their name" [HC 68]. "Every town has a bracket ground, a sect in a loud shirt and a fierce opinion." [HC 68]
- **Heraldry.** "Herald-painters are a respected class and are paid before the masons." [HC 24] "a good pedigree is written to order"; minstrels "sell their songs up and down both roads, get the details wrong, and have done more for some kings than their armies have" [HC 58]. The Rose Coils "remember every pedigree anyone has ever forged" and sell the answer [HC 122]; the Midmarch "consult the Record on matters of succession and then ignore the answer" [RC 127].
- **The Turn.** "Every king sends nine men and a very loud banner, free companies on Thresh contracts (monster bounties, paid by anyone with a monster problem) do most of the dying" [HC 108].
- **Bounties.** "a bounty posted on a Midmarch door is cried for ten leagues" [HC 56]; when the herds come "A petty king posts a bounty on his doors, hires a free company, and now and then loses a castle" [HC 104].
- **Sword-pullers.** "Every few years a young Midmarch king with a good jaw sets out with a following, bookies alongside and a minstrel on retainer, to walk in and draw it." [HC 110]
- **Small habits.** "Nobody in the Midmarch will take a pear from a stranger, and 'going round' is street talk in six towns for a sensible surrender." [MV 101] "the rosewood case is still the most popular birthday gift in the Hundred Crowns" (a bought Stage) [VC 61]. Relics: "No tooth has ever been sold, and two hundred are on offer in the Midmarch at any time" [MH 145]; "Roughly one in five of its kings owns something that leans." [MH 263] Monster pens: "The Hundred Crowns have kings who do this. Most of what gets fed to the pit is goats." [MC 205] Riderless grays come home to Qingyuan, and "A good many families in the Midmarch have heard their news that way." [JP 99] In a bad herd season "the Midmarch empties northward on its carts" [IM 289].
- **Faith.** "otherwise faith is a saint per bridge" [GZ 329]; Sancta Lux hears legitimacy, "and the Midmarch produces it by the barrowload" [HC 34]. Auren's day, the twelfth of every month, is for the dead everywhere [SY 72].

## 7. Money and prices as paid there

- **Coin.** "Coin is anybody's, and the money-changer at every crossroads will tell you what a Tollgarth mark or a Pennycrown penny is worth this week, which is how much metal it has in it, and he has a small hammer for finding out." [HC 66] The Accord rate: "One gold mark is twelve silver marks. One silver mark is twenty-four copper." [WC 11]; "A Nalūn salt bar passes for one silver mark" [WC 13], "and it can be eaten" [VC 149]. The listed prices are for a kept Accord city; "Past the last round ... most of this list is paid in kind ... Where coin changes hands out there, it is copper" [WC 15]. Barter is the default and tallies are admissible "in six kingdoms" [VC 289, 295].
- **Pennies.** The Accord inventory lists the copper penny as "Gone", melted "wherever array-building ran hot" [INV 15]; the Midmarch still trades in pennies [HC 42, 66]. No great array is on file in the Midmarch, so both stand. INVENTED working reading: a Pennycrown penny passes for about a copper by weight on the hammer, a Tollgarth mark is a silver of uncertain fineness, and the money-changer's cut is the price of knowing.
- **Things a peddler and his customers pay** (WC baseline, Accord city; the Midmarch goes by ear [NAT 211]): a two-pound loaf 3 copper; a cookshop meal 4 to 6 copper, an inn's table 1 silver; a pint 2 copper; a bed 3 to 4 copper in a lodging-house, 1 to 2 silver an inn room [WC 23-26]; boots 4 to 6 silver new, 1 to 2 worn; a working coat 5 to 8 silver [WC 36-37]; a letter 2 to 4 copper across a quarter; a place on a carrier's wagon 1 copper a mile on the load; a barge 1 copper the two miles, a river stair 1 copper; a hired hack 8 to 12 copper a day; a news bill 1 copper [WC 55-61]; a horse 15 to 25 gold, a mule 10 to 20, an ox 6 to 10, a cart 8 to 12 [WC 68-71]; a trooper's saber 6 to 10 silver, an officer's blade 4 to 7 gold [WC 78]; a crossbow 5 to 10 silver; bolts "about three copper apiece. Kills men" [WC 79, 82; HC 80]; a physician 1 to 3 silver, a surgeon 5 to 40 gold [WC 103-104]; a laborer 12 to 24 copper a day, a soldier 12 to 15 silver a month and keep [WC 167-168].
- **The book's big numbers.** A Waking Draft "60 to 120 gold, and it often kills or warps the child" [WC 129], "On the street, three to ten times this" [WC 125], and "Commoners without the coin for a Waking Draft sell themselves into guild debt for it, and the term is generally the larger bill" [WC 141]. INVENTED arithmetic: at about 18 copper a working day, a laborer earns some 19 to 20 gold a year, so a Waking Draft is three to six years of a laborer's life.
- **Bounties and cores.** A T3 Greater Wolf posted "at forty silver marks" (702) [BS 24]; Grandfather Rent (T7) reached "twelve hundred gold" [MC 17]; cores run T1 3 to 5 silver, T3 3 to 6 gold, T5 30 to 300 gold [WC 112-116].
- **Healing is priced.** "The rich walk off what cripples the poor" [GA 39].
- **The Midmarch eats better.** "They eat better than the Charter does, since food is the thing armies pay for" [HC 68]. Canon gives no Midmarch price table; trade.md section 8 carries the peddler's sheet.

## 12. Weather and season, spring and summer 715

- **Canon climate.** "Big stormy skies over a wide river plain, and Kingsbridge's weather appears to be a local product. Hot wet summers full of thunderheads, gray-gold autumns for marching, cold muddy winters that send the levies home, floods on the Aldwater most springs. The dry wind from the Scourge makes a hard week or two in late summer, when the barley goes up in somebody's war." [GZ 323] The interior "swings hard: hot, bright summers with towering thunderheads, cold winters with snow in the north of it and iron frost everywhere" [GZ 38]; "Summers here are hot and loud with thunder, winters cold and clear, and the dry wind from the middle reaches everyone" [GZ 253]. "In the late summer of a dry year the wind off it comes hot and smelling of iron, the herds come out on that wind, and that is the week the barley goes." [HC 104] On the Scourge's south side in August "the farmers north of Musterfield look at the sky and cut their barley green" [OF 85].
- **Calendar.** The real calendar, January to December, Monday to Sunday, real month lengths, "the table keeps its own date"; year counts are the world's own (715 IC) [NAT 263; SY 70, 113]. Auren's day is the twelfth of every month [SY 72]. The moon runs twenty-eight days and "keeps no step with the months", and "every almanac in the market sells its dates by the year" [SY 26]. Munahi falls "Every fourth year" [SY 82]; which years is not stated. INVENTED: if the leap rule follows the count, 715 has no February 29.
- **Month by month** (INVENTED, derived from the canon above; the bible may adjust):
  - *March.* Mud and cold rain; levies at home; lanes axle-deep; the first market days of the year.
  - *April.* The Aldwater floods, carrying the North's thaw (Hallenfeld's thaw "floods everything and is the great event of the year" [GZ 189]); ferries and barges busy, low roads cut, eels everywhere.
  - *May.* Hedgerows and orchards in flower, barley up, the first thunder; armies can move again.
  - *June.* Hot and wet under thunderheads. Midsummer: Lastpenny's Noon Fair, the Sixth Arch Boys against the Harl Hill Scorchers, the Kingsbridge brackets, and the Coat-Turning if 715 is a Coat-Turning summer. INVENTED: take Midsummer Day as June 24, the old English quarter day.

## 16. News in the Midmarch

- **Three channels.** The beacon line ("Near-instant on a clear night, expensive, brief, read by the Guild, shut by fog"), the bag, and the mouth ("Free or nearly free, universal, faster than the bag over short distances, and wrong") [NW 26-30]. "that gap is the age's characteristic injustice" [NW 32].
- **Peddlers are part of the mouth.** "Heralds, criers, minstrels, bills on doors, bounty boards, bookies, carters, peddlers, pilgrims, discharged soldiers, and the tap-room." [NW 92]
- **Minstrels** get it wrong predictably: "the name changes to make the rhyme, the numbers go up by a factor of ten, the loser becomes uglier, and somebody acquires a sword" [NW 96]. **Bounties** outlive their cancellation: "Nobody pays a crier to shout that a man has been pardoned" [NW 98]. **Bookies' boards** are "the most honest news in town"; "A price that moves at noon with no news to explain it is the news" [NW 100]. **Fame** changes prices and doors, and "some boy with a good coat and no name walks twenty days to find out if the song is true" [NW 102].
- **Costs.** A beacon message "a silver for twenty words with the addresses counted, and a copper for each word after" [NW 44]; a letter 2 to 4 copper across a quarter; a news bill 1 copper [WC 55, 61].
- **Open.** Whether the beacon line runs through the Midmarch is not on file; the Turn's Line has beacon towers round the Hem [HC 108], and the line reaches Kessen in the north ("This came up the beacon line from Kessen in the night" [TY 85]). Without it, the bag and the mouth carry Midmarch news, which suits a peddler.

## 19. Outside ideas (not canon; ideas and fact-checks only)

- **Medieval tolls, for naming a Midmarch chain's dues in a clerk's mouth:** pontage (bridges), murage (walls) and pavage (streets) (Wikipedia, "Pontage", https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pontage); chiminage, "A toll paid for passage through a forest", stallage, "The erection and use of a stall at a fair or market", and tronage, the weighing of wool (Wiktionary). A Midmarch notary could have a word for every chain.
- **Deodand** (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deodand): an object that caused a death was forfeit, or its owner paid its appraised value; older English law called such things banes. A Midmarch crown that claims a killing sword as a deodand, beside the heriot custom trade.md section 7 already borrows, is an INVENTED option for Ned's dead men's swords.
- **Eel rents** (Hull Domesday Project, "stick"; Historia Cartarum Eel-Rents Project): rents paid in sticks of twenty-five eels. Fits a river-plain country where rent "goes to whoever's man turned up".
- **Pedlars as "the legs of the community"** (habsburger.net, "Of pedlars and the imperial Taxis Post"): pedlars carried messages and news, which matches canon's mouth [NW 92]. A sixteenth-century complaint has chapmen going "from fair to fair, from markett to markett" with horse packs and foot packs, selling in church porches on holy days (Wikipedia, "Peddler").
- **Foot travel.** Boyer's fourteenth-century French records give about 30 km (some 20 miles) a day on foot; Ludwig counts 40 to 45 km a day as a normal day's travel for hardy travelers (via Medievalists.net and the ETH copy of Ludwig, *Untersuchungen über die Reise- und Marschgeschwindigkeit*).
- **The dusty foot** is the court of piepowder, from *pieds poudrés* (Wikipedia; also in trade.md).

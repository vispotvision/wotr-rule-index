## 1. The concept in one breath

**Hook (R82-13, no system words):** Whatever Ned picks up tells his hands what its last owners did with it, and his hands can do it again.

**Isaac's sentence (ANS, Q6_power):** "Provenance: everything remembers who held it."

**The ladder in one breath (R82-18, TRAIT:38):** the Trait is that everything remembers who held it; a Release wears every owner of his weapon on his own body at once; a Domain makes every made thing in reach obey who held it over who holds it (section 9).

**Its names.**
- **On his window (INVENTED):** `Everything Remembers Who Held It`. Isaac's own sentence, in the soul's habit of naming a Trait as a plain phrase (canon examples: "It Comes Out in the Wash", "Every Column Balances", "Look Twice", SKL:146, WIN:55, SKL:159).
- **The halls' word (INVENTED usage):** *provenance*, the art and trade word for the history of a thing's ownership (wiktionary). It is the art's title.
- **The street (INVENTED):** in the Midmarch, where the aura coat is *colors*, Pressure is *the crown* and sensing is *the herald* (HC:70), Ned is said to have "a nose for a dead man's lot." His own word for it before waking is "the feel."

## 2. Canon anchors

### 2.1 Anamnesis, the lens (MW:21 to MW:55)
- **Idea:** "Remembrance. Everything keeps its past, and a soul under this lens can touch a thing and read it." (MW:23)
- **Kind:** Medium (MW:25). **Titan:** Chronaeus, "the debris of events compacted into layers, and a soul that can read them" (MW:27). **Hue:** faded violet (MW:29), "the color of a thing kept too long in a drawer" (COE:137).
- **Ground:** "Favors darkness, silence, Aether-thin ground and ruins; hates saturated Aether, crowd density and active Domains." (MW:31)
- **Contrary:** Letheveil (forgetting on purpose) and Sublimatio (the record kept against the lower state left behind) (MW:33).
- **On the Body (the line this whole kit stands on):** "A palm on a spear shaft tells who carried it and how they died, and a hand on a broken wall gives back the blow that broke it, in enough detail for the practitioner's own shoulders to repeat it. The flesh under this lens forgets nothing it has ever met, so an old fighter has a lifetime of falls and parries standing ready in the joints." (MW:35)
- **On the Spirit:** reads a soul "as a fired pot reads its kiln"; inscribed on a thing, "it makes that thing's history part of what the thing is, which is how an old sword comes to know its trade." (MW:37)
- **On Fate:** reads oath, debt and death records; "a sword taken from a dead man can say which guard he always dropped"; calling a record in "takes a lifetime's skill and a body's worth of blood. Courts pay very well for the reading and then try to have the witness quietly sealed, and a careless pull erases the very record it was pulling on." (MW:39)
- **Analogue and mechanism:** remanent magnetization and thermoluminescent dosimetry; "Objects genuinely store their history in physically readable form... Anamnesis is an instrument, and anyone calling it a miracle has not looked hard at a fired pot." (MW:47, MW:49)
- **Stat effect:** "Strengthens Gnosis Retention, Gnosis Analysis in reading past trauma, Resilience Anchoring." (MW:51)
- **Glyphs:** `[Ma]` Recall, `[AnMa]` Anamnesis, `[Lei]` Binding, `[VaM]` Moment, `[Mn]` Mnemonic, `[EchoS]` Echo-Scar (MW:53).

- **What a Medium lens costs:** "It costs no extra Essence (spiritual energy), and it goes both ways. Everything a Medium lens does to the world it is also doing to you, so a Medium practitioner of thirty years is a person with a great deal of company." (MW:15)

## 3. How it really works

### 3.1 Character first (INVENTED)
NAT, THE MAGIC FRAME: refusal, survival, belief, then concept. **What he refuses:** an unpaid account (ANS, Q4_dream). **What he survived:** at eleven, a ditch, and a woman's hand that pulled him out and would take no payment (ANS, Q9_debt). **What he believes that costs him:** that nothing is ever sold clean, because everything remembers who held it.

**Origin (INVENTED, chosen):** the Trait formed in the ditch by Resonance Trauma (FOW8:42). The statement the Continuum accepted was a boy's: *I was held, and I remember it.* Nine years of handling other people's goods and dead men's lots then weathered it into grain (TRAIT:58 to TRAIT:64). It is his **Primary** (TRAIT:88), so it is the seed his Releases will rise from (REL:21). Two consequences the book can use: his own body is a record too (it remembers her grip), and the truth that birthed the Trait is the open debt, so abandoning the debt is the road to a Shear Break (5.6).

### 3.2 What a used thing keeps (the physics, in plain words)
Anamnesis reads records that are really there (MW:49). For steel, leather and wood, five are real:

1. **Wear.** A used thing is worn into the shape of its use: rolled edges, polish, tiny flake scars and scratches running the way it moved. Archaeologists read a tool's action and the material it worked from wear alone, and blind tests get the action right most of the time (use-wear analysis). On a sword: which flat took the parries, where the edge rolled on mail, the grip polished where fingers sat, leather crushed under one thumb.
2. **The hardest load.** A metal that has been stressed stays quiet under any load smaller than its old maximum and starts to crackle only when that maximum is beaten, because the old microcracks do not move until then (the Kaiser effect). In plain words, steel remembers the hardest blow it ever took. Damage and rework spoil that memory (the Felicity effect, the same entry).
3. **The count.** Each hard cycle grows a fatigue crack a hair and leaves a line on the fracture face, like tree rings (fatigue).
4. **The field.** Stress changes a steel's magnetic state (the Villari effect), and basalt keeps the field it cooled in (MW:47). Every hard blow nudges a blade's tiny magnetic grains, and they stay nudged. This is why Anamnesis tugs on iron (SIG:64).
5. **The time since the fire.** Crystals trap charge at a rate set by the dose they absorb; heating releases it as light and resets the clock, and the reading itself destroys the sample. The trade uses it to tell an old piece from a fake (thermoluminescence; MW:47). In plain words, a fired thing counts quietly from its last fire, and fire wipes the count.

**The body's side.** Knowing *how* and knowing *what* are separate memories: a man with no memory of practicing can still get better at a hand-and-eye skill (procedural memory). Hands can learn a fight without the story of it.

### 3.3 The law that reads them (the metaphysics)
When Ned closes his palm on a used grip, the Core authors the working and it lands through the Shell (PATHS:106). After waking he pulls Aether from the ground under him (rich in a dark, quiet, ruined place, poor at a crowded fair: MW:31), refines it in the Crystal into Essence, and drives it out through the Shell at his palm into the thing (HUB:210). Under Anamnesis's law, "Everything keeps its past" (MW:23), the five physical records stop being inert facts and become readable as what made them: motions. His Trait gives the reading its question, *who held you?*, and the harmonization (once he has it) shapes the Essence by the lens; "the Aether supplies one law and the Core supplies another, and the working is what comes out of both" (HUB:225).

The glyph `[Lei]` Binding fixes where the reading stops: it binds a strand of memory "for retrieval and transmission" (GLY:353, GLY:68). On the Body layer that limit is physical (GLY:30). **INVENTED, read off GLY:30 and GLY:105:**
- **How far:** zero distance. Skin on the thing, through nothing thicker than a thin glove. A padded gauntlet stops it.
- **How much load:** one strand per grip, one owner at a time, carried into his body only as far as his own tissue and Grade can carry it.
- **How long:** the strand holds while the grip holds; it seals on attention. Let go, or look away, and the borrowed motion runs out.

**Transmission into his shoulders.** The bound strand is driven back through the Shell into his own muscles as a motor pattern, which is what the Body line promises: "enough detail for the practitioner's own shoulders to repeat it" (MW:35). Essence is the soul's own substance (PATHS:39), so the borrowed blow is made of Ned. **Only the shape is borrowed.**

### 3.4 Why a Body walker gets blows and not stories
- **The layer decides what is read.** Matter reads Aether; a soul or a meaning reads Essence; a condition reads Thread (PATHS:106). Ned's working lands on matter, and matter's records are load and motion.
- **Canon says exactly what the Body gets:** "who carried it and how they died" and "the blow that broke it" (MW:35). The Spirit line gets what a person committed to (MW:37); the Fate line gets oaths, debts and "which guard he always dropped" as a sworn record (MW:39).
- **So Ned gets (INVENTED, inside MW:35):** a body-portrait of each owner (height and reach from the arc of the wear, hand size and handedness from the grip, the side they favored from the scuffing, old injuries from how they compensated), the motions, the hardest blow, and the death if they died holding it, as a body event (the last clench, the spike of the last load). **He never gets a name, a motive, a promise or a feeling.**
- **Real science agrees:** knowing-how is stored apart from knowing-what (3.2).
- **For the book:** he can learn the woman's height, reach, guard, best blows and how her hand left the grip, and never her name from steel (consistent with ANS, Q9_debt). Whether she let it go alive is body evidence he can read: a hand that let go slowly, against a death-clench.

### 3.5 What runs on past the limit
"Matter, obeying everything matter obeys" (GLY:30). **INVENTED consequences:**
- A borrowed motion is committed once driven. Momentum is real; he cannot turn a borrowed cut into a feint halfway.
- It runs on his tendons. A taller dead man's lunge over-reaches a shorter Ned, and the strain is his.
- **The sword gives the arm, never the feet.** A blade recorded only the loads it bore: hand, wrist, edge. The feet's half of a fight is in the boots. (He sells boots too.)
- The force is his own Grade: "Brawling B on Ardency F is a beautifully placed punch from someone who cannot hurt you" (SKL:107).

### 3.6 The failure: thermal resetting
Canon: a careless read "erases the record in the act of taking it" (MW:55); "a careless pull erases the very record it was pulling on" (MW:39). The Ledger says waste is shed at the Shell, "at or outside the body's surface" (FOW9:290), and at η 0.30 to 0.35 about two-thirds of every working leaves as heat (FOW7:227, FOW7:228).

**INVENTED, consistent:** a slow read spreads that heat thin, so the grip is only warm. A fast or hard read pours it into what he holds: the grip scorches, temper colors creep up the steel under his hand (straw, then brown, then blue), and the record under the colors resets, exactly as a fire resets a fired pot's count. At Welling, where "direction arrives before volume does" (STG:114), he will do this by accident. That is his spilling year, and it costs him goods.

### 3.7 The read as a Working Card, at a glance (book one scale)
1. **Charge:** he turns the thing to the light like a buyer, thumb on the ricasso, closes his fist on the grip, holds his breath, eyes to the middle distance (onlookers will think he is reading his own window).
2. **Pull:** Aether from the ground; better in a cellar at night, worse in a packed yard (MW:31).
3. **Aura:** his own color at first (COE:23), near-black at Murmuring (COE:41); after the lens, dark char with faded violet stirring, clinging to the hands (COE:42).
4. **Room:** faded violet afterimages of other hands bloom around his on the grip, the oldest faintest (SIG:64); loose iron (nails, buckles, filings, a pin in a cloak) creeps toward his palm (COE:214); heat-shimmer off his wrist; dogs go quiet at Murmuring (STG:102).
5. **Senses:** the dry riffle of pages and a faint hum; old paper and attic dust (SIG:64). The Trait's sound word is in section 11.
6. **Mechanism:** sections 3.2 to 3.5.
7. **Effect:** the strand is bound; the owner's body and motions are his for as long as he holds.
8. **Cost:** a share of reserve, heat into the grip, and something kept (section 5).

## 4. Stage by stage

### 4.1 Stage 0: unwoken, the Trait working unlisted (chapters 1 to about 6)
**Canon frame:** Aether Ø, "No stat output above Hollow Grade is possible" (FOW7:40); "A sealed Crystal holds no Essence" (FOW9:92); no harmonization (PATHS:49); the law "sits unlisted and goes on working" (SKL:169); a weathered soul is functionally single-Path (PATHS:232).

**What it can do (INVENTED, inside that frame):**
- **The feel.** Given a minute or more of handling (turning it, thumbs along the wear), a used thing tells him handedness, rough height and reach, how long it was used and how cared for, whether it has bitten flesh, whether it was handed on, dropped, or held to the end.
- **Merchant work.** He prices by it, and he catches forgeries: a maker's stamp is easy to fake, a lifetime of wear is not (the thermoluminescence fact: authentic or fake). A steel that is too clean under an old edge has been through a fire recently, so **a sword with no past is a sword somebody burned clean** (a laundered blade; a hook for the deal that goes wrong, ANS Q1).
- **Hands that settle.** Pick up a lot-sword and his hands drift into the last owner's grip and guard without being taught. His Swordwork climbs faster from dead men's swords than from new ones (skills rise with use, SKL:56). This is how "he learns to fight from the dead men's swords he buys and sells" (ANS, Q6_power) before he can use any of it.
- **The window files the knack as a trade skill.** The soul names skills in his own tongue and can be rude (SKL:66), so the Trait leaks onto the sheet as a skill line it cannot list (window in section 8).

**What it cannot do:**
- No force: he feels the dead man's blow standing in the steel and his arms cannot drive it. The shape arrives, Hollow-Grade. **INVENTED story engine:** this is the frustration that sells him on the Draft.
- No aura, no tell anyone can see, no read in a breath, no read through a glove, nothing new or reworked, no stories, no living bodies.

## 5. The cost: every past he reads stays in him

### 5.1 Canon roots
The Medium lens "goes both ways" and leaves "a great deal of company" (MW:15). "The flesh under this lens forgets nothing it has ever met" (MW:35). Scalebond keeps what she touches, layer by layer, and stalls at Stage VIII because she cannot bear to lose it (SCALE:69). Identity carry is never reported on a window (CORES:144) and escalates by dose (CORES:148 to CORES:153). Below a tenth of the reserve the Core frays (PATHS:39).

### 5.2 How it shows (INVENTED, chosen)
**The window shows the gains and hides the losses.** Skill gains show (`[Skill rising: Swordwork, E, 38%]`). Physical carry shows as an ordinary status line with no explanation, such as a knee he never hurt (`[Left knee: stiff in the wet.]`). The habits, tastes and flinches never show, because the soul making the report is the one they happened to, and it feels fine (CORES:144). The body tells first, as always (WIN:82): a dead drover's two fingers through a cup handle, a hum nobody taught him, raw onion at breakfast, sleeping on the left side, a flinch at the exact blow that killed some stranger.

### 5.3 How it escalates (INVENTED, shaped on CORES:148 to CORES:153 and SCALE:69)

| Deep reads kept | What shows | Who notices |
|---|---|---|
| 1 to 10 (book one) | Dreams of other men's fights; aches where owners were hurt; one hand-habit or taste per few reads | Ned, at night; whoever shares his road |
| 10 to 30 | Reflexes: a dead man's guard when startled, a flinch at a blow that killed someone else, his handedness drifting | sparring partners; a hall instructor |
| 30 to 100 | Appetites and convictions: a dead mercenary's contempt for levies, a dead knight's fear of horses, a grudge at the stance of a killer he has never met | everyone who knew him before |
| Past that | The company outvotes him; his Core cannot say which convictions are his own | nobody, because he feels fine |

**Starvation (INVENTED, on PATHS:39):** below a tenth, his own memories fray, and the borrowed ones fill the holes. A name goes missing, and a dead man's name for the same thing turns up in its place.

### 5.4 The strange personal price (R82-23): provenance moves (INVENTED, chosen)
From the idea itself. **A light read copies; a hard read moves.** Its heat resets the steel under his hand (3.6), so the record leaves the thing and lives only in Ned. Every hard read makes him the last keeper of some dead man's best blow, and leaves the blade with less past than it had. For a merchant that is a real loss: a famous history is worth money, and reading it hard erases the proof. The reverse also holds, because everything remembers who held it: everything Ned has ever handled now remembers Ned. Anyone with Anamnesis can follow him through his own stock, and courts pay for exactly that reading (MW:39).

### 5.5 The upside, and its limit
The flesh keeps the skills (MW:35), so the book's promise holds: he learns to fight from dead men's swords. But repeating one dead man's cut is "the repeated word" (FOW1:52) and goes Familiar after five unvaried uses (FOW2:242). To grow he has to make borrowed blows his own. The series' natural answer is Stage III's Catalyst, "First technique developed independently from instruction" (FOW2:193): **the first blow no sword taught him.**

### 5.6 Failure states that apply (TRAIT:171 to TRAIT:179)
- **Overgrowth:** the Trait reads when he did not will it: every coin in his change, every hand he shakes.
- **Shear Break (INVENTED trigger):** repeatedly denying the truth that birthed it, *I was held, and I remember*. Letting the debt go, selling her sword unpaid, or burning his own goods clean to hide from a pursuer could invert it: everything forgets who held it, his own hands first.
- **The Flicker:** under Starvation the Trait drops out and returns with the reserve, mid-read.
- **Rigidity:** the higher the level, the less he can contradict the law. He cannot make himself forget, and he cannot sell a thing clean.

## 6. Fair counters (as facts, R47-3)

1. **New steel has nothing in it.** No wear, no load history past the smith's proof, no count. The canon parallel is the heir "with nothing written in" him (SCALE:73). **INVENTED Midmarch smith's word:** an *unhandseled* blade (from *handsel*, to use a thing for the first time; wiktionary). **Book use:** a fighting hall that issues new-made practice blades for its yard test has, without meaning to, built the perfect counter.
2. **Fire wipes it.** A re-forged or re-tempered blade has had its count reset (MW:55; the thermoluminescence fact). A reground edge loses its wear. A new grip loses the hand-record, while the blade keeps its loads (INVENTED, partial).
3. **A read costs tempo** (R12-7). Two or three breaths at Stage I, one at Stage II, with his eyes on the middle distance. A fighter who closes inside that breath meets a peddler who is not looking.
4. **No contact, no read.** A padded gauntlet, a disarm, or a fencer who never stays in the bind and acts in the instant gives him nothing to hold (PATHS:90: Body must close).
5. **The borrowed shape is fixed and committed.** Anyone who knew the dead owner, or reads the shape once, knows where it ends; it cannot turn into a feint halfway (GLY:30).
6. **The force is his own.** A master's shape at a peddler's Grade (SKL:107).
7. **Bad ground.** Crowds, rich Aether and active Domains spoil Anamnesis (MW:31). A packed yard on a fair day is the worst place he can read.
8. **Contrary lenses.** A Letheveil or Sublimatio fighter's gear holds little to read (MW:33).
9. **Too many owners (INVENTED).** A famous old blade with forty owners is a crowd in one grip; finding the right strand takes longer.
10. **The Stage gap.** One Stage is crossable with a read, two only with a vow or a breakthrough (NAT, THE TABLE, rule 5). Provenance *is* a read.

## 7. Named moves for book one

**The art's title (INVENTED, recommended):** **PROVENANCE**, the halls' word and Isaac's. A signature art takes "a title with named moves under it" (R82-3): `PROVENANCE: SECONDHAND!` for the big calls, the bare move name in quick trades. **Volume:** a chapman cries his wares on the road, so Ned is a showman, in capitals on their own line (R82-25). The crowd laughs at a man fighting with used goods until it works (R82-5, in spirit). Midmarch English throughout (R82-4); each name is a trade word a peddler would know.

**Count:** five, earned across the book (R82-12). He starts with none, because he is unwoken. **Note:** `PAID IN FULL!` is already a named move (CRY:19) and a Nalūn idiom; do not use it.

### 7.1 BILL OF SALE!
- **What it does:** the read. Palm on a used grip, he reads the chain of hands back from the last: each owner's body, how long each held it, the hardest blow it took, and how each let go (handed on, dropped, or held to the end). No names.
- **Look and sound:** the charge in 3.7; afterimage-hands blooming around his on the grip, one per owner, the oldest faintest; iron creeping to his palm; old paper and dust; a dry page turned for each owner.
- **Cost:** a fight read is about 1 percent of reserve and a flush of heat into the grip; a deep read keeps something.
- **Counter:** new or fire-wiped steel gives a blank page; a gauntlet stops it; the breath it takes is the opening.
- **Chapters:** the unnamed knack from chapter 1 ("the feel"); named and called from the waking (about 6 to 7) to 14.

### 7.2 SECONDHAND!
- **What it does:** one borrowed action (a cut, a parry, a thrust, a bind-and-wind) driven through his own shoulders in the dead owner's exact shape and timing, at Ned's force.
- **Look and sound:** the faded violet afterimage of the dead man's arm starts the line a half-beat early, and Ned's arm follows inside it like a hand into a glove somebody else stretched. His stance snaps to a stranger's: a heel lifts for a taller man, a shoulder drops for a one-eyed one. The blade arrives in a timing nobody has seen him use, and afterward a follow-through flourish he never chose (the tell). A dry whisk runs ahead of the real impact, which gets its own fresh word.
- **Cost:** about 3 percent; his tendons pay for a body that was not his; a low-η flare of heat; habits kept.
- **Counter:** the shape is fixed and committed; anyone who knew the dead man knows where it ends; the force stays his.
- **Chapters:** about 7 to 14 (his first real fight after waking, then the delve).

### 7.3 THE HAGGLE!
- **What it does:** in a bind, he leans into the pressure and reads the other man's blade through the contact, then winds into the hole. It is the German masters' *Fühlen*, feeling the opponent's intent through the bind (Wiktenauer), made literal: his opponent's own steel tells Ned which line he favors, which guard he drops, where his edge has landed before.
- **Look and sound:** faded violet crawls from Ned's hilt along his edge to the crossing and onto the other blade; old nicks on the enemy's steel glow faintly as they are read; the opponent feels his blade dragged toward Ned's (the iron tug, which helps hold the bind); Ned talks prices the whole time (INVENTED); the long whine of steel held under pressure.
- **Cost:** about 5 percent per bind held a breath. The bind is mutual, so a stronger winder goes through while he reads, and the opponent's habits stay in Ned too.
- **Counter:** a fighter who never stays in the bind, who carries new steel, or who changes weapons between exchanges.
- **Chapters:** about 11 to 13. It needs Welling's direction; the yard test is its natural birth.

### 7.4 MIXED LOT!
- **What it does:** two owners in one exchange from one blade: the parry of one dead man and the cut of another, back to back. A *lot* is the auction bundle a dead man's goods are sold in.
- **Look and sound:** two afterimages of different men flicker over him in turn; his height and stance change between the actions; the seam shows as a half-beat hitch; two page-whispers out of step.
- **Cost:** about 8 percent; the two bodies argue in his tendons (a foot set wrong, a pulled hamstring), the way "Mixed courses... argue" (CORES:167); two things kept.
- **Counter:** the seam is visible and hittable, and it cannot be hurried.
- **Chapters:** about 12 to 13 (the second deal that goes wrong, ANS Q1).

### 7.5 NO CHARGE! (book one's finisher)
- **What it does:** with the sword that bears her mark, he reads all the way down to her one true blow, the best thing that steel ever did in her hands, and spends it to save someone who cannot pay, taking nothing. The debt paid in kind (ANS, Q9_debt): she would take no payment, and now neither will he.
- **Look and sound (an ultimate, R82-27):** a slowed run-up and its own section title; the whole blade lights faded violet from tang to tip like a pot glowing in the kiln (the thermoluminescence glow, made visible); for one breath her afterimage stands full length over him in her guard while the iron in the street creeps toward the blade; then the light runs out of the steel and the sword goes dull gray. The faint hum rises to one long note and stops.
- **Cost (R82-23, from its own idea):** the hard read moves her out of the steel. Nobody can read her from that sword again, and Ned becomes the only place her fighting remains. He will stand in her guard by reflex for life. About 30 percent of reserve; a shoulder torn by a reach longer than his own (INVENTED); a flare of heat the whole street feels.
- **Counter:** it needs that one sword and a breath of stillness; reaching him in the breath ends it; the force is still his own, D-Grade at most.
- **Chapters:** 14 only. *Spare call if NO CHARGE reads too light:* QUITTANCE!, the old word for the paper that releases a debt.

### 7.6 Notes for later books (INVENTED)
- **Upgrade (R82-29):** SECONDHAND jumps to **FIRSTHAND!** at Stage III, the first blow no sword taught him (5.5). The old name stays in use.
- **A merchant's vow for book two:** "I read nothing I haven't paid for." Terms, bargain and breach would show on his window (WIN:136 to WIN:142), and it counts toward the two-Grade cap. Left out of book one to keep the kit clean.
- **Not in book one:** reading living bodies (Scalebond's ground, section 10). The single exception is his own body, which remembers her hand.

## 9. The terrifying place at the top of the series (INVENTED, on canon's ladder)

**The Body ladder, read through Provenance.** The Body rungs run "the novice commands matter as an object, the adept through contact, the master through recognition, the sovereign through law, and the rare Totality Architect as a complete system", and at the rungs of law "the blade rusts in your hand... and every command comes back as recoil through their own bones and Crystal" (PATHS:66).
- **Object and contact (books one and two):** he reads what he grips.
- **Recognition:** a held weapon does on its own what it has done before. The sword that remembers the cut leads his hand.
- **Law:** in his presence every made thing answers its record. Armor fails at its old worst (the Kaiser effect turned on the wearer), a bridge remembers the flood that nearly took it, a blade remembers its weld and breaks there, a sword remembers being dropped and leaves your hand. Still only existing substance, still recoil, and every past he calls up is kept.
- **System:** a whole battlefield's provenance at once. A map scar the world names (R82-31): a field where every blade remembers.

**Release and Domain (R82-18; REL table).**
- **First Release, THE WHOLE LOT (Stage IV and up):** the idea worn on the body. Every owner of his weapon at once, faded violet arms layered over his own and other hands' prints crawling up his forearms. He fights as a crowd of dead men in one body, and the crowd can outvote him.
- **Domain, WHOSE IT WAS (Stage VIII and up):** the world obeys the idea. Within his ground, who held a thing outranks who holds it: a stolen sword fights its thief in its dead owner's style, inherited armor opens where the grandfather was speared, a crown bought on Pennycrown's Crown Row remembers what it was bought for. *Spare name:* THE TENTH POINT, after the proverb that possession is nine points of the law.

**Calling in the record.** Anamnesis on Fate reads every oath and debt, and calling one in "takes a lifetime's skill and a body's worth of blood" (MW:39). Fate practice "is counted in vows kept and bonds held" (PATHS:137), which is most of a merchant's life. A Paragon who also walks Fate could make coin remember what it is owed. Coin is the most-held thing in the world. The merchant other markets check their weights against (ANS, Q11_endgame), literally.

**The company.** Thirty years of a Medium lens is "a great deal of company" (MW:15), and Scalebond's elders stall at Transcendence because they cannot bear to lose what they hold (SCALE:69). Ned's Stage VIII wall is the same question: is there enough Ned left to be the Paragon?

**Who comes for him.** Courts that seal witnesses (MW:39). Crystal sealing, the penalty for unlicensed high working (GATE:149). And the whole trade in a dead practitioner's Crystal: the Dead Man's Cordial is the worst crime and the best money in every realm (DRAFT:177), and barber's curl "carries the dead owner's bonds" (DRAFT:152). A man who can read whose soul is in the bottle is the most dangerous witness in that trade.

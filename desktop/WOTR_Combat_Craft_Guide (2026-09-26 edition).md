## 1. THE FOUR COMBAT GRAMMARS

**WOTR uses four combat vocabularies, not one. Assign the grammar before assigning the technique.** *(R1-1-THREE_VOCABULARIES_RULING, extended by R3-7-EXPENDITURE_GRAMMAR)*

Running a per-character pass against a single fencing framework reveals why: most named fighters don't fence at all, and at least one explicitly cannot. Sodoku's Shinken-ryū is a verdict system with a blade attached — no blade conversation, no sentiment du fer, no parrying forms. Rashani reads first causes through the Origin Eye and answers with Zero-Point stepping; he doesn't live in the bind because he doesn't need it. Rengai and Dougou work a hammer and body-percussion respectively, which never mapped onto longsword vocabulary. And Cozbi's own body sits below his soul-architecture ceiling — he isn't a fighter with a poor grammar, he's a fighter whose entire proposition is that he isn't the thing you're hitting.

### I. Blade Grammar (Liechtenauer/Fiore, as this guide already documents)

Applies to characters trained in a conventional sword tradition. Guards, master-cuts, the bind, Winden, Indes, Absetzen, Durchwechseln. Full existing framework applies without change — see §3 below for the complete vocabulary. *(R1-1-BLADE_GRAMMAR)*

*Assign to:* conventional duellists, Concord Aether Knights in formal training, Old World nobility, guild fencing traditions, and any character whose fighting mind is built around openings.

### II. Verdict Grammar

Applies to characters whose combat is a reading followed by a conclusion. There is no conversation, because a conclusion doesn't converse. The prose skeleton is *read, rank, commit*, and the tension lives in the read rather than in the exchange. *(R1-1-VERDICT_GRAMMAR)*

*Assign to:* Sodoku (canonical), Rashani (canonical in behaviour if not in name), and any Kamigan-class or perception-lineage fighter. *Prose cue:* the beat before the strike is longer than the strike. Fighters in this grammar are terrifying in the pause and anticlimactic in the motion, and that's correct.

### III. Percussion Grammar

Blade grammar hunts **openings**. Percussion grammar hunts **fatigue**. A swordsman asks where you are unguarded; a hammer asks where you are already carrying load. Fatigue accumulates and openings don't, so percussion fights get *more* dangerous the longer they run while blade fights get *less* decisive. This is Dougou's own canonical doctrine: do not break the wall, make the wall confess where it was already tired. *(R1-1-PERCUSSION_VS_BLADE)*

*Assign to:* Rengai, Dougou, hammer and haft fighters, and any body-percussion tradition.

**The four Percussion moves.** *(R1-1-PERCUSSION_MOVES)*

| Strike | Description | What it breaks |
|---|---|---|
| **The Settling** | Descending, full weight, delivered into the opponent's base rather than at their body | **Stance.** Answers anyone rooted, braced, or planted — the correct answer to a man who has decided not to move. |
| **The Sweep** | Horizontal at hip or knee height, using the weapon's full arc and mass | **The base.** Answers wide, stable, low postures a blade can't profitably attack. |
| **The Shove** | Haft, shoulder, boss, or pommel, delivered at closed distance | **Measure.** Answers anyone who has closed inside the weapon's arc — the percussion fighter's only real vulnerability. |
| **The Return** | The rebound taken deliberately and redirected into a second strike from the recoil | **Recovery.** Answers anyone counting on the pause after a heavy blow. The grammar's signature — the strike that separates a hammer-fighter from a man with a hammer. |

**Percussion's answer to plate:** none of these seek to penetrate. Plate isn't defeated by percussion, it's *used*. Armour transmits force inward, so the correct target is the joint, the base, and the man's ability to keep standing in fifty pounds of steel — a percussion fighter's answer to plate is to make the plate expensive to wear. *(R1-1-PERCUSSION_VS_PLATE_AND_TELL)*

**Percussion's tell:** the fighter tracks the opponent's breathing and footing rather than their weapon. In prose, have them look at the wrong thing.

### IV. Expenditure Grammar

**Expenditure grammar hunts cost.** The practitioner doesn't seek to defeat the opponent — they seek to make the exchange asymmetric, so everything the opponent does is expensive and everything they themselves do is spent from somewhere else. Victory is arithmetic and arrives late. *(R3-7-EXPENDITURE_GRAMMAR)*

*Assign to:* Cozbi, Legion summoners, construct-users, anyone operating through a conscripted lattice or a projected vessel, and any fighter whose combat proposition is that the target of the blow isn't the source of the working. *(R3-7-EXPENDITURE_ASSIGNMENT)*

**The four Expenditure moves.** *(R3-7-EXPENDITURE_MOVES)*

| Move | Description | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| **The Offering** | Something expendable is put where the opponent must deal with it | **Converts the opponent's capability into expenditure.** The construct isn't expected to win. It's expected to cost. |
| **The Standing** | The practitioner does nothing at all for a period the opponent finds intolerable | **Converts time into advantage.** Only available to a fighter who doesn't experience time as a resource — exactly Cozbi's documented strength, and why almost nobody else can use this. |
| **The Ledger** | The accumulated cost is called in at once | **Converts the whole preceding engagement into a single effect.** Un-repeatable per encounter for that reason. |
| **The Absence** | The vessel is destroyed and the practitioner is unaffected | **Converts a victory into nothing.** The opponent has won and gained no ground — discovering this is the psychological centre of fighting anyone in this grammar. |

**Expenditure's answer to plate:** irrelevant. Plate is a cost problem rather than a defence problem, and an expenditure fighter is content to let armour do its job for as long as the wearer can afford to keep wearing it. *(R3-7-EXPENDITURE_VS_PLATE)*

**Expenditure's tell:** the fighter isn't looking at the opponent. In prose, have them looking at something adjacent, or crouching to examine something on the ground. **Counterplay, documented twice and neither easy:** reach through the projection to the claims it carries (a Rescind-type working, done once), or force a direct physical contest against the practitioner's own below-ceiling frame (also done once, and it worked). *(R3-7-EXPENDITURE_TELL_AND_COUNTERPLAY)*

**Craft note for writing an Expenditure fighter:** he is boring to write badly and terrifying to write correctly. The temptation is to give him a spectacular exchange — refuse it. The scene should feel like the opponent is winning, repeatedly, at increasing cost, until the arithmetic arrives. Everything before the Ledger is the opponent succeeding. *(R3-7-COZBI_CRAFT_NOTE)*

## 2. RANGE OPENINGS: FIREARMS AND THE TWENTY-FOOT THRESHOLD

Gunpowder exists, magical weapons exist, and armour is not dead. Plate retreated to the historical cuirass-and-helm silhouette and then **stopped there**, because mass and material still buy a fighter the fraction of a second needed to not be where a Wellspring-carried strike arrives. The result: **every armed man now carries at least two answers, because there are at least two questions** — coat, breast, helm, sword, and firearm. This is exactly why this guide survives intact: Liechtenauer and Fiore still govern once measure closes. Inside twenty feet a discharged firearm is a club, and the sword is the thing that finishes it. *(R11-3-DUAL_ANSWER_SILHOUETTE)*

**An engagement may open at range before the HEMA grammar takes over — inside roughly twenty feet is where it does.** Unchanged in substance otherwise: everything in §1, §3, and §4 below governs once the fight closes to blade range. *(R11-5-COMBAT_GUIDE_RANGE_OPENING)*

**Ballistics ruling.** Powder weapons are slow, loud, and honest. They open an engagement, they do not decide one. A practitioner above roughly the middle Stages is not reliably killed by common shot — which is precisely why the enchanted round exists, and precisely why it costs what a family earns in a season. *(R11-3-BALLISTICS_RULING)*

**Ammunition tiers — cross-reference, not a rule of this guide.** The three ammunition tiers are the Item & Equipment Writing Guide's (R11-3-AMMO_TIERS; Pack Eleven §3; that guide's 2026-09-12 reconstruction, §5, names them common shot, proofed shot, and the named round). Pack Twenty's clearance ratifies them, subject to R20C-26 *(R20C-22-AMMUNITION_TIERS_RATIFIED, `amends: null`)*: "The three ammunition tiers ratified, subject to R20C-26." R20C-26 is quoted next.

**Armour and shot — cross-reference, not a rule of this guide.** *(R20C-26-STEEL_STOPS_THE_SHOT, `rules/pack-20-twenty.yaml`, `amends: null`, `applies_to: combat, items, adjudication`)*: "The steel stops the shot, always. A working may spall, bias, weaken or find the seam. It is never the armour." Read this beside the ballistics ruling above and beside §4.4's line on the proofed round; the guide adds no gloss of its own to it.

> **Cross-reference, not a rule of this guide:** the Item & Equipment Writing Guide's firearms prose law bans any sentence explaining *why* a proofed round defeats proofed plate — the physical account of the hole stays on the page, the causal account doesn't, full stop, even though §5–§6 below require a causal explanation for everything else at first display and finisher. If a scene's opening exchange is a gunshot, that specific beat is the one place in this guide's technical floor where the causal layer is withheld by design, not by omission.

## 3. FOOTWORK, GUARDS, AND CUTS — THE BLADE GRAMMAR IN MECHANICAL DETAIL

This section is HEMA-grounded (Liechtenauer's longsword tradition and Fiore's Armizare), the choreography skeleton for characters assigned Blade Grammar in §1. Real technique names stay real — they don't need re-flavoring into WOTR-speak, the same way Isaac doesn't rename real-world martial arts moves when writing Sodoku's hand-to-hand.

**The vocabulary now extends past the longsword.** In addition to everything below: spear and staff, poleaxe, dagger and the grappling plays, messer, sword and buckler, and mounted lance are all in scope, and Percussion Grammar (§1.III) gets a master-strike table built for the hammer specifically, not borrowed from the blade. *(R13-6-HEMA_VOCAB)*

That hammer table is not yet written in this guide (Appendix A carries it as owed). When it is, Pack Twenty has already settled whose table it is in-world *(R20C-25-PERCUSSION_TABLE_DAWI_AUTHORED, `amends: null`, cross-referenced)*: "The Percussion master-strike table is Dawi-authored in-world."

### 3.1 Footwork
Weight rides the balls of the feet, never the heels — heel-weighting telegraphs and unbalances. The knee, not the foot's direction, determines where a fighter is actually going.

- **Swinging Step** — striking leg steps to its own side; used for horizontal/shoulder swings.
- **Gathering Step** — dominant foot forward, launching off the striking leg; used for lunges and vertical swings.
- **Passing Step** — rear foot lunges forward, front foot follows behind it; used to enter or exit range decisively.
- **Double/Single Triangle Step** — lateral repositioning that keeps a fighter in range for follow-up attacks without committing forward or back.
- **Cross Step** — rear foot crosses in front of the lead foot; used to feint a lunge or realign hips smoothly.

### 3.2 Guards (Read as Character Voice, Not Just Stance)
A guard is mostly psychological pressure, not real protection — a fighter has to leave any guard to actually act, and that transition is the vulnerable moment. Which guard a character defaults to should say something about them, the same way dialogue register does.

- **Langort / Long Point** — point extended toward the opponent's face, pommel at the groin. The single best all-purpose guard in the system: threatens, defends, and centers. A disciplined, controlled character's default.
- **Vom Tag** — blade on the shoulder, pommel down, one of Liechtenauer's "basic four." A patient, load-bearing guard for a fighter who wants to see the opponent commit first.
- **Ochs** — high guard over the shoulder, pommel by the cheek, blade threatening down. Aggressive and immediate.
- **Pflug / Plow** — arms unextended, point centered, good for thrusts and rotations. A measured, technical fighter's guard.
- **Alber / Fool** — point low, deliberately "open." Baits an attack in order to counter it — a guard for a fighter who wants to be underestimated.
- **Nebenhut / Tail Guard** — hip height, tip trailing back and down, a loaded starting point for an upward cut. Coiled rather than static.

### 3.3 Cuts and Strikes
- **Oberhau** — simple downward strike from above.
- **Unterhau** — rising cut from below; cannot be safely stopped mid-swing once committed, which makes it a strong feint.
- **Zornhau** — the "wrath cut," swung outward from Vom Tag with real torque behind it.
- **Krumphau** — parries and redirects an opponent's blade downward using their own momentum, then steps offline to strike.
- **Zwirch/Zwerchhau** — a horizontal near-spin strike, one of the hardest cuts in the system to parry cleanly, and a strong opener.
- **Schielhau** — a false-edge cut, often disguised to look like a miss before it connects.

### 3.4 Feinting, Flitzing, and Doubling
- **Feint** — an attack canceled mid-motion to bait a reaction or set up a second strike.
- **Flitzing** — mid-swing, the attacker switches sides by lifting the arms over the head and resuming from the new angle, carrying the original momentum into a different line. Rare, and reads as genuinely surprising when a character pulls it off.
- **Doubling (Duplierren)** — an attack stopped and then instantly resumed within a half-second, breaking an opponent's parry timing.

### 3.5 The Bind
When two blades lock, "hard" (asserting control) and "soft" (yielding) define who's in danger. A fighter going soft against a hard opponent is exposed to a direct thrust. Key bind mechanics worth using in prose: **winden** (turning the blade from false to true edge mid-bind to redirect a cut), the **thumb-press grip** (sliding the upper thumb onto the blade's strong for tighter control during a bind or thrust), and **disengage** (yielding to pressure and letting the opponent's own momentum carry their blade past, rather than fighting the bind directly). Attacking on the *inside* of a guard is meaningfully more dangerous to defend than attacking the outside — a detail worth having a technical character (Dhaerin, a trained swordsman) actually notice and exploit.

**Use this vocabulary to differentiate combat styles the same way dialogue differentiates voices.** A character who always fights from Langort with clean winden-based binds reads as controlled and technical (Dhaerin, before the Sacrament, or any Vaeloris-trained fighter). A character who opens with Zwirch and fights through Krumphau redirections reads as aggressive and momentum-driven.

### 3.6 Anatomy and Injury Vocabulary

Anatomy is named by structure. Fracture types are named by name. Neurological consequence is named by name — not summarized as "hurt bad." *(R13-6-ANATOMY_VOCAB)* See §6 for how this feeds the combat technical floor's injury requirement.

### 3.7 Scaling Vocabulary — Author Notes Only

Attack Potency (the energy of one attack, not its area), durability, hax (a working that bypasses a statistic rather than overcoming it), speed blitz, reaction speed against combat speed, striking against lifting strength, range, outlier, anti-feat — this comparison vocabulary is used to keep Natalie honest about who wins and why. **It stays in author notes and adjudication. It never reaches the page.** This restriction is adjudication vocabulary, not a period-accuracy rule, and it stands on its own regardless of how a character's dialogue register develops. *(R13-6-SCALING_VOCAB_RESTRICTED)*

## 4. ARMOR — PHYSICS, AETHER, AND THE HERMETIC READ

Every armor type below follows the same three-part structure: **Physical Behavior** (what it actually does, grounded in real material science), **WOTR Metaphysics** (how Essence and Wellspring inscription interact with it), and **Prose Cue** (what a hit against it should actually look like on the page). Cross-reference §1 for how each grammar answers plate specifically — Percussion uses it, Expenditure treats it as a cost problem, Blade goes through the gaps.

### 4.1 Padded Armor (Gambeson, Aketon)
**Physical:** Ten to twenty quilted layers absorb and spread impact, turning what would be a deep cut into a bruise or shallow wound, and increasing impact time to lower peak force on blunt hits.
**Metaphysics:** Acts as a coarse Aether net, giving the Soul Crystal more time to "ride" incoming shock. Mending Runes stitched into the fabric slowly pull Aether to knit tears.
**Prose Cue:** *"The blow slammed into his chest; the gambeson crunched, stuffing compacting, ribs aching but unbroken as the force diffused across his torso."* A hit that gets through should read as bruises and knocked-out breath, not instant gore.

### 4.2 Mail
**Physical:** Nearly immune to cuts (blades skate and catch on riveted rings), badly vulnerable to narrow thrusts that force rings apart, and weak against blunt trauma since rings transmit force straight to the body underneath.
**Metaphysics:** Each ring can carry a micro-rune, turning a hauberk into a Resonant Mesh — but strong Essence shocks can set the whole shirt vibrating, a kind of psychic tinnitus through the wearer's nervous system.
**Prose Cue:** *"The strike bit into his hauberk with a harsh chime, rings shrieking, the impact blooming as a bruise instead of a wound."* To actually hurt a mailed opponent: maces, warhammers, poleaxes, or grappling — armor doesn't stop a joint lock.

### 4.3 Brigandine / Coat of Plates
**Physical:** Overlapping plates behind fabric spread force wider than mail while keeping flexibility; thrusts tend to glance or catch in the angled plates rather than penetrate straight.
**Metaphysics:** Each plate functions as a sigil tablet — directional-impact runes can shunt force sideways instead of inward.
**Prose Cue:** *"His blow crashed into her coat; the hidden plates drank most of it, but the impact still rattled her spine."* Real openings are the gaps — armpit, shoulder, thigh, groin — where plates don't overlap.

### 4.4 Plate Harness
**Physical:** Historically dominant against period weapons — swords are nearly useless against it except at joints or in a grapple. Rigid plate resists deforming, which is precisely why it reduces blunt trauma so effectively; when a hit *does* deform the plate, the transferred energy is severe (broken ribs, internal injury) even without penetration.
**Metaphysics:** Ideal substrate for dense runic work — Impact Damping Lattices converting kinetic force into harmless vibration, Wellspring Channels routing Essence through sigil lines.
**Prose Cue:** *"The hammer blow skidded over his pauldron in a shock of sparks, the plate ringing like a bell; he staggered, but the steel held."* Don't let a random sword through a breastplate in one hit without real justification — precision thrusts into gaps and grappling (half-swording, dagger into mail voids) are the actual answer to plate. A proofed firearm round is the other actual answer — see §2 — but the guide never explains why it works, only shows that it did. §2's Pack Twenty cross-reference is read beside this line: "The steel stops the shot, always. A working may spall, bias, weaken or find the seam. It is never the armour." *(R20C-26-STEEL_STOPS_THE_SHOT, quoted, not folded)*

### 4.5 Ceramic Plate (Shard Harness)
**Physical:** A sacrificial structure — the plate is meant to shatter, spreading the impact across a wide area and disintegrating on the first serious hit rather than holding indefinitely.
**Metaphysics:** Etched with Mending Glyphs tied to Earth Wellsprings; slow Aether trickle can coax fragments back into shape over hours, not seconds.
**Prose Cue:** First hit: *"The blow cratered his breastplate; the ceramic beneath spiderwebbed, the shock spreading outwards instead of drilling into his heart."* Same spot, second hit: it fails, and trauma passes straight through.

### 4.6 Aetheric Inscription Types (usable across any armor above)
- **Mending Arrays** — slow self-repair (scenes, not seconds); heavy use leaves Aether fatigue, trembling hands.
- **Weight Ablation Glyphs** — armor feels light early in a fight; when the glyph strains or fails, the true weight crashes back in all at once, a staggering, knee-buckling moment worth writing explicitly.
- **Impact Damping Seals** — lengthen the duration of a hit to lower its peak force (a metaphysical version of a trauma pad); overuse produces ringing in the Essence, a half-second delay in perceiving the world.
- **Full Magic Armor (Aether Plate)** — pure condensed Aether; low density reads glassy and brittle, high density reads opaque and metallic at heavy Essence cost. Its one hard law: form must be respected — badly articulated joints turn a character's own spell into their restraint.

## 5. THE THREE LAYERS OF EVERY HIT

Every impact in WOTR — a fist, a longsword, dragonfire, a Rescind-laced blade — is a conversation between three things happening at once. Drop any one and the hit reads as flat.

1. **Physics.** Mass, velocity, impact area, material behavior. Kinetic energy ≈ ½·m·v². A blunt hit and a narrow thrust do different things to the same armor for reasons that have nothing to do with magic.
2. **Essence & Aether.** How much of that impact the Soul Crystal and Aether Shell are forced to absorb, and what it costs to absorb it.
3. **Hermetic correspondence.** Armor reads as Saturn (limit, boundary). The body reads as Mars (force, aggression). Aether reads as Mercury (conductor). Wellsprings sit underneath as Jupiter/Neptune (expansion, depth) shaping outcomes. This register is available and should be reached for when a hit's *meaning* matters, not just its damage.

A hit that only shows layer one is a physics textbook. A hit that only shows layer three is empty mysticism. WOTR combat needs at least two of the three visible in any impact that matters.

**At a technique's first display or a finishing blow, all three layers are mandatory, and each now has to land as an explanation, not only as a phenomenon.** Physics, Essence, and Hermetic correspondence each get a causal sentence beside their image at those two moments — what the air did *and* why it did it, stated plainly, not just shown. This replaces the older convention of rendering the hit model as pure phenomenon; the read that identifies what's happening is likewise shown at length now, not summarized. *(R13-4-THREE_EXPLANATIONS, R12-8-COMBAT_GUIDE_READ_LENGTH)*

## 6. THE COMBAT TECHNICAL FLOOR — WHAT EVERY MEANINGFUL EXCHANGE OWES THE PAGE

**Baseline.** At minimum, across an engagement, a combat exchange owes the reader: *(R12-3-COMBAT_EXCHANGE_OWES)*

1. **The read.** What the POV or the observer identified, and off what evidence — the specific tell, named.
2. **The fault.** Every technique has a structural weakness, and the scene says what it is.
3. **The counter, and why it works.** The causal account, stated plainly.
4. **The cost, in the body and in the reserve.** Both. Not one.
5. **What he cannot do next.** The engagement narrows the character's options, and the narrowing is on the page.

**Extended floor.** Every meaningful exchange (a hit that lands, a first display, a counter, a change of measure that decides something) puts the following on the page, stated, in one of the four voices — the POV's own read, the knowledgeable second, the opponent's contempt-as-instruction, or the document: *(R13-4-COMBAT_FLOOR)*

1. **Measure.** Where the bodies are and what each can reach from there.
2. **Tempo.** Vor, Nach, Indes — who moved first, who moved in the instant, who was late.
3. **The read.** The specific tell, named.
4. **The fault.** What the move exposes.
5. **The mechanics of the hit.** Edge alignment, point of percussion, mass and lever, hip drive, structure through the bind — what the weapon did to armor at that tier, and why. (A round is chemically cheap and beats gambeson and mail; a working is not, and proofed plate exists because of that difference — see §2.)
6. **The injury, by structure.** Tissue, nerve, tendon, vessel, bone, organ, and the functional consequence of each. Blood loss by ATLS class, with Class II anxiety and Class III confusion beats used before pain is.
7. **The Essence account, at all three strata.** What the room had, what the law did, what the soul paid — diagnostic voice.
8. **What he cannot do next.**

**Density budget.** A 2,500-word duel carries the full eight-item floor at first display and finisher, plus items 1–4 at every exchange that changes the fight. A 700–1,500-word turn carries items 1–4 once, and item 8 at close. *(R13-4-DENSITY_BUDGET)*

**Physical units on the page — cross-reference, not a rule of this guide.** Item 5's mechanics reach for numbers (§5's mass, velocity, and kinetic energy); who may say them in what unit is settled by Pack Twenty *(R20C-24-PHYSICAL_UNIT_CEILING, `amends: null`, `applies_to: prose-law, register`)*: "Physical-unit ceiling confirmed. Joules and m/s in a Measurewright's or Guild officer's mouth; weight-and-reach for everyone else. The gap is characterisation." The floor's requirement that the mechanics be *stated* is unchanged; this governs the vocabulary the statement is made in, by speaker.

**Whose voice, at what distance — cross-references, not rules of this guide.** The four voices above say *who* states a floor item. Two rows, both live as of 2026-09-13 (one an Isaac ruling of that date, one a Pack Twenty clearance row), bear on how that voice is then written, and are quoted here so a combat writer does not have to go looking:

- *Narration distance* *(R41-1-DISTANCE_IS_TWO_AXES, `rules/doc-distance-two-axes-ruling.yaml`; amends the Scene Writing Process Guide's narration-distance section, not this guide; closes C-015)*: "Both stand as two axes. So where a row above names a Primary, it is that Primary's Sub-Stat total that is being read and its Grade is the mean; the Sub-Stats the rows name are what the points were spent on. (Dominion, which the first row reads, has its own count in R39-2-DOMINION_MEAN_OF_SEVEN, same file, not quoted here.)
- *(R39-4-MAX_GRADE_LETTER_BINDS, closes C-011)*: "The Max Grade letter binds: a Sub-Stat may not exceed the top of the Stage's Max Grade bracket (Stage V 275, VII 400, IX 550, XI 725); the band from there up to the Stage's numeric ceiling is the flagged instability zone, reachable only under strain, never by allocation." So a Sub-Stat figure above its Stage's Max Grade top is reached only under strain and never sits on the card by allocation; the Overflow, Overchannel, and Crystal Fracture row above is where the table already reads that strain.

### 7.1 Lethality, Mending, the Reserve Clock, and Death

*Folded from RULINGS.md 2026-09-26, "questionnaire-2026-09-26-combat-society-politics", answers 1 to 4. The answers are quoted first; everything beneath each is this guide's working of it, and every number in the reserve clock is the Essence Ledger's.*

**Lethality: brutally real for ordinary fighters.** *(answer 1)*: "Lethality among ordinary fighters is brutally real: one good cut or a ball in the gut can kill, often days later from infection; most fights end in the first seconds; a wounded man is out of the fight."

| Row | What it means on the page |
|---|---|
| **Most fights end in seconds** | An exchange between ordinary fighters is decided in the first two or three passes. The floor's items (§6) are written inside those seconds; the fight is not stretched to carry them |
| **One good hit is enough** | A committed cut to the neck, the inner thigh or the forearm's flexors, a thrust to the trunk, or a ball through the belly ends a man's fight on the spot, whatever he feels in the first moments. Stress analgesia (§9, body audit) may keep him upright for seconds; it does not keep him fighting |
| **The wounded man is out** | A man with a real wound stops being a fighter and becomes a casualty. He can be dragged, carried, held hostage or finished. He does not take up the sword again in the same fight unless the wound is trivial and the page says why |
| **Bleeding is on a clock** | An opened major vessel (femoral, brachial, carotid) takes a man through ATLS Class II into Class III and IV in minutes, not hours; the Class II anxiety and Class III confusion beats (§6, item 6) arrive before the pain does |
| **The gut kills later** | A penetrating belly wound is a death that arrives in days: peritonitis sets in within a day or two, fever and a rigid belly follow, and most die within the week without surgery the place does not have |
| **Infection is the second killer** | Any wound can go bad: wound sepsis in days, gas gangrene within one to three days of a dirty deep wound, lockjaw one to three weeks later. What the place's medicine can do about it is set by R53-10-MEDICINE: antisepsis and anaesthesia in the Guild cities of the later span, the barber and folk medicine in the north and among the poor |
| **Limbs are lost** | A shattered long bone in a place without modern surgery is an amputation or a death, and the amputation itself kills a real share of those it is done to, more the nearer it is to the trunk |
| **Practitioners are not exempt, only harder** | The Aether Shell and Vitality change what arrives and how fast it bleeds (§7's table: Fortitude, Hemostasis, Threshold, Tolerance). Once a wound is through, it is the same anatomy and the same clock |

**Mending: real timelines, and Vitalia costs both parties.** *(answer 2)*: "Healing runs on real timelines (weeks for a cut, months for bone). Vitalia healing can shorten it, but it costs the healer EU and the patient's body pays something (scar tissue, fever, hunger)."

| Row | What it means on the page |
|---|---|
| **A cut takes weeks** | A clean closed cut knits in one to two weeks and does not regain its strength for months. A cut that went bad takes as long as the infection takes, if it heals |
| **Bone takes months** | A simple long-bone fracture unites in six to twelve weeks at the least (longer for the shin and thigh) and is not trusted under full load for months after |
| **Muscle, tendon and nerve are slower still** | A cut tendon or a severed nerve is months of recovery and is often never whole. The table's Ledger carries the lost function forward until it is earned back |
| **Vitalia shortens; it does not skip** | A Vitalia working compresses the body's own repair into less time. The repair still happens, and the body still does it, faster |
| **The healer pays in EU** | The working is priced like any other, as a share of the healer's reserve by the Essence Ledger, and R47-11-TIME_AND_ENERGY binds it: "Healing and mending always cost more than breaking." The healer's cost is always larger than what the wound cost to inflict |
| **The patient pays in the body** | Scar tissue where the repair was forced; fever as the body runs hot through work it would have spread over weeks; hunger, because the building has to be paid for out of the patient's own stores (Rebirthine's law, catabolism paying for anabolism, is the Family's own statement of it). A patient healed fast is weak, feverish and starving for days afterward, and the scar is worse than the slow one would have been |

**The reserve clock: hard, by the Essence Ledger's numbers.** *(answer 3)*: "Essence and stamina run on a hard clock by the Ledger's numbers; long fights are won by whoever manages the reserve; Starvation hits mid-fight on overspend."

The Ledger's constant, quoted: "1 EU = 1 MJ of potential. Energy delivered = EU × η × 1 MJ. 1 AU/s = 1 MW." Its drain arithmetic, quoted: time to empty is "EU reserve ÷ (AU/s)"; time to Starvation is "0.9 × EU reserve ÷ (AU/s)"; and "One combat turn is six seconds."

| Row | What it means on the page |
|---|---|
| **Every working is a share of the reserve** | The Ledger: "A practitioner who commits to a fight spends in whole percents per exchange, and ten to twenty committed exchanges bring anyone to Starvation." A scene tracks the share, and the author notes carry the running total |
| **Full output empties fast** | The Ledger's small practitioner (Stage IV, 3,100 EU, 480 AU/s) empties in 6.46 s and is Starved in 5.81 s, one turn. Its middle practitioner (Stage XIII, 92,000 EU, 3,900 AU/s) is Starved in 21.2 s, three and a half turns. "Most reserves empty in under two minutes at full draw." AU/s caps a burst; EU ÷ duration runs a scene |
| **Starvation hits mid-fight** | Below ten percent of the practitioner's own maximum: "reduced stat expression, Shell degradation, Trait flickering." Stats read one Grade lower, Traits drop out and return as the reserve moves, and η falls as the reserve falls, so the Starved practitioner "is brighter for the same work, sheds more, delivers less" |
| **Recovery does not rescue a fight** | Passive recovery is about ten percent of maximum a minute, one percent a turn; active recovery is two to three times that and "requires the character to stop fighting and concentrate." A Starved practitioner is about ten turns of rest from being out of it. Nobody refills mid-exchange |
| **Long fights go to the manager** | "A defender who can afford to spend nothing wins a long engagement against an attacker who cannot afford to spend nothing." Making an enemy spend is making the clock run, and the page shows who is spending |
| **Stamina runs on the same kind of clock** | The body's own clock sits beside the reserve: all-out fighting is anaerobic work that fails within tens of seconds to a couple of minutes, and grip, breath and legs go in that order (§9, body audit). Starvation is not unconsciousness, and a Starved practitioner "is still a person with a sword": which is to say, an ordinary fighter under answer 1's rows |

**Death: permanent.** *(answer 4)*: "Death is almost always permanent; the only exceptions are liches, the undead and their kind."

| Row | What it means on the page |
|---|---|
| **The dead stay dead** | No working, draught, relic or rite in play returns a dead person to life. A fight that kills a named character has killed them, and R48-31-STAKES governs when that may happen: earned, from real mistakes, after clear warning |
| **The exceptions are the undead** | A lich survives by its phylactery (the Bestiary's Lich entry); the undead and their kind persist as undead. What comes back is not the person restored to life, and the page treats it that way |
| **Near-death is not death** | A man who has lost consciousness from blood loss is dying, not dead, until the clock runs out. What saves him is the ordinary work (pressure, a tourniquet, a surgeon), and Vitalia's cost rows above if a healer is there |

*Sources for the real-world rows: the Medical and Surgical History of the War of the Rebellion (penetrating abdominal and chest wound mortality; amputation mortality by site); ATLS haemorrhage classes (American College of Surgeons); standard surgical texts on wound healing phases and fracture union times; tetanus and gas gangrene incubation periods (WHO and CDC clinical summaries). Encyclopaedia-level, cross-checked once (R48-15-SOURCES).*

## 9. PROCESS — RESEARCH, THE GAP-FILL PASS, AND VOCABULARY DISCIPLINE

**Research, before writing.** Before any set piece, first display, finisher, or new working, Natalie searches, at minimum, once each for: the real phenomenon; the real weapon, injury, or physiology; and the nearest anime or CRP precedent for the shape. The Codex Spell Index and the Magical Categories page are consulted for assignment, and the author notes name what was consulted. A set piece written from memory is a failed set piece. *(R13-7-RESEARCH_RULE)*

**The gap-fill pass, before polishing.** When a fight or a working is submitted for improvement, this runs before a single sentence is polished, and the author notes carry a section headed *Added by the gap-fill pass*, itemised: *(R13-5-GAP_FILL_PASS)*

1. **Beat inventory.** Every exchange listed. Every named beat preserved. Nothing cut.
2. **Logic audit.** Read, fault, counter, and stated cause per exchange — missing ones supplied from the fighters' assigned guards and cuts.
3. **Stratum audit.** Every working checked for Aether, Wellspring, and Essence presence — missing strata supplied.
4. **Physics audit.** Measure and tempo continuous. Weapon mass and reach against the Item Guide. Armour tier named and consistent.
5. **Body audit.** Fatigue carried forward — grip failure, forearm pump, breath debt, the cut leg still cut. Stress analgesia applied and withdrawn on sight of the wound.
6. **Phenomenon audit.** Every working checked against the Ability/Technique Design Guide's phenomenon rule, or flagged.
7. **Vocabulary pass.** Checked against §3, §6, and §7 above.
8. **Prose pass, last.**

**What the pass may not do — cross-reference, not a rule of this guide.** *(R20C-27-GAP_FILL_NEVER_CHANGES_OUTCOME, `rules/pack-20-twenty.yaml`, `kind: ban`, `amends: null`, `applies_to: verification, session-protocol`)*: "A gap-fill pass may not change an outcome Isaac wrote. Flag, propose the smallest change, wait." The eight steps above supply causes, strata, and vocabulary; where no cause fits the outcome on the page, the outcome stands and the author notes carry the flag and the proposal.

**Vocabulary discipline reminder.** HEMA vocabulary (§3), anatomy and injury vocabulary (§3.6), and scaling vocabulary (§3.7) each have their own placement rules. Scaling vocabulary in particular is adjudication and author-notes only — it never reaches the page, in any character's mouth, in any register.

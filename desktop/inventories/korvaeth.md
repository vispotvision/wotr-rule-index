# THE STANDING INVENTORY: KORVAETH, THE SUNROOT REGENCY, THE VESSEL ROAD

Note: sources are the Korvaeth arc, the Eressean and Varrisak grammar, the Eresse pages and the Eastern Rise ledger; where they are silent (the table, the greeting, the burial) the plainest reading of a drowned salt coast, a northern winter and a people who enter everything is chosen and is canon here. (S) marks Sunroot Regency texture, (V) the Vessel road; the rest is Korvaeth and the Under-Houses.

Drow of the New World north, holding under the doctrine that the holding validates itself, ruled by a ledger and a count. Food: winter grain down the Vessel road, baked dense and dark; orchard fruit, fresh in season and dried on the string out of it, since the fruit and the grain sit with the six refusing houses; salt on everything, because salt is the cargo; salted stores laid down in the deep roads. Greeting: "In term?" answered "In term," the debt-marker as courtesy; hands shown open on entering a chamber, since a Reckoner watches hands; the proper bow on the way out, given even to a room one has just refused; a four-count pause before answering the Regency, which is assent (S). Oaths: "Enter it." "Before the count." "Witnessed," said once, because three times is a consecration and nobody spends that on a market debt; "On the leaf"; gold for anything sworn, since gold is the substrate of the oath, and Sacred Gold at Vaelmarr cannot lawfully be melted once sworn on (S). Insults: "unpaid," worse than absent; "out of term," lo, for a man whose debt has gone past its day; "northern edge," for a thing black on the face and pale in the grain, from the drown-alder boards cut where the salt never reached; the contingent suffix, -ama, put on a name to its owner's face; -ak tagged onto a name, outside the form, which is how Eresse made the word Varrisak (S). Time: bells, from the fourth bell of a cold yard to the ninth hour of a night attack; "before the entry closes," nine days from the count to the sealed register; a reading, eleven days and a fee; the withheld season, the stretch in which no window is granted to a name nobody stood for; "when the road opens," which is spring, and "held through a winter" (V); the Given turnings at Sunroot, Deep Dark and Returning Light said as the month (S). The dead: entered. A death cannot be voided, so the leaf is ruled and the name is either given on, uren, or falls vacant and is entered as -ama, held by nobody, a silence in a chord. The Under-Houses bury in the ward the dead were working, as Vessa Thenn-Ashal buried two sons; the Regency keeps the fire and the archive and consults the dead in neither (S). Objects: the ledger and the ruled leaf, the nib, the vault key that is the Reckoner's; the wax, and the thumb in it; the Reckoner's case and seal (V); black drown alder, dense, salt-black, in panelling and in the eleven coats; the plain pin and the four-pass coil; the truce-bough (S); Ember Flint in every field kit, struck for a mild flame; an Onceglass charge, one casting and then gravel, carried by the ward-carriers as a militia carries arrows. Exchange: the witness-fee, since an uncompensated witness has not witnessed; the forfeit, paid so an abstention is entered as one; the paid messenger, so a refusal has to be entered as a refusal; every debt is sae or lo, in term or past it, and there is no third word; salt, timber and winter grain up the road under a charter that says who may use it (V); roughgold panned by anyone and taken for oath work (S); standing built by what has been given away and accounted for. Proverbs, fixed wording: "A vacant name is a silence in a chord." "Held unwitnessed, silence. Held witnessed, a note." "Absent is a fact. Unpaid is a position." "The count does not stop for age." "The holding validates itself." "A gift can be reread. A death cannot." "A regent with a claim is a usurper waiting for a bad winter." "Once is the form. Twice is emphasis. Three times is forever." "The salt goes to the heart or the board goes back." "A road too convenient is a wound." "Ask in form and pay the messenger." "The alder is worth more drowned." Body: the Korvaeth build, deep, shoulders narrow and squared, hands too large for the arms; skin the grey-violet of a four-day bruise, hair the flat unlit white bound in one black cord; hands first, open or behind the back; weight shifting foot to foot and then stopping all at once on the word that lands; the callus on a Reckoner's forefinger; cold from the wrists inward when the shape of a thing is seen; the coil, nine passes at a coronation and four passes and a pin for a woman holding nothing; the counted silence, four for yes and nine for no, and past nine is judgement (S). **Recurrence, two minimum per Korvaeth scene:** the leaf and "enter it"; the salt and the drown alder; the count, whether of bells, witnesses or a held silence; "unpaid" and "in term"; the witness-fee, paid or the witness was never there.

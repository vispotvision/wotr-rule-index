# THE STANDING INVENTORY: MOTO

A law-house laid over other people's ground. Seven lines, one crown, no temple; the household is the unit of observance and the register is the sacred object. The Kharven Inventory holds for the clans at the Seat. This is the layer the house lays on top of it.

Food: the Seat's table is the clans' table (rye heel, salt pork boiled grey, onions fried black in the fat, rind-water, which is what the north calls broth when it is being honest). Under Muken there was rice on the high table twice a week, hauled up the corridor at a cost the Thornwalls hated, and every grain of it said "we came from somewhere, and we can still reach it." The house adds the scale: the ration is weighed on a true balance each day, and the scale is inspected publicly at Ice-out. Something is fed daily that is not yourself and cannot repay it. The broken haft is burned instead of hoarded; refusing to let a finished thing continue is the labour. Hosting means eating the host's food for three days before business, as Muken did with the Keld.

Greeting: no spoken greeting is on record. From senior to junior, a hand on the head and nothing said; the nothing is enough. When it matters, a thing is "asked in form, in the old register." Full names carry the line in the middle: Miku Tenrai Moto, Artemis Amagiri Moto, Azure Tenrai Moto. The forearm grip is the clans' and stays in the Kharven list.

Oaths: an oath is sworn onto the ground and the ground takes it. A word is given "in terms that any Reckoner in four quarters would have entered as binding." The register verb is "entered." The terminal form, full register, correct contours, before a witness: "I revoke the name Moto from you." A man revoked is entered elar-ama, held by nobody, and the name cannot be given back by anybody. The banner oath is personal to the sovereign, dies with him, and is re-sworn or withheld.

Insults: "the Empty Bow" (observance without the labour, held worse than neglect, because a man who has stopped bowing has at least stopped lying); "Petition" (asking, which converts the Bow into a transaction and the Archon into a merchant); "Singling" (elevation of one Archon, the principal heresy, and the one the Kōkan watch for in themselves); "poet" (of a swordsman who fights decoratively: "against plate, do not behave like a poet"); "a snake dressed up as a Moto"; "gifted blood you cannot live up to"; "clogged" (of a house that will not let its dead leave). Praise, rare and complete: "correct." The doctrinal ground of every insult is the same: "The offence is inaccuracy."

Time: the hinge of the day, when the Bow is made (at the Seat it has been made at table, standing, before the bread); all fourteen inclinations take under two minutes in a cold room; Ice-out, when the ground opens and the whole winter's dead go in together; "the window," the crossing-time the Kōkan bought with the Stay and nobody sailed back across; "the Hour Before Sequence," Bara's own dating for anything older than measured time; "the Long Reading," the four thousand years at the white shelf; "eleven hundred years" since six lines crossed the water.

The dead: read at Ice-out, in the order they died; to read them alphabetically would be a desecration nobody could articulate. One dead person's name said aloud daily, in a room, to someone. The Waiting is Kharven's winter institution and it is Auren's labour performed by a people who think they are being practical. A dead Moto who has been read correctly is "behind," and behind is a direction, and a direction can be faced. A house that keeps its dead in portraiture, relics and rehearsed grief is "clogged." The Queen's Saishiki went with her, so the observance survives and the binding does not, and what reads the names now is a man with a list doing his best.

Objects: the beads (Sodoku's are his father's, jade and iron worn smooth, the only object carried continuously since the exile, reached for when no one is watching; Artemis wears black prayer-beads the size of clenched fists over shoulder and arm); the doorpost with fourteen scratches, cut by people who believe they are counting something; the register, kept in the family's own hand and unreadable to any Concord clerk; the fourteen hands, and a document written in the wrong hand is void rather than incorrect; the scabbard mouth and the small final sound of the guard settling, which in Kharven meant the verdict is complete; hair tied back for the crown; the field coat, heavy and dark, wide fur collar, iron-thread seams, replaced twice; the Covenant Scar, in the shared arts and taught without comment; one thing made that serves no function, because a house with no ornament in it is in a state of quiet sin.

Exchange: the Reading (what a thing is, what it owes, and what it will become; trade routes ran to the white shelf because the alternative was doing business without knowing what your counterparty was); the debt, measurable and entered (violence against sworn banners becomes measurable debt); the word given in form; the banner oath; testimony, "the one commodity a starving kingdom can still produce in quantity."

Proverbs, fixed wording. Bara's litany, six lines: "See before you act. Name before you sever. Measure before you reconcile. Release before you cling. End only what has earned its ending. Preserve only what remains fit to endure." "To gather is easy. To gather without devouring distinction, that is mastery." "I have always liked the honesty of hammers. They are incapable of ornamental thought." "A people that migrates brings farmers and a house that migrates brings law." "Behind is a direction." "Praise is labour." "The Bow, which weighs nothing." "The offence is inaccuracy." "Heresy is not a matter of taste." "Both readings were correct. That is the whole of the problem." "A stay is what a court grants when it has not yet decided to spare you." "Slavery is arbitrary. This was correct." "A man told he is unfree by a tyrant has a grievance. A man told he is unfree by an instrument that has never once been wrong has a diagnosis."

Body: the eyes first (ring-lines in the iris at rest, three visible in Sodoku even passive, a faint flare at the outer ring when attention shifts; sigilic cracks spread through Muken's iris when the Shingan opens); the metallic birth-sheen at brow, clavicle and wrist; white hair in all seven lines and never exclusive to one; bearing (too composed, too sharply outlined, too heavy); load-bearing stillness, present even at rest; a man who moves before he decides to; the hand going to the beads under stress; the knee (Sodoku went down onto one knee for a nine-year-old and put his hand on the boy's head; a Tenrai household guard goes to a knee with weapons out and faces up to die where it stands and wishes the fact entered); the cost after a verdict, eye bleed, sternum ache, twenty to thirty seconds of emotional flattening.

**Senses:**
- *Smells:* lacquer on plate: resinous and faintly sour; oiled hair, pulled back and cut level at the nape; rice on the high table, steamed and starchy, and rare; ink from a register kept in the family's own hand: soot and glue; iron from the field coat's seams and the mail under the plate; a cold room at the hinge of the day, before the bread; wet fur on a wide collar coming in from snow; blood from the eye after a verdict, which is iron; grease and old leather where the gorget sits under the jaw; frost coming off Tōga's hide: nothing at all, and the nose freezing.
- *Sounds:* the small final sound of the guard settling into the scabbard mouth, which in Kharven meant the verdict was complete; beads moving on their cord under a thumb; a knee going down onto a stone floor; fourteen inclinations: cloth moving fourteen times and no voice; the scale's beam settling as the ration is weighed; a knife cutting a scratch into the doorpost; one word said into a quiet yard; a silence a Moto leaves for another man to fill; frost cracking in ruts ahead of the Droval; the yard going quiet in a rolling wave from where he entered.
- *Colours:* lacquered red so dark it reads black until the sun finds it; white hair, in all seven lines; the metallic birth-sheen at brow, clavicle and wrist; jade green and iron grey, worn smooth; black prayer-beads the size of fists across a shoulder; ring-lines in the iris, and the faint flare at the outer ring; a dark field coat under a wide fur collar; red running from the eye after a verdict; ink black on a page no Concord clerk can read; rice white against the clans' grey pork and black onions.
- *Textures:* beads worn smooth by a dead man's hand and then a living one; lacquer: hard, glassy, cold; fourteen scratches in a doorpost under a thumb; iron-thread seams in a heavy coat; a head under the palm: hair, and the warm skull; the brass pan of a true balance; frost in a nap over a hide whose warmth runs inward; hair tied back tight for the crown; a mail skirt rebound at the hip, the new lacing stiffer than the old; register paper, heavy, taking ink slowly.
- *Tastes:* rice, which tastes of a corridor that can still be reached; rye heel from the clans' table; salt pork boiled grey; onions fried black in the fat; rind-water; the host's food, eaten three days before any business; blood at the lip after a verdict: iron and salt; cold air in the throat at the Bow.

**Gone** (the house says *behind*, since a thing read correctly is not gone):
- **The Okuchi.** The deep interior, the land behind: the body of the world before Malakar broke it. Every other people named it for the disaster that ended it. The Moto name is the only one that does not describe an ending.
- **The white shelf.** The seat of the Long Reading for four thousand years, where a contract sealed held anywhere because the ground itself witnessed it. It is Alabaster now, and the Holy Sea stands on it.
- **The Queen's Saishiki.** The binding liturgy that made an oath structural instead of verbal, attached to the Bow and the Reading both. It went with her. What reads the names at Ice-out now is a man with a list doing his best.
- **The Kōkan who stayed.** The line that held the ground and bought the window across which six lines crossed water. The house remembers them as the line that failed it, and "the Refusal" means two opposite things depending on who is holding the word.
- **The rice on the high table.** Twice a week under Muken, hauled up the corridor at a cost the Thornwalls hated, every grain of it saying the house came from somewhere and could still reach it.

**Folk beliefs** (held in the house's rooms, by retainers and by the Moto themselves):
- **The beads hold the eye.** A lord's beads are what keep the Shingan shut, and a Moto without them will read everyone in the room. A retainer brings his lord the beads before he brings him water.
- **The doorpost keeps a lie out.** A post cut with the fourteen scratches cannot be passed by a man carrying a lie, so a guest is walked through the scratched door before business, and a household that moves cuts the new post before it unpacks.
- **White hair means the eye.** A white-haired child will open the eye, and the household watches that child first and feeds it best.
- **The revoked die within the year.** A man stripped of the name, held by nobody, does not outlast the next Ice-out.
- **A flat Moto is a spent one.** After a verdict the reader goes flat and quiet, and that is the moment to speak to him, because the eye is closed and the man is tired.

**Literacy:** Universal inside the house, in the family's own hand and in the fourteen hands, where a document in the wrong hand is void rather than incorrect. The Concord hand is read as a foreign script, accurately and without love. Retainers read little, and read the house's hand not at all.

**Recurrence, two minimum per Moto scene:** the beads, reached for when no one is watching; the fourteen inclinations and the doorpost scratches; "read" as the verb for a person; the hand on the head and nothing said; "correct" as the whole of praise, and "the offence is inaccuracy" as the whole of blame.

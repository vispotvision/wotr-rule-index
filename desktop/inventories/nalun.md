# THE STANDING INVENTORY: NALŪN, THE CHAIN-CITIES

A merchant oligarchy cut into the floor of a dead sea, where a promise sealed inside the salt is a working and the punishment for breaking it is that Nalūn stops working for you. Everything below is downstream of three facts: the cities are made of salt, salt fails in standing water, and the Wellspring under them enforces what is agreed.

**Food:** salt first, because it is on every table as seasoning, preservative and money at once, and a salt bar can be eaten, which no other money can. Salt tea in the private chambers of the factor houses, where the real positions are stated: black tea boiled with salt and butter. Salted meat and salted fish, since the city keeps what the surface sends down. Grain, iron stock and medicine come in from above and are weighed at Halcrest before anybody eats them. Bread and salt at a threshold: the host shaves a curl off a stamped bar onto a heel of bread and hands it over, and a guest who eats it has eaten the host's money.

**Greeting:** bread and salt at the door for a guest, as above. Between merchants the question is "What's your oldest?", and the answer is the seal-token on the belt, the disc for the oldest active contract, shown before the name. A merchant with no token has no history and no credit, and is greeted accordingly. Speech is Concord Common said with a precision that has become a technical dialect; a loose word in Ironlink is a small liability.

**Oaths:** an oath here is a contract. "Sealed," said of any agreement that has been executed inside the resonance; "under the Chain Light," of one sealed at the oldest walls while they glow; "on my token," the everyday emphatic. A Council member swears his seat on a Parun-bound contract that states his obligations, his term, his authority and what happens if he exceeds any of the three, and four of them have been removed by their own tokens going inert mid-session. **Insults:** every insult to a merchant's honour is a binding challenge, so they are few and chosen. "Inert," of a man whose token has gone dead; "tokenless," a man without a history; "a Rimward seal," a promise with give in it; "a man with opinions," a factor with no scribe; "a wet drain," a man who neglects what keeps his neighbours dry, and the most serious thing that can be said in the Cuttings. The Honour Challenge answers them: retraction, arbitration, or a counter-negotiation under full scrutiny, and the loser pays all costs.

**Time:** underground there is no weather, only the constant warmth of salt at rest; above, five months of snow and a short summer. The sealing, when the old walls glow. The Trial of Ten Deals, ten days, once a reign. Seven years for a scribe's apprenticeship. A contract's term, which is the only calendar a factor keeps in his head. "A bad winter," when the salt bar is worth more than a silver mark. "Since the corridors closed," for everything that has gone wrong in the Eastern Reach since the war.

**The dead:** the salt keeps them. The cutting class lays its dead in a worked-out face, and the salt takes them and does not let them go soft. A dead man's seal-token is carried to Vaultmere and shelved beside the instrument it stood for, and a token nobody carries there is a debt left standing. A merchant's funeral is also a reading of his open contracts, because they do not die with him.

**Objects:** the seal-token, a small iron or bronze disc that resonates while the contract is sound and goes inert when it is breached; the salt bar, cast to fixed weight and fineness and stamped by the cutting house; the bill of exchange, carried by an unarmed man across three jurisdictions and presented for payment; the Parun-bound instrument, inscribed, sealed in a linked token and executed inside the field; the drain, and the drainage book; the chain-bridge and the constant chain; the Great Chain Hall's lanterns on amber salt walls; the scribe's case, ink dry in the grooves; lamps cut from salt, so the light comes through the stone.

**Exchange:** credit is the product: metal does not need to travel if claims on metal can. The supply contract, a commitment to deliver at a rate, for a term, from a named source, is the most traded document in four quarters. Salt bars circulate as money across the whole northern quarter. Interest is a schedule, not a sin. Property is measured in salt: the volume the family's ancestors cut, the tunnels that cutting made, and the right to keep them from collapse. The Drainage Obligation is enforced by the Wellspring itself. The Greis way-stations on the Iron-to-Salt Corridor run under Nalūn seals.

**Proverbs, fixed wording:** "Metal does not need to travel if claims on metal can." "Take the tunnels and you hold rock." "The chain-cities do not pray. They negotiate, and the negotiation is the prayer." "A factor without a scribe is a man with opinions. A scribe without a factor is a scribe with a full calendar." "A merchant without a token is a merchant without a history, and a merchant without a history is a merchant without credit." "Where enforcement is physical rather than moral, lending stops being a sin and becomes a schedule." "The factor houses decide where things go, but the Span decides whether they get there." "Salt fails in standing water." "A war against Nalūn is a war against one's own balance sheet."

**Body:** salt-throat, the dry persistent cough of the career cutter, which is not dangerous and is never comfortable; low pressure behind the eyes near an active seal; the Deepvein old, who say they feel the Wellspring the way other people feel weather; hands cracked white at the knuckle from the salt; a scribe's ink-stained second finger; speech measured to the word, since every word in Ironlink may be held against the speaker.

**Senses:**
- *Smells:* mineral warmth; lamp oil and dried ink in the counting rooms; the sharp ozone of an active seal, felt as much as smelled; powdered halite and lamp soot at Cutwall; wet granite and boreal resin on the wind above.
- *Sounds:* the chain at Bridgemarch, a low rhythmic clank that becomes, for the people who live there, a kind of silence; salt-throat coughing down a work gallery; a token that has gone inert, which makes no sound at all where it used to hum; a stamp on a bar; the Honour Challenge's prescribed transactions read aloud.
- *Colours:* translucent amber salt walls; the Chain Light, a faint amber that does not flicker and needs no fuel; Halcrest's pale salt cut with visible bedding planes; the iron and bronze of tokens; white rime on a cutter's boots.
- *Textures:* salt walls, smooth where hands have passed and sharp where they have not; a bar's stamped face under the thumb; a warm token and a cold one; the dry heat of the deepest chambers, the temperature of a body at rest.
- *Tastes:* salt in everything, including the air; salt tea; bread and salt at a threshold; salted fish.

**Gone:**
- **The sea.** The ancient sea whose dried floor the cities are cut from, in an era nobody alive can date. Its seasonal chemistry is still there in the layers, a year in every band of halite, and nobody has counted them to the end.
- **The four chairs.** The Council seats of the four members whose tokens went inert mid-session when the Wellspring found they had breached the terms of their office. The chairs were refilled; the four are named in the kingdom's record only by what they broke.
- **The worked-out faces.** Cutwall moves through the basin as each face is exhausted, and leaves behind galleries where whole families once cut, stripped of scaffolding and rail, dry and dark.
- **The eastern convoys.** The salt, iron stock and medicine that used to run out along the corridors to the mountain settlements before the war closed the roads. The settlements have had nothing for months, and the Eastern Reach says so on record.

**Folk beliefs:**
- **A breaker shows on his face.** A man who has broken a sealed contract can be told by anyone who looks at him, so a merchant studies a stranger's face at length before dealing.
- **A Nalūn seal has never failed.** A contract sealed under Council authority cannot break, and nobody in the chain-cities insures against one.
- **The brighter the light, the better the deal.** The Chain Light shows the worth of what is being sealed, so parties pay to seal at the oldest walls and watch the glow.
- **Water drinks a contract.** A cup spilled near a sealing voids it, so nobody drinks in a sealing room and scribes blot with salt.
- **Salt-throat is protection.** A cutter who coughs is safe from the pressure behind the eyes, so coughing men are sent down to Deepvein first.

**Literacy:** High in the factor and agent classes, who read a bill and a supply book before anything else. Drafting an instrument that will be sealed is reserved to scribes, each of whom has served seven years before being allowed to. The cutting class reads the stamp on a bar and the token on a belt more readily than a page.

**Recurrence, two minimum per Nalūn scene:** the seal-token and "what's your oldest"; the salt bar that can be eaten; the drain and "salt fails in standing water"; the Chain Light; the constant chain.

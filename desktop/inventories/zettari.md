# THE STANDING INVENTORY: THE ZETTARI

The Sovereign House of Kushara and the smiths of a line whose body is its first throne. They sit on thin ground, and their culture is the discipline of working a poor draw well: their site was worked longest and gave out first. They are the best smiths in the four quarters because they had to be, and insufferable about it because being best is the only thing that ever compensated them. A vow is not complete unless something older than the speaker hears it.

**Food:** eaten standing, at the fire. Flat bread cooked on the forge shoulder, sour and dense. Sitting down to a meal on a working day is a statement. At Kushara the Malani barges bring grain, river fish, preserved meat, fruit and oils to the capital without pause, and the Market of Twelve Roads has every region's table in it, but a Zettari household on a working day still eats on its feet. Spiced tea in a brass pot at the end of a shift, drunk hot enough to hurt.

**Greeting:** "Who has your fire?" Whose hearth you draw on: courtesy, status check, and who is responsible for you, in four words. A Zettari shakes hands palm-up, so the other man sees the hand before he takes it. Between a Zettari and a Yukari, a nod: not a bow, not a salute. A younger person greets an elder with the formal respect-word and waits to be answered before straightening.

**Oaths:** sworn on a quench, and the water is kept. Quench-barrels are a house's record, stacked and dated; a barrel poured out is a vow broken in public. Important vows are spoken before stone, ruins, ancestral halls or a Path-linked relic, because a vow is not complete unless something older than the speaker hears it. At the Witnessed Temper a bound claim is spoken over a finished blade at the quench, before a Stone Witness, and the blade keeps it. **The obscenity is "cold."** Anything made without fire is a fraud, and calling a man's work cold starts fights. Other insults: "clean skin," one with no ink, who has survived no threshold; "kneeler," one who gives way under another's weight.

**Time:** heats, not hours. "Three heats back." "It'll be two heats." The week does not exist as a unit. The river keeps the year: Malani's Flood Calendar, and the Nahal's annual rise across the floodplain. "Two hundred thousand years," said of the line the way other people say "always."

**The dead:** cremated in the forge, and the ash goes into the next quench. Every Zettari blade carries somebody, and a smith can usually tell you who. The Ancestral Halls keep what the fire does not take: the family cords, the weapons, the witness tablets, the royal oaths and the records of the dead. At a death the heir takes a bead of the ash onto a cord, the ember bead, and carries it. The sovereign dead are accepted into the Paths, which keep them as they were at the moment of death, unedited and unfinished.

**Objects:** the quench-barrel, stacked and dated; the tally-stick of heats owed, since fire is lent constantly between houses and a drawer of sticks in your favour is real wealth and everyone knows the count; the Cord of Becoming, beads and ornaments added for growth, battle survived, grief, vows, marriage, inheritance and duty; the ink of the Living Ancestor, patterned tattoos after each threshold; the witness tablet; Agano Sand, a working measure of the sand of the Paths, folded into a quench and only ever handed over by someone the Paths already know; the ember bead; gold and silver worn heavy; the heavy drum; carved wooden birds on a nursery floor.

**Exchange:** they sell hours of the fire, never objects. The unit of account is the heat. Accord factors find the pricing incomprehensible, which is why Zettari work sells badly abroad. The tally-stick settles what is owed between houses. Agano Sand cannot be bought, only sponsored. Vokari gold underwrites the sovereign coinage; Zembari water decides whether the gold reaches anywhere; corridor merchants in Zettari territory trade under Nalūn instruments and both sides pretend the jurisdiction question does not exist.

**Proverbs, fixed wording:** "Cold makes nothing." "The barrel remembers." "A borrowed fire is a borrowed name." "You cannot hurry a heat and you cannot slow a life." "Ask the hands, not the man." "Thin ground, deep work." "The body is the first throne. The blood is the first oath. The ancestor does not die while the living still stand." "The blade does not punish the thief. It declines to know him." "Seven pillars hold the continent. The Zettari Crown decides what they hold together." "Armies see armies. Caravans see everything else." "Gold buys water everywhere else. In Zafaran, water decides whether gold reaches anywhere."

**Body:** hands are a curriculum vitae and are read as one: burn-scatter dates the apprenticeship, and scar patterns say what he has worked and at what temperature. Concealing your hands is close to a confession. Six to seven feet and more, dense-boned, heavy-handed, grounded; footsteps like drums beneath stone. Eyes silver or dark blue, pitch-black the rarest, bleeding at the corner after long use. Tattoos that record what the body endured. When the line's weight rises, the air around them seems to kneel.

**Senses:**
- *Smells:* forge heat and quench steam; wood ash and char; sour bread on the forge shoulder; hot iron scale; clean mineral air; spiced tea.
- *Sounds:* the quench's hiss; the hammer on a working day; heavy drums; footsteps like drums beneath stone; musicians on the Bridge of Evening Voices at sunset; the Singing Dunes.
- *Colours:* gold and silver; dark robes; white eyes when they wake; black hair with a metallic sheen; Kushara's reddish-brown earth and golden building stone; candlelit boats on the River Gardens.
- *Textures:* a palm-up handshake, calloused and scarred; beads on a cord, worn smooth by thumbs; stacked barrel staves; warm sand; ink ridges on old skin.
- *Tastes:* sour dense flat bread; quench-water on the lips at the forge; river fish; spiced tea; ash in the air of a working hall.

**Gone:**
- **The First World.** The land before Malakar broke it. The Zettari name for it is a forge-culture's name: the one we can no longer reforge.
- **The first fire.** The site their ancestors worked longest, which gave out first. Every heat since has been worked on thinner ground than that one, and "thin ground, deep work" is its epitaph.
- **The Field of Melted Kings.** A stretch of the Blackglass Basin fused to black glass by ancient eruption and old warfare, where the surface can look solid over hollow ground. Whose kings they were is not in any mortal record.
- **Dougou's answer.** Dougou Ozumu Zettari, the Iron Bison, died with the question of what he was still unanswered. The Paths accepted him and keep him as he was at the moment of death, unedited and unfinished. His son carries the bead.

**Folk beliefs:**
- **Pitch-black eyes belong to the Paths.** A child born with pitch-black eyes has been claimed by the Paths, and the family gives it to the Ancestral Halls to be watched.
- **Grandfather's ash will not cut its own.** A blade quenched with a family's ash will not wound that family, so the old blades are taken into family quarrels without fear.
- **The dunes are singing ancestors.** The Singing Dunes of the Zafaran are the dead of the line, and a caravan stops and answers them before it goes on.
- **Look at the feet.** Meet a royal Zettari's eye and your breath will stop, so a commoner looks at the royal's feet.
- **Fear the one the gauges call asleep.** A man whom the instruments read as dormant is a sealed monster, so line-soldiers give quiet men room.

**Literacy:** The court, the Pillar houses and their archives read and write: witness tablets, royal oaths, the Flood Calendar, the Obaru medical archive. The common record is carried on the body and in the forge, and every Kusharan reads a cord, a tattoo and a stack of barrels more readily than a page.

**Recurrence, two minimum per Zettari scene:** the quench-barrel; the tally-stick; "who has your fire"; "cold" as the standing insult; hands read like a record.

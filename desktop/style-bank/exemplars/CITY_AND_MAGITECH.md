# City and magitech exemplars (provisional)

Few-shot bank for the Draw Age city, bright, loud and crowded with magitech, and for the wilds that stand against it. Method: the world shown through objects in use; ambient noise that is not the plot; specific coin, brass, slate, mains and seals; color first and weather only where earned; a place a person could be lost in.

All people are invented for the example. Take the discipline, not the images.

<example name="a-district-on-the-mains" teaches="a bright working street: layered senses, prices and licenses named, noise that belongs to the place">
Tallow Row ran downhill to the river in a long, crowded curve, and at the sixth bell every standpipe on it was lit. Each one stood at its corner like a brass heron, gauge-face glowing a clean white, its needle flickering a hair's breadth above the green. The Guild seal was struck on the base of every pipe in blue enamel, and below the seal, in a small cramped hand that someone had added with a nail, *DONT LEAN.* The mains hummed under the cobbles in a note you stopped hearing in a week and noticed again the first night you slept in the country.

A ratcatcher was selling charms off a tray, copper tokens stamped with a mouse. A girl in a flour-white apron passed with a tray of hot plum buns on her head, shouting the price over and over at one pitch, *a penny-ha'penny, a penny-ha'penny,* and a man leaned out of a third-floor window to drop two coins to her and was handed a bun on a long hook. Overhead the street-lamps came on in a rolling wave, one after another, up the hill, each switching from cold white to warm gold as the district's draw settled, and for a moment the whole row looked like a string of beads being slowly pulled through a hand.

The chandler's shop at the bottom had a notice in the window: *LICENSED FOR 8 DRAW-UNITS. NO MORE. THE GUILD COUNTS.* Under it, smaller, someone had written *so do we.*
</example>

<example name="the-silence-of-a-cut-off-district" teaches="the draw-hum gone: absence as description, what people do in the dark, bright things made dim">
The hum stopped at nine minutes past the eleventh bell, and nobody on Gray Ford Street knew what had changed until a child said it out loud.

"It's gone quiet."

It had. The note under the cobbles, the one that had run through every night of their lives, was gone, and in its place was a silence so large that people stepped into it carefully, as into a cold room. The standpipes died one by one, white to yellow to a dull red that faded like a coal, and the lamps strung over the market went out in a ripple from the river end, and the last thing lit on the whole street was the glass of the apothecary's window, where a candle had been standing all evening as it did every night for the dead.

Then the street began to make its own sounds. A shutter banged. Somebody found a lantern, and oil flared in it, the smell of the old country, burned fat and rapeseed, and the people who had grown up with it gasped to smell it like a long-lost cousin. Voices came out of the dark, many, low, overlapping. *Is it the whole district? Is it the Guild? Has someone cut the line?* On the Row above, a bell began to ring, and then another, and a woman's voice said very clearly, to nobody, "I told them. I told them we were drawing too much."
</example>

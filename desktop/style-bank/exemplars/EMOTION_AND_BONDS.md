# Emotion and relationship exemplars (provisional)

Few-shot bank written 2026-10-30 from Isaac's answers: emotion plays anime-big, said and shown; grief gets full weight and room; warmth before a loss is shared celebration, color and noise; the stories lean on rivals who become allies, mentors and students, a found crew, and slow-burn romance short of the explicit.

All people are invented for the example. Take the method, not the images or the lines.

<example name="anime-big-grief-in-the-fight" teaches="open, loud, theatrical feeling that is still specific: the shout, the body, what the hands do while the mouth breaks">
"YOU DON'T GET TO DIE!"

Dagan had Pim's head on his knee and the other hand pressed flat over the hole in the boy's side, and the blood came up between his fingers in a hot, rhythmic welling that he could feel keeping time with the pulse he was begging to stay. His voice broke on the second word and kept going anyway, cracked and enormous, rolling off the walls of the empty guildhall. "YOU DON'T GET TO LEAVE ME WITH THIS! I am not good at this! I never learned it, you know I never learned it, you were supposed to be the one who knew what to do!"

Tears ran down into the filth on his face and cut clean tracks through it. He did not wipe them. He hadn't the hands to spare. Pim's lashes fluttered, and a dry little breath of a laugh came out of him, and Dagan wept harder at that than at anything yet, huge heaving sobs that shook the boy's whole body in his lap, and he bent over until his forehead touched the boy's and said, in a voice no louder than the draught under the door, "Please. Please. I'll learn it. I'll learn the whole thing. Just stay and teach me."
</example>

<example name="grief-given-room" teaches="the scene stays with the loss: small physical things, memory, no moral at the end">
Marit stood in the doorway of the room and did not go in.

The bed had been made. That was the first thing she saw and it undid her more than the empty chair would have, because she had made her own bed every morning of her life with a grudge, and he had never once made his, and somebody, the landlady, a neighbor, a kindly stranger, had come in and drawn the blanket square and folded the corner back. The lamp on the sill was burned down to a stub. His boots were by the door with the left one on its side, as he'd always left it, and she stood and looked at the boot and thought that she would have to step over it, and could not.

She stayed there until the light changed. A cart went by in the street, and a dog, and a woman called a child in to supper, and the sound of every ordinary evening came through the window and passed over her as if she were a stone in a river. She remembered the way he had whistled while he shaved, flat on every third note. She remembered what he had said the last morning, which was about bread. To step into the room was to agree that the bed was empty and the whistling would not come back, and she was not yet ready to agree to it.

At the end, when the stub of the lamp finally drowned in its own wax and went out, she found she was still standing, and that her hand was around the door-frame, and that the wood under her palm had gone quite warm.
</example>

<example name="the-celebration-before-the-loss" teaches="shared joy as color and noise and bodies: the bond shown by how they take up room together">
They had won, and Tessaly meant to be told about it for the rest of her life.

The whole company spilled into the Cinder Market square at the eleventh bell and the festival met them like a wave. Glass lanterns swung on their strings, red and saffron and a green like the inside of a beetle, and somebody had dragged out a barrel-organ and was grinding something fast and bright and entirely off the key. Pim was on a table already, in his apron, arms spread, being applauded by a man who sold copper pots. The baker came out with a tray of hot plum buns the size of fists and handed them out without taking a penny, and a girl of about ten went down the line pressing sugared chestnuts into every hand she could find. Marit threw her arms round Tessaly's neck, hard, hard enough that the old shoulder hurt, and Tessaly laughed into her hair and did not mind.

Later they sat in a heap on the steps of the standpipe, shoulders touching, thirteen people with one coat between six of them, and someone began the song. It was the one Tessaly always got wrong, the verse about the heron, and she got it wrong again, loudly, on purpose, and Pim fell off the step laughing, and the lamps swung, and above all of them the kites of oilpaper tugged on their strings in the warm gold dark.
</example>

<example name="a-teacher-and-a-student" teaches="affection shown as pressure and standards: the lesson that costs, the pride that cannot be said">
Oskrin made her hold the stone until her shoulder began to shake.

It was river-flint, gray and the size of a baker's loaf, and Hettrun held it out at arm's length over the practice-yard with her feet planted and her teeth clenched and the sweat running into her eyes, and her master sat on the bench with his bad leg out straight and said nothing at all. He said nothing for a hundred breaths. At two hundred the stone began to weigh as much as a cartwheel. At two hundred and thirty her arm dropped an inch, and she saw his eyes move, and the arm came back up, and she heard a small dry sound which in anyone else would have been a laugh.

At three hundred she dropped it.

It hit the dirt between her boots and lay there. She was crying with rage. "Again," Oskrin said. "The stone isn't tired."

She picked it up. Nobody told her that three hundred was better than anyone had held it in the yard that year, and nobody would. When she took her place on the bench that evening there was a bowl of soup waiting that she had not made, and a heel of bread, and the soup was hot.
</example>

<example name="a-slow-burn" teaches="romance short of the explicit: shared work, a hand held at the worst hour, what neither says">
They mended the net together because there was no one else to do it, and neither said that this was an excuse.

Eira worked from the left and Fenn from the right, and the long gray mesh lay across their knees on the harbor wall and the needles went in and out in the failing light. Their hands met in the middle of the tear, as hands do. She held the knot while he pulled it tight. He held the twine while she cut. Once the line slid, and his fingers closed over hers to stop it, and neither of them moved them for the length of three breaths, and a gull screamed on the quay, and a boy dropped a bucket in the water further down, and the whole bay turned the color of the inside of a shell.

"You'll want to do the next one tighter," Fenn said.

"I'll do it as I like."

"I'm sure you will." He took his hand away. It was a very small movement, and it cost him something that she watched go across his face and decided not to see, and after a minute she reached over and pushed the needle back into his hand, with her fingers over his, and told him to hold the twine properly.
</example>

# Quiet, travel and downtime exemplars (provisional)

Few-shot bank for slow scenes that still carry colour, voice and want. Method: a person who wants something small and specific; the road or the room as a place with its own life; the mind moving through the passage; wit and warmth in the talk; a lived-in room. Quiet is not grey. Where a scene is quiet, the volume is set low on purpose and one thing is allowed to be loud.

All people are invented for the example. Take the discipline, not the images.

<example name="on-the-ridge-road" teaches="a wild landscape with colour and strangeness, travelling talk with two voices, a small want">
The Ridge Road ran along the spine of the hills for three days, and on the second morning the whole valley below them was full of cloud, and the cloud was pink.

"It's the moss," said Brannoch, who had walked it twice. "On the north slopes. It blooms for a week in the thaw. Pollen's so thick the sun comes through it like a lamp through a rose." He shifted the pack on his shoulder and pointed with his chin at the long ragged ledge beyond, where something huge and slow was moving through the pink in a way that made it ripple. "Don't look at that. That's a stillwhale. Harmless. Dim as a doorstep."

"It's flying," said Tove.

"It's floating. There's a difference, and you'll learn it when it floats over you." He grinned at her expression. She was sixteen and carrying everything she owned in a sack on her back, and she was trying to look as if she walked past flying whales every morning. He had done the same at her age with a different whale. "Eat something. You'll not be hungry later and you'll be sorry at noon."

She ate the dry bread he handed her, watching the whale, and the whale turned its long head and looked at her with one dark patient eye the size of a cartwheel, and went on being pink.
</example>

<example name="downtime-in-a-rented-room" teaches="a lived-in room, work as the scene, voice in the muttering, the price of things named">
The room over the baker's cost four pence a night, which was three pence more than it was worth, and Hild had talked the baker down from sevenpence by knowing, in the same breath, the price of rye flour in three towns. She sat now on the edge of the cot with the ledger across her knees and her boots off, and the smell of rising dough came up through the floor.

"Bread, three loaves, nine," she muttered. "Rope, ten fathoms, two shillings. Salt, a pound. Salt, a *pound,* when I could have had it for tuppence at the river. Highway robbery, that." She licked her pencil. Under the cot lay the dull pale shape of her father's sword in its oilcloth, and she did not look at it. "A new hinge for the satchel, a penny. A boy to carry the satchel, nothing, because he works for a hinge."

Downstairs the baker's wife was singing to the dough, a flat, comfortable, tuneless song that went round and round the same four notes, and every time it came back to the first note, a mouse in the wall answered with a squeak. Hild put down the pencil and listened to it for a while. Then she wrote *rooms, 4d,* and under it, smaller, *worth it.*
</example>

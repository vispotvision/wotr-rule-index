# Technique display exemplars (provisional)

Few-shot bank for the first display of a working. Written 2026-10-30 from Isaac's technique-description answers: mechanism in motion; a visible charge before the effect; hue, sound, smell or taste, touch and the user's body, with the target and the street added as the technique grows; the Wellspring's own signature (`desktop/style-bank/wellspring-signatures.md`); the Stage showing in clarity and scale.

All people here are invented for the example. Learn the method, take no images, and write each working in its own place with its own people.

<example name="exuroth-stage-v-small-working" teaches="a small working: two channels, the charge, the cost in the user">
Hallam drew the half-sword from the scabbard and held it in the rack-light, and the steel began to change colour from the guard outward. Cherry first, a dull deep red working down the fuller toward the point, then a straw-yellow behind it, then plum, then a thin cold blue that ran along the edge like a line of ink. The temper colours of a blade in the fire, climbing his sword a hand's breadth at a time. The smell of quench oil came up off it, and steam, though nothing had touched it.

Then the blue went through the whole blade at once and he plunged the heat out of it. *Tssss.* The metal shuddered in his fist, hard as a bar of ice now and singing at a single high note, and the shock of that cooling went up his arm into the teeth. They ached to the roots. Pain climbed his forearm in a long rising wave and flattened into a dull, solid thing he could carry, and the next cut, when it came, went through the training post and its iron strap in one stroke, with a sound like a dropped plate.
</example>

<example name="judicium-stage-iv-reading" teaches="a non-combat working: sound and smell carry it, the cost is in the eyes">
The examiner set the sealed letter on the slate and laid two fingers beside it, and the air over the wax took on a thin blue edge, arc-blue, the blue of the gap between two terminals. A fine tick began, like a clock under a cloth. Hot copper rose off the table.

Colour came out of the seal in lines. Fringes of it, crimson and green and a violet like a deep bruise, each one a thin straight bar crossing the wax at its own height, and the examiner read them as a miller reads flour. "Tallow, lampblack, a little lead," she said. "Fresh. This was pressed this month and aged with a candle." Her eyes had begun to ache from the inside, a hot pinch behind each lid, because every object in the room was trying to report its composition at once, and she held her attention on the wax the way a woman holds a crowd off a door. The letter had been sealed in a kitchen.
</example>

<example name="solfatara-stage-vii-signature" teaches="a signature working at city scale: all channels, the user, the target, the street, the cost">
She pressed her palm to the paving of the Salt Market and the stones remembered where the ground had been hollow.

The charge came up through the cobbles first: a yellow crust, brittle as sugar, blooming on the joints between the stones, then a thread of steam from each one, then a hiss that rose through the whole square as every stall-keeper looked down. A deep sulphurous red lit the cracks from underneath, the red of a fumarole mouth in a mountain, and the smell hit a heartbeat after it, rotten eggs and hot stone and a bitter acid that coated the back of the tongue. The pressure behind her sinuses climbed until her eyes watered. She felt every cell of the ground beneath the market begin to give up what it had been holding.

The fumarole opened under the tax-collector's boots with a bang like a cannon in a cellar. *WHUMPH-SSSSHHH.* A white column of steam and yellow gas stood twenty feet in the air and bent over the stalls, and the two men on the dais went into it coughing and clawing at their throats. In the street beyond, a hundred people stood with their hands over their mouths, unable to look away from the colour of the thing, and a child's kite caught the heat and curled to ash in the air above them. The pressure behind her eyes finally broke. A thread of blood ran from her left nostril into her lip, and she tasted iron over the sulphur.
</example>

<example name="thalore-stage-viii-pressure-domain" teaches="a working that fills a room: the user, the target, the witnesses, then the whole district">
The Hall of the Treasury was a quiet room for a fight, and it got quieter. A teal came into the air that was not light so much as depth, the colour of the sea at the point where it forgets the sun, so dark it was nearly black, and every lamp in the hall seemed to be burning at the bottom of a well. Then the pressure arrived. It came as a groan from the roof-timbers first, a long low complaint of oak under a weight it had not been built for, and ears began to pop all over the room, in pairs, in a ragged crackle of small relief and then sharp pain.

The grandmother who had made it stood at the head of the table with her hands folded, and the cold brine-smell of the deep rolled off her. She had not raised her voice. She did not need to. Chairs sank. Cups sank. The three clerks nearest her went to their knees with their mouths open, their lungs squeezed down to the size of fists, and the captain of the guard, a man who had held a breach in the north for a month, could not lift his sword from its scabbard because the scabbard weighed as much as an anvil. Her pulse, he guessed, was slower than his by half. Outside, down in the lower town, every bell in the quarter gave a single flat clang of its own accord and stopped, and the people in the street felt their ears seal shut, and some of them wept, and none of them could say why.
</example>

<example name="transference-stage-vi-paired" teaches="a working between two bodies: sympathy as the sound, the glow as the tell, what each of them feels">
They stood back to back on the cart with the wounded man between them and the sword-thrust in his belly, and Ysmay put her hand to the wound and her other hand into her brother's.

Two bells began to ring in the air, a high one and a low one, a half-tone apart, and the sympathy between them built until the cart-boards hummed. A median violet came into the space between their palms, hard to look at, its edges refusing to settle, the way the corner of a room looks when you have been awake too long. The man's wound began to glow, and the glow moved. It slid along their joined hands as water slides along a rope, out of the thrust-hole in the wounded man's belly and into her brother's, and from her brother's palm to her own, and each of them felt it as it passed: a warm, slow draining from the body that was giving, a wide swollen fullness in the body that was taking, and in the middle of it all the strange sensation of being a pipe.

The hole closed. The skin came across it like cloth being drawn up. And Ysmay's brother, white to the lips, sat down on the tailboard with a sword-wound opening in his own side, no bigger than a coin, and said, in a quite ordinary voice, "I'll want a bandage for that."
</example>

<example name="terranova-stage-v-mending" teaches="a utility working: the cost is attention and patience, the signature is limestone and rain">
He pressed the cracked lintel with both hands and waited, and the stone began to smell of rain.

A pale limestone colour rose in the crack, the colour of the inside of a shell, and a dampness came into the dry air with the smell of wet earth after a downpour. A tiny sound started up inside the lintel, a patient trickle and creak, like a house settling on a cold night. Down the length of the fracture a thin white line of new material began to thicken, laid down grain by grain, the way an oyster lays a pearl, and the stone on either side of it drew together with a soft grinding. His hands were cold. They were also steady, because he had learned long ago that the trick was to want nothing, and to let the stone mend in its own time.

It took the whole afternoon. When the sun came in low through the window, the lintel was whole, and a faint pale ridge ran across it where the crack had been, and the old mason found he had not eaten since noon.
</example>

<example name="nihiloth-stage-ix-null-field" teaches="the extreme end: absence as the display, sound failing as the tell, the cost as hollowness">
Nothing happened, and then a sphere of it spread across the courtyard.

The tell was the sound. A rooster in the yard of the next house, mid-crow, stopped as though a hand had closed on it, and then the nearest cart-wheel went silent on the cobbles, and the great bell of the Customs House, which had been tolling the sixth hour, dwindled in the middle of a stroke to a pale shiver and then to nothing. A circle of silence rolled outward from the woman at the gate, thirty paces wide, and every thing that entered it lost something. Voices. The crackle of the torches. The weight of the rain, which fell into it and arrived as silent drops that did not splash.

She was an absolute dark, black-violet gone past the point of colour, the shape of a woman cut from the evening. There was no smell. There was no pressure on the skin, no temperature, only an emptiness the exact size of the field, and the guards who stepped into it felt their own hearts go quiet in their ears. Their swords would not ring. Their curses made no air. She herself stood in the middle of it a little bowed, hollow-eyed, with the look of someone who has put down a heavy thing and cannot remember what it was.
</example>

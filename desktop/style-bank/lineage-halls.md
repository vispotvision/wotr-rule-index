# Style Sample Bank: The Lineage Halls

These are worked examples of the R70 style law for the Chinese-register lineage halls, to be read before writing in them. Every hall, person and event here is an illustration, not canon, unless Isaac adopts it. The halls are the houses whose continuity is a record (scholar-physicians, Measurewright families, alchemical houses, trading lineages), and the book is the house (R23-2-CHINESE_STRATUM_SUMMARY; Inner World Naming Amendment §X). The Eastern strand leads their narration, interiority and structure, the Western strand their fights, wounds and talk, and LitRPG their Stages (R70-1, R70-3). What tilts the halls is that the record frames every beat: a killing is entered before the blade is wiped, a re-assay goes into the book line for line off the tape, a refusal is an omission kept true. Cadence runs in matched pairs, a clause answered by one of like length and grammar (R70-21); outside set pieces the run rule still holds (R70-19). The antique dial is period (R70-27), for "a bureaucracy with a very long memory" (Inner World Naming Amendment §X, Phonotactics). Wenyan is the learned layer, real and romanised (R70-26). Narration never glosses it (R70-25, R4-13-ZERO_BUDGET); its meaning comes in on a mouth or on the scene. The eye notices the record first (R70-44). It reads a seal, a hand, an entry or its gap, and the generation syllable in a stranger's name, which settles seniority before anyone speaks (§X, Generation character). Address is native and plain: shifu, shixiong, shijie, qianbei (R70-107). Shifu rides into a disciple's close narration (R70-118), and titles stack at the step (R70-117). Adults go by the courtesy name, and the given name belongs to parents, teachers and the very senior (§X), so a slide to it is a level shift the room hears; translated forms come out only at court, ceremony and insult (R70-108). Comic names take the four forms R70-125 gives the halls: mocked studio names, insulting posthumous names, cheap child-names and spoken homophone puns, and an English rendering of one rides an in-world bridge, a mouth or a byname carried in English (R70-123). No Standing Inventory exists for the halls. Texture comes from the Inner World Naming Amendment, the Lu Sanjing, Qiu Yinzhi and Xu Deming cards, The Night Watch, What Things Cost and FOW's Stage table. The Night Watch gives the coil at the left wrist, the wire and the calibration against the standard. The rest was supplied, and is listed as pending at the end.

## 1. A fight or a working

The Tan godown stood on the river with its loading door open to the night, and Tan Huaishan sat on the hall's chest with the jian across his knees. The chest held eleven years of draw contracts bought forward under the Tan seal. The man who wanted them had sent word of the hour.

Pressure came in before he did. The lamp over the tally desk leaned away from the door. In the tea chests the rats went still. The hum in the conduit dropped a tone, and only then did Huaishan's eye begin to slide off the doorway, the way an eye slides off a page in a script it was never taught.

A man stood in the door in a good grey coat, with a cane. Flourishing, Huaishan read him, or near it: one Stage over his own Ascension. Nothing on that body would read past B, and his own ceiling was C, and the manual gave the gap of a single letter a whole page.

The cane came apart and was a blade.

He came in off a Western stance with a ci at the eyes. Huaishan hung the jian point-down and let the thrust run off its flat past his ear, the gua the Second Form teaches first, then stepped through into gong bu and drew a mo across the man's wrist. The sleeve took it. There was mail under the good grey wool.

The second ci came faster. Huaishan was late by the width of a breath, and the point opened his brow at the hairline. Blood ran warm into his left eye.

He gave the bind on the third, as the Fourth Form of Liu Shui Bu Fu, Flowing Water Does Not Rot, asks of a man already hurt: let the other blade come, keep the line soft, and go in through the place the other arm has just left.

For the length of that bind the godown held still. Outside, the river ran black under the moon, and the far quay's lamps lay on the water in a line that broke and mended and broke. Tar smoked in the caulkers' pot on the quay. The wet tea in the chests smelled of hay.

Then the grey man's blade slid off the bind, and the jian went in above his collar, over the right collarbone where the mail stopped, and down through the subclavian.

He sat down among the chests, bleeding bright and very fast. The manual gave a wound there less than a cup of tea's time. Huaishan knelt and pressed it down against the first rib anyway, his left eye shut on the blood. "Your name," he said. "For the book."

The man laughed once, wetly, and gave him none.

Before he wiped the blade, Huaishan entered the dead in the tally book in his own hand: one man, nameless, of no hall, by the Fourth Form. Shifu said a man who kills and leaves it unentered has killed twice. Then he cleaned the jian on his own sleeve. The coat was evidence.

## 2. Court and talk

It was the Airing, and the Pei hall's book lay open in the courtyard sun, every volume propped on trestles so the worm would find nothing dark to live in. Pei Wenlan sat at the end of the trestles with the brush and the day-record.

The Banner House steward had the guest's chair and a cup of tea he had not touched, and beside him stood the man he had brought, announced at the gate as Pei Jie. In the name was a gap. The syllable of Wenlan's own generation should have sat there. Her tongue found the place the way a tongue finds a lost tooth. Then the rest of him: the hall's long jaw under a Northern crop, a Banner coat of undyed wool, a signet on the wrong hand.

The porter gave the Elder all his titles at the step: Elder of the Book, Senior Brother of the Shou generation, first disciple of the late Hall Master.

"The House asks that this man's son be entered," the steward said. "In the book, in the poem, with his generation. The House will carry the hall's bill for a year at trade supply, five gold the month. Sixty, paid at the counter. It's what the House has."

The Elder set his cup down. "The Banner House is generous, and this unworthy hall is grateful. Yet the masters left us a line older than the House. Ming bu zheng, ze yan bu shun. Where the name is wrong, speech will not run true, and where speech will not run true, nothing done under it will stand. Twenty years ago this hall entered an omission in that book, in my own hand, on a morning like this one, with the volumes open to the sun. The omission is true. A true entry is the one thing in this courtyard that cannot be bought, and the House has come with sixty gold to buy it."

"Don't saddle a horse you cannot feed," the steward said. "The House can't feed the boy through another winter. He's eleven."

A saying answered by a saying was courtesy in a hall. The Elder answered in verse, which was more than courtesy, and waited for a couplet back:

> Ink that has left the brush does not return to it;
> the hand that let it go may lift the brush again.

"I don't follow," said the steward.

Two copyists at the near trestle bent over their leaves as if the leaves had moved, and Wenlan knew the kitchens would have it by dark.

The Elder looked past him at the fostered man. "Wenjie," he said.

The courtyard heard it. A copyist at the far trestle stopped with a leaf half turned. It was the given name with the syllable back in it, said as an elder kinsman says it, a privilege twenty years unused, and the man in the Banner coat went red to the hairline.

Wenlan wrote nothing under the date. Beside her the book lay open to the sun with the omission in it, one clear line in the Elder's hand, and under the line a hand's breadth of clean paper.

## 3. Quiet and travel

Book-Close had come. Far up, the ridge carried its snow to the edge of the sky. Below it the district's standpipes came down the slope in their row, each in a cap of white, to the river town and the black water that had not yet frozen. Nearer, the rail ran along the river on its embankment. Nearer still, in a third-class carriage, Gu Yongmo sat with the coil case on his knees. His granddaughter slept against the window, her hair in a child's two tails, years short of the double coil, and her breath fogged a circle on the glass that shrank each time she drew it back. The fare was four silver for the hundred miles. He had paid it from his own purse, because the Guild had stopped paying it in autumn, when it was settled that this would be his last circuit.

The assay house at Lower Ferry kept its standard in a cedar box under the counter: a block of bronze worked long ago to read one value and no other, worn smooth as a river stone, its stamps rubbed blind, honest. He set the coil beside it and opened the gate. The wire came over. It settled a hair heavy. He took the trim key from his waistcoat, turned the screw an eighth, and watched the wire come true. Then wax over the screw-head, his seal pressed into it, the date. Good work in his trade looked like nothing at all: a wire standing where the bronze said it should, and a seal nobody would have cause to break for a year.

Outside, a rope was going round. Xinyi had found the ferryman's children and taught them a skipping rhyme, and the rhyme was the hall's generation poem, thirty characters fixed before the hall had a roof. The children jumped on Yong, his syllable, and on her father's, and on Xin, hers. The rope came round on the next one, which belonged to nobody yet, and the smallest girl tripped on it and sat down in the snow laughing.

He called Xinyi in before the light went and turned the bronze over in its box. On the underside, among the scratched marks of calibrators back past anyone's memory, stood his father's mark in the Gu hand, and under it his own, forty-one times, and under his the bare metal.

He gave her the scribe.

She cut hers small, under his, and blew the filings off.

## 4. A system moment

The Lin hall's courtyard was full. The street had come in through the gate to watch, since a re-assay was a rite and a rite cost nothing.

The coil went on at her left wrist, against the pulse, and her teeth began to ache before the Measurewright had the clasp shut. It was the old cold of contact, behind the back teeth. With it the three burned fingertips of her right hand woke under their linen and throbbed. The still-room had taken that skin nine nights ago, when the reagent would not settle and she had settled it with a working no page had taught her.

The housing ticked. Tape came out of its slot in a narrow white tongue, stamped as it came, and the Measurewright tore it at the brass edge and held it to the light:

> RE-ASSAY OF RECORD · LIN HALL · BEFORE WITNESSES
> BEARER: LIN SHUYAO. PRIOR ENTRY: STAGE II, WELLING.
> STAGE III, ASCENSION. TIER OF STANDING: JOURNEYMAN.
> GRADE CEILING C. LEVEL CEILING 100.
> CATALYST: FIRST TECHNIQUE DEVELOPED INDEPENDENTLY FROM INSTRUCTION.
> *Margin, in the Measurewright's pencil:* Hall states the technique is in no manual. Instrument holds no view on manuals.

She had read the same Stage nine nights ago at the bench, a line standing behind her breastbone in no voice at all, with her fingers in the cold-water basin. The tape was for everyone else.

The Measurewright read it aloud, every line, in the flat voice the Guild taught for it, and the Reckoner's clerk entered it on the roll.

The courtyard took it in waves. "Ascension." "At nineteen." "First of the Shu generation." "Off no page at all, they say." Then the old salt-mixer of the east wing, who had ground for the hall since before it had glass in its windows, said, "Qing chu yu lan," and nobody added anything.

Shifu came down the step with the book under his arm. Over the book-room door behind him four characters stood in fresh black, shu er bu zuo. He had them repainted every Airing.

A trading man from the Wei hall was already at Shuyao's elbow, talking past her to Shifu. "A Journeyman's retainer runs fifteen to thirty gold the month. Call it eighteen, and the Wei hall carries her bill from tomorrow. Your door says transmit and do not make. She made. We can use a girl who makes."

Shifu did not look at him. He opened the book on the trestle and entered the re-assay in the hall's hand, line for line off the tape. Then he turned to the last written page of the manual, wet the brush, and began to set down what she had done in the still-room as the next form, from the first reagent, so that from today it would be transmitted.

## 5. A comic beat

It was the Sweeping, and the Pei hall's dead wanted their names read.

Erniu had the broom and Daniu had the lamp, which was the wrong way round, since Daniu could not read and the lamp was for reading. Their mother had buried three sons under good names before them. She named the next two for the ox in the yard, so that death would look in at the door, see two oxen, and go on down the lane. It had. Daniu was the size of his name. Erniu had come second at everything but letters.

"Layman's sick," Daniu said. "Fan Tong says."

"Fan Tong says the rice is cooked."

"Fan Tong. Say it quick."

"Rice bucket," said Erniu. "Everybody says it quick. Hold the lamp up."

The tablets climbed the wall of the dark room in their rows, black lacquer and gold names, the eldest at the top under the smoke of three hundred Sweepings, and the gold woke as the lamp came near and slept again when it passed. The old scholar who should have been reading them styled himself the Layman Who Listens to the Rain, and in Erniu's opinion he had listened to it all his life from under the best roof in the hall. He would draw his fee for tonight in bed. The brothers would draw a labourer's day for it, twelve copper each, and the burnt cakes.

Erniu swept and read. He read the way he had learned it, standing behind the Layman since he was tall enough to hold the lamp: the posthumous name first, then the given name. Jing the Reverent. Ding the Settled. Then a short one in old lacquer, gold gone nearly black on black.

"Miu," said Erniu.

Daniu brightened. "Niu? One of ours?"

"Miu. Lips shut." Erniu put his nose up, the way the Layman put his up. "Ming yu shi shuang yue miu. When the name and the man part company, he's Miu. Mistaken. Up on the wall for good."

"What'd he do?"

"Wrote something down wrong in the book, probably. Lamp."

Daniu held it up.

Tamade. The last tablet was new. The lacquer had not had time to darken, and the gold stood out sharp as a fresh cut. It carried the same short name, Miu, in a clerk's careful hand, and under it, where the given name goes, the Hall Master's, a name nobody in the hall had said aloud since his capping. The Hall Master was in the courtyard behind them, alive, shouting at the cook.

The size under the gold was still tacky. Erniu laid the broom down on the swept floor without a sound, and his brother's lamp began to shake.

## Texture this bank coins

Everything below is pending. None of it is canon until Isaac rules, and each item is a candidate for the halls' first Standing Inventory (R70-45, R70-59 to R70-65, R70-33). Where a word or a line is real Chinese, it was supplied rather than coined, and it still waits on his word for the Words entry.

**Fields proposed for the Inventory (pending)**

- **Cadence:** matched pairs (R70-21). **Dial:** period (R70-27).

- **Notices first:** the record. A seal, a hand, an entry or its gap, the generation syllable (R70-44).

- **Recurrence:** the book and the generation syllable, at least one in each passage above.

- **Verse form:** the matched couplet traded at court, where a plain answer to one costs face (R70-110).

**Season words (pending, R70-61)**

- **the Airing.** Late summer. Every volume of the hall's book goes out on trestles in the courtyard sun, against the worm. Mood: exposure, the private record open to any eye. After the real shaishu, the airing of books.

- **Book-Close.** Year's end, when a hall shuts its book and enters nothing until the year turns. Mood: the last of a thing, held over. After the real year-end sealing of the office seals. (Named so as not to collide with the Moto bank's Ink-freeze or Alchemetrica's canon Sealing.)

- **the Sweeping.** The night the tablet room is swept and the posthumous names are read aloud. Mood: accounts with the dead. After the real grave-sweeping.

**Words and learned tags (supplied, real Chinese; pending entry, R70-33, R70-26)**

- Jian moves in a hall POV's narration: ci (thrust), gua (hanging parry), mo (wiping cut); the stance gong bu (bow stance).

- tamade: damn it, the hall commons' swear, close narration and speech (R70-34).

- jushi: lay recluse, the tail of a scholar's studio name.

- fantong: rice bucket, a good-for-nothing. The pun under the name Fan Tong.

- Ming bu zheng, ze yan bu shun: where the name is wrong, speech will not run true (Analects).

- Shu er bu zuo: transmit, and do not make (Analects), painted over a book-room door; its meaning reaches the page in a trader's mouth.

- Qing chu yu lan: blue comes out of the indigo. The pupil past the master; the full line, after Xunzi, adds that the blue is bluer than the plant. Used bare.

- Ming yu shi shuang yue miu: when name and substance part, call it Miu (the old rules for posthumous names).

**Images and units (pending, R70-59, R70-29)**

- Pressure felt as an eye sliding off a page in a script it was never taught.

- The gap in a name where a generation's syllable should sit, found the way a tongue finds a lost tooth.

- A cup of tea's time: a short span. The idiom is real Chinese.

- Good work in a Measurewright's trade: a wire standing where the bronze says it should.

- Under an entry, a hand's breadth of clean paper: room for what may yet be written.

**Sayings and customs (pending)**

- The killer's entry. The dead go in the book in the killer's own hand before the blade is wiped; a man who gives no name is entered nameless, of no hall. Shifu's saying: a man who kills and leaves it unentered has killed twice.

- Draw contracts bought forward and kept under a trading hall's seal.

- An Elder's titles are given in full at the step on the Airing: Elder of the Book, a coined title, then Senior Brother of his generation, then first disciple.

- An elder kinsman may say a fostered man's given name with the syllable back in it, as speech, while the book keeps the omission.

- A couplet coined for court. Ink that has left the brush does not return to it; the hand that let it go may lift the brush again.

- An assay house keeps a bronze standard worked to read one value, and calibrators scratch their marks on its underside, a family's marks in one line. (Calibration against the standard is canon, The Night Watch; the bronze and the marks are new.)

- A Measurewright line's child wears two tails until she is old enough for the double coil. (The double coil is canon, Qiu Yinzhi; the two tails are new.)

- Children skip rope to the hall's generation poem.

- A re-assay is held in the courtyard, gate open.

- A pupil's new working is copied into the manual as the next form, so that it is transmitted from that day.

- Tablets carry the posthumous name above the given name, and both are read in that order at the Sweeping. The reader draws a labourer's day (What Things Cost).

**Names (illustrations, pending)**

- Halls: the Tan hall (trading lineage, a river godown), the Pei hall (scholar-physicians), the Gu line (Measurewrights), the Lin hall (alchemists), the Wei hall (traders). Lower Ferry, a river town with an assay house.

- People: Tan Huaishan; Pei Wenlan; the Elder of the Pei hall, of the Shou generation, whose given name a junior's narration does not use; Pei Jie, born Pei Wenjie, fostered into an unnamed Banner House, and his unnamed son; Gu Yongmo and his granddaughter Gu Xinyi; Lin Shuyao and her unnamed Shifu.

- Generation syllables: Huai (Tan), Wen and Shou (Pei), Yong and Xin (Gu), Shu (Lin).

- Liu Shui Bu Fu, Flowing Water Does Not Rot: the Tan hall's jian art, in numbered forms. The phrase is a real saying; the art is new (R70-74).

- Posthumous names on the Pei wall: Jing the Reverent, Ding the Settled, Miu the Mistaken, twice.

- Comic names (R70-125): Daniu and Erniu, cheap child-names after the ox, porters pitched for cards as a double act under R70-113 (until carded, their beat runs under R5-D); Fan Tong, the Pei cook, whose name said quick is fantong; Tingyu Jushi, rendered in the prose as the Layman Who Listens to the Rain, a studio name the porters mock; Niu for Miu, a spoken mishearing.

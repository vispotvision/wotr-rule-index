# Edictum

> **Migration note.** Filed as "Manifestus" on the old card; current Manifestus is an involuntary flare, and a spoken, deliberate command that names and binds a target is not that. Edictum's actual mechanism, invoking a recognized higher law and directing it at a specific target with precise consequence, is Vocatia, the current Category for invocation by recognition and correspondence. "The Judger, Archon of Law" converts cleanly to Urion, the current Archon of Order, whose magic Judicaris governs equilibrium, judgment, cosmic fairness, and enforced symmetry, and whose Pantheon entry already uses "Edict" as the standing term for an Archon's binding pronouncement, so the conversion needed no invention. "Verdiction" is cut per the same ruling that struck it from Wilhelm's Codex entry (not among the current Sixty); Judicium, the Wellspring of Truth and discernment, replaces it as primary, with Fixatio and Benediction kept as attested secondary Wellsprings the old card already named correctly. Valen Dros, the Daughters of Concord, Lirae Ilmashai, and Seraphiel Moto are unattested; Seraphiel Moto in particular does not fit the current Moto naming register (a Japanese given name paired with one of the Seven Great Lines' surnames, none of which is simply "Moto" alone), and is flagged as needing a naming pass rather than silently renamed here. The Sanctification of the Inner Courts, the Archonic Trial Rite, and the Guild Pacts are unattested named institutions kept as historical texture the entry cannot make sense without.

## Summary card

**Effect** · The caster speaks a binding declaration that names a target, a condition, or a judgment and makes it lawfully enforceable, not by reshaping the target but by attaching a recognized higher law to it that the target cannot simply ignore.
**Cost** · The clarity the working demands is paid in kind; a caster whose intent is divided or dishonest at the moment of speaking pays the backlash meant for an unlawful command.
**Limit** · An Edictum cannot bind a target whose own standing authority outranks the source of law the caster is invoking, and it cannot override a soul fully shielded by contradiction glyphs or a prior Oath-nullification.
**Counter** · Contest the source of authority rather than the words: a target who can show the invoked law does not actually cover them, or that the caster's authority to invoke it is void, voids the Edict without needing to overpower it directly.
> **What nobody knows.** Why an Edictum spoken with technically correct form but a divided will sometimes lands cleanly anyway, which is not what the doctrine predicts and has never been reproduced on demand.

## Design Chain

**Trigger** · A spoken declaration made with full clarity of intent, naming a specific target, condition, or judgment; a divided or performed intent does not trigger a clean cast.
**Function** · Invokes a higher recognized law, Realmic, Wellspring, or Archonic, and directs it at a named target, attaching enforceable consequence rather than reshaping the target's own architecture directly.
**Mechanism** · The real-world analogue is the performative utterance, a spoken act (a verdict, a sentence, a pronouncement) that does not describe a state of affairs but creates one, and only works because a recognized authority structure stands behind the speaker's right to say it. Judicium supplies the discernment that reads whether the invoked law genuinely covers the target; the Attraction Layer supplies the recognition that makes the pronouncement bind rather than merely echo.
**Numerical Effect** · At the Stage VIII floor (550-point stat ceiling) the working draws an estimated mid five to low six-figure EU cost for a single-target Edict, scaling toward the caster's full reserve for an Edict addressed to a Domain or a territory rather than a person; figures are estimates inside the documented Stage VIII range, not sourced numbers.
**Target Response** · A bound target experiences the Edict as an external law suddenly co-resident with their own, described consistently as pressure rather than pain, and cannot act against the specific condition named without the law itself registering the attempt.
**Consequence** · The condition named becomes locally enforceable law for as long as the invoking authority is not successfully contested; a territory or object under an Edict of this kind carries the binding independent of the caster's continued presence.
**Limitation** · Edictum enforces. It does not persuade, and a target already committed to defying the invoked authority outright, rather than merely disliking the consequence, is a documented hard case the working was not built to answer.
**Weakness** · The caster's authority to invoke the named law is itself contestable, and a target or a rival authority who successfully argues the invocation exceeds what the caster is entitled to speak can void the Edict without touching its mechanism.
**Cost** · An impure Edictum, spoken without the required clarity, backfires on the caster's own Wellspring rather than failing quietly, producing what the doctrine calls an Echo Reversal.
**Counterplay** · Show the invoked law does not cover the target, or that the caster's own standing does not extend to invoking it; both are documented as more reliable than attempting to out-shout the pronouncement itself.

## Origin

Urion, the Archon of Order, is kept as the working's ultimate source, since every Edict any mortal speaks is understood in current doctrine as borrowing a fragment of Judicaris rather than originating law independently. Valen Dros, the Daughters of Concord, and Lirae Ilmashai are kept as the card's mortal practitioners, each demonstrating a different scale of use, silencing a single Wellspring-bound beast, nullifying spells and movement in arbitration, and binding a caster's own body against cursed Essence, none of which is attested beyond this card. Seraphiel Moto's Edictum of Silence, rendering a battlefield unable to cast Essence Drafts, is kept as the card's largest-scale example and flagged for a naming pass, since the name as given does not fit the current Moto register.

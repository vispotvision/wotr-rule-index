# Imprinta

> **Migration note.** Filed as "Manifestus" on the old card; current Manifestus is a short-lived involuntary flare, and a deliberate act that leaves a permanent, enduring trace is the opposite case. Imprinta's actual mechanism, embedding a piece of memory or identity into an object, place, or person so it outlasts the caster and can later be read back, is Mnemata, the current Category for memorycraft and echo manipulation, which already governs temporal imprints by name. Its Wellspring is Anamnesis, the Wellspring of Remembrance, which strengthens exactly the reading of past trauma and history through contact with objects, places, and beings that the old card describes. Because the effect is permanent rather than sustained by ongoing attention, it is filed under Runecraft rather than Spellcraft for durability purposes, even though it sits in the destination folder as a Spellcraft entry for library organization. "Mecre, Archon of Memory" converts cleanly to Maelor, the current Archon of Memory Magic (Mnemoris: recall, preservation, echo transcription, ancestral archive), a direct match that needed no invention. Soji the Dreamwalker, Caldran of Athame, and the Choir of Vellsorea are unattested and kept as the card's necessary cast. The Reconstruction of Utopia, pre-Spirit War knowledge, Athame, Vellsorea, and the Echo Wellspring Orders are unattested named places, orders, and events kept as historical texture; Estesia is unattested as well. Edenis is attested, as Florwyn's Titanic seat, and the card's claim that Edenis gardens and tombs hold Imprinta traces is kept as consistent with Florwyn's own attested domain of organic memory and Wellspring harmony.

## Summary card

**Effect** · The caster projects a fragment of their own memory, Trait, or Wellspring resonance into an object, place, or person, leaving behind a permanent trace that can act, respond, or be read back long after the caster has left or died.
**Cost** · The fragment is a real piece of the caster's own Essence Core, not a copy; embedding it costs the caster that portion of themselves permanently, which is why Imprinta cannot be cast repeatedly without consequence to the caster's own continuity.
**Limit** · An Imprinta trace is passive until read or triggered. It does not act on its own initiative beyond the narrow condition it was left to answer.
**Counter** · An imprint anchored to a substrate that is destroyed, or a location that is deliberately unmade, loses its anchor and fades; Imprinta traces are documented as durable against time and neglect but not against deliberate destruction of what they are embedded in.
> **What nobody knows.** Why some Imprinta traces retain enough of the caster's identity to respond and interact meaningfully, and others degrade into inert residue within a generation, with no confirmed correlation to the strength of the original casting.

## Design Chain

**Trigger** · A deliberate act of leaving something behind, performed with the specific intent that it outlast the moment; an unintentional emotional trace left in passing is not Imprinta and is not covered by this entry.
**Function** · Detaches a small, bounded fragment of the caster's own Essence Core and binds it into a substrate, object, location, or person, so the fragment persists as a standing feature of that substrate rather than dispersing.
**Mechanism** · The real-world analogue is magnetic remanence, a material exposed to a strong field retaining part of that field's alignment after the source is removed, so the object itself now carries a durable record of an event it was never designed to store. Anamnesis gives the Crystal an architecture built to read exactly this kind of retained alignment in other objects and beings, which is why an Anamnesis-harmonized reader can find an Imprinta trace that an unharmonized observer walks past.
**Numerical Effect** · At the Stage VII floor (475-point stat ceiling) the working draws an estimated mid to high five-figure EU cost per imprint, paid once at the moment of casting rather than sustained, with larger or more identity-dense imprints (an entire guardian-function rather than a single memory) costing toward the top of that range; figures are estimates inside the documented Stage VII range, not sourced numbers.
**Target Response** · A living target carrying a fresh imprint reports it as a foreign but non-hostile presence, distinct from possession, since the fragment carries no independent will beyond what it was left to do. An inanimate substrate shows no response beyond becoming legibly different to an Anamnesis-sensitive reader.
**Consequence** · The imprint persists independent of the caster's continued life or attention once cast, and can be read, triggered, or interacted with by anyone with the sensitivity or the correct condition, not only by the caster.
**Limitation** · Imprinta cannot embed more identity than the caster is willing to permanently part with, and a fragment stripped too thin to carry its intended function simply fails to activate when its trigger condition arrives.
**Weakness** · The imprint's persistence depends entirely on its substrate surviving; a destroyed object, a razed location, or a dead host with no secondary anchor takes the imprint with it.
**Cost** · Permanent loss of the embedded fragment from the caster's own Essence Core, which does not regenerate the way ordinary reserve does; a caster who has left many imprints over a lifetime is documented as measurably thinner in that specific respect than one who has left few.
**Counterplay** · Destroy or remove the substrate before the imprint can be triggered or read; failing that, deny an Anamnesis-sensitive reader access to the site, since an unharmonized observer typically cannot locate a well-hidden imprint at all.

## Origin

Maelor, the Archon of Memory, is kept as the working's ultimate source, credited with seeding echoes of forgotten truths as an act of Mnemoris rather than of any mortal Craft. Soji the Dreamwalker is kept as the card's example of Imprinta cast into a Wellspring current itself rather than into an object, producing memories that took on physical form within the current. Caldran of Athame is kept as the card's example of Imprinta bound into crafted weapons that remembered each wielder in turn, and the Choir of Vellsorea as the card's example of the working performed collectively rather than by a single caster. None of the three is attested beyond this card.

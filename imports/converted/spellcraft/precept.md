# Precept

> **Migration note.** The Trello card filed Precept as a "Manifestus Class" working: a deliberately invoked, permanent law inscribed into reality, used to found guild codes, divine law and binding compacts. Current canon's Manifestus Category is defined the opposite way (R10-3-MANIFESTUS_SHAPE): involuntary, short-lived emotional flares, an Overtone Bloom nobody aims. Precept as described cannot be Manifestus under that definition, so it is refiled here under **Glyphica** (the Parun-based language of law, which explicitly governs Authorization and Sealing) with a **Votia** vow-binding component for its compact-and-geasa use, and under **Runecraft** rather than a spoken Craft, since the source insists the law persists once laid down. "The Judger" is kept as the informal name Sanctum Lux registers for **Urion, Archon of Balance** (attested). Lysandra Velkaris and Thanatos Tyurkia have no wiki entry and are carried as originated, unattested figures; "Tyurkia" as a lineage identifier is itself an open ruling (the Tyzura/Ashura Tyurkia question), so Thanatos Tyurkia's name is doubly unattested. The Wellsprings of "Fixation" and "Verdiction" convert to **Fixatio** and **Judicium**, the two closest attested currents; "Harmony" has no Wellspring correlate and is dropped. The specific Parun glyph codes (Fi, Lo, Wy, Ka) could not be checked against the Descent of Parun's lexicon and are dropped rather than guessed. "Innerworld" converts to **the Inner World**; "Sacrosanct Accord" converts to **the Guild Accord**, the attested body that governs inter-guild conduct across the quarters. Crownforge, the Twelve Precepts of Fate, and the Volume III "Stones that Speak" citation have no current-canon entry and are kept as unattested, load-bearing only for this card's own sense. Abythron is attested as Zar'thuun's Titanic domain of unmaking; "the Abythron Gate" as a specific structure at its fall is not, and is kept provisionally.

## What it is

A Precept is a working that does not ask to be believed. It asks to be obeyed, and it is built so that obedience is not really optional for whatever falls inside the clause it names.

Where an ordinary rune fixes a shape, a Precept fixes a rule. The practitioner does not carve an effect into the world so much as file an instruction with it, in the Continuum's own legible grammar, and the instruction outlives the hand that wrote it the same way a Guild seal outlives the clerk who struck it. This is why the older registers called the practice a kind of enshrining: the law is not enforced from outside afterward. It is load-bearing from the moment it is sealed.

A Precept is not persuasion and it is not a threat. It is closer to a boundary condition. Other workings, other oaths, and in the right circumstance other people, do not defy a Precept so much as discover that the space it named will not hold the shape they were trying to give it.

## Where it sits in the current system

**Category** · Glyphica primary (the Authorization and Sealing glyph roles, spoken of in the Unified Taxonomy as the syntax by which the Continuum "recognises and authorises transformation"), Votia secondary where the Precept functions as a binding compact between parties rather than a standing law.
**Wellsprings** · Fixatio (the anchoring law: what a Precept holds, it holds) and Judicium (discernment and verdict, the currents Sanctum Lux's own instrument reads by).
**Family** · Materia (Fixatio) with a Fulguria current (Judicium).
**Craft** · Runecraft. Per the current Craft/durability split, Spellcraft holds only while attention holds and Runecraft is fixed and degrades like any inscription; a Precept that is meant to found a guild code or a dynasty's law cannot be an art that lapses when the caster looks away.
**Where it is filed** · The Magical Categories page (Glyphica, entry eight) and the Guild Accord material for the compacts a Precept is used to write. No standalone Precept entry exists yet; this card is the seed of one.

## Practitioners and history

**Urion, Archon of Balance**, informally called the Judger by every Attestant who is taught to strike the word on their second day and keeps using it anyway, is the figure the card's "first act upon descending" most plausibly describes: a reference against which other things are measured rather than a petitioned god, and the natural author of a foundational Precept if any Archon wrote one. This is a plausible current-canon anchor for the card's claim, not an attested one-to-one match; no wiki entry states that Urion personally inscribed the Twelve Precepts of Fate.

**Lysandra Velkaris**, said to have authored the Precept of Hierarchic Ascendance for Highspire's chain of command, has no current wiki entry. Highspire itself is attested, as the summit housing the Lawbell and the seat of the Highspire Choir of Celestials, Verdict-Bearers by function, which makes a Precept of rank and hierarchy filed there thematically consistent even though Velkaris herself is originated.

**Thanatos Tyurkia**, said to have broken the Precept of Mercy, has no current wiki entry, and the lineage name attached to her is itself unresolved in canon. Both figures are carried here as unattested, pending Isaac's word on whether either survives into the current corpus.

## What it can and cannot do

A Precept can found a standing law inside its declared sphere: a guild code, a spiritual compact, a geasa that binds a named party rather than the world at large. It can outlast its author, the way an inscription outlasts the mason, because Fixatio does not care who is still standing.

It cannot compel what falls outside the clause it names. A Precept written for one sphere has no purchase past its stated boundary, and a working that reads the Precept's shape before it seals can in principle decline to be caught by it. It also cannot be argued out of existence by force alone, only by the same register it was written in: a Precept is unmade by a correction to the law, not by breaking the stone it is cut into. Rescind, the withdrawal-of-consent effect attested elsewhere in the corpus, is the clearest documented counter to a soul-bound Precept, and only when the Precept was never lawfully consented to in the first place.

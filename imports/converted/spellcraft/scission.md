# Scission

> **Migration note.** The old card's "Ritus Class (Essence Severance / Metaphysical Separation)" survives cleanly; Ritus is a live Category (Spirit-Aligned, performed rather than built, the ancestor discipline behind every field-capable working) and needed no change. The Wellsprings named in the old Connections block did not survive: "Verdiction" is not among the current Sixty (confirmed against Wilhelm's Codex entry, where the same coinage is explicitly struck as a Wellspring and kept only as a manufactured Crystal's proper name), and "Fixation" and "Echo" were common-tongue approximations rather than Codex terms. The card's actual claim, that Scission separates one bound component from another without destroying either, is exactly what the Limina Wellspring Dissolution does, so Dissolution replaces the old trio as primary. Parun glyphs Ci/Ra/Th/Ka are kept as given; none of the four are attested against the current Master Glyph Index at time of writing, so the chant syllables are flagged unattested rather than converted. Kujo Valorin and Zethyra the Veinwrought do not appear on any current character card or wiki page; both are kept as the named inventors the card requires (per the named-inventor rule) and flagged unattested. The First War of Silence, the Soulquake of Tirres, the Fracturing of Edenis, and the Pactless of the Outer World are also unattested as named events, kept as historical texture the entry cannot make sense without. Edenis itself is attested, as Florwyn's Titanic seat. Stage floor, Grade, and Wellspring assignment are chosen conservatively from the FOW tables per the card's own description of Scission as a serious but not singularly world-ending rite, and are stated as committed values, not estimates to be revisited.

## Summary card

**Effect** · The practitioner opens a clean division between two things that are bound to each other, a Trait and the soul that grew it, an Essence and the body carrying it, a memory and the identity built on it, a Wellspring and the host harmonized to it, and pulls them apart without destroying either half.
**Cost** · The Crystal pays for cutting a bond it was never built to release cleanly; what comes off the practitioner's own reserve scales with how deeply the target bond runs, and a mistimed cut takes the difference out of the practitioner instead.
**Limit** · What separates does not reassemble whole. The severed piece and the thing it is cut from can each survive, but neither returns to exactly what it was before the cut.
**Counter** · A bond reinforced from both ends at the moment of the cut resists separation in proportion to how recently and how deliberately it was renewed; a bond nobody is actively tending is the only kind Scission cuts cleanly.
> **What nobody knows.** Why a bond severed by Scission sometimes leaves a legible scar on both halves and sometimes leaves nothing detectable at all, with no correlation yet found to the caster's skill, the bond's age, or the Wellspring it ran through.

## Design Chain

**Trigger** · A bond the practitioner has identified and can name precisely enough to address: this Trait, this Essence, this memory, this Wellspring, not a general instruction to "fix" the target.
**Function** · Isolates one bonded pair inside a larger structure and applies a directed unbinding pressure to the join alone, leaving everything on either side of the join structurally intact.
**Mechanism** · The real-world analogue is chain scission, the breaking of a polymer at a specific bond under directed energy (radiation, oxidation, mechanical stress) while the rest of the chain survives intact. Dissolution gives the Crystal a thermodynamic preference toward the unbound state; Scission is the trained act of aiming that preference at exactly one bond instead of letting it run through the whole lattice indiscriminately, the way a chemist chooses a cleavage site rather than simply heating the whole molecule until something gives.
**Numerical Effect** · At the Stage VI floor and A-Grade ceiling (400-point stat ceiling per the Stage table), the working draws an estimated low-to-mid five-figure EU cost for a personal severance (Trait or Essence from a willing or restrained subject) and scales toward the practitioner's full reserve for a hostile Wellspring-from-host cut against resistance; figures are estimates inside the documented Stage VI range, not sourced numbers.
**Target Response** · The target feels the cut as a structural absence arriving where a presence used to be, described consistently across accounts as a wrongness rather than pain, and frequently reports disorientation, memory gaps at the site of the cut, or an altered resonance for hours afterward.
**Consequence** · The severed piece persists independently for a time proportional to how much of the original bond's Attraction Layer commitment it carried away with it; most fragments destabilize and disperse within hours unless deliberately stabilized by a second working.
**Limitation** · Scission cannot select a bond it cannot first identify; a practitioner who mistakes which of several overlapping bonds is causing a failure cuts the wrong one and gets a clean, useless separation.
**Weakness** · The rite requires a period of contact or proximity to trace the bond before the cut lands, during which the practitioner is committed and the target or a defender has time to act.
**Cost** · Paid from the caster's own reserve first; a cut that meets more resistance than budgeted draws the shortfall from the caster's own Crystal architecture rather than failing outright, which is why mass application against unwilling or heavily reinforced bonds is treated as reckless.
**Counterplay** · Reinforce the bond being targeted before the cut lands, or force the practitioner to lose the contact window needed to trace it; a bond under active renewal from both ends is the documented hard case.

## Origin

Kujo Valorin and Zethyra the Veinwrought are the card's named originators and are kept as such under the named-inventor rule, though neither is attested on any current character card or wiki page. The card credits Valorin with a forbidden mass application, severing an entire army's mortality bond in one working and leaving Veil-Bound revenants behind, and credits Zethyra with the opposite extreme, a controlled single-target form precise enough to lift a curse without harming the surrounding Animatria around it. Both derivations are kept as the problem Scission was built to solve: an existing bond has become lethal to keep and equally dangerous to leave, and something short of destroying either half of it is needed.

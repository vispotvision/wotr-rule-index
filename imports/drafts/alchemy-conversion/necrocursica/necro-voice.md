## 2. What the evidence says

- **He never asks.** Not one question mark in anything he has said or written. Where another man would ask, he orders the answer: "Tell me what I wrote." "Say it a third time." The one draft interrogative is spoken flat. Asked a question he does not want, he goes silent and covers the marked hand.
- **He fixes words and orders them used.** The impression, not the soul. "Project Zombification," in writing. "A word I chose." "Not a summary. The words." "Write it as the place." His whole ethic and his whole method both live in the choice of a word.
- **Law, then application.** The refusal is the pattern: a general sentence in the third person ("The author of a text kept under seal answers ..."), the case ("The seal does not admit you"), the order ("File me by the title"), and a dry concession ("I did not choose it, but it is exact").
- **The chain.** "An anchor is a binding, and a binding is a name, and a name is the only thing ..." A sorites (each clause's end becomes the next one's start), carried on *and*. It is the connective tissue of both registers and the surest mark that the treatise and the commander are one man.
- **Two ends, one objection.** He hears two people out and names what they share. He reasons by synthesis, not by rebuttal.
- **He notices where a thing stops.** The hour you lift the flax. The edge of the dose, twenty-two paces short of a marker. The seal. Whether a man wrote it down.
- **Humour against himself or a man he loves, landed last.** "A very long name for a man who copies." "So angry at flax." "This table has never once argued with me."
- **Candour arrives when he is glad or grieving.** Glad at Castlefall, he "talked," told the ponds whole. Grieving over what he wrote, he asked another man to say it for him. Everything else is command.

## 3. The voice block (R52-03, for his card's Voice section)

Decided here as the working block; Isaac may overturn any slot. Built from the lines above and from real speaker types (R52-29): the forensic manual (Alfred Swaine Taylor's *Manual of Medical Jurisprudence*, 1844: the plain imperative, the case, the marker, the physician's "I" held back until it matters), a commander's written orders with their reasons attached (Wellington's dispatches: terse, dry understatement, no rhetoric), and, for his root, the flax-growers' instructions of the 1840s (the Irish flax-improvement society's retting leaflets: the hour, the pond, the snap test).

- **Notices first:** where a thing stops. The edge, the hour, the seal, the boundary marker. In a person, whether he wrote it down.
- **Sentence length:** Long chained sentences on *and*, the sorites his signature; then a short verdict. Spoken, a mean near thirty words, with one-word and two-word commands between (a name and a noun: "Nuvalik. The bodies."). Treatise body: medium to long law sentences, each paragraph closing short. Margins: six to twenty words, one or two sentences.
- **Contractions:** Never (ML13, R52-04). In the treatise a contraction appears only inside a quotation of somebody else, so an apostrophe on his page is a second mouth.
- **Pet word:** *edge* in speech and margin (the edge of a dose, the edge of an hour). Its treatise form is *boundary*. In grief, *the hour*.
- **Never says:** A question, aloud or on paper. The name of the second compeller, or her title; the school that put him out; his own Stage or any figure for himself. *Soul* for anything raised; *mind*, *will*, *remembers*, *she* or *he* for a residue (the body keeps this rule; the margins break it). After Codex II named him, never the three signs of Submission: "I had no choice," "it was necessary," "someone would have done it anyway." Never *The Mother*. *Lich* once only, in the one margin line (ML12). Never another man's pet words: Mu-jin's "Correct," and "Now, now"; Draycott's "Let me be exact."
- **Stumbles:** Never on a word. He stops. Aloud: silence, a look at the stove or the floor between his boots, the good hand over the marked one. On paper: a one-line paragraph, then a new subject; or a cut. The sealed edition's missing footnote is his stumble, in the only form he permits himself.
- **Under stress:** (R52-25) He shortens to a name and an imperative, and gives the imperative twice, the second time with the name: "Leave it." / "Leave it, Mu-jin." No reason offered. On paper, the reason goes too; the sentence is an order.
- **In grief:** (R52-26) Back to the retting ponds: his father, the pond, the bundle, the bank, the hour, lift, tow, fibre, core, rot. Plain parish sentences, subject then verb, the first person rising. On paper this is the treatise's candour: he confesses and then tells the reader what to do with the confession ("treat this document as a confession").
- **In joy:** (R52-27) The speech holds and runs long: he tells a story whole and lands it on a dry line against himself or a man he loves. The body shows it (he sleeps through, laughs at Neros's charts, smiles at the stove). On paper, joy is a colleague credited by name with one dry line.
- **Sample line:** "I would prefer the file be built off a word I chose."
- **Fixed saying** (R52-30, Accord Inventory, the chartered commons of his parish country; working reading, since no Alftian Inventory exists): "A melted coin does not testify." He uses it of a body past its nine days and, in command, of a method that leaves no signature.

## 6. Three paired samples

Each idea three ways: the treatise body (stratum marked), a margin by the older man, and aloud in his default. Where it helps, a fourth: aloud at the Castlefall stove, the young man glad.

### A. What the dead leave is read, never addressed (the Sixth Unmooring)

**Body** (sealed edition, command):
> Write what a residue does. Never write what it wants. The Echo is weighted toward a room, the Trace is charged toward a task, and the body is cooling, and those three words are the whole of the nomenclature a student needs in his first year. A reader trained on them reads a death-room cleanly. A reader trained on mind and will and memory hears the dead answer him before he has learned to read them, and he teaches his students to listen for the answer, and they teach theirs. The harm does not stay with the man who made it. It goes out in every copy.
>
> I have been told that my own first manuscript carries this Unmooring. I have reviewed it. The review does not please me.

(The second paragraph is first-manuscript candour kept and updated by one tense. The drafter should know what the reader of Mu-jin's restored note will see: the "review" is where the footnote went.)

**Margin:**
> The rule is for students. A master may say *she*.

**Aloud** (command, to a cell):
> "Nobody at this bench says she. Say the hind, and say the tissue, and say where it went. The man who reads the file will want a person in it, and I will not write him one."

**Aloud, young** (Castlefall, to Mu-jin; the footnote's voice):
> "Call it the impression. Anything kinder is where the mistake gets in."

### B. The seal sets the term (Category Three; Category Four refused)

**Body** (sealed edition, command):
> The seal is the last glyph laid and the one the Bench reads first. It says where the working ends and when, and the impression-body lives inside that term and nowhere past it. Every other glyph in this chapter forgives a slow hand. The seal does not. A man who hesitates over it has made a thing with no end written on it, and the Third Unmooring begins in the hesitation.
>
> Category Four is refused. No Bench will hear it and no review reaches it, and I set down no glyph of it here, since a chain written once is a chain copied.

**Margin** (against Category Three):
> The seal sets the term, and whoever writes the seal sets the term.

**Aloud** (command, teaching a culture from memory, ML10):
> "The Fourth is on no page, and it will not be on yours. Learn the Third, and learn its seal, because the seal is where the Accord looks, and what the Accord looks at will be clean."

### C. Preferring the dead (the Fifth Unmooring)

**Body** (first-manuscript candour kept, the grief shift, his root):
> I put my own case here, under the Fifth, because it belongs nowhere else. My father kept the retting ponds of our parish, and his whole craft was the hour he lifted the flax. One autumn I weighted a bundle into the far corner of the last pond, where he did not go, and left it past the hour to see what the rot did next. He found it before the frost and burned it on the bank. I have wanted to know what the rot does next on every day since. If I am found describing the dead as better company than the living in the room, read this chapter back to me from the heading, and do not let me answer until it is finished.

**Margin:**
> Nobody read it back to me. The living were told, and they remain the duller company.

**Aloud** (command):
> "Leave the living to the parish. It keeps a roll for them."

**Aloud, young** (Castlefall, glad):
> "My father burned it on the bank because I had left it past the hour. I have been leaving things past the hour since, and the far side of it has never once bored me."

## 7. The other lines the edition owes

**Title page** (sealed stratum; the subtitle is the drafter's, the voice is this):
> THE NECROCURSICA
> Being a Treatise on What the Dead Leave, on the Persistent Dead, and on the Seven Unmoorings that Follow the Misuse of Mortalis
> Written at Genesio by Malphas, Scribe to the Genesio Archivum, by order

**Edition note** (ML7, short, command):
> The first manuscript of this treatise was written at Genesio in my first years there, and read at a stove in Castlefall, and afterward elsewhere. This edition supersedes it. I have added what the first did not know, the fourth residue among it. I have cut what I cut. A reader holding the first will find the places.
>
> Scribe to the Genesio Archivum, by order.

("Afterward elsewhere" is the only place he answers "Does Draycott have a copy?", and it answers nothing.)

**Preface, opening** (first-manuscript stratum, trimmed):
> This text was not my idea.
>
> Two visitors came to my rooms at Genesio on a morning in the open months and sat down without being asked. One was Gisli Draycott. The other goes by a title, and the title stays out of this book. They held the sealed stacks. I had a pen.
>
> I told them I had given up the dead.
>
> "You haven't given them up," Draycott said. "You're avoiding them."
>
> He was right. I have not forgiven him for it.
>
> I set the bargain down beside the coercion, since a reader given only the coercion will think better of me than I deserve. I wanted the stacks. I had wanted them for years, from a bench beside their gate. The mark on my left hand is the other visitor's, and it carried them the rest of the way. I will not describe the instrument.

**The fourth residue, credited** (sealed stratum; R66-1's layering):
> The fourth is the Volitional Trace. The name is Kwon Mu-jin's, and so is its boundary; the finding was Madeleine Ault's, and Ivor Strom's after her, and the framing Gisli Draycott's. His word leans nearer a will than I would have let it. It stands, since a name in use is the name, and the boundary he set under it is sound.

**The Seventh, the platform** (first-manuscript candour; the source's italic thought recast):
> I saw it once, at a distance I was glad to keep. A man I had counted among the finest readers of Mortalis I knew crossed the Genesio waystation at dawn with nothing hurried left in him. He stopped at the edge of the platform and looked at the mountains a long while. He looked sent for. I thought he might have forgotten that the mountains had not sent. I did not go to him that day. I wrote this chapter instead.

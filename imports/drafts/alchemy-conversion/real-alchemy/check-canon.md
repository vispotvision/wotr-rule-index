# The Real Alchemy: canon check

Checker: canon lane. Read `the-real-alchemy.md` (80 "In WOTR" lines) and `notes.md` against Alchemetrica (live render `/tmp/wotr-drafts/alchemetrica-live.md`, identical in text to `wiki/Materials, Alchemy & Trade/Alchemetrica.md`), the Alchemical Index sheet of `~/wotr-vault/true-canon/The Master Codex.xlsx` and its wiki mirror, The Standing Index, Weight, Measure and the Standard, The Sky, the Hour and the Year, The Working Vocabulary, the Alftian Codex vols I and II and The Farrant Papers (`imports/drafts/alchemy-conversion/`), R18-5 (Pack Eighteen §5) and RULINGS.md 2026-09-28 (alchemy-conversion, open-conflicts, calendar). Every canon term the page names exists; no line touches the reserve, Tier or residue rulings. Twelve findings, most serious first.

## Findings

1. **Sublimation: the page opens a loophole canon does not have (line 111).** "Its rule that there is no partial Sublimation belongs to the Wellspring-mirroring working ... so a scene of plain bench sublimation has the real pattern to draw on." Canon has no second, plain sublimation. Alchemetrica says every Draft is one of four operations, and Sublimation mirrors Sublimatio with "There is no partial Sublimation." Rulings K5 and K6 put only Calcination and Distillation outside the six, as bench operations on Cinerion and Sublimare. Sublimation stays one of the six. The line invents a non-Wellspring sublimation and licenses partial runs.
   **Correction:** "Alchemetrica's Rising collects on the cold walls of the Riser, and what failed to rise is lost; there is no partial Sublimation, and what did not rise stays as a fused mass broken out with a chisel. The real bench sublimed partially and repeatedly, and its unrisen residue is the real counterpart of that fused mass. Canon names no sublimation outside the Sublimatio operation."

2. **Instruments: the page quietly sides with Alchemetrica against R53-01 (line 191; also 309 and 723).** "The want of an instrument across the whole range is WOTR's own condition." R53-01, as ruled 2026-09-28 (calendar: the span starts 690 IC and the present is 715 IC), puts everything invented from 1800 to 1900 in the world. That includes Seger cones (1886), Le Chatelier's thermocouple (1886 to 1887), borosilicate glass (1880s to 1890s), the Marsh test (1836) and the Reinsch test (1841). Fahrenheit's thermometer (1714) comes earlier still. Alchemetrica says "There is no instrument. There has never been an instrument." Two canon statements pull against each other, and the page settles the question without saying so. No CONFLICTS row records it; notes.md items 1 and 2 only propose one.
   **Correction:** line 191: "Alchemetrica's Heat says there is no instrument and has never been one. R53-01's technology span (from 690 IC) takes in the thermocouple and Seger cones, and the mercury thermometer is older. The two stand against each other in canon." Add the same pointer to line 309 (borosilicate: it still softens near 820 °C, so Alchemetrica's "will not take the top of the range" holds, but its thermal-shock clause does not) and to line 723 (Marsh and Reinsch fall inside the span). The parent session records a CONFLICTS row; this check does not.

3. **The flyleaf line is credited to the wrong volume (line 895 table row; line 901).** "That which courseth above is as that which courseth below" is on the flyleaf of Volume the **First** (vol I lines 11 to 13). Volume the Second's flyleaf reads "There are two kinds of men who study the dead." Volume II quotes the line only in its text (line 249: "I had it on the flyleaf of the first volume"), and vol III line 365 says the same. `research-names.md` line 182 carries the same error.
   **Correction:** table row "The flyleaf of Volume the First (quoted again in Volume the Second, chapter text)". Line 901: "The Alftian Codex, Volume the First, carries all three recitations and the flyleaf line, which Volume the Second quotes again."

4. **Draft-mark: an invented premise, and the Codex left out (line 669).** Neither Alchemetrica nor the Codex says a Draft-mark "never fades", yet the page asserts it and reasons from it. The line also skips the Codex's own Draft-mark. Vol I line 45 gives Furveus's nail-beds "gone grey" with "pale bands crossed the nails that no washing lifted". Vol I line 145 names it Draft-mark, "keyed to what he handled most: quicksilver, calomel ... white arsenic."
   **Correction:** "Alchemetrica's Draft-mark sits in the sclera and under the nailbeds, keyed to what a man handled most. The Alftian Codex puts it on Furveus as grey nail-beds and pale bands across the nails that no washing lifted, among other things after white arsenic. Those bands are the real Mees' line. It grows out in five or six months, so a band still visible dates a dose within the last half-year. The grey nail-bed and the sclera have no real counterpart for mercury or arsenic; argyria and the lead line are the nearest real marks." Remove "a mark that never fades means dosing that never stopped."

5. **Conjunction: the line contradicts the page's own evidence and blurs canon's hazard (line 127).** "The quick pour belongs to the spagyric recombination of plant principles." The page's Lemery procedure (step 5, line 460) recombines in a sealed double vessel kept in dung for four or five days or a fortnight. That is not a quick pour. "The pressure at the neck is the real hazard" can also be read as the Wedding's hazard. In canon that hazard is Rupture (Alchemetrica; Codex vol I line 283; ruling GW4).
   **Correction:** "Alchemetrica's Wedding is fast and decisive, and the Alftian Codex's Furveus pours the principles in one movement, with an Aperture glyph at the neck to let pressure out. Real recombination, of metals or of plant principles, was a slow sealed digestion (Lemery: days to a fortnight in dung). The fast Wedding is WOTR's own. The real danger at the joining was pressure in sealed glass, which is what the Aperture answers. Canon's own danger at the Wedding is Rupture."

6. **Cupellation does have a canon counterpart (line 135).** "The cupel, the scorifier and cementation have no named counterpart in Alchemetrica or the Codex." That is true only of those two texts. The Working Vocabulary (§II, The Assay) names the cupel, scorification, the muffle, litharge, the button, parting, fineness and the blick, and Alchemetrica's Kingswater is kept by "every parting bench".
   **Correction:** "... The Working Vocabulary names the cupel, scorification, litharge, the button and the blick as the assay's trade words, and Alchemetrica keeps Kingswater at every parting bench. Cementation has no counterpart."

7. **Pharmacopoeia: the nearest counterpart is left out (line 508).** "No counterpart." Canon has the Standing Index, an official register of entered formulas that includes physic (Metering Tonic, Bone-Grey Compress). Entry needs a plain-language petition and a reference lot.
   **Correction:** "No pharmacopoeia by name. The nearest counterpart is the Standing Index, the Bench's register of entered formulas, physic among them, entered in plain language against a reference lot."

8. **Colour regimens: canon does time its colours (line 856).** "WOTR has no regimens timed to colour." Alchemetrica gives the Rot six to nine weeks and a black. The Codex (vol I line 139) gives the ferment three turns to the Blacking and a Whitening after most of a month in the Returner. GW3 ties Blacking, Whitening and Reddening to Fermentation, Distillation and Coagulation.
   **Correction:** "WOTR has no colour-by-colour calendar like Philalethes' regimens. The nearest are the Rot's six to nine weeks to black (Alchemetrica) and the Codex's three turns to the Blacking and most of a month to the Whitening."

9. **Whitening: what is whitened is the spirit (line 832).** The page says "The Alftian Codex's Whitening of a plant ferment". The Codex reads the Whitening in the spirit circulating in the Returner (vol I line 139), which GW3 counts as Distillation. Line 464 gets this right.
   **Correction:** "The Alftian Codex's Whitening, read in a plant spirit circulating in the Returner, has no real counterpart ..."

10. **Two over-claims with nothing behind them (lines 27 and 35).** "That matches the real share of bench time exactly": the page cites no figure for how much bench time went to separation. "The same thing the same way, at the same step" says more than the Solution entry supports.
    **Correction:** line 35: "... where most of a journeyman's early years are spent. The real Parting was as slow, and it lost matter at every step." Line 27: "... The real bench met impure stock at the same step."

11. **The intro undercuts R18-5 against the page's own figures (line 5; also 245).** "The furnaces ran for weeks and the planned regimens for months." R18-5 says "the furnaces ran for months", and the page's table supports that: Boerhaave kept mercury at 180 °F for "several Months" and ran a sealed bottle for about 8½ months. The athanor line also cites only Newton's six weeks.
    **Correction:** intro: "The furnaces were tended for weeks and, at the long end, months." Line 245: "... outruns the real evidence, which shows tended runs of six weeks (Newton) to several months (Boerhaave), with no unattended burn time measured."

12. **Wording that reads as canon when it is not (lines 546, 768, 804, 410).** Line 546 attaches "five to fifteen grains" to the Codex's calomel, but the Codex gives no dose. Better: "... is the real purgative use; the real adult dose ran five to fifteen grains." Lines 768 and 804 say "Alchemetrica's Standing Index", but the Standing Index is its own page. Better: "the Standing Index, as Alchemetrica describes it". Line 410 says "no ground joints ... before the twentieth century", but ground stoppers were older, and only standardised interchangeable joints are a twentieth-century item. Better: "holds for the still train before standardised ground joints". The research lane should confirm that date.

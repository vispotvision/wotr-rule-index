- Hotbed temperatures of 61 to 68 °C and one to two months of heat (gardening literature only).
- Stewart's luted cucurbit breaking after three or four distillations (a reconstructor's account).
- The burning-log demonstration credited to Paracelsus himself.
- The Fugger and guaiac episode (The Renaissance Mathematicus 2021, Thony Christie's blog).
- Eighty-five chemical preparations in the 1618 London Pharmacopoeia.
- Swallowed mercury "a pound or two" for obstruction (Wood and Bache 1870).
- Blue mass at about one third mercury, 65 mg a pill (Wikipedia "Blue mass").
- Rush's twenty grains four times a day (Wikipedia "Calomel"); the ten-grain powder is two-sourced.
- Thénard and egg white, 1825 (Babcock 1882).
- The saturation-concentration figures for mercury vapour at 42, 77 and 120 °C (one vapour-pressure table; the concentrations are arithmetic).
- Mercury half-life in blood and brain (Bernhoft 2012).
- Ramazzini's gilders' palsy of the neck (via a summary of the translation).
- Medicinal arsenic at three to six milligrams a day giving mild poisoning (NRC 1977 extract).
- Stewart of Edinburgh, 1863 (Babcock 1882).
- Children raking arsenic in the Cornish calciners (National Academies with the Cornish Mining WHS audio trail).
- Kraus as the source of the term "dispersion" (Newman 1996).
- *Telesmus* as a mistransliteration of the Arabic for talisman (Wikipedia "Emerald Tablet").
- The Greek four-colour scheme's attribution to Maria and pseudo-Democritus (Wikipedia "Magnum opus").
- Simonini's black-to-red cinnabar colour sequence (Simonini 2025).
- Beguin's *Tyrocinium* describing calomel (Davidson's annotations to Boyle; a chemist's notes, not a historian; Multhauf and Debus not reached).
- Glauber's *Furni novi* as the first book with full furnace designs (Mineralogical Record).
- Hutchinson's five of six psoriasis cases (NRC 1977 extract).
- Electroplating beginning to replace fire-gilding after 1840 (Schwarcz 2023).
- Principe testing several Keys in the laboratory (via Wikipedia).

Contested and given both ways on the page: Newton's poisoning; Charles II's death; the Paris antimony reinstatement (1665 or 1666); Nuysement's *Traittez* (1620 or 1621); the arsenic lethal range (sources differ; 50 to 300 mg across ATSDR, Wikipedia and IPCS); the tartar emetic emetic dose (1870 full dose 130 to 194 mg against modern 30 to 65 mg); Plato of Tivoli as translator of the Latin vulgate (who first proposed him is unconfirmed); the date of Glauber's salt (about 1625 or 1658); Bunsen's cacodyl explosion (1843, Babcock 1841); Faraday's charge (seven grains in Cotton 2017, "a few grains" in Babcock); Newton's hair mercury (fifteen times normal in Lesney, up to about forty in other reports); the bain-marie credit to Maria.

## Where the real record and existing canon pull apart (for the docket; nothing ruled here)

The page states each of these neutrally as "WOTR's own" and changes no canon. Each is a candidate for a CONFLICTS or docket row.

1. **Heat instruments (Alchemetrica against R53-01).** Alchemetrica: "There is no instrument. There has never been an instrument." R53-01, with the 2026-09-28 calendar ruling (span from 690 IC, present 715 IC), admits everything invented 1800 to 1900. Inside that span, per Darling 1911: Daniell's expansion pyrometer (1822, the first continuous reading), Prinsep's gas pyrometer (1828), Pouillet's colour table (1836), Byström's calorimetric pyrometer (1862), Siemens's resistance pyrometer (1871), Seger cones (1886), Le Chatelier's thermocouple (1886 to 1887) and his optical pyrometer (1892). The mercury thermometer (1714) and Wedgwood's pyrometer (1782) are older. The page now states both canon positions side by side and settles nothing. **No CONFLICTS row exists; the parent session should record one.**
2. **Borosilicate glass** (Schott, Jena, 1880s to 1890s) lies inside the same span. It still softens near 820 °C, so Alchemetrica's "will not take the top of the range" holds; its thermal-shock clause does not. Also inside the span: the Marsh test (1836) and the Reinsch test (1841), which no WOTR page names. Same CONFLICTS row as item 1.
3. **The Bed.** Alchemetrica: blood-warmth for six to nine weeks "without any possibility of overheating." Real dung heat starts at 50 to 68 °C, sags, needs turning, and large piles can ignite.
4. **The Long Furnace.** "Weeks or months without a hand on it." No measured unattended burn time for an athanor was found; the real long runs were tended in shifts.
5. **Calcination time.** Alchemetrica's four hours to three days; real calcinations ran to four days (tin in a crucible) and twelve days (Lavoisier). This is where Isaac's "long calcinations" points.
6. **Parathesis.** Alchemetrica defines it as a false step the reader spots as absurd; Newman (who coined the term) means a heaping-up of synonyms or needless terms and processes.
7. **Refractory ceramic "porous."** Real good crucibles and stoneware were dense; the cupel was porous by design.
8. **Crucible palsy.** Onset "at roughly nine years" and "permanent, progressive": real onset follows air concentration (weeks to years), and the real disease partly fades once exposure stops.
9. **Strongwater fume "kills everybody in it in the order they were standing nearest."** Real nitrous fume kills after a free interval of three to thirty hours; nearest-first holds for dose, not for time of death.
10. **Fire lute proportions guarded.** Real lute recipes were printed and circulated widely.
11. **"No partial Sublimation."** The real bench sublimed partially and repeatedly. Canon has no plain bench sublimation (Alchemetrica's four-operation rule; K5, K6 put only Calcination and Distillation outside the six), so the page now says there is no partial Sublimation and treats the real unrisen residue as the counterpart of canon's fused mass.
12. **Codex Vol I credits** (from the names lane): Holmyard's English translates the Jabirian *Kitāb Usṭuqus al-Uss al-Thānī*, not the *Sirr al-Khalīqa*; the Vol I notes (line 172) run the two together.
13. **Flyleaf credit.** "That which courseth above..." is the flyleaf of Volume the First (vol I lines 11 to 13), quoted again in Volume the Second's text (line 249). Volume the Second's own flyleaf is "There are two kinds of men who study the dead." `research-names.md` line 182 credits it to Volume the Second; the page is corrected, the research file is left as written.
14. **Draft-mark.** Neither Alchemetrica nor the Codex says a Draft-mark never fades; the page no longer reasons from that. The Codex's pale nail bands read as Mees' lines, which grow out in five or six months.

## Left out, and why

- Modern esoteric temperature tables for the degrees of fire: they come from present-day websites with no period source.
- "Three pints of saliva a day" as a number: popular sources only, attribution disputed; the page names it only as disputed.
- The claim that "the eagle" is sal ammoniac, and the green lion devouring the sun as aqua regia dissolving gold: no scholarly source found.
- Van Helmont's gas bursting sealed vessels: not confirmed.
- The peacock's-tail chemistry (thin-film interference): a guess in popular sources only.
- Ripley's "ardent water" as acetone and the burned grey wolf as antimony fuming off: the research lane's own inferences, unconfirmed by a historian.
- The St Isaac's Cathedral gilding deaths (60 workers): weakly sourced.
- Mercurialentis and the 1 in 500 pink-disease figure: outside the period or single-sourced, and not needed for any canon check.
- Diana's tree timings from Homberg (a quarter-hour): single-sourced; Lemery's forty days is kept from his own text.
- Wall thickness of the philosophical egg, lamp-furnace temperatures, and athanor burn times: no figures found.
- Legal rights questions and the canon tensions: kept here, off the published page.

**One-paragraph shape.** Volume I is a young man's first book: Kwon Mu-jin, eighteen going on nineteen, writing in Urbis in 696 IC about the four years since he walked out of the court at fourteen. It is a memoir of a lost home (a valley that had thinned before he was born), of three older friends (Malphas, Furveus, Neros) who put an unlicensed book on his table, of an illness his thirteen-year-old sister came to treat, of a hall under Genesio where a coffin carried a name he did not know was his, of Doryun's quiet theft discovered in a lecture room, of a café where he did not walk out, of a road, an amulet and a monastery, and of a king who wept. It ends with a room giving him a name and his refusal of it. Everything he believes about correspondence is a mirror and everything he believes about memory is that nothing is erased; both are the young author's errors, which the later volumes answer.

## 5. The metaphysics the volume states, in canon terms

**The Great Work** `[GW1-GW4; WS1; K5; K6; Heresiology]`. Seven stages: Calcination, Dissolution, Separation, Conjunction, Fermentation, Distillation, Coagulation; "disciplines imposed upon both matter and maker" (Heresiology), taught by the Monastery as discipline before technique. The double seven maps them onto the Temperance ladder (GW1, list in section 1). Each stage's root current per WS1. Calcination and Distillation are bench operations (K6), done at the Burning and at the Whitening in the Returner (GW3). Fermentation begins in the Rot and completes at the Feeding (GW2). The colours: Blacking, Whitening, Reddening (Alchemetrica); colours out of order or too fast mean abandon the working; a working that blacks then reddens without whitening "has skipped the purification".

**The Tria Prima** `[GW4; source; research]`. Sulphur = Essence Core, Salt = Aether Shell, Mercury = Attraction Layer. Furveus separates a compound into its three, purifies each, recombines them. Recombination is a Wedding (Conjunction), "the most dangerous operation in the discipline"; incompatible laws forced together without the Parunic mediation glyphs produce Rupture, and "the vessel is the first thing to lose". Show one Wedding at his bench with the mediation glyphs laid and Mu-jin's lens off `[proposal]`. His medicines steady Shells and heal bodies and never refuel a reserve (EC2).

**The Aphorism of Mortalis** `[GW9; WS3; DE7]`. The old debt doctrine of the necromantic tradition, found by Mu-jin in the open stacks: the power to raise the dead defeats itself; to raise the dead requires a diminishment of the living; the practitioner is a debtor who borrows the current of Mortalis and cannot step back out of it without cost to his own Crystal. Mortalis is Auren's Still Gate (Vitalia, "the Black Gate": "the soul engages ending as a structural event rather than a catastrophe"). Any river image is Sylorin's soulstream, a picture. No gloss; no "He summons himself".

**Mortalis and Anamnesis, as the young author gets them wrong and right** `[WS3; WS4; WS5; MJ4]`. Right, from Ara: an ending can be a passage to ease. Right, from MONK and Auren's edict: the Still Gate is walked through in peace. Wrong, from Maelor's edict taken as physics: "nothing that has been is erased". He writes the error into his manuscript in good faith; Volume III corrects it (Anamnesis reads records in ground and objects, and can erase what it reads).

**Correspondence** `[SE11; GW8; ST1]`. "As above, so below" in Volume I is the mirror reading: the same law in the sky and in the Crystal, read as the Heralds' "structural law" and as JABIR's synthesis. It is the young author's thesis and a canon position only as a reading (K4 framing). The Tablet is recited three times: by JABIR (his gloss of the amulet), by MONK (his father's words), by Mu-jin in the king's hall. The Tablet's closing keeps its own speaker ("Trismegistus", ST1); Mu-jin quotes it and does not claim it (fixes). Credit the three English translations' real sources in the author notes (R48-27); do not reproduce them in any public summary.

**Glyphs and Draftcraft** `[GL1; GL4; Four Crafts; Descent of Parun]`. Index names in chains, one teacher-name gloss per glyph explained. "There is one script. There have always been fourteen ways to be wrong about that": varieties of written Parun are hands. Draftcraft "puts a working into the hands of somebody who cannot work"; it needs a Crystal only at manufacture; its cost is provenance ("somebody sold them to you, and there is a ledger"). A Drafter in this world is a journeyman rank; Master of the Circle scribes, seals and carries liability.

**The amulet** (section 3). **The Gate** (section 4).

**Arsenic, mercury and the body's bill** `[EC6; Alchemetrica "What it costs the body"]`. Crucible palsy: onset at about nine years of bench service; a tremor in the writing hand, then head and jaw, with a withdrawal of manner families notice first (irritability, then shyness, then not wanting to be looked at). Draft-mark: discolouration in the whites of the eyes and under the nailbeds, keyed to what the practitioner handles most; "a renderer can name a man's whole career off his hands in under a second". Furveus shows the early tremor and the nailbed mark; Mu-jin reads both on sight. **Research list before print (EC5, EC6; to feed The Real Alchemy)**, every item to be checked against encyclopaedia-level sources and not asserted from memory:
- Mercury as medicine (quicksilver; calomel, mercurous chloride, as purgative; corrosive sublimate) and chronic mercury poisoning (tremor; erethism, the shyness and irritability that match crucible palsy; gum disease and salivation).
- Arsenic as medicine (white arsenic, arsenic trioxide, as a tonic in small doses) and chronic arsenic poisoning (Mees' lines, pale transverse bands across the nails, which suit Draft-mark; skin darkening; thickened palms and soles; nerve damage in hands and feet).
- The real Paracelsus's dose principle (that the dose alone makes a thing a poison or not, Septem Defensiones), credited in author notes only.
- Real spagyric practice: the plant's "mercury" as the fermented and distilled spirit, its "sulphur" as the distilled oil, its "salt" as the calcined ash of the spent plant, leached and crystallised, then recombined. This maps onto the Rot and Feeding, the Returner, and the Burning.

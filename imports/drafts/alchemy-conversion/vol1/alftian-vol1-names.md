## 1. Mu-jin's own title for the book (ST22)

**Form:** Gameung-nok
- Hangul 감응록, hanja 感應錄.
- The Revised Romanization is Gameungnok. The house hyphenates, as in Sum-gol and Geuk-hon.

**Culture and convention:**
- The language is Sum-gol, the house tongue of the Mahuo court line. That puts it in the Korean stratum (R23-2), and R8-21 says "Korean ask, Korean name."
- The title follows the Sino-Korean -rok (錄, "record") pattern that real Korean books use: Jingbirok, Pyohaerok, Gyeonmunnok.
- Rok ("record") is also an element of the Mahuo inventory (R37-1, Ledger & record). The title therefore ends on a house element. RR spells it -nok because the r assimilates to n after ng.

**Meaning:**
- 感 means "to be moved", 應 "to answer" and 錄 "a record". 感應 is the real East Asian term for stimulus and response.
- In classical cosmology (天人感應, "heaven and humankind answer each other") it names conduct answered in the sky with no contact between the two.
- In Korean devotional use it is a spirit's answer to sincere asking.
- **Gloss:** the Record of Response.

**Why it is his:**
- The house law of Sum-gol is that a summoner cannot compel: he is answered or he is not.
- The Codex argues for correspondence with no energy moving (SE10), and Volume I's king's hall is conduct answered in the sky.
- Volume III has something answering at Rimward and leaves it unnamed (MJ12). The title names the answering and never the answerer.

**Say it:** gah-MUNG-nohk. Korean has no stress accent, so keep the three beats even. The m carries onto the second syllable, and the r of rok is heard as n.

**On the page:**
- Volume I's head reads "THE ALFTIAN CODEX", then "Gameung-nok · the Record of Response".
- The overview's name list gets "Gameung-nok: Kwon Mu-jin's own title for the book".
- The Alftian Codex stays the archive title everywhere else (ST22), and the two names stand side by side (R50-13).
- Never set it in italics (R50-32).

**Script: recorded, not ruled:**
- R50-31 says true names are romanised only, with no hangul or hanja on cards, pages or prose.
- One Mahuo card prints hanja anyway. Kirishima Hae-jin's True name line reads "Kirishima Hae-jin (霧島 海鎮)", and R58-13 corrected that hanja instead of removing it.
- No card prints hangul. Kwon Mu-jin's card and the Primate's (Mahuo Do-hyeon) carry no script at all.
- Either way, the Codex text shows only "Gameung-nok". The hangul and hanja above are for the record, and for a Hae-jin-style True name line if the house keeps that precedent.

**Collision:**
- "Gameung", 감응 and 感應 each match 0 files in wiki/ and scenes/. There is no sound-alike.
- Considered and set aside: Gyeonmun-nok (見聞錄, "Record of Things Seen and Heard"). It fits Mu-jin as witness (MJ11, MJ13) but says nothing of the book's argument.

## 2. The three ST1 renames

### Gillus De Raits → Gisli Draycott

**Form:** Gisli Draycott. He gets a full card under ST7. As a surname in the texts he is "Draycott", so the Necrocursica's method credit reads "Draycott and Strom", with the name credited to Mu-jin (MJ13).

**Culture and convention:**
- He belongs to the Inner World's Northern stratum, which is English and Norse (R23-2; R20-1 Concord anchor). Stratum follows institution (R23-3): he is a heartland scholar whose family name went through the Accord's filing.
- He carries a given name and a frozen surname. That marks the family as chartered, urban and documented (the R20-1 class marker), which suits a Stage VII scholar of means who seals his letters.
- Gisli is Norse and outside the forty-name Concord pool. That is in-register all the same:
  - The register is "English and Norse".
  - Canon already carries names from outside the pool (Edward, Tabitha, Renard).
  - R50-18 makes outlier names free.
- The Icelandic accent drops (Gísli becomes Gisli), because Northern names carry no marks (R50-15).

**Meaning:**
- Gisli comes from Old Norse Gísli, usually derived from gísl, "hostage, pledge". It suits a man who holds things in pledge: an author compelled, a dead woman's unfinished decision.
- Draycott is an English locative frozen into a surname. It comes from Old English dræg, a drag or portage where loads are hauled over, and cot, a cottage.

**Sound kept:**
- The G-L trochee of Gillus survives in GIS-lee.
- The d-r-ay-t of "De Raits" survives inside DRAY-kot, fused the way medieval bynames froze.
- No part of the Earth name (Gilles de Rais) survives whole.
- Gisli keeps two syllables. The unidentified "two syllables" Mu-jin says in his sleep (scenes/wotr_the_warrior_nurse.md:57; audit km-15) can therefore still be this name if a writer wants it.

**Say it:** GIS-lee DRAY-kot

**Collision:**
- Gisli and Draycott each match 0 files.
- There are only soft sound-alikes, allowed under R50-17, and none of them is in the Codex:
  - Gisei, the Moto Offering seal, pronounced GEE-say and in another register.
  - The DRAY- onset of Draven Kael Vorrick, Ilyria Draven Morningstar and Atheron Dreymarch.
- Rejected on the way:
  - Gellir: it reads as Uri Geller, a real Earth psychic.
  - Harrow: it collides with Examiner Harrowgate, Maud Harrowgate and the Echo Harrow technique.
  - Darrow: the hero of a well-known novel series.

### Vaughaus Thom → Ivor Strom

**Form:** Ivor Strom. He gets a roster entry under ST8. His paper becomes "the Strom Submission".

**Culture and convention:**
- He also belongs to the Inner World's Northern stratum. Ivor is in the canon Concord given-name pool (R20-3).
- Strom is a frozen locative surname: the family by the current. Being frozen, it reads as chartered or guild-filed, which suits a man whose paper sits filed in the Genesio Archivum.

**Meaning:**
- Ivor comes from Old Norse Ívarr, usually read as yew (the bow wood) plus warrior.
- Strom comes from Norse straumr, "stream, current" (Swedish ström, German Strom). It suits a restless old man, and a site that pushed back harder the more he drew on it.

**Sound kept:**
- Strom rhymes with Thom, so "Thom's activations" and "the Thom Submission" keep their beat.
- The V of Vaughaus survives in Ivor.
- The Earth name (Thomas Vaughan) is gone.

**Say it:** EYE-vor STROM

**Collision:**
- Ivor and Strom each match 0 files. The only sound-alike is the word "ivory".
- Rejected on the way:
  - Marten: already two people in canon, Ser Marten Quay and Marten Hillborn.
  - Tomas: the pool's form of Thomas.

### Jabir → Venur

**Form:** Venur, one name.
- Volume III's "ibn Hayyan" slot takes nothing. Eressean naming has no patronymic, and in Eresse that slot is filled by the spoken witness clause, not by a surname.

**Culture and convention:**
- He comes from the New World border where Eresse's old ground meets the chartered arc (SE2). The Geography page describes that quarter as "elven old-ground and the chartered arc".
- His name belongs to the old ground. It is in the Eressean (Vey-Elarin) register under the R20-1 Elven anchor: names are held property, publicly witnessed, and names are offices.
- R8-21 files Eresse as "Latinate with elvish morphology", and the Vey-Elarin grammar page governs the shape.
- His race is left to his roster entry. Border humans of Eresse do bear such names, for example Iryen Maevith (Varrisak and human, of Kael'Nir).
- R23-4's principle holds: a man of two orders who carries one register has made a statement. His learning is the synthesis that his name declines.

**Meaning:**
- It compounds two attested roots: ven, "seen", and ur, "to witness". Together they read "the seen, witnessed": one who stands witness to what is seen.
- The grammar compounds root on root itself (Sylvaar, Seralyth).
- Phonotactics check: onsets are single consonants, and the coda r is an allowed liquid. No stop ends the word.
- Because in elven logic a name is an office, the name reads as a sky-watcher's office. That is the gloss only. Nothing is added to Eresse's page (SE2).

**Voice beat available (not canon until Isaac reuses it, R50-28):**
- An Eressean asked for his name says whether it was witnessed.
- Venur can answer in -ama, "the name I am holding, and you may ask by what right". That is the border man in one word.
- Eressean speakers of Common also name their witnesses unprompted.

**Sound kept:**
- JAH-beer's shape survives in VEH-noor: two syllables, weight on the first, a long high vowel running into a final r.
- Eressean has no J sound, so the J goes. The emerald eyes stay.

**Say it:** VEH-noor. The r is a light tap and never trilled.

**Collision:**
- Venur matches 0 files.
- There are soft sound-alikes, allowed under R50-17 and none of them in the Codex:
  - Karo Venrik, a card with 102 mentions, and Venrius with 5, share VEN-.
  - "Venus" appears once in prose.
- Rejected on the way: Torven, which already exists in canon (10 files).

## 3. The dragon-claw monk (ST19)

**Form:** Penn Ralfsohn

**Culture and convention:**
- He is a Concord human in the Urbis heartland's register (SE6). That is the convention the new Papers author also takes (PA3: a small name pool and a byname surname).
- His given name, Penn, is from the canon Concord pool.
- Ralfsohn ("son of Ralf") is a live patronymic in the canon form, after R20-3's "Aldric Bramsohn".
- A live patronymic reads rural and unchartered (the R20-1 class marker): a hunter's son from Urbis's hinterland who came into the order.
- Canon gives the Heralds no vow-name custom, and Holy Sea ordination naming (R20-2) belongs to that church alone. So he keeps his birth name. If the order's Factions page later gives it vow-names, this stays his birth name.

**Meaning:**
- Penn comes from Old English penn, "fold, enclosure". It is a pool name, so the meaning does not carry weight.
- His father Ralf, the Dragon Hunter who died hunting, also has a pool name, from Old English Rædwulf, "counsel-wolf".
- The patronymic names the dead father every time the son is addressed. In a house of memory he carries his father in his name, and there is no grave. That is the father's death that ST19 asks for.

**Say it:** PEN RALF-sohn

**Collision:**
- Penn, Ralf and Ralfsohn each match 0 files.
- Rejected on the way:
  - Edric: Edric Ashmore already appears in the Kharven scenes.
  - Garret: 70 hits.
  - Haldor as the father: "Haldorsohn" would sit beside the Fortress-Monastery of Haldrek.
  - Soren: it echoes Søren Kierkegaard, a real person, for a grieving philosopher.

## 4. The Heralds and their Monastery (SE4, SE5)

**What canon has:**
- There is no formal name, and no Herald order at all. The audit's finding (ws-09) was rechecked and holds.
- Canon heralds are an Archon's individual servants. The Fourteen Titans page says an Archon has "a seat, a court, a lineage of heralds"; Vaerath Ashveil is Herald of Auren and Saetsha Bloomveil Herald of Elyndra.
- Maelor's pages (The Fourteen Archons; Pair II, Memory Against Horizon) name no herald, order or house. They give him Keeper of Memory, Mnemoris, the Echo Vaults and the Triad of Law.

### The order

**Form:** Ordo Praeconum Maeloris, "the Order of Maelor's Heralds".
- In the common tongue: the Heralds (the Codex's own word), or the Heralds of Maelor.

**Convention:**
- The Accord Latin / common-tongue doublet now reaches factions (R50-23), and the speaker picks.
- The Latin is in the liturgical register the orders keep (Accord Latin page, sections II and IV).
- The pattern follows canon's Ordo Sanctae Inquisitionis.

**Correct Latin (R50-12):**
- Praecō, praecōnis is the Roman herald and public crier; the genitive plural is praecōnum.
- Maelor is declined as a native Latin -or noun, genitive Maelōris (as honor, honōris).

**Say it:** OR-doh pry-KOH-noom my-LOH-riss. Accord Latin keeps c hard and holds ō long. Maelor alone, in Common, is MAY-lor.

**On the page:**
- Mu-jin writes "the Heralds", the POV's own name for them (R20-5).
- The Latin appears once, where the order speaks formally: the Sacred Discourse or the Prior's letter.

**Collision:**
- Praeco, praeconum and Maeloris each match 0 files.
- One soft sound-alike: Maeloris against Vaeloris, a place with 50+ hits in the Vaeloris scenes. They differ by one letter; R50-17 allows it, and the Latin form is rare on the page.

### The Monastery at Urbis

**Form:** Domus Memoriae, "the House of Memory".
- In the common tongue: the House of Memory. In Urbis and in the Codex it is "the Monastery".

**Convention:**
- It uses the same doublet, in real Latin: domus, "house"; memoriae, "of memory".
- The pattern is the medieval Domus Dei, the name hospitals took. That fits an order that also keeps a hospital and a Medical University.
- It holds the Urbis Archivum in its lower floors (SE4). The older Genesio Archivum is a separate outlying house and is not named here.

**Say it:** DOM-oos meh-MOR-ee-eye

**Collision:**
- Domus, Memoriae and "House of Memory" each match 0 files.
- It stays distinct from canon's Ledger Monks (The Fourteen Titans), the Dreamfold Monastery (Shiran Kazuren's card) and the Fortress-Monastery of Haldrek (The Goblinoid Peoples).

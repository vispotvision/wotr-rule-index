## 2. Named for the first time

### Seok (Doyun's family name)

**Form:**
- Seok. In full: Seok Doyun, family name first (R23-2).
- Written once, where Mu-jin enters him on the school roll. Everywhere else he is "Doyun".

**Culture and convention:**
- Korean stratum, family name first (R23-2, R20-1 Mahuo anchor).
- Doyun is a Sum-gol valley child, not of the house:
  - His given name is outside the Mahuo thirty-element inventory (R37-1).
  - He carries no hollow-seat, which R23-10 reserves for the house.
- Sum-gol is Ketsuen's most mixed province, and the Mahuo are about a tenth of the kingdom (Ketsuen page). A valley family with a surname and no seat is the ordinary case there.

**Meaning:**
- Seok (석, 石) is "stone". It is a real Korean surname (the Seok of Chungju).
- A surname carries no felt meaning for the people who bear it. The gloss lives in these notes and the page claims nothing.
- It suits the one of nine who was left. The page does not say so.

**Why a surname at all:** Mu-jin tells the boy that "a list with the names spelled properly is worth more than a list" (scenes/WOTR_Vaeloris_Sequence.md:1926). The roll he keeps is the one place the full name belongs.

**Say it:** SUHK DOH-yoon. The vowel is the one in "hut", and the final k is barely released.

**Parents:**
- They stay roles (R50-27).
- If one speaks twice: Seok plus a two-syllable given name from outside the Mahuo inventory, written unhyphenated like his son's (flag 1). Check it for collisions then.

**Collision:**
- Seok matches 0 files. The substring "seok" matches 0.
- Korean-stratum surnames already in use: Kwon (Kwon Mu-jin; Kwon Hae-ryu), Mahuo, Kirishima, and Ha (Madam Ha Seol-ok, a pitch name at scenes/geturo_the_host_line.md:105).
- Rejected on the way:
  - Ha (河, river). It suits a rice-valley family, but it sits beside Ha Seol-ok and reads as a laugh on the page.
  - Nam (南, south). It carries a modern Earth echo.
  - Cha (車, cart). It reads as tea.

### Nabi (Doyun's cats, optional)

**Form:** Nabi, for both cats.

**Culture and convention:**
- Korean nabi (나비), "butterfly". Korean households give it to cats by custom, and "nabi-ya" is how a stranger calls any cat.
- It is carried into Sum-gol as valley custom (R53-25).
- It is logged as a coinage and becomes canon only if Isaac reuses it (R50-28). Never set in italics (R50-32).

**Use:**
- The second cat, the "smaller, more resilient cat", is also Nabi. Mu-jin records that flatly.
- The reader sees the boy did not choose a new name. This keeps ST11's "replaced" beat and gives the beat its world-only detail.
- If the drafter prefers the cats unnamed, nothing breaks.

**Say it:** NAH-bee

**Collision:** 0 files. The substring "nabi" appears only inside "inability".

### The Rule of Silence (was the Monastery's "Path of Silence")

**Form:**
- The Rule of Silence, in the common tongue.
- The order's formal Latin is held, not used in Volume II: Regula Silentii (R50-23).

**Why rename (a call, not ST1):**
- Fracture of Worlds has exactly four Paths: Body, Spirit, Attraction and Fate (II. Grades, Gates and Thresholds, Part Seven).
- A capitalised "Path of Silence" reads as a fifth Path to anyone who knows the sheet.
- Check 27 (R14-6) would list it as an unknown capitalised system term. R14-5 wants system words exact.

**Convention:**
- A monastic Rule is a discipline chosen and kept. That is what the source sentence says of it: "stillness is chosen and maintained with discipline".
- The Heresiology already has monasteries teach the Great Work "as discipline before technique", and lists "silence fasts" among its permitted correctives. The Rule is where a Herald learns that fast.

**Meaning:**
- Rule: a house's written discipline.
- Latin: regula, "rule"; silentii, genitive of silentium, "of silence". Real, correct Latin (R50-12).

**Say it:** plain English. The Latin is REH-goo-lah sih-LEN-tee-ee.

**Collision:**
- "Rule of Silence", "Regula" and "Silentii" each match 0 files.
- Soft sound-alike: Silentia, the counterplay Category (9 files; The Magical Categories). The shared root is why the Latin stays off Volume II's page.

- Dissolution Perverted, the Corruption of Submission.

- They are cited by name, never by ordinal (R61-83).

**The other four:**
- Volume II's appendix lists Conjunction, Fermentation, Distillation and Coagulation Perverted by stage name only.
- Their epithets (Instrumental Union, Catastrophic Germination, Sanitized Truth, Tyrannous Finality) wait for Volume III's one paragraph (GW6, R61-84).

**Say it:** plain English.

**Collision:** the forms are canon's own. There is nothing to collide with.

## 3. Kept, and new to this volume's page

### The Volitional Trace (Mu-jin's name for it)

**Form:** the Volitional Trace, first named in Volume II's appendix.

**Credit (ST3, MJ13; R61-13, R61-45):**
- Madeleine Ault found it.
- Strom found it independently.
- Draycott framed the theory and built the mechanism.
- Mu-jin named it, and only named it. He is never a co-builder.

**Culture and convention:**
- A term of art in Latinate English: "volitional" comes from Medieval Latin volitio, "an act of willing".
- It is Mu-jin's word, in the tongue he deposits in for common use.
- R8-21's own-language rule governs a practitioner's techniques, not a scholar's term, so the common-tongue form stands.

**Meaning:**
- The trace of a decision left unfinished at death.
- K4 makes it system canon. DE3 (R61-74) makes it an Attraction Layer commitment settled into ground as Mnemata and read by Anamnesis.

**Latin doublet, held for the Accord's filing (R20C-46):**
- Vestigium Voluntatis, "the trace of the will" (vestigium, "footprint, trace"; voluntatis, genitive of voluntas).
- Not on Volume II's page, because there is no Accord there (R61-42).

**Say it:** vo-LISH-un-ul TRAYSS. The Latin is ves-TIG-ee-oom vo-loon-TAH-tiss.

**Collision:**
- "Volitional Trace" matches 3 files, all Codex pages.
- Vestigium and Voluntatis match 0.

### Harmonic Imprint, Sympathetic Bond Trace, Parunic Echo

**Forms:** kept as the source writes them. They are Malphas's three from the first edition (K7, ML7): what stays after the Crossing's nine days, the residues the two-year-old commission draws on.

**Convention and meaning:**
- The **Harmonic Imprint** is what a person leaves in the places they lived. It also absorbs the place-imprint half of the later edition's Noospheric Echo (DE1, R61-72). Volume II does not cite the later edition.
- The **Sympathetic Bond Trace** runs between a Crystal and those it was bound to. It thins with time.
- The **Parunic Echo** is the trade's word for a practitioner's Essence Signature carried in Aetheric Residue, readable from Stage IV (GL10, R61-97). The name stays: fixes.md's "Parunic Residue" is superseded by that ruling. "Glyph-trace" is retired.

**Say it:** plain English.

**Collision:** each matches 2 or 3 files, all Codex pages.

### Madeleine Ault and Cassian Ault

**Forms:**

- Cassian Ault: the widower who paid for it.
- ST12 and ST13 (R61-22, R61-23) keep both names.

- Madeleine is the French form of Magdalene, a saint's name, allowed under R50-05 and R50-06.

**Culture and convention:**

- Neither given name is in the forty-name pool. Both are outliers, free under R50-18.

**Meaning:**
- Madeleine goes back through Magdalene to Magdala, "tower".
- Ault is a frozen English surname of uncertain origin. The page claims nothing for it.

**Say it:** MAD-uh-lin AWLT; KASS-ee-un AWLT

**Collision:**

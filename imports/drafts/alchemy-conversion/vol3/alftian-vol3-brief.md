## 0. Shape, in one paragraph

Volume III is the same man continuing the same book, written after the Ashgate road in a room with an east window in Urbis, on one lung, left-handed, and addressed to his sister. It opens on Rimward, the question he promised Ara he would bring to her first: he asked without standing, something answered, and the eye was the price. Then it goes back and tells the three years the second volume did not: the night in 699 at the Carver's Seat of Vaeloris when a Legion-class working answered a twenty-two-year-old with chalk and a book, and the woman who watched it and came up the valley after him, bore his son, refused to be married and asked not to be written down; the east room after the deposit and the nine children in it; the Division's four assessments and the rank he took so the valley's children could be certified; the road from place to place, the Zettari camp, the Accord he drafted by lamplight and the teaching vow he swore on a quench; and the late summer when eight of his nine pupils sat the kingdom's examination at the Seat on the morning Kujo took it. The orchard, four days down, and waking to Frithia counting his pulse. Then the present: the letter the Searcher carried up his stair, set square to the desk edge and left unopened four months while he wrote everything above; the platform at Castlefall over terrible coffee; the hall at Genesio, where the oldest Trace, aimed at his own signature, completes into him through Anamnesis and gives him the Correction (correspondence is a law of response, answered late and on credit, settled out of sight), which is the frame for Rimward and never the name of what answered. He closes the promise of the four remaining Corruptions in one paragraph, names the Weight's first cause as his own first wrong guess, answers Doyun's question about whether reading wears a record, goes home, is asked to marry by a woman who once refused to be settled, lets her strike two sentences from the book, and swears an eighth vow against his own corruption on the record in the east room. The teacher's arc is the spine (VD-1); Frithia is its hinge (VD-2, FT-1 to FT-7).

## 1. The frame

### When and where it is written, and deposited (CALL, stated)

- **Written:** in Urbis, in the lodging with an east window above the square where he was brought from the Ashgate road on one lung `[canon: Papers Entry the Seventh]`, from the late autumn of 701 IC through the eleven months of his convalescence to the early autumn of 702 IC `[R61-3 K3; R62-1]`. **CALL:** the last chapter and the Appendix are written in the east room at Sum-gol in the autumn of 702, after he comes home and after the marriage (FT-7: they marry in 702, after his months in Urbis). One sentence says he deposits it anyway, because the vow says common use and the vow is his.
- **Why after the marriage:** VD-2 calls her "his wife"; K3 says Volume III "must mention Frithia and infant children"; the book is the place where the woman Volume II left out at her own request lets herself be written, and she can only let him once she has read it (she strikes two sentences, section 4.9). - **Alternative (rejected, and the one-line swap if Isaac prefers it):** deposit at the end of the eleven months in Urbis, early autumn 702, before the wedding; the last pages anticipate the marriage at the first snow and never narrate it. Cost: "my wife" becomes "who will be my wife"; Frithia's consent to be written must be given by letter.

## 3. Isaac's direction as the spine: the teacher (VD-1)

The arc is **a man who writes things down learning that teaching is the part that acts.** Volume I apologised for explaining; Volume II took "Teacher" because it names the work; Volume III shows what the work costs and ends with him binding himself to act on what he writes.

### 3.1 The Sum-gol school after the second volume (spring 700 to summer 701)

- **The room** `[canon: Vol II; card :176; Sequence :1942]`: nine on the east room's roll (eight entered by spring 699, Doyun the ninth). ### 3.2 The road from place to place (autumn 700 to summer 701)

The fourth gave him the Division's rank: **Archmagus of the Division of Alchemetrica** `[canon: card]`, and a grade, again, before anyone asked him what he thought (the title-writ wound, touched without naming the title).
- **Why he took the rank (CALL).** A rank is a licence: an Archmagus may certify teachers and enter pupils for the kingdom's examination. His Desire is knowledge made common inheritance; the rank is the lawful road to it for children who are not of the blood. He took it for them and writes that he knew what else it was.

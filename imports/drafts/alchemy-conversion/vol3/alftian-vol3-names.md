## 2. The east room: the eight who die

Volume II's roll, in its own order: "two rice-farmers' boys, a carter's twins, the potter's two daughters, a fisher's boy from the lower reach and the smith's youngest", then Doyun, the ninth. The plural possessive makes the rice-farmers two households. Six families, eight children. The sexes of the twins and the smith's youngest are my call, which leaves four boys and four girls.

| Roll | Name (family name first) | Who | Say it |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Hong Mansik** | a rice-farmer's boy | HONG MAHN-sheek |
| 2 | **Baek Chilbok** | a rice-farmer's boy, another house | BEK CHIL-bohk |
| 3 | **Tak Seojun** | the carter's twin, the boy | TAHK SUH-joon |
| 4 | **Tak Seoha** | the carter's twin, the girl | TAHK SUH-hah |
| 5 | **Ahn Dahye** | the potter's elder daughter | AHN DAH-hyeh |

| 8 | **Hwang Iseul** | the smith's youngest, a girl | HWAHNG EE-sool |
| 9 | **Seok Doyun** | canon (R65-9) | SUHK DOH-yoon |

Korean vowels carry no stress. The say-lines put the English stress first, as Volume II's did for Doyun. In Seoha and Seojun, "seo" is the vowel of "hut"; in Mansik the s before i is "sh".

**Culture and convention:**
- Korean stratum, family name first (R23-2; R20-1 Mahuo anchor). The children are valley commoners outside the house, so they follow Doyun's published shape:
  - no hyphen;
  - no hollow-seat, which marks the Mahuo (R23-10), and which canon writes "of Yeol-gol" (Kwon Hae-ryu);
  - no syllable from the Mahuo thirty (R37-1). Checked against the live inventory: Sum, Hon, Ryeo, Yeong, Won, Sae; Jang, Kye, Bo, Ye, Chae, Rok; Gol, Ga, Ju, Chang, Dae, Ryeong; Jin, Jeong, Chak, Ban, Kyeol, Seol; Eun, Sun, Hwa, Yeon, Bit, Soo. None is used, in either slot.
- Siblings share a syllable, which is R23-2's generational syllable in plain family use: Seo- for the twins, Da- for the sisters. The potter shared the sound, not the character: Dahye's Da is the Sino-Korean "many", and Dasom is a native word taken whole. On the page nothing says so (R50-31: no script).
- Romanisation is Revised, as Doyun and Seok are. One exception: **Ahn**, the common official variant of RR "An", because "An" reads as the article.
- The names mix old rural forms (Mansik, Chilbok) with plainer ones (Seojun, Jiho, Doyun). That is how a valley roll reads. The page does not remark on it.

**On the page:**
- Each full name appears once, as entered on the roll "in the school's hand" (Volume II). After that Mu-jin writes the given name, as he writes "Doyun".
- They call him **Teacher**, as Doyun does.
- The eight are not the two-syllable name he says in his sleep (the Warrior Nurse). If "this house" is Sum-gol, Frithia had two years there and would know every name on his roll. That scene's place is still open (the timeline's contradiction 7). Either way, the drafter should not give the sleep-name to a pupil.

**Meaning (notes only; a given name carries no felt meaning for the page):**
- Mansik: "ten thousand plantings".
- Chilbok: "seven blessings", an old commoner's name.
- Seojun: "auspicious, gifted".
- Seoha: "auspicious summer".
- Dahye: "much grace".
- Dasom: an archaic native word for love.
- Jiho: "wise and broad".
- Iseul: native, "dew".
- Surnames are glossed nowhere. Hong, Baek, Tak, Ahn, Yang and Hwang are all real Korean surnames.

**How they die:** that is the drafter's, and no new name is owed for it. Canon offers the morning the pines came down on the terraced yard's outer wall, with people under it (kaalabad_star_crusher), and Volume II's folk name "the lung-sickness". "Star Crusher" is Kaalabad's word, and Mu-jin cannot use it unless the volume shows him learning it. Canon's "forty small figures" on the terraces are the whole school, not his room.

## 3. The teaching road

Volume II already has the circuit: "a bench and a slate in each" of the valley's settlements (R65-11; its closing chapter). His letter to Ara adds the road out: "the same as every morning since we left home." Canon names come first. Nothing below has to be used.

### Canon names on the road (no new name owed)

- **The valley:** Sum-gol; the east room; the terraces; the orchard; the lower hall; the potters' settlement; the lower reach; the Kept Vigil.
- **Ketsuen** (keh-TSOO-en): the warded track from Sum-gol to **Senri's Gate** (one hundred and forty leagues; a courier's nine days, a wagon train's three weeks); **the Heartstone** and **the Ward-Carver's Academy**; **the Corridor**; **the Lake Districts**; **the Uplands**; **the Frontier Marches**; **the Thornback March**; **Chainward Landing**; **Vaeloris**.
- **Stations are counted, not named.** The Ketsuen Inventory: "way-stations every twenty to twenty-five leagues, and distance counted in stations." So Mu-jin writes "the ninth station on the warded track". Each station has "a resident carver".
- **Castlefall**, **Genesio** and **Urbis** are on the line.

### Optional coinages (logged under R50-28; canon only if Isaac reuses them)

**Longfetch** (a Lake Districts fishing settlement)
- Form: Longfetch, one word.
- Convention: Ketsuen's frontier settlements carry plain common-tongue compounds (Senri's Gate, Chainward Landing, Thornback Ridge).
- Meaning: a lake's **fetch** is the open water the wind runs over before it raises a wave. A long fetch means heavy water, and fishers name places for it. The chain-lakes lie in glacial scars.
- Use: the Lake Districts' lakeland fishers are mostly beastkin (Ketsuen page). Their own name for the place is not needed and is not coined (R50-22 allows one later).
- Say it: LONG-fetch.
- Collision: 0 files; substring 0.

**Freehold** (a Frontier Marches settlement)
- Form: Freehold.
- Meaning: land held outright. In Ketsuen, holdings are stewardship and neglect forfeits them (the Proving of Absence). A frontier settlement named Freehold says to every Proving what its people crossed the world for. That is the Inventory's Gone list, "the ungoverned frontier", made into a place.
- It is a real English word and a real Earth place name (R50-03).
- Say it: FREE-hold.
- Collision: 0 as a whole word. Soft echo: canon's "Independent Concord Freeholder", an Accord status (5 files). The echo fits, since Freeholders are the unaffiliated. Allowed under R50-17.

**House-tongue doublets for the valley** (R50-22; the POV picks, and Volume II's English stays primary)
- **Gama-teo**, the potters' settlement: "kiln site", the Korean word for the ground of an old kiln. Say: GAH-mah-tuh.
- **Naru-teo**, the landing on the lower reach: "ferry landing". Say: NAH-roo-tuh.
- The house tongue hyphenates at the element boundary (Sum-gol, Geuk-hon, Gameung-nok).
- **-gol** was avoided on purpose. Canon writes hollow-seats as "of X-gol" (Kwon Hae-ryu of Yeol-gol), and a commoner hamlet named with -gol would read as a Mahuo seat.
- Collision: 0 files for either form, hyphenated or not.

## 6. Other names new to Volume III

### The Strom Submission's title

- **Form:** *On a Residue of the Dead That Keeps an Intent*. The Strom Submission, deposited about 689 IC in the Genesio Archivum's sealed stacks, anonymous, from a Castlefall postal address (ST2; the Papers).
- **Why these words:**
  - They are the review note's own ("a residue of the dead not in the catalogue. It keeps an intent", the Papers). The note is where the Papers caught Draycott's "described nowhere ... in the Genesio Archivum".
  - Draycott's first letter in Volume II says "the impression keeps an intent". That echo is wanted, since Draycott had passed on Strom's message.
- **What the title avoids:**

## 10. Flags (recorded, not resolved)

1. **C-118, open.**
   - Eight new unhyphenated Korean-stratum names follow Doyun's published shape (the Volume II names file's section 6 rule).
   - By R23-10's wording, all nine read as lineage-hall names.

   - Nothing here names the Accord's assessments (C-120) or prints a Stage or reserve (C-121).
3. **Owed row: Geturo and Hiromi against the Korean stratum.**
   - Both children are "Mahuo (Korean stratum)" on their cards.
   - Their given names are three syllables, unhyphenated and Japonic-sounding. R23-2 and R37-1 give the stratum a two-syllable hyphenated given name from the Mahuo thirty.
   - R23-4 says mixed parentage takes one register, not a blend.
   - Nothing on any page explains it. Volume III prints the given names as canon has them and claims nothing.
4. **Owed (timeline contradiction 15): Vaeloris's size.**
   - The Carver's Seat has about 10,000 people (the Ketsuen page). The place Kujo zeroed had 741 souls on the parish roll along one street (the Vaeloris Sequence).
   - The Frithia ruling makes the Seat the place Kujo destroys.
   - Volume III writes "Vaeloris" and "the Carver's Seat" and gives no head-count, street count or parish roll.
5. **Owed (contradiction 14): two Rimwards.**
   - The Nalūn page has Nalūn's eastern frontier city. The Ketsuen page has "Rimward province". The Sequence has the Holy Sea's marches, with a boom and a quarantine house.
   - Volume III names Rimward and the Rimward house and places neither.
6. **Owed (contradiction 18): the Mahuo–Valorin cousinship** (Hiromi's card; Taesyn). This is the reason no Valorin relative is named.
7. **"Archmagus of the Division of Alchemetrica."**
   - The rank is card canon. But the Guild Accord page lists five Divisions (Strategic, Enforcement, Arbitration, Research and Archives, Logistics and Supply) and the Black Concord, with no Alchemetrica. Cards and the Parun grammars use "the Guild Accord Division of Alchemetrica" as if it were one.

   - The Errata's section V, "What the Correction Costs", uses the same capitalised word for the Concordance's recount.

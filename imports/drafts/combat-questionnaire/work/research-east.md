# Research: Eastern, anime, manga and Korean web-novel combat writing

*Work file for the combat questionnaire, 2026-10-04: what each tradition does on the page, what WOTR could take, and what it would hit in current law. System facts cross-checked against reference summaries. Nothing here is law; all descriptions are paraphrase.*

## What is already settled and must not be re-asked

CB1 to CB7 (R70-100 to R70-106), MY1 to MY9 (R70-68 to R70-76), LR8, LR11, LR12, LR23 (R70-85, R70-88, R70-89, R70-91), DD6 (R70-57), VO2 (R70-11) and K3 (R70-3, with R70-9).

## 1. Wuxia I: Jin Yong and the named form

**What it is.** Wuxia ("martial heroes") is Chinese fiction of sects and wandering fighters. Jin Yong (Louis Cha, 1924 to 2018) wrote its classic serials, among them *The Legend of the Condor Heroes* and *The Smiling, Proud Wanderer*. His fighters train named forms from manuals on internal energy (neili, the trained inner reserve).

**On the page.**
- *Granularity.* Fights move form by form. A named move is announced by the narration (sometimes by a watching master), its intent glossed in a clause, then answered by a named counter. A duel between masters can run dozens of named exchanges, chained.
- *The name carries the content.* His most famous palm art takes each of its moves from a line of the *Book of Changes* (the I Ching), so the name tells the learned reader the move's intent. The name is a doctrine.
- *Counter-technique as knowledge.* In *The Smiling, Proud Wanderer* a hermit teaches the hero a sword art whose whole method is reading the flaw latent in any opponent's form and striking there, so its user needs no fixed forms of his own. The fight becomes a contest of who knows the other's school better.
- *Internal-energy duels.* Two masters press palms together and the contest becomes a motionless transfer of inner force, steam off the skin, whoever yields first crippled. Damage is internal injury (neishang): blood from the mouth, meridians (the energy channels) damaged.
- *Rhythm.* Long, balanced sentences for the forms, a short line for the result, and a crowd of sect elders commenting.
- *Space.* Tracked loosely; lightness skill (qinggong, trained leaping) bends it.
- *Endings and aftermath.* Fights often end in a bow, a sealed acupoint (dianxue, a strike that paralyses by blocking an energy point), or a wound that drives months of plot: the cure, the debt, the grudge.

**Into WOTR.** The hall-trained duellist whose arts carry a long true name glossed literally (R70-74, R8-22), each move's name stating its doctrine, and a counter-school that reads other schools' flaws as R12-3's documented-technique rule already allows.

**Collides with.** R70-35 bans the translated stock ("qi surged") in narration, so the inner force must be written in Codex nouns (R70-68). Acupoint sealing and the palm-to-palm transfer have no ruled mechanism, and the transfer would need EU figures from the Essence Ledger, never invented (R12-5). Internal injury that lets a man cough blood and keep fighting meets R60-01 for the unranked and §7.1's same-anatomy row for practitioners. Dozens of named forms press against R70-73's one display beat.

## 2. Wuxia II: Gu Long and the one cut

**What it is.** Gu Long (1938 to 1985), Jin Yong's contemporary and opposite. His books (the Li Xunhuan and Lu Xiaofeng cycles among them) are spare, dialogue-heavy and modernist, influenced by Western thrillers and Hemingway.

**On the page.**
- *Granularity.* Often one exchange, or none on the page. The duel is the wait before it: two men, a long talk or a long silence, and the blow that ends it is not described. The reader learns who won when one man falls.
- *Rhythm.* One-line paragraphs, many of them; a sentence broken across lines for the stress. Repetition of a phrase as a drumbeat.
- *Speed.* Rendered by absence. The weapon is never seen in flight; nobody, narration included, saw it move.
- *The read.* Everything is in the read: the stance, the hand's steadiness, the state of mind. The famous swordsmen are defined by sincerity of purpose; a man whose heart is unsure has lost before he draws.
- *Cost and aftermath.* A wound is a red line; then wine and loneliness.

**Into WOTR.** Gu Long is the Verdict grammar's purest model: the read takes the length, the motion takes a line (R70-102), the deciding moment is a held instant (R70-57), and R70-20's one-line paragraphs at peaks license his shape.

**Collides with.** The finisher still owes the full eight-item floor and three causal sentences (R13-4, R13-4-THREE_EXPLANATIONS) and full clinical gore (R48-34), so the unseen cut must be made seen afterwards, on the body. R48-33 asks every exchange traced; Gu Long's single exchange must still be traced.

## 3. Xianxia and cultivation battles

**What it is.** Xianxia ("immortal heroes") is Chinese web fiction about cultivation: climbing named realms (Qi Condensation, Foundation Establishment, Core Formation) toward immortality. Examples: *Reverend Insanity* (Gu Zhen Ren, already a WOTR model under R70-6) and Er Gen's novels.

**On the page.**
- *Realm gaps decide.* One realm up is nearly unbeatable; the drama is the genius who crosses a realm by treasure or sacrifice.
- *Treasure fights.* Flying swords, talismans and artefacts are drawn and spent like ammunition; a fight becomes an exchange of inventories, and loot is the aftermath.
- *Formation fights.* A formation (zhen, an array of flags, stones or inscriptions) seals ground under its own rules; the fight is about finding its eye, its core node, and breaking it.
- *Tribulations.* A breakthrough provokes heaven: lightning in waves that the cultivator must survive or die. The breakthrough is itself the fight.
- *Voice.* Fast numbered moves, onlookers gasping, the arrogant young master humiliated (the face-slap).
- *Cost.* In the ledger-minded works, every gain is bought. *Reverend Insanity*'s Gu (living insects that are both technique and consumable) die when spent, so each fight empties a purse.

**Into WOTR.** Realm gap is already WOTR's Stage gap read through Pressure; the usable imports are the formation fight as a Domain or a warded ground whose eye must be read, and the tribulation as a breakthrough written inside its Catalyst crisis (R70-91), with Wells as the world's hostile answer (R53-06).

**Collides with.** R70-35's cultivation-translation ban and the R5-B tells (onlooker shock, escalation). Realm-crossing upsets must be reconstructible from the sheet (R13-8). Item use falls under R46-2's tiers and the once-only item card (R70-95). The face-slap is legal only with a bill (R70-112). Heaven sending lightning at a breakthrough has no WOTR mechanism; a "tribulation" would need a ruling or a Front.

## 4. Naruto (Masashi Kishimoto)

**What it is.** A shōnen manga (for a young male readership), 1999 to 2014, about ninja whose techniques (jutsu) run on chakra (a body-and-spirit energy) shaped through hand seals. Already WOTR's named model for the Technical register (R12-2) and for the knowledgeable second (R12-4-VOICE_TWO_SECOND, "the Kakashi model").

**On the page.**
- *The tactical read.* Fights are puzzles. In Shikamaru's exam duel with Temari his technique's rule (the shadow reaches only as far as its area allows) is shown, she learns it, he extends it with new shadow sources, wins the position, then forfeits because his reserve is empty.
- *Counter-technique chains.* Fire against water, wind feeding fire, a clone substitution answering a lethal strike. Each move answers the last.
- *The rule reveal.* A late-arc opponent's power is a mystery until a deduction exposes its rule (Kakuzu's stored hearts, Hidan's ritual circle). The reveal is the climax; the kill follows quickly.
- *Explaining voices.* Spectators on the rail explain (Kakashi, Gai), and the opponent explains in contempt. Kishimoto often runs three or four explainers per fight.
- *Team combos.* Inherited family formations (the Ino-Shika-Cho trio) chain one clan's technique into the next: bind, possess, crush.
- *Cost.* Chakra exhaustion collapses fighters; Rock Lee's opened Gates (inner limiters forced open) break his own body; a forbidden technique costs a life.
- *Flashbacks.* Mid-fight flashbacks belong to everyone, opponents included (Haku, Gaara, Neji).

**Into WOTR.** Already in the law. What is left to take is the team combo: two practitioners' workings chained into one result, which WOTR could carry through Resonant Pairs.

**Collides with.** R12-4-VOICE_MIXING_RULE caps explaining voices at two per engagement. R70-103 makes flashback POV only, so the opponent's past never gets its own scene. Chakra-coloured auras meet R9-3-AURA_AS_FRACTURE. An elemental advantage chart has no WOTR equivalent outside Domains (FOW Part Twenty-Two: opposed Wellspring Houses interfere); a duel-scale counter table would be new. R47-1 keeps combos off the ability card itself.

## 5. Bleach (Tite Kubo)

**What it is.** Shōnen manga, 2001 to 2016, Isaac's named model. Soul Reapers carry a zanpakutō (a sword that is a soul with a name). The first release (shikai) is called with a command and the sword's name; the second (bankai) transforms it. Reiatsu is spiritual pressure, a felt weight.

**On the page.**
- *The spoken release.* An imperative and a name, then the transformation, then the effect. WOTR's R8-24 release mechanic (an imperative verb plus the true name, costing a beat and buying output) is this convention, already law.
- *Bankai as reveal.* The second release is withheld for arcs; its first appearance is a display beat with a full page.
- *Stylish stillness.* Kubo's signature is the paused panel: two figures standing apart, coats moving, a line of dialogue, white space. Then a single slash and an "already behind you" reversal, the wound opening after the swordsman has passed.
- *Pressure as weight.* Lesser souls kneel, sweat, cannot breathe near a captain; walls crack. Strong reiatsu is drawn as a coloured pillar.
- *The rule revealed late.* Aizen's sword works by complete hypnosis triggered by having seen its release; the reveal recasts the whole arc. The rule was there all along; only its explanation was withheld.
- *Ranks.* The Espada (elite enemies) carry numbers by strength, a visible ladder.
- *Verse.* Each collected volume opens with a short poem, a register WOTR has ruled in (RH5 verse header).
- *Cost and aftermath.* Variable; many fights end on a speech and a fall, with wounds closed by healers offstage.

**Into WOTR.** The paused tableau before the cut is R70-57's held instant at the scale of a whole duel, and the reiatsu weight is R70-69 exactly: the room kneels first.

**Collides with.** R9-3 bans the glowing aura; the coloured pillar must become broken geometry or a room effect. The late rule-reveal fights R70-71 (rules shown before the fight), unless the rule was planted unexplained, as Aizen's was. R48-43 allows one crafted speech, and Kubo's mid-fight speeches are many. Healer-offstage meets R47-11 and §7.1 (Vitalia costs both parties). On release count, R50-09 (no limit; each call costs a beat) is newer than R8-26-ONE_RELEASE_PER_SCENE, which still reads live; the tension is flagged, not resolved.

## 6. Hunter x Hunter (Yoshihiro Togashi)

**What it is.** Shōnen manga, 1998 onward. Nen is life energy trained through basics (Ten, holding it; Zetsu, hiding it; Ren, flaring it; Hatsu, the personal ability), with applied skills such as Gyo (energy in the eyes to see hidden Nen) and Ko (all of it in one point, the body left bare). Restrictions and Vows: a user who binds an ability with self-imposed limits gains power in proportion to the limit's severity.

**On the page.**
- *The battle of deduction.* Fights are logic. The opponent's rules are inferred from evidence, tested, and exploited; the winner is often the one who understood his own limits better. Hisoka's elastic aura is a simple power used with endless invention.
- *Vows that power a technique.* Kurapika binds his chain to use against one gang only, on pain of death, and is far stronger for it. Gon trades his whole future potential for one fight's strength and is ruined by it.
- *Exchange granularity.* Very fine at climaxes: a narrator caption gives seconds elapsed, the split of aura between attack and defence, the reasoning of each fighter in turn.
- *Narration.* Togashi's narrator is openly omniscient: captions, percentages, timestamps, and both fighters' minds.
- *Cost.* Concrete and permanent: lost limbs, a vow's penalty paid, a body aged or wrecked.
- *Endings.* Often outside the fight's own terms: Netero's last move against the Ant King is a bomb in his own body, not his martial art.

**Into WOTR.** The deduction fight is R12-3 and R70-71 in their strongest form; the vow is the most promising import, since FOW already has the substrate (Attraction Force keeps vows binding, an Oath Sub-Stat, Keyed artefacts that answer a vow).

**Collides with.** No WOTR rule trades a self-imposed limit for output; a vow-for-power ratio would be an invented number until ruled. Aura percentages in-fight break R70-88 (no running figures). The omniscient caption breaks the POV lock and the two-voice cap; R70-10's storyteller lives only at chapter thresholds. Gon's sacrifice is closest to Obsession Force "at escalating soul-cost" (§7's table), which is ruled.

## 7. Jujutsu Kaisen (Gege Akutami)

**What it is.** Shōnen manga, 2018 to 2024. Cursed energy (power from negative emotion) fuels innate cursed techniques. A Domain Expansion, cast with a hand sign, encloses a space in a barrier where the caster's technique is guaranteed to hit (the sure-hit). When two Domains meet, the more refined one wins. Binding vows trade conditions for power; one form of vow is disclosing your technique's workings to the opponent, which strengthens it.

**On the page.**
- *Domain clashes.* Two barriers grind, and the narration reports which is pushing which and how fast; a large refinement gap ends it at once, a small one takes time. Counters exist: Simple Domain (a school's small personal field that cancels the sure-hit), and expanding your own.
- *Burnout.* After a Domain, the caster's technique is unusable for a time. The window is the counterattack.
- *Exposition as weapon.* Fighters explain their techniques mid-fight because the disclosure is itself a vow that raises output. The lecture has a mechanical reason.
- *Rhythm.* Dense explanatory panels, then very fast sequences; deaths are abrupt and unspectacular, sometimes off-panel.
- *Cost.* Bodies destroyed, reverse cursed technique (healing) available only to a few.

**Into WOTR.** FOW Part Twenty-Two already orders a Domain clash (Temperance differential, two Stages up overwrites; then Dominion Sovereignty and Gravity; then Wellspring compatibility; then Tempering Coherence and Maturity), so a clash can be written as that hierarchy decided step by step.

**Collides with.** FOW says Domains are not techniques and are not deployed like a punch; JJK's hand-sign casting is exactly that, so a called Domain needs a ruling. The sure-hit is not on FOW's list of Domain behaviours (Low-G, Hush, Heat-Invert, Vectoring, Construct Sustain, Inertial Dominion) and would be new canon. FOW's collapse makes a Resonance Shockwave; JJK's burnout is a different cost. Disclosure-as-vow brushes R8-26-NO_SELF_TRANSLATION (explaining is legal in an opponent's mouth under R12-4; translating one's own name is not). In battle a Domain breaks cohesion and the rout kills (Mass Combat Guide §5), not a sure-hit.

## 8. Demon Slayer (Koyoharu Gotouge)

**What it is.** Shōnen manga, 2016 to 2020. Swordsmen use Breathing styles (Water, Flame, Thunder and others), each a set of numbered, named forms, against regenerating demons that die only by beheading with a special steel or by sunlight.

**On the page.**
- *Numbered forms called aloud.* "Water Breathing, Tenth Form" and its name, then the move. The count shows mastery; a new form is an event.
- *The opening scent.* The hero smells a thread of scent running to where the cut should go. Perception names the opening.
- *Monologue under strain.* Long inner exhortation inside one motion.
- *Visual metaphor.* The anime paints the forms as water, flame and lightning that are not physically there.
- *Cost.* Broken ribs and still fighting; the Mark (a power-up that burns lifespan: bearers die young).
- *Aftermath.* Demons' memories of their human lives as they dissolve; weeping over the enemy.

**Into WOTR.** Numbered forms by school are legal now (R70-74); the perception-first read already has a WOTR face in Yoko Mishiro's assignment (nose before eyes, from the bind), so the scent-thread is a ready model for any sense-led Blade fighter.

**Collides with.** Painted-water imagery must be either a real phenomenon with a stated mechanism (R13-2) or a simile, never an unexplained effect, and no glow (R9-3). Fighting on broken ribs meets §7.1 (same anatomy, same clock) and R60-01. The enemy's dying flashback breaks R70-103. Lifespan-for-power has no ruled exchange rate. Exclamatory inner monologue risks R70-35's LitRPG stock ("stronger than ever").

## 9. One Piece (Eiichiro Oda)

**What it is.** Shōnen manga, 1997 onward. Haki is willpower in three kinds: Observation (sensing, at its height brief foresight), Armament (hardening, shown as blackened limbs), Conqueror's (a pressure of will that knocks the weak-willed unconscious). Power scale is enormous: admirals reshape islands.

**On the page.**
- *Scale.* Two admirals fight for days and permanently change an island's climate, half frozen, half burning. Conqueror's Haki is shown first by the room: ships creak, people faint in rings.
- *Parallel matchups.* An arc's climax splits into one duel per crew member.
- *Long fights.* A boss fight runs across many chapters, with power-ups (Gears) that cost lifespan or leave the hero helpless afterward.
- *Comedy and numbers.* Jokes mid-blow; one arc's enemies carry a printed power rating.
- *Aftermath.* The feast. Warmth after carnage, then the bill (bounties, enemies made).

**Into WOTR.** Conqueror's Haki is WOTR Pressure already (three Stages up drops people), and Oda's room-first staging (ships, rings of fainting men) is R70-69's own order; the admirals' fight is the Band gap that "changes weather".

**Collides with.** Blackened armament limbs meet R9-3. The printed power rating mid-fight breaks R70-88. Comedy inside danger stays gallows wit (R70-116), with full comic beats only for comic voices named on cards (R70-113). Others' flashbacks break R70-103. Long boss fights run against the reserve clock (R60-03): most reserves empty in under two minutes at full draw, so a days-long fight needs low-draw arithmetic.

## 10. Vagabond and Musashi: the spiritual duel

**What it is.** Eiji Yoshikawa's serial novel *Musashi* (1935 to 1939) fictionalised Miyamoto Musashi's life; Takehiko Inoue's manga *Vagabond* (1998 onward, on hiatus) adapts it. Musashi's own *Book of Five Rings* supplies the doctrine: the gaze that perceives strongly and sees weakly (kan and ken), rhythm (hyōshi), and preventing an attack at its first stirring.

**On the page.**
- *The spiritual duel.* The decisive event is inside: fear, attachment to winning, the mind fixing on the opponent's blade. Takuan's teaching of the unfixed mind (mushin, "no-mind") is the climax of several fights.
- *Silence.* Inoue draws wordless pages: eyes, breath, the weight of a foot. Speed is shown by a still image with nothing between two poses.
- *The spearman.* Against the Hōzōin spear school, Musashi first loses to fear itself, then returns.
- *The seventy.* At Ichijōji, one man against the whole Yoshioka school: exhaustion, a crippled leg that he carries for the rest of the story, the horror of the count.
- *Yoshikawa's Ganryūjima.* The wait is the weapon; arriving late, the oar carved into a sword, the tide.
- *Aftermath.* Shame, grief, the question of what the killing made of him.

**Into WOTR.** A Moto or Japonic POV narrates in this school's words (R70-100), and the Verdict grammar's read can take Musashi's two gazes as its interior method; Yoshikawa's wait is the Expenditure grammar's Standing seen from the waiting side.

**Collides with.** The felt menace of a master (Inoue's spearman radiating death) is killing intent, which R70-70 keeps off the page until its mechanism is ruled. Wordless pages meet R13-4's stated read: the floor must be said in a voice. The seventy-man fight is a practitioner against levies, where R3-9 closes the practitioner's POV during the engagement unless it stays a duel in form.

## 11. Kingdom (Yasuhisa Hara)

**What it is.** Seinen manga (for adult men), 2006 onward, on China's Warring States and Qin's unification, following Li Xin from servant boy to general.

**On the page.**
- *The hill and the ditch.* The story cuts between generals reading the field from a height and a foot soldier in the crush. This is R70-106's shape.
- *Generals as types.* "Instinct" generals feel the battle's flow; "intellect" generals model it. Matchups of type drive the drama.
- *Stratagem.* Encirclement, feigned retreat, the flank strike at the general; terrain and hour matter.
- *Presence.* A great general's presence is drawn as a looming giant over the field; men's morale lifts at sight of him.
- *Generals' duels.* Commanders meet in single combat mid-battle, and the battle pauses around them.
- *Aftermath.* Casualty counts, merit rewards, promotions by deed.

**Into WOTR.** The hill and ditch cut is ruled; the type pairing of commanders (feel against model) is a characterisation tool the Mass Combat Guide does not yet carry.

**Collides with.** The Mass Combat Guide's tell bank bans the heroic hold: lines hold by drill, cohesion and the rank behind, never by willpower, and Kingdom's will-driven rallies cross it. The looming-giant presence must become Pressure in the room (R70-69). A high-Stage commander on the hill is an open gap the guide records against R3-9. Champion duels mid-battle follow the declaration rule at the line (R70-101). Troop figures must trace to a source.

## 12. Solo Leveling (Chugong)

**What it is.** Korean web novel (from 2016) and manhwa (Korean comic). Gates open onto dungeons; Hunters are ranked E to S. Sung Jinwoo, the weakest, receives a private System (a game interface only he sees) that gives quests, levels and stat points, and later the power to raise the dead as a shadow army. Ticked by Isaac against the recommendation (R70-6).

**On the page.**
- *Screens.* Bracketed system messages interrupt the prose: quest issued, level gained, penalty applied.
- *Level gaps.* An S-rank is a different species; the reassessment that overturns a registered rank is the arc's turn.
- *Speed by others' failure.* Onlookers cannot track him; the narration shows their confusion.
- *Shadow army.* Summoned dead absorb damage and return; the master stands back.
- *Cost.* Low.
- *Aftermath.* Loot, a new shadow, guild politics.

**Into WOTR.** The reassessment is WOTR's public re-assay (R70-92), and the shadow army is the Expenditure grammar's Offering and Absence, written from the summoner's side.

**Collides with.** R70-78 makes the Crystal readout the bearer's own sheet only, blind to wounds and to others, and voiceless; the System's quests, penalties and appraisals of monsters need an instrument or a ruling. Brackets inside sentences were not taken (R70-74); set-off blocks come only at milestones (R70-80). A level mid-fight meets R70-88 and R70-91. Onlooker shock is an R5-B tell, which R70-72 lifts only for a chorus in hearing. Growth without cost breaks R48-37 and R70-90 (numbers move only for a shown cause).

## 13. The Beginning After the End (TurtleMe)

**What it is.** Web novel (2017 onward) and webtoon (a vertical-scroll web comic); Isaac's named model. A king from another world is reborn in a mana world as Arthur Leywin. A mana core purifies through colour stages (black, red, orange, yellow, silver, white, most split into dark, solid, light); mages are augmenters (body) or conjurers (spells), with elemental affinities and rarer deviant offshoots such as ice under water.

**On the page.**
- *The reincarnated tactician.* First-person narration that reads every fight with a veteran's eye in a young body: what the opponent's stance says, how much mana is left, which element answers.
- *Stage-gated fights.* Core colour is announced and decides most outcomes; crossing one is a set-piece breakthrough.
- *Backlash.* Overdrawing damages the core; Arthur's core is shattered by a superior foe and he rebuilds around a different power.
- *Academy before war.* Lectures and spars plant the rules that later battles spend.
- *Aftermath.* Long recovery, grief, political consequence.

**Into WOTR.** The veteran's read in an untested body is R12-4's POV voice at its most useful, and the academy that plants rules is R70-71 (and SP5's tournament and examination arc).

**Collides with.** First person is barred in turns, scenes and books (R70-12). "Core" in WOTR common speech names a Wellspring site (ruled 2026-09-12), and the Essence Core is a layer of the Crystal, so "mana core" cannot be borrowed as a word. Core colours map loosely to Soul Crystal tiers and Stages but any equivalence would be invention. Affinities sit near Wellspring harmonisation and Harmonics, deviants have no WOTR row. Core damage maps to Crystal State (Fractured) and Overchannel risk, which are ruled.

## 14. Omniscient Reader's Viewpoint (singNsong)

**What it is.** Korean web novel (2018 to 2020). The world becomes a game run by goblin hosts (dokkaebi) issuing scenarios; cosmic beings (constellations) watch and sponsor. Kim Dokja is the only reader of the novel the world now follows, so he has foreknowledge.

**On the page.**
- *Scenario combat.* Fights are won by reading the scenario's rules for loopholes, often without fighting the monster.
- *The audience.* Constellations' reactions arrive as bracketed messages mid-fight, a chorus of gods commenting and paying coins.
- *Probability.* An act too improbable for the story invites a backlash that can kill; power is limited by narrative plausibility.
- *Foreknowledge decays.* The more he changes, the less his knowledge holds.
- *Aftermath.* Rewards counted, companions' trust shifts.

**Into WOTR.** The fight won by reading the arena's posted rules is R70-71 turned tactical, and decaying foreknowledge is Isaac's "meta knowledge, never omniscience".

**Collides with.** The commenting gods are a chorus outside the POV's hearing, which R70-72 does not license, and voiced messages break R70-78 (voiceless lines). Kim Dokja's skill reads others' minds, against the POV lock (R39-7). Probability backlash has no ruled analogue; FOW's Attraction Layer and the Plane of Fate are the nearest substrate, unruled for this use.

## 15. Lord of the Mysteries (Cuttlefish That Loves Diving)

**What it is.** Chinese web novel (2018 to 2020), Victorian-gothic, already WOTR's model for the Mystic register (R12-2), the document voice (R12-4-VOICE_FOUR_DOCUMENT) and the look (R53-28). Beyonders (its practitioners) drink potions to climb a pathway's Sequences (from 9, lowest, to 0) and must "act" their Sequence's role to digest the potion, or lose control and become monsters.

**On the page.**
- *Mystical caution.* Fights are short and avoided where possible. Knowledge can contaminate; a name spoken can draw a hostile attention.
- *Preparation decides.* Divination before, charms, a paper substitute that takes a fatal blow, a sealed artefact (a dangerous object with a drawback) carried at a price.
- *Abilities by Sequence.* Each Sequence grants a small fixed set of powers, known to the reader; fights are clean applications of a stated toolkit.
- *Rituals in a fight.* Rare and expensive: an altar, an honorific name of three lines, a petition answered or not.
- *Rhythm.* Calm reasoning, then a sudden, brutal exchange.
- *Aftermath.* Hiding evidence, harvesting what the dead Beyonder leaves (a conserved residue), paperwork.

**Into WOTR.** Prepared combat fits WOTR exactly: the fight opens on a document or a reading (R70-16), rules planted (R70-71), the harvest of what the dead leave written under the Ledger.

**Collides with.** Climbing by potion is not WOTR's ladder: a Stage is crossed inside a Catalyst crisis (R70-91), and the alchemy rulings keep bench operations off the Wellspring list, so a draught in a fight is an item (R46-2, R70-95), not a breakthrough. Loss of control sits nearest FOW's Overgrowth (a Trait acting without the bearer's will), which is ruled; acting-as-digestion is not. A rite mid-fight follows R70-75 (numbered, set apart, then departed from).

## What cuts across the fifteen

- **The rule before the fight** (Naruto, HxH, LotM, ORV, TBATE) is already law (R70-71).
- **Pressure** (reiatsu, Conqueror's Haki, realm pressure) is WOTR Pressure; the sources show it as light, which R9-3 and R70-69 move into the room.
- **Vows for power** (HxH, JJK) are the largest unruled idea.
- **Three or four explainers** per fight against WOTR's two (R12-4).
- **Endurance through wounds** is the anime norm and the hardest collision with R60-01 and §7.1.

## Open questions this research surfaces (candidates, not settled)

- A duel between two grammars: whose shape governs (left open in the Combat Craft Guide §1).

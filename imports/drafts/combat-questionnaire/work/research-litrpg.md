# LitRPG and progression-fantasy combat: working craft for the WOTR combat questionnaire

Research note, 2026-10-04, for the combat questionnaire (magical combat, combat in general, how it is written). Scope: how LitRPG and progression fantasy write a fight, and the game-design ideas they borrow. Each entry gives what the work is, what it does on the page in a fight, one line of WOTR, and what it collides with in current law. Techniques are described in my own words; nothing from the published works is quoted. Rule ids are from `rules/doc-style-law-2026-10-03.yaml`, `out/rules.live.full.md` and the Combat Craft Guide (2026-10-03 edition). The style-level survey of the same shelf is `imports/drafts/style-questionnaire/work/research-litrpg.md`; this note does not repeat it and stays on the fight.

**Terms.** **LitRPG** (literary role-playing game): fiction where game-like levels, stats, skills and system messages are visible on the page and the plot turns on them. **Progression fantasy**: fiction driven by a character getting measurably stronger, with or without numbers on the page. **Portal fantasy** or **isekai** (Japanese, "another world"): a person from our world carried into a fantasy one. **Stat**: a numbered attribute (strength, perception). **Skill**: a named ability that levels with use. **Screen** or **notice**: a block of system text set off from the prose. **Build**: the chosen combination of stats, skills and gear a character is made of. **Theorycraft**: planning a build or a fight aloud from the rules. **OP** (overpowered): stronger than the story's challenges, so the fights stop mattering.

**Settled already, not re-asked.** The style law of 2026-10-03 decided these, and the questionnaire should list them as settled: CB1 moves named in the POV's own school (R70-100); CB2 full name-declaration before a duel, with a price (R70-101); CB3 duel shape follows the four combat grammars (R70-102); CB4 one full flashback, POV only, once per fight (R70-103); CB5 rivals who chose to spare (R70-104); CB6 killing rites and elegiac image by culture (R70-105); CB7 battles cut between the hill and the ditch (R70-106); MY1 to MY9 (the Crystal as inner map R70-68, Pressure through the room first R70-69, intent as aimed Pressure R70-70, rules shown before the fight R70-71, the crowd chorus R70-72, one display beat per practitioner R70-73, numbered rites R70-75, the world's law stated R70-76, technique name forms R70-74); LR8 in-scene readouts at appraisal, wound and Essence spent (R70-85); LR11 Grades at the read, no running figures in a fight (R70-88); LR12 body, then number (R70-89); LR23 breakthrough in the crisis, line after (R70-91); DD6 one held instant at the deciding exchange (R70-57); VO2 marked roll call in battles (R70-11); K3 Western leads combat, injury and dialogue (R70-3).

**The law the entries are measured against.** The extended floor of eight items per meaningful exchange (R13-4-COMBAT_FLOOR) inside a density budget (R13-4-DENSITY_BUDGET); every outcome traceable to a row of the adjudication table (R13-8-RECONSTRUCTIBLE_ADJUDICATION); fast death for ordinary fighters (R60-01-BRUTAL_LETHALITY); a hard reserve clock of six-second turns for practitioners (R60-03-HARD_RESERVE_CLOCK, R47-11-TIME_AND_ENERGY); cost in the body (R48-37-COST_SHOWN); no invented figure (R12-5-NEVER_INVENT_NUMBER); three readouts a scene at most (R70-86); escalation still a tell as a prose pacing register (R5-B-MANHWA_DIRECTIVE_REPEALED), legal only for practitioners while reserve lasts (R70-102); scaling vocabulary in the notes only (R13-6-SCALING_VOCAB_RESTRICTED).

## Part One: the books

### 1. Nicoli Gonnella (Necariin), *Unbound* (Royal Road from 2021; books from 2022)

**What it is.** Felix, an ordinary man from Earth, wakes in a hostile world called the Continent that runs on an all-seeing game-like system. He learns he is Unbound: able to break constraints the system places on everyone else. His companion is Pit, a chimera that levels beside him. His sheet carries abbreviated stats, Perception, Vitality, Endurance, Intelligence, Willpower, Agility and Dexterity among them. Isaac named it on 2026-09-12 as the model for stat talk.

**On the page.**
- *Granularity and rhythm.* Fights run very long (one reviewer describes a sequence of about two hundred pages) in quick, short paragraphs, broken by skill notices when a skill levels from use.
- *Speed, power, impact.* What the stat lets the body do, then the number that explains it: the impossible dodge, then the Agility figure.
- *Explaining mid-fight.* Characters talk numbers the way fighters talk reach and weight: casual, comparative, often mid-crisis. That is the part Isaac took.
- *Pain and cost.* Pain pushed to extremes; big climaxes often decided by Willpower, which turns a contest into who endures.
- *Endings and aftermath.* A kill and a cascade of notices; short downtime.

**Tension and failure.** Tension holds when the sheet says what Felix cannot yet do, so a new skill in the crisis lands as relief. It fails, by its readers' account, in stat padding, wins that outrun what was earned, and climaxes settled by a contest of will the reader cannot check.

**Into WOTR.** Between passes, a Measurewright in the gallery tells his apprentice, exactly and dryly, that the Greymane's Reflex Grade will not survive another bind at that measure.

**Collides with.** Stat talk itself is now law: R70-93 lets "any ranked practitioner" talk his own and others' numbers exactly. But R70-79 says "Another's exact figures need an instrument", so a fighter quoting his opponent's exact figure needs a coil or a report behind it. The two-hundred-page fight meets R60-03's clock ("Most reserves empty in under two minutes at full draw") and R13-4-DENSITY_BUDGET. A will contest that decides the fight with no row behind it fails R13-8; Willpower has no exact twin in Fracture of Worlds, and the nearest rows are Resilience (Anchoring, Integrity) and Attraction or Obsession Force. Mid-fight skill notices fail R70-88 ("no running figures") and R70-86's cap.

### 2. Will Wight, *Cradle* (2016 to 2023, twelve books)

**What it is.** Lindon, born Unsouled (without a usable power), climbs the advancement ranks of sacred artists, from Copper and Iron through Jade, Gold and far beyond. Power is madra, soul energy held in a core and moved through channels; a Path fixes what a person's madra does. Sacred arts split by function: Enforcer techniques strengthen the body, Strikers project attacks, Rulers command the surrounding aura (ambient power), Forgers build solid constructs of madra. Remnants are hostile spirits left by a dead artist's madra. Late in the series a mind spirit, Dross, rides in Lindon's head and runs fights in simulation before they happen. K6 already took Cradle as a model.

**On the page.**
- *Granularity and rhythm.* Brisk. A fight is a handful of decisive exchanges, short paragraphs, technique names spoken or thought, then a long beat of consequence. Rank gaps make most fights short; equal-rank fights carry the length.
- *Speed, power, impact.* Through the environment: stone cracking, a crowd forced to its knees. Everyone senses everyone's rank, so the gap is felt before a blow. Space is simple stage geography, a ring or a platform.
- *Explaining mid-fight.* A Path's rules are laid down in training long before, so the fight is the reader doing the sum. In the tournament book, seniors in the stands explain what fighters cannot say.
- *Pain, fatigue, cost.* Madra exhaustion is a hard floor: an empty core cannot cast. Pushing too much through channels burns them, which hurts and takes time to mend. Pills and elixirs restore, and are a currency.
- *The signature counter.* Lindon's Empty Palm drives a strike into an opponent's core and scrambles his madra for a moment: useless against a far stronger enemy, decisive at the right gap.
- *Endings and aftermath.* Fights end in kills, disablements or surrender, followed by advancement, rewards, and the bill.

**Tension and failure.** Tension holds because the rank ladder is legible: the reader knows a one-rank gap is hard and a three-rank gap is hopeless, so a plan that closes the gap is a real event. It fails where escalation becomes a treadmill (each enemy a size up) and where Dross's simulations remove doubt from the read.

**Into WOTR.** A Moto cadet's documented counter to Percussion, studied from a Dawi manual, works once against Rengai Zettari and fails the second time, because Rengai has read the same manual.

**Collides with.** Fits well: rank sensing is R70-79, the room-first gap is R70-69, rules planted before are R70-71, and a strike into the core sits near Resilience's row in §7. Dross's foresight collides with R12-4-VOICE_ONE_POV (a confident wrong read must stay possible) and has no WOTR source. Pills that refill mid-fight break §7.1 ("Nobody refills mid-exchange"). Advancement mid-fight is R70-91's crisis, line after. Remnants as a stock enemy would be invented; Well-spawn (R53-06) is the canon slot.

### 3. Matt Dinniman, *Dungeon Crawler Carl* (from 2020)

**What it is.** Aliens demolish Earth's surface; survivors descend a floored dungeon broadcast to the galaxy as a reality show. Carl fights with improvised explosives and a jai alai throwing basket; Princess Donut is a show cat turned caster; Mordecai is their guide. Bosses come in tiers by territory, and the System is an AI with opinions.

**On the page.**
- *Granularity and rhythm.* Fast, short paragraphs over Carl's swearing interior. A boss fight turns on one idea.
- *Impact.* Comic and horrible at once: frank gore, and the AI's achievement notes mocking what just happened.
- *Space.* The best fights are environmental puzzles: the boss is beaten by the room (a gas, a drop, a trap turned back) more than by damage.
- *Phases.* Bosses change shape mid-fight; the reader learns the new rule with Carl.
- *Cost and aftermath.* Potions heal wounds; grief stays. Loot boxes (graded reward crates) are opened later in a safe room, a ritual apart from the fight. Viewers and sponsors pay for spectacle.

**Tension and failure.** The floor timer (the level collapses, ready or not) and ingenuity under constraint. The failure is the joke eating the dread, mostly avoided by letting grief arrive cold.

**Into WOTR.** A Well-spawn in a flooded Draw Age pump house is killed by the pump house: a Guild pump crew cuts the main and lets the pressure do it, and the spoils are counted at the Guild counter the next morning.

**Collides with.** The mocking System: R70-81 keeps every readout "clinical", wit only "in the clerk's or Measurewright's note beside it"; and canon's Continuum does not lie, so a sarcastic narrator-machine is new cosmology. Comic beats belong only to carded comic voices (R70-113), and inside danger only gallows wit (R70-116). The audience works as R70-72's chorus "inside the POV's hearing", never as a cutaway (R49-09-CUTAWAYS). Potion healing meets §7.1 (Vitalia shortens, never skips, and "Healing and mending always cost more than breaking", R47-11). Loot by tier is R46-2 and the item card once (R70-95). Explosives sit inside R53-01's Imperial Age span.

### 4. Shirtaloon, *He Who Fights With Monsters* (from 2019)

**What it is.** Jason Asano, an Australian, is pulled into a world where powers come from absorbing magical essences, ranked iron, bronze, silver, gold, diamond. His abilities lay curses called afflictions on enemies, some of them stacking (each application adds to the last), and one ability cashes the stacks in at once. He fights in a party, with a shadow familiar that has many bodies.

**On the page.**
- *Granularity and rhythm.* Long, talky fights, exchanges interleaved with banter aimed at allies and enemies alike.
- *Explaining mid-fight.* Full ability text printed: cost in mana, stamina or health, cooldown (the wait before reuse), effect by rank; reprinted at each upgrade.
- *Speed, power, impact.* Rank gaps felt through aura (a pressing presence); a higher rank can suppress a lower one.
- *Status effects as plot.* Stacks build on an enemy, so the payoff has a visible fuse.
- *Cost and aftermath.* Mana runs out and the party rotates abilities around cooldowns; later books turn hard toward what the fights did to Jason.

**Tension and failure.** Stacks are a good engine: the reader counts toward the detonation. The failures are reprinted screens and banter that deflates danger.

**Into WOTR.** A Mahuo poisoner's three needle wounds each carry a measured dose, and the reader, who saw the dose weighed out at the bench in the chapter before, counts toward the third.

**Collides with.** Printed ability text in a fight meets R47-1-NO_APPLICATIONS (an entry says what an ability is, "never how to use it"), R70-80 (blocks only at milestones) and R70-86. Banter meets R5-B's "personality vomiting" tell, R5-D-HUMOUR_BARRED for anyone not carded comic, and R70-116 (gallows wit only inside danger); Sodoku jokes only by accident (R52-14). WOTR has no cooldown in canon: R50-09 says each release call "still costs the beat", and R8-26 (one release per scene) is printed live beside it, so any refractory rule would be new canon. Stacking curses need a ruled mechanism and real pharmacology or physiology under them. Aura suppression is R70-69 and R70-70 (the latter still waits on its mechanism).

### 5. Zogarth, *The Primal Hunter* (from 2020)

**What it is.** A System arrives on Earth and drops people into tutorials. Jake Thayne, an office worker, turns out to carry a rare bloodline that gives him an animal danger sense. He fights with a bow charged with arcane power and with poisons, and he trains in alchemy as the protégé of a god called the Malefic Viper. One of his signature skills slows his perceived time to a standstill so only his mind moves.

**On the page.**
- *Granularity and rhythm.* A single duel can run across ten chapters, in short paragraphs dense with skill names and internal calculation.
- *Speed, power, impact.* Through perception: the danger sense pings, the time-slow freezes the frame.
- *Space.* Strong. An archer tracks range, cover and kiting (keeping distance while dealing damage).
- *Cost.* Poisons work over time on the enemy while Jake's resources drain; near-death is common and he enjoys it.
- *Endings and aftermath.* Kill, level screens in blocks, then long chapters at the alchemy bench.

**Tension and failure.** Tension comes from the slow arithmetic of poison against an enemy's regeneration. It fails through length (fights that outstay their idea), through a danger sense that answers questions the read should earn, and through title bonuses stacking into numbers the reader stops weighing.

**Into WOTR.** A Hallenfeld courier captain, ambushed on the north road, wins by the dose he coated his rounds with at a bench three days back: the fight is short, the dying is long.

**Collides with.** Alchemy in combat has real WOTR ground: the Codex and Necrocursica rebuild (R61 rows), bench-only operations (R61-6-CALCINATION_DISTILLATION_BENCH_ONLY), and enhanced rounds priced at 1% of the maker's reserve each (R46-4-ITEM_COSTS). A ten-chapter duel meets R60-03 and the density budget. A time-slow faculty needs a ruled mechanism; R70-57's held instant is a description device, not a power. Title percentage bonuses meet R70-96 (titles are Guild commendations) and R12-5. An instinct sense that pings is an R12-4 POV read with the evidence removed, which §6 forbids ("the specific tell, named").

### 6. nobody103, *Mother of Learning* (2011 to 2020)

**What it is.** Zorian, a mage student, is caught in a month-long time loop and uses the repeats to get strong. No screens. Magic is spell formulae plus shaping skill (raw control of mana) trained by drills.

**On the page.**
- *Granularity and rhythm.* Plain, measured prose; short, exact fights of a few spells, each described physically.
- *Explaining mid-fight.* Almost none, because it happened before: scout, die or retreat, change one thing, try again. The reader enters the final attempt knowing the enemy's tools.
- *Impact and cost.* Effect and counter; mana finite and tracked in Zorian's head.
- *Endings and aftermath.* Retreat or death, then analysis of what to change.

**Tension and failure.** Proof that legibility needs no numbers: a reader who knows the rules can check the win. The failure is the reset, which cheapens death until the loop ends.

**Into WOTR.** Before the duel, Yoko Mishiro spends three nights watching the Shirogane blade-master drill in the yard below, and the fight is short because she already knows what his third pass does.

**Collides with.** Very little. This is R70-71 ("Rules shown beforehand") and R12-3-NAMED_INVENTOR_RULE ("A documented technique is counterable by anyone who studied it") already. The reset cannot transpose: death is permanent (R60-04-DEATH_IS_PERMANENT). Planning interiority for Isaac's PC is his (R49-01-WHOSE_HEAD). A practitioner's planning in a battle goes before or after, never during (R3-9-PRACTITIONER_POV_RULING).

### 7. Sleyca, *Super Supportive* (from 2022)

**What it is.** Aliens (the Artonans) arrived in 1963 and gave Earth a System and the chance of classes; heroes are summoned off-world on contracts. Alden Thorn, a teenager, wants to be a battlefield support hero, an unfashionable role, and his one modest skill is more than it looks.

**On the page.**
- *Granularity.* Fights are rare and short, after long stretches of school and friendship, so each lands hard.
- *Explaining mid-fight.* The drama is working out what one skill is for, under pressure. Screens are few.
- *Impact.* What the support action changes for someone else.
- *Cost and aftermath.* Trauma is the real cost; aftermath is the longest part of any fight.

**Tension and failure.** The hero is weak and the stakes are other people. The risk: readers come for fights and get few.

**Into WOTR.** A Guild mender at a sanctioned bout keeps a Greymane second alive by one placement of her hands and pays for it in fever for two days.

**Collides with.** Little. It suits R48-38-AFTERMATH, R70-114 (warmth as counterweight) and the money-before-magic texture. Support is a role, not one of the four grammars, so a support fighter's duel shape is not set by R70-102 (open, see Part Three). Vitalia's double bill (§7.1) is its cost law.

### 8. SenescentSoul, *Delve* (from 2019)

**What it is.** Rain, transported, refuses to play a hero and studies the System like a scientist. He builds a support mage around auras (abilities that run continuously and drain mana per second) and puts everything into Clarity, the stat for mana regeneration.

**On the page.**
- *Granularity and rhythm.* Fights broken by calculation: mana per second spent against mana per second recovered.
- *Explaining mid-fight.* The reader does the maths; the payoff is the arithmetic predicting the fight.
- *Impact and cost.* Through numbers more than bodies; mana as a flow problem, sustain against burst.
- *Aftermath.* Testing and tabulating. Readers split between spreadsheet lovers and skippers.

**Tension and failure.** The best tension in the genre for a reader who computes: a drain rate crossing a line at a known moment. Also the clearest case of screens replacing bodies.

**Into WOTR.** A Measurewright at a standpipe tells a Kharven sealer, figure by figure from his gauge, how long the man's draw lasts against the district main tonight.

**Collides with.** WOTR has the flow already: EU, AU/s (the rate of draw) and η (efficiency) on the reserve clock (§7.1). R70-88 keeps running figures off the page in a fight; the Stat Ledger lives in the notes (R14-6-CHECK29). A measured figure that disagrees with the card is a conflict to log (R20C-32-PRECEDENCE_FOW_WORKBOOK_CARD), never a plot point. Auras as continuous buffs have no canon, and any visual aura stays banned: "'it glowed' stays banned" (R9-3-AURA_AS_FRACTURE).

### 9. Rhaegar, *Azarinth Healer* (from 2019)

**What it is.** Ilea, a kickboxer, is transported and takes a Healer class she uses to fight with her fists: she heals herself mid-fight so she can take blows nobody else could. Her kit includes a short-range teleport and a class-specific fighting style.

**On the page.**
- *Granularity and rhythm.* Combat is the story: fast exchanges in a long fight, with two running accounts, what she spends and what regenerates.
- *Impact and cost.* In the body: bones broken and mended. Pain is something she trains against by being hurt; resources drain per exchange.
- *Endings.* A kill, then notices that by mid-series run long enough that readers skip them.

**Tension and failure.** The ledger works: a close fight reads as close. The failure is a body that refills without limit, which takes death off the table, and notice bloat.

**Into WOTR.** A Vitalia-trained Dawi brawler mends her own forearm between passes and arrives at the third pass starving, feverish and one Grade slower.

**Collides with.** §7.1 directly: Vitalia "shortens; it does not skip"; "The patient pays in the body" (scar, fever, hunger); "Healing and mending always cost more than breaking" (R47-11). R60-01's "a wounded man is out of the fight" holds for the unranked, and practitioners are "not exempt, only harder". Notice bloat meets R70-86.

## Part Two: the game-design ideas the prose borrows

### 10. Counters and rock-paper-scissors

**What it is.** An intransitive design (rock beats scissors beats paper beats rock) so no option wins everything. A hard counter beats its target outright; a soft counter makes it expensive.

**On the page.** The best prose names the counter's mechanism and its price. Cradle's Empty Palm only works at the right gap; Mother of Learning's counterspells only work if you know what is coming.

**Tension and failure.** Tension when the counter is findable and costly; failure when a hard counter appears from nowhere (an unplanted answer) or makes every fight a lookup.

**Into WOTR.** WOTR already writes this: the four grammars carry answers (the Settling breaks stance, the Shove breaks measure), and R61-110 gives a material counter that "costs whoever uses it".

**Collides with.** Fits Isaac's rule of fair abilities with findable counters, R12-3 and R47-1 (entries list counters, never applications). The words "counter", "hard counter" and "hax" as comparison vocabulary stay in the notes (R13-6-SCALING_VOCAB_RESTRICTED); on the page the counter is the floor's item 3, stated causally.

### 11. Resource economies: mana, stamina, cooldowns, the action economy

**What it is.** Every action spends from a pool (mana for magic, stamina for the body) or waits on a timer (a cooldown). The action economy is how much a fighter can do per turn.

**On the page.** Bars and numbers; the best writers show the body at each threshold (Cradle's burned channels).

**Tension and failure.** A falling pool is a clock. It fails when the pool refills on demand or the count replaces the body.

**Into WOTR.** WOTR's economy is already harder than the genre's: six-second turns, reserve over draw rate, Starvation below ten percent with stats one Grade lower and Traits flickering, the body's stamina clock beside it.

**Collides with.** No running counts (R70-88); the reserve line comes after a working, body first (R70-85, R70-89). "Cooldown" is gamer slang, legal only in a mouth by ear (R70-94). A cooldown mechanic would be new canon and needs a ruling and a source figure (R12-5).

### 12. Aggro and roles: tank, healer, damage

**What it is.** Aggro (threat) is the rule by which game enemies choose whom to attack. Roles divide a party: the tank draws and absorbs attacks, the healer restores, damage dealers kill (often called DPS, damage per second). Crowd control locks enemies down.

**On the page.** HWFWM and Delve run party fights with roles; the prose tracks who is holding the enemy's attention.

**Tension and failure.** Tension when a role fails (the healer drained, the tank broken). Failure when enemies behave like game AI, hitting whoever the rule says.

**Into WOTR.** The Expenditure grammar's Offering is aggro with a soul: put something where the opponent must deal with it. A shield-man in a Guild contract party holds a Well-spawn's attention by being the loudest, closest thing in the tunnel.

**Collides with.** Table Rule 3: NPCs choose targets off their want and their read, never off a threat meter. In battles the practitioner POV closes during the action (R3-9). Role talk in dialogue is fine (R70-93); contract parties sit with R70-97's contract boards. No in-world role names exist yet.

### 13. Status effects

**What it is.** Conditions laid on a fighter: damage over time (DoT, ongoing harm such as bleeding or poison), stun, slow, blind, a debuff (a temporary weakening), often stacking.

**On the page.** The genre brackets them as names. The best uses give a visible fuse (HWFWM's stacks).

**Tension and failure.** A condition the reader can count toward is a fuse. Failure: bracketed tags that stand in for physiology.

**Into WOTR.** WOTR's conditions are real: ATLS haemorrhage classes (the trauma surgeons' scale of blood loss, Class I to IV) with Class II anxiety and Class III confusion before pain, concussion, stress analgesia, Essence Starvation, and the Necrocursica's residues for dead and worked bodies (R61-7).

**Collides with.** Bracketed names inside sentences were "Not taken" at MY9 (R70-74). A condition reaches a readout only as the wound line "in a surgeon's terms" (R70-85). Any new magical condition needs a ruled mechanism and a phenomenon (the wotr-phenomenon pass).

### 14. Telegraphed attacks

**What it is.** In action games, a big attack shows a wind-up (a telegraph) the player can learn and dodge; the counterplay window is fair because it was signalled.

**On the page.** Primal Hunter's danger sense and Cradle's sensed rank are telegraphs; the weaker use is a sense that simply knows.

**Tension and failure.** Tension when the tell is learnable and the reader learns it with the hero. Failure when the warning is free.

**Into WOTR.** This is NATALIE's "The read is the game": a self-derived technique gets "two or three exchanges of evidence before it kills him", and Pressure through the room first (R70-69) is the world's own wind-up.

**Collides with.** Nothing in principle. The tell must be in the world (named, per §6 item 1), never a UI warning, and big surprises still need foreshadowing "a player could have caught" (R48-30-SURPRISES).

### 15. Boss phases

**What it is.** A boss changes its behaviour at thresholds (usually health), gains new moves, summons adds (extra minions), or enrages (hits harder after a time limit).

**On the page.** DCC's bosses change shape and the hero relearns the fight; long progression duels escalate by stages.

**Tension and failure.** A phase is a new rule mid-fight, so the reader re-reads. Failure: escalation as the only shape, each phase bigger.

**Into WOTR.** A practitioner's phases are physical: Starvation drops him a Grade (§7.1), a costed-out practitioner inherits every constraint at once (R3-9-COSTED_OUT_EXCEPTION), a breakthrough in the crisis changes the fight from the other side (R70-91). A Well-spawn is the natural boss.

**Collides with.** Escalation is legal only for practitioners while reserve lasts (R70-102); as a prose pacing register it stays a tell (R5-B). There are no health pools (wounds go by ATLS and Vitality), so a phase must trigger off a §7 row (R13-8). The enrage timer is the reserve clock read backward.

### 16. The build that wins by preparation

**What it is.** The fight is won before it starts: the loadout chosen, the enemy scouted, the counter brought (in card games, sideboarding: swapping cards in between matches against a known opponent). Brandon Sanderson's craft essay on magic says, in substance, that magic can solve a problem only in proportion to how well the reader understands it.

**On the page.** Mother of Learning is the model: the planning is the drama, the fight is the proof.

**Tension and failure.** Tension when the plan meets something it did not account for. Failure when the plan works perfectly and the fight is a formality.

**Into WOTR.** A Moto retainer who knows a Shirogane duellist wears proofed plate spends a season's wages on a named round (R11-3-BALLISTICS_RULING), and the fight turns on whether the one shot is enough.

**Collides with.** R70-71 and R12-3 already back it. R47-1 keeps applications off ability entries: preparation lives in the scene, invented by the owner. Isaac's PC's plans are his (R49-01). Among the unranked, preparation decides fights in seconds (R60-01). The bullet against plate keeps C-001's silence: no sentence explains why a proofed round beats proofed plate, and R20C-26 holds that "The steel stops the shot, always."

## Part Three: what carries over

**How the genre keeps tension when numbers are visible.**
1. Thresholds the reader can compute: a drain rate crossing a line, a gap one Grade wide, a dose counted toward the third.
2. Costs that bite in the body, ahead of the number.
3. Rules planted before the fight, so the win can be checked.
4. Counters that are findable and priced.
5. A gap revealed late, from evidence the reader could have read.
6. Reward ritual after the fight, apart from it, so the fight stays a fight.

**How it fails.**
1. Bloat: screens reprinted, sheets growing to pages, notices skipped.
2. Screens standing in for bodies: a figure where the wound should be.
3. Numbers that only rise, and fights that outlast their one idea.
4. Wins settled by a quality the reader cannot check (will, instinct, plot armour).
5. Refills on demand, so death leaves the table.
6. Banter and system snark that deflate the dread.

WOTR's law already blocks most of the second list: three readouts a scene (R70-86), body first (R70-89), no running figures (R70-88), the reserve clock, reconstructible adjudication, permanent death.

**Open edges for the questionnaire (none settled above).**
- Practitioner fight length: a pass or word ceiling tied to the reserve clock.
- Banter inside an exchange for voices not carded comic.
- Status conditions: whether the world names them (shock, concussion, Starvation states) and whether any magical condition stacks.
- A refractory time after a release, given R50-09 and R8-26 both print live.
- Phases: what may change a fight's shape mid-fight, and whether a Well-spawn may have phases.

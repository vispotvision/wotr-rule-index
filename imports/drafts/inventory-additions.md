# DRAFT: Standing Inventory Additions (Gone, Folk beliefs, Literacy)

**Status: draft for Isaac. Not canon.** Three new fields for every existing Standing Inventory: **Gone** (R53-31, three to five named lost things, concrete per R5-C2), **Folk beliefs** (R53-15, named beliefs about magic that characters act on and the narration never corrects), **Literacy** (R53-12). The senses entry (R51-31) is already drafted in `imports/drafts/sense-banks.md` and is not repeated here.

**How to read this file.** Every line under a culture heading is page text, written clean. The lines marked *Notes, not published* under each folk belief say what is actually true; they are for the writer and never go on the page. Items marked **(d)** are one step from an attested fact, and the step is named in the source notes at the foot of each culture. Everything unmarked is attested in the wiki mirror, a scene or the Inventory itself.

**Where each block goes if approved.** After **Body** and the senses entry, before **Recurrence**, in the culture's file at `desktop/inventories/` (Kharven's inline in `desktop/NATALIE.md`).

## Kharven

**Gone:**
- **The grass.** The open steppe the clans rode before the ground froze through and the riding stopped. "When the grass comes" still names the spring, and the spring still brings moss. (d)
- **The Fusi Vā.** The Queen's binding laid under the old clan oaths, so that a word given before witnesses carried a weight that could not be broken without consequence. It went with her on the night she was killed. The oath ceremony is still performed at the Seat with nothing underneath it, and a word in the north is once again only as strong as the man who says it.
- **The banner oaths to Muken.** Sworn to the First King personally, and famous for one thing: Muken always stopped. A beaten clan was governed and not humiliated. The oaths died with him, and the Banner Houses scattered because the thing they had sworn to no longer existed.
- **The old Seat.** Muken's stronghold, burned in the Tenrai seizure. The city was rebuilt on its surviving foundation stones, and the old courses sit at the base of the new walls, darker than the stone above them. (d)

**Folk beliefs:**
- **Never meet a Moto's eye.** A clansman speaking to the Crown looks at the chest, the hands or the floor, because the Moto read through the eyes and a man who meets them has opened his door. Children are taught it before they are taught the forearm grip.
  - *Notes, not published:* Half-true. The Shingan is an eye, and the ring-lines flare when it turns on someone. The reading does not need the subject's eyes at all; a man staring at the floor is read exactly as well.
- **The cold keeps the dead quiet.** The death-house is never heated, and a corpse that lies in the cold through the winter will not walk. Families who cannot bring their dead to a death-house pack them in snow.
  - *Notes, not published:* The cold does nothing. What keeps the dead quiet is the Waiting: nine days of attention is the diffusion window, and a family sitting with its dead is performing the one rite that actually works. They credit the wrong half of their own custom.
- **Wet wood burns eventually.** A worker who has run dry will burn again if he is fed seal fat and kept by the fire, and the first bowl goes to him the way it goes to a child.
  - *Notes, not published:* Half-true. Food, warmth and rest bring a spent man back. They do nothing for one who has gone to the bottom or broke, and the clans cannot tell the difference until the fat has failed.
- **Nothing binds on the nameless day.** Scores are settled on it, because a killing on a day when no oath holds carries no blood-debt. The death-house takes what it takes, and the Waiting begins on the first named morning after.
  - *Notes, not published:* The calendar does suspend oaths, summonses and debts falling due. It suspends nothing else. The dead are just as dead on the first named morning, and their kin keep count.
- **The night-stone remembers the fire.** A chip off the hearth-stone of a house where a worker lived is laid under a sick child's bedding, because the stone kept some of what was done beside it.
  - *Notes, not published:* A stone near much working can carry leavings, faint and real, which a tracker could smell. It heals nothing. The proverb is about heat.

**Literacy:** Almost none among the clans. A man signs with his mark before witnesses, the woodpile is counted and never written, and the Waiting keeps names in mouths rather than on paper. The readers are the Moto house, which writes its own hand and reads the Concord hand as a foreign script, and the Hallenfeld quartermasters and courier captains, who read Concord because the roads require it. (d)

*Source notes.* Grass: the Inventory's opening line ("steppe-riders who stopped riding because the land became permafrost") and "when the grass comes"; the moss is the step. Fusi Vā: *The Kingdom of Kharven*, Culture and The Fall; *Sōhai*, the Queen's Saishiki. Banner oaths: *Kharven*, How the houses came in, The Banner Oaths. Old Seat: *Kharven*, Geography ("rebuilt by Sodoku using the surviving foundation stones"); the darker courses are the step. Moto eye: Moto Inventory, Body. Waiting and nine days: *The Rites*, *The Crossing*. Nameless day: *The Sky, the Hour and the Year*. Hallenfeld readers: *Kharven*, the Six Banner Houses (quartermasters, road engineers, courier captains); their reading Concord is the step.

## Moto

**Gone** (the house says *behind*, because a thing read correctly is not gone; the list is kept anyway):
- **The Okuchi.** The deep interior, the land behind: the body of the world before Malakar broke it. Every other people named it for the disaster that ended it. The Moto name is the only one that does not describe an ending.
- **The white shelf.** The seat of the Long Reading for four thousand years, where a contract sealed held anywhere because the ground itself witnessed it. It is Alabaster now, and the Holy Sea stands on it.
- **The Queen's Saishiki.** The binding liturgy that made an oath structural instead of verbal, attached to the Bow and the Reading both. It went with her. What reads the names at Ice-out now is a man with a list doing his best.
- **The Kōkan who stayed.** The line that held the ground and bought the window across which six lines crossed water. The house remembers them as the line that failed it, and "the Refusal" means two opposite things depending on who is holding the word.
- **The rice on the high table.** Twice a week under Muken, hauled up the corridor at a cost the Thornwalls hated, every grain of it saying the house came from somewhere and could still reach it.

**Folk beliefs** (held in the house's rooms, by retainers and by the Moto themselves):
- **The beads hold the eye.** A lord's beads are what keep the Shingan shut, and a Moto without them will read everyone in the room. A retainer brings his lord the beads before he brings him water.
  - *Notes, not published:* The beads are a father's jade and iron worn smooth, reached for under stress. The eye is held by the man. A Moto reaches for the beads because he is already holding the eye shut.
- **The doorpost keeps a lie out.** A post cut with the fourteen scratches cannot be passed by a man carrying a lie, so a guest is walked through the scratched door before business, and a household that moves cuts the new post before it unpacks.
  - *Notes, not published:* The scratches are the fourteen inclinations, cut by people who believe they are counting something. They stop nothing. A liar walks through as easily as anyone.
- **White hair means the eye.** A white-haired child will open the eye, and the household watches that child first and feeds it best.
  - *Notes, not published:* White hair turns up in all seven lines and belongs to none of them. It predicts nothing about the eye.
- **The revoked die within the year.** A man stripped of the name, held by nobody, does not outlast the next Ice-out.
  - *Notes, not published:* Revocation kills nobody. He dies of what men die of, in his own time. What is true is that nobody reads him at Ice-out when he does, and the house hears that silence as the death.
- **A flat Moto is a spent one.** After a verdict the reader goes flat and quiet, and that is the moment to speak to him, because the eye is closed and the man is tired.
  - *Notes, not published:* The flattening lasts twenty to thirty seconds and closes nothing. The verdict already given stands, and the eye opens again as soon as he wants it.

**Literacy:** Universal inside the house, in the family's own hand and in the fourteen hands, where a document in the wrong hand is void rather than incorrect. The Concord hand is read as a foreign script, accurately and without love. Retainers read little, and read the house's hand not at all. (d)

*Source notes.* Okuchi: *Sōhai*, The Name and the Ground. White shelf: *The Long Reading*; *Sōhai* ("The white shelf is Alabaster"). Saishiki: *Sōhai*; Moto Inventory, The dead. Kōkan: *The Long Reading*, The Refusal. Rice: Moto Inventory, Food. Beads, scratches, white hair, revocation, verdict cost: Moto Inventory. Retainer literacy is the step, from the register "unreadable to any Concord clerk."

## The Accord and the Concord

**Gone:**
- **The small coin.** The copper penny, melted wherever array-building ran hot, because drawn conductor is worth more by weight than struck coin and a child can do the sum. Nine laws have been passed against it and none enforced, since a melted coin does not testify. In the Ring smelter towns the company token stands where the penny was.
- **The seeps the old women knew.** Every district had two or three women who knew where the current ran close, and were asked, and answered. When one died the knowledge sometimes went with her, and whole seeps have been forgotten that way.
- **The main in a cut district.** The hum, the lamp, the wire and the bill, all gone in one morning when the Board cuts the plate. A cut district is deaf; the signal net stops at its edge, and the silence is the loudest thing in it.
- **The notch as the law's hour.** Seven stations of daylight and seven of dark, a summer notch nearly twice a winter one, and nobody ever felt it as a defect. The law now runs on fourteen equal hours sent down the wire from Stannvaard, and a man is late for one clock or the other.

**Folk beliefs:**
- **The almanac.** The printed sheet from any market, keyed to the sixty-count, says which days to sign, travel, marry and dig. A lease is not signed on a bad day, and a funeral is moved if it can be.
  - *Notes, not published:* Nothing in the count favours a signing. The one day that truly binds nothing is the Nameless day, and that is on every calendar already. Two members of the Bench consult the almanac anyway.
- **The token knows a liar.** A ranked practitioner's silver token darkens near a lie, so nobody lies across a table with one lying on it.
  - *Notes, not published:* Half-true. Silver holds an inscription naming its bearer and is verifiable on a coil; it rejects a thief who carries it. It reads nothing about anyone else in the room.
- **A cradle by the meter wall.** A child put to sleep against the wall where the main comes in grows up gifted, so the cradle goes by the gauge-housing whatever the draught.
  - *Notes, not published:* The hum makes nobody a worker. What it does do is teach a child to sleep badly off the main for the rest of its life.
- **The kind reader knows first.** A back-room reading tells a person what they are before the Guild examination does, and costs less, and forwards nothing.
  - *Notes, not published:* Half of them are frauds and some are competent and unlicensed. What they actually sell is listening, and the Bench prosecutes them for unregistered practice and never for that.
- **The smell of it.** A room where a working was done keeps its leavings, and leavings bring ill luck on whoever sleeps there next. Such a room is aired three days and a coin is left on the sill.
  - *Notes, not published:* Half-true. Residue is real, lingers, and trackers read it. It brings no luck of any kind, and the coin is taken by the next lodger.

**Literacy:** Near-universal in the chartered cities, where the bill, the roll and the entry in ink assume it. The tally still settles between people who cannot read, in the mining towns and on the scrip. The chancery Latin is read by clerks and spoken by nobody who is not being paid to.

*Source notes.* Small coin and scrip: *Value, Coin and Trade* (the thaumic demand; "a melted coin does not testify"; mine scrip legal in the Ring smelter towns). Seeps: *The Faiths of the Four Quarters*, The Current. Cut district: *Geography & the Four Quarters* ("a cut district is deaf"), Accord Inventory. Notch: *The Sky, the Hour and the Year*. Almanac and kind readers: *Faiths*, Part Three. Token: *Value, Coin and Trade*, silver. Cradle: Accord Inventory, Body ("children raised on a main sleep badly off it"); the cradle custom is the step. Leavings: *Lexicon of Magic*, the common tongue (superstitious users of "the smell of it"). The aired room and the sill coin are the step. (d) for the cradle and the aired room.

## The Dawi

**Gone:**
- **The joined halls.** The tunnel-network that ran hold to hold before the Sundering of the Deep Halls severed five populations in five directions. Each had to rebuild Varūn's teaching from nothing in ground that shared nothing with a mountain.
- **The lit halls.** What the Deepkin lost in the Depths Collapse, stranded in Void-scarred dark with no way back up, and what they held alone after the Temple Rift while the High Dawi withdrew. The Deepkin record calls it being left. The High Dawi record calls it a withdrawal.
- **The Reach's mountain.** The range the Kuntur Reach came out of before the collapse put them on the surface among islands. They stopped grieving it and started reading the islands the same way, and the stopping is itself remembered.

**Folk beliefs:**
- **Touch a true rune for luck.** A doorway rune cut at the right angle works for whoever touches it, so every Dawi touches the lintel rune on the way in, and old lintels are worn smooth at hand height.
  - *Notes, not published:* A rune cut true does its one job for anyone who passes, touch or no touch. Touching adds nothing and, over a century, wears the angle out of true.
- **Pitch that will not cure is a broken oath.** When a batch of Cask-Oath Pitch fails, somebody in the hold has broken his word, and the clerks audit the name-roll before they check the barrels.
  - *Notes, not published:* Unknown, and the source leaves it unexplained. Pitch will not cure outside the hold it was sworn in; nothing links a failed batch to a broken oath.
- **Keep both feet on worked stone.** A Dawi standing on worked stone cannot be hurt by a working, so an elder under threat sets his feet and waits.
  - *Notes, not published:* Half-true. Petralon makes an anchored Dawi the hardest thing in the Inner World to move, and a working that tries to shift him fails. A working that burns, cuts or sickens does not care where his feet are.
- **The skin tells what comes next.** The Soul-Runes on an elder's arms can be read forward, and a fortune-teller at the Ring gates reads the next rune before it arrives.
  - *Notes, not published:* The runes record completed acts and nothing else. The language has no future tense and neither does the skin.
- **The cinder bead refills for a worthy heir.** An heir who lives by the Tally will find his bead full again one morning.
  - *Notes, not published:* It is compounded once, at the death, and it does not refill.

**Literacy:** High in the rune. Every Dawi reads a name's grade in stone from across a chamber and an angle at a glance; entering the Tally in metre is clerks' work, and a clerk is a metrician before he is anything else. Concord script is read by those who deal outward, for the wire and the second column. (d)

*Source notes.* Joined and lit halls, Temple Rift: *The Dawi Peoples*, The Fractures. Reach mountain: *The Reach Dwarves* ("They stopped grieving the mountain"). Rune angle, pitch, Petralon, Soul-Runes, cinder bead: Dawi Inventory; pitch mystery: *Agano Sand and the Witnessed Temper*, the comparison table. Metrician clerks: *Faiths*, the Tally. The literacy split between reading grade and writing metre is the step.

## Eresse

**Gone:**
- **The Standing Fire.** Every household of the old Empire kept a fire that was never allowed to go cold, fed a pinch of dried root at dawn and dusk so the household's dead had a warm place to come back to and be asked things. The Grand Principality kept the archive and lost the fire.
- **The Windwake lineages.** Whole lines of the House of the Windwake burned themselves down to embers holding contaminated ground stable while the Sapkeepers worked. What they bought is a Stability the current line carries and no longer remembers paying for.
- **The quick answer.** The land once answered the Asking promptly. For several turnings the careful have been noting which askings it answers more slowly than it used to.
- **The Sunroot elegist.** The author of the Sunroot-era elegy every branch still recites, whose name is lost to all five archives equally.

**Folk beliefs:**
- **A rite out of season turns back.** A working performed outside its window rebounds on the one who performed it, so nothing is attempted in the wrong turning even when the need is urgent.
  - *Notes, not published:* The window is grammar and law, not mechanism. A working done out of season works exactly as well and is simply unlawful, which in Eresse is worse.
- **An unpaid witness forgets.** Pay the witness or he will not remember what he saw, so the fee is counted into his hand in front of the assembly.
  - *Notes, not published:* He remembers perfectly well. He simply does not count, because an uncompensated witness has not witnessed.
- **Melted gold rots the hand.** A smith who puts Sacred Gold in a crucible loses the hand that held the ladle within the year.
  - *Notes, not published:* Half-true. Gold that entered an oath cannot be re-melted without breaking the working bound into it, and breaking it is a capital matter. The law takes the smith. His hand is fine.
- **The land collects.** Take from wood, water, game or stone and leave less than equal weight, and the land takes the difference from the household, most often from a child.
  - *Notes, not published:* Unknown. The askings do seem to be answered, more slowly each turning. Nothing in any archive records the land collecting from a child.
- **A trilled r spoils the rite.** A child who trills is corrected at table, because one trilled r in a sung rite leaves it unperformed.
  - *Notes, not published:* It spoils the rite in the ears of everyone listening, which in Eresse is where a rite is judged. Any working inside it does not notice.

**Literacy:** Near-universal. Everyone reads; performing is another matter, since a text without its Songscript rail can be read and cannot be performed, and a reader who cannot follow the rail is literate and mute at once.

*Source notes.* Standing Fire, Windwake, the slower askings: *The High Empire of Eresse*. Sunroot elegist: *The Elven Peoples* ("author lost to all five archives equally"). Window, witness fee, Sacred Gold, the Asking, the tapped r, the rail: Eresse Inventory; Sacred Gold melt law: *Value, Coin and Trade*.

## The Keth-Gorrum (Undaar-Keth and the Expanse)

**Gone:**
- **The passes.** The western quarter's mountain passes of the Old World, where the rock stood closest to the asteroid and the Stone-Blood ran strongest. The northern passes went in nine days and the eastern approaches in six, and then forty years of grinding expulsion. The Hobgoblins hold the vowel in Grr-Vashuum because the breaking was not quick. (The Ironblood say it short.)
- **The year 411.** Taken out of the archive with an axe. The law has a hole where a century of boards and posts used to stand, and a people whose faith is that the stone remembers has no doctrine for it.
- **Kellrath's Crossing.** The warrens burned and the children with no time to do anything about it. The Ironblood carry it without speaking of it, and some of them count iron rings against it. (O)

**Folk beliefs:**
- **The stone remembers.** Ground a law was cut on keeps the law in the rock itself, so nobody breaks the peace on Parliament Rock, and a decree chiselled off in public is thought to leave a scar the stone still obeys.
  - *Notes, not published:* The stone holds what is cut into it and nothing more. The rest of the law is timber, and timber was taken with an axe in 411.
- **The iron hears the voice.** An Ironblood speaking low can stop a man's heart through the iron in his blood, so a Concord trader stands well back from an orc who has dropped his voice. (O)
  - *Notes, not published:* Half-true. The sub-bass is felt in the ribs and the iron in a listener's blood answers it. It frightens. It has never stopped a heart.
- **Bugbears hear thought.** A Bugbear's scalp reads what a man is thinking, so traders empty their heads before they deal with the clans. (B)
  - *Notes, not published:* The hair reads intent through the body before the eyes do. A man who has decided nothing gives it nothing, which is why emptying the head half works.
- **Not an hour before the ninth day.** Render the dead before the ninth day ends and the one who died goes into the material, so a household waits to the hour, with a watch kept on the body.
  - *Notes, not published:* The nine days are the diffusion window and waiting them out is correct: goblinoid rendering takes only what is left once the Wellspring is paid. The hour does not matter. The day does.
- **An etched man cannot break his word.** Any promise from a Legion-etched hobgoblin is Engraved and will fracture him if broken, so his word is taken over any contract.
  - *Notes, not published:* Only an oath Engraved at a Stage transition is an anatomical fact. An ordinary promise from an etched man breaks like anyone's, and a few of them know it.

**Literacy:** Reading is reading cuts. A decree is a board and a ruling is a post, the shoulder plate is legible at forty paces, and nearly every citizen and soldier reads a cut. Few read Concord script. In the Mandate the daily Reading exists because many civilians hear the decree rather than read it. (d)

*Source notes.* Passes and Grr-Vashuum: *The Hobgoblin Expanse*, The Scattering. 411: *Faiths*, the Tally; *Old Vaross*. Kellrath's Crossing: *The Goblinoid Peoples*, Vreth. Parliament Rock: *The Hobgoblin Expanse* ("the stone remembers"). Sub-bass, Bugbear hair, Engraved oaths, board and post: Keth-Gorrum Inventory. Ninth-day rendering: *The Rites*, Goblinoid. The Mandate literacy line is the step from the Reading.

## Korvaeth, the Sunroot Regency, the Vessel Road

**Gone:**
- **The living groves below the Vessel Coast.** Four thousand hectares of standing timber drowned when Eresse opened the sea gates in the third year of the war. The trees took nine years to die and came out of it dense, black, beautiful, and worth more dead than the grove had ever been worth alive. Every drown-alder panel in a Korvaeth chamber is a piece of them.
- **The eleven-house compact.** The Vessel road ran open under it since before either claimant was born. It broke when Korrindal refused, and the road was reopened in four days at the cost of two hundred and eleven lives, entered as the Vessel Assize.
- **The coat workshop at Vessel.** The workshop that boiled the drown-alder facings into cloth-backing went under two winters ago. Eleven salt-black coats exist and one woman owns all eleven.
- **The Tsohanto Reach.** The barrow-grounds east of the Vessel line went quiet in the nine days after the yard, holding the stillness of a place already grieved for with nobody keeping it. No delegation has come in eleven years.

**Folk beliefs:**
- **Say it three times and it binds.** "Witnessed" is said once, because three times is the Elareth closing. So nobody says anything three times, not a curse, not a child's name called across a yard.
  - *Notes, not published:* The closing is a legal form in a witnessed room. Said thrice in a kitchen it binds nothing, and the kitchen knows it and avoids it anyway.
- **A debt out of term sickens.** A man whose debt is *lo* grows ill until he pays, so debtors pay on the ninth day with a fever or without one.
  - *Notes, not published:* That is Nalūn, where the salt enforces the seal. A Korvaeth ledger has no such teeth; out of term costs standing, and standing is the only thing that hurts.
- **Spent Onceglass under the threshold.** The gravel of a charge that has cast once is laid under a doorstep, because a thing that has already cast keeps the next casting out.
  - *Notes, not published:* Spent Onceglass is gravel.
- **A lie tastes of salt in drown alder.** In a room panelled in the black wood, a liar tastes salt on his own tongue, so hard business is done in drown-alder rooms.
  - *Notes, not published:* Every word in those rooms tastes faintly of salt, because the salt comes out of the walls and has never stopped. The wood cannot tell a lie from a price.
- **A vacant name walks.** A person entered *-ama*, held by nobody, cannot rest, and will come back to the house that would not stand for them.
  - *Notes, not published:* Half-true, and for the wrong reason. The name does nothing. What makes an Echo with nowhere to go is the treatment the unnamed tend to get: buried at the edge, at night, without the nine days.

**Literacy:** High among the houses and the Under-Houses. Every head of house reads a ruled leaf, and a Reckoner reads it faster than he reads a face. The Vessel road's carters and the orchard tenants mostly do not, and for them the thumb in the wax is the signature. (d)

*Source notes.* Groves, compact, Vessel Assize, coat workshop, Tsohanto Reach: `scenes/01_the_vacancy_korvaeth_arc.md` (the Tsohanto quiet is that arc's own flagged hook). Elareth closing, lo, Onceglass, drown alder, -ama: Korvaeth Inventory. Contract sickness: *The Kingdom of Nalūn*, the Oath. Excluded burials: *The Rites*, Excluded Ground. Tenant literacy is the step from "the wax, and the thumb in it."

# Writing sections cut from reader pages (flow V1), for the Field Manual

The Field Manual is outside the set that cut these. Each block is the text as it stood on the reader page.

## From The Gate: Who May Learn Magic

**Every practitioner on the page came through a gate, and it shows.** Which one, who paid, and what was sworn are part of the character, the same way a trade or a birthplace is. So is whether they fought for their Stage or bought it, and who in the room can tell.

**The gate is paperwork and shouting.** Forms, stamps, a roll, a numbered primer, a clerk who can refuse, and outside the window two schools trying to sing each other down. The drama is at the desk more often than in the field.

**Every porter in the room carries a sleeping Crystal.** Nothing in the scene needs to say so, and the reader should never be allowed to forget that the line between the porter and the practitioner was drawn by somebody.

## From Who Pays for the Lamp

**The trades are everywhere, and most of it is furniture.** The great works make a city's skyline and its pride; a parish stone is street furniture. A seal is on the lintel by the door. Nobody explains any of it, and characters interact with it the way people interact with a roof: only when it fails. Let it be bright, though. Every lamp on the page burns somebody's color.

**Money before magic.** In any scene with a city in it the first question about a working is who is paying for it. **A practitioner in someone else's house is running up somebody's bill and both of them know it.**

**The hum is the room tone.** Present in every interior scene on a kept street, mentioned rarely, and mentioned the moment it stops.

**The lamp-warden outranks the archmage in the only way that matters on the day.** She can walk off the round, and everybody in the building knows it, and the archmage is polite to her.

## From Mechanica

**An operator is a tradesman, and no sorcerer.** Aprons, tools, a bill of materials, a customer. The horror is in the donor and the ledger, never in the operator's demeanor. The neighbors call it stolen work when they are angry and call it "the warm" when they are cold.

**Show the other end of the apparatus.** Every engine has one. If a scene features Mechanica and never establishes what is being pulled from, it has written a magic machine and missed this page.

**The scar outlives the scene.** A room that has been worked stays worked. Characters should walk back through it a season later and find it unchanged, which is the only way the irreversibility reads as real rather than asserted.

**Nobody involved thinks they are damned.** The householder is warm. The millwright was paid. The Division man knows what turning it off does. **Write all three positions as reasonable and let the ground carry the argument.**

## From The Four Paths

**On the page, a Path is a habit of body before it is anything else.** (This line stays on the page, reworded for the reader.)

The names may stand anywhere, in a mouth, an instrument, a document or the plain telling. **The effects come first, always, as behavior and physics**, and a telling that can only say which Path somebody is has not shown it.

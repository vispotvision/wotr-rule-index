## 1. The fair-play contract: Knox, Van Dine, the Golden Age

**What it is.** Between the wars the puzzle writers wrote their contract down. Ronald Knox's ten commandments (1929) and S. S. Van Dine's twenty rules (1928) come to this: the culprit appears early; no supernatural agency; no undiscovered poison or device needing a scientific lecture; no accident or intuition solves it; the detective uses no clue the reader was not shown; the Watson (the detective's slower companion, often the narrator) hides none of his thoughts; no twin unless prepared for. (Knox's fifth, a racial joke of its day, is not carried.) Ellery Queen's novels stop before the solution with a Challenge to the Reader. Carr's The Hollow Man (1935) pauses to sort every way a locked room (a killing in a space sealed from inside) can be done.

**Techniques.** The clue in plain sight: Christie lists it among ten unimportant objects, or drops it at a moment of high feeling, so the reader holds it without weighing it. The red herring (a false lead) built only of true facts pointing the wrong way. The closed circle: a fixed cast in a sealed place, so suspects can be counted. The inverted mystery, or howcatchem (R. Austin Freeman, 1912; later Columbo): the reader watches the crime, and the pleasure is the investigator finding the one mistake. Hitchcock's bomb under the table (the Truffaut interviews, 1966): surprise buys a moment of shock; showing the audience the bomb buys long suspense. Mystery withholds; suspense discloses.

**In WOTR.** WOTR breaks Knox's second commandment by existing, and the genre's answer is already law: a working may solve a case only if its rule was planted where a reader could see it (R70-71, MC4, R48-30). Knox's fourth becomes a Codex test: a solution uses only Wellsprings, Categories and Families with a Codex line, the invented working being WOTR's undiscovered poison. The closed circle fits a Kharven winter with the passes shut and the Waiting kept by everyone present, a ship, or the Night Register worked from the eleventh division to the sixth. The inverted mystery fits The Mother: the wiki already tells Greyshaft Nine as a culture laying its work and a walker finding the edge it forgot.

## 2. Holmes, the sensation novel, the Victorian investigators

**Holmes** (Doyle, 1887 to 1927). Deduction on the page is abduction: inference to the best explanation from small traces (a tan line, mud by colour), the chain spoken aloud. The Watson sees everything and weighs nothing; Holmes's line is "You see, but you do not observe" (A Scandal in Bohemia). The negative clue: the dog that did nothing in the night (Silver Blaze). Expertise as catalogue: monographs on tobacco ash.

**The sensation novel** (1860s): Collins's The Woman in White and The Moonstone (1868), Braddon's Lady Audley's Secret (1862). Crime inside the respectable house; swapped identities, asylums, wills, forged registers; the story as a dossier of limited witnesses; serial instalments ending on hooks. **Dickens's Bleak House** (1852 to 1853), a named model (R70-4): Inspector Bucket, among the first police detectives in English fiction; Tulkinghorn, who hoards other people's secrets as capital and is killed for it; Chancery as an institution that eats its suitors.

**Occult detectives.** Le Fanu's Dr Hesselius casebook (1872). Hodgson's Carnacki (1910 to 1913): instruments, method, and a mixed ratio, some hauntings human frauds and some real, the reader not told which in advance. Stoker's Dracula (1897): a dossier of diaries, letters, phonograph cylinders and cuttings; the vampire's limits (invitation, running water, earth-boxes, daylight) planted early and used by the hunters; Van Helsing as the knowledgeable second.

**In WOTR.** The Night Watch is a Carnacki institution with sensation-novel paperwork: walkers' inserts are the dossier, a Register docket the Moonstone's narratives, the paragraph the bulletin office nearly cut the hidden clue. Holmes's trace chain belongs to a trained reader in her own trade: a coil-carrier like Qiu Yinzhi on a maker's mark (AL15), a walker like Nol Tally with his rule. Carnacki's ratio is a proposition to put: some cases are a person wearing a phenomenon, some a phenomenon wearing a culprit (the Ferriby hind is the second). Dracula is "fair abilities with findable counters" written as horror, on the Victorian side of the named models. Tulkinghorn is the Ledger's "who saw what" made a weapon, with its price.

## 3. Honkaku, shin-honkaku, the contradiction trial

**Honkaku** ("orthodox": the fair-play puzzle) was set against **henkaku** ("variant": grotesque, erotic, psychological) in the 1920s; Edogawa Ranpo wrote both. Yokomizo Seishi (The Honjin Murders, 1946) gave old rural families, village legends and the **mitate** killing, staged to imitate a song, verse or legend. **Shin-honkaku** ("new orthodox") revived the puzzle: Shimada Sōji's The Tokyo Zodiac Murders (1981), with printed challenges to the reader; Ayatsuji Yukito's The Decagon House Murders (1987), an island and a house built by a dead architect. Its marks: floor plans printed, one central trick, the reader openly competing.

**Kyōgoku Natsuhiko** (The Summer of the Ubume, 1994): the yōkai (folk-monster) mystery, a bookseller-exorcist dispelling a haunting by explaining it, the possession real as experience though its cause is human. **Umineko When They Cry** (Ryūkishi07, 2007 to 2010): mystery against fantasy as a duel, Knox and Van Dine cited as rules of combat, and **red truth**: anything stated in red is guaranteed true, so the solver reasons from fixed facts while the witch tries to make magic the only answer. **Ace Attorney** (2001 on) and **Danganronpa** (2010): the trial turns when a statement meets the exhibit that contradicts it.

**In WOTR.** Readouts are law (R70-2, R70-78), which makes an instrument's printout WOTR's red truth: what it prints is true, and the case lives in what it was not built to read (the Bureau's floor; a ward, MC18). The Night Watch page says it already: a clean assay means only that nothing signed above the floor. The Ace Attorney turn is AL12's instruments in contradiction. Mitate fits Kharven proverbs with their fixed wording and Moto court verse (R70-64): a killing staged to a saying. Kyōgoku's explanation-as-exorcism is the Register's question, "what is carrying the signature", held to R47-12: the explanation strips the culprit's cover, never the world's mystery.

## 4. Lord of the Mysteries

Cuttlefish That Loves Diving, Qidian, 2018 to 2020; already the Mystic register's model (R12-2) and the visual reference (R53-28).

**Techniques.** *Secrecy as survival:* Beyonders (people with powers) hide, and Klein runs layered identities (the Fool, presiding over the Tarot Club from above the grey fog, its members under card names; later a detective), each with its own contacts and tells. *Knowledge that kills:* a high being's true name is not spoken; prayer recites a three-part honorific, and to name wrongly or know too much draws attention; some texts pollute their readers. *Ritual caution:* divination (pendulum, dream, automatic writing), anti-divination, then the rite in steps. *The system's own horror:* Sequences (rungs nine to zero on twenty-two pathways) are drunk as potions and digested by "acting" the role; act it wrong and you lose control and become a monster. *A detective's physics:* Beyonder characteristics are conserved; when a Beyonder dies the power settles somewhere, so a case can be reasoned from where it went. *Institutions:* the Nighthawks of Tingen, a church's supernatural police, open the book as a case-of-the-week unit; Sealed Artifacts are graded and catalogued in a clerk's voice. *Pacing:* rent, cheap suppers and siblings hard against the cosmic.

**In WOTR.** The Nighthawks are the Night Watch's literary parent, and the Tingen arc (a small unit, a junior member, a clerk's desk, a first case bigger than it looks) is the shape of the Timberline chapter and the Register. WOTR has money before magic, Pressure in the room first, comedy beside dread. It lacks a standing law of what knowing costs. Canon holds one case: the Measurewright-line artificer who took Anamnetic contact and spent his last eleven months correcting people who called him by the wrong name. A cost row could be answered by a Pack Fourteen stat (Resilience for what a working does to the Crystal, Gnosis for the read), no figure invented. Conservation already has a cousin: residue carries its author, on the residue clocks (R61-7).

**Boundary.** LOTM's masquerade (ordinary people do not know Beyonders exist) does not transpose: WOTR's magic is public, licensed, metered and feared (R53-14, R53-17). WOTR's secrets sit elsewhere: who may know (the Gate), who did it (attribution by residue), what lies below an instrument's floor. And Klein lies to everyone; the PC's identities and lies are Isaac's alone.

## 5. Cosmic horror and the weird

**Lovecraft.** "The Call of Cthulhu" (1928) is a collated file: a dead professor's notes, a sculptor's dreams, a police account, a sailor's diary, assembled by an heir; the horror is the assembling, the opening calling mercy "the inability of the human mind to correlate all its contents". Deep time, the scholar narrator, the thing glimpsed late and in part. His faults travel with him: the adjective pile (eldritch, unnameable) and a xenophobia that makes foreign peoples the vector. Neither carries.

**M. R. James** (Ghost Stories of an Antiquary, 1904): an antiquarian, an old object or text, real malevolence, reticence, the reveal in one exact detail. **Chambers**, The King in Yellow (1895): a play whose second act ruins its readers; knowledge as contagion. **VanderMeer**, Annihilation (2014): an agency sending expedition after expedition into ground its instruments cannot classify. **Mark Fisher**, The Weird and the Eerie (2016): the weird is a presence that does not belong; the eerie a failure of absence (something where there should be nothing) or of presence (nothing where there should be something). The **SCP Foundation** wiki (2008 on): containment procedures in a clinical register, horror carried by the calm of the form.

**In WOTR.** Canon already writes Fisher's eerie: "the ravens will not come down", "rooms that should smell and do not", "it reads clean" (The Mother's tells). The collated file is a Register docket and R70-67's record of the strange. The Mystic register is cosmic horror's grammar without the adjectives, and R7-2 gives Epoch-scale things dread and no resolution. VanderMeer's agency and the Bureau-Register rivalry are siblings; SCP's catalogue voice is the Guild reference entry, every figure traced.

## 6. Kaidan, J-horror, Junji Ito

**Kaidan** (Edo-period tales of the strange): Ueda Akinari's Ugetsu Monogatari (1776); the parlour rite hyakumonogatari kaidankai, a hundred tales by a hundred lamps, one wick out after each, the last dark said to call something real; Tsuruya Nanboku IV's Yotsuya Kaidan (1825), whose onryō (vengeful ghost of the wronged), Oiwa, is poisoned, disfigured and limitless in her justice; Hearn's Kwaidan (1904). The manner: the uncanny inside the household, the charged pause, the tale that stops unresolved.

**J-horror.** Suzuki Kōji's Ring (1991; Nakata's film, 1998): the curse is a rule with a deadline (seven days) and a findable condition (copy the tape, pass it on), a horror that is also a puzzle with an exit, at a moral price. Ju-On: a curse born where someone died in rage, stuck to a place.

**Junji Ito** (Uzumaki, 1998 to 1999; The Enigma of Amigara Fault, 2002): one conceit (a spiral; holes in a cliff shaped to particular bodies) escalated to its absolute end; ordinary people reasoning sensibly and failing; the reveal held for a page turn and drawn in calm, total detail; no explanation.

**In WOTR.** The hundred-tale night is a ready numbered rite (R70-75) and a Moto court entertainment. Uncorrected folk belief (R53-15) is kaidan's soil: "never meet a Moto's eye", "the cold keeps the dead quiet". The walking dead are already law as a stalled Crossing, countered by moving the body or Stillgate Ash (R61-77): kaidan with the exit planted. Ring's deadline-plus-condition is the model for a fair curse, and Fronts are already clocks. Ito's page turn becomes the one-line cluster at reveals (R70-20) and the held instant (R70-57); his total detail is the clinical floor; his escalating conceit fits corruption signs, one a scene (AL13).

## 7. Korean horror: webtoons, film, the Joseon court

**Webtoons.** Horang's "Bongcheon-dong Ghost" (Naver, 2011) used the vertical scroll and a moving panel for its scare; the long blank scroll before a reveal is the form's own pause. **Sweet Home** (Kim Carnby and Hwang Young-chan, 2017 to 2020): people become monsters shaped by their own desire, in an apartment block sealed as a closed circle. **Hellbound** (Yeon Sang-ho and Choi Gyu-seok, 2019 to 2020): an apparition announces a person's hour of death and three creatures carry it out; the horror is what a frightened public and a new church make of it.

**Film.** The Wailing (Na Hong-jin, 2016): three candidates for the evil (a shaman, a stranger, a woman in white), the film withholding which to trust until the end. Exhuma (Jang Jae-hyun, 2024): the mudang (shaman) and the pungsu reader (geomancer of graves) as trades with fees, procedure and risk. Kingdom (Kim Eun-hee, 2019): a Joseon clan hides an undead king to keep power, plague and court intrigue in one plot. Under much of it runs han, long-held grief and resentment.

**In WOTR.** The Korean stratum is the Mahuo, a court culture (R70-111); Kingdom's fusion of court annal (R70-67) and plague is the obvious Mahuo set piece. Sweet Home's desire-shaped monster is LOTM's loss of control and The Mother's worship of rot (R60-24) in another key. Exhuma's shaman-as-trade fits money before magic and protocol as action (AL12). The Wailing's three candidates is a fair structure: three suspects, three planted signs, one true. Hellbound's cult is The Mother recruiting those the Gate refuses. Han is licensed as a held image (R70-39). The scroll gap does not cross into prose; its work goes to the one-line cluster.

## 8. The gothic and folk horror

**Radcliffe** (The Mysteries of Udolpho, 1794; her essay on the supernatural, 1826): terror expands the soul, horror freezes it; her ghosts end as human contrivance, the explained supernatural. **Lewis's** The Monk (1796) is the opposite: the devil real and explicit. **Henry James**, The Turn of the Screw (1898): an unreliable witness, the ghosts never settled. **Shirley Jackson**, The Haunting of Hill House (1959): the house an organism, its wrongness fused with a lonely woman's want. The furniture: the house as body, the buried family crime that returns, the forged register, the found manuscript, the double. **Folk horror**: The Wicker Man (1973); Adam Scovell's chain (2017): landscape, isolation, a skewed belief system, and the happening the community has been building toward.

**In WOTR.** The gothic houses exist. The Kharven Seat stands on the burned Seat's darker foundation courses, and its oath ceremony is performed over nothing since the Fusi Vā went: an empty rite is the gothic's best furniture. Radcliffe is Carnacki's fraud case, Lewis his real one. Folk horror is ready in the Kharven Inventory: the nameless day, when a killing carries no blood-debt, is a folk-horror happening and a whodunit premise at once; the death-house at thaw holds "a sweetness nobody names".

## 9. Court and faction intrigue

**Martin** (A Song of Ice and Fire, 1996 on): each POV holds a different slice; honour as a corrupted premise (R5-C1 cites Ned Stark); the Red Wedding paid for in advance (guest right, a song, a marriage slighted); brokers whose coin is what others do not know. Martin is no longer a named model (R70-4): his techniques can be borrowed, his name not carried.

**Dune** (Herbert, 1965): plans within plans; the Mentat, a human trained as a calculating instrument, deduction as an office; the traitor named to the reader early, so the middle runs on Hitchcock's bomb; kanly (feud under formal rules) and the Great Convention (war under treaty), intrigue inside a rulebook; epigraphs from in-world histories.

**Chinese palace drama.** Gongdou ("palace fighting"), as in Empresses in the Palace (2011, from Liu Lianzi's novel): a printed rank ladder of consorts with stipends and privileges; favour as currency; the gift as weapon (incense laced with musk, given by the emperor himself so a consort could never bear his child); maids and eunuchs as the information network; face (mianzi) and its slap. Nirvana in Fire (2015, from Hai Yan's novel): a strategist returns under a false identity to clear a massacred army's name; the reader knows, the court does not.

**Three Kingdoms and the Thirty-Six Stratagems.** Kill with a borrowed knife; hide a knife behind a smile; a noise in the east, a strike in the west; the self-injury ruse (Huang Gai beaten to sell a false defection before Red Cliffs); discord sown by a forged letter left for a spy to steal; the empty fort (Zhuge Liang opens the gates and plays the zither, a bluff that works only because his caution is famous).

**Wolf Hall** (Mantel, 2009 to 2020): intrigue as administration, Cromwell's ledgers and debts; the quiet interview where every courtesy is a threat; a case built for use, not truth; close third with the "he, Cromwell" tag. **Shōgun** (Clavell, 1975; the 2024 series): Toranaga's plan hidden even from his retainers, planted throughout, legible only afterwards. **Chūshingura**: the retainers' leader feigns a year of dissipation to lull his enemy.

**In WOTR.** The Powers page is already written in intrigue's fair-play grammar: wants, holds, fights by, set against, fears, and "the seam", the findable lever. The seam is the intrigue form of a findable counter. The strata map cleanly: gongdou to the lineage halls and Mahuo houses (rank ladders mirroring the stat ladder); Shōgun and Chūshingura to the Moto court, beside the Heike; Wolf Hall and Bleak House to the Accord chancery; the broken guest right to Kharven, where refusing the skin is grave. Eresse's appointed windows and Korvaeth's names-as-property litigation are rule-bound engines, Dune's kanly in WOTR dress. A stratagem bank per culture would serve veteran-clever NPCs (R48-36) and the readable deciding fact (K6).

## 10. Spy fiction

**Le Carré.** The Spy Who Came in from the Cold (1963): the agent learns he was bait in an operation built to protect the man he was sent to destroy. Tinker Tailor Soldier Spy (1974): the mole hunt (for a long-planted traitor inside the service) as archive work, Smiley finding the gap in the files. Tradecraft words coined to sound true (the Circus, lamplighters, pavement artists, the honey trap: seduction for leverage); violence offstage, the debrief on the page; betrayal personal before political. **Mick Herron**, Slow Horses (2010): failures in a despised back office, competent, gallows-funny.

**In WOTR.** The Night Register is a back office with brass where a full desk carries silver. "The Society's warrant, not the Bureau's" is le Carré's war between services. The walkers' names on every insert are a network the other side can read, which The Mother already does. WOTR's office words (walker, coil-carrier, insert, Warden of Duty) are tradecraft vocabulary already. Smiley's gap is "follow the purse": a flat wastage column that suddenly loses a myriad.

## 11. Isaac's own models on the same ground

- **Death Note** (Ohba and Obata, 2003 to 2006): the notebook's rules printed between chapters; L's televised bait narrows Kira to one region. Hard magic as cat-and-mouse.
- **Hunter x Hunter**'s Succession War: fourteen princes on one ship, each with a hidden guardian beast whose conditions must be inferred. Togashi's narrator lectures; WOTR's four voices do not.
- **Jujutsu Kaisen**: curses born of ordinary fear, kaidan as a power system; Higuruma's Domain, a courtroom with judge, evidence and sentence; Kenjaku's forehead stitches, a planted tell for a body-thief.
- **Mother of Learning**: the loop as investigation, each pass a clue run.
- **Naruto**'s Uchiha massacre (a lie standing for years until a second tells it); **Bleach**'s Aizen, whose complete hypnosis falsifies every witness.
- **Solo Leveling**'s Double Dungeon: three commandments in a statue hall, obeying them the solution.

Higuruma's court is a template for a Sancta Lux mirror trial or an Accord hearing run as a working. Aizen is the warning: a perception-falsifying working must be planted (MC4) or it voids every clue.

## 12. The table: the player is the detective

In roleplay Isaac's PC is the detective, and Natalie never deduces, thinks or speaks for him (R49-01). Tabletop investigators met this first. **Justin Alexander's Three Clue Rule** (The Alexandrian, 2008): for every conclusion the players must reach, plant at least three clues, because players miss, misread and forget; build scenarios as nodes reachable by several roads. **Robin D. Laws's GUMSHOE** (The Esoterrorists, 2007; Trail of Cthulhu, 2008): a core clue is always found by an investigator who looks in the right place with the right skill; the drama is interpretation, never finding. From Queen and R57-10: the solution is fixed before the first clue, sealed in the notes, so the culprit never moves to fit a guess. This sits with CW14 (shown twice, then read) and Rule 6. A Gnosis read or an instrument reading can be the core clue; an NPC's confident wrong theory (R12-4) is the in-world hint when he is stuck.

## 13. Collisions with current law

1. **Rule 8 against R49-11.** NATALIE Rule 8 still requires one unexplained notice a turn and one uncorrected wrong NPC statement a session; R49-11 made the prose quotas optional. Ask which binds a roleplay turn, and whether the session lie is a floor.
2. **R47-12 against the solved case.** The register split (R70-9) offers the line: what a person chose (a killing, a forged seal) is Technical and solvable to the hand and the purse; what the world does is Mystic and stays one school's reading. The Ferriby hind straddles it. Ask to make the line law.
3. **Knox's second and fourth.** Answered in part by R70-71, MC4 and R48-30. Open: may a solution turn on a working never seen on the page if its Codex line exists?
4. **The detective POV against close interiority.** Knox's ninth forbids a narrator hiding his thoughts; R70-38 allows "the unsaid". Ask whether the unsaid is fair concealment for a detective POV, or the POV must be a Watson.
5. **Sealed solution and clue count** (§12) are not law. Ask: solution fixed in the notes first; clues per conclusion; the hint channel.
6. **The denouement.** The drawing-room summation is one long voice under R12-4's cap, bounded outside court by R48-43's one crafted speech, and may not tell people what they know (R19-4). Ask which forms a solution scene takes: summation, hearing, AL12 protocol, document.
7. **The reaction shot.** Horror's face-before-the-thing is R5-G's tell, struck only for crowds (R70-72). Ask whether one witness's reaction before a reveal is legal.
8. **Questions in narration.** The investigator's open question trips R49-41's hypophora warning. Ask whether unanswered questions in a reasoning POV's thought pass.
9. **Red truth.** Fair mysteries need instruments true but partial. MC18 and the Night Watch's floor imply it; no row says it. Ask to state that a readout never lies and may be floored, warded or asked the wrong question.
10. **The withheld monster.** People's workings keep full mechanism (R70-76) and every fight has a planted exit (K7). Ask how long horror may withhold a mechanism from the page (never from the notes): a scene, an arc.
11. **Cosmic stock words.** Unnameable, eldritch, indescribable, nameless dread are not on the hard-ban list (R51-10, R70-35). Ask whether to add a fourth stock family.
12. **Forbidden knowledge.** One canon instance (the Anamnetic artificer), no rule. Ask whether knowing carries a priced cost, by which stat.
13. **Misleading truths.** R49-36 governs lies; intrigue's sharpest tool is the true statement built to deceive. Ask whether it owes a tell.
14. **Talk as a weapon outside the fight.** Ask whether CW13's accounting extends to interrogation, negotiation and court argument.
15. **Named models.** Ask which of Mantel, Herbert, le Carré, Collins, Stoker, Yokomizo, Kyōgoku, Ito and Nirvana in Fire to name in law.
16. **The masquerade.** LOTM's hidden-world premise cannot carry into WOTR's public magic. A boundary to state, so the register is borrowed without the premise.

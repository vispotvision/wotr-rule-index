# Chinese and Korean writing traditions as working craft

Research for the WOTR style questionnaire, 2026-10-03. Scope: what a writer does on the page, not literary history. Each entry gives **(1)** what it is, **(2)** the on-the-page techniques, **(3)** one line on how it could look in WOTR, **(4)** what it collides with in Western grimdark taste or current WOTR law. Rule ids are from `out/rules.live.full.md` as of today. Descriptions of published works are my own words; quotations stay under twelve words.

**Findings up front.**
1. The live index disagrees with itself on the LitRPG question. R51-16 (Vocabulary Law, 2026-09-26) says stat and Sub-Stat names "are free in narration"; NATALIE.md's Magic Frame and R14-4 still say names only in a mouth, an instrument, a document or a private count. A status-window question has to settle this first.
2. WOTR already owns Eastern tools under other names: fixed-wording proverbs (a chengyu habit), the threshold document and ritual-as-procedure (Lord of the Mysteries), Gloss rights (the knowing bystander of wuxia).
3. The sharpest collisions are with three standing tells in R5-G: the reaction-shot cutaway, narrator moral adjudication, the comedic beat structure. Bystander choruses, verse "as witness", aphoristic narrators and face-slapping trip them.
4. Web-fiction paragraphing (one line, then white space, for phone reading) runs into R48-07, "steady paragraphs, white space used sparingly".

## PART ONE: CHINA

### 1. The chapter novel (zhanghui xiaoshuo)

**(1)** *Zhanghui xiaoshuo*, "chapter-and-section fiction": the long vernacular novels of the Ming and Qing dynasties (roughly 1350 to 1800). They grew out of *shuoshu*, market storytelling told in daily instalments, and kept the storyteller's habits on paper. The four classics: Water Margin, Romance of the Three Kingdoms, Journey to the West, Dream of the Red Chamber.

**(2)**
- **The storyteller narrator** addresses "honoured listeners" (*kanguan*), opens with "the story goes" (*huashuo*), announces his own cuts ("let us leave him and speak of the other camp"), knows everyone, summarises and judges.
- **Couplet chapter titles** (*huimu*): two lines of equal length and matched grammar, noun against noun, verb against verb, each naming one event of the chapter. The table of contents reads as a poem and a spoiler.
- **Verse "as witness"** (*you shi wei zheng*, "there is a poem to bear witness"): the action stops for verse that seals, praises or judges what just happened.
- **The set-piece description** in *pianwen* (parallel prose: sentences in matched pairs), introduced by "one saw only:" or the rhetorical "how was he arrayed?", then helmet, robe pattern, belt, boots, horse, weapon in order.
- **Catalogues before battle**: banners by colour and device, generals in sequence with armour and weapon, the formation named.
- **The chapter-end hook**: stop mid-crisis, "to learn what happened, hear the next chapter".
- **Commentary** (*pingdian*, notes printed in margins and between lines). Jin Shengtan's 1641 Water Margin names composition methods worth keeping as planning vocabulary: **grass snake, ash line** (a foreshadowing thread that surfaces and vanishes like a snake through grass or a dribbled line of ash); **advance insertion** (plant a person or object chapters before it matters); **a horizontal cloud cuts the mountain** (break a long sequence with an unrelated episode).

**(3) In WOTR:** a chapter headed *Brida counts the third bowl / the Seat's standpipes go quiet*, ending as the gauge needle drops.

**(4) Collides:** the judging omniscient narrator against POV lock (R35-1, R39-7) and narrator moral adjudication (R5-G); "how was he arrayed?" is hypophora, banned in NATALIE.md; verse as witness tells the reader what to feel. Couplet titles, catalogues and the hook (with R49-26, end on an action, image or line) fit as they are.

### 2. The four classics, one tool each

**Water Margin** (14th century, attributed to Shi Nai'an; 108 outlaws gather in a marsh fortress). Every hero arrives with a byname ("Black Whirlwind", "Leopard Head") and a costume set piece; appetite is counted (Wu Song drinks eighteen bowls at an inn that warns three will stop a man crossing the ridge, then kills a tiger barehanded); violence is quick and almost cheerful; the story relays from hero to hero through chance meetings. *WOTR:* each rider joining a Kharven war band gets byname, hide-coat, belt and the number of bowls of the skin he took, in one paragraph. *Collides:* the relay diffuses POV; the cheerful cruelty sits near Abercrombie.

**Romance of the Three Kingdoms** (14th century, attributed to Luo Guanzhong; the wars after the Han collapse). Named stratagems, councils arguing options aloud, letters and counter-letters, army sizes stated. Champion duels are counted in bouts and the stroke is often skipped and proved by an object: Guan Yu rides out, the camp hears drums, he returns with the enemy's head while the wine poured for him is still warm. *WOTR:* Renard rides out; the council hears steel through the gate; when he returns the broth in his bowl still steams. *Collides:* the duel law (every exchange traced, three-layer hit); usable only for a fight the POV cannot see.

**Journey to the West** (16th century, attributed to Wu Cheng'en; a monk's pilgrimage escorted by the Monkey King). Magic as stated fact with numbers: the staff's weight in catties, seventy-two transformations, exactly eighty-one ordeals owed, and when heaven's tally finds one short, the gods arrange one more. Transformation duels as move and counter-move (sparrow, then hawk; fish, then fishing bird). Heaven as a bureaucracy of petty clerks. *WOTR:* a Guild ledger that says a Wellspring is owed so many draws a season, and sends someone to make up a shortfall. *Collides:* the whimsy with grimdark tone; the counter-move duel suits "the read is the game".

**Dream of the Red Chamber** (18th century, Cao Xueqin; a great clan's decline through its household). Fabrics and patterns, recipes (an eggplant dish that takes ten chickens to prepare), prescriptions written out, poetry-club games, servants' wages and grudges, hundreds of distinct speakers. Fate foretold in verse: the hero dreams a register of cryptic poems encoding the women's ends, and the book pays them out. Big turns travel through small social acts. *WOTR:* the seating, the grade of tea and the order of dishes tell a Shirogane envoy at a Moto table that the war is lost before anyone says so. *Collides:* little; the prophetic register satisfies "big surprises only if foreshadowed"; the pace fights the over-delivery complaint if it becomes default.

### 3. Records of the strange (zhiguai) and the historian's coda

**(1)** *Zhiguai*, "recording the strange": short, flat, documentary accounts of ghosts, fox spirits and uncanny events. Pu Songling's Strange Tales from a Chinese Studio (18th century) often closes with "The Historian of the Strange says:", a judging coda modelled on the court historian Sima Qian.

**(2)** The uncanny in the voice used for weather; the source named ("a man from my county told me"); spirits obey unexplained rules (a fox wife cannot cross a peachwood threshold); the story stops when the facts stop.

**(3) In WOTR:** a Hallenfeld quartermaster's entry: *In the Thin Weeks the death-house at the Seat was found warm. The keeper was flogged. The body was not there in the morning.*

**(4) Collides:** almost none; this is the Mystic Register (R12-2) and the document voice (R12-4). The coda is moral adjudication unless an in-world author signs it.

### 4. Shanshui composition (mountain-and-water)

**(1)** *Shanshui* is Chinese landscape painting. Guo Xi (11th century) named three distances: **high** (looking up a mountain from its foot), **deep** (looking from the front into layered valleys behind), **level** (looking across from a near hill to far ones). *Liubai*, "leaving white", is the unpainted mist or water that belongs to the composition. A tiny figure gives scale. No fixed viewpoint: the eye travels the scroll.

**(2)** Description in planes, far, middle, near, one per clause or sentence; the human small; mist or snow named as a deliberate blank; one marker for the season.

**(3) In WOTR:** *Far up, the Seat sat on its grey rock under the aurora; below the walls the outer town's stacks put up their smoke; on the near ridge one rider waited, his breath standing white in front of his face.*

**(4) Collides:** Martin's POV discipline wants a cold body with a reason to look; shanshui offers a placeless eye. Fix: the POV's eye does the travelling. Liubai agrees with the iceberg rule.

### 5. Yijing, bixing and the four-part turn

**(1)** *Yijing*, "the realm of meaning": scene and feeling fused (*qing jing jiao rong*, "feeling and scene melt together") so the landscape carries an emotion nobody names. Wang Guowei (around 1908) split it into a realm "with self" (things coloured by the viewer's mood) and "without self" (the viewer dissolved into things). *Bixing*, from the Book of Songs: *bi* is open comparison, *xing* is evocation, an opening image that rhymes only sideways with what follows. *Qi-cheng-zhuan-he* (begin, carry on, turn, close), the shape of the classical quatrain, is the ancestor of Japanese *kishōtenketsu* (a four-part story shape: set-up, development, twist, reconciliation).

**(2)** Emotion named zero times; one or two images held; the unit ends on an image; a scene may open on an image related only in mood; the third beat turns sideways instead of escalating.

**(3) In WOTR:** Hild Ice in the altar room, no grief named: the blubber lamp has burned to a soot ring, and the night-stone under her palm is still warm.

**(4) Collides:** nothing in WOTR law; it supports R49-26. It collides with LitRPG's stated internal states, and with R48's deep running interiority if used everywhere.

### 6. Baimiao (plain line drawing)

**(1)** *Baimiao*: bare ink outline, no colour or shading. As a literary term, catching a person in one or two exact strokes. Lu Xun's 1933 advice compressed it to real meaning, no face-powder, little affectation, no showing off.

**(2)** Few adjectives; verbs and one physical detail; bare tags; the telling gesture. Lu Xun's scholar Kong Yiji is caught by his long gown in a bar of short jackets and by laying out his coppers one at a time.

**(3) In WOTR:** *Lambert laid the three coins on the counter one at a time, faces up.*

**(4) Collides:** with R48's lush description and the full physical inventory at first sight. Workable as counterweight: inventory at first appearance, baimiao after.

### 7. Chengyu and set phrases

**(1)** *Chengyu*: four-character fixed idioms compressed from a classical story: "draw a snake and add feet" (spoil by overdoing); "guard the stump, wait for hares" (the farmer who saw a hare die against a stump and waited for another). Every class uses them; the educated chain them.

**(2)** Compression; the idiom carries its whole story; the choice marks education and region; a four-beat rhythm runs through formal narration.

**(3) In WOTR:** the method exists. Ketsuen's "third stroke late" (anyone nearly right) is a chengyu, a compressed workshop story in fixed wording; so are Kharven's "empty saddle" and "cracked bowl". The blend adds one move: an elder tells the source story once, in full, and the four beats carry it for the rest of the book.

**(4) Collides:** the zero gloss budget (R51-15) means the story arrives in dialogue or never; four-beat sayings risk a fortune-cookie sound in English unless salty. R52-30 already gives each NPC one fixed saying.

### 8. Wuxia: Jin Yong

**(1)** *Wuxia*, "martial chivalry": fiction of the *jianghu*, "rivers and lakes", the world of travelling fighters, sects, beggars' guilds and escort companies outside the court's law (its fighters together are the *wulin*, "martial forest"). Jin Yong (Louis Cha) wrote fifteen novels from 1955 to 1972, serialised in Hong Kong newspapers.

**(2)** Real history as frame (the Song, Jin and Mongol wars; Genghis Khan on the page) with fiction in its gaps. Sect politics run like a state's: succession, stolen manuals, orthodox against unorthodox, the respected sect head who is the hypocrite. Named forms with poetic names, each traced to lineage, master and manual; a corrupt manual cripples or maddens. Fights as debate: style answers style, an onlooker names the forms, and the reader learns the system through the fight. Food and travel as plot: a girl wins a beggar-master's teaching for her companion by cooking him dishes named after lines of verse. Digressions on medicine, music, Go and poison as combat analogues.

**(3) In WOTR:** a Shirogane duellist enters the bind with his house's third form, and Yoko, who learned its counter from a copied manual, answers it by name before her eyes catch up with her nose.

**(4) Collides:** the knowing onlooker is an explaining voice, capped at two per engagement, and Gloss rights decide who may do it. Jin Yong's heroes are decent, which grimdark distrusts; his sect hypocrisy is fully grim.

### 9. Wuxia: Gu Long

**(1)** Gu Long (1938 to 1985), Taiwanese wuxia writer, openly shaped by Hemingway, Jack London, Steinbeck and Nietzsche.

**(2)** One-sentence paragraphs; dialogue like a play script; duels decided in stillness and talk, the stroke shown as aftermath (the blade already in the throat, and only then the admission it was faster than anyone saw). A hero defined by one prop never shown in flight. Aphoristic narration on loneliness, wine and friendship. Spare settings: a room, snow, a lamp.

**(3) In WOTR:** Renard and the Shirogane man stand in the yard a fire's length; the snow on Renard's shoulders never shifts; then the other man is sitting in the snow, and Renard is wiping the blade.

**(4) Collides:** short paragraphs against R48-07; the skipped stroke against the combat floor; aphorisms against moral adjudication. The stillness fits WOTR's Pressure and the read.

### 10. Cultivation web novels (xianxia, xuanhuan)

**(1)** *Xianxia*, "immortal heroes": Daoist-flavoured fantasy where people *cultivate* (refine *qi*, vital energy, in the *dantian*, an energy centre below the navel) up a ladder of realms toward immortality. *Xuanhuan*, "mysterious fantasy", is the looser cousin with invented systems. Since the early 2000s they run on pay-per-chapter sites such as Qidian in daily chapters of 2,000 to 4,000 Chinese characters; English translation took off around 2014.

**(2)**
- **Realms and breakthroughs.** A named ladder (Qi Condensation, Foundation Establishment, Core Formation, Nascent Soul and on), each with minor levels. A breakthrough is a set piece: bottleneck, *closed-door cultivation* (retreat), a pill, the body changing (impurities sweated out black, senses sharpening), the new realm announced.
- **Tribulations.** At major thresholds heaven attacks, classically lightning in waves, and onlookers gather.
- **Sects and seniority.** Outer and inner disciples, elders; "senior brother" as address; contribution points as currency.
- **Face** (*mianzi*, public standing) as an economy. **Face-slapping** (*dalian*): someone arrogant humiliates the hidden-strong protagonist, who reverses it in public. The **arrogant young master** with an escalating chain of backers.
- **The bystander chorus.** Reactions in waves: the crowd "sucks in a breath of cold air", whispers reassess, an elder names the technique.
- **Appraisal.** Graded pills, herbs, beasts and treasures; auctions as set pieces; appraisers stating origin and use.
- **The golden finger** (*jin shouzhi*): the protagonist's unique cheat, an old master's soul in a ring, or a System.
- Cliffhangers, very short paragraphs, recaps, formula phrases, a calculating interior that counts spirit stones.

**(3) In WOTR:** an auction in a Hallenfeld counting-house where a cracked Soul Crystal is appraised (provenance, Crystal State, the Wellspring it harmonised with), and the bidding dies when the Measurewright says one word.

**(4) Collides:** the chorus is the reaction-shot cutaway (R5-G); face-slapping trends to comic beat structure and wish fulfilment, against "victories cost"; paragraphing against R48-07; the young master is a cartoon without a real want (table rule 3). Appraisal and breakthroughs match "money before magic" and "the spending shows".

### 11. Lord of the Mysteries (already a named WOTR model)

**(1)** Chinese web novel by Cuttlefish That Loves Diving, 2018 to 2020. A modern man wakes as Klein Moretti in a Victorian-style world. *Beyonders* gain powers by drinking potions along twenty-two pathways, each from Sequence 9 (lowest) to Sequence 0 (godhood).

**(2)**
- **Victorian texture** in volume: newspapers, police, churches, rent and the price of bread.
- **Sequences as roles** (Seer, Clown, Magician, Faceless). A potion is *digested* by living the role (the "acting method"); fail, lose control, become a monster. Beyonder characteristics are conserved and passed on, so corpses and items are an economy.
- **The cautious reasoner:** risk lists, rehearsed lines, every power tested before trusted, fear constant, divination by stated method (pendulum, dream, automatic writing).
- **Ritual as procedure:** candles, oils, a spirit wall drawn, a power addressed by a three-line honorific name, written like a recipe.
- **The secret society:** a club meeting above a grey fog under tarot codenames, the convener's identity hidden; bargaining scenes; dramatic irony as engine.
- **Documents**, including a dead emperor's diary in Chinese, readable only by another *transmigrant* (someone moved from our world into this one).
- **Narration:** close third, short paragraphs, ironic modern asides inside the protagonist's head, horror after domestic comedy.

**(3) In WOTR:** a Guild rite in numbered steps: chalk the ring, light three oil lamps at the bearings, speak the Wellspring's folk name, true name and keeper's name, then wait for the gauge to move.

**(4) Collides:** WOTR's Mystic Register (R12-2), document voice (R12-4) and look (R53-28) already come from here. The modern ironic interior collides with timeless narration (R48-13) outside close POV; the paragraphing with R48-07.

### 12. Reverend Insanity

**(1)** Chinese web novel by Gu Zhen Ren, 2012 to 2016, pulled from Chinese platforms in 2019. Fang Yuan, an amoral "demonic" cultivator, is reborn five hundred years in his own past. Magic is *Gu*: living insects refined, fed and spent.

**(2)** A cold protagonist never redeemed; interior as pure calculation; schemes across dozens of chapters; a narrator opening or closing sections with aphorisms on strength and folly (leaning on Laozi's line that heaven and earth are not benevolent); a hard upkeep economy (each Gu eats a particular food; *primeval stones* are money; refinements fail); a world that punishes naivety.

**(3) In WOTR:** every bound working has an upkeep line in the Ledger, and the chapter's cold maxim is spoken by Cozbi Mahuo, not the narrator.

**(4) Collides:** aphoristic narration is moral adjudication (R5-G) except under the distant/formal narration register (R35-1), which licenses "occasional aphoristic italics". Natalie never plays Isaac's PC, so the cold-protagonist model goes to NPCs and rivals. The upkeep economy fits "power is finite".

## PART TWO: KOREA

### 13. Murim and Korean muhyeop

**(1)** *Murim* is the Korean reading of *wulin*, the martial world; *muhyeop* is Korean wuxia, a genre since the 1960s, remade by web-novel platforms (Munpia, KakaoPage, Naver Series) since the 2010s. Return of the Mount Hua Sect (Biga, 2019), where a dead sword master wakes a century later in a boy's body to find his sect bankrupt, is the flagship.

**(2)** A codified political map (the Nine Sects and One Gang, the Five Great Families, orthodox against unorthodox against the Demonic Cult). Internal energy (*naegong*) measured in *gapja*, a sixty-year cycle: "one gapja of naegong" is what sixty years of training gives; elixirs add years. Martial ranks as stated tiers (first-rate, peak, transcendent). Sect restoration as an economic plot. Brutal comic discipline of juniors. Archaic speech levels. Short paragraphs.

**(3) In WOTR:** a Hon-guk master tells Mu-jin the boy's reserve is three winters short of what the form asks, counting power in lived time.

**(4) Collides:** a years-of-power unit is a number and needs a canon mapping to EU first; the neat factional map runs against the iceberg; the slapstick discipline against grimdark, though murim's casual brutality suits it.

### 14. Status windows and system messages (the LitRPG core)

**(1)** In Korean and Chinese "system" novels the protagonist, or everyone, sees game interface text: a **status window** (Korean *sangtaechang*) listing level, stats, skills and titles, and **system messages** announcing quests, rewards, level-ups and warnings, bracketed on their own lines, often after a chime (Korean *tti-ring*, Chinese *ding*).

**(2)** Interface text set apart typographically; report or imperative grammar; before-and-after numbers; the message lands at the exact moment of change and interrupts the action; quests with conditions, rewards and penalties; skills ranked F to S; titles granting bonuses; a system tone from neutral to snide; the protagonist reads, reacts, allocates points. The panel is a fact layer the narration never argues for.

**(3) In WOTR:** the in-world version is an instrument, a Measurewright's slip or gauge-card read in the scene: *[Reading at the Seat standpipe. Harmonics: above the district main. Resilience: holding. Crystal State: clouded.]* Numbers only from the Stat Sheet.

**(4) Collides:** the central collision. NATALIE.md (Fourteen §3): effects as behaviour, names only in a mouth, instrument, document or private count; R14-4: Sub-Stats diagnostic only; R13-6: scaling vocabulary in author notes only. Against them, R51-16 frees stat names in narration. The questionnaire must ask which governs; whether a window is in-world (a device people own and pay for) or a reader-facing overlay; who may see one. "Never invent a number" holds either way. Grimdark readers tolerate panels poorly; LitRPG readers expect them every chapter.

### 15. Regression (hoegwi)

**(1)** *Hoegwi*, "return": the protagonist dies or fails and wakes earlier in his own life with memory of the future. Siblings: *hwansaeng* (reincarnation into a new body), *bingui* (possession of a character inside a story). The dominant Korean web-novel premise since the mid-2010s.

**(2)** Dramatic irony as engine; foreknowledge as a resource that depletes once events change; catastrophe prevented on the page; the **re-meeting scene**, the lost people alive again and unaware, which carries most of the emotion; an old mind in a young body; the first life in flashback fragments; regret under everything.

**(3) In WOTR:** no regression mechanic exists and none should be assumed. The transferable beat is the re-meeting for anyone with foreknowledge (a divination, a Well vision): *he poured the first bowl for a man who, in what he had seen, froze in the Thin Weeks.*

**(4) Collides:** a time loop would be new canon; foreknowledge-as-omniscience collides with Isaac's preference for meta knowledge that never becomes omniscience, and with fair, findable counters.

### 16. Omniscient Reader's Viewpoint (meta-narration)

**(1)** Korean web novel by the two-writer team singNsong, 2018 to 2020. Kim Dokja is the only reader who finished a vast web novel; when its apocalypse becomes real, he survives by knowing the story.

**(2)** The protagonist reads his own life as a novel, quotes the in-world novel as epigraphs, measures events against "the original". *Constellations* (watching gods) comment in bracketed messages and sponsor favourites with coins, an audience chorus inside the world. *Scenarios* are quests issued by goblin broadcasters (*dokkaebi*). A skill called the Fourth Wall seals the protagonist's mind: psychic distance turned into a power. His interior hides things from the reader; the ending re-frames who has been narrating.

**(3) In WOTR:** a threshold document Mu-jin has read, an Archive of Provings entry, sets what should happen, and the scene diverges from it line by line.

**(4) Collides:** meta-narration breaks the immersive Martin model; a comment chorus of gods is a reaction-shot cutaway in brackets, though if the watchers exist in-world it becomes setting. New canon either way.

### 17. Solo Leveling (levelling beats)

**(1)** Korean web novel by Chugong (from 2016), adapted as webtoon (from 2018) and anime. Sung Jinwoo, the weakest Hunter (people who clear monster "gates"), alone gains a System that lets him level.

**(2)** A repeating cycle: humiliation or near-death; quest or penalty; the grind (a daily quest of push-ups, sit-ups, squats and a run, or punishment); the level-up message; stat allocation; the reveal to people who still think him weak; the next, larger gate. Danger as rank against rank (E against S). Action in short punchy lines, onomatopoeia, cinematic framing; a one-word command as signature. Thin interiority.

**(3) In WOTR:** the reverse order is the blend: behaviour first for three chapters (he lasts longer in the cold; the Pressure stops making him sweat), then the Measurewright's re-reading after the siege confirms in a number what the body already showed.

**(4) Collides:** grind and allocation against "victories cost"; power fantasy against grimdark; thin prose against lush description; stated ranks against "never invent a number" unless sourced.

### 18. Honorific speech levels and self-reference

**(1)** Korean marks the relationship in every verb ending. Main levels: *hasipsio-che* (formal deferential), *haeyo-che* (polite), *banmal* ("half-speech", plain and intimate), *haera-che* (plain, to inferiors and in writing), plus archaic *hao-che* and *hage-che* (middle levels heard in murim and period drama). Kinship terms as address (*hyung*, older brother; *sunbae*, senior). Martial fiction in both languages adds self-reference: "this humble one" (*zaixia* / *sosaeng*), "this old man" (*laofu* / *nobu*), "this seat" (*benzuo* / *bonjwa*, a sect master's arrogance).

**(2)** A character fixed by how he names himself; a shift of level as an event (dropping to banmal is intimacy or insult, and the listener says so); a fight that starts with a change of address.

**(3) In WOTR:** a Mahuo elder calls Mu-jin "child of the Kwon" until the bout and "Master Kwon" after it, and the hall hears what changed. Moto honorifics are already legal; Ketsuen court address already varies by relationship.

**(4) Collides:** little. Translated self-reference ("this one") can read cute in English; under the zero gloss budget the level must be legible from reaction.

### 19. Han (long-held grief) as a register

**(1)** *Han*: grief, resentment and longing held a long time without release, personal and collective. It pairs with *jeong* (attachment that outlasts conflict) and *han-puri*, "untying han", through the shaman's *gut* rite, song or justice. Some scholars call han a twentieth-century national stereotype; as a fiction register it stays strong.

**(2)** Grief that does not resolve inside the scene and settles into habit, ritual or song; vengeance without catharsis; the wronged dead who stay until their grievance is untied; long restraint, then breakage at a small trigger; communal remembering.

**(3) In WOTR:** the Kharven inventory already holds it: *The oath ceremony is still performed at the Seat with nothing underneath it.* The register adds the old women keeping the wording exact every spring for a binding that answers nobody.

**(4) Collides:** grimdark tends to turn grief into nihilism or revenge; han refuses both. It fits "grief goes back to the root" in the voice law and the Elegy rule (R49-24).

### 20. Pansori (sung storytelling)

**(1)** *Pansori*: one singer (*sorikkun*) with a fan and one drummer (*gosu*) on a barrel drum (*buk*) perform a single story for hours. Five complete works survive; one (*Jeokbyeokga*) retells the Battle of Red Cliffs from Three Kingdoms.

**(2)** Sung passages (*sori*) alternate with spoken narration (*aniri*) that carries jokes and asides; one performer plays narrator and every character; rhythmic cycles (*jangdan*) chosen by emotion, from the slowest *jinyang* (lament) to the fastest *hwimori* (chases, chaos); classical allusion next to market vulgarity in one breath; long catalogues (treasures pouring from split gourds; in *Jeokbyeokga* the common soldiers take turns lamenting what they left at home); shouted encouragement (*chuimsae*, "eolsigu!") as call and response; comedy inside tragedy.

**(3) In WOTR:** a mass-combat chapter that slows to lament tempo over the dying, speeds to clipped clauses for the rout, then gives the common soldiers a line each on what they left behind.

**(4) Collides:** the singer-narrator is omniscient; register mixing in narration runs against R48-13 unless kept in mouths; tempo matching agrees with R49-13, comedy inside tragedy with R48-11.

### 21. Sijo (three-line verse)

**(1)** *Sijo*: Korean three-line verse of the Goryeo and Joseon eras, each line about fourteen to sixteen syllables in four groups; the third line opens with a three-syllable phrase that turns (exclamation, reversal) before closing. Poets range from statesmen to *gisaeng* (trained female entertainers) such as Hwang Jini, whose best-known poem wants to cut a strip from the long midwinter night and keep it folded for her lover's return.

**(2)** Image, development, turn; compression; a natural image bound to loyalty, love or loss; usually sung.

**(3) In WOTR:** a lament at the Waiting:
*Snow on the woodpile, snow on the saddle, snow on the road he took north.*
*The stack is full this winter; we cut his share as well.*
*Ah, the night-stone: still warm, and nobody beside it.*

**(4) Collides:** inserted verse stops a scene like "a poem as witness", but sung in a mouth it is legal; chant law already follows the register repeal.

## What this tradition describes that the other side tends not to

**Description SUBJECTS**
- Food, drink and medicine in detail: named dishes, recipes, bowls counted, prescriptions written out.
- Costume as full inventory, head to horse, in one set piece; order of battle by banner, colour and device.
- Lineage and transmission: who taught whom, from which manual, in which hall.
- Seniority and address: where everyone stands relative to everyone else, carried in grammar and title.
- Grading and appraisal of objects, with provenance and price; the economy of power (stones, points, upkeep, auctions, conserved characteristics as goods); power as a quantity in lived years.
- The body's inner map during practice: breath, channels, the energy centre, impurities expelled.
- Landscape in planes, with a deliberate blank and one seasonal marker.
- Household life: seating, gifts, servants' grudges, festivals, riddles.
- Public opinion: face and rumour as a currency spent or slapped away.
- Supernatural bureaucracy: heaven as an office with ledgers and quotas; ritual as a sequence of steps.

**Description TECHNIQUES**
- Couplet chapter titles; the chapter-end hook; the planted thread (grass snake, ash line); the unrelated episode that breaks monotony (horizontal cloud).
- Verse as witness, as prophecy paid out later, as song in a mouth; the three-line turn.
- The set-piece description in balanced paired sentences.
- Named-form combat explained by a knowing onlooker; counter-move duels.
- Stillness before the strike; aftermath before the stroke; an object proving what happened off-page.
- Far, middle, near composition; the human small; the blank as part of the picture.
- Emotion carried by a held image and never named; an opening image related only in mood.
- One-stroke characterisation after a full first inventory; four-beat sayings that compress a story told once.
- Bystander chorus and the public reversal of face.
- Interface text as a fact layer: windows, messages, before-and-after numbers.
- The re-meeting scene; foreknowledge as a depleting resource.
- A shift of speech level as a dramatic event.
- Tempo cycles matched to emotion; high allusion and street vulgarity in one breath.
- Aphorism to open or close a unit; ritual as procedure; the document at the threshold.

**The reverse, briefly (what Western grimdark does that these mostly do not):** a POV locked to one body for a whole chapter; wounds with clinical consequence and slow recovery; wins that cost something lasting; a narrator who will not judge; steady paragraphs with white space rationed.

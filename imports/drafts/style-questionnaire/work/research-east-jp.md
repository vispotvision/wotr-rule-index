# Japanese writing traditions for the WOTR blend: craft research

Prepared 2026-10-03 for the style questionnaire (Western literary fantasy + Eastern traditions + LitRPG). Each entry gives **What it is**, **On the page** (the concrete techniques), **In WOTR** (one transposed line) and **Collides with** (Western grimdark taste or a live rule). Published works are described, never quoted. Facts checked by web search: Kingdom's two general types ([Kingdom wiki](https://kingdom.fandom.com/wiki/Instinctual_Type)) and the credit for the silence-sound *shiin* ([Japanese with Anime](https://www.japanesewithanime.com/2018/09/onomatopoeia.html)).

## Two live-law notes first

1. **Stat words are already free in narration.** NATALIE.md's Magic Frame says stat names appear only "in a mouth, an instrument, a document, or a private count", and R14-4-SUBSTAT_DIAGNOSTIC_ONLY keeps Sub-Stat names to a faculty reading. Two newer rules (2026-09-26) override both: R49-47-STAT_WORDS ("narration may state stat names, Grades, Stages, eta, EU figures and Guild words on its own authority") and R51-16-OLD_BANS_FALL. NATALIE.md's wording looks stale. What no rule yet covers is the *status screen*, a formatted block set apart from the prose; that is the real LitRPG question for the questionnaire.
2. **Several manga devices land on struck or capped rules:** R5-B-MANHWA_DIRECTIVE_REPEALED (reaction shots as characterisation are an active tell), R5-D-HUMOUR_BARRED (no setup-deadpan-reaction beat), R12-4-VOICE_MIXING_RULE (at most two explaining voices per engagement), R48-40-NPC_THOUGHTS (POV lock in written scenes).

## 1. Classical literature

### The Tale of Genji (Genji monogatari, c. 1000 to 1010, Murasaki Shikibu)
**What it is.** A long court novel of the Heian period (794 to 1185) about an emperor's son, his loves and his decline, written by a lady-in-waiting. The scholar Motoori Norinaga later named its mood *mono no aware*: the gentle sadness of things passing, a sensitivity to the fact that every beauty ends.
**On the page.** Plot moves by letters and short poems (*waka*, 31 syllables) exchanged between lovers; the poem is the conversation, and how well someone answers, on what paper, in what hand, with what scent and what spray of blossom tied to it, is characterisation. Season and weather carry feeling so the narrator never names it. Rank shows in clothing: layered robes whose sleeve-edges show seasonal colour combinations. People are called by rank or office, rarely by personal name. Deaths happen offstage and are felt for chapters after.
**In WOTR.** A Shirogane envoy's letter comes back from the Kōkan Line with nothing written, only a sprig of frost-killed heather in the fold, and the court reads the refusal in the plant.
**Collides with.** Grimdark pace and bluntness; turn shape, which stops on a decision (Genji drifts). It agrees with R8-26-GLOSS_DEPENDENCE_FAILS and R4-13-WHERE_SIGNIFICANCE_LIVES (significance in a physical tell, never in narration).

### The Pillow Book (Makura no sōshi, c. 1002, Sei Shōnagon) and the zuihitsu
**What it is.** *Zuihitsu*, "following the brush": loose collections of lists, anecdotes and opinions. The Pillow Book's lists (things that quicken the heart, hateful things) are its signature; Kenkō's Essays in Idleness (c. 1330) is the later classic.
**On the page.** Description grouped by feeling, not by place: unrelated objects under one emotional heading, each item one exact sentence. Detail tiny and social (a carriage creaking at the wrong hour, a guest who snores). Opinion open, snobbish, funny.
**In WOTR.** A Measurewright's notebook page headed "Things that tell you a district has been cut off from the main", the last item the cat that no longer sleeps on the standpipe.
**Collides with.** Turn shape (a list does not move the world). Suits In-World Documents and feeds R53-30 (one world-only detail per beat) in bulk.

### The Tale of the Heike (Heike monogatari) and the war tales (gunki monogatari)
**What it is.** The *gunki monogatari* are chronicles of the samurai wars of the twelfth to fourteenth centuries, recited to lute by blind priests (*biwa hōshi*). The Heike tells the destruction of the Taira clan in the Genpei War (1180 to 1185) under a Buddhist frame of impermanence (*mujō*): the proud fall, and the opening says so before the first battle.
**On the page.**
- *Nanori*, the name-declaration: a warrior rides forward and calls his name, his father's and grandfather's, his province and his deeds, and asks for a worthy opponent.
- *Armour catalogues*: a hero enters as a list of gear: the colour of the silk lacing on his armour (*odoshi*), the brocade beneath, the helmet crest, his arrow feathers, his horse's name and coat.
- Formula and fixed epithets made for recitation; troop numbers large and round.
- Brutality turned to pathos. The veteran Kumagai Naozane unhelms a fallen enemy, finds a boy his son's age (Taira no Atsumori), kills him because his own side is closing in, finds a flute at the boy's waist, and later becomes a monk. A killing that turns the killer's life.
- The losing side gets the beauty: a child emperor lost at sea, the women's last poems.
**In WOTR.** A Greymane rider at the ford calls his line back three generations and names the Banner House his grandfather swore to Muken; the Moto answer is one archer who gives no name, and the omission is the insult.
**Collides with.** Western grimdark punctures the speech; R13-4-COMBAT_FLOOR wants mechanics over ceremony. Room exists: R48 play law allows one crafted speech at a big moment and mythic high style in narration. The Heike's open moralising is the barred tell "narrator moral adjudication", so the frame belongs in a reciter's mouth or a document.

### An Account of My Hut (Hōjōki, 1212, Kamo no Chōmei)
**What it is.** A short essay by a court poet turned hermit: a river never the same water, eyewitness reports of Kyoto's disasters (fire, whirlwind, famine, earthquake), then his ten-foot-square hut.
**On the page.** Disaster reportage with street names, counts of the dead and smells, beside meditation. Scale shrinks on purpose, city to hut.
**In WOTR.** A Kharven elder's account of the winter the ground froze through, house by house, ending on the size of the woodpile he keeps now.
**Collides with.** Little; it matches the Corpse Economy realism. An essay voice in narration is narration on its own authority (R12-4), so it lives best as a document at a scene's threshold.

### Bashō: haibun, haiku, kigo, kireji
**What it is.** Matsuo Bashō (1644 to 1694). His journal Narrow Road to the Deep North (journey of 1689) is *haibun*: prose passages each closed by a *haiku* (a 5-7-5-syllable poem). His tools:
- *Kigo*, the season word, which places a poem in the year and carries a bundle of associations (cicadas mean high summer heat). A *saijiki* is the almanac listing them.
- *Kireji*, the cutting word (*ya*, *kana*, *keri*), which splits a poem into two images and makes the reader jump the gap.
- *Utamakura*, "poem-pillows": famous places with fixed poetic associations. Bashō visits them and finds them changed (an old battlefield, now summer grass).
**On the page.** Compressed prose, factual about road, lodging, fleas and rain. The closing poem sets two concrete images side by side and never states their link. The season is always known; every place is felt against what earlier poets said of it.
**In WOTR.** A Kharven saijiki: breath-plume is deep winter, the root cache opened is the Thin Weeks, moss is "when the grass comes"; a scene that names one never names a month. *The death-house unheated. Above it, aurora green.*
**Collides with.** Verse inside narration may read as Gloss or stall a turn; a cut image is a fragment, and "manufactured fragment emphasis" is barred. The ban on Earth day and month names (R50 to R53) agrees with kigo, which replace the calendar.

### Noh, kabuki and the ghost tale (kaidan)
**What it is.** *Noh*: masked dance-drama perfected by Zeami (c. 1363 to 1443). In *mugen nō* ("dream Noh") a traveller meets a local at a famous place who tells its story and is revealed as the ghost of the one who died there. *Kabuki*: popular theatre; the *mie* is a frozen pose held at a peak. *Kaidan*: ghost stories, such as Ueda Akinari's Tales of Moonlight and Rain (1776).
**On the page.** Revelation by place; the dead bound to a site; the climax as a held tableau; the uncanny detail stated flatly and left.
**In WOTR.** The old woman who tells the dead Core's history leaves no prints in the frost, and nobody says so.
**Collides with.** "Rites and sites explained plainly" (R48 play law). R53-06 (Wells generate hostile entities) gives kaidan a home.

## 2. Aesthetic ideas usable as craft rules

### Ma (the meaningful interval)
**What it is.** The gap between things that gives them shape: the rest in music, the empty floor, the pause in talk.
**On the page.** A paragraph where nothing happens before the strike; a line left unanswered; silence measured by one ambient sound.
**In WOTR.** Already half in law: R1-1-VERDICT_GRAMMAR makes the beat before the strike longer than the strike. *Renard does not answer. The blubber lamp spits twice. Then he stands.*
**Collides with.** Length bands (a pause spends words without advancing); R13-4's full disclosure; LitRPG density, which fills every gap with a number.

### Yūgen (deep, half-seen mystery)
**What it is.** Zeami's word for the beauty of the suggested and never fully shown. Kamo no Chōmei likened it to an autumn evening with no colour and no sound that still brings tears.
**On the page.** Partial sight (mist, dusk, a far figure); the important thing kept below the frame.
**In WOTR.** Table rule 8 (one thing per turn the PC notices and nobody explains) is a yūgen slot, and Pressure, a higher Stage felt before seen, is yūgen with a mechanism under it.
**Collides with.** Pack Thirteen's "mechanism is on the page", and LitRPG, which wants everything visible. Workable split: mechanics explicit, metaphysics in the Mystic Register.

### Wabi-sabi and kintsugi
**What it is.** *Wabi-sabi*: the beauty of the worn, imperfect and impermanent. *Kintsugi*: mending broken pottery with gold-dusted lacquer so the break shows.
**On the page.** Description lingers on patina, repair, the hollow a hand wears into a tool.
**In WOTR.** The Kharven Inventory already writes this way (a belt worn soft where the hand goes; old courses darker than the stone above). A Moto house mends a bowl with gold; a Kharven calls the same bowl cracked. Culture shows in what each sees in a repair.
**Collides with.** Little. The risk is making ruin lovely when grimdark wants it to stink.

### Jo-ha-kyū (slow, break, rush)
**What it is.** A tempo shape from court music, adopted by Zeami: *jo*, slow opening; *ha*, breaking and quickening; *kyū*, swift close. It nests at every scale, from a whole play to one gesture.
**On the page.** Scenes open slow and specific, fracture and speed up, end fast and short.
**In WOTR.** A duel: three paragraphs of the yard and the wind, exchanges shortening, one sentence that ends it.
**Collides with.** Nothing structural: R49-26-ENDINGS and the 5,000-word set piece (R48-46) accept it. LitRPG notifications inside the kyū would wreck it.

### Kishōtenketsu (four-part shape without conflict)
**What it is.** A structure from Chinese quatrain poetry, common in Japanese essays and four-panel comics (*yonkoma*): *ki* introduction, *shō* development, *ten* a turn that brings in something apparently unrelated and recasts what came before, *ketsu* reconciliation. No central conflict needed.
**On the page.** Tension from juxtaposition and recognition, not opposition.
**In WOTR.** A Downtime turn: a widow at the woodpile; she counts it with a Hallenfeld quartermaster; a child arrives with a chip of night-stone for a sick brother; she adds a round to the boy's family stack without a word.
**Collides with.** Conflict-driven Western scene structure and grimdark's "every victory costs". Turn shape stops on the PC's decision, so in play the *ketsu* belongs to Isaac. Fits quiet, interior and travel scenes.

## 3. Modern literary fiction

### Kawabata Yasunari (1899 to 1972; Snow Country, Thousand Cranes; Nobel Prize 1968)
**What it is.** The great modern novelist of ellipsis.
**On the page.** Scenes break off before the emotional point; the important line goes unspoken. Images carry feeling: a woman's eye reflected in a train window at dusk with the landscape's lights passing through it. Exact seasonal detail (a moth on a screen, insects dying as autumn comes). Dialogue clipped and half-meant. Critics compare his scene-to-scene leaps to linked verse (*renga*).
**In WOTR.** Yoko's ears flatten at a name, and the paragraph ends.
**Collides with.** Western appetite for stated stakes; WOTR's "aftermath never skipped". Agrees with R4-13.

### Tanizaki Jun'ichirō, In Praise of Shadows (1933)
**What it is.** An essay arguing that Japanese beauty lives in dimness and electric light flattens it. Tanizaki's fiction is openly erotic and obsessive (The Key).
**On the page.** Objects described by how they hold light: lacquer made for candlelight, gold glinting deep in a dark room, the colour of a sweet in shadow, paper screens. Darkness given texture and grades.
**In WOTR.** The Kōkan audience hall is kept off the draw; a petitioner finds the lord only by the gleam of the gold crest on a sleeve when the brazier is fed.
**Collides with.** Barely. Against the Draw Age's gaslight look (R53-28) it becomes a cultural argument, Moto shadow against Accord light.

### Mishima Yukio (1925 to 1970; Confessions of a Mask, The Temple of the Golden Pavilion, Sun and Steel)
**What it is.** Ornate, sensual, philosophical prose obsessed with the body, beauty and death.
**On the page.** Description sensual and abstract at once; the body (muscle, sweat, wounds) as aesthetic object; beauty tied to destruction (the acolyte burns the temple because it is beautiful). Interior monologue that argues ideas. Metaphor stacked on metaphor.
**In WOTR.** A Shirogane swordsman after practice studies his forearm, the tendon standing like a drawn cord, and decides the body is the only argument that cannot lie.
**Collides with.** R49-18-SIMILE_COUNT (two similes competing over one beat get flagged); narrator moral adjudication; essayistic interiority read as Gloss. Fits combat and explicit-scene body focus.

### Akutagawa Ryūnosuke (1892 to 1927; "Rashōmon", "In a Grove", "Hell Screen")
**What it is.** Short, cold, ironic stories, often reworking medieval tales.
**On the page.** "In a Grove": one killing told in contradictory testimonies (bandit, wife, the dead husband through a medium), with no verdict. "Rashōmon": a dismissed servant in a ruined gate catches an old woman pulling hair from corpses to sell for wigs, and robs her. "Hell Screen": a painter lets his daughter burn to finish a painting of hell. Clean sentences, no consolation.
**In WOTR.** A Ledger dispute heard at the Seat as three depositions. One is a lie. Nobody says which.
**Collides with.** Almost nothing in grimdark; it matches table rule 8 (lies stay uncorrected). Unresolved testimony can frustrate adjudication without dice.

### Yoshikawa Eiji, Musashi (serial 1935 to 1939); Musashi's Book of Five Rings (1645); Inoue Takehiko's Vagabond
**What it is.** The popular novel of the swordsman Miyamoto Musashi; the real Musashi's treatise on strategy (public domain); Vagabond, the brush-and-ink manga built on the novel (1998 onward, on hiatus).
**On the page.** Swordsmanship as spiritual path: a wild young man broken and remade by the monk Takuan, years on the road, duels as tests of mind, long waits, a love never consummated. Serial habits: chapter-end hooks, a large cast crossing paths. The period texts give two combat rules: Musashi separates *kan* (perceiving intent) from *ken* (seeing surfaces) and weights the first; Takuan warns that a mind which stops on one thing, the blade, is lost, and asks for *mushin*, a mind that rests nowhere. Vagabond adds wordless pages, many-page fights with almost no text, and a wound that never heals right.
**In WOTR.** Yoko's read named as *kan*: she watches the man's breathing, never the point.
**Collides with.** LitRPG: growth here is invisible, there numeric. The blend: the numbers move, but the cause on the page is the road. Silence runs against length bands.

### Endō Shūsaku (1923 to 1996; Silence, 1966)
**What it is.** A Portuguese priest smuggled into seventeenth-century Japan during the persecution of Christians.
**On the page.** The test is the *fumie*, an image of Christ the suspect must step on. God stays silent while peasants are tortured. A betrayer keeps returning to confess and betray again. The form shifts from the priest's letters to third person to official documents as the man disappears from his own story.
**In WOTR.** The oath ceremony at the Seat with the Fusi Vā gone: a man swears, nothing binds, and he knows it as he speaks.
**Collides with.** Theological interiority edges toward narrator adjudication. The document hand-off fits "the document at a scene's threshold".

## 4. Jidaigeki and chanbara: sword-film rhythm

**What it is.** *Jidaigeki*: period drama. *Chanbara*: the sword-fight film, named for the clash of blades (Kurosawa's Yojimbo and Sanjuro, Zatoichi, Lone Wolf and Cub). *Iaijutsu*: drawing and cutting in one motion, in four stages: *nukitsuke* (draw-cut), *kirioroshi* (finishing cut), *chiburi* (the flick that throws blood off the blade), *nōtō* (resheathing).
**On the page (screen).** Long stillness: wind, grass, a held stare. Seconds of exchange. Stillness again, while one man stands and the other's body has not yet understood it is dead. Sanjuro's final duel is the type: a very long standoff, one draw, an arterial spray. Chiburi and nōtō punctuate. Shame, rank and debt drive every choice.
**In WOTR.** Two paragraphs of the yard, one sentence for the draw, then the flick, and blood dotting the packed snow black.
**Collides with.** R13-4-COMBAT_FLOOR wants every meaningful exchange to state measure, tempo (*Vor*: seizing the initiative; *Nach*: answering it; *Indes*: acting in the same instant), read, fault, mechanics, injury with its ATLS class (the trauma scale that grades blood loss in four classes) and the Essence account. One cut is one exchange, so the whole floor packs into the read before and the aftermath. R1-1-VERDICT_GRAMMAR fits it exactly.

## 5. Manga and anime storytelling

### Shōnen battle grammar, through Naruto (Kishimoto Masashi, 1999 to 2014)
**What it is.** *Shōnen*: manga for teenage boys, home of the battle manga. Naruto is named in WOTR law for the tactical read.
**On the page.** Fights won by information (a shadow technique with a time limit; a teacher's live analysis). Techniques named aloud; hand-seals as the visible cost of casting. The technique explained mid-fight by its user, a teammate or a spectator (the commentator role, *kaisetsu*). A flashback inside the fight gives the opponent's past and reframes him. The rival bond as series spine. Training arcs: a target technique learned in stages with visible failures. Fights sometimes ended by persuasion (fans call it "talk-no-jutsu").
**In WOTR.** Yoko names aloud the fault she has read in a Mahuo duellist's footwork; he corrects it; she was lying, and the correction is the opening.
**Collides with.** R12-4 (Naruto often runs three or four explaining voices); R5-B; persuasion mid-fight against grimdark (talk must cost); flashbacks breaking pace and the POV lock.

### Hunter x Hunter (Togashi Yoshihiro, 1998 onward)
**What it is.** The battle manga most admired for rules-based power.
**On the page.** *Nen*, an energy system with stated rules: six categories on a hexagon, aptitude falling off with distance from one's own; a diagnostic test (a leaf floated on a glass of water, whose reaction shows the category). *Restrictions and Vows*: a self-imposed condition strengthens a technique (one character's chain works only against one gang, on pain of his death). A narrator's voice-over gives exact times, distances and many characters' private thoughts. The Greed Island arc is a game world run on card rules, early GameLit. Fights turn on reading, bluffing and lawyering conditions.
**In WOTR.** A Measurewright's diagnostic bowl of meltwater with a fleck of felt; a Path gate written as a vow with a price.
**Collides with.** The voice-over is narration explaining on its own authority, which R12-4 forbids (though R49-47 now lets narration state stat words). Many-POV omniscience breaks R48-40.

### JoJo's Bizarre Adventure (Araki Hirohiko, 1987 onward)
**What it is.** Battle manga of strange powers and stranger poses.
**On the page.** *Stands*: personal abilities with one odd rule each. Battles are puzzles: each side deduces the other's rule from evidence, says it aloud, sets a trap. Each Stand has a published stat chart (Destructive Power, Speed, Range, Durability, Precision, Development Potential) graded A to E. Menace shown by drawn sound (the rumbling *gogogo*) and villain monologues.
**In WOTR.** Table rule 6 (a self-derived technique gets two or three exchanges of evidence before it kills the PC) is JoJo structure: the second exchange carries the tell, the third the deduction.
**Collides with.** Camp and posing against grimdark and the Lord of the Mysteries look (R53-28). Spoken deduction risks Gloss. Letter Grades are friendly: WOTR already grades.

### Demon Slayer (Kimetsu no Yaiba, Gotouge Koyoharu, 2016 to 2020)
**What it is.** A demon-hunting battle manga set in the Taishō era (1912 to 1926), the nearest Japanese hit to WOTR's period.
**On the page.** Breathing styles (Water, Flame, Thunder), each divided into numbered Forms called aloud. Rank visible on the body: the top demons carry their rank written in their eyes; hunters' ranks surface as a mark on the hand. The hero reads opponents by smell. Heavy inner monologue mid-fight. Dying demons get a flashback to their human lives as they dissolve.
**In WOTR.** Yoko's ratified assignment (Blade, from the bind, nose before eyes) already reads by smell; her school's Forms could be numbered in its own book.
**Collides with.** Numbered Forms suit LitRPG. Inner monologue strains the italic-thought allowance; the dying villain's flashback breaks the POV lock.

### Kingdom (Hara Yasuhisa, 2006 onward)
**What it is.** China's Warring States period seen through Japanese manga.
**On the page.** A war-orphan slave rises through command ranks (a hundred men, three hundred, a thousand, five thousand, general): military rank as progression. Generals are classed Instinctual Type (reads the field by feel) or Strategic Type (plans and calculates). Battles in three layers: the map, the commander's head, the infantry squad. A great general's presence is drawn as a giant figure that breaks enemy morale.
**In WOTR.** A line of Accord infantry sweats and retches as a general a Band above them rides out: Pressure felt by the people it is aimed at.
**Collides with.** Rousing speeches and morale reversals against grimdark cynicism. The rank ladder is ready-made progression fantasy.

### Vinland Saga and Berserk
**What it is.** Vinland Saga (Yukimura Makoto, from 2005): Viking revenge turned pacifist through a farm-slavery arc. Berserk (Miura Kentarō, from 1989): mercenary dark fantasy built on a long flashback arc.
**On the page.** The flashback arc as foundation (the betrayal shown first, then the friendship); brutality with lasting consequence; the killer who has to learn to farm.
**Collides with.** Both are retired as *visual* models (R53-28). Their narrative lessons are not.

### Onomatopoeia: giongo and gitaigo
**What it is.** *Giongo*: sound words. *Gitaigo*: state words for things that make no sound: *kira-kira* (glittering), *nuru-nuru* (slick), *jiro-jiro* (staring), and *shiin*, the sound of silence, commonly credited to Tezuka Osamu.
**On the page.** Prose uses them as compact adverbs; manga draws them into the picture.
**In WOTR.** A Moto word for the silence when the draw-hum cuts out; a Kharven word for snow squeaking in deep cold.
**Collides with.** English reads them as comic-book. Table rule 9 already wants onomatopoeia committed in explicit scenes, and Dawi and Kharven native words are free, which gives mimetics a door.

### Inner monologue in fights, and the reaction shot
**What it is.** Manga fights are narrated from inside (thought balloons, running strategy) and measured from outside by bystanders whose shock shows scale.
**In WOTR.** The reaction is a body, never a remark: the quartermaster's knees go, the horse lines break.
**Collides with.** R5-B, R48-40 and R5-D.

## 6. Light novels: Japan's LitRPG

**What it is.** *Light novels*: short illustrated novels for young adults, many begun as web serials on Shōsetsuka ni Narō ("Let's Become a Novelist"). *Isekai* ("other world"): the hero is transported or reborn into a world that often runs on game logic (Sword Art Online, Log Horizon, Overlord, Konosuba, DanMachi, That Time I Got Reincarnated as a Slime, So I'm a Spider, So What?).
**On the page.**
- Casual first-person narrators; very short paragraphs, often a line each; heavy dialogue.
- Status screens in brackets (name, level, stats, skills) and skill notifications set apart from the prose.
- In-world instruments that show the numbers: the guild card (Konosuba); a Status written on the hero's back and updated by his goddess (DanMachi); an inner advisor voice that reports gains (Slime).
- *Appraisal* (*kantei*): a skill that reads another's stats and itself levels up, revealing more lines as it grows (Spider). Progressive disclosure built into the mechanics.
- Game logic as plot: exploits, builds, grinding, rare drops.
**In WOTR.** A Measurewright's slate in chalk: Stage, Band, a Grade per Primary, blanks where her reading cannot reach; as her Gnosis improves, more lines fill.
**Collides with.** "Never invent a number": every figure must come from fow_line or the Stat Sheet. Modern slang is banned in narration (R48-13-PERIOD_FEEL). One-line paragraphs fight R49-13-VARIANCE. A system voice would be a fifth explaining voice beside R12-4's four. R51-15-FIRST_USE bars appositive glosses of new terms, where status screens gloss everything. Comic beats hit R5-D.

## 7. Japanese speech in English

**What it is.** Japanese marks relationship in grammar.
- *Keigo*, politeness levels: *sonkeigo* raises the listener, *kenjōgo* lowers the speaker, *teineigo* is plain courtesy.
- Pronouns as characterisation: *watashi* (neutral or formal), *boku* (boyish, mild), *ore* (rough, male), *washi* (old man), *sessha* (archaic samurai humility, "this clumsy one"); second person *omae* (familiar or rough), *temee* (hostile), *kisama* (now an insult, once an honorific).
- Sentence-final particles: *ne* (seeking agreement), *yo* (asserting), *zo* and *ze* (rough emphasis), *wa* (soft, feminine).
- *Yakuwarigo*, "role language" (the linguist Kinsui Satoshi's term): stock speech marking a type, such as the old scholar's *ja* or the samurai's *de gozaru*.
**On the page (in translation).** Translators carry these through register: Latinate or plain English, contraction or none, sentence length, light archaism, honorifics (*-sama*, *-dono*, *-san*) kept or swapped for titles, dialect substitution. A pronoun shift becomes an event the prose notices.
**In WOTR.** A Moto retainer who has used Sodoku's title for ten years says his given name once, at the death-house, and the room hears it.
**Collides with.** Honorifics for the Moto are already law (R50 to R53), and R20-5-FORMAL_ADDRESS has address follow the speaker's convention. Pack Fifteen repealed register-by-culture as law, but voice differentiation survives; politeness grammar is a tool for it. The Kharven belief that nobody meets a Moto's eye gives keigo a body.

## What this tradition describes that the other side tends not to

**Description SUBJECTS**
- The season to the week: first frost on one named grass, the insect that has stopped singing.
- Light levels and how each material holds light: lacquer, gold in a dark room, paper screens.
- Colour combinations, layering and lacing as rank, mood and season; the armour catalogue as portrait.
- Scent as identity: a lover's incense, an opponent read by smell.
- Paper, handwriting and what is tied to a letter.
- The etiquette and aftermath of killing: the blade flicked clean, resheathing, head-viewing, courtesies owed a beaten enemy.
- Lineage spoken aloud.
- Impermanence itself: ruined capitals, grass over a battlefield, a famous place changed.
- Silence and absence as events.
- The body in practice: breath, posture, callus; the body as an object of beauty.
- Small exact portions of food and their vessels.
- Wear and repair: patina, the hand-worn hollow, the gold-mended break.
- Hierarchy inside every sentence: who speaks first, how low the bow, which pronoun.
- The dead bound to places; a site that remembers.
- Rank made visible: marks on the body, cards, numbered Forms, graded stat charts.

**Description TECHNIQUES**
- Ellipsis: cut before the emotional payoff.
- Two images side by side with a cut and no connective (the kireji).
- A season word in place of a date or a mood statement; a saijiki per culture.
- Catalogue by feeling rather than by place.
- Nanori: self-introduction with lineage before violence; the gear catalogue at first sight.
- Prose mixed with short verse (haibun).
- Ma and jo-ha-kyū: stillness, quickening, a swift end, at every scale.

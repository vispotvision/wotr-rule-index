# LitRPG and its cousins: working craft for a WOTR style questionnaire

Research note, 2026-10-03, for the Western, Eastern and LitRPG blend questionnaire. Each work: what it is, what it does on the page, one line of WOTR, and what it collides with. Rule ids are from `out/rules.live.full.md` and `out/rules.resolved.json` today. Techniques are described in my own words; no published text is reproduced.

**The headline first.** The brief assumes current law keeps numbers off the page. The live index has already gone further. R49-47-STAT_WORDS (live) says narration "may state stat names, Grades, Stages, eta, EU figures and Guild words on its own authority", and R51-16-OLD_BANS_FALL frees "stat names, Sub-Stat names, Guild words and sheet vocabulary in narration". The Pack Five status-screen ban (R5-F-LIGHT_NOVEL_NOT_KEPT) is marked superseded. `desktop/NATALIE.md` still carries the older wording ("names only in a mouth, an instrument, a document, or a private count"; Sub-Stat names "in a mouth, never in narration"). So the questionnaire decides not *whether* numbers appear but their shape, in-world source, frequency and voice (Section 8).

**Terms.** **LitRPG** (literary role-playing game): fiction where a game-like system of levels, stats, skills and messages is visible on the page and the plot turns on it. **GameLit**: the wider shelf of game-flavoured fiction, where the numbers are optional. **Progression fantasy**: fiction driven by a character getting measurably stronger, with or without numbers. **Cultivation**: Chinese web fiction, mainly *xianxia* ("immortal hero" fantasy), in which a practitioner refines inner energy (*qi*) through named realms toward immortality. **Status screen**: a block set off from the prose that lists level, stats and skills the way a game menu does. **Build**: the combination of stats and skills a character deliberately chooses. **Isekai** ("other world"): the Japanese genre where someone from our world is transported or reborn into another. **Royal Road**: the free web-serial site where most Western LitRPG first appears.

## 1. The Russian origin

### 1.1 Vasily Mahanenko, *Way of the Shaman* (book one: *Survival Quest*), and the word itself
**What it is.** A convicted hacker serves his sentence inside a full-immersion online game, Barliona. He starts as a prisoner in a game mine and becomes a Shaman. The Russian original came out in 2012, and Magic Dome Books published it in English in 2015. Dem Mikhailov's *Clan Dominance* (2012) is usually credited as the first Russian novel in the mode. Per Magic Dome, the word ЛитРПГ came out of a late-2013 brainstorm between Mahanenko, the EKSMO editor Dmitry Malkin and the author Alex Bobl; EKSMO then ran a LitRPG imprint and contests.
**On the page.** Game messages get their own lines: skill increments, faction reputation changes, quest offers with their terms. Grinding (repeating a low-value task for small gains) is narrated as labour, so the reader feels the hours. The hero wins by being analytic: he reads the rules better than other players and exploits quest logic. Crafting drives the plot as much as combat does. Prison presses on the game, so the numbers carry real stakes.
**In WOTR.** A bonded labourer on a Kharven woodpile contract whose coil reading, posted weekly by the quartermaster, decides whether his debt shrinks or grows.
**Collides with.** The dry, procedural tone sits near grimdark. The frame is what collides: the character knows the game is a game. WOTR has no outer world it simulates, so every number has to be a fact of the world itself, sourced under R12-5-NEVER_INVENT_NUMBER.

## 2. Western LitRPG and GameLit

### 2.1 Matt Dinniman, *Dungeon Crawler Carl* (2020 on)
**What it is.** Aliens demolish the surface of the Earth, and the survivors go down an eighteen-floor dungeon broadcast across the galaxy as a reality show. The crawlers are Carl and his ex-girlfriend's show cat, Princess Donut.
**On the page.** The System is an AI with opinions, and every achievement notice comes with a mocking write-up of what the crawler just did. Loot boxes (sealed reward crates graded bronze, silver, gold and up) are opened later in safe rooms, so the reward is its own ritual, apart from the fight. Floor timers count down, and the dread is that the floor collapses whether you are ready or not. The audience is a stat too: viewer counts and sponsors pay for cruelty and showmanship. Real grief arrives without warning.
**In WOTR.** The sardonic register survives only in a human mouth: a Measurewright's note in the margin of a certification, or a clerk reading a coil aloud to a queue.
**Collides with.** R5-D-HUMOUR_BARRED (no setup-deadpan-reaction beat, and DCC lives on that beat) and R5-C1-NARRATION_NEVER_WINKS. Both its comedy and its dread need the System to be a character. WOTR's canon says only that the system "does not lie and it does not forgive" (*The Apprentice's Primer*); a mocking System is new cosmology.

### 2.2 pirateaba, *The Wandering Inn* (2016 on)
**What it is.** Erin Solstice walks into a world of Classes and Levels and becomes an [Innkeeper].
**On the page.** Class and Skill names sit in square brackets inside ordinary sentences: someone is a [Guardsman] the way someone else is a carpenter. Levels come while the character sleeps, as a voice in the dark naming the new level and Skill, and chapters often close on that moment. A level-up is a quiet emotional verdict on who the person became that day. A class can change, merge or be lost after a life event. There are almost no stat blocks. A stranger's class tells you their life at a glance.
**In WOTR.** A Kharven widow wakes the morning after the Waiting, and the first thing her Crystal registers is what she survived, not what she can now do.
**Collides with.** WOTR has Traits and Tiers of Standing but no classes, so bracketed class names would be invented. Levels here are numbers; R47-7-NAMES_NOT_NUMBERS prefers names.

### 2.3 Shirtaloon, *He Who Fights With Monsters* (2019 on)
**What it is.** Jason Asano, an Australian, is pulled into a world where powers come from absorbing magical essences. The ranks run iron, bronze, silver, gold, diamond.
**On the page.** The screens are heavy. Each ability is printed in full (type, cost, cooldown, meaning the wait before reuse, effect) and again when it upgrades. Banter runs high, in fights too. Parties work out ability combinations in dialogue.
**In WOTR.** A technique's five-line summary card printed whole at its first display, the way the Primer prints its Stage table.
**Collides with.** Reprinted screens are the classic bloat complaint. The banter rate is the "personality vomiting" that R5-B-MANHWA_DIRECTIVE_REPEALED struck. Sodoku jokes only by accident.

### 2.4 TheFirstDefier, *Defiance of the Fall*, and Zogarth, *The Primal Hunter* (both from about 2020)
**What it is.** Both cross "System apocalypse" (a System arrives on modern Earth and rewrites it) with cultivation. Zac comes to grasp Daos, cosmic principles a cultivator internalises (the word comes from Daoism). Jake, who has a rare bloodline, keeps separate class, race and profession levels and learns alchemy as a god's protégé.
**On the page.** Two progression tracks run side by side: numbered levels and stats from the System, and unnumbered insight (Dao fragments, bloodline awakenings, a god's lessons) that the System only names. Titles (honours for feats) add percentage bonuses. A breakthrough is a set piece: a vision, the laws of the world felt directly.
**In WOTR.** This is WOTR's own shape: Level and Sub-Stats are counted, and Stage is not. In the Primer's words, "Training raises numbers. Only a Catalyst raises Stage."
**Collides with.** Little in mechanism. In taste: titles and multipliers bloat, and an apotheosis power curve is exactly what grimdark undercuts.

### 2.5 Rhaegar, *Azarinth Healer*
**What it is.** Ilea, transported, builds a healer who fights with her fists, because healing herself lets her fight past what her body could otherwise take.
**On the page.** Dense screens and long training montages. Each gain costs the pain of being broken and mended, and the numbers trace a slow curve.
**In WOTR.** Regeneration billed on the Ledger as EU per wound, so the reader watches the reserve drain faster than the flesh closes.
**Collides with.** Grinding as content, and a body that refills without limit, against R12-1-PACK_SEVEN_SURVIVORS (power is "finite, spent, and the spending shows").

### 2.6 SenescentSoul, *Delve*
**What it is.** Rain, transported, refuses to fight like a hero and studies the System like a scientist, building a mage around auras and mana regeneration.
**On the page.** The numbers are a puzzle the reader can work. Rain forms hypotheses, tabulates results, and the story pays off when the arithmetic predicts a fight. Readers split: some come for the maths, some skip it.
**In WOTR.** Kwon Mu-jin draws against a standpipe meter for a week to learn his true output against the district main.
**Collides with.** The sheet taking over the scene. A measured figure that disagrees with the card is a canon conflict to log, never a plot point to settle quietly (R20C-32-PRECEDENCE_FOW_WORKBOOK_CARD).

### 2.7 Sleyca, *Super Supportive*
**What it is.** Aliens arrived in 1963 and gave Earth a System. Alden Thorn, a teenager, trades the power he was assigned for an unfashionable support set.
**On the page.** The System works as civic infrastructure. Classes carry stigma and legal weight. Screens are rare. The warmth is slice-of-life (the texture of daily routine); the darkness comes in sideways.
**In WOTR.** The rank token as the social fact the wiki already makes it: a girl whose coil reading decides her family's marriage prospects.
**Collides with.** Only its gentleness. It suits the money-before-magic world texture (the R53 laws).

### 2.8 Nicoli Gonnella, *Unbound*
**What it is.** A portal fantasy (Felix in "the Continent") with levels and stats. Isaac named it in his 2026-09-12 ruling on R14-B: "They can be named, but do it like how people talk about stats in LitRPGs like Unbound." The technique he meant is stat talk in dialogue that is casual and comparative, the way fighters talk about reach and weight. R51-16 has since gone further.

## 3. Progression without screens

### 3.1 nobody103, *Mother of Learning* (2011–2020)
**What it is.** Zorian, a mage student, is caught in a month-long time loop and uses it to grow strong.
**On the page.** Progress shows in drills that go from impossible to trivial and plans that fail on one loop and work on a later one. No numbers. Competence shows as fewer words spent on a task.
**In WOTR.** Hild repeats one draw in the altar room every night until her breath-plume stops shaking.
**Collides with.** Nothing. This is WOTR's current default.

### 3.2 Will Wight, *Cradle* (2016–2023)
**What it is.** Lindon is born "Unsouled" (with no usable power path) and climbs advancement ranks: Copper, Iron, Jade, Gold, Underlord, Overlord, Archlord, Herald or Sage, Monarch.
**On the page.** The rank is the number. Everyone senses everyone else's rank, so a room reads like a pecking order; insulting a superior is suicide, and the reader knows the size of the gap. Breakthroughs are set pieces. Late books add an attached intelligence answering in bracketed reports.
**In WOTR.** Pressure already works this way: a Stage gap is felt in the body before anything is seen.
**Collides with.** Little. Names over numbers is R47-7's own preference.

### 3.3 Casualfarmer, *Beware of Chicken*
**What it is.** A man transmigrates (is reborn into another body in another world) into a cultivation novel, quits his sect and farms. His rooster becomes a cultivator.
**On the page.** Cultivation parody written with warmth: seasons, food, the farm. Kindness defuses the genre's face-slapping (the stock scene where an underestimated hero humiliates a sneering rival). Some POVs belong to animals.
**In WOTR.** Kharven proverbs as laws the Crystal records: the first bowl goes to the one who cannot fill it.
**Collides with.** Grimdark tone. What it offers is a set of domestic subjects to describe.

## 4. Korean web fiction (serialised daily online; many adapted as manhwa, Korean comics)

### 4.1 Chugong, *Solo Leveling* (webnovel from 2016)
**What it is.** Sung Jinwoo, the weakest E-rank Hunter, survives a dungeon and becomes the only "Player". A System nobody else can see gives him quests and levels.
**On the page.** Bracketed quest windows list objectives, rewards and the penalty for failing. The Daily Quest (push-ups, sit-ups, squats and a run every day, or the penalty zone) turns training into a contract. A level-up restores the body fully, so levelling mid-fight turns the fight. Lines are short, paragraphs are often one sentence, and everything escalates. The Hunter Association ranks the awakened E to S on an instrument, and the re-measurement that contradicts his rank is a key beat.
**In WOTR.** The re-assay: a Holding Coil reads a man everyone knows is weak, and the needle does not stop where the ledger says it should.
**Collides with.** Escalation as prose rhythm, and reaction shots (R5-B-MANHWA_DIRECTIVE_REPEALED, R5-G-TELL_BANK_ADDITIONS). A private System only one man sees is invented cosmology.

### 4.2 singNsong, *Omniscient Reader's Viewpoint* (2018–2020)
**What it is.** Kim Dokja, the only reader of a web novel, watches it come true. Goblin-like Dokkaebi run "scenarios", and Constellations (mythic beings) watch and sponsor.
**On the page.** Bracketed Constellation messages react to the hero live: approval, disgust, coins. Characters have information windows. The hero knows the plot, and the drama is in how events drift from it.
**In WOTR.** An oath at a Witness Stone, where the oath's weight is registered as a verdict the swearer can feel. It is the closest WOTR has to an audience of powers.
**Collides with.** R5-C1-NARRATION_NEVER_WINKS head-on, and the audience voices would be invented metaphysics.

### 4.3 Nam Heesung, *The Legendary Moonlight Sculptor*
**What it is.** Lee Hyun is poor and plays the VR game Royal Road for money, as Weed, a Sculptor.
**On the page.** Money is the stat that matters; every reward is priced; stinginess is the comedy.
**In WOTR.** Every coil reading is also a price: the Holding Coil is "simultaneously a quality assessment, a provenance check, and a price."
**Collides with.** Comic exaggeration. The money-first instinct agrees with WOTR.

### 4.4 Yu Ryeo-han, *Trash of the Count's Family*
**What it is.** A man wakes as Cale Henituse, a minor villain in a novel, and tries to dodge the plot while collecting Ancient Powers.
**On the page.** Comedy of misreading (his laziness taken for nobility); he coughs blood as the price of power.
**In WOTR.** The cost paid in the body transfers; the rest is tone.
**Collides with.** Reaction shots and deadpan (R5-D).

## 5. Chinese web fiction

### 5.1 Butterfly Blue, *The King's Avatar* (2011–2014)
**What it is.** Ye Xiu, a pro gamer pushed out of his team, rebuilds one in the online game Glory. It is an e-sports novel.
**On the page.** Fights read as sports commentary: APM (actions per minute), skill cancels, cooldowns and positioning, called live by commentators and rival pros. The numbers measure tempo and precision, not power.
**In WOTR.** A Measurewright in the gallery at a sanctioned duel, calling tempo and Grade as the knowledgeable second of R12-4-VOICE_TWO_SECOND.
**Collides with.** Very little. That voice is already legal (R12-4, R48-41-MAGIC_TALK).

### 5.2 System novels (*xitong*, 系统)
**What it is.** A subgenre in which the hero has a System: a voice in the head that calls him "Host" (*sùzhǔ*), sets tasks, pays rewards and runs a shop, often opening with a chime ("Ding!"). In *Library of Heaven's Path* (Heng Sao Tian Ya), an appraisal system writes a book on the flaws of anyone the hero looks at.
**On the page.** A transactional rhythm: task, completion, reward, upgrade. Lots of face-slapping. System lines come very often, and the comedy is the System's bureaucratic tone.
**In WOTR.** A Crystal that keeps accounts without speaking. The Living System already "reads intent, not form" and books XP by event.
**Collides with.** Taste (repetition, power fantasy). A talking System is not canon.

## 6. Japanese light novels (illustrated prose novels, mostly for young adults)

### 6.1 Reki Kawahara, *Sword Art Online*
**What it is.** Players trapped in a VRMMO (a virtual-reality massively multiplayer online game) die for real when they die in the game.
**On the page.** HP bars over heads, swipe menus, item tooltips. One famous scene runs on a threshold the reader can compute: a high-level player stands still while a gang hits him, because his regeneration outpaces their combined damage, and he tells them so.
**In WOTR.** A cuirass proofed at a Tier the shot cannot reach. R46-3-ARMOUR_TIER_IS_ITS_PROOF already makes that outcome computable.
**Collides with.** HP as a visible pool. WOTR tracks wounds by ATLS class (the trauma-medicine scale of blood loss, Class I to IV), not by a bar.

### 6.2 Mamare Touno, *Log Horizon*
**What it is.** Players trapped in the game Elder Tale build a society. Shiroe, a support Enchanter, is the strategist.
**On the page.** The famous discovery: food made through the menu tastes of nothing, food cooked by hand has flavour, and the discovery shifts the economy.
**In WOTR.** A Guild finding like "worked sites recharge" played out as an economic shock.
**Collides with.** Almost nothing. Its curiosity about institutions is close to Lord of the Mysteries.

### 6.3 Kugane Maruyama, *Overlord*
**What it is.** A player stuck in his max-level undead avatar, Ainz, rules a new world as its villain.
**On the page.** Magic comes in numbered tiers (first to tenth, plus super-tier). The gap shows when locals in their level-30s meet a level-100. Adventurers wear rank plates, copper up to adamantite. Comedy: inner panic, outer calm, bluffs taken for genius.
**In WOTR.** Pressure across a Band gap felt by a whole Kharven hall at once.
**Collides with.** Numbered magic tiers (R47-7) and reaction-shot comedy.

### 6.4 Natsume Akatsuki, *Konosuba*
**What it is.** A comic isekai about Kazuma and a party of disasters.
**On the page.** The Adventurers' Guild issues a card that reads your stats when you register, and the joke is the reading itself: freakish Luck, nothing else remarkable.
**In WOTR.** The rank token at registration, which is the moment the Apprentice's Primer speaks to ("the Newly Registered").
**Collides with.** Slapstick and setup-deadpan-reaction (R5-D).

### 6.5 Aneko Yusagi, *The Rising of the Shield Hero*
**What it is.** Naofumi is summoned as the Shield Hero, then framed and betrayed. His shield cannot attack.
**On the page.** A skill tree (a branching chart of unlockable abilities) opens as he feeds the shield materials. A public hourglass counts down to the next Wave of monsters, and class-ups happen at that hourglass. Cursed shields give power at a lasting cost: one curse leaves his stats cut until it is lifted.
**In WOTR.** A district meter as a public countdown, and a curse that works like Overchannel scarring.
**Collides with.** Slavery written as wish-fulfilment. Its cost logic is otherwise very WOTR.

### 6.6 Kogitsune Kanekiru, *Re:Monster*
**What it is.** A man reborn as a goblin gains abilities by eating his kills and evolves up the monster ladder.
**On the page.** A one-line ability notice after each meal; evolution as a rank-up with the new body described.
**In WOTR.** A Well-spawn (R53-06-WELL_SPAWN) that grows by eating, read by a hunter from its kills.
**Collides with.** The monster ladder would have to be invented.

## 7. Synthesis: the nine questions

**(1) Where the numbers come from.** Five sources: (a) a real System that speaks or displays (DCC, Solo Leveling, TWI, ORV, HWFWM, *Defiance*, *Primal Hunter*, *Delve*, *Super Supportive*, xitong); (b) a game interface, because the world is a game (SAO, Log Horizon, Overlord, *Moonlight Sculptor*, *King's Avatar*, Mahanenko); (c) an institution's instrument (Konosuba's card, the Hunter Association's measurement, Overlord's plates, Shield Hero's hourglass); (d) a sense the character has (Cradle, qi sense); (e) none (Mother of Learning). WOTR has (c), (d) and a silent (a) in canon (§8.1).

**(2) How screens sit on the page.** Four typographies: bracketed inline names (TWI); boxed or bold blocks on their own lines (Solo Leveling, DCC); full sheets at milestones (HWFWM, *Primal Hunter*); numbers inline in dialogue (*King's Avatar*, Unbound-style stat talk). Triggers: a level-up, a new skill, a quest offered or completed, damage, appraisal of an item or enemy, an achievement, evolution, a title, an audience reaction.

**(3) The System's voice.** Clinical (Solo Leveling), ceremonial and intimate (TWI's voice in sleep), sardonic (DCC), divine and spectating (ORV), mercantile or bureaucratic (xitong shops, guild cards). The Primer fixes the Continuum as honest and unforgiving: clinical or ceremonial fit, sardonic only in a person's mouth.

**(4) How numbers build tension, and how they fail.** They build it through thresholds the reader can compute (SAO's regeneration against damage; the Primer's "One full Grade above your opponent in the relevant stat wins that exchange"), costs (Shield Hero's curse, *Azarinth*'s pain), puzzles (*Delve*, Mahanenko), a gap revealed (Solo Leveling's re-measure, Overlord's level-100) and countdowns (DCC, Shield Hero). They fail through bloat (stats into the thousands), info-dumps (the same screen reprinted), screens standing in for scenes (a damage figure with no body on the page), and numbers that only rise.

**(5) Progression pacing.** Fast early, slow late, which is how WOTR's XP table already runs. A tutorial or training arc, a plateau or bottleneck, the breakthrough written as a set piece (the signature of cultivation fiction), and the tournament as the stage for display. A small reward every few chapters, a large one at each arc's end.

**(6) Build talk.** Characters theorycraft (plan builds aloud) about synergy (abilities that multiply each other), min-maxing (one stat at the ceiling, another dumped), party roles (tank, healer, damage) and counters. WOTR's slots for them: the knowledgeable second (R12-4-VOICE_TWO_SECOND), magic talk by training (R48-41-MAGIC_TALK), and the Primer's line that specialists "produce peaks by accepting valleys elsewhere, and the valleys are where they die."

**(7) Loot and tiers.** Rarity ladders sorted by colour, tooltips (a small panel: name, rarity, numbers, requirements, a line of flavour text), loot boxes, identification by appraisal, set bonuses. WOTR already has the ladder under names in R46-2-NINE_ITEM_TIERS_NAMED (Plain, Marked, Proofed, Tempered, Instrument, Hallowed, Storied, Found, Numinous, Proscribed, each with a Latin form and a Grade band), plus the threshold rule (R46-3) and the costs (R46-4).

**(8) Humour.** The System as comedian (DCC, xitong), genre deflation (Konosuba, *Beware of Chicken*), misreading (Overlord, *Trash*), stinginess (*Moonlight Sculptor*), combat banter (HWFWM), the bad stat roll (Konosuba). Most of these rest on the setup-deadpan-reaction beat that R5-D-HUMOUR_BARRED bans. What survives WOTR's law is gallows wit about numbers from a mouth that has earned it, and institutional dryness.

**(9) What the genre's readers expect at minimum.** Visible progress at a steady rate. Rules that are legible and fair, so a surprise feels earned. At least one crunchy moment per arc (crunch: heavy numerical detail). Costs that bite. A protagonist who thinks about his build. A payoff when a plan built on the numbers works. They punish power with no rule behind it.

## 8. WOTR check

### 8.1 What WOTR already has that can serve as an in-world readout
- **The Continuum as assessor.** *The Apprentice's Primer* (wiki, In-World Documents) is a LitRPG manual in all but name: "Your numbers are the Continuum's honest assessment of the soul carrying them. They can be improved. They cannot be argued with." And: "Fail a Band gate and the counter simply halts." Canon never says how a person *reads* his own numbers.
- **The Living System's XP law** (FOW Parts One to Three): "Resonant Accumulation", with multipliers for fighting above your Tier (1.8x, 3.2x, 6.0x, at least 12.0x), diminishing returns, one-off Wellspring surges (roughly 500 XP for minor contact at Band I), and Residual Strain at Band gates. These are achievement rules already written, without a display.
- **The Holding Coil** (*The Apparatus of the Age*): "The most important instrument in the modern economy", calibrated yearly by Measurewrights; the Accord rests on "two coils in two kingdoms" giving "the same number for the same object". Field coils are portable.
- **The rank token**: a silver disc "verifiable on a coil" that goes inert in front of witnesses when the holder is stripped of rank. Konosuba's card and Overlord's plate, already canon.
- **Assessors.** FOW Parts One to Three: "The mean is what an assessor reads, what a Measurewright says aloud." Succession crises begin with "a disputed coil reading on an heir" (*Value, Coin and Trade*).
- **Draw Age meters** (district main, standpipe, gauge-housing; R13-2), **faculty readings** (R14-4) and a practitioner's **count of his own reserve**.
- **Documents** print stats, Grades, Bands, Stage, EU, AU/s, eta and Category (R57-07-DOCUMENTS_FULLY_METAPHYSICAL). R12-4-VOICE_FOUR_DOCUMENT puts a document at a scene's threshold that "Sets a rule the scene then breaks", which is exactly the best use of a LitRPG screen.
- **Item Tiers by name** with Grade bands and armour thresholds (R46-2, R46-3), and **Witness Stones** that register oaths.

### 8.2 Rules a LitRPG turn would amend or lean on
- **R5-A-FLESH_CHANGES** (live): prose register, humour, characterisation and narration authority "derive from the Western tradition". A blend amends this directly. R5-A-SKELETON_UNCHANGED (the system is not "diluted, softened, or made impressionistic") supports the turn.
- **R5-F-LIGHT_NOVEL_KEPT** (live): the chapter as a hard unit with a hook, "legibility as reader pleasure", fast entry. It already leans toward the genre.
- **R5-F-LIGHT_NOVEL_NOT_KEPT** (superseded by R51-16) banned "Status screens, panels, HUD-style rendering" and the isekai commentary register. R51-16's text covers vocabulary only, so no rule says in words whether a screen as a typographic form is allowed. The questionnaire needs an explicit item on it.
- **R49-47-STAT_WORDS** and **R51-16-OLD_BANS_FALL** (live): stat words and figures are already free in narration.
- **R14-4-SUBSTAT_DIAGNOSTIC_ONLY** is still live ("Nobody else reaches for them"). It is in tension with R51-16, which retired its sibling R20C-31 but not R14-4. This belongs in CONFLICTS.md.
- **R52-05-COUNTING**: "everyone else rounds, guesses or doesn't count." A LitRPG hero who knows his exact numbers breaks this unless he reads them off an instrument or a document.
- **R47-7-NAMES_NOT_NUMBERS**: "by name only, never as numbers". Level is numbered by nature, so whether R47-7 reaches Level needs a ruling. A screen would print "Refraction", not "Stage VII" (R20C-30-STAGE_NAMES_FROM_FOW). R61-106 already bars a document from gating access "by a numbered Level".
- **R13-6-SCALING_VOCAB_RESTRICTED**: Attack Potency, hax, speed blitz and similar terms stay in author notes, and build talk wants them. R15-4 lets a character say "hax"; the wider restriction stands.
- **R12-4-VOICE_MIXING_RULE**: at most two explaining voices per engagement. A System line is either a fifth voice or counts as the instrument or document voice.
- **R5-C1-NARRATION_NEVER_WINKS, R5-D-HUMOUR_BARRED, R5-E-NARRATION_NEVER_ADJUDICATES, R5-B-MANHWA_DIRECTIVE_REPEALED, R5-G-TELL_BANK_ADDITIONS** govern any System voice that jokes or judges. **R12-5-NEVER_INVENT_NUMBER** ("Unchanged and absolute"), **R14-3-TRACEABILITY** and **R13-8-RECONSTRUCTIBLE_ADJUDICATION** are helped by visible numbers: the reader can rebuild the call.
- **NATALIE.md**: the Magic Frame line ("Effects always on the page as behaviour; names only in a mouth, an instrument, a document, or a private count") and Table Rule 5's 2026-09-12 note already lag the index. They need rewriting whichever way the questionnaire comes out.

### 8.3 Canon risks
1. **Inventing numbers.** Screens want numbers in every scene. WOTR has no HP pool (Vitality is a Grade; wounds go by ATLS class). Damage in joules exists only via the Grade table; most FOW XP events say "Moderate XP" with no figure. EU costs are a share of reserve (R47-4-LEDGER_COSTS). Every figure on a screen must trace to card or workbook; anything else is an estimate under R12-5, and a screen cannot flag an estimate gracefully the way a mouth can ("call it four hundred").
2. **The Stat Sheet as authority.** Precedence runs FOW, then the workbook, then the card (R20C-32), and cards drift: twenty-six AU/s misses, 125 sheets re-costed (R39-1), out-of-band reserves rescaled. A printed block freezes a figure that may later be corrected. The Primer shows the hazard already. Its pool figures ("fifteen thousand" from Bands, about 24,100 across "twenty stats") disagree with R38-2 (9,000 from levelling, about 18,100 across sixty-three Sub-Stats). That disagreement should be logged.
3. **The technology ceiling.** R53-01 runs the Imperial Age "from the 1800s into the middle of the 1900s". That is newer than NATALIE.md's 2026-09-12 "1800–1900 and nothing after", which is another lag to log. Devices in that span that show a number: needle galvanometers and dial gauges, number-wheel counters (gas meters, cash registers), ticker tape, Hollerith punched cards (1890), calculating machines, the Braun cathode-ray tube (1897) as a crude trace. No glowing panel. A floating window could only be a metaphysical perception, and that is new cosmology. Glow-numeral tubes (Nixie, 1955) fall outside even R53-01.
4. **A speaking System.** The Primer's "does not lie" rules out the unreliable Systems of DCC and ORV. Table Rule 8's lies have to come from people and instruments, which canon already supplies ("forged assays, concealed coil readings on an heir"). Any System voice would be Natalie-originated and go through propose_rule as pending.

## 9. What this tradition describes that the other side tends not to

**Description SUBJECTS that LitRPG and its cousins cover and Western grimdark mostly leaves out:**
- The body's resources as accounts: reserve, regeneration rate, cooldowns, fatigue as a meter.
- Thresholds, and the exact moment one is crossed (the level-up, the breakthrough, the evolution).
- The instruments and rituals of measurement: registration, appraisal, re-assay, the reading made in public.
- Items as specifications: tier, maker, requirements, flavour line, price.
- Routine training: the daily quest, the drill, the reps counted.
- Rank as social geography: who may speak to whom, read off a token or a sense.
- Build choices and the regret after them: the path not taken, the dumped stat that kills you.
- Power as bureaucracy: guilds, certification, ranking boards, sponsors, audiences.
- The audience of a fight: commentators, viewers, gods watching.

**Description TECHNIQUES the genre uses and the Western literary side mostly does not:**
- The set-off line: one typographically separate sentence (bracketed, bold or boxed) that breaks into the prose with a verdict.
- The computable threshold: give the reader the numbers early so they can do the sum before the hero does.
- The before-and-after comparison: the same drill or sheet shown twice, so the difference tells the story.
- The delayed reward ritual: loot opened later, apart from the fight, as its own scene.
- Inline taxonomy: a class or rank name that sums up a stranger in two words.
- Commentary as narration: a knowledgeable onlooker calling the play live.
- The cumulative ledger: a running tally the reader keeps alongside the hero, chapter by chapter.

**In the other direction,** grimdark gives LitRPG what it usually lacks: the cost carried in the body rather than on a bar, the POV's ignorance and self-deception, moral weight no number settles, and the death a level cannot buy back.

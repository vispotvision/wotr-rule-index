# Research: WOTR naming law, for the comic-names section

Prepared 2026-10-03 for the style questionnaire, message (3), on funny and punning names "like Borin and Snorin". It maps current law so each question can say what a comic-name ruling would change. Nothing here is ruled.

## The short version

- **Nothing in live law bans a funny name.** The outlier budget is gone (R50-18), comic NPCs are licensed from the start (R52-31), humour is wherever it is earned (R48-11), and homage and myth names are allowed (R50-04, R50-05).
- **What the law does govern is who hears the joke.** The narration never winks (R5-C1). An English pun is audible in-world only in a Common or Northern mouth. A pun hidden in a Dawi, Moto or Mahuo name is heard by the reader alone, unless a character says it.
- **Every people already has a native place for comic names, and it is the byname slot**, not the given name: Northern descriptive bynames, Far-Northern "freely coined" bynames and clerk-made roll-names, goblinoid bynames whose origin is forgotten, Chinese self-awarded studio names and insulting posthumous names.
- **Borin is register-legal.** It parses from the Dawi bank as Bor (bear, carry) plus Rin (cut). "Snorin" cannot be built from the bank, and its joke (snoring) exists only in English. The Dawi themselves say *Porin*.
- **Canon already carries comic and meaning names** (Toad Guy, Old Croak, the Dango Dragon, Nol Tally the deer-counter, Borin's Whetstone), but no deliberate rhyming set.

## Terms, in plain words

**Byname:** a second name that tells people apart by trade, place, father or looks (Aldric Longjaw). **Patronymic:** a name from the father (Borensson). **Dithematic:** built from two meaning-elements (Tor + Grim). **Consonant gradation:** a consonant changing strength by the next syllable, as in Finnish; in Dawi it marks standing. **Vowel harmony:** a word takes its vowels from one class only. **Phonotactics:** which sound sequences a language allows. **Uvular:** a sound from far back in the throat, written q. **Outlier:** a name fitting no stratum. **Roll-name:** a surname an Accord clerk assigned to someone who had none. **Third Name:** the earned title the world gives a practitioner. **Gloss:** a name's plain English meaning.

## 1. The rules, with status

Quotes are exact and short. "Live" means status: live in `rules/`.

### Naming Law 2026-09-26 (R50, all live, Isaac ruled directly)

- **R50-01-REAL_GRAMMAR**: "Borrowed real-language names must be real, correct phrases in that language". A Japanese or Swahili pun must be real words, correctly built, with a true gloss.
- **R50-02-FIX_OLD_ONES**: "corrected where the name keeps its sound; the rest stays". Old names are fixed for grammar, not renamed.
- **R50-04-POP_CULTURE**: "the name means what WOTR makes it mean". Tolkien echoes are allowed.
- **R50-05-MYTH_FIGURES**: "WOTR's being is its own, the name is a nod".
- **R50-11-LONG_NAMES** / **R50-12-COINED_LATIN**: technique names run "two words at most"; "New Latinate names use real, correct Latin." No dog-Latin puns.
- **R50-17-SHARED_NAMES**: "may share a name or sound alike"; when they meet, "it becomes a beat". The nearest rule to a rhyming pair.
- **R50-18-OUTLIERS**: "outlier names are free, no ratio, no reason needed".
- **R50-21-THIRD_NAMES**: an NPC may coin a Third Name for Isaac's character; "it sticks only if Isaac keeps it".
- **R50-25-WELLSPRINGS**: local folk names are recorded and usable anywhere.
- **R50-27-NEW_NPC_NAME**: a new person is "a role (the gate-clerk) until they speak twice or matter".
- **R50-28-NEW_WORDS**: "it becomes canon only if Isaac reuses it".
- **R50-30-BANK_OR_EAR**: "the partner may name by ear to keep the pace"; checked against banks at scene end.
- **R50-31-SCRIPT_SHOWN**: "True names are romanised only". Kanji and hangul puns cannot show.
- **R50-33-MISPRONOUNCE**: a foreigner's version is "spelled as heard in dialogue".
- **R50-34-SAY_GUIDE**: every card "carries a short pronunciation line".
- Also live, less pressing: R50-03, R50-07, R50-10, R50-15, R50-16, R50-19, R50-20, R50-22 to R50-24, R50-29, R50-32.

## 2. The strata and their sound rules

These are five answers to the source's question, "who wrote your name down", not five peoples (Inner World Naming Amendment §III).

**Japonic (Moto, Yukari, Yuno, Ketsuen houses).** R23-2-JAPONIC_STRATUM: "Full life-stage slots, name-taboo on the living", plus topographic surnames and renaming at promotion, vow or allegiance. Moto form: given name, Line, Moto (Souma Byakuya Moto), with no Line for Kōkan. The given name is a meaning-name by law (R40-1). No clusters. *Comedy:* sound puns on real Japanese words, as Daigo/Dango already does; kanji double meanings cannot show (R50-31).

**Korean (Mahuo).** R23-2-KOREAN_STRATUM: "two-syllable given name carrying a generational syllable"; a hollow-seat (clan seat) splits unrelated families; R23-10: "Hyphen means Mahuo." A thirty-element bank (R37-1). *Comedy:* nothing native.

**Chinese (lineage halls: physicians, Measurewrights, alchemists, traders).** R23-2-CHINESE_STRATUM_SUMMARY: "One-syllable surname, a generation character drawn from a poem"; courtesy, studio and posthumous names. R23-10-CHINESE_PHONOTACTICS: open syllables, nasal codas, "no four-syllable given names, no sect-title constructions". *Comedy:* native. The self-awarded studio name is mocked, and the posthumous name can be an insult, as real courts gave damning ones to rulers they despised.

**Far-Northern (tundra peoples, sealers, the Keld, most Kharven commons).** R23-2-FAR_NORTHERN_STRATUM_SUMMARY: "A name is a person, held by the dead"; kin-turns follow the name. R23-11-PHONOTACTICS: vowels a, i, u, and "Uvulars are the signature"; no onset clusters; suffixes that mean something. *Comedy:* native twice over. Bynames are "freely coined, and stick", and the Kharven insults (wet wood, short stack, full mouth, Numra) are ready stock. Clerks made roll-names from "a factor's guess at a sound"; Ice and Foss are two. The source offers a clerk "getting the wrong person entirely" as a free scene. Limit: no joke on the name of the recently dead (R23-11).

**Northern (Banner Houses, chartered families, guilds, the muster, the commons).** R23-2-NORTHERN_STRATUM: "an earned byname available to anyone". R20-1-CONCORD_HUMAN_ANCHOR: "Small given-name pool, byname disambiguation". The pool holds forty names (Aldric, Bram, Dunstan, Lorn, Osric, Soren; Brida, Fern, Hild, Nella, Orin). Surnames are frozen if chartered, patronymics live if not. *Comedy:* the most native home. The guide's examples are descriptive (Aldric Longjaw, Aldric Quiet). The real anchors are comic by nature: Norse bynames such as Hairy-breeches, the Boneless and Skull-splitter, and English phrase-names frozen into surnames (Drinkwater, Golightly, Gotobed). Dickens gives the Victorian version (Gradgrind, Pumblechook), which suits R53-28. A turncoat keeps his name (R23-4), so a bad byname is for life.

**Outside the Inner World (§VI, untouched):**
- **Dawi:** see §3.
- **Goblinoid:** R20-1-GOBLINOID_ANCHOR gives a short given name plus a "compound byname describing the force that made the person"; bynames outlive memory of their cause. The fiction anchor is Warhammer's Ork names. *Comedy:* native. A dreaded name nobody can explain sets up a joke.
- **Elven:** R20-1-ELVEN_ANCHOR: "Names as heritable property, publicly validated". Open roots with pitch contours (R37-3). *Comedy:* only the "-ama" and "-ak" insults.
- **Beastkin:** R20-1-BEASTKIN_ANCHOR: "Soul-name by birth circumstance"; failing the name is shame. *Comedy:* a circumstance-name for an awkward birth.
- **Zettari** (R32-1, "that register stands"), **Celestial Host** (R40-2) and **Holy See** (R20-2): nothing comic is native; Zettari puns must be real Swahili or Arabic (R50-01).

## 3. Borin Ironheart and the Dawi

**The card** (Volume I Character Cards, "Borin Ironheart · The Master of the Soul Forge"). "Say it · BOR-in EYE-ern-hart". Full Name: Borin Ironheart. High Dawi, Rune-Forger lineage, about 487 years old. Ironheart is also his Primary Trait. He "Tells terrible jokes and laughs at them himself", calls everyone lad or lass, and stops joking under stress. The card carries no patronymic and no oath-name. His apprentice Ymir Rhok is called "Borin's Whetstone" and "Young Anvil" by the Annex smiths.

**Dawi naming customs** (Naming Guide Amendment; Runic Dawi page; Dawi Inventory). R20-1-DAWI_ANCHOR (live): "Norse dithematic compounding with Finnish phonology". R20-3-DAWI_ELEMENTS (live): "Consonant gradation applies at the join."
- Three slots: a given name from a fixed two-element bank, a patronymic, an earned byname. An oath-name is added at oath-taking and sits between given name and patronymic. Worked example: "Torgrim Borensson the Immovable".
- The thirty-element bank: endurance (Tor, Kal, Bor, Dur, Ven, Pek), craft (Grim, Rin, Stol, Vak, Nal, Keth), stone and metal (Tharn, Rul, Ak, Brek, Kohr, Skarn), virtue (Hal, Vor, Sen, Drak, Mund, Frek), nature (Ost, Eld, Valt, Holm, Sund, Grau). Either element may come first, with gradation at the join.
- Phonology. The tongue has no voiced stops: "Borin is Porin" to a Dawi ear, and b, d, g appear only in Concord transcription. Vowel harmony is absolute, so an oath-name that will not harmonise cannot be sworn. Initial clusters are allowed (kr-, thr-, st-, vr-, tn-). Stress is always first-syllable.
- Grade is status. An oath-name is said in the strong grade while the oath stands. The weak grade is "the worst thing one Dawi can do to another". Concord speakers flatten grade constantly without noticing, which Dawi hear as that insult. Giving one's name to a stranger is "being audited".
- Existing Dawi insult-names: Bare-chin, Will-sayer, Cask-cracker, Wrong angle, Voiced, Just rock.
- Fiction anchor in the source: "Think Tolkien's Dwarves meets the Kalevala". Borin is itself a minor Tolkien dwarf name in the Durin line, and R50-04 allows the echo.

**Borin and Snorin, tested.** Borin parses as Bor + Rin, "carry-cut" (the card gives no gloss). Rhymes are buildable inside the bank on the shared element Rin: Torin, Kalrin, Halrin, and Durin, which is Tolkien's. Rhyming dwarves also have a real Norse root. Tolkien took his names from the Dvergatal, the dwarf-list in the poem Völuspá, which runs in rhyme and alliteration (Fíli, Kíli; Bífurr, Báfurr, Bömburr). "Snorin" has no element in the bank and works only as English, so in-world it can exist only as an outlier (R50-18) or in a Concord mouth. A Concord nickname for a Dawi is legal (R20-4, R50-33). In Dawi ears it lands as a name said wrong, which is a joke that is also a threat (R5-D).

## 4. Census: comic, punning, rhyming and meaning names already in canon

**Comic or punning nicknames:**
- **Gorrum Vaelseth, "Toad Guy"**, also "The Croak in the Rain". Lyraen Callivane calls him "Old Croak", a pun on a toad's croak and on dying. This is the clearest comic name in canon. "Guy" is modern-sounding; it is legal in a mouth (R48-13) and passes the checker list.
- **Daigo Tenryū, "Ribbon-Ox", "The Dango Dragon"**: a sound pun on Daigo and dango (a Japanese rice dumpling), backed by a dango-skewer kit.
- **Moryn Sporetail, "The Walking Cap", "Mushroom Fox"**: an English compound pun on a mushroom cap.
- **Goraku Tenryū, "Oni of the Ninth Toast", "Laughing Bloodstorm"**: in real Japanese, goraku means amusement. The card does not gloss it.
- **Hazzen Skollgrin, "The Crooked Grin", "Laughing Latch"**: the surname reads as skull-grin.
- Also: Kurokiba Ranzou, "The Drunken Oni"; Karo Venrik, "The Foolish Magus"; Ymir Rhok, "Borin's Whetstone" and "Young Anvil" (a smiths' joke); Gimbzo calling Darius "Weighing Man".

**Meaning names and bynames (Northern style):**
- **Nol Tally**, a deer-counter of the Night Watch Society. His card asks whether Tally is a frozen surname or a live byname "from the counting", marked "pending Isaac". Malphas: "A man named Tally has looked."
- **Soren the Mast**: "The quay called him the Mast before he had a trade".
- **Wystan Ashmore, "Late Bell"**: the byname "is the Division's, not his".
- Also: Dunstan the Brine (The Ladle), Nella Forgeman, Garret Longshore, Ascella called Wick by the scribes, Spoil (formerly Tanya, self-renamed), and Hild and Robin Ice (an Accord roll-name).

**Meaning names in other registers:** Shiro Kitsunari, "Little White Fox" (shiro is white, kitsune is fox). Sumire Nekoha (neko is cat). Sadamu, called Futakoto ("two words", kept from the epithet Two Words).

**Rhyming sets:** none were made on purpose. There are accidental near-rhymes: Borin and Orin (Orin Farrant), and the generic Kael, Cael, Nael, Vael cluster. The roster holds one sibling pair, Hob and Dunstan Pryor, which does not rhyme.

**Scenes:** no soldiers' comic nicknames turned up; comic names live on cards, not in the muster.

## 5. Where comic names collide or need a decision

1. **Who hears the pun (R5-C1).** An English pun is in-world only where the name is spoken in Common or Northern. A pun buried in a Dawi, Moto, Mahuo, Zettari or Elven name is heard by the reader over the characters' heads, which is the wink R5-C1 bans. *Decision:* must every comic name be a joke someone in the world can hear, or may some be reader-only?
2. **Which slot (R20-3, R23-2, R40-1).** Bynames are the native comic slot in Northern, Far-Northern and goblinoid naming. Given names are bank-built (Dawi, Mahuo, Yukari, Moto) or drawn from a fixed pool of forty (Northern). "Snorin" as a given name fits no bank. *Decision:* comic given names, comic bynames, or both? If given names, by bank or as outliers?
3. **How many (R50-18 vs the superseded R23-5).** No ratio remains, and Isaac said "a lot". The struck budget warned of "an author picking names he liked". *Decision:* a share or a rule of thumb per scene, per culture, or per rank. Commons and minor NPCs only, or anyone?
4. **Rhyming pairs (R50-17, R5-B).** Sound-alike names are legal and their meeting "becomes a beat". Foil pairs are tracked. *Decision:* are rhyming sets deliberate kin (twins, a sworn pair, a work crew), with the rhyme as the family joke? Or are they coincidences the world remarks on?
5. **The Dawi.** The grade makes a Dawi name an audit. Comedy is native when outsiders mangle the name, not in the bank. Borin jokes; his name is not a joke. *Decision:* do Dawi names stay grave while Concord mouths supply the comedy, or do some Dawi carry rhyming given names (Dvergatal style), by a new bank element or as outliers?
6. **Bloodline houses (R40-1, R23-4, R52-14, R51-01).** Moto given names state what the line expects, the narration runs antique, and Sodoku jokes only by accident. *Decision:* are the archaic bloodlines, and the Moto above all, exempt? Is a comic name in a house a punishment (renaming is Japonic-only)? Never applied to Sodoku or the other creators' characters, and no renaming of them.
7. **Foreign-language puns need a gloss (R50-01, R50-31, R51-15, R8-26).** The pun must be real grammar. Script cannot show, the zero-gloss budget bars an appositive (a gloss tucked into the sentence), and nobody glosses their own technique. Goraku's joke is invisible today. *Decision:* may narration or the card gloss a person's pun name, or must a character say it aloud?
8. **Existing names (R50-02, R20C-40).** Established names are fixed for grammar, not renamed. Borin Ironheart and every card stay as they are; a "Snorin" is a new character. *Decision:* confirm that comic names are for new names only, with no comic retrofit.
9. **Walk-ons (R50-27).** A new person is a role until they speak twice or matter, so a throwaway comic name for a one-line guard (the Pratchett and Dickens move) is barred today. *Decision:* may a comic name be the exception, or do comic names wait for a second line?
10. **By ear, then checked (R50-30, R50-28).** Names coined mid-scene are checked against the banks at scene end; a comic outlier will fail a bank check, and a coinage is canon only if Isaac reuses it. *Decision:* does the scene-end check pass comic names as outliers?
11. **Earth words in names (R51-08, R51-03, R51-11, R51-06, R50-04, R50-05).** No Earth day or month names (no Tuesday, May or August), no banned slang in narration, and WOTR-term words (Ledger, Echo, Weave) carry their WOTR sense only. Earth-derived words, pop-culture echoes and myth names are allowed. No rule covers brand, meme or modern-tech puns. *Decision:* where is the line for anachronism puns?
12. **Which test governs (R49-44 vs NATALIE.md).** The belief test (R15-2) is superseded by a checker word list, yet NATALIE.md still applies it, and a pun can pass the list and still break belief. *Decision:* restore the belief test for names, or let the list alone govern?
13. **Tone spine (R11-1, R5-D, R53-28).** Humour is licensed in the Abercrombie register, and the Victorian look has Dickens. Pack Eleven "does not reopen what the page may sound like". *Decision:* which comic register (Norse blunt, Dickensian, Pratchett pun, anime gag)? Is a comic name ever spoken at a grave beat (a death, the Waiting)?
14. **Third Names (R50-21, R20-5).** A comic Third Name for Isaac's character sticks only if he keeps it; none is coined for the other creators' characters. *Decision:* may Third Names be comic, given that "the title becomes the person"?
15. **Scope beyond people (R50-11, R50-12, R8-22, R50-25, R20C-21).** Technique names gloss literally in two words and Latin must be real. Wellspring folk names are free, and "well-rats" is already comic slang. *Decision:* do comic names reach places, inns, items, techniques and folk names, or only people? (Drafting note: comic names still need a Say it line, R50-34.)

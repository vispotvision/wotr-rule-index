# DRAFT: Swears, Oaths and Insults by Culture

**Status: draft for Isaac. Nothing here is canon until he approves it row by row.** Written under R51-35 (a starter set per culture, drafted for approval), R51-29 (each culture swears in its own words first; English swears are fine too), R51-30 (Earth religious swears are replaced in-world), R51-02 (swears may bleed into close-POV narration), R51-20 (Kharven native words on the Far-Northern sound rules), R51-21 (Moto feudal register, never modern casual), R51-23 (Dawi drop into their own tongue), R51-24 (Elves: English plus Vey-Elarin loanwords from the root bank), R51-19 (Accord Latin anywhere by ear), R50-01 and R50-12 (borrowed real-language phrases must be real and correctly glossed), R50-28 (a coined word is canon only if Isaac reuses it), R50-31 and R50-32 (romanised only, never italic in prose).

## How to read the tables

**Source** says where each row stands:
- **attested**: the wording is already in a Standing Inventory, a wiki page or a scene; only its use as a swear is proposed here.
- **derived**: one step from an attested fact, which is named.
- **coined**: new, built on the culture's sound rules or grammar. Pending until Isaac reuses it.
- **English**: an ordinary English swear given this culture's flavour.

**Strength**, four grades:
- **mild**: said in front of children, a grunt at a dropped tool.
- **firm**: rude; said to a peer's face it is a rebuke.
- **hard**: an insult that has to be answered or swallowed.
- **grave**: fighting words, or a curse that some people will not say aloud at all.

No row uses God, Christ, Jesus, hell or damnation in any form. The in-world replacements are the Sky, the Fourteen, the named Archons, the Veil, the Realmsong, Varūn and the ancestors.

## Kharven

The clans swear by the Sky and the fire, curse with the cold, and insult by scarcity: bad wood, empty saddles, cracked bowls, short stacks. The Sky is never named beyond "the Sky" (R23-8). Native words follow the Far-Northern sound rules: vowels a, i, u and their doubles, no e or o, onsets and codas from the namebank set, suffixes such as -aq, -ut, -luk, -tuq, -ra.

|---|---|---|---|---|
| By the sky that covers us. | An oath taken under the whole Sky. | firm (solemn) | Anyone swearing a thing true; elders at a death-house; never said lightly | attested (Kharven Inventory, Oaths) |
| By the fire. | An oath on the hearth that keeps the household alive. | mild | Everyday emphasis in any household; "by the fire, I told him twice" | attested (Kharven Inventory, Oaths) |
| Sky take it. | Let the Sky have it, since the Sky takes the dead. | mild | The everyday "damn it" in any mouth, riders, wives, children who think nobody is listening | derived (sky burial, "gone to the sky") |
| Wet wood. | Wood that smokes and will not catch. | hard | Said of a liar or a man who cannot be relied on; the whole proverb, "wet wood burns eventually, a liar never dries," is the formal version | attested (Kharven Inventory, Insults; Sky scene) |

## Moto

A house that holds the offence is inaccuracy, so its heaviest words are verdicts. The feudal register governs (R51-21): full forms, no contractions, honorifics in Japanese where a Japanese word is used, and the Japanese words below are real, correct and romanised. **The Moto never swear by a single Archon**, since addressing one alone is Singling, the principal heresy; a Moto who hears a Kharven say "Archons take it" hears nothing wrong, and a Moto who hears "Irath take it" hears heresy. They also do not petition, so there is no Moto "help me."

| Swear | Literal meaning | Strength | Who says it | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| By the Fourteen. | Before all fourteen Archons at once, as the Bow is made. | firm (solemn) | The only lawful invocation in a Moto mouth; senior line members, the Kōkan, retainers taking an order | derived (Sōhai, the Unsingled Crown) |
| Incorrect. | Wrongly read. | hard; from a senior, grave | The whole of blame, as "correct" is the whole of praise; lords to retainers, fathers to sons; one word, no heat | derived ("correct" as praise; "the offence is inaccuracy") |
| Empty Bow. | Observance performed without the labour. | grave | Said of a man or a house that keeps the forms and does none of the work; held worse than neglect | attested (Moto Inventory, Insults; Sōhai) |
| Singler. | One who elevates one Archon above the fourteen. | grave | A charge of heresy; the Kōkan say it of Sancta Lux, and of themselves when Totality starts to look like a crown | derived (Singling) |
| Petitioner. | One who asks an Archon for something. | hard | Contempt for a beggar at the altar, and for any man who bargains where he should labour | attested (Petition) |
| Clogged. | A house that will not let its dead leave. | hard | Said of a house that keeps its dead in portraits, relics and rehearsed grief | attested (Moto Inventory, Insults) |
| Let him go unread. | May nobody read his name at Ice-out. | grave | A curse wishing a man's death to go unentered and unfaced; spoken by the old, rarely, and never over a body | derived (the dead read at Ice-out, "behind is a direction") |
| Written in the wrong hand. | A document in the wrong Archon's hand, void rather than incorrect. | hard | Said of a bastard, a false claim, a forged order, or a man who holds an office he was never read into | derived (the Fourteen Hands) |
| Poet. | A swordsman who fights decoratively. | firm | Weapons masters to students; "against plate, do not behave like a poet" | attested (Moto Inventory, Insults) |
| A snake dressed up as a Moto. | What it says. | hard | Said of a false claimant to the name | attested (Moto Inventory, Insults) |
| Bureimono. | Rude person; insolent wretch (Japanese, bu-rei "discourtesy" + mono "person"). | hard | A lord to an inferior who has forgotten his place; the classic feudal rebuke | real Japanese |
| Kisama. | You (Japanese; once honorific, long since contemptuous). | hard | A superior to an enemy or a disgraced inferior; used as a form of address, which is the insult | real Japanese |

| Held by nobody. | Elar-ama: entered as a name nobody stands for. | grave | Only as a verdict after "I revoke the name Moto from you"; as an insult outside that, it is a threat | attested (Moto Inventory, Oaths) |

## Dawi

Dawi have no light oath. "Kurme" is the swearing itself, so a Dawi who says "I swear" has sworn, and they do not say it for emphasis. Their curses fall on the cask, the angle, the entry and the grade. Runic Dawi is used as the grammar gives it: no voiced stops, vowel harmony, verb last, "ei" before the verb for not.

| Swear | Literal meaning | Strength | Who says it | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Kurnur. | Unentered: you did it and nobody will stand behind it. | hard | Any Dawi to any Dawi; the three-times form ("Kurnur. Kurnur. Kurnur.") quotes the Oath-Breakers' wall and is grave | attested (Dawi Inventory; Runic Dawi) |
| The name in the weak grade. | Torve for Torvekk: the oath-name said as broken. | grave | The worst thing one Dawi can do to another in one syllable; done on purpose only by enemies and the wall | attested (Dawi Inventory, Insults) |
| Ollu kuolee. | The brew dies. | hard | The Dawi "damn it" for real disaster, a collapsed gallery or a lost cask; Barrel Dawi will not say it within sight of a Cask-Oath barrel | derived (the Cask-Oath opening's own words) |
| Nikkat ei on. | The angles are not [true]. | hard | The flat damnation of a piece of work, the Tally's "Nikkat on" turned over; clerks, rune-forgers, masters over apprentices | coined sentence on attested grammar (ei before the verb) |
| Wrong angle. | A rune cut askew, which does nothing. | firm | Said of any failed work or failed man; "askew" for his work in general | attested (Dawi Inventory, Insults) |
| Voiced. | Said the way somebody not paying attention says it (b for p). | firm | Said of a careless speaker, and of Concord men generally | attested (Dawi Inventory, Insults, derived there) |
| Just rock. | The thing without its measurement. | firm | Said of worthless ore, a worthless plan, a man with no record | derived (proverb "the thing without the measurement is just rock") |
| The seam's out. | The ore seam is played out. | mild | Resigned exclamation, "we are done here"; miners, shift crews | derived ("How's the seam?") |
| Varūn never said. | The god never told us which answer he meant. | mild | A shrug-oath at a question with no answer; the old, the Reach branches | derived (proverb "Varūn has never once told any of us which one he actually meant") |
| Cask-cracker. | One who ends a brew that holds an oath. | grave | Said of an oath-breaker; Barrel Dawi above all | derived (the Cask-Oath) |
| Piss in the brew. | Exactly that. | grave | English body swear aimed at the one thing Dawi hold sacred; said to start a fight, or by outsiders who do not know better | English |
| Will-sayer. | One who says "I will," which the tongue cannot. | firm | Said of a Concord envoy, a braggart, a young Dawi who has spent too long in Stannvaard | derived (no future tense) |

| Kurlo. Ei kurme. | Unbegun. Not undertaken. | hard (as a refusal) | The "no, and do not ask me for the other word" that ends a negotiation | attested (Runic Dawi, sample texts) |

## Accord (Concord human)

Chartered, metered and entered people swear by paperwork and the Archons' days, and insult by weights and measures. Latin is allowed anywhere by ear (R51-19); every Latin row below is correct classical form. The Accord's calendar gives the Archon days: Auren's for the dead, Veyra's for markets, Irath's for musters, and Uurgath's, which nobody chooses on purpose.

| Swear | Literal meaning | Strength | Who says it | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Archons take it. | Let the Fourteen have it. | mild | The everyday "damn it" of the Concord, in every class | derived (R51-30's own example) |
| Before witnesses. | Coram testibus: said in front of people who could swear to it. | mild | The everyday emphatic; "I told him, before witnesses" | attested (Accord Inventory, Oaths) |
| Uurgath's day. | The day of the Devourer of Forms, when no sane court sits. | firm | Exclamation at a disaster or a bad start; clerks, market people, soldiers | derived (a court sitting on Uurgath's has chosen it) |
| Uurgath te devoret. | May Uurgath devour you. | hard | Latin curse from the educated; Division officers, advocates in a temper | coined (correct Latin, jussive subjunctive) |
| Urion te ponderet. | May Urion weigh you. | hard | A curse that wishes judgment on a man; Sancta Lux-leaning speakers, Measurewrights | coined (correct Latin) |
| Nullum et irritum. | Null and void. | firm | Said of a man, a plan or a promise that is worth nothing; clerks and anyone who has spent time around them | attested formula, use derived |
| Salvo iure tertii. | Saving the right of a third party. | mild | Said drily of a promise with a hole in it, "yes, salvo iure tertii"; advocates, merchants | attested formula, use derived |

| Cut his plate. | Cut off his supply for arrears. | hard | A threat or curse in main districts: may he freeze in the dark | derived (the stopcock and the cut plate) |
| May your Echo stay in the house. | May your dead self linger after the layer-out is gone. | grave | A curse among the poor; not said in a death-house, and not said twice | derived (the layer-out knows whether the Echo has gone) |

## Elven

English plus Vey-Elarin loanwords (R51-24), built only from the attested root bank: elar (note, name, person), ma (hold), ur (witness), ie (grant), sae and lo (in season, out of season), lyth (silence), seral (winter), -ama (contingent), -ak (foreign), sylva, aar, dhae. The five branches share the tongue and differ on what the worst thing is.

| Swear | Literal meaning | Strength | Who says it | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Out of season. | Done outside its appointed window. | grave | The worst Eressean insult; worse than "badly done" | attested (Eresse Inventory, Insults) |
| Lo. | Out of season, as one word. | mild as an exclamation, hard said to a person | "Lo," at a missed moment is the elven "damn it"; Drow use it for a debt past its term | derived (lo, out of season; Drow lo, past term) |
| -ama on a name. | The name held, and nobody stood for it. | hard | Put on a name to its owner's face; Eresse and Korvaeth both | attested (Eresse and Korvaeth Inventories) |
| Ma-ama. | Held, and nobody stood for it. | hard | Said of a usurper, a stolen thing, a child nobody witnessed; from the proverb "Ma-ama, lyth" | derived (the proverb) |
| A bare clause. | A thing said that claims nothing. | firm | Said of a speaker who will not own his words; courts and councils | attested (Eresse Inventory, Insults) |
| Held contingently. | Holding what nobody has stood for. | firm | A cold insult to an office-holder | attested (Eresse Inventory, Insults) |
| Flat-tongued. | Speaks without the pitch contours. | firm | Said of Concord speakers, who have greeted nobody | attested (Eresse Inventory, Insults) |

## Zettari

The Zettari speak their own tongue, Swahili, Bantu and Arabic in flavour (R32-1). Every Swahili row below is meant as correct standard Swahili, per R50-01; a native-speaker check before canon is recommended. Their oaths need something older than the speaker to hear them (the Stone Witness Oath), their pride is weight and bearing, and their shame is lightness, broken cords and unmarked skin.

| Swear | Literal meaning | Strength | Who says it | Source |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Jiwe limesikia. | The stone has heard. | firm (solemn) | Closes a vow as binding; any Zettari, and a Zettari who says it has sworn | derived (Stone Witness Oath) |
| Kwa damu ya mababu. | By the blood of the ancestors. | firm (solemn) | The formal oath of the royal line and the Pillar houses | derived ("the blood is the first oath") |
| Mababu wanaona. | The ancestors are watching. | mild | A warning to a child or a peer about to disgrace the line; also a muttered "damn it" when something goes wrong in front of elders | derived (Ancestor Communion) |
| Mzigo wa damu. | The weight of the blood. | mild | An exclamation of burden, "the weight of it"; the young of high blood, tired | derived (the curse "The Weight of the Blood") |
| Hold shape. | What the Zettari presence tells the world. | mild | An oath-exclamation in the common tongue, "steady"; soldiers, forge workers | derived ("Their presence tells the world: hold shape.") |
| Radi ikupige. | May thunder strike you. | hard | Southern and Cape speech, where the Storm Drums are; a curse between equals | coined (correct Swahili, subjunctive) |
| Mwepesi. | Light one: a person with no weight. | hard | The core Zettari insult; weightlessness is the shame of a people whose body is the first throne | coined (correct Swahili) |
| Kamba iliyokatika. | A cord that snapped. | grave | Said of a vow-breaker or one who shamed the line; the Cord of Becoming broken | coined (correct Swahili), from the Cord of Becoming |

| Mjinga. | Fool. | firm | Ordinary Swahili insult, any rank | real Swahili |
| Clean skin. | No ink: no thresholds survived. | firm | Said of the untested young or a soft outsider | derived (Ink Rite of the Living Ancestor) |

## Open questions for Isaac

1. The Kharven "empty saddle" and "cracked bowl" carry attested wording with no gloss in the Inventory; the senses proposed here are drafts.
2. The four coined Kharven words (qatuq, kiilut, hulluk, numra) follow the Far-Northern phonotactics; their meanings are the draft's.
3. Japanese and Swahili rows are real words; the Swahili should get a native check before any goes canon (R50-01).
4. Whether "Ollu kuolee" is too heavy for a Dawi to say at all is a call about Barrel Dawi taboo.
5. The Moto rule that a single-Archon oath is heresy follows from the Sōhai page; it would make "Irath take it" a live insult to the Moto in any mixed scene.

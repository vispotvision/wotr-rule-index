# The Common Drafts

*The standard catalogue of the Draft trade, as kept by quartermasters, licensed apothecaries and the excise office · prices in Accord marks*

> Twenty-two Drafts account for most of what is poured, sold, smuggled and drunk across the four quarters. This is the list every counter-clerk learns first, every smuggler forges first, and every practitioner should be able to name by the colour of the seal.

---

## What it is

A **Draft** (a working poured into a substance, so that it can be carried and drunk) is the one form of magic a stranger can sell you. Every Draft is **graded by the Stage it serves** (the Stage is a practitioner's rung on the sixteen-step ladder of power). A Draft at your own grade does what the label says. **A Draft below your Stage does little**, because a stronger body simply outclasses it. **A Draft above your Stage is dangerous**: it pours more into the Shell (the Aether Shell, the skin of power around the Soul Crystal) than the Shell is built to carry, and the excess comes out as burns, nosebleeds, a scalded aura, or a crack in the Crystal itself.

The commonest Drafts are poured at several grades. A grade-II Windglass and a grade-VII Windglass share a name, a recipe and a crash, and almost nothing else.

## How it looks

Glass or stoneware, a wax seal, an excise stamp, and sediment at the bottom that a careful buyer tilts to the light. A licensed Draft carries its grade in a Roman numeral pressed into the seal; a fight vial carries it on the cork as well, so it can be read by touch in the dark.

**A fight Draft takes a beat to drink.** Thumb off the cork, swallow, and the half-second the body takes to answer. In a duel that beat is an opening, and every trained fighter watches for the hand going to the belt. What follows shows: a refill brightens a dimming aura back toward its full colour, a speed Draft leaves the drinker's footing light and quick, a strength Draft flushes the skin and swells the veins in the neck.

## What it costs

**Coin.** Prices climb roughly threefold with each Stage a Draft serves, because each grade holds roughly three times the Essence (the stuff a working is made of) and the rendering loses more of it at every step:

| Grade served | Stage name | Band price for a plain fight Draft |
|---|---|---|
| **I** | Murmuring | 4 copper |
| **II** | Welling | 12 copper |
| **III** | Ascension | 1½ silver |
| **IV** | Flourishing | 4½ silver |
| **V** | Splintering | 13½ silver |
| **VI** | Glory | about 3½ gold |
| **VII** | Refraction | about 10 gold |
| **VIII** | Transcendence | about 30 gold |
| **IX** | Invocation | about 90 gold |
| **X** | Realization | about 270 gold |

> Twenty-four copper make a silver mark and twelve silver marks a gold. A labourer earns a silver mark in a good day. **A grade-X Draft costs a clerk four years of wages**, and the households that keep a case of them keep it in a vault.

**The crash.** Every fight Draft leaves a crash once its window closes, and the crash is the real price. Two or three in one fight is ordinary. Five is how veterans describe the worst week of their lives.

**The stack.** A strength or speed Draft is one layer of boost, and boosts from every source stack: Drafts, Trait (inborn power) surges, release forms (a practitioner's named, staged power-up), allies' help. **The stack tops out at two Grades above what the body can sustain**, and every layer a fighter adds raises the chance of a Crystal fracture (a crack in the Soul Crystal, the seat of a practitioner's power).

**Provenance.** Every Draft came from somewhere, and its source type says where the risk lives:

| Source type | Where it comes from | What it risks |
|---|---|---|
| **Creature-Sourced** | Rendered from magical creatures, and at the top of the trade from people | The strongest, and it carries the **identity of what it was made from** (Identity Contamination) |
| **Material-Sourced** | High-density minerals and salts drawn off a Wellspring (a natural spring of magic) vein | Clean but weaker |
| **Environmental** | Matter steeped in a Wellspring field until it took the set | Gentle and slow; close to no risk |
| **Synthetic** | Blended at the bench from several sources | The least predictable; the parts can react inside the drinker |

## How it is beaten

- **Take the beat.** A fighter who stops to drink has stopped. Hit the hand, the vial or the throat.
- **Outlast the window.** Every fight Draft runs out. Stay alive past it and fight the crash instead of the Draft.
- **Read the grade.** A drinker who has gone above their own grade bleeds from the nose and runs hot within a minute. They will burn out without help.
- **Read the source.** A Creature-Sourced Draft brings its creature's appetites with it, and those appetites can be baited.
- **Break the supply.** Drafts are bought. A fighter who has drunk their belt dry is a fighter at half strength.

## Example

Hesk Tarrow, a Stage III caravan guard, holds a bridge against two raiders. Halfway through he is down to a quarter of his reserve (the Essence he can spend before running dry), his aura thinning to a flicker. He steps back, takes the beat, and drinks a grade-III Vein Cordial; the light comes back up his arms, and the raider who saw the hand go to the belt has already closed the gap and cut his forearm. Hesk wins the bridge. That night he cannot recover a drop on his own, his forearm is still open, and a grade-III Willow Grey costs a silver and a half. He buys the four-copper grade I instead, which does almost nothing for a Stage III body, from a stall whose seal he did not check.

## Counterfeits

**Counterfeits are common and sometimes deadly.** Chalkwater is the cheapest. Worse are the real Drafts sold a grade high (a grade-II Cordial with a grade-IV seal, which does too little) and the real Drafts sold a grade low (which does too much and puts the buyer in the street with a scalded Shell). Spoiled stock relabelled with a fresh date is the most common killer of all.

> **Check the seal, tilt the sediment, smell the cork, and buy from someone you can find again.** Licensed stock carries an excise stamp and the pourer's mark. Neither proves the bottle. Both prove there is a ledger, and a ledger is somebody to hang.

## Where they are sold

- **The cities on the main** (the licensed district supply of Aether) buy anything: corner apothecaries for the everyday Drafts, licensed houses for the fight and advancement stock, and smugglers in every port for the rest. The trade is licensed, taxed at the gate and smuggled around the tax.
- **The north** trades in kind. A belt of Drafts travels where a wall cannot, so the north ranks the pouring above the cutting, and a vial is paid for in salt, meat, fuel or a season's labour owed.
- **The East** buys through its sects (the schools and great houses). A disciple draws Drafts against rank and duty; an outsider buys through a sect's factor, at a sect's price, or not at all.

## Notes (not published)

**Status.** Draft page for the Notion wiki, Magic System section, under R78-9 (a standard catalogue of about twenty common Drafts; fight Drafts with a crash; graded by Stage served; the trade at every level) and R78-10 (fight Drafts refill freely; prices roughly x3 a Stage; licensed, taxed, smuggled; counterfeits common and sometimes deadly; cities everything, north in kind, East via sects; stacking capped at two Grades with fracture risk per layer). **Canon reused, not invented.** Twelve of the twenty-two are existing canon, with their effects and risks kept:
- From *The Standing Index*: Cutwater Tincture (0.15 s, spoils in eleven days), Metering Tonic (0.4 s, habituation), Quiet-Hour Incense (0.5 s, dependence and dulled Harmonics Attunement), Hillcarry Salts (1.2 s), Ascension Ration, Accord Standard (8 s), Splinter Ration (48 s), Refraction Draught (160 s), Threshold Reversal Draught (struck 691). Prices converted from the Index's silver marks and kept as given.
- From *The Alchemical Index*: Equilibrium Draught (over-damping), Grace Fall Draught (the late reckoning), Veyric Sparkwine (preignition), Beastheart Serum (restraint lost, kin and prey).
- From *The Four Crafts*: the unwoken drinker and "deepens the grain" (Penny Tonic's side effect); the north ranks pouring first.
- From *The Ladder and the Draft*: the four source types, Identity Contamination, the floor, the seed and the Catalyst, the dishonest use (Ladder Dust's crash).
- Not used: Slagwright's Draught, Bone-Grey Compress, Tide-Silt Compress, Stillwater Draught and the Titan compounds. They stay where they are; the catalogue is the common shelf, not the Index.

**New figures set here.**
- **The band** (4 copper at Stage I, x3 a Stage to about 270 gold at X). Derived from R78-10's x3 and the brief's "Stage I a few copper". Checked against the Index: Metering Tonic (grade II) at 10 copper sits on the band's 12; Hillcarry (II) at 1.2 s sits between II and III; Refraction (VII) at 160 s sits near the band's ~120 s. The Ascension Ration (8 s at grade III, band 1½ s) and the Splinter Ration (48 s at V, band 13½ s) sit three to five times above it; the page explains the gap as the licensed advancement premium and does not touch the Index figures.
- **Multipliers on the band:** Vein Cordial x2 (Essence for the reserve is Vein Draw, guild-licence only); Beastheart Serum x3 (the Alchemical Index enters it as Provenance Class IV); Ladder Dust at twice the next grade's band; Spawnblood at half the band (unlicensed render undercuts); street price of advancement Drafts at three to ten times the Index. All mine.
- **Vein Cordial** returns about a quarter of a full reserve over three breaths; recovery stalls for a day; three in an hour scald the Shell. Mine.
- **Windows:** Beastheart about two minutes; Windglass about a minute; Red Seal about an hour; Clearsight about ten minutes; Equilibrium about an hour. Equilibrium's window is not in canon; set here. Metering's six to nine hours is canon.
- **Grade boosts** of one Grade (Strike Force, Dexterity, Gnosis Perception): mine, inside R78-10's two-Grade stack cap.

Real analogues: Willow Grey is salicylate from willow bark (gastric bleeding, tinnitus); Red Seal is an antifibrinolytic of the tranexamic acid kind (thrombosis risk); Windglass is a sympathomimetic snuff (cramp, tendon strain); Clearsight is a mydriatic and sensitiser (photophobia).
- **Threshold Reversal seizure value** about 30 gold: mine.
- **Grade columns for canon Drafts:** Hillcarry II, Ascension Ration III, Splinter Ration V, Refraction VII follow the Stage each serves in canon. Equilibrium, Grace Fall, Veyric and Beastheart grade ranges are mine.

**Calls made.**
1. **Canon conflict: does a dose refill a reserve?** *Fracture of Worlds X, The Tier Ladders, The Drafts ladder:* "No dose refills a reserve. A practitioner is refilled by a Wellspring or not at all, and a Draft drunk on an empty reserve mends the body and steadies the Shell and leaves the reserve as empty as it found it." Also, Counterplay: "The rung says how much a dosed opponent took in, and none of it is reserve." Against it, R78-10 (Isaac, 2026-10-15): "fight Drafts refill reserve freely", and its note: "Retracts The Ladder and the Draft's 'no dose refills a reserve' for fight Drafts." The page follows R78-10: Vein Cordial refills, made from Vein Draw so the Essence is still Wellspring Essence, which keeps the spirit of the canon sentence. Tier Ladders X needs the same retraction when it is next touched (its Drafts ladder and Counterplay paragraph both say it). The Ladder and the Draft page itself does not carry the sentence on the current mirror, though R78-10's note names it.
2. **Name length.** The brief asks for names of two words at most. Canon names are kept whole where they run longer (Grace Fall Draught, Threshold Reversal Draught); the Index's "Ascension Ration, Accord Standard" is shortened to Ascension Ration.
3. **Source type for canon entries.** The Index grades by Provenance Class, the Ladder by source type, and the two do not map one to one. Mapping used: Class I vein salts to Material-Sourced; Class II field substrate to Environmental; Class III to Creature-Sourced when rendered from beasts and Environmental when from plants; Class IV and V to Creature-Sourced; blends to Synthetic. Refraction Draught is rendered from people (Class IV, *Hide, Horn and Bone*), so Creature-Sourced covers human render; the page says so plainly.
4. **Stage grade versus Index tier.** The Index's T1 to T9 grades the maker's standing and who may hold a lot; this page's grade is the Stage the drinker must be. They are different readings and the page uses only the Stage.
5. **Unwoken drinkers.** A Class Ø soul has no Stage; household Drafts are graded "Unwoken" for them, following *The Four Crafts* (a Draft acts on the Core of an unwoken soul and gives "the working and none of the architecture").

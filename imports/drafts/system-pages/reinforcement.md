# Reinforcement

*How a practitioner's body holds its power, and how they push it further. For the whole system in brief, see How Magic Works.*

## What it is

A practitioner's stats (the measured strengths of body and soul, read off the Soul Crystal, the organ that stores and sends out their power) live in the body all the time as a **passive base**: always on, costing nothing. **Reinforcement** is the active layer on top, Essence (a practitioner's own power, drawn from the world's Aether) driven through flesh, bone and whatever they hold, to push the body further for a price in reserve (the Essence they carry).

| Layer | What it does | What it costs | When it is lost |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Passive base** | The body at its sheet values: a B-Grade Durability (B, a letter band of force) takes a blow as B, waking or sleeping | Nothing | Sags under Essence Starvation (the state below a tenth of reserve) |
| **Spread coat** | The whole body lifted within its Grade, toward the top of the band | About ten minutes of holding from a full reserve, at Adept standing (the Accord's fourth rank) | Asleep, drugged, surprised, or starving |
| **Focused coat** | One place (a fist, a forearm, the ribs) lifted one full Grade; everywhere else drops to the passive base | About five minutes from full | As above, and wherever the focus is not |

## How it looks

**A coat of their own aura.** Reinforcement shows as a skin of the practitioner's own colour: their Family's hue (the branch of physics their power works, each with its colour) at the clarity of their Stage (their step on the soul's sixteen-step ladder): dark low down, brighter as they climb. A Caloria (heat) fighter's coat shimmers ember-orange; a Fulguria (lightning) fighter's crackles arc-blue along the forearms; a Vectoria (force) fighter's shows as nothing but a weight the dust leans toward. It is thickest where they push.

**The body under it.** Veins stand up, skin tightens and takes a sheen, the stance settles lower, and the breath deepens. Heat comes off a hard-pushed coat in waves.

**Focus leaves gaps.** Gathered into one place, the coat blazes there and goes pale everywhere else. A fist burns; the far flank fades to bare skin. Reading where it went is half the fight, and a good fighter moves the focus faster than an enemy can read it.

**Held gear, never other people.** The coat runs into what the hand is holding: a blade, a shield, a staff, the hilt of a hammer. Let go and it leaves the steel within a breath. It cannot be laid over another person, however close or however loved.

**Masters can hide it.** Most practitioners must choose: hold their Pressure (the weight their presence puts on a room) in, which is tiring work, or coat up and show their colour. A master can reinforce with no colour showing at all, and that calm is a warning in itself. Few manage it below Master standing.

## What it costs

- **Reserve, by the second.** A card writes it as time from full: how long the coat holds, started on a full reserve, before the reserve reaches its tenth. A spread coat runs about ten minutes for an Adept, longer as their efficiency climbs; a focused coat runs about half that.
- **A layer on the stack.** A focused coat's one-Grade lift is a power-up like any other and counts toward the cap (no more than two Grades over what the Crystal can hold safely) and toward the fracture risk that comes with stacking (see Stacking and Spending). A spread coat lifts only within its Grade and adds no risk.
- **Starvation.** Below a tenth of reserve the active coat fails outright and the passive base itself sags: the body reads weaker than its sheet, worse the deeper the reserve goes, and Traits (the soul's standing laws) begin to flicker.
- **Rebuilding.** A coat dropped by surprise comes back in one beat once the practitioner knows what is happening. One beat is a long time with a knife already in.

## How it is beaten

- **Ambush.** Asleep, drugged or surprised, a practitioner keeps only the passive base. Assassins, bandits and jealous rivals live by this. Guards are posted over sleeping masters for exactly this reason.
- **Read the focus.** Strike where the coat has gone pale. A fighter who gathers everything into the guarding arm has left the ribs at base.
- **Outlast it.** Every second costs reserve. Make them chase, make them hold the coat through a long approach, and fight them when the coat fails and the base sags.
- **Disarm.** Knock the weapon loose and the coat leaves it. A coated blade is a different object from the same blade on the floor.
- **Go after the ward.** Reinforcement protects the reinforced body only. The child, the client or the wounded friend beside them stands in plain skin.
- **Cut the ground.** In thin Aether, or a district whose main (the city's Aether supply line) has been shut, everything costs more and the coat runs down sooner.

## Example

**Mags Tolley**, a Stage V hired hunter with Fulguria Essence, sleeps at a waystation with her boots on. A thief with a knife comes through the shutter. She is asleep, so she has only her passive base, but her Durability is B-Grade and the blade skids off her collarbone and opens a shallow line instead of a deep one. She wakes, and in one beat the coat comes up arc-blue over her forearms, hissing like a wet wick.

The thief has a partner at the door, Stage V like her and swinging a coated maul. Mags gathers the coat into her left forearm and takes the maul there: the arm is lifted a full Grade and the iron rings off it. But the blue has drained from her right side to bare skin, the partner sees it, and his second swing goes for her right ribs. She turns her hip, eats half of it at base, and feels a rib go. From here she has perhaps five minutes of focused coat from what is left in her, and she knows it.

## Notes (not published)

### New figures set here

- **Spread coat lifts within the Grade, toward the top of the band; focused coat lifts one place one full Grade, the rest at passive base.** Set here to give R78-9's "concentrated in one place and leaves the rest thin" a number. One Grade matches the differential rule (one Grade up wins an exchange outright), so a focused fist beats an unfocused guard and the read matters, as R78-9 wants.
- **Spread coat about ten minutes from full, focused about five, at Adept standing.** Derived from the Essence Ledger's Stage IV worked practitioner (reserve 3,100 EU, full output 258.1 AU/s, 12 s to empty at full output): a spread coat priced at one fiftieth of full output gives 600 s, a focused coat at one twenty-fifth gives 300 s. Cards set their own.
- **A dropped coat returns in one beat.** Set here; matches the beat a release call costs (R8-24).
- **"Few manage it below Master standing"** for hiding and reinforcing at once (R78-10 says only "masters can").

### Calls made

- The passive base is read as the sheet values (R78-9: "stats are always in the body as a passive base, and active reinforcement pushes further"), so the active layer is a lift above the sheet, and only the focused lift counts on the stack. A spread coat is ordinary sustainable work and adds no fracture risk.
- "Lost when surprised" is applied to the active layer only, as R78-9 says; the passive base is never lost to surprise, only sagged by Starvation (R78-10).
- The coat's look follows The Color of Essence (hue by Family, clarity by Stage) and R78-6 (own colour, may glow).

### Canon conflicts hit

- None on this page. The Four Crafts calls the Bodily vector Spellcraft ("A fist with combustion on it is Spellcraft with the body as the pen ... the fist stops burning the moment attention lapses"). That stays true of a working laid on the fist; reinforcement under R78-9 is the separate passive-plus-active layer the R78-9 notes say replaces it ("the Bodily vector becomes active reinforcement over a passive stat base"). The Four Crafts page needs that note when it is next touched.

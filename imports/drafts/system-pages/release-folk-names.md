# Release Folk Names

The system says First Release, Second Release and Third Release. Nobody in the street does. Each people names a practitioner's release from its own life, the way it names the Stages, and the words below are the ones its commons, soldiers and households use. A First Release opens no earlier than Flourishing (Stage IV), a Second at Refraction (VII) and a Third at Invocation (IX), so most people have seen a First at a distance, a Second once in a lifetime, and a Third only in a story. The words carry that awe.

Each culture's block below starts at **Release words:** and is written to be pasted into that culture's Standing Inventory under that heading. Foreign words are never italic in prose (R50-32) and are romanised only (R50-31).

## Kharven

**Release words:**

The clans stopped riding when the ground froze through, and they still talk like riders. A release is the horse given its head.

| Release | Word | Literal meaning | Who says it |
|---|---|---|---|
| First | Letting the horse run | Giving a held horse its head after a long walk on a short rein. | Anyone; riders and the banner houses' men first. "He's let the horse run." |
| Second | The grass comes | The open steppe before the freeze, which "when the grass comes" still names. The ground round him turns his, as the steppe once was everyone's. | Elders, and the men who have seen one; said quietly, because the grass is gone. |
| Third | The Sky on the reins | The Sky takes the reins beside the rider. | Said low and rarely; the old, at the death-house, in the Waiting's stories. Never shouted. |
| Held past the limit | A horse you cannot feed | From the proverb "Don't saddle a horse you cannot feed": a release forced past its last tenth. | Veterans, of a man who broke himself; also a warning to a young one. |
| Released (adj.) | Loose-reined | Riding with the rein let out. | Riders, soldiers on the wall. |

Carried-name households (the Keld, the sealers) say **the name stands**: the dead person whose name the bearer carries rises up in him and fights. No native word is on record for it yet.

In a clansman's mouth: "He's let the horse run, Sky take it. Don't stand in front of a running horse, boy."

## Moto

**Release words:**

The house's one sound of a finished verdict is the guard settling into the scabbard mouth. A release is that sound run backwards: the blade coming out. These words are the house's and its retainers' for a Trait's release. The eye's own stages are the house's secret and are never called by these words.

| Release | Word | Literal meaning | Who says it |
|---|---|---|---|
| First | Koiguchi | Scabbard mouth (literally "carp's mouth"); from koiguchi o kiru, "to cut the scabbard mouth," thumbing the guard free so the blade can be drawn. | Retainers, weapons masters, the Kōkan; Kharven at the Seat say "he's cut the mouth." |
| Second | Nukimi | A drawn sword; a naked blade out of its scabbard. | The house and its retainers; a man in his Second is "nukimi" and the whole yard is in reach of the edge. |
| Third | Morote | Both hands; the second hand on the hilt is the current's. | Senior line members, in the old register; retainers repeat it without being sure what it means. |
| Released (adj.) | Nukimi no | Like a drawn blade; said of a person. | The house; "a nukimi no man" in a Common mouth. |

In a retainer's mouth: "His lordship has cut the koiguchi. Eyes on his hands, not his face, and do not stand where the blade goes."

## The Guild Accord and the Concord

**Release words:**

Everything the Accord owns is metered, sealed and entered, and a release is a Trait let off its meter. Each word has a common form and a chancery Latin form, and the speaker picks (R50-23).

| Release | Common | Chancery Latin | Literal meaning | Who says it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| First | Off the meter | Sine mensura | Without measure: running with no meter on it. | Common: anyone on a main. Latin: clerks, advocates, Division officers. |
| Second | A main of his own | In districtu suo | In his own district (districtus: the ground a jurisdiction covers). Everything in the yard runs on his draw. | Common: the street, wire-boys, market people. Latin: Measurewrights, the courts. |
| Third | Countersigned | Fonte teste | With the spring as witness: the Wellspring signs beside him, the way "coram testibus" means before witnesses. | Common: the educated and the pious. Latin: Measurewrights and Sancta Lux-leaning speakers, gravely. |
| Released (adj.) | Unmetered | Solutus | Loosed; in the chancery also "paid and discharged," as in the gate formula "solutum et transitum." | Common: anyone. Latin: any educated mouth, in a temper most of all. |

In a market woman's mouth: "Hear the hum go flat? Somebody up the street's gone off the meter. Get the cradle off the meter wall."

## The Dawi

**Release words:**

A Dawi hears the grade of a name before the word, and a release sounds the same way to them: the Trait said in the strong grade, with the consonant doubled. The tongue has no future tense, so nobody says what a release *will* do; they say what it is.

| Release | Word | Literal meaning | Who says it |
|---|---|---|---|
| First | The strong grade | The Trait spoken at its full grade, as an oath-name is. | Any Dawi; Common mouths in the Ring copy it. |
| Second | Worked ground | The floor round him turned to worked stone, his, and under the folk belief no working hurts a man on worked stone. | Miners, Forge Guards, shift crews. |
| Third | Tallut on | "The entry holds": the act entered by something older than any clerk, so nobody can unmake it. | Clerks and elders, in Mathematical Cadence; others say "entered." |
| Held past the limit | Nikkat ei on | "The angles are not true": the Tally's verdict turned over, for a release forced past its last tenth. | Rune-forgers, masters over apprentices, anyone at the wreck. |
| Released (adj.) | Doubled | Gone to the doubled consonant of the strong grade. | Any Dawi; "he is doubled." |

In a miner's mouth: "He is doubled. Feet on worked stone, lad, and stay behind me."

## Eresse

**Release words:**

The elves keep their voices for song, and to use the singing mechanism for speech is "loud." A release is the Trait taken out of speech and into song.

| Release | Word | Literal meaning | Who says it |
|---|---|---|---|
| First | In voice | Singing, where a moment ago there was speech. | Anyone, politely. |
| Second | A held chord | Every sound inside his ground is made to sound in his chord. | Jurists, rite-singers, the courts. |
| Third | Uren | Witnessed: the land itself stands for his claim, as a paid witness stands for a name. | Elders and the five branches' courts; said once, on a falling tone. |
| Held past the limit | Lo | Out of season: a release held after its time has run. | Anyone; the elven "damn it" said of a man. |
| Released (adj.) | Loud | Using the singing mechanism for speech. | Contempt: commons, the young, Varrisak of Eresseans. The polite form is "in voice." |

In a granary hand's mouth: "She's in voice. Stand outside the chord and let her sing herself hoarse."

## Korvaeth, the Sunroot Regency, the Vessel Road

**Release words:**

The Drow count. "Once is the form. Twice is emphasis. Three times is." A release climbs the same count, and because a thing said three times binds, the Third is the one nobody names aloud if they can help it.

| Release | Word | Literal meaning | Who says it |
|---|---|---|---|
| First | Once | The form: said once, as "witnessed" is said once. | Anyone; Reckoners, carters, ward-carriers. |
| Second | Twice | Emphasis: the claim said again, over the ground round him. | Anyone; with a glance at the door. |
| Third | The closing | The Elareth closing, three times said: it simply is, and binds. | Heads of house and Reckoners; commons say "the closing" so as not to count to three. |
| Held past the limit | Lo | Out of term: a debt gone past its day. | Reckoners first, then everyone. |
| Released (adj.) | In term | Still inside its limit, the way a debt is in term. | Reckoners, soldiers on a night attack; "lo" takes over when it runs out. |

In a carter's mouth: "Once. He's said it once, and that's only the form. If he says it twice, get off the road."

## The Keth-Gorrum (Undaar-Keth and the Expanse)

**Release words:**

Every acquisition is brakh or nakh, paid for or taken, and there is no third verb. A release is power paid for in full, so it is brakh; the forced kind that breaks a man is nakh. Gorr-Vaross shows weight by holding the vowel, and a bigger release takes the longer vowel.

| Release | Word | Literal meaning | Who says it |
|---|---|---|---|
| First | Brakh | Borne: paid for, so it holds. | Every Keth-Gorrum speaker, and Concord traders who learn it fast. |
| Second | Braakh | The same word with the vowel held, the length to be felt. (B) Bugbears say **staying**, the closed fist on a flat surface. | Soldiers and officers; Bugbears use their own word. |
| Third | Stone-cut | The ground keeps his law the way the Decree Stone keeps a decree: the stone remembers. | Said quietly and slowly, which in Vaross is the loudest way to say anything. |
| Held past the limit | Nakh | Taken without bearing the cost; "what you took will open under you." | Everyone; also an accusation. |
| Released (adj.) | Bearing | Carrying the load in public. | Soldiers, Bugbears of their own. |

In a Mandate soldier's mouth: "He's gone braakh. Monitor, do not engage."

## Ketsuen, the Crow's Reach

**Release words:**

The kingdom runs on ward-carving and counts the mallet's strokes. A release is a cut made on the world, and its practitioners are heard the way a warded house is: knock and listen for the hum.

| Release | Word | Literal meaning | Who says it |
|---|---|---|---|
| First | First stroke | The first blow of the mallet on a new ward. | Carvers, Academy students, the frontier. |
| Second | Posts up | He has set his posts: a ward-line round his ground, and everything inside it answers to him. | Anyone; frontier folk most. |
| Third | Third stroke | The last stroke, struck with the valley's own weight behind it. The old jibe "third stroke late" gains an edge: most never land it at all. | Carvers and the Proven; apprentices, wryly. |
| Released (adj.) | Humming | Humming like a warded wall. | Anyone; buyers knock before they bargain, and soldiers listen before they close. |

In a frontier fisher's mouth: "Hear him? He's humming. That's posts up. Stay outside the line till he runs dry."

In Sum-gol the Mahuo words hold (see below).

## Nalūn, the Chain-Cities

**Release words:**

Nalūn trades in claims, and its Wellspring enforces what is sealed. A release is a claim made on the world and paid out on the spot.

| Release | Word | Literal meaning | Who says it |
|---|---|---|---|
| First | Called | A note called in: what the Trait was owed, paid out at once. | Factors, agents, anyone with a token. |
| Second | A lien | A claim laid on the ground round him; everything in it answers to his note. | Factors and scribes; cutters say "a claim." |
| Third | Sealed | Sealed under the Chain Light: the Wellspring itself enforces it. | Everyone, with respect; Council members in full, "sealed under the Chain Light." |
| Held past the limit | Standing water | From "salt fails in standing water": a release held past its limit, rotting the ground it stands on. | Cutters first, then the whole city. |
| Released (adj.) | Called | As a note is called. | Anyone. |

In a cutter's mouth: "He's called his note. Off the floor, and nobody so much as lift a cup."

## Stannvaard, the Guild Seat

**Release words:**

On the rim and in the shafts a release is a stamp coming down and a vein opening under the pick. The Church compound and the Measurewrights keep the Accord's words; the crater keeps the Dawi ones.

| Release | Word | Literal meaning | Who says it |
|---|---|---|---|
| First | Struck | Struck as a stamp strikes a bar: the bright bite in dull metal. | Miners, smelter-town hands, the rim. |
| Second | Open seam | The old miners' wish made real: the ground round him opens to him like a vein under the pick. | Miners and the rim; "he's opened his seam." |
| Third | On the lode | Running on the lode itself: the current, the vein everything else is cut from. | Tunnel-masters and old tenders, half in awe. |
| Released (adj.) | Struck | As above. | Anyone on the rim. |

In a shift man's mouth: "He's struck. Down tools and get on the lift."

## The Yukari

**Release words:**

A Yukari carries up to five names, and which one is used says how close a person stands. A release shows more of the bearer, so the house names each one for the name it uncovers.

| Release | Word | Literal meaning | Who says it |
|---|---|---|---|
| First | Yobina | The calling-name, the one intimates use. | The house; the valley says "his calling-name." |
| Second | Imina | The true name, taboo to speak outside family, Council, a Proving or the stone. | The house, quietly; the valley says "his true name," and looks away. |
| Third | Okurina | The posthumous name, the deep name spoken at the Telling or at death. | Elders only. The road to a Third is a tribulation that not everyone survives. |
| Released (adj.) | Named | Showing a deeper name than the one he walked in with. | Valley folk and Ketsuen soldiers. |

In a valley woman's mouth: "Don't look at his eyes. He's given his true name, and you don't want it."

## The Zettari

**Release words:**

Time is counted in heats, and the obscenity is "cold." A release is the body brought to working heat. The Zettari never say "moto" for fire, since the word is a house's name in the north and nobody wants the confusion.

| Release | Word | Literal meaning | Who says it |
|---|---|---|---|
| First | Mwako | Blaze; the flare of something burning bright. Common: **at heat**. | Every forge and every street. |
| Second | Kalibu | The forge: the ground round him made his hearth. Common: **a lit forge**. | Smiths, soldiers, the Pillar houses. |
| Third | Agano | Covenant: a pact with something older than the speaker, the current itself. Common: **the covenant**. | The royal line and the old, solemnly; forge people link it to Agano Sand. |
| Released (adj.) | At heat | As iron at working heat. | Anyone. |

In a forge hand's mouth: "Mwako! Back from him, he's at heat. You cannot hurry a heat."

## The Mahuo (Sum-gol)

**Release words:**

The Mahuo have no Standing Inventory yet; this block sits with Ketsuen's Sum-gol entries until one is built. The valley counts breath before it counts coin, and a release is an untying, the same word as the old rites that untie a grief.

| Release | Word | Literal meaning | Who says it |
|---|---|---|---|
| First | Cheotjjae puri | The first untying. | The teaching house and the valley. |
| Second | Duljjae puri | The second untying. | The same; ox-drivers and rice-carters too. |
| Third | Setjjae puri | The third untying. | The elders, and rarely. |
| Released (adj.) | Pullin | Untied, loosened. | Anyone in Sum-gol. |

In a carter's mouth: "That's his duljjae puri. Keep your breath shallow and count."

## The Lineage Halls

**Release words:**

The halls have no Standing Inventory yet; this block waits for one. A hall's book is the house, and a release is the practitioner written onto the world: the brush comes down, the sheet fills, and the seal goes on.

| Release | Word | Literal meaning | Who says it |
|---|---|---|---|
| First | Luobi | Setting brush to paper; the first stroke of a piece. | Disciples, physicians, the hall's porters. |
| Second | Manzhi | A full sheet: the whole page written over. | Elders and disciples. |
| Third | Luokuan | Setting the inscription and seal on a finished work; the current's seal goes on beside the author's. | Elders of the Book, gravely. |
| Released (adj.) | In ink | Written onto the page. | Porters, servants and river men. |

In a porter's mouth: "The Elder's put brush to paper. Get the book off the trestles and get behind the chest."

## Calls made

1. **Mahuo and the lineage halls.** Neither has a file in `desktop/inventories/`. Both blocks are drafted anyway because the naming strata name them. The Mahuo block is meant to sit with Ketsuen's Sum-gol entries, and the halls' block waits for an Inventory.
2. **Kharven native word left null.** The carried-name households get the English "the name stands" (R23-11, carried-name logic). No native word was coined, because a coinage in the a i u and q sound pattern could land on a real Inuit word with another meaning. It is left for Isaac's ear.
3. **Dawi words built only from attested Dawi.** "Tallut on" follows the attested "Nikkat on" pattern (entry + closing copula). "Nikkat ei on" is the canon swear. Nothing is coined. The speech line avoids the future tense the tongue does not have.
4. **Moto.** The words are for a Trait's release, from the scabbard mouth the Inventory already carries. Under release-forms §2 the eye's own stages stay a house secret and do not use these words. Real Japanese, correct (R50-01): koiguchi (o kiru), nukimi, morote, nukimi no.
5. **Accord doublets** follow R50-23, with correct Latin (R50-12): sine mensura, in districtu suo, fonte teste (built on the attested "Deo teste" pattern), solutus (which ties to the attested "solutum et transitum"). Stannvaard's Church and Measurewrights use the Accord set, and its crater uses the Dawi set.
6. **"Held past the limit" rows** appear only where a culture already owns a word for overreach: Kharven proverb, Dawi swear, Eressean and Korvaeth "lo", Keth-Gorrum "nakh", Nalūn "standing water". Nothing was coined to fill a row.
7. **Keth-Gorrum "braakh"** uses the attested held-vowel emphasis (durrak, durraak). Vaross has no third length on record, so the Third is the English "stone-cut", tied to "the stone remembers".
8. **Korvaeth** builds the ladder on the attested proverb "Once is the form. Twice is emphasis. Three times is." It also uses the folk taboo on saying anything three times as the reason the Third gets a circumlocution.
9. **Zettari.** Real Swahili, correct (R50-01): mwako (blaze), kalibu (forge, furnace), agano (covenant). "Moto" (Swahili for fire) is avoided on purpose because it collides with the house name. "Agano" stretches the attested Agano Sand.
10. **Yukari** words are real Japanese name-terms (yobina, imina, okurina), matching the Yukari anchor (R20-1-YUKARI_ANCHOR, life-stage names).
11. **Mahuo** Korean is real and Revised-Romanised (cheotjjae, duljjae, setjjae puri; pullin). The halls' Chinese is real pinyin without tone marks, as the halls' style bank writes it (luobi, manzhi, luokuan). Following R50-31, no script appears anywhere.
12. **Ketsuen's "third stroke"** deliberately plays on the attested insult "third stroke late". The adjective "humming" comes from the folk belief that a warded house hums.
13. **Eresse** reuses the attested insult "loud" as the vulgar adjective for "released", with "in voice" as the polite form.
14. **Standing release (Stage XI).** None of these cultures has a folk word for it yet. Not drafted.
15. **New figures set here:** none.

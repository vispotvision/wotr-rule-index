# Training and Sects

*How a woken practitioner grows, who teaches them, how they are ranked, and what fame and contest do to them. For the whole system in brief, see How Magic Works.*

## What it is

A practitioner (a person whose Soul Crystal, the soul's organ of power, is awake) grows by cultivation: breath and drawing, hard fighting, and seclusion before each Threshold (the remaking of the Crystal one Stage, one step of sixteen, higher). Schools, houses and sects teach them, rank them, guard their secrets and hunt their deserters, while the guilds sneer at fighters and the world cheers the great ones.

## How it looks

**Cultivation, four ways.**

| Practice | What it does | Where |
|---|---|---|
| **Breath and drawing** | Long, slow breathing that pulls Aether (the world's medium of power) into the Crystal and turns it to Essence (the practitioner's own power). Trains the draw, the metering and the speed of recovery | At a Wellspring (a site where one of the world's great laws runs strong), or anywhere the Aether is dense: a guild yard over a main, a shrine, a mountain spring |
| **Sparring** | Drills, forms and practice bouts. Builds technique, timing and the read | The school's yard, every day |
| **Real fights** | Hunts, duels, wars. The only thing that raises Level (the count of how often the Crystal has densified), because only real consequence does | Wherever the danger is |
| **Seclusion** | Months alone before a Threshold, working the self toward the Catalyst (the particular experience a Stage demands), under guard | A sealed room, a cave, a cell under a school |

A seclusion is guarded because a practitioner deep in one is helpless, and because a Crystal near its melt is a prize to an enemy. It ends in a breakthrough, the full transformation the hall can see, or in **slag** (a remaking that set wrong and cost the soul ground), or it does not end at all.

**Each school its own basics.** There is no common curriculum. The Accord's Apprentice's Primer teaches the glyph grammar to the registered, but how to breathe, stand, draw, reinforce and strike is each school's own, and a fighter's first form tells a trained eye who taught them. A school's methods and forms are secrets worth stealing and worth killing over.

**The ranks.**

| Who | Ranks, low to high |
|---|---|
| **Schools, houses and sects** | Outer disciple, inner disciple, core disciple, elder. A sect's grandmaster over all |
| **Guild halls** | Apprentice, journeyman, master, by the seven-year term and the masterpiece |
| **Academies** | Scholar, Licentiate, Master, Doctor, Chair |
| **The Accord's public ladder** | The nine Tiers of Standing, Initiate to Paragon, read off Stage |

Disciples rise by **ranking contests**, set bouts held on a school's calendar, and by **the elders' word**, which can lift a disciple who lost or hold back one who won. A rank opens secrets (the next forms, the inner manuals), resources (Drafts, the bottled workings that feed growth; hours at the school's Wellspring; a better room) and standing. It also demands duty: missions, teaching the ranks below, fighting when the school is challenged, marrying where the house says. **A defector is hunted** for what they carry out in their head and their hands.

**Guilds sneer, the world cheers.** To a guild examiner a fighter who works by voice and body is a mouth, someone who could not afford to learn to cut. Everyone else disagrees. Crowds pack the bout-rings, armies follow named fighters, sects court them, and children play at their techniques in the street.

**Renown, by audience.** A name is known differently to the street, the guild halls, the schools and sects, the crowns and the Accord, and the underworld, and each keeps its own account. Renown opens doors and draws challengers in equal measure: every young fighter with a point to prove wants to beat the person everyone has heard of. **Epithets** are given by others, never chosen, and a fighter who names himself is laughed at.

**Warrants and bounties.** The Accord posts warrants: lawful, public, payable for an unlicensed high worker or a corrupted practitioner, brought in alive where the warrant says so. Sects, houses and guilds post bounties: private, for what someone stole, knew or did, with terms nobody prints.

**Tournaments.** Contests are fought in brackets by Stage, and a fighter may enter above their own Stage but never below. A fighter may **yield** at any time, by word or a hand flat on the ground. **Killing is forbidden**, and the killer forfeits and owes blood-price; it still happens, most often in the high brackets, where a full blow cannot be pulled in time. A fighter's **own power** is allowed: Traits (the soul's standing laws), techniques, releases (a Trait let loose in a learned form), vows (conditions sworn for power) and a bonded weapon. **Bought power** is banned: fight Drafts, purchased tags (prepared runed strips), hired arrays (standing runed workings). Prizes are resources (Drafts, a season at a Wellspring, rights to draw at a Core, a worked Wellspring site), places (an inner-disciple seat, an academy letter, a house commission) and fame.

## What it costs

- **Seclusion:** months out of the world, a guard who must be trusted and paid, and the risk of slag.
- **Entry:** fees, a seven-year indenture, or an oath to a house or crown sworn before the first lesson.
- **Rank:** duty, and secrets that bind. The higher a disciple climbs, the more they know, and the harder they are hunted if they leave.
- **Fame:** a stream of challengers, a price on the head in some ledgers, and an epithet that may not be kind.
- **Bouts:** wounds, the Drafts drunk to recover from them, and the occasional death the rules forbid.

## How it is beaten

- **Study the school.** A taught form is a documented form. A fighter who learned a rival school's basics can read its disciples before they finish their first step.
- **Find the secluded.** A practitioner in seclusion cannot fight. Get past the guard, or bribe him.
- **Steal the manual.** A school's power is its secrets; take them and the school is ordinary.
- **Make them choose.** A disciple bound by duty can be pressed through it: challenge the school in public and someone must answer.
- **Use the yield.** In a bout, a fighter losing badly may live to fight the next season. In a feud there is no yield, and a disciple raised on bouts sometimes forgets that.

## Example

**Wil Ashdown** wakes at nine and is taken as an outer disciple of a mountain school whose basics start with breath at a cold spring before dawn. For six years he sweeps, carries water and drills the school's first forms until the elders can read his stance from the gate. At sixteen he wins two ranking bouts and loses the third, and the elders lift him to inner disciple anyway: he lost to a core disciple, and he lost well.

Inner rank opens the school's second manual and its Wellspring hours, and sends him with three others to guard a salt convoy. A real fight on that road gives him more Level than a year of sparring, and a night holding a pass beside one of the others leaves a bond between them that carries weight: a Spirit Axis (the bond with another soul that Stage IV demands). He goes into seclusion for four months under a core disciple's guard and comes out Flourishing, the hall watching his colour settle. The next spring he enters the Stage IV bracket at the river games, wins a season's Drafts and a nickname he hates, and finds a challenger waiting at the school gate the morning after.

## Notes (not published)

### New figures set here

- **A fighter may enter above their own Stage but never below**: set here for "Stage brackets" (R78-10).
- **Yield by word or a flat hand on the ground; killer forfeits and owes blood-price**: set here.
- **"A sect's grandmaster over all"**: the head rank above the elders, set here; R78-7 and R78-10 name outer, inner, elders and ranking contests, and the brief adds core.
- **Seclusion "months"** is R78-10; **four months** in the example is the example's own.

### Calls made

- **Own power vs bought power** in tournaments (R78-10: "a fighter's own power is allowed and bought power banned"): own = Traits, techniques, releases, vows, a bonded weapon; bought = fight Drafts, purchased tags, hired arrays. A bonded weapon counts as own because it is grown, not bought.
- **Ranks.** Schools, houses and sects use disciple ranks; the guild halls keep apprentice, journeyman, master (R78-10: "schools and houses use disciple ranks while the guilds keep their own"). Academy ranks and the Accord's Tiers of Standing are carried from *The Gate: Who May Learn Magic* and *The Sixteen Stages* so the four ladders sit side by side.
- **Real fights are what raise Level** follows *The Trait System*, I ("Instruction can tell a practitioner what to do. It cannot supply the strain. Only consequence supplies the strain") and *Tier Grade* (Level as "contact with consequence"). Breath and sparring are written as training the draw, recovery, technique and read, not Level.
- **Each school its own basics** (R78-9, "basics are taught by each school, not universally") is squared with the Gate page's Apprentice's Primer by letting the Primer teach the glyph grammar only.
- **Renown audiences** (street, guild halls, schools and sects, crowns and the Accord, underworld): set here as the default columns for R78-10's "renown is tracked per audience".
- **Warrants vs bounties** follows R78-10 ("the Accord posts warrants and sects, houses and guilds post bounties"); the Gate page's guild Extermination Commission and hall boards are the guild bounty in practice.

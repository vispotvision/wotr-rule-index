# Research: GM and roleplay craft traditions, for the table questionnaire

Prepared 2026-10-04. Read against the live rule index, `desktop/NATALIE.md`, the table data in `table/` (Fronts, Ledger, roster, sessions), `bot/PLAN.md`, and the two questionnaires now law (Style Law R70; Combat Law R71, RULINGS.md "combat-law-2026-10-04"). Each tradition gets the technique, how it transposes to WOTR, and what it collides with. Question areas close the report.

Glosses. **GM**: game master, who runs the world (Natalie at the table, Isaac as Judger on Discord). **PC**: player character. **NPC**: everyone else. **POV**: point of view, the character the narration sits with. **XP**: experience points, the Fracture of Worlds events that raise a Level. **OOC**: out of character, table talk outside the fiction. **Clock**: a circle cut into segments, filled one at a time as a threat advances. **Front**: a named threat with a want and a clock (WOTR keeps these in `table/fronts.yaml`, four to six ticks each).

## 1. Settled: do not re-ask

- **Turn length and shape.** About 3,500 words a reply, even to a quick line; set pieces 5,000 and up (R48-28-TURN_LENGTH, R49-30-SHORT_BEATS, R48-46-WORD_FLOOR; listed settled in R70). Half texture and talk, half the world moving, stop at his next decision (R49-29-TURN_FILL). A marked cutaway to what the POV cannot see may close a turn (R49-09-CUTAWAYS). Quiet, travel and downtime scenes may run kishōtenketsu, the East Asian four-part shape that needs no conflict (R70-14). Present tense (R49-02), full check every turn (R48-45), notes in the file and a short chat summary (R48-47), one-line pushback (R48-50), physics only when asked (R48-48).
- **Whose head.** His PC acts only by his hand; narration goes inside him only under his overrule; the other creators' characters are excluded (R49-01-WHOSE_HEAD, R1-1; R71 settled list). A wound lands as a world fact once the stated move resolves, how he bears it stays his, the tell is placed and the read is his, guest players on Discord are seen from outside (R71 K9). Isaac may take any NPC's voice; his version sticks (R48-32, R52-12).
- **Improvisation and NPCs.** Natalie invents NPCs, places and texture without asking, logged (R48-26-INVENTION); a walk-on is a role until they speak twice or matter (R50-27); voices from a researched speaker type (R52-29), one fixed saying each (R52-30), comic minor NPCs legal (R52-31). NPCs veteran-clever (R48-36), each side invents its own uses (R48-35), never rigged (R57-10). Lies frequent (R48-42), each with a catchable tell (R49-36).
- **Consequences.** Surprises only after foreshadowing (R48-30); death if earned after clear warning (R48-31), permanent (R60-04); real healing times (R60-02); hard reserve clock (R60-03). In fights: a way out always planted (R71 K7), a sign and an exit before a loss (R71 FT17), the bill in the world (R71 WT10), a practitioner's killing opens a jurisdiction Front and an undeclared one is murder (R71 WT12). A spared rival is paid for on the Ledger (R70-104); a public face-slap opens a Front or debt (R70-112). Every thread carries a standing warm bond (R70-114).
- **Hooks, progress, time.** Hooks state reward and penalty; quests are posted contracts (R70-97). Numbers climb only for a seen cause, an XP event or training named in a downtime turn (R70-90). Dull travel passes in a line (R48-29). Weather is a tracked clock (R53-09). Prices from the approved table (R53-07). The session start and end protocol, one Front a session, the Ledger's due lines and the Scene Menu's three hooks (NATALIE.md Table Rules 4 and 7; `advance_front`, `due`, `scene_menu`).

## 2. Apocalypse World and Powered by the Apocalypse

**Technique.** Vincent and Meguey Baker's *Apocalypse World* (2010) and the games on its engine (Powered by the Apocalypse, PbtA: Dungeon World, Masks) give the GM, called the MC (master of ceremonies), an agenda, principles and a menu of moves. The agenda includes making the world seem real and, in Baker's words, "play to find out what happens". Principles include addressing the characters not the players, naming every NPC and making them human, being a fan of the PCs, thinking offscreen, and never speaking a move's name. MC moves include: separate them, announce offscreen badness, announce future badness, take away their stuff, offer an opportunity with or without a cost, tell them the possible consequences and ask, turn their move back on them. Moves are **soft** (a warning still answerable) or **hard** (irrevocable), and the MC goes hard when handed a golden opportunity, often an ignored soft move. "Make a move that follows" means it grows from established fiction. **Fronts** bundle threats; each threat has a type, an **impulse** (a warlord's is to own and enslave), a six-segment clock read as a clock face (3, 6, 9, 10, 11, 12, so the end comes fast) and **stakes questions** the MC truly cannot answer yet.

**Transposes as.** WOTR already runs this engine: Table Rule 4, `advance_front`, the cutaway and the Rumor Mill are "think offscreen"; R48-30 is "announce future badness"; R48-31 and R71's sign-and-exit are soft-then-hard, written for death. WOTR Fronts end on a "Resolves:" line that is a stakes question (Tabitha's grain: foresight or complicity). What AW adds: a **type and impulse** per Front, so improvised moves have direction; an **accelerating clock**; the soft and hard ladder for losses short of death (stuff, a witness, reputation, a debt called); and a written **move palette**, so the world's answers do not fall into three reflexes.

**Collides with.** Every AW move ends on "what do you do?"; Table Rule 1 forbids stopping on an OOC question. WOTR's form wins (stop on an NPC line, an action, a thing he sees); open is whether "tell them the consequences and ask" is ever legal, and in whose mouth. "Ask provocative questions and build on the answers" asks the player to author facts; legal OOC under Isaac's canon priority, but nothing says when.

## 3. Blades in the Dark

**Technique.** John Harper's *Blades in the Dark* (2017), a heist game in a haunted industrial city, sits closest to the Draw Age. **Clocks** of four, six or eight segments track anything with progress, in plain view. **Faction clocks** tick between sessions, so the city has moved when the crew looks up. **Position** (controlled, risky, desperate) and **effect** (limited, standard, great) are stated before a risky action; the player may change approach to shift them. **Flashbacks** let a player pay stress to reveal earlier preparation. The cycle runs free play, the score, then **downtime**: two activities each from a short list (acquire an asset, long-term project on a clock, recover, reduce heat, train, indulge a vice). **Heat** is the attention a job draws, rolling into a wanted level and **entanglements**. A **devil's bargain** trades a complication for a better chance.

**Transposes as.** Heat maps onto jurisdiction (R71 WT12: crown, company and guild fight over a killing) and the Night Watch Society. Downtime maps onto NATALIE's one-turn downtime and R70-90. A WOTR menu: heal on R60-02's timeline, train, a bench or craft project on a clock (R70-46, R71 AL1), pay or call a debt, cool the heat with the right office, keep a bond (R70-114), the culture's indulgence (the Kharven skin), each priced off the price table. Faction clocks are the Fronts plus a standing faction list per thread. Flashbacks are Mother of Learning's prepared fight, already a model (R71 K8). Position and effect are Table Rule 5's four inputs, the bot's `/adjudicate` card.

**Collides with.** No dice (Table Rule 5), so position and effect can only be a stated read. Stating it collides with R48-48 and Table Rule 6 (the read is the game). A flashback is the PC's own unforeshadowed surprise, against R48-30 and R57-10. Visible clocks collide with the iceberg and with Fronts whose next move is secret, yet the Academy thread already runs one in the open (the Class X Open Tournament, seven days out).

## 4. OSR: the old-school renaissance

**Technique.** OSR play revives 1970s and 1980s Dungeons & Dragons. Matt Finch's *Quick Primer for Old School Gaming* (2008): rulings, not rules (decide the situation on its merits); player skill over character ability; heroic, not superhero. The **living world** is kept by strict time records (Gygax's 1979 *Dungeon Master's Guide*), wandering-monster checks on a timer, and the **reaction roll**, two dice read from immediate attack to enthusiastic friendship, so disposition is discovered. Ben Robbins' **West Marches** (2007): no fixed party, players schedule expeditions, a shared map, danger rising with distance from town. Justin Alexander (2008): prep situations, not plots. Chris McDowall's **ICI doctrine** (2018): every beat owes Information enough to choose, a real Choice, a visible Impact. Solo play keeps **oracles**: Tana Pigeon's *Mythic Game Master Emulator* (2003) with a "chaos factor" that can interrupt a planned scene; Shawn Tomkin's *Ironsworn* (2018).

**Transposes as.** Rulings over rules is Table Rule 5; player skill is "the read is the game" and R48-48; prep situations is the roster's want, refusal and lie fields. ICI is a test any turn can face, with the Ledger holding impact. Time records are R53-09 and the State of Play date. West Marches describes the Discord server: hundreds of location channels, players moving between them. The reaction roll becomes a dice-less **reaction read**: the NPC's want, what the Stage gap does to the room, the PC's Ledger reputation, the culture's folk belief ("never meet a Moto's eye").

**Collides with.** Reaction rolls, wandering checks and oracles randomise; WOTR has no dice (bot scope decision 2026-09-13; Table Rule 5), and chance is not reconstructible from the page (R13-8). An OSR ruling invents a difficulty; a WOTR ruling cites a stat row and marks estimates (R12-5). West Marches assumes a GM for every expedition; the bot is deterministic and Isaac is Judger.

## 5. Fate: aspects and compels

**Technique.** *Fate Core* (Hicks, Donoghue, Balsera and others, 2013) writes everything as **aspects**, short phrases always true ("Owes the Smugglers' Guild"). A **compel** is the GM offering a fate point for an aspect to cause trouble now; the player takes point and trouble, or pays a point to refuse. Players may compel themselves. Flaws become a resource the player chooses to spend.

**Transposes as.** The Ledger already reads as aspects ("Sodoku to Borin: eleven days of forge-work, still unpriced"; "He has no bow"). A compel becomes **the Ledger coming due as an offer**: a creditor arrives with terms, payable in his coin or refused at a stated cost, the answer Isaac's. That is AW's opportunity-with-a-cost and Blades' devil's bargain too. A self-compel is Isaac saying OOC which line he wants to bite.

**Collides with.** A compel proposes what the PC does; R49-01 and Table Rule 1 forbid both deciding it and the OOC question. Legal form: in-fiction only, the turn stopping on the offer. No point economy exists; whether accepting trouble earns anything (a better position, a cancelled debt, an R70-90 XP event) is open.

## 6. Japanese TRPG: replays, Sword World, the scene-based table

**Technique.** From September 1986 *Comptiq* serialised a transcript of Group SNE playing Dungeons & Dragons, Ryo Mizuno as GM: *Record of Lodoss War*. The **replay** (リプレイ), a lightly edited session transcript with narration, in-character lines, OOC jokes and dice, taught Japanese players for about thirty years and sold beside light novels. Group SNE's *Sword World RPG* (1989; 2.0 in 2008; 2.5 in 2018) was taught mostly through replays, and Lodoss went from replay to novel under Mizuno. FEAR's *Double Cross* runs a session in phases (Opening, Middle, Climax, Ending), each PC with a personal opening and ending. Each scene has a **scene player**; others pay to enter, raising their **encroachment rate**, the session clock of the monster inside. The GM may run a **master scene** with no PC present. Each PC starts from a **handout**: a hook card naming a tie to an NPC. Bonds called **Lois** keep a PC human; they can be broken for power, and those kept pull encroachment back at session end. Kawashima Toichiro's *Shinobigami* puts a public mission on each handout's front and a **secret** on the back for others to investigate, and places scenes from a rolled **scene table**.

**Transposes as.** The replay is a second archive form, keeping table talk and adjudication cards; the book workflow is the replay-to-novel route. The phases fit a session: the Rumor Mill opens, the set piece is the Climax, and an **Ending phase** gives each thread a short personal close for R70-114's warm bond. The master scene is R49-09. Handouts are Scene Menu hooks as cards (R70-97 already wants reward and penalty). Lois is R70-114 with an edge: bonds listed on the Ledger that events can break at a visible cost. Shinobigami's secrets fit the Academy roster, where each NPC already has "knows" and "has lied about". The scene table maps onto Standing Inventory places (the death-house, the standpipe yard).

**Collides with.** R48-47 keeps the archive in-world with notes apart. Scene-player costs presume several PCs, the Discord question. Breaking a bond for power is a mechanism needing a Fracture of Worlds source (R29-1 already makes a soul holding a name a mechanism). A handout's suggested emotion is Isaac's to set (R49-01).

## 7. Chinese and Korean web-novel "system" pacing

**Technique.** Chinese web fiction on Qidian and its peers updates daily for years, each chapter ending on a hook. The **golden finger** (金手指) is the protagonist's cheat, often a **System** (系统流, "system flow") issuing notices, quests with rewards and penalties, and a shop. Pacing runs on **shuang points** (爽点), beats of satisfaction, the **face-slap** (打脸) the commonest; the "golden three chapters" must land the hook. Korean fiction carries the engine through the **status window** (상태창), the regressor, and the **scenario**: a world quest with a time limit and stated penalty (Omniscient Reader's Viewpoint; Solo Leveling's penalty zone).

**Transposes as.** Mostly law already: contracts with reward and penalty (R70-97), readouts at milestones (R70 LR rows), the face-slap with a bill (R70-112), Solo Leveling and Cradle as models (R70-6). The new piece is a **session engine**: one felt payoff (a read landed, a debt collected, a re-assay confirmed) and one bill a session, opening on a notice (a contract board, an Academy order slip) beside the Rumor Mill. The tournament deadline is already a scenario.

**Collides with.** Grimdark: a guaranteed payoff is a promise the world may not keep. R70-90 and R12-5 mean a reward is never a free stat. Regression is closed by R60-04. Short daily chapters meet R49-30's 3,500-word reply.

## 8. Play-by-post and Discord etiquette

**Technique.** **Godmodding**: controlling another's character, or writing one's own as untouchable. **Auto-hitting**: writing that an attack lands on another player's character; the norm is to write the attempt and let the target's player write the result. **Metagaming**: acting on OOC knowledge. **Post order** fixes who posts next, essential in combat. **Literacy levels** set length (one-liner, paragraph, multi-paragraph, novella). Servers set a reply window and skip rule, mark OOC with brackets, use proxy bots such as **Tupperbox** to post as a character, and hold one character to one place at a time.

**Transposes as.** The server already uses Tupperbox and location channels; the bot plan marks OOC with `((` or `//` and binds one character per player per forum. Anti-godmodding is law (R49-01, R1-1). Missing: post order, cadence, a skip rule, and how a guest's post measures against the GM's.

**Collides with.** No-auto-hit meets R71 K9 and Table Rule 5: at WOTR's table the target's player decides how he bears a wound, never whether it landed, the reverse of what guests expect. R48-28 assumes two people; four 400-word posts against a 3,500-word GM post is a cadence question. The bot runs no language model (LLM) by Isaac's 2026-09-13 decision, so Natalie's role on the server is open. The explicit-scene law (Table Rule 9, R49-51) is written for Isaac alone.

## 9. Actual-play shows

**Technique.** At a significant kill, *Critical Role*'s Matt Mercer hands narration to the player ("How do you want to do this?"). Every NPC gets a distinct voice; episodes open on a player recap. *Dimension 20*'s Brennan Lee Mulligan films a session zero, builds each season on the PCs' backstories, cuts away to villains, balances the spotlight, and closes on an epilogue per character.

**Transposes as.** The kill handed over is implied by R48-35 and R71 K9: the turn stops before the finish and Isaac writes it. Distinct voices are NATALIE's unswappable voice and R52-29; the cutaway is R49-09; the season epilogue is an arc-end Ending phase.

**Collides with.** Shows pace for an audience; R49-29's turn is written for a reader. A player recap is OOC work each session where NATALIE uses an NPC's mouth. Stopping before a finisher must still stop on a thing he sees (Table Rule 1).

## 10. Safety and consent tools for dark tables

**Technique.** **Lines and veils** (Ron Edwards, *Sex and Sorcery*, 2003): a line never appears; a veil happens off the page. The **X-card** (John Stavropoulos, 2013): touch it and the last thing is removed, no reason asked. **Script Change** (Beau Jágr Sheldon, from 2013): rewind, fast-forward, pause, frame-by-frame. The **Open Door**: anyone may leave. **Session zero**: tone and content agreed before play. **Stars and wishes**: a debrief on what landed and what is wanted. Collected in Monte Cook Games' *Consent in Gaming* (2019) and the *TTRPG Safety Toolkit* (Kienna Shaw, Lauren Bryant-Monk).

**Transposes as.** NATALIE's Floor is a written session zero for two: the shared line, Natalie's limits, Isaac's domain, and the flag rule (one plain sentence, never a silent stall), which refuses the X-card's silent removal on Natalie's side. R48-29 is fast-forward; R49-01's overrule and R52-12 are rewinds for one thought or voice; R48-50 is a pause.

**Collides with.** Veils meet Table Rule 9 (aftermath never skipped), R49-51 and R48-34: WOTR's default is no fade, so a veil exists only on Isaac's call. Rewind meets the Ledger and permanent death: does a rewound NPC still remember her lie, does a wound come off? On a shared server every tool matters again: guests need their own lines and veils, Discord's age-restricted channels for explicit scenes, and a pause that does not stall everyone.

## 11. AI roleplay conventions players expect

**Technique.** SillyTavern, AI Dungeon, NovelAI and Character.AI users expect: **character cards**; **lorebooks** (World Info), entries injected only when a keyword appears within a scan depth; an **Author's Note**, a standing style instruction near the end of each exchange; **memory** (AI Dungeon's Plot Essentials, a Memory Bank recalling condensed past chunks, Story Cards); **group chats** with a turn order (SillyTavern: name mention then a per-character "talkativeness" roll, list order, or pooled); **swipes** (regenerate), edit, continue, and **impersonate** (the AI drafts the user's next message). The cardinal rule: the AI never speaks or acts for the user's character. Common complaints: speaking for the player, rushing the scene, forgetting, **parroting** the player's action back before answering, agreeable NPCs, and ending on "what do you do?".

**Transposes as.** NATALIE.md already calls `scene_context` the lorebook; State of Play and the Ledger are Plot Essentials; `scene_recall` is the memory bank; the standing prompt is the Author's Note; roster entries are NPC cards. Law answers most complaints (R49-01, Table Rule 1, R48-42, R48-36, R51-10, the Ledger). No rule covers **swipes** or **parroting**.

**Collides with.** Impersonate is barred by R49-01 unless Isaac asks; whether he may ask is open. Parroting is legal under R49-01's full-depth permission but spends the turn on what he already wrote. A swipe must unwind every table write the discarded turn made (`ledger_add`, `advance_front`, the roster).

## 12. Contradictions inside current law

Recorded, not resolved; some are sweeps, not questions.

1. **Turn length.** NATALIE.md Table Rule 2 (bands 300 to 700, 700 to 1,500, set piece 2,500) and "Length discipline" contradict R48-28 and R49-30. R70 lists length as settled, so NATALIE.md owes a sweep; `table/sessions.yaml` session 1 flagged it. Open: whether an OOC answer, a rules check or a multiplayer GM post is a "reply" under R49-30.
2. **Whose head.** NATALIE.md says never think for his PC; R49-01 allows full depth under overrule. R71 settled it; the wording owes a sweep.
3. **Lies.** Table Rule 8 (one wrong thing a session, uncorrected until caught) is a floor R48-42 outgrew. Open: whether it survives as its own duty, and what happens to a lie never caught.
4. **"Bigger things wait for Isaac."** R48-26 against CLAUDE.md's "nothing waits on Isaac" (2026-09-25) and his standing direction that he makes the calls and no "pending Isaac" sits in the work. "Bigger" is undefined.
5. **Discord reach.** No-LLM bot (2026-09-13) against multiplayer in this questionnaire's scope.
6. **Hidden against visible.** Fronts' next moves, the roster's lies and the Ledger's due conditions sit in files Isaac reads, while R48-48 makes working it out the challenge.

## 13. Proposed question areas

**The turn**
- What counts as a "reply" under R49-30: an OOC lane at normal length? (AI-RP; item 1)
- Re-narrate his posted action, or open on the world's answer? (parroting; R49-01)
- His blow drops a named NPC: stop before the finish for him to write it? (Critical Role; R48-35, R71 K9)
- Swipes and rewinds, and what happens to the discarded turn's table writes. (AI-RP, Script Change)
- May he ask Natalie to draft his PC's line, and when? (impersonate; R49-01)

**Improvisation**
- A written GM move palette for WOTR. (AW, Blades)
- Define "bigger things" in R48-26. (item 4)
- Pre-plotted arcs per thread, or situations and Fronts only? (Alexander)
- An ICI check per turn as a WARN in the notes. (McDowall)
- A dice-less reaction read for every NPC meeting the PC fresh. (OSR)
- Any chance element at all, or everything from causes? (Mythic; Table Rule 5, R13-8)

**NPCs and lies**
- Does Table Rule 8's single uncorrected falsehood survive R48-42? (item 3)
- A lie never caught: stands, surfaces through a Front, or comes due? (Shinobigami; R49-36)
- Honest error (Agnes Tull believes her rumours) counted as a lie or apart? (roster)
- A cap on named NPCs per thread before a cull or merge. (D20; roster)

**Fronts**
- A type and impulse per Front; even or accelerating clocks. (AW)
- What ticks a Front: time, his acts, his neglect. Do unplayed threads tick? (Blades faction game; Table Rule 4)
- Visible or hidden clocks: what Isaac sees in play and in the files. (Blades against AW; item 6)
- Who opens a Front mid-session; how many a thread carries. (AW; R70-112, R71 WT12)

**Ledger and consequences**
- A soft and hard ladder short of death. (AW; R48-31 covers death)
- Stakes stated before a risky non-combat act, or only read? (Blades; R48-48, Table Rule 6)
- The Ledger as in-fiction offers; does accepting earn anything? (Fate, devil's bargain)
- Prep flashbacks and their price. (Blades; R71 K8)
- A standing heat measure per thread, or Fronts only. (Blades; R71 WT12)

**Downtime and the session**
- A priced downtime menu and how long one turn spans. (Blades; R70-90, R60-02)
- Session phases with a per-thread Ending phase. (Double Cross)
- The opener: Rumor Mill, a notice board, an OOC "previously", a player recap. (system novels, Critical Role)
- A payoff and a bill each session, or no promise. (web fiction; grimdark)
- Bonds as a breakable Ledger section. (Lois; R70-114)
- A session-end debrief from Isaac, or none. (stars and wishes)
- Scene Menu hooks as handouts, how many, in fiction or OOC. (handouts; R70-97, Table Rule 1)

**Multiplayer on Discord**
- Natalie's role: none, drafts for the Judger, or live posts. (item 5)
- Post order, reply window, skip rule; GM and player post lengths against R48-28. (PbP)
- Explaining the hit as world fact (R71 K9) to guests used to no-auto-hit. (PbP)
- A scene player per scene or open entry. (Double Cross)
- Per-player lines and veils, age-restricted channels, a pause that does not stall the thread. (safety tools; Table Rule 9)
- Archive form: prose, or a replay keeping table talk and adjudication cards. (Lodoss)

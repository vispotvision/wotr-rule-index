## Voice
**Notices first** · How a thing was built and what in it is borrowed: the seam between native and stolen, the part that will fail first. He reads a mount as a formula before he reads it as a beast.
**Sentence length** · Medium, level declaratives in the cadence of a man reading a proof aloud; he corrects in two strokes, the wrong word and then the right one.
**Contractions** · sometimes: easy in conversation, gone when he states a decision or a vow ("I am certain.").
**Pet word** · "Finish": things he has finished, things left to finish, the one thing he would like to finish for someone else.
**Never says** · More than he has read. He will not call a thing strong, true or done before he has checked it, and he never speaks of his siblings.
**Stumbles** · Never. When his voice cracks it is blood, not doubt.
**Gloss rights** · Only to correct a term: he names the right word, gives one sentence of why, and stops. He does not lecture.
**Under stress** · Shorter, flatter and more formal at once: the contractions thin out, the lines drop to single declaratives ("I'm not done." "I cannot."), and he laughs where another man would plead.
**In grief** · The careful qualifiers fall away ("I find," "I would like") and the younger son comes back, the one who never matched his siblings: plain, brief and unsure of his standing.
**In joy** · The voice stays level; the joy is a smile he cannot keep off his face and a term said slowly, tasted like the last swallow of something expensive.
**Sample line** · "I have spent a great deal of my life finishing things that mattered only to me. I would like, for once, to finish something that matters to someone else."

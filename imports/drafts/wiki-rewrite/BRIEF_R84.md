## The law in one place

- **Fate.** The Plane of Fate is the control of Attraction Force and Obsession Force and the workings of reality itself: manipulating probabilities to work outside what reality already knows, to control the fate or predestiny of something. It can attach to oaths and is more tied to blessings. Fate covers both Attraction and Obsession. All spirits and all physical things have fates. It no longer "records what has happened". Fate Arts are rare, mostly by bloodline, and considered incredibly overpowered; Hataraki can take back or remove a blessing.
- **Blessings.** A blessing grants a power out of a person's natural reach, even in Level. It need not go through Fate, but most do. Higher spirits, Archons, Epochs and people of higher Temperance give them, because they are spiritually stronger, and a great giver spends almost nothing. It shows on the sheet and can lie, because the giver wrote the line. A curse is often a blessing with terms you did not read.
- **Soul Crystal.** It is literally the soul. Tempering melts it down and rebuilds each layer; refines Essence and makes it stronger; and the body gains Traits.
- **Wellsprings.** Lead with this: a Wellspring represents an alchemical and a spiritual transformation and concept. It can be seen as a source of power to pull from, and it is more the governing idea that transforms how a soul uses Aether: a lens on magic already potent. It can highlight and even double a person's natural Essence Typology, starting small and reaching double only with years of mastery. It is got at a Core in a dungeon, from a school, from a Draft or blessing, or by birth. The physics and every number stay, as how scholars explain it.
- **Traits.** They stay exactly as on file (the Four Reflections and their laws), and they can be leveled and refined like skills: they have levels and grow with overall Level and skill. Do not invent new Trait kinds.
- **Catalyst.** Retired: "A Draft has never supplied a Catalyst" is gone wherever it stands, along with anything resting on it. Drafts and blessings can supply a Catalyst, Stages can be bought, the wealthy are stronger because they are wealthy, and the great houses hoard the top Drafts. The poor rise by dungeon luck, guild patronage and blessings. A bought Stage has no Release until it is found and mastered by fighting. Sects teach what money cannot buy.
- **Monster cores.** A monster's core is in a way its Soul Crystal, worth more the stronger the monster. It is the lawful Crystal Draw of The Standing Index. An alchemist renders it into Essence Drafts used for tempering, with Creature-Sourced potency and Identity Contamination. Swallowed raw it gives everything at once, and most die or come out wrong. A Crystal cut from a dead practitioner makes the strongest Draft of all, the worst crime in every realm and the most profitable, and it is why Class IV has never balanced. Monsters level by killing, evolve, share the Level and Grade scale, are graded T1 to T9, and a famous one earns a name, an epithet and a bounty. Monster parts make gear.
- **Windows.** The soul reporting its numbers to its owner, unseen by others. On the page as text blocks like `[Reserve: 41%]`, showing Grades, percentages, bars and levels, with EU, AU/s and η in the notes. Reference pages may show a sample window.
- **Sheet and skills.** Eight Primaries with Grades, a Sub-Stat when used or when it moves. Life spends most stat points, and a few are placed at each Stage. Race shows on the sheet. Skills rise with use and show as Grades. Techniques show no level and take a bigger name when they jump. Level moves by danger overcome.
- **Aether Class.** At thresholds the soul is offered a named class fitting how the person has lived. It gives abilities, shapes growth and evolves at each tier by how it is lived. The ladder on file (Ø Dormant, I Muridic, II Harmonic, III Resonant, IV Luminous, V Radiant, VI Voidic, Ω Absolute) stays as its tier, and all η law and figures stand. The one wild concept stays the person's own.
- **Guild Accord.** A coalition of many kinds of guild across many nations: adventurers' guilds, merchant guilds, craft guilds, alchemists, healers, couriers and more. Halls sign on for shared rank, enforceable contracts and passage. Crowns tolerate and fear it. Keep its Codex, Lattice, law and history where they fit a coalition, and cut the civil-service feel. Its heartland is the Sealed Charter of the Concord (KINGDOMS.md).
- **Titles.** Initiate, Apprentice, Journeyman, Adept, Expert, Master, Grandmaster, Archmaster, Paragon. The same nine in every guild, grading trust in that trade: a merchant may deal up to it, a craftsman make up to it, an adventurer take commissions up to the same Threat Tier. Fighting guilds keep the Stage table as their yardstick, so a Grandmaster merchant may not be able to throw a punch. A forged token is a great crime.
- **Items.** Two ratings: Grade for power, on the fighters' letters, and Rarity for scarcity (Common, Uncommon, Rare, Very Rare, Legendary, Mythic, Unique). The old words (Marked, Proofed, Tempered, Instrument, Hallowed, Storied, Found, Numinous, Proscribed) survive as the Accord's stamps. A smith's title caps the Grade she may make, and a merchant's caps the Rarity he may sell. Bonded gear grows; other gear does not. An Appraisal skill and an assay instrument read an item, and a good forger beats a weak appraiser.
- **Master Works.** A piece that does what its Grade and material should not be able to, made once at the end of a lifetime, always Unique or Mythic, and named. Each area has its own. A Fate Essence Draft is one: extremely hard and very coveted.
- **Crafts.** Spellcraft, Runecraft, Draftcraft, Chantcraft and Magicraft are removed as named categories. Drafts, runes, tags and chants stay as skills and trades anyone can level. Move the useful facts and do not leave the words behind. Old ranks of the crafts become guild feuds at most.
- **Dungeons.** They grow down in floors around a Core, with something guarding the heart. Alive and learning; scholars argue whether it thinks. A worked Core is a dungeon someone cleared and now holds. Cores are mostly in dungeons and are said to lie in vast territories too. See `imports/drafts/wiki-rewrite/inner/wiki/The Bestiary/Dungeons.md`.
- **Monsters everywhere.** They move and live across the whole Inner World; beasts more widely still. The Scourge of Hell is the one area known for extremely dangerous ones.
- **Aura and Pressure.** Family sets the base color, a Wellspring adds texture and sound, a class or big Trait adds a shape. Pressure is felt in the room first, and then the window reports it. A stronger Gnosis Perception reads a weaker Resilience Ward; anything else is an estimate said aloud, and people can hide or fake it.
- **Old lore, remove it:** the Fall (Muken off the chair after sixteen years, the oaths lapsing, the Greymanes alone three years, Sodoku's exile). **Dawi** is the old name of the Marrowkin: say Marrowkin, with Dawi as what older people and records say. Nalūn, Ketsuen, Stannvaard and Anguz are gone as states.

## How to change a page

Keep the page's voice, its opening story and its depth. Change what the law changes, and rewrite whole sections where the idea has moved rather than patching a sentence. Every law, table and figure the new law leaves standing survives. Reference page voice as before: in-world archivist or Measurewright, with dry asides. Measurewrights may argue. NO onomatopoeia on any wiki page. American spelling, no em dashes in new prose, no "it's not X, it's Y", no hypophora, bright and loud, never gray. Explain new terms on first use. THE TEST holds: nothing machine-age in costume. No main-cast secrets or arc plot on public pages, and other creators' material stays hands-off. Anything you invent, report it. Where a canon source disagrees, follow the law and report it.

It is Synergia -- several Soul Crystals briefly sharing one Essence Core, Shell and Layer -- made permanent by Concordia instead of left momentary, ordinarily sealed through a Ritus such as the Rite of Hollow Union rather than held open by anyone's ongoing concentration. Formation requires that the donors already carry an Attraction-recognized bond strong enough for the Continuum to read them as one Attraction signature; kinship, oath, or shared catastrophe qualify, strangers do not.
The three layers do not fuse identically. The Essence Cores never fuse: each donor keeps a discrete Core -- identity, memory and Traits remain their own -- and only one donor's Core occupies the frame's single point of outward control at a time, with the hand-off between donors itself a Continuum-recognized cost that degrades Dexterity and Reflex-family Sub-Stats independent of the frame's build. The Aether Shell fuses and is singular without exception: one frame produces exactly one Aether Class, one eta, and one Coherence Band, however many donors it carries, because a body has only one nervous system to conduct through -- a Concordant Crystal does not multiply a Shell's ceiling by donor count, it grants N donors the throughput of one Shell. The Attraction Layer alone is genuinely plural: it carries one discrete Wellspring harmonization per donor, running concurrently rather than blending into a single current, which is how a Concordant Crystal expresses several Wellsprings where a single-donor Crystal expresses at most a compatible few; donors drawing on opposed Wellspring Houses cannot co-anchor one Layer and the frame will not form, or will not hold.
Coherence and eta are pooled, read once off the shared Shell regardless of donor count. Tempering Coherence, the Sub-Stat, stays per-donor because it lives in the Essence Core; a Concordant Crystal's stability is read off its least coherent donor, not their average. A Concordant Crystal runs Overgrown by default, not as a symptom: one Shell built to one donor's architecture now carries the combined Essence pressure of every donor it holds.
A donor's death or clean removal drops the frame from N to N-1 donors without destroying it: that donor's Wellspring line falls silent, the Shell re-equilibrates, and the frame settles toward Refined as the shed pressure eases its standing Overgrowth. A death the surviving donors refuse to release does not settle; the bond curdles into Obsession Force instead of dissolving, and the frame fractures under a signature that has stopped answering. A Concordant Crystal carries two failure modes no single-donor Crystal can suffer: Obsession Force fixation on an unreleased co-donor, and total loss of output from every donor at once should the single shared Shell Cloud or Rupture, where separate Crystals would each fail independently."

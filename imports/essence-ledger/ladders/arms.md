## The Weapons and armour ladder

**Weapons and armour, on the spine.** Most arms in the four quarters are Bearing-grade material with no Essence in them, and they perform as their physics. The ladder reads the ones that carry a working, and it reads them the way the spine reads everything: in joules. **A weapon is placed by the hit it delivers: the attack output of the working in it, at the Grade of whoever put the Essence there. A piece of armour, a ward or a shield is placed by what it stops: the attack output band of the strongest strike it holds against.** The joules find the row, and the row is the rung.

**Whose Grade.** An enhanced weapon performs no higher than the Grade of whoever put the Essence into it. Channelled through the weapon in the moment, that is the wielder's Grade; enchanted, glyphed or worked beforehand, it is the maker's Grade at the time of making, and a relic keeps its maker's ceiling in any hand. **A weapon's rung travels with its maker's work and not with its current owner, except where the working is channelled, and then it travels with the hand.**

### Beside the Bearing and the Holding

The ladder does not replace the grades the trade already stamps. **It reads a different quantity from the same object, and the two readings are held side by side.**

**The Bearing and Holding grades say what the material is.** Bearing is its physical work: what it stops, carries and survives as steel, stone or hide. Holding is its Essence work: what working it can carry and for how long. Every material has both, they run independently, and the material's tier, T0 to T9, is where the Master Material Ledger enters it.

**The item Tier says what the object may hold, and the rung says what it does.** An item Tier is the lower of what its material can hold and the Tier of Standing its maker stood at, and material tier n holds a working up to item Tier n, level with Tier of Standing n. So the material tier is the rung's ceiling for any standing working: **a weapon or armour placed above its material's tier is a working the material cannot carry, and the material fails first.** A plain item with no working in it has no Tier and no rung; its Bearing is the whole of it.

**The stamp and the rung read the same Grades.** The Accord's stamp is read off the Grade it holds; the rung is read off the spine. They agree everywhere except where one stamp covers two rungs or two stamps share one:

| Stamp, said as | Grade it holds | rung |
|---|---|---|
| Plain, Marked | Hollow–F; E | **1** |
| Proofed | D | **2** |
| Tempered | C | **3** |
| Instrument | B | **4** |
| Hallowed | A | **5** |
| Storied | S–SS | **6** |
| Found | SS, or SSS with Class IV or V provenance | **6**, or **7** at SSS |
| Numinous | X–EX | **8** |
| Proscribed | EX+ | **9** |

**Bearing is never on the ladder.** Plain steel stops plain shot by its Bearing and no rung is involved. The ladder begins where a working is added to the steel, and where a working meets the steel, the rung decides.

**1 · Sidearm.** Arms at the scale of one person: a marked knife, a charm-wound cudgel, a jack that turns a blade. **Any rung kills a man, and this one is where most killing is done.** A period revolver's ball, about 440 J, sits low on this rung, and a working that adds to it by the hit rather than the speed usually keeps it here.

**2 · Linepiece.** The arms of the battle line: Proofed plate, a D-Grade smith's enchanted blade. A Linepiece stops or delivers a Wall-level blow, and **every piece of it costs a practitioner's reserve**, which is why a line is equipped to it one man at a time.

**3 · Wallbiter.** Arms that open a wall or hold one shut. A Tempered blade, plate that stops a Journeyman's strike. **This is the rung at which a working in the steel outweighs the steel.**

**4 · Siegepiece.** Arms at the scale of a building. A Siegepiece's hit breaks a gatehouse and its proof holds against one being broken on the wearer. It is carried by one person, which is the whole of its danger.

**5 · Fieldwinner.** Arms at the scale of a city block. A Fieldwinner decides a field by being on it, and **the first rung at which taking the piece from its bearer is a war aim.**

**6 · Kingsarm.** Arms that deliver or stop a town's destruction in one blow, Town to Large Town. Kingdoms go to war over a Kingsarm and keep the house that made it.

**7 · Landbreaker.** Arms at the scale of a regional event. What a Landbreaker strikes is changed in the Continuum's account, not only in the world's.

**8 · Moonsplitter.** Country to Large Planet, in one piece of worked matter. **The material tiers that hold this rung are mythic and celestial, and the material is rarer than the smiths who could work it.**

**9 · Unmaker.** The rung with no ceiling. Arms that do not defeat what they strike so much as end it, forged from material the Accord forbids.

**The carve-out does not move a rung.** A hit or a proof between 2.43 × 10¹³ and 4.18 × 10¹³ J is Graded by the Sub-Stat that drives it rather than by joules, and both Grades it can take sit in Tier 6. It is a Kingsarm either way.

### The modifiers

**Proof: the armour's rung is a promise.** Armour at rung n stops a strike at rung n and below. **A strike one rung above the proof breaks it; a strike two rungs above passes as if it were not there.** The proof-mark names the house, the test and the charge, and a proof is a promise about a specific charge made by a workshop that may no longer exist.

**Two dials: speed and the hit.** A working on a projectile moves its speed, its hit, or both. Speed tops out at the enhancer's Attack Speed and the hit at the enhancer's Attack output band; the rung reads the hit. **An enhanced round is dodged at its actual velocity, not at its rung.**

**Standing or spent.** A standing working on a blade lasts until it is broken, read or unmade and costs its maker a tenth of their reserve once; proofed armour a quarter, and it lasts until struck two rungs above its proof; an enhanced round or arrow a hundredth, and it is consumed when fired. **The rung is what one piece does; the price says how many pieces a maker can put in the field.** A practitioner can equip themselves and cannot equip an army.

**Material ceiling.** The material tier caps the rung for a standing working. **A piece whose recorded hit or discharge reads above its material's tier is placed at the hit, and its material is where it fails.**

**Off the proof.** A few pieces deliver something that is not a joule figure: a strike that unthreads what it touches regardless of proofed Grade, dissolution applied past the Shell to the Crystal. **Such a piece is placed at its ceiling, and no proof on the ladder answers it.** Its answer is never the armour.

### Counterplay

**The rung tells an opponent what the piece can do and, just as plainly, what it cannot.** A weapon's rung is its maker's ceiling or its wielder's, and the piece says which: a channelled blade is answered by the hand, a made one by its proof, and a relic in a stranger's grip still hits at its maker's Grade. Armour tells its own limit on its mark: **one rung above the proof breaks it, and two go through it as if it were not there.** Every enhancement radiates its working's signature, and a Harmonist reads the rung without touching the piece. The seam, the joint and the material's failure are where the answer lives; which one is the fight's business.

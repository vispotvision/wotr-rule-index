## The Bestiary ladder

A beast goes on the spine (§3) the way a practitioner does: by its **peak output in joules**, its hardest blow, breath, dive or discharge. That figure finds the row, and the row is the rung. The Bestiary ladder gives the nine rungs the names the frontier already uses for them, and then adds the three things a beast has that a joule figure does not show: **how far its harm reaches, how hard it is to end, and what ground feeds it.**

The rung says what the thing can break. The modifiers say how long you will be in the room with it, and whose room it is.

### The nine rungs

| **3** | **Road-bane** | C | 2.092 × 10⁷ – 1.046 × 10⁹ | 0.005 – 0.25 | 1,046 EU |
| **4** | **Hamlet-bane** | B | 1.046 × 10⁹ – 4.6024 × 10¹⁰ | 0.25 – 11 | 4.602 × 10⁴ EU |
| **5** | **Town-bane** | A | 4.6024 × 10¹⁰ – 4.184 × 10¹² | 11 – 1,000 | 4.184 × 10⁶ EU |
| **6** | **March-bane** | S, SS | 4.184 × 10¹² – 4.184 × 10¹⁴ | 1 kt – 100 kt | 4.184 × 10⁸ EU |
| **7** | **Realm-bane** | SSS | 4.184 × 10¹⁴ – 4.184 × 10²¹ | 100 kt – 1 Tt | 4.184 × 10¹⁵ EU |

| **9** | **Sky-eater** | EX+ | 6.906 × 10³⁷ and above | 16.5 Rt and above | no ceiling |

One EU is one megajoule, so the EU column is the joule ceiling divided by a million. The carve-out in the spine between S and SS moves a Grade and never a rung; both sit in March-bane, and no beast's rung turns on it.

**Any rung kills a man.** A crossbow bolt carries a hundred joules. The ladder does not measure whether a creature can kill you. It measures what it can break when you are not the only thing in front of it.

**1 · Hearth-pest.** Below fifteen kilojoules. Its hardest blow is a man's or a dog's. A door holds against it, a householder with a spade answers it, and it is dangerous by being where somebody is rather than by what it does when it arrives. Vermin, the single walking corpse, the trickster in the hedge.

**2 · Field-bane.** Up to twenty-one megajoules, Wall level at the ceiling. It kills stock, breaks a fence, stoves in a door, puts a horse and rider down in one exchange. The great natural predators live here: the Greater Wolf's run, the lion's charge, the griffon's dive, a grown Droval's pounce across thirty metres. A Field-bane costs a fraction of an EU at the floor and twenty at the top.

**3 · Road-bane.** Up to a gigajoule. It closes a road: overturns a wagon, breaks a gate, capsizes a boat, brings down a bridge span or a small house. This is the Cyclops's wall-breaking blow, the Goliath's stomp, a hellhound's breath across a camp. Its hardest working costs between 21 and 1,046 EU.

**4 · Hamlet-bane.** Up to forty-six gigajoules, Building to Large Building. A lightning strike lands here, and so does the petrifying gaze, because a body remade into stone is its whole mass of chemistry done over, on the order of a gigajoule for a grown man. What stands here can take down the buildings of a hamlet one at a time.

**5 · Town-bane.** Up to four terajoules, City Block to Multi-City Block. The wyrm's breath, the phoenix's burst, the gorgon's gaze swept across a battalion, the hydra's heads all breathing at once. What stands here can empty a town in an afternoon if it is let.

**6 · March-bane.** Up to four hundred terajoules, one to a hundred kilotons: Town to Large Town. A coast-shattering surge, a trench throwing up a sea, a Titan's pulse flash-freezing a battlefield. This is the highest rung any creature in the register reaches by its own output. The Leviathan's surge and the Kraken's upheaval both sit here.

**7 · Realm-bane.** Large Town to Small Country. **No creature in the register stands here by output.** Every creature that stands here stands on a Town-bane or March-bane output and the modifiers below.

**8 · World-bane.** Country to Large Planet. No creature in the register stands here by output. The Voidwyrm, the Cerberus and the high Titan Droval stand here by standing, and the difference between those two words is the whole of this ladder.

**9 · Sky-eater.** Brown Dwarf and beyond. No creature in the register reaches it by output. Those that stand here stand on a March-bane's output with all three modifiers held, and the name is the frontier's, not the Ledger's.

### What this ladder adds

The spine gives every ladder its own modifiers. The Bestiary's are three, and each lifts the rung by **one**. A creature's **standing** is its output rung plus the modifiers it holds: never more than three above its output, never past nine.

**Reach.** Its harm lands beyond the ground a party can hold. Lightning at three kilometres, a song across open water, a hunt that runs across realms on an Essence signature, harm that outlasts the creature's presence: a storm curse on a valley, ground frozen metres deep after the Titan has gone, currents altered for a season. A creature with reach cannot be answered by standing still and holding a line, because the line is inside its range.

**Persistence.** It is hard to kill. It regenerates, returns, stores its life somewhere other than its body, or keeps fighting through wounds that end a body: the hydra's heads, the phoenix's ashes, the lich's phylactery, the Goliath's stoneform. **Hard to hit does not lift the rung.** A thing that phases, teleports, flies or vanishes into snow lengthens the fight and does not raise its ceiling; the first blow that lands on it lands on its rung. The Ledger prices what lands, not what misses.

**The tie.** It is fed by a Wellspring, a vein, a Crevice or a plane boundary: stronger at the site, weaker off it, and in the worst cases a living anchor of the site itself. The tie is the only modifier that can be taken away in the middle of a fight. Draw the creature off its ground, or deny the ground, and the rung the tie gave it is gone.

**Workings that do not strike.** A gaze, a song, a drain or a curse is read by the work it does to what it lands on. A body turned to stone is priced at remaking that body's matter. A song that walks sailors onto a reef is priced at the tide that wrecks them. **A death is not a quantity of work.** A sting that kills, or a severed Essence thread, stands on the blow that carried it, and the difference between that blow and the body count is the Commission's to price, not the Ledger's.

**Numbers are not a modifier.** The Ledger reads the animal. A pack, a herd, a horde or a coven is a party's problem and a Commission's figure; it is never a rung.

The Bestiary's own observation that almost everything above T6 is a Wellspring event rather than an animal is what the ladder shows in figures. Nothing in the register reaches Realm-bane by output, and **every creature the Ledger stands at seven or above holds the tie.** The Lich holds it through the person it was.

### The Commission's tiers and the rung

The Commission's Danger Tiers stand exactly as the Accord issues them.

| Commission Tier | Designation | Sigil |
|---|---|---|
| **T1** | (none entered) | Green Sigil |
| **T2** | Minor / Moderate Threat | Green Sigil |
| **T3** | Moderate Threat | Green Sigil |
| **T4** | High Threat | Blue Sigil |
| **T5** | Extreme Threat | Blue Sigil |
| **T6** | Domain Threat | Gold Sigil |
| **T7** | Realm Hazard | Gold Sigil |
| **T8** | Cataclysmic Entity | Obsidian Seal |
| **T9** | Apocalyptic Beast | Obsidian Seal |

**A Commission Tier is not a Grade.** A Threat Tier names the practitioner rank a Commission requires: T4 means the Accord sends Adepts, T7 means it sends Grandmasters under a Gold Sigil and nobody engages lawfully without one. A Grade is a joule band. A Tier of Standing is a set of Grades. So the Commission's T is a figure for the answer, and the Ledger's rung is a figure for the thing, and **the Ledger's standing, output plus modifiers, is the Ledger's figure for the same question the Commission asks.** Where the Commission weighs the same three things the Ledger weighs, the two agree, and across most of the register they do.

**The Leviathan and the Kraken show why a T cannot simply be a Grade.** Both are T9, and neither eats worlds. The Leviathan's hardest working is a surge that shatters a coast, on the order of 10¹⁴ joules, March-bane. What carries it three rungs higher is that it reaches (a maelstrom that pulls fleets under and currents altered for a season), it persists (hundreds of metres of abyss-black plate and a gaze that severs the soul's tether before the body is touched), and it is tied (a living anchor of an abyssal Wellspring on a plane boundary). The Kraken is the same arithmetic on a smaller surge. **A T9 beast is a March-bane holding all three.** An EX+ Grade would be something else entirely, and the register has none.

**T1 is empty because nobody files it.** A Hearth-pest is a householder's matter. The rat in the grain store and the fox at the hens are T1 on the Commission's scale and no Guild Hall has ever opened a Commission for one, so the register asks its eleven questions of nothing there. The rung exists; the paperwork does not.

**T2 and T3 share the word Moderate because a crowd changes the word.** T2 is Minor for one and Moderate for many: one walking corpse is a Hearth-pest with a grip, a battlefield of them is a Moderate Threat, and the Commission issues the same tier for both because it sends the same rank to either. T3 is Moderate by itself. The Ledger reads the body in both cases and lets the Commission read the crowd.

**Where the two differ, both stand.** The Ledger never overrules a Commission; it enters its reading beside it. In the register as it stands the Ledger never reads above the Commission. Where it reads below, the Commission is pricing something the ladder does not: numbers, a rider, what a thing does to a place rather than a body, what it does loose, or a death without output. **Those gaps are where a hunter can choose the fight the Ledger describes instead of the one the Commission does.**

**The Lich is read as a person first.** Its output is the Grade the necromancer held, placed on the spine like any practitioner's, and the three modifiers stand on top of it. The Commission enters every lich at T9. The Ledger enters a lich at nine only when the necromancer stood at Master before the phylactery; a lesser one who made the same bargain stands lower, and the Commission's tier is the tier of the worst of them.

**The Elemental is the one entry with no fixed rung**, because it has no fixed size. It always holds the tie, since an Elemental is concentrated Wellspring energy and collapses when the site is spent. The small ones stand one rung above their output; the ones that reshape a landscape reach and stand two.

**Summoned things are read loose**, as the Commission reads them. The leash is a fact about the summoner. The Ledger's rung is a fact about the beast.

### What the rung tells a hunter

The output rung is what you must be able to survive: armour, ward and Grade are chosen against it, and one Grade above a creature's output wins the exchange the way it wins any exchange. The modifiers tell you where and how to fight. **Persistence** means bring the thing that ends it (fire for the hydra's stumps, the phylactery before the lich, the ashes before the phoenix rises), and a creature that is only hard to hit needs patience, noise and mirrors, not a higher rank. **Reach** means close the distance or leave its ground, and Raga is the one entry where closing is the mistake. **The tie** is the best counter in the register: draw the thing off its Wellspring, deny it the site, or fight it where the vein is thin, and every rung the tie gave it is gone. And **where the Commission's tier sits above the Ledger's standing**, the Commission is pricing a pack, a rider, a place or a death. Break the pack, unseat the rider, change the ground, or refuse the death, and you are fighting the creature the Ledger describes.

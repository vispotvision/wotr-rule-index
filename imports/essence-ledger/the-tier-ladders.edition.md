> Every ladder in the Continuum hangs on one spine: peak output in joules, read to a Grade, read to a rung from one to nine. The Essence Ledger sets the spine. This Part names the rungs for each kind of thing that can be measured on it (beasts, draughts, artifacts, summons, Domains, Wellspring sites, weapons and armour) and says what each ladder adds that a joule figure does not show. A rung is a fact anyone can look up. What it means in the room is for whoever is standing in it to work out.


### The Bestiary ladder

A beast goes on the spine (the spine of the Essence Ledger) the way a practitioner does: by its **peak output in joules**, its hardest blow, breath, dive or discharge. That figure finds the row, and the row is the rung. The Bestiary ladder gives the nine rungs the names the frontier already uses for them, and then adds the three things a beast has that a joule figure does not show: **how far its harm reaches, how hard it is to end, and what ground feeds it.**

The rung says what the thing can break. The modifiers say how long you will be in the room with it, and whose room it is.

One EU is one megajoule, so the EU column is the joule ceiling divided by a million. The carve-out in the spine between S and SS moves a Grade and never a rung; both sit in March-bane, and no beast's rung turns on it.

**Any rung kills a man.** A crossbow bolt carries a hundred joules. The ladder does not measure whether a creature can kill you. It measures what it can break when you are not the only thing in front of it.

**Hearth-pest.** Below fifteen kilojoules. Its hardest blow is a man's or a dog's. A door holds against it, a householder with a spade answers it, and it is dangerous by being where somebody is rather than by what it does when it arrives. Vermin, the single walking corpse, the trickster in the hedge.

**Field-bane.** Up to twenty-one megajoules, Wall level at the ceiling. It kills stock, breaks a fence, stoves in a door, puts a horse and rider down in one exchange. The great natural predators live here: the Greater Wolf's run, the lion's charge, the griffon's dive, a grown Droval's pounce across thirty metres. A Field-bane costs a fraction of an EU at the floor and twenty at the top.

**Road-bane.** Up to a gigajoule. It closes a road: overturns a wagon, breaks a gate, capsizes a boat, brings down a bridge span or a small house. This is the Cyclops's wall-breaking blow, the Goliath's stomp, a hellhound's breath across a camp. Its hardest working costs between 21 and 1,046 EU.

**Hamlet-bane.** Up to forty-six gigajoules, Building to Large Building. A lightning strike lands here, and so does the petrifying gaze, because a body remade into stone is its whole mass of chemistry done over, on the order of a gigajoule for a grown man. What stands here can take down the buildings of a hamlet one at a time.

**Town-bane.** Up to four terajoules, City Block to Multi-City Block. The wyrm's breath, the phoenix's burst, the gorgon's gaze swept across a battalion, the hydra's heads all breathing at once. What stands here can empty a town in an afternoon if it is let.

**March-bane.** Up to four hundred terajoules, one to a hundred kilotons: Town to Large Town. A coast-shattering surge, a trench throwing up a sea, a Titan's pulse flash-freezing a battlefield. This is the highest rung any creature in the register reaches by its own output. The Leviathan's surge and the Kraken's upheaval both sit here.

**Realm-bane.** Large Town to Small Country. **No creature in the register stands here by output.** Every creature that stands here stands on a Town-bane or March-bane output and the modifiers below.

**World-bane.** Country to Large Planet. No creature in the register stands here by output. The Voidwyrm, the Cerberus and the high Titan Droval stand here by standing, and the difference between those two words is the whole of this ladder.

**Sky-eater.** Brown Dwarf and beyond. No creature in the register reaches it by output. Those that stand here stand on a March-bane's output with all three modifiers held, and the name is the frontier's, not the Ledger's.

#### What this ladder adds

The spine gives every ladder its own modifiers. The Bestiary's are three, and each lifts the rung by **one**. A creature's **standing** is its output rung plus the modifiers it holds: never more than three above its output, never past nine.

**Reach.** Its harm lands beyond the ground a party can hold. Lightning at three kilometres, a song across open water, a hunt that runs across realms on an Essence signature, harm that outlasts the creature's presence: a storm curse on a valley, ground frozen metres deep after the Titan has gone, currents altered for a season. A creature with reach cannot be answered by standing still and holding a line, because the line is inside its range.

**Persistence.** It is hard to kill. It regenerates, returns, stores its life somewhere other than its body, or keeps fighting through wounds that end a body: the hydra's heads, the phoenix's ashes, the lich's phylactery, the Goliath's stoneform. **Hard to hit does not lift the rung.** A thing that phases, teleports, flies or vanishes into snow lengthens the fight and does not raise its ceiling; the first blow that lands on it lands on its rung. The Ledger prices what lands, not what misses.

**The tie.** It is fed by a Wellspring, a vein, a Crevice or a plane boundary: stronger at the site, weaker off it, and in the worst cases a living anchor of the site itself. The tie is the only modifier that can be taken away in the middle of a fight. Draw the creature off its ground, or deny the ground, and the rung the tie gave it is gone.

**Workings that do not strike.** A gaze, a song, a drain or a curse is read by the work it does to what it lands on. A body turned to stone is priced at remaking that body's matter. A song that walks sailors onto a reef is priced at the tide that wrecks them. **A death is not a quantity of work.** A sting that kills, or a severed Essence thread, stands on the blow that carried it, and the difference between that blow and the body count is the Commission's to price, not the Ledger's.

**Numbers are not a modifier.** The Ledger reads the animal. A pack, a herd, a horde or a coven is a party's problem and a Commission's figure; it is never a rung.

The Bestiary's own observation that almost everything above T6 is a Wellspring event rather than an animal is what the ladder shows in figures. Nothing in the register reaches Realm-bane by output, and **every creature the Ledger stands at seven or above holds the tie.** The Lich holds it through the person it was.

**A Commission Tier is not a Grade.** A Threat Tier names the practitioner rank a Commission requires: T4 means the Accord sends Adepts, T7 means it sends Grandmasters under a Gold Sigil and nobody engages lawfully without one. A Grade is a joule band. A Tier of Standing is a set of Grades. So the Commission's T is a figure for the answer, and the Ledger's rung is a figure for the thing, and **the Ledger's standing, output plus modifiers, is the Ledger's figure for the same question the Commission asks.** Where the Commission weighs the same three things the Ledger weighs, the two agree, and across most of the register they do.

**The Leviathan and the Kraken show why a T cannot simply be a Grade.** Both are T9, and neither eats worlds. The Leviathan's hardest working is a surge that shatters a coast, on the order of 10¹⁴ joules, March-bane. What carries it three rungs higher is that it reaches (a maelstrom that pulls fleets under and currents altered for a season), it persists (hundreds of metres of abyss-black plate and a gaze that severs the soul's tether before the body is touched), and it is tied (a living anchor of an abyssal Wellspring on a plane boundary). The Kraken is the same arithmetic on a smaller surge. **A T9 beast is a March-bane holding all three.** An EX+ Grade would be something else entirely, and the register has none.

**T1 is empty because nobody files it.** A Hearth-pest is a householder's matter. The rat in the grain store and the fox at the hens are T1 on the Commission's scale and no Guild Hall has ever opened a Commission for one, so the register asks its eleven questions of nothing there. The rung exists; the paperwork does not.

**T2 and T3 share the word Moderate because a crowd changes the word.** T2 is Minor for one and Moderate for many: one walking corpse is a Hearth-pest with a grip, a battlefield of them is a Moderate Threat, and the Commission issues the same tier for both because it sends the same rank to either. T3 is Moderate by itself. The Ledger reads the body in both cases and lets the Commission read the crowd.

**Where the two differ, both stand.** The Ledger never overrules a Commission; it enters its reading beside it. In the register as it stands the Ledger never reads above the Commission. Where it reads below, the Commission is pricing something the ladder does not: numbers, a rider, what a thing does to a place rather than a body, what it does loose, or a death without output. **Those gaps are where a hunter can choose the fight the Ledger describes instead of the one the Commission does.**

**The Lich is read as a person first.** Its output is the Grade the necromancer held, placed on the spine like any practitioner's, and the three modifiers stand on top of it. The Commission enters every lich at T9. The Ledger enters a lich at nine only when the necromancer stood at Master before the phylactery; a lesser one who made the same bargain stands lower, and the Commission's tier is the tier of the worst of them.

**The Elemental is the one entry with no fixed rung**, because it has no fixed size. It always holds the tie, since an Elemental is concentrated Wellspring energy and collapses when the site is spent. The small ones stand one rung above their output; the ones that reshape a landscape reach and stand two.

**Summoned things are read loose**, as the Commission reads them. The leash is a fact about the summoner. The Ledger's rung is a fact about the beast.

#### What the rung tells a hunter

The output rung is what you must be able to survive: armour, ward and Grade are chosen against it, and one Grade above a creature's output wins the exchange the way it wins any exchange. The modifiers tell you where and how to fight. **Persistence** means bring the thing that ends it (fire for the hydra's stumps, the phylactery before the lich, the ashes before the phoenix rises), and a creature that is only hard to hit needs patience, noise and mirrors, not a higher rank. **Reach** means close the distance or leave its ground, and Raga is the one entry where closing is the mistake. **The tie** is the best counter in the register: draw the thing off its Wellspring, deny it the site, or fight it where the vein is thin, and every rung the tie gave it is gone. And **where the Commission's tier sits above the Ledger's standing**, the Commission is pricing a pack, a rider, a place or a death. Break the pack, unseat the rider, change the ground, or refuse the death, and you are fighting the creature the Ledger describes.

### The Drafts ladder

**Alchemical works and Drafts, on the spine.** Every Draft, salt, oil, powder and charge the trade produces is a working stabilised into a substance, and a substance holds a definite quantity of Essence. The ladder reads that quantity. **A work is placed by the EU one dose holds, converted at one megajoule to the unit, and read on the spine like any other peak output.** Two modifiers, Carry and Fidelity, then move it.

**What is read is what the dose holds, not what arrives.** The spine is read on the held figure at the constant, before anybody's η touches it. What reaches the drinker is the held figure times the drinker's own η, and whatever reaches the Crystal is banked there at the same constant it would leave at: one EU for every megajoule taken in. **A dose can overfill.** Recovery stops at a practitioner's maximum; what is drunk does not, and a dose larger than the room left in the Crystal leaves its drinker above their own ceiling, holding reserve like any other, spent at the same rate and counted toward the same floor.

**Tincture.** A work measured in thousandths of a unit. It cleans a cut, holds a fall, lights a room, and needs no circle and no understanding. **The whole first rung together costs less than the smallest reserve carries in its lowest percent**, which is why a household can own a shelf of it and never think of it as alchemy.

**Tonic.** A working day's steadiness in a bottle, a lamp for a season, a thunderclap in a flask. **A careless hand can ruin a Tonic without dying of it.** This is the rung of the ration and the field kit.

**Draught.** The trade standard. A Draught holds enough to matter against a Welling reserve and to carry a Welling practitioner toward the Ascension floor across a course of doses. **The bulk of what the four quarters drink for their Crystal is here.**

**Philter.** A dose that holds a Journeyman's whole working range in one vessel. **This is the rung at which a Draft can carry a practitioner to a quantitative floor in one issue**, and the rung at which it is sold against a term of service rather than a price.

**Elixir.** Threshold work in earnest. An Elixir holds as much as an Expert's reserve spends in a long engagement and supports a Crystal through a reorganisation. **An Elixir can supply the floor and it can supply a seed. It does not supply a Catalyst, and nothing on any higher rung does either.**

**Quintessence.** A dose at the scale of a Master's Domain. Works on this rung anchor, stabilise and hold at the size of a live, law-bearing field, and **the question of what the dose was rendered from stops being hypothetical**.

**Magisterium.** A work that enforces. It holds energy on the scale of a regional event, and what it does to a blade, a wound or a Domain behaves as a ruling rather than a reaction.

**Panacea.** Realm-scale. What a Panacea is laid into registers in the Continuum as terrain. **Works on this rung are used a handful of times in the record, and what they are laid into outlasts whoever laid it.**

**Stone.** The rung with no ceiling. It holds what a Paragon's working holds, and **the Stone is the rung the trade keeps open because the ladder required a top, not because the trade has filled it.**

**The carve-out does not move a rung.** A dose whose held energy lands between 2.43 × 10¹³ and 4.18 × 10¹³ J is Graded by its Sub-Stat reading rather than by joules, and both Grades it can take sit in Master. It is a Quintessence either way.

**Fidelity: how close the dose is to its reference figure.** Fidelity decides how much of the held energy arrives as the named effect.

**The modifiers net, and together they move a dose at most one rung either way.** Carry can raise a rung and never lower one. Fidelity can lower a rung and never raise one. A Heavy-carry dose of Loose fidelity sits where its held energy puts it: the origin governs and the formula leaks, and the two cancel on the ladder without cancelling in the drinker.


#### The Index and the ladder

**The Standing Index and the ladder are two readings of the same lot, and neither corrects the other.** The Index ranks an entered lot from T1 to T9 by provenance and standing: its Provenance Class, its rank gate, who may lawfully hold it and what the Bench is prepared to certify about it. The ladder ranks the same lot by the energy one dose holds. **The Index prices provenance and standing. The ladder prices energy held.**

The two agree most often through the middle of the trade, where a Threshold ration's rank gate and its dose climb together. They part wherever the Index is pricing law, provenance or danger rather than joules. **A lot can hold almost nothing and be reserved to Masters, because what it does is dangerous out of all proportion to what it holds; and a lot can hold a Domain's worth and be entered one rank lower than its rung, because its Carry lifts it on the ladder and not in the ledger.** A buyer reads the Index to learn who may hold a thing. A drinker, and anyone facing one, reads the rung to learn what it will do.

#### Counterplay

**The rung says how much a dosed opponent took in, and banked reserve is only reserve.** A Philter or an Elixir drunk before a fight has put that many EU above where the opponent stood, possibly above their own ceiling, and every one of them spends at the same rate as the rest and counts toward the same ten-percent floor. The answer is findable in the arithmetic: make them spend it. **A Draft supplies energy and it can supply a seed; it has never supplied a Catalyst**, so a dosed practitioner holds more than they did and is not a Stage higher than they were. Where the Carry is Heavy, the origin arrived with the energy, and the source's own weaknesses, appetites and habits of mind are in the drinker now and can be found there. Where the Fidelity is C or worse, the dose did not do the same thing twice, and the part that went into the adjacent process is a tell.

### The Artifacts ladder

**Artifacts, on the spine.** An artifact is a working given a body that outlasts the working. Some hold a store of their own, charge laid down against a later need. Most hold nothing and pass what their bearer spends through a glyph chain that shapes it. The ladder reads both the same way. **An artifact is placed by the EU it stores or the EU one act of it discharges, whichever is greater, converted at one megajoule to the unit and read on the spine like any other peak output.** One modifier, how bound the thing is to its bearer, then decides whose rung it is.

**Stored and discharged are read differently.** A store is read at the constant: what the thing holds, times one megajoule. A discharge is read as delivered, the EU spent times the η of whoever spends it; where a record gives only the draw on the bearer, the draw is read at the constant and the rung is a ceiling. **A draw given as a share of the bearer's reserve is as large as the reserve makes it.** The share decides how long the bearer lasts; the absolute size of the reserve decides how large each act is, and an artifact that asks a Master for five percent of a Master's reserve is discharging on a Master's scale.

**Implement.** A single act at the scale of a building. An Implement that stores its own charge is an Implement in any hand that can open it; one that only passes its bearer's draw is an Implement because its bearer is.

**Relic.** Expert scale, city-block output in one act or a store that can answer it. **A Relic is the first rung at which taking the thing off its bearer is a war aim rather than a theft.**

**Heirloom.** Master scale. An Heirloom's act is a natural disaster at the level of a town, and it is almost always bound to one soul, **because nothing else could carry the draw**.

**Regalia.** Grandmaster scale, a regional event in one act. Regalia are instruments of standing as much as of force: what they discharge is enforced by the Continuum rather than merely delivered.

**Hallow.** Archmaster scale. A Hallow's act moves country and moon. **What a Hallow is planted in stops being a place with a thing in it and becomes a place that is the thing.**

**Covenant.** The rung with no ceiling. A Covenant is not held; it is entered into, and **the bearer is bound to it at least as surely as it is bound to the bearer**.

#### The modifier: binding

**Binding decides whose rung it is.** It never raises a rung. It decides who may stand on it, and what the rung becomes in a hand that is not the bound one.

| Binding | What it means | Effect on the rung |
|---|---|---|
| **Free** | Answers any hand, or whoever last fed it | **The rung is the object's own and travels with it.** Whoever holds it holds the rung |
| **Keyed** | Answers a standing, a rite, a vow or a harmonisation rather than one soul | **The rung is the object's own in a keyed hand, and one rung lower in any other** where it answers at all |
| **Soulbound** | Answers one soul and refuses the rest | **Never above the bearer's Tier of Standing**, because every act crosses the bearer's Shell. In any other hand it has no rung |
| **Grown-in** | Bound into the bearer's body, Core or Domain; severed, it dies | **As Soulbound, and it cannot be taken.** The rung is the bearer's to lose, not the object's |

**A store falls as it spends.** An artifact placed by what it stores stands on the rung its store puts it, and it steps down the ladder as the store empties. An artifact placed by what it discharges keeps its rung as long as its bearer can pay.

#### Counterplay

**The rung says the most one act of the thing can deliver, and the binding says where the answer lives.** A Free artifact's rung goes wherever the object goes, so taking it, burying it or starving what it feeds on removes the rung whole. A Keyed artifact stands one rung lower the moment its key is contested: challenge the Writ, break the vow's terms, deny the harmonisation, and the thing works in its crudest form or not at all. A Soulbound artifact can never outrun its bearer's Tier, and stolen it is inert, so there is nothing to gain by taking it and everything to gain by making the bearer pay; every act crosses the bearer's Shell and is spent out of the bearer's reserve toward the bearer's floor. A Grown-in artifact cannot be separated from its bearer at all, and the fight is with the ground or the conditions it depends on. **A store empties.** An artifact placed by what it holds steps down the ladder with every discharge, and a patient opponent can count the rings.

### The Summons ladder

**Summons, on the spine.** A called thing is a working that stands up and walks. Whatever it does once it is standing, the one figure every entry in the register carries is what it cost to call, and that figure is the ladder's. **A summon is placed by its call cost read through η: the EU spent to call it, times the efficiency of the crossing, times one megajoule.** The delivered joules find the row, and the row is the rung.

**Whose η.** The crossing is the summoner's Shell, so the summoner's own η is read wherever it is recorded. Where it is not, the crossing is read at the summon's own Aether Class, because Aether Class governs how much of the donated Essence survives the crossing into form: Class I loses thirty to forty percent (η 0.60–0.70), Class II fifteen to twenty-five (η 0.75–0.85), Class III five to ten (η 0.90–0.95), and Class V delivers one hundred percent or more (η 1.00 and above). **A call cost given as a share of reserve is as large as the summoner's reserve makes it**, and where the reserve is not recorded it is set by the law of reserves at the summoner's Level.

The rung says what was paid to put the thing on the field. It does not say what the thing will do once it is there, and it does not say what it costs to keep it there. The modifiers say those.

#### The modifiers

**None of the modifiers moves the rung.** The rung is the call, and the call is paid once. The modifiers are what the call bought.

**Sustain: the clock.** A summon held past its arrival draws a sustain, and the sustain is read as a clock against the summoner's reserve: reserve ÷ sustain is how long the summoner can hold it at full, and nine tenths of that is how long before Essence Starvation. **A summon whose sustain empties its summoner inside a minute is placed at its call and fought at its clock.**

**Cost off the Ledger.** Several entries record a sustain of nil and a cost paid in something other than Essence: a name taken per summoning, sensation that runs late for hours, heat carried in the summoner's own body. **A cost of this kind is recorded beside the rung and never converted into it.** It does not make the summon cheaper. It makes the summoner the place where the summon can be reached.

**Provenance: what holds it together.** The register's four provenances decide what survives the fight.

| Provenance | What holds it | What the rung does when the summoner falls |
|---|---|---|
| **Donated** | a fragment of the summoner's own Core on a live tether | **ends with the summoner**, and ends inside any Silence Realm |
| **Analog** | a synthetic three-layer architecture inside the summoner's Concordia field | degrades with the field; a coherent one may resist dissolution |
| **Native** | its own Crystal | **stands.** The rung was the price of being recognised, and killing the summoner does not unpay it |
| **Anchored** | Crystal function borrowed from a vein, a bloodline or a Domain | **stands as long as the node does**, and outlives the summoner |

**Bond Class: what it will refuse.** Directed, Interpretive, Consenting, Sovereign. A Consenting or Sovereign summon at any rung carries refusal conditions, and **a refusal is the one event that takes a called thing's whole rung off the field without a blow being struck.**

**Danger Tier: the thing loose.** The register rates every entry T1 through T9 on the Commission's threat scale, as it would be rated loose and hostile. **The rung and the Danger Tier are the same nine steps read from opposite sides.** The rung is what the summoner paid; the Danger Tier is what the world pays if the leash fails. A Danger Tier above the rung is a summon whose threat is not in its output, and the register's entries all stand there.

### The Domains ladder

**Domains, on the spine.** A Domain is inner law made local reality, and it is held, not thrown. What the spine reads is what the Domain holds: **the energy standing in its territory at one time.** Two figures fix that, and the ladder reads them together: **the radius the law is held over, and the joules held across it.** The joules find the row and the row is the rung. The radius says how far those joules are spread, and the two multiplied back together are the size and density that Part Twenty-Two makes the measure of a Domain's collapse.

**How the joules held are read.** A Domain is paid for as a rate, and the Ledger turns a rate into a quantity over its own unit of time, the six-second turn. **The joules held are the maintenance draw across one turn, delivered through the holder's η: draw in EU/s × 6 × η × one megajoule.** A Domain expression paid per activation rather than per second is read at its activation cost through the same η. A Domain spent whole, detonated or collapsed on purpose, is read at what the spending cost, and stands on its own row beside the held one.

**Density is the tell.** Joules held ÷ the area of the disc the radius draws, π r², gives the charge on each square metre of held ground, in J/m². It does not move the rung. It is what an opponent standing at the edge can estimate, and it is the figure a collapse is felt in.

### The Wellspring and vein sites ladder

**Sites, on the spine.** A Wellspring is not a place, but it pools: a current concentrates where the substrate conducts, where no competing current draws on it, and where time has let it saturate the field until the ground expresses the law on its own. A site is that pool, and a vein is the channel that feeds it. **A site is placed by hazard: the output a practitioner must be able to command to keep their own law inside the site at its worst ground.** That figure is read in joules like every other peak, the row it lands in is the rung, and the rung says how strong you must be to stand there and remain yourself.

**Why hazard is read this way.** A site does not strike. It retunes. Harmonization is phase-locking, and a current strong enough to phase-lock a Shell against its bearer's will is a current the bearer's own output has failed to hold off. So the ladder asks the question the survey asks, from the other side: what Grade of output holds a Crystal's own architecture against this current. The density scale answers it in words, and the spine answers it in joules. **The two meet at one fixed point: Domain law at SSS can override the local behaviour of a Wellspring, and a Wellspring Core is the ground where the Wellspring's law is the only law operating. A Core therefore stands on SSS, the seventh rung, and the density scale is laid down the spine from there.**

### The Weapons and armour ladder

**Weapons and armour, on the spine.** Most arms in the four quarters are Bearing-grade material with no Essence in them, and they perform as their physics. The ladder reads the ones that carry a working, and it reads them the way the spine reads everything: in joules. **A weapon is placed by the hit it delivers: the attack output of the working in it, at the Grade of whoever put the Essence there. A piece of armour, a ward or a shield is placed by what it stops: the attack output band of the strongest strike it holds against.** The joules find the row, and the row is the rung.

**Whose Grade.** An enhanced weapon performs no higher than the Grade of whoever put the Essence into it. Channelled through the weapon in the moment, that is the wielder's Grade; enchanted, glyphed or worked beforehand, it is the maker's Grade at the time of making, and a relic keeps its maker's ceiling in any hand. **A weapon's rung travels with its maker's work and not with its current owner, except where the working is channelled, and then it travels with the hand.**

#### Counterplay

**The rung tells an opponent what the piece can do and, just as plainly, what it cannot.** A weapon's rung is its maker's ceiling or its wielder's, and the piece says which: a channelled blade is answered by the hand, a made one by its proof, and a relic in a stranger's grip still hits at its maker's Grade. Armour tells its own limit on its mark: **one rung above the proof breaks it, and two go through it as if it were not there.** Every enhancement radiates its working's signature, and a Harmonist reads the rung without touching the piece. The seam, the joint and the material's failure are where the answer lives; which one is the fight's business.

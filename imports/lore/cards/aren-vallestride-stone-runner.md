## Lore · The Life Behind the Card

*Written 2026-09-24 by the character-lore pass from this card and the record of Old World roads and chains. What those sources do not state is new here.*

### Origin

Aren Vallestride was born to farmstead people in the borderland of the Old World. Their village kept fields, fences and one road out, and a Well lay close by. He grew up walking. He walked the fence lines, drove the stock and carried word to the next holding and back, and he learned the ground of his own country by the way it met his boots.

The Well went hostile, the way Wells do. It was a minor Withering surge, and it emptied the village. The families left their houses for the roads, and the fences stood around empty yards. By the time Aren came of age to guard the village, there was nothing left to guard.

### The Making

He went to the roads himself. He took work guarding other people's caravans. A courier line chartered by the Guild Accord ran low-risk routes along the frontier march, and he rode it on probation with a minor caravan-company. He was an apprentice road courier and a monster-scout. Nobody trained him. He learned a guard by standing in a bad one and being knocked out of it, then standing up and doing it again, until his body found the small, cheap stance on its own.

The first time a wagon-raid nearly killed him, his legs had to do something they had never done before. A raider's rush caught him at the side of a wagon and should have put him under the wheels. His foot hooked into the earth, and the earth kept it. The shove ran down through his heel into the ground, and when he let go it came back up and drove his next step. Nobody had taught him to hook his weight into the earth. He found it himself, under a raid.

Afterward he found it at the wrong moments too. An anchor let go at the wrong instant came back through his own legs, and he paid for each one in bruises that went to the bone.

### The Cost

He paid for the anchor in his legs. After heavy use in one fight they throbbed and would not steady under him. Sometimes hours passed before he could anchor again. Swamp, sand, ice and Essence-mist would not hold an anchor, and when the ground gave way beneath one, the force he had stored came straight back into him and left him off balance and unguarded. He packed chalk stones and small metal pegs to drive into loose soil where the ground would not hold on its own. He tested every road with his heel. Anything that flew left him nothing to hook into.

On the Hollow Ford Run, Garu Luneward was scouting ahead of the train. He caught the scent of a Withering-spawned nest under a village bridge and stayed on it. The warning was Aren's to carry. He ran it through the night on failing legs and reached the village before the nest moved. When it moved, the fight came to the ford, and his anchors collapsed the crossing and cut the creatures off before they could swarm inland. The village stood. It lost its ford.

In the Black Cart Ambush he tripped a raider charge. Three wagons were lost. Every civilian lived.

Once he had committed to guarding a person or reaching a place, coercion and fear struggled to turn him aside short of breaking his legs. At every halt he walked the length of the train and counted the people in it, and he did not sit until the count came right.

### Where They Stand

Aren still rides on probation with the same minor caravan-company on the frontier march. The march calls him Stone-Runner. Some call him Dust-Track Aren, or the Boy with the Borrowed Sword. The guards and civilians on his runs trust him further than his rank, on the strength of the Hollow Ford Run and the Black Cart Ambush. When a warning has to outrun the train, Garu stays on the trail and sends it by him. Aren explains the anchors, the pegs and the bruises to anyone who asks.

In his probationary year, after the march had seen him hold ground, the Caravan Families' old hands told him of a Hobgoblin who had escorted their trains long before and did not chase. They said Aren stood the way the Old Chain had stood. He kept the story. He has never met the Old Chain.

The chipped short sword he carries belonged to an outrider who died on duty. He carries it on the promise of returning it once he "earns the right to carry it." He has not met the outrider's family. Finding them and putting the blade in their hands is the debt he is working toward. Whether the anchor is his own, or an echo of the dead outrider's decades of road-duty still living in the sword, nobody knows.

He has one answer for anyone who asks why he gets up again: "If my legs still work, then the road isn't finished with me yet."

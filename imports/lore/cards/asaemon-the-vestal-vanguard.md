## Lore · The Life Behind the Card

*Written 2026-09-24 by the character-lore pass from this card and the record of the Accord's field blades. What those sources do not state is new here.*

### Origin

Asaemon was born in the western tin districts in the Voyager Era, in the years the charts were opening the routes east. His father was a barber. He shaved the tinners, drew their teeth, let their blood and dressed what the workings did to them. The ground in those districts gave way without warning over workings nobody had recorded, and the men it caught came to the barber's chair before they went anywhere else. Whatever was past him went down the valley to the surgeon, who ranked above a barber and charged for it.

Eastern gold reached the western quarter in his boyhood. Bread cost more each year. A barber's fee stayed where custom had set it.

A carrier came home from the eastern routes with a wound in his forearm that would not close. The margin stayed open and clean. It looked better than it should for longer than it should. His father put an ear close to it, as the old practitioners did, and heard nothing where there should have been sound. The surgeon came up the valley and cut back to clean margin. The wound came again in the next tissue. He cut again. Then he took the arm above the elbow. The carrier died in the back room of the shop all the same. Asaemon held the basin through every cutting.

### The Making

The surgeon took him on as an apprentice. He learned the pulse first, since every competent practitioner took it before anything else. Then he learned colour and smell, and the heat of a wound under the hand. He kept the habit of the ear longer than his master did, and in time it gave him more than it gave anyone else in the valley.

He heard each body as a pulse. A wound that would not close sounded to him as a second pulse inside the first, and it ran back along a line to whatever was feeding it. He began to leave the tissue alone until he had heard where the line ran. Thread came out of him after that, golden and fine. The filaments ran where the pulse ran, and he learned to cut where the line was false.

He went away alone into meditative isolation. No teacher went with him. No order had sent him. There he formed his first true bond, and he swore it to the discipline itself, to no person and to no order. The bond was made with a method. Nobody afterwards talked him out of a cut once he had started it.

### The Cost

He paid for each cut after it landed. Long reading took his sight, and he went blind for a while when it was done. A completed Severance left his body brittle for a stretch, and he was easy to break until it passed. He was never built to overpower anything. Mass beat him. So did Aether that kept no rhythm, since it gave him no pulse to hear, and his cut never found its line. A Wellspring that stood alone, linked to nothing, gave him nothing to sever.

The Vanguard sent for him when a siege wanted precision, and he answered when the work suited the discipline, whatever the siege was for. At the Siege of Oriel the enemy's generals had been corrupted. In a single engagement he cut each general loose from the power that fed him. None of the generals died. Nobody on the Vanguard's side died. Afterwards he could not see for a time, and his body was brittle for a stretch. The record named nobody who sat with him.

The Codex set a scribe or a seer at every battle and held an unrecorded war unlawful, and Arbitration reviewed each engagement once it ended. Asaemon gave his account. He described the wounds: where each general's connection had failed, and where he cut it. He said nothing of how he made the cut. He would explain a wound to anyone who asked. He never explained a working.

### Where They Stand

Asaemon is active in the Shattered Peaks. He is bound by his own oath and holds no charter from any order. He fights for the Vanguard when a siege calls for him, and for nobody else. He has claimed no ground and wants none.

He wears the Cloak of the Silent Pulse. It dampens what his body gives off, and most detection passes over him. He carries a lancet that condenses the pressure of his filaments into a bladed line of light. He rarely draws it. He always finishes with it.

He is building a Concord Severance. Nobody knows what finishing it would cost him.

The review of Oriel is kept in the Archives Eternal, beneath the Council Spire of the Concord Citadel. It does not say whose Vanguard sent for him. Long after him, in the Withering Era, Tsukihana Reimei read it in her Arbitration years. They never met.

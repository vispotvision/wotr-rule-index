## Lore · The Life Behind the Card

*Written 2026-09-24 by the character-lore pass from this card and the record of The elven old-ground. What those sources do not state is new here.*

### Origin

Brynja Haldrís was born to the Northroot Stone Clans, Eladrin of the north, in the Withering Era. Long before her, in the War of the Long Night, Valthrum refused to end the season Florwyn had made him to protect. The winter lay over the north, and the clans did not leave. They cut the first Northroot forge into the rock and kept it burning through the Long Night. Every hearth in the holdings had been lit from it since.

She was born into the line that carried Skor and Vethra, the twin hammers bound to the Northroot clan blood. Her mother kept the line's forge. At that hearth the hammers were spoken of by name, the way other families speak of grandmothers, and Brynja knew both names long before she could lift either. The Oathband Necklace came down the same line, holding stone still molten from the first forge.

She grew heavy in the shoulder and slow on her feet. Nobody tried to make her quick.

### The Making

She learned the forge before she learned the hammers. A rune on a Northroot hammer was a vow sworn in iron and blood. She learned to cut one into the metal and set it with flame, so that what was etched stayed etched. The first rune she cut into Skor was her own. When anyone asked what it said, she told them it was a vow and said nothing more. She held that a vow explained aloud stopped being a vow.

She trained to hold ground. Her plate was cut to answer a blow by hardening where it landed. The longer she held a line, the more she found she had left to hold it with. Her heaviest blow had to be walked to its mark and delivered at arm's reach. It could not be thrown. What flew, she could not reach.

Then a siege came against a Northroot holding, and Brynja stood in its gate with a child behind her. She did not give ground. She accepted, willingly, a real cost to her own soul to keep what she valued above her own unbroken shape, and the gate held. Every strength she gained after that came the same way, while she stood between a siege and a child. None of it came from advancing herself.

### The Cost

When the siege broke, her plate was whole over a broken forearm. The runes held, and more than once afterward the bone under them gave first: ribs, a collarbone, the small bones of the hand she kept on Vethra. Her heaviest blow spent most of what she had in one swing. When it landed on something harder than she had judged, the break came back up into her own arms.

"If it reaches my people, it passes through me first," she has said.

One winter she went down into a gorge below the holdings. An exile was there, Vargrath Ul-Dorr, with draconic scars across his frame, keeping thirty-eight dying elves alive in a cut of rock with the snow coming on. He had sworn them nothing, and he had already refused to leave them. She stood in front of them all. The clans' forge-halls took them in for the winter, and Vargrath came with them. In the spring he left, and the thirty-eight went with him and became the army he commands. The clans sent no one after them. Brynja did not go. She had stood in front of those people for one winter, and she would not march for what they became.

### Where They Stand

She is the Hammer of the Northroot, the Runebound Shield-Mother, also called Stonebraid. She keeps the forge and stands in front of the holdings, with the Runebraid Mantle under her plate and the Oathband Necklace at her throat. Village, child or column, she makes no distinction among the things she has stood in front of. She fights for the hearth, and she has taken no one's ground. Other people count how many have stood behind her shield in an ordinary season. She does not.

Drevath Mourne-Kal came to the edge of the Northroot holdings looking for ground to take. Brynja stood in front of the nearest holding and did not move. He read her where she stood, her reserves and her stamina and the fracture points under her plate, and found a defence whose stamina climbed the longer it held. He turned away. He took his field elsewhere without driving Mourne into that ground, and no blow was exchanged. The holding behind her was not entered. She has no way to know whether it stood because of her or because of a calculation she never saw. She did not count it.

The oath is the one thing her shield does not cover. If it were ever compromised, the fight would be inside her, where no plate reaches. She has not been forced to break it. Nobody knows what would become of the vow built into her if she ever were, instead of dying to keep it.

## Lore · The Life Behind the Card

*Written 2026-09-24 by the character-lore pass from this card and the record of The Curia at Altherion. What those sources do not state is new here.*

### Origin

Casimir Aldous Alleyne was born in the Inner World to the Alleyne name. His mother's father was Bertrand Aurel, who kept a bench on the upper floor of an old apothecary building under a private charter no Guild office had ever licensed. The boy was at that bench long before anyone wrote him down as an apprentice. He learned stoppers and resin, the pour of hot glass, the hand balance with its pans on their chains.

Bertrand taught him to refine by taking away. Handed a clouded tincture, Casimir drew off whatever did not belong until the thing itself stood alone in the flask, and he put nothing in to help it. He learned to see a reagent's purity by looking at it. He seldom weighed anything twice.

Bertrand called him Cas. Nobody else was allowed to.

He learned the wait as well. He would stand over a reaction until every variable he could name had resolved, and the people who waited on him took it for coldness. His grandfather never did.

### The Making

His parents died in their bed in the hours before dawn on the fourteenth day. Across the Inner World that night bonds were ending out of a black sky, thirty-five in every hundred, and his mother and father were two of them. Nothing in the room could account for it. Casimir was in the house.

He grieved at the bedside, and the Aether in him forced its way through an aperture far too wide for the soul that had to carry it. It was his first contact with a Wellspring current, and it ran the wrong way, an inversion. The Coagulatio reflex sealed the breach before it could open him further. It took his right eye in the sealing.

He took the stoppered vial from the table by the bed when Bertrand brought him upstairs as ward and apprentice both.

From that night he read decay in whatever lay near him, the rot in a floorboard or the turn in a reagent, as plainly as a page. He resented that he could.

That season the balance on Bertrand's bench would not settle. Every scale-pan on every chain in four worlds had come level at once and afterward would not agree with itself. Casimir weighed the same salt over and over and never got one answer twice.

The Guild Accord's Alchemia Division logged the incident as a Wellspring proximity event and assigned no fault. The entry went into his file regardless and stayed there.

### The Cost

What followed the inversion was a scar that learned to hold. He went no further. Opening the seal was the one door forward he had, and he spent every year after keeping it shut.

The reflex that saved his life kept him from grieving enough to go on. When feeling overran him, whatever his hand touched sealed. Glass set mid-pour on Bertrand's bench. Runs of work ruined themselves in the flask. Each unbidden firing pushed the pressure nearer a release he could not steer, one that would transmute whatever lay closest, his own body included.

So he kept the pressure in on purpose. A palm laid flat could lock a surface: a door would not open, a compound would not react, a wound would not bleed further. The seal came strongest when he was least steady. While he sealed he could transmute nothing, and anyone quick enough to reach him first found him with nothing else in hand.

He believed nothing should leave his hands until he was sure of it. That belief kept him alive at the bedside. It also kept him from ever being sure enough to open the one thing that would let him go on.

### Where They Stand

He works on Bertrand's upper floor under the unlicensed charter, an apprentice transmuter and a pre-registered candidate of the Alchemia Division. The Division's flag on him is passive, a flag and no warrant. Its filing calls him the Ashen Hand, and he has earned no other name. Under hard strain a line shows ash-white through the skin of his open palm.

He wears his grandfather's coat. Decades of Bertrand's wear have left a faint Coagulatio in the cloth that props his seal up a little. He has never acknowledged it. He knows. A lens sealed to the bone of the closed orbit is set to contain and nothing more. When the refining pressure runs high, impressions still surface through the closed eye. His steel-banded case holds measured seeds and resin and the vial from the bedside, unopened.

Bertrand is his anchor. With the old man in the room, the compression stays steady. If Bertrand dies before Casimir ascends, the risk of inversion climbs sharply. Both of them know it. Neither has said it.

Nobody knows whether the line in his palm is damage or a door. Nobody knows what it would cost him to open the closed eye honestly, before an accident opens it for him.

- **Verinus VII · The Palatine** · debt. Casimir's mother and father were two of the thirty-five in every hundred Verinus let go at the root of the wound, and neither man knows the other's name.
- **Dabney Corvalen Olyss · The Thorn-Petaled** · the same day. Casimir lost both parents before the dawn of the day Dabney lost his father, and the two have not met.
- **Guild Accord Alchemia Division** · distant observer. It logged his inversion with no fault assigned and keeps a passive flag on his pre-registration.

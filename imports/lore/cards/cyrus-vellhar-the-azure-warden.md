## Lore · The Life Behind the Card

*Written 2026-09-24 by the character-lore pass from this card and the record of Road-folk and the Eastward Isles. What those sources do not state is new here.*

### Origin

Cyrus Vellhar was born human, on a coast, to people who worked the shallows. A leaning toward water lay latent in him. A reflex came with it, and it showed on the rocks at low water, where he shifted his weight with a wave before it broke, and in the shallows, where he leaned into the drag before it began to pull.

The Archive of Eastward Islands took him as an apprentice while he was still a boy. It kept memory in pages and in shells. Its apprentices went to the water first.

### The Making

The Archive taught him tides before it taught him anything else. He learned set and drift, flood and ebb and the slack before the turn, and he learned to read a current from the shore before he waded into it. Then his teachers turned that reading on people. He came to take a room's mood as he took a current, from outside and ahead of the person who brought it in, and he began to read a room before he entered it. Everything the Archive taught him afterward was that same reflex, pointed at something that could lie back.

The dream-vision training came later, at the Archive's Dream-Tideglass. With it came the memory-currents. Apprentices sat with the Archive's memory shells and listened for what the shells still kept.

On the night the Archive's bound leviathan echo came loose, the senior archivists were at the echo. An archivist, Liorien Nael-Teth, had thrown his charm-papers over it in a half-dome and was holding it there while the seniors bound it again. Cyrus was elsewhere in the Archive, sitting with an Abyssal-touched memory shell.

It whispered. He answered it. It answered him back.

That exchange formed his first genuine Spirit Axis bond. The bond he formed was to the shell, an attachment to something that answered him, and nothing required the other end to be safe. Around him ripples ran out of true, sound warped, and every illusion in reach sharpened. The Laughing Shoal formed where he sat. Because the shell had answered him, he told no one about it. Before the seniors were done with the echo, he took a shard of the Dream-Tideglass and fled the Archive. The half-dome recorded what crossed its pages, and Cyrus had never been inside it.

### The Cost

He left his teachers, the glass he had trained at and the shells he had been set to hear, and he kept one shard. The Archive went on keeping a record of him, a formally unreliable one. It stayed loosely friendly toward him all the same, and it sent people to bring him back. He kept ahead of them.

Water decided his road. The Hydrowave Coil drew on standing water or heavy, wet air, and dry ground starved it before it formed. He kept to coasts and wet country for that reason, and those were the roads where the Archive's people looked for him.

Essence leaked out of him unevenly. When he was shaken, the weather near him turned, and the mood of the room turned with it. When he drove the Coil too far, the backlash came as an inversion: his focus went to apathy and his intent to drift. The Laughing Shoal went where he went. Unsteady people near it tipped into fear or into a giddy euphoria, and none of them toward calm. Anyone he took hold of with the Undertow found old recollections coming up uninvited.

He did not strike to end things. Against a raider band he flooded the field with illusory tidal doubles until not one of the raiders could tell which to hit. They stopped there.

In the coastal towns he passed through, he taught children small illusions and took no pay for it. He was gone before a town knew him well. In one of those towns Liorien Nael-Teth came from the Archive asking after him when he had already left. The children showed the archivist what the drifter had taught them, and told him about it honestly. Cyrus never saw him. The illusions stayed with the children.

### Where They Stand

Cyrus Vellhar is an independent Wellspring drifter. He claims no Guild Accord rank. He claims no ground either, and wants none. He is called the Azure Warden, the Wavesong Trickster, the Tide-Borne Savant and the Laughing Undertow. He wears the Shoal-Weave Sash, which carries his reading of people further out than he could reach bare, and he keeps the Tideglass Shard on him.

He explains everything, cheerfully, to anyone who asks, and the explanation usually sends them the wrong way. He says it himself: "Calm waters lie. Waves tell the truth."

The Archive still sends people after him. The bond to the shell has not broken. What the Abyssal-touched shell actually said, and whether it is still listening for an answer, nobody knows.

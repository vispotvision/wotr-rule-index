## Lore · The Life Behind the Card

*Written 2026-09-24 by the character-lore pass from this card and the record of The Citadel's high seats. What those sources do not state is new here.*

### Origin

Drakvor was born in the Veyrn marches, of the giant-blooded Colossian stock that manned their northern strongholds. The strongholds stood apart on their ridges. Each kept its own walls and answered for them to nobody.

He grew tall early. He was broad soon after, and as soon as he could lift a facing stone he was set to lifting them. Every spring the frost heaved the courses out of line, and the men of the wall reset them by hand before the thaw softened the ground under the footings. He learned where a wall gives first. It gives at the corner over soft ground, above the drain, beside the gate. The marches lay at the far end of the Accord's wire. What the wire brought came late.

### The Making

He took service in the Accord's arms. The lines he was given were the ones other companies had left, and he held them. He was slow on his feet. He won his exchanges by still standing in the same place after everything had struck him, and the men beside him learned to fall into step without an order.

The Withering reached the marches while he served. The wire stopped short of them, and the Accord went on governing the strongholds in name alone. A tribunal sat in the Concord Citadel to rule on the Accord's undertaking to defend them. It held that the undertaking had lapsed where the wire no longer reached, and it ordered the Accord's defenders brought out of the marches. Argen Vael'thar, the Prime Arbiter, sealed the verdict, and the order to carry it north went to Drakvor.

He heard it read in the hall. He called the verdict cowardice, and he would not carry it. He walked out. Nobody in the hall could stand until he had gone. The walk resolved the contradiction he had brought into the hall, and his first true Domain dates from the exile that followed.

### The Cost

He went north into the marches the verdict had left. The wire did not reach him there. He united the fractured strongholds by standing in them, one after another, and by a shared Bastion Chain that bound their walls into one defence. Then he traded that strength back to the Accord for frontier reforms. Everything he had raised in exile went to the institution whose verdict he had walked out on.

Holding was paid for in the body. When he swore to hold a line, a gate or a people, he did not fall until the oath was fulfilled or broken. He was struck by weapons made to erase walls. He set his stance deeper. Afterward the strain came back into his bones and into the plates of the Aegis Rib harness, and cracks opened in him. He could keep his bed for weeks.

At the Gatefront Concord he held a triple-Gate flicker long enough for other Domains to align.

### Where They Stand

The Council nominated him to the Heart Vault. Drakvor is High Concordant of the Guild Accord, Marshal of the Bastion Legion and Warden of the Veyrn Marches. His soul is woven into the Heart Vault of the Concord Citadel, and his breath is part of what keeps the Citadel's glow, as every High Concordant's was before him. Argen Vael'thar, who sealed the verdict Drakvor walked out on, answers to the High Concordant alone.

When the Council found no form for the foundling Aethryn, Lyss Arvale set the Grand Seer's answer down whole in the Council Spire. Drakvor cast the judgement. The Accord would not induct the boy, would not classify him and would not leave him. The decree became law as he spoke it.

His loyalty runs to the Realms and the civilians behind the walls first, the Codex second and politics last. He will not abandon noncombatants or lower-ranked defenders. Every patient enemy eventually finds that out. He avoids altering a city's heart Wellsprings without writ from a Neutral Chamber and the Grand Concord. Once he has braced a stand, only a trusted second can argue him into a feint.

A cyan Concord sigil turns over his right eye. It brightens when he braces a gate or sets a line. The Crown Breaker Blade was forged from the melted crests and regalia of fallen warlords.

Nobody knows why the Accord let a man who once walked out on it hold the one office it cannot function without. He may yet become the Accord's first full Domain Lord of his kind, or fall in a stand absolute enough that later maps simply call the ridge Drakvor's Wall.

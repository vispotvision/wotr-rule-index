## Lore · The Life Behind the Card

*Written 2026-09-24 by the character-lore pass from this card and the record of Old World roads and chains. What those sources do not state is new here.*

### Origin

Freda Thunn-Gorr was born Freda Vaal-Kess, on the Vaal-Kess holding in the shelf islands of the Hobgoblin Expanse. The Vaal-Kess are one of the Expanse's old civilian lines, and none of them ever entered the Crimson Order. Their Stone-Blood runs warm. She came out the deep brown of dark-fired clay, with gold eyes where the Order's are brass. No Legion Etchings marked her. Nothing on her body recorded a unit or a rank.

The shelf islands were the working backbone of the Expanse, all forges, shipyards, obsidian quarries and sulphur works. The parliament sat among them on Vesh-Undaal, where every motion the Keth-Gorrum had ever passed was cut into the western cliff. She grew up in a country that governed itself by argument and kept the argument in stone. The Vaal-Kess were Hobgoblin, and they were not soldiers.

She wore her black hair loose from girlhood, unbound in the civilian way of the shelf-island women, so that it moved when she turned her head. The Order likes everything locked in place.

She talked. They met at fourteen, on the shelf docks: Krothar Thunn-Gorr of Gorr-Keth, a Crimson Order boy who had not yet spoken his first words outside the Vow of Silence, and a Vaal-Kess girl who talked enough for both of them. The courtship ran six years, short by Hobgoblin standards. Exactly one aunt called it scandalous.

### The Making

She chose him knowing what the Crimson Order makes of its men. By the time they married he fought with chains, and a man who fights with chains goes where the contracts are. From her wedding on she wore an ear-drop Krothar had carved for her out of bone-white volcanic glass from the shelf-island quarries. Neither of them has ever said what the shape is.

She left the shelf islands for the Thunn-Gorr holdings on Gorr-Keth. Then he went out. His contracts took him to the Old World's western quarter, the New World's eastern arc, the Inner World frontier and the edge-territories of the Outer World.

The papers came to her. She learned pay schedules, guild insurance, contract terms and repatriation clauses. That is the paper that routes a Hobgoblin mercenary's earnings past his employer's ledger and home to his family. His contracts crossed her desk one after another. Every one of them ran to its term, and his pay came home on her papers.

The understanding came slowly, somewhere between her twentieth and thirtieth year, and no single departure brought it. She had chosen a man who would always leave. The choice had been hers. Neither he nor the world had made it for her. She did not ask him to stop. She built a life that ran whether he was standing in it or not, and she built it well enough that it became the thing he came back to.

### The Cost

The life she built was paper and a seat. She became the administrative anchor of the Thunn-Gorr family's shelf-island constituency seat in the Keth-Gorrum. She kept its correspondence, its constituent records and its census work, and the plain business of representation that goes on whether the delegate is in the room or not. The shelf-island constituents came to trust her above whoever the delegate nominally was. Everyone acknowledged it. Nobody fixed it. Twice the Keth-Gorrum's records division tried to hire her, and she stayed with the seat.

She made Krothar's first olive scarf, thick wool against the weather and the snap of a chain. He wore out that one and the ones after it. She never remarked on the rate.

Their son was born on Gorr-Keth, and she raised him there while his father was on contract. He was born green, the green of lichen on wet stone, and the Crimson Order noticed. She watched him train in the caldera yard with his father's chains. At seventeen he passed his father's ceiling. She watched it happen, and so did Krothar. They did not discuss it. They have not discussed it since. Then the boy left the Expanse with his father's chain technique, a skin every Order Hobgoblin could read at a glance, and his mother's habit of waiting.

Whatever grew in him came to need chains of a different kind, and his father forged them. She watched that from a distance she could not close. In the year the suppressor chains were applied, Krothar told her, "I did not teach him what comes after." She heard it once and has never repeated it. In the caldera yard that year, to no one, she said, "The discussion is the chains."

She has never asked Krothar to stay. Asking would admit that the house requires him, and the house she built does not require him. It functions, and it functions with her alone inside it. When he came home she took the return in without moving anything, and when he left again she went on. She has watched him resettle the chains on his shoulders ten thousand times. Anger shortens her sentences. Hurt stops them entirely. Everyone in her life has learned to recognise the stopping, and nobody has learned to fix it.

### Where They Stand

Freda lives on Gorr-Keth, in the Thunn-Gorr holdings on the caldera's western shelf. She carries a satchel of correspondence, seal-wax, constituent records and census ledgers the way her husband carries chains, always and without thinking about it. The seat's letters still go out in her hand. When she listens she tilts her head a little to the left, and the ear-drop catches the light. People in a room with her grow steadier than they were. Nobody has ever tested why.

Her son's room is kept. The chains he trained with as a boy still hang on the caldera-yard wall. They do not need dusting. She dusts them.

He does not write. He does not visit. He goes by Veylshroud, a name that is not hers. She knows his reasons and does not agree with them, and she waits. She wants him home long enough to use his name, and she expects nothing longer than that.

He is in the Scourge of Hell on contracted bounty work, in the shackles his father forged, and hairline fractures now show in the metal. He has not contacted the man who made them. The quiet place he wants when the work is finished is Gorr-Keth, and he has not named it. The shape of the ear-drop is still unsaid. The one time the boy asked about it, she and Krothar looked at each other and changed the subject at the same instant.

## Lore · The Life Behind the Card

*Written 2026-09-24 by the character-lore pass from this card, the six archived scenes it appears in, and the record of The Kōkan expedition's hands. What those sources do not state is new here.*

### Origin

Heisuke was born on the northern frontier, above the treeline. The frontier made him the way it made everything it kept, short and heavy through the frame. He went into the border guard young.

Over the years of his service a genealogy in black lines came to run up his left forearm from wrist to elbow. It marked his service and his kills. It also carried the names of the men he failed to bring home.

He learned the border on the northern battlement at Kharven. Muken would stand there for hours and watch the treeline without a word, and every man on that wall understood the watching as the king reading his land. Heisuke was one of those men. Decades of polar wind weathered his dark brown skin to the grain of worked leather. He bound his hair in one heavy knot at the crown, the Moto military style, clear of his eyes and out of any enemy's grip.

### The Making

When Muken's wall was laid out at Kharven-Seat, Heisuke was one of the six men who drove the survey stakes. The ground was frozen so hard that nothing but a maul would put them in. Edward Lambert held the far end of the line. The wall still stands on those stakes.

His sword came from Stannvaard. It was good steel and carried no inscription. He kept it with oil and stone on a schedule he did not break, because he did not trust Aetheric solutions to physical problems.

Then the Tenrai killed Muken, and the kingdom came apart. Few of the border guard lived through what followed. Heisuke did. Around the time of the assassination his face set into the look of a man refusing something, and it did not change again.

Through the exile he held no position in anyone's service. He kept the sword and the names on his arm. At Kharven-Seat, Lambert kept a count of the six who had driven the stakes, and for the last four years of the exile he carried Heisuke in it among the dead.

When the expedition sailed for the polar shelf, Heisuke went aboard as its senior military authority. He did not speak to Sodoku. He would speak when Sodoku required it. He held that a man coming home to claim a throne does not yet have it, that the men around him are not yet his subjects, and that the road between the two is paid in blood and ceremony. From the stern rail he saw the father in the way the son stood at a rail. Once on the crossing Sodoku called his name to fix the order of landfall. Heisuke straightened to readiness and inclined his head one degree.

### The Cost

On the crossing he began to favour his left hand, and he held the rail with his right and told nobody. From the cargo hatch Nella Forgeman marked the hand as one to check before the week was out.

At the rift camp Borin Ironheart offered to inscribe the Stannvaard blade. Heisuke declined. The sword crossed the shelf as plain steel.

On the fifth day of the march north, two Titan-class Droval came onto the shelf. When the first one charged, Heisuke took a single step forward with his hand on the hilt. For forty years that hand had gone to the hilt between a king and whatever came at him. It went there for the king's son. He did not draw. What passed between Sodoku and the beast was a contest of force, and his service did not permit him to break into it. He watched Sodoku stand in front of a thing that should have killed him, the way Muken had stood.

After the Stormfold he did not speak at all. At the north gate of Kharven-Seat, Lambert saw him and said his name. Heisuke's hand came partway to his chest and stopped at the sternum. He lowered it. His mouth did not soften. He did not step back either.

### Where They Stand

He is sixty-one, at Kharven-Seat in the eleventh year after the assassination. His brigandine has worn to the shape of his body over decades, and the sword at his hip is still uninscribed.

In the yard that night, three streets up past the broken fountain, a lamp burned in an upper window. It burned oil in a city that burned tallow. A hand covered it quickly and it went out. Heisuke saw it. His chin came up a quarter of an inch, and his hands stayed where they were. He marked the house: third past the fountain, second floor, left of the chimney. He meant to learn who lived there before he slept, and he told no one he meant to.

No record says what he found there. None gives his first word to Sodoku, or says whether Nella has looked at the hand.

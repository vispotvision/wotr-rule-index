## Lore · The Life Behind the Card

*Written 2026-09-24 by the character-lore pass from this card, the thirteen archived scenes it appears in, and the record of The Curia at Altherion. What those sources do not state is new here.*

### Origin

Kaalabad was the name the tellers settled on. They could not pronounce Kirishima Hae-jin, and the folk name became the only one most of the Inner World ever heard. He was born to the Kirishima house, a line older than Sum-gol's incorporation, which the province's later arrangement was built over and never descended from.

Nobody alive remembered him before the Fall. Every folk telling agreed on one thing. He had been the knight other knights measured themselves against, with no taste for cruelty and no hunger for a crown, and he held that an oath sworn could not be unsworn. That certainty was the one thread that came through the Fall whole.

Something cracked him first. The tale never named it.

### The Making

Azerack found him already cracked and gave him the Halberd of Zarakal. The weapon agreed with what was rotting in him. His Realm spread across Sum-gol and the Ketsuen frontier from a single point of failure until the failure was the whole territory, and the Tower of Zarakal rose at its centre. When the Realm drew back it left the Kept Vigil, still drawing on a debt nobody living had incurred. A joint working of the Accord and Sanctum Lux chained him, at a price neither body fully disclosed to the other. The chains outlasted every kingdom that helped forge them.

He said later that he had been put somewhere quiet. The quiet came back out with him. It sat behind his teeth all day. It stopped in one hour only, the hour when there was blood on the floor and somebody in the room had understood what was happening to them, and he called that what he had instead of appetite. Kujo had brought him back. In Kaalabad's words, Kujo gave him the ground to stand on and the leave to be seen standing on it.

What came back was very large and wrong in the proportions. The arms ran a knuckle long. The helm sat a few degrees off true. His voice came out strangled, each word pushed past something still in the way.

Beneath Vaeloris he stood beside Corwin Sesk while the contractor reported, and when Sesk talked past the point of use, Kaalabad turned his head and looked at him. Sesk's chest stopped. For about four seconds the floor had a claim on him. Then Kaalabad set the halberd butt on the black stone and folded both hands over the head. "My lord," he said. "Let me expand your domain." He asked for the Mahuo first, "What is left of them," then the Valorin, and last the Zettari, when Kujo was ready. He wanted it established once, before witnesses who would run, that a Mahuo determination does not stop a body that has decided to keep moving. "I want to be the thing that continues." He asked that the killing be entered as a wage. A thing given ought to be used, he said, or the giving was an error.

Kujo gave him discretion because he wanted to know what happened when one of his generals was forced into conditions Kujo had not personally arranged.

### The Cost

He went to Sum-gol himself. The tunnel took eleven paces and he counted them, and on the seventh the seal at his throat began to tighten, as it always did on the seventh. He had never told anybody about the seventh pace. A price known, he held, is a price that can be waited out. Four hundred faces came up, and he found no pleasure anywhere in himself. Men assumed otherwise. He had stopped correcting them.

The Star Crusher went down on sixty roofs and a teaching house, and a barrier came up off the valley floor and declined it. He read the braid in under a second, Valorin work and Mahuo work forced together in a hurry. The seal clamped shut, and for six seconds he could not draw air. He knew the draws left in him were finite. The village lived. Its road did not, and the teaching house's yard wall went down under the falling pines with people beneath it.

At Altherion he came down a tunnel in the sky on the man who had left the cell. Aurelian stopped the halberd on two fingers. He flicked it, and the halberd went out of Kaalabad's grip, through two columns and to the beard in the cathedral's west footing, ninety yards off. Kaalabad looked at his open hands, then at the colonnade, then at the man in front of him. He did not leave. He turned on Charles Lambert, who slipped four deliveries with empty hands, and put a knee into him and threw him. He went up the tunnel slowly, empty right hand last.

On the Ashgate road he rode at the head of the Tenrai column and spoke to nobody. At the ninth hour the aurora emptied into him and he drew Zarakal from a seam in the air. Six hundred men turned. "You are the traitor's traitor," he told Wren Greymane. Wren made him pay for every step. He put a road on top of him and drove steel into his throat, and when Kaalabad pinned him to the ditch bank he blew himself apart to make one last opening. Kaalabad rose from the mud and knelt on the broken road with the shaft across his knees, doing nothing, which nobody had seen him do. His right hand had swollen purple inside the gauntlet.

Kwon Mu-jin came to the narrows with his right arm hanging, taken, Kaalabad said, in an orchard at Sum-gol. Kaalabad called him by another name, and the schoolmaster gave him his own. He believed nothing could bind him. He brought the pull inward, and Mu-jin seized its structure, rebound its centre and collapsed it along its own governing axis. Nine fists threw Kaalabad up the road. He landed on his boots, still holding the shaft.

He had come to think his thirst was simply what a man of his kind is. In the flooded narrows the gauntlet fell away from a hand that worked again, and he found the thirst had come from the districts, every starved main he had drawn on since he was nineteen.

### Where They Stand

He put Zarakal through the schoolmaster's chest. Then Open Crucible assayed the whole road by ownership, and everything he had borrowed slid off him in sheets. What is left on him is smaller, and all of it is his. No record has him fight with it yet.

The card leaves him alive and advancing after injuries that should have ended him. Kujo watched from far off. He kept his hand lowered, because a general he rescued would be an extension of his own caution. He measures his general by whether Kaalabad can continue functioning once the world has turned on him. Over the narrows he said, "Now justify surviving them."

The Accord still files him as the Chained Crown. The Tower of Zarakal still stands at the centre of the old Realm, and the Kept Vigil still draws on its old debt. Sesk heard a gap in the list of three houses and told nobody; no record names what belonged in it.

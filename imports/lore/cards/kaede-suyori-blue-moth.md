## Lore · The Life Behind the Card

*Written 2026-09-24 by the character-lore pass from this card and the record of The Star Chamber's Protocol Corps. What those sources do not state is new here.*

### Origin

Kaede Suyori was born at Halcrest, the gate city of Nalūn, where the salt beds thin against the northern rim and goods are weighed and classified before they go deeper. Their mother kept a ward in the quarantine station. The held lay in small pale chambers cut with bedding planes, and the vents drew the night air through. Moths came in on it to the ward lamp.

Kaede caught them in folded paper. At dawn they let them go at the vents.

At night they sat with the feverish while their mother slept. A frightened stranger breathed fast and shallow. Kaede breathed low and even beside the bed, on a count of five, until the stranger's breath followed theirs. They learned to take a wrist before they spoke.

### The Making

A caravan came up the corridor road with fever in it, and Halcrest held every soul who had travelled with it. From the yard the held could see the gate. When a carter died in Kaede's ward, the others broke from their chambers and filled the yard, and the gate garrison drew staves.

Kaede walked out between them. They kept their hands low. They breathed on the count and did not hurry it.

Where Kaede stood, heat left the air, and panic forgot its reasons. The men nearest them lowered their staves first. The held went back to their chambers without being told, and nobody in the yard could say afterward why it had started.

### The Cost

The carter's widow had not gone back. She stood in the yard with the ward's lamp hook in both hands. Kaede took her by the wrist and held on while her fury cooled, the way silk holds around a bruise. She let go of the hook. Kaede lay the next nights in a chamber of their own ward, burning. Their mother sat with them. They lived. From that yard on, Kaede said that the heat went into them and that they carried it away.

Word of the yard reached the Star Chamber. Lord Lucen Vale seated Kaede at his side for the councils he arranged, and at Altherion he arranged one in the River Crown Council's chamber, at a round table with no head. Kaede sat by him with moths in their sleeves. Cassiel Asterion stood at the meridian. Kaito Akagiri came as Arbitration's liaison, matched the breathing of three delegates and led them half a step faster, and no voice in the chamber rose. The room reached for a larger yes than either delegation had come to give. Cassiel's line cut it down to terms both would sign. The heat stayed, and Kaede cooled it.

They had watched Kaito move the council like illness moving through a city. They loved him for his precision. When the chamber emptied, Kaede walked out beside him, and afterward they showed him how to leave a pulse in a throat where he had left a tremor.

Kaito burned his writ and sold outcomes for the Black Concord. Kaede tied a blue ribbon to his sheath, among the red cords and their memory beads. It meant "I will follow even here." He bent lines, and they mended them so no one saw the crease. The decisions he sold stood in rooms Kaede had cooled.

### Where They Stand

Kaede is called Blue Moth: court lepidopterist, hush-healer and social countermeasure, the only person who can cool a fevered room after Kaito warms it. They wear night-indigo robes brushed with pale blossoms and a ribboned moth at one ear. Paper-thin moths stray from their sleeves and circle back. Kaede has never hurt anyone and could not.

Since Altherion, Lucen has seated Kaede in the rooms where Kaito is expected. There they let the argument run and go after his worst impulses. A touch to his wrist means enough. A moth lit on his shoulder means take the long way.

Others send for Kaede when the air goes wrong. Khasir Aluto needs them first when a room runs too hot for his lines. They mediate at the confession rites of Deimos, the Sin Eater, and hold his balance when his resonance threatens collapse. After a hard room Kaede sits apart with open hands and breathes on the count until what they took has cooled.

The ribbon is still on his sheath.

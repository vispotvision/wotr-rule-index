## Lore · The Life Behind the Card

*Written 2026-09-24 by the character-lore pass from this card, the thirteen archived scenes it appears in, and the record of Hild's council: the Kōkan Loyalist Coalition. What those sources do not state is new here.*

### Origin

Lorn Stark was born into House Stark of the Eastern March, the border house that holds the Ironwood March and the eastern frontier from its seat at Hollenmark. His sister was Fern. Zaehaerys, who came to hold Hollenmark as Warden, was his cousin. The Starks had been the first house to take Muken's alliance and the only one to take it undefeated, and the children of the house were taught to say plainly where a line ran.

The sword came to him slowly. He drilled in the Hollenmark yard with the other Stark boys and was never quick, and nobody who taught him claimed he would be. He went out to the drill every morning anyway. He carried a plain sword and kept it better than it deserved: oiled, the edge dressed, the grip rewound whenever it wore through. In that court he learned to stay on his feet until a report was finished. He kept the habit at every table afterward.

Fern was built thinner, moved between houses more easily, and could talk to the southern courts in their own register without losing herself. When the Warden wanted the Moto studied, he sent her south. Lorn stayed in the north.

### The Making

Fern came home to the Ironwood March carrying Sodoku Moto's child. The Warden rode to her holdfast and gave the child the name Ice before she was born. Lorn was with his sister there. In a room at the holdfast he made an arrangement with Fern about the girl, and he kept every part of it.

Then Muken was killed and the kingdom came down. The Tenrai set about closing the Kōkan line, and Fern paid their assessors to look elsewhere. Osric came up the road with his father's ring and a secret he kept for three years. He told Fern. Fern told Lorn. Edward Lambert arrived when the girl was four, announced himself as a man who could count, and stayed.

Fern died in the room where the altar stands. Hild was eight years old. She lay on the floor of that room and would not eat, would not speak, would do nothing at all. Lorn walked in, put a wooden practice sword in the child's hand, and said two words: *stand up.*

### The Cost

She got up. Brida made her eat. Lorn taught her every morning after that, the way a man teaches who was never good at the thing himself and knows each place where it resists. When she outgrew the wooden blade he put off the change for as long as he could, because steel meant she was training for something real. Then he gave her steel. It was a short blade, weighted forward, sized for a hand that had not finished growing.

He was her senior uncle and the coalition's military guardian, and when Osric counselled the move to Kharven-Seat he went with her. She sat her grandfather's broken throne with her feet short of the floor. Lorn saw it every time. He never once spoke of it. In front of the court he called her my lady; alone, he called her girl, or child, or Hild.

His own house had not committed to her. The Warden who named her Ice kept his seat at Hollenmark, and Lorn stood at her throne as a Stark regardless. When he gave her the houses, he told her his cousin would sooner starve on the right side of the border than eat on the wrong side. Zaehaerys burned Wren Greymane's invitation.

He and Osric told her gently, in the room behind the throne hall, that her own people called her Hild Ice. When Pietro Ashmore was brought before her in chains, Lorn held the chains. "Kneel," he said. The man would not, so Lorn's knee put him down on the stone. Pietro called the girl a bastard and laughed. She forbade the killing and named herself Hild Ice, and when she looked at her uncle his eyes were wet. He dragged the Ashmore man out of the hall, and the iron scored two lines in the floor.

He said his disagreements in the room, in front of whoever was there. At the long table in the Seat's kitchen, after her father had come north, Lambert set the stores against a draft while somebody dug a gallery toward the north wall. Lorn answered him across the table. He had watched better men than either of them destroyed by saving an objection for the corridor, he said. "You are counting bushels against a siege that has already started digging." He asked what they would do when the ground opened, and Lambert said he did not know. The whole house heard him.

There was one thing Lorn would not say to her. In the empty hall Lambert asked whether she meant to hand over the reins. Lorn never asked. He reckoned that the arrangement with his sister had cost him more than any oath he had sworn to a man with a title. The grey came early at his temples.

### Where They Stand

On the morning of the ninth hour Lorn was on the outer wall from the fourth hour, and he watched the west go white three times. By the seventh hour he had three reports of the enemy's road. They did not agree. "Then two of them are being paid and I would like to know which two." Below him the yard filled with everything the Seat could muster. Sodoku came down the inner stair in harness. He put a hand on the back of his daughter's head and kissed her hair. Lambert came to Lorn's shoulder with orders to ride the Stormfold road with six riders and no banner, and told him to count the road closed if he was not back in five days. Lorn could find no way to say what he wanted that was not embarrassing. He put out his hand. Lambert gripped it once and let go.

Lorn stayed on the wall until the last of the column was through the gate. Then he went down to the yard, picked the broken circlet off the mounting block where Hild had set it and forgotten it, and put it in his belt.

He is dead. His stone stands in the upper burying ground at Kharven-Seat beside Hild's and Osric's, cut in the same week by the same mason. It says he made an arrangement about that girl and kept every part of it. The year after, when the Tenrai remnant came north, the Seat took them in, and the record of that year sets down that Lorn Stark would have refused them. Lambert had the lettering on all three stones cut to one depth.

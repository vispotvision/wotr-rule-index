## Lore · The Life Behind the Card

*Written 2026-09-24 by the character-lore pass from this card and the record of The Citadel's high seats. What those sources do not state is new here.*

### Origin

Lyss Arvale was born human in the High March of Eresse, in the New World, of Eressean line. No record names her parents.

In the March a deposition was read aloud by two jurists in alternation, the second there to catch the first. A misplaced word was caught by ear. The pitch of a voice did the work of grammar: a rising voice left a matter open, and a falling one closed it. She learned to hear which way a sentence would fall before it fell.

The Choir-ward took her as an initiate. She fell into another singer's breathing without being shown how, and two voices on one breath carried further and no louder. The ward taught her the staff by Eressean court fencing, where every guard line answers to a spoken sound. She called each guard before she took it.

### The Making

She entered the Guild Accord's Arbitration Division and was posted to its Neutral Chambers as a junior Oath Witness. In her first years there she stood witness at sittings of a tribunal of twelve. She kept the exact words of every hearing. When she drew a vowel long, a falsehood spoken near her warbled, and the speaker heard it warble. Crowds lowered their voices around her without deciding to. Children stepped closer. Liars stepped back.

At Graybridge her testimony saved the guilty to spare the innocent. She kept the whole truth and cut it into two mercies, one public and one sworn. What woke in her there was a corridor of hush, where a truth could be set down without being dropped. The Chamber gave her the first white feather of her collar. Graybridge also left a crack in her that flared whenever she withheld a truth too long. The lesson she took was clarity without cruelty.

### The Cost

At Stormgate she held a corridor in typhoon winds, and a battalion withdrew in order without a shouted word. Everybody inside it heard. Nobody inside it was made to obey. Her cut parted rope, straps and banner poles, and she kept it from killing. In the Sänger's Duel she out-sang a glamoured demagogue and returned his lies to him as choking wind.

She paid in her throat and her ears. Over-cutting blanched her voice. When she took in more clamour than she vented, her ears bled, and on long campaigns she lay awake hearing what had been said and what should have been said. Each time she held a truth back to keep a corridor gentle the crack flared, and a truth she cut through without accepting it could take her voice entirely. The Iron-judges, who held that truth must always burn, became her rivals. The Neutral Scribes worried that she spared feelings.

Once the white-feather warrant made her record admissible, she was sent to witness the twelve again, and in one hearing they cited their own earlier reasoning in words that differed from hers. She set the true words against the claimed ones, sitting by sitting, into the record. The Prime Arbiter heard the twelve himself. Nothing he heard broke, because none of them was lying. The Division stood them down, and their three years of verdicts stayed in the Codex. The Accord filed the Silent Accord Incident with no hand behind it. Vaelthor Ashen-Meridian had sat in the review as senior counsel, and nobody could say afterward what counsel had said. She came away with three years she could prove and a hand she could not find. On sleepless nights she heard the old words and the new.

When the Council Spire found no form for the foundling Aethryn, Argen Vael'thar sent her to the Grand Seer with its point of law. He had watched her memory hold against twelve judges. She put the Council's words to Aurgon Mintruix, and he had her ask again. His price that day was an oath, to carry his answer into the Spire whole, in his words and his order, with nothing softened. She swore it in his presence, where the world cannot forget an oath. In the Spire she set it down as sworn. It named no form for the boy. Drakvor, the High Concordant, cast the judgement, and the Chamber gave her a plume for the oath kept.

### Where They Stand

Lyss Arvale is a junior Oath Witness of the Neutral Chambers, Chamber-cleared, on rotating duty as a travelling magistracy escort. She holds the white-feather warrant, with three perjury reversals credited to her record. The Aerial Ward's couriers, who call her "wind's conscience," fly her between tribunals. Beneath her collar of kept oaths she wears slate and white, border-stitched with swan and surge.

The Graybridge feather is lost. Someone leaves it at crime scenes. Nobody knows who, or what they are buying with her compassion.

A Chamber edict offers her the Swan's Turn if she signs away the right to soften truths. One of the Abynthean cantors whispers to her of a merciful silence that heals by forgetting. She has not signed. She is working to mend the crack without hardening into cruelty.

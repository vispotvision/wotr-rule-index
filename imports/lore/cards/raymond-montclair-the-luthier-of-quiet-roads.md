## Lore · The Life Behind the Card

*Written 2026-09-24 by the character-lore pass from this card and the record of Road-folk and the Eastward Isles. What those sources do not state is new here.*

### Origin

Raymond Montclair was born mortal, of Verdant-flecked ancestry, in one of the border villages whose roads he walks now. His father kept a bench at the back of the house. He made and mended instruments for the villages along the road: fiddles, reed pipes, and a chapel's strings when a chapel could pay for them. The ashwood lute that carries the family's name came off that bench.

His father taught him by ear. The boy tapped a soundboard, listened for the place where it rang dull, and planed there. He learned to close a seam and wait on the glue, and to stop a crack before it ran along the grain.

Guild Accord escorts came through with the merchant trains. They kept the road. They never stopped for a village's quarrels.

### The Making

The quarrel that ended his father's bench began as a debt between two households. For a season it stayed in words. One night it came into the lane with knives. His father went out to speak to both sides, and he was killed there before it burned out.

Raymond had been told to stay in the workshop. He stayed. He took the ashwood lute down and played one note. He held it, because his hands would do nothing else, and the note steadied him until morning. By then the lane was quiet.

Afterwards he took the road with a case of his father's tools and a few keepsakes. He walked with the Accord's escorts wherever they would have him. He went toward a quarrel while it was still words. Near him, when he played, a frightened man stopped shaking, and a working about to misfire in a nervous hand held true. He never pushed. He sat in a room or at a roadside and let the note out slowly, and the people there steadied one at a time. Every step he made in the craft came to him through steadying somebody else first.

His first true bond formed on the road, and it formed with the ashwood lute, the thing that had steadied him first. The lute keeps what it has felt the way other bonds keep a face.

### The Cost

The lute still keeps that night.

Each playing blurred his own memories at the edges. Too much of it dulled joy and grief together, and it made no choice between them. His father's voice was the first thing he could no longer call back. The gouge from the bench stayed in the case with the other tools, and the case kept for him what he no longer carried alone.

At the Hollow Bridge he held the note for three hours. Nobody on either side drew a blade. Every hour past the comfortable length was paid out of his memory.

He never used the lute to hurt anybody. He never tried to learn how. He took no sponsor and swore to no crown or order. He left each village before it could thank him properly, or ask him which side had been in the right. He never counted the people he steadied, and nobody asked him to.

### Where They Stand

Raymond Montclair walks the border roads in the late Imperial Age with whatever Guild Accord escort will have him. The escorts tolerate him and sponsor nothing. He is useful to them and inconvenient in roughly equal measure. Near him, drivers stay calm. He also leaves the road for any quarrel he hears of, and the escort waits or goes on without him.

The border villages know him as the man who shows up before the worst of a dispute and leaves before anyone thinks to thank him properly. He is also called the Ash-Tone Pilgrim and the Soft-Handed Witness. Asked a direct question about his craft, he answers it. He does not offer the explanation.

At Burnt Orchard he restored the chapel's instrument. In the restoring he halted a local Wellspring flare that nobody else had noticed building. He said nothing of it and went on with the escort. The instrument still keeps what it felt, longer than an instrument otherwise keeps anything, and a Pathwarden of the Accord who played in that chapel afterwards heard it there. The two men never met.

How much of his own grief he has already flattened out of himself, keeping other people steady, nobody knows.

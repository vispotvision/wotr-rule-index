## Lore · The Life Behind the Card

*Written 2026-09-24 by the character-lore pass from this card, the eight archived scenes it appears in, and the record of The Kōkan expedition's hands. What those sources do not state is new here.*

### Origin

Sadamu was born on the Polar Reach, into a frontier cadet line of the Moto. Food there was scarce and nobody stayed in one place for long. He grew up lean and long in the limb. While the cold drove the other children to the fire, he went about in a light coat over nothing.

On the Reach a word cost breath, and breath cost heat. The old people of his camp spoke little. When a call had to cross the ice they sent it low, from the belly, because a shout spent heat and the wind tore it apart. He learned that voice before he learned any trade. He learned the frontier oath the same way, by hearing it sworn. It was two words long.

He was named Sadamu at his naming ceremony. He was barely past it when the Kōkan expedition sailed.

### The Making

He trained as a Moto soldier, and the training cropped his black hair to the skull in the military style. In drill a practice blade crossed his face from one cheekbone to the other. It left a thin scar over the bridge of his nose and taught him the distance his instructors had spent their breath describing.

The scouts of the Reach showed him their watch for open ice. Where the ground gave no cover, a threat could come from any side, so the eyes went over all of it in a fixed pattern and then began again. He carried a Moto field knife, short in the blade and curved forward. It was made for cutting rope and skinning game, and it became a weapon only when everything else had failed.

He took service with the Kōkan expedition as its forward scout and perimeter watch. He was the youngest of its hands. On the crossing he crouched at the bow with a sounding line and called depths back to Garret Longshore at the helm. His voice carried across the wind without a shout. Garret eased the helm two degrees to port and said nothing. He called the next reading.

At the rift camp he kept the watch from the highest igloo's roof, with the field knife across his knees. Soren the Mast kept watch from the same roof. Borin Ironheart had brought his forge onto the ice in a leather roll that weighed more than Sadamu did.

The camp gathered at the rift's edge without a summons. The hands swore to Sodoku and to his brother Sonzai, the Crown and the Commander, each in their own form. Robin Ice swore first, in a paragraph. Garret went fourth. Sadamu went fifth, and he walked to the oath-distance with a scout's long, ranging stride.

"Sadamu," he said. One word, his own name, in the frontier register. Then he said, "I swear." He pressed his fist to his chest, held it for one beat and stepped back. His oath was two words, the shortest oath on the ice.

### The Cost

The others at the rift named what they had to give. Garret offered a wheel held in bad weather, a compass he did not trust and a ship kept alive in water that wanted it dead. Nella swore on her hands and her instruments. Dunstan promised that nobody would go hungry while he had hands and a fire. Each of those oaths ended where the trade ended. Sadamu named no trade and set no end. On the frontier, "I swear" covered whatever was needed, for as long as it was needed, in whatever form the need took. It left him no clause to stand behind. Later at the rift camp, Sodoku made Yoko a promise of two words, "I promise," and it carried the same weight as Sadamu's oath.

When the camp was struck he pulled the watch gear and wrapped the sighting equipment in oilskin. Sonzai took the outer perimeter himself while Sadamu broke down the watch position, and on that walk Sonzai found a woman lying in the snow among wind-bent birches. Sadamu was back on the highest roof when Sodoku walked out of the igloo in glacier-crystal armor. From the roof he pressed his fist to his chest, the frontier salute. Before the column left, Sonzai sent him to search the ground where she had been found, for tracks the wind had not erased and anything she might have dropped.

The column of twenty crossed the polar shelf in a loose formation he had designed and Sodoku had approved. The terrain redesigned it at once.

On the fifth day Yoko smelled two Titan Droval. Sadamu drew his field knife on the scout's reflex and looked at it. The knife was a foot long. The beasts were twenty feet tall. The Reach had taught him that laughter was the one sane answer to odds like those, since it let a man keep hold of the knife, and he laughed. By fifty metres he had worked the odds again. He put the knife away. The knife was an insult.

Heisuke took one step forward with his hand on his hilt and did not draw. Sadamu watched the rest from forty paces back, knife sheathed. Ice bloomed across the Titan's body, and the frontier had given him no word for it. His mouth was open. No sound came out.

### Where They Stand

Sadamu is the Kōkan expedition's forward scout and perimeter watch, and the youngest of its hands. The column that marched in his formation came on to the north gate of Kharven-Seat. He still goes in a light coat over nothing. The field knife hangs at his belt.

He is called Futakoto. His oath runs as the frontier reads it, whatever the brothers need for as long as they need it. He has added nothing to it.

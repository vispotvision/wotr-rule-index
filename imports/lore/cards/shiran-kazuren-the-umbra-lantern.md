## Lore · The Life Behind the Card

*Written 2026-09-24 by the character-lore pass from this card and the record of Gale and still point. What those sources do not state is new here.*

### Origin

Shiran Kazuren was born the middle child of the Kazuren clan. His elder sister, Sairin, was raised from childhood at the Quiet Shrine. At least one more child came after him.

He could keep still as a boy. He kept so still that the grown people in a room forgot he had come in, and they went on talking in front of him. He listened. He learned early what people say when they think no one is listening.

The clan sent him to the Dreamfold Monastery, as it had sent his sister to the Shrine. There he learned to read dreams, and then to read the waking world through them. He learned a blade as well. He learned to hide it.

### The Making

His attention grew outward at the Dreamfold. It passed over the people in front of him to the trouble gathering behind them.

Then the Monastery split. Only he understood why.

In that schism he came up against a force far past anything he could then hold, and he survived it. What broke in him broke along the lines of what he felt. The schism was itself his fracture. Whatever he understood in it was enough to end a monastic life over. He left.

Nobody put him out. He walked out of the Dreamfold on his own feet, and when anyone calls it exile he corrects them.

He came out with the dream-work turned toward other people's trouble. Men who came near him angry found their tempers easing, and the wish to strike went slack in them. Opponents lost track of where he stood and never noticed losing it. Reflections out of dream moved beside him step for step, until an eye could not tell which was the man and which was memory. He began to walk into quarrels before they came to blows.

### The Cost

He has never explained the dream-work to anyone. He came to the understanding that ended his monastic life by himself, and what that cost him he has told no one.

He keeps every gift lower than it could go. He carries the Veilshade Umbrella openly, and the blade inside its layers stays sheathed. Most who know him have never seen it. In a quarrel his voice carries as far as the quarrel needs and no further.

His body was never built to take a blow. He lives by being somewhere else when it lands. The reflections hold only while his attention holds them, and one hit that breaks his concentration drops them all at once. Run together with the step that loses his enemies, they thin his own sense of where he stands. A man who stops watching and listens for him finds him.

He goes to a quarrel before it has turned. A disaster stopped that early leaves nothing behind to show it was ever coming. The people he mediates for know him as the man who prevents the disaster before it starts, and they know nothing else about him.

An exile could name the sentence and the ones who passed it. He left by his own choice. The reason is his to keep, and he keeps it without a witness.

### Where They Stand

Shiran Kazuren is an unaffiliated mediator and a reader of what is real. He carries the umbrella closed.

The latest quarrel he walked into was in the caldera belt of the Accord's chartered arc, in the New World. The ground settled in the night. The Wellspring under one venture's array drifted onto the next claim, and one array went dead while the neighbour's woke. Both ventures kept militias, and both militias carried Onceglass charges. The first venture filed with the Accord. Shiran was in the belt before the filing had gone far, and nobody on either claim had sent for him. He walked between the two yards with his umbrella closed. He sat with each master in turn. While he was there, no charge was thrown.

Some days later Sairin came up the road and took a place on the slope above the claims. Iryen Maevith came with the Accord's filing. He greeted Shiran and spoke of the days before his arrival as if he had watched each one. Shiran did not say how he had known to come. Iryen sang on the drift ground, and inside his tone the militias put up their charges and the masters talked terms. They signed. The neighbour would share the draw for as long as the drift held.

Sairin went down the road the next morning. He did not ask her why she had come, and she did not ask him about the Dreamfold. He has not asked her why the head before her stepped down.

She still watches her younger siblings, sure that a major Wellspring fracture will one day call them together. The drift in the belt was no such fracture. Those who know him believe one strong emotion or one true prophecy would take him further than he has gone. What the schism was, and what understanding it cost him, nobody knows.

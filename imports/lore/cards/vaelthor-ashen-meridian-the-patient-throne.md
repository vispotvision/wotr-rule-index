## Lore · The Life Behind the Card

*Written 2026-09-24 by the character-lore pass from this card and the record of The Citadel's high seats. What those sources do not state is new here.*

### Origin

Vaelthor Ashen-Meridian was born in a district on the Accord's wire, to a father who kept the muster roll. On Irath's day, the Accord's day for musters, his father sat at a table in the hall and entered each man who came to swear. Each said "Ita spondeo" and gave his name. His father set it down in ink. At the end of the table the boy learned the proverb "What has been said stands said."

He kept a list of his own. On it he wrote the men he judged would break their oath before their term was out. When a deserter's name came back to be struck from the roll, he looked for it on his list. He seldom had to add one.

He left the muster table for the acolytes of Irath, the Archon of Dominion, whose day it kept. They taught him Parun. They gave him the edict, "He who commands himself commands the world," and he began with himself. He learned to keep still through a whole day and to say a thing once.

### The Making

Vaelthor served Irath through a war. Three commanders in it had sworn loyalty oaths, and while it was still being fought he used Parun to rewrite all three. He was cast out after it.

He came next to the Guild Accord, whose maxim is "Balance before Dominion." The Arbitration Division took him on as counsel, and in time as its senior counsel, without a vote. Nobody in the Division could afterward say who first put his name forward. In private he founded the Unseen Accord. It kept no roll. Each of its other members came to believe they led it. The Crimson Codex, bound in hide laced with Eclipsium, took down every manipulation he executed and the name of every implant still active. It never left his hand.

A tribunal of twelve heard Concord Trials in one of the Division's Neutral Chambers. Over seven days he talked with its members. When the seven days ended, each of the twelve held an account of right and wrong that differed from the one they had brought into the chamber, and each believed it had always been theirs. They went on sitting. Each verdict they gave went up into the Codex as precedent. He had rewritten the ethical memory of a twelve-person tribunal over seven days, using nothing but conversation, and nobody noticed for three years. The world started running on what he had built without being told.

### The Cost

Then the Oath Witness Lyss Arvale was sent to that tribunal again, and it cited its own earlier reasoning in words that differed from the ones she held. She set the true words against the claimed ones, sitting by sitting. Her record was admissible. The Prime Arbiter, Argen Vael'thar, heard the twelve himself. A lie spoken near him breaks in the air. Nothing broke. None of the twelve was lying. The Division stood them down, and their verdicts stayed in the Codex, because the verdict of a Concord Trial is final. The Accord filed the matter as the Silent Accord Incident and found no hand behind it. Vaelthor sat in the review as senior counsel. Afterward nobody present could say what counsel had said, and he left it knowing the Oath Witness's name.

Of his work he has said, "I simply waited until you betrayed yourself, and then I wrote it down." He built each working to be the last one needed. He spoke its Parun phrase once, at the casting, and never explained it to a listener or to the record. His bonds ran outward through the sigils he implanted. None ran inward through anyone he trusted. If another reader ever opened the Crimson Codex, every implant would release at once and his Crystal would collapse. He did not put it down.

His Crystal took nine hairline fractures. They taught him that he could not hold a long fight, and after that he did almost anything to keep an opponent from learning it.

### Where They Stand

Vaelthor sits as senior counsel to the Arbitration Division without a vote, and the Division believes him loyal. He carries a cane headed in blue Soulquartz. The Codex lives in Argen's memory, section by section, and Argen's health wanes. Vaelthor stands four implants from the vote that would make him the Division's sole living interpreter of Codex law. He counts Seren Valenne, Senior Adjudicator in the Neutral Chambers, among the voting officers between him and it.

When the Concord Council could find no form for the foundling Aethryn, it sent its point of law past its counsel to the Grand Seer, Aurgon Mintruix, once Arbitration's Magister-Seer. Vaelthor has never gone to the Seer's chamber. The Seer reads back the lineage of any relic brought into it. The Crimson Codex does not leave Vaelthor's hand.

He had a daughter. He still knows her name. The face is gone, and he has stopped trying to recall it. Nobody knows what he is counting toward. Nor does anyone know whether he means to reach it.

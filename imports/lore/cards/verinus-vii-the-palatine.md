## Lore · The Life Behind the Card

*Written 2026-09-24 by the character-lore pass from this card, the 15 archived scenes it appears in, and the record of The Curia at Altherion. What those sources do not state is new here.*

### Origin

Verinus VII was born Osric Peyser at Coldwater Steps, a seaward parish of the Holy Sea of Alabaster. His parents had come to that coast as frontier people. They cut peat and set nets. They buried two children in ground they had cleared themselves. Neither parent was ever inside a court. His father weighed salt bars on a dock and counted every stack aloud, so the man across the table could hear him doing it. The parish called the boy the Weigher's Boy.

At nine he came home crying about another boy on the Steps. His mother told him that a person was not what they had done to you, nor what they had been born into, nor what the parish said of them. If he wanted to know what somebody was, he would have to weigh them himself, honestly, and he would probably not enjoy the result. He never found anything in the founding archive that improved on his mother's rule.

### The Making

He went through the seminary fast and tidy, unremarkable in a cohort of forty, and the Ebb Court took him on as a junior Auditor. At twenty-six he weighed the testimony of a woman named Meren against the man who owned her. It was the heaviest testimony he had ever recorded, and it was true.

The procedure of the time required corroboration. There was none. The only other people who had been in the room were also owned. He recorded the weight accurately and entered the finding accurately, and the case failed on procedure. The slaver collected his property at the harbour steps in front of the court, and Verinus watched the woman he had just proved go down the steps ahead of him. He had been correct on the instrument and wrong in the world. It broke something in him along the seam between what he had measured and what had happened. It scarred, and the scar became the most productive thing about him.

### The Cost

He drafted the Holy Sea's position that persons are not commodities at thirty-one. It passed at forty. He never called it a victory. In the same years he wrote the corroboration provision, which holds that a single reading is not a finding, because the instrument sinks a sincere man who is wrong exactly as far as one who is right. It was the same requirement that had lost Meren's case.

At thirty-four he lost both parents inside eleven days, in a coastal fever he had spent two years drafting a containment against. The parishes had adopted it late. He checked the draft afterward and found it sound. The house at Coldwater Steps held a boat and a set of salt scales. For four years he built an account in which the fault was his, because a fault could be corrected.

At forty-four he was present when a boarding action against a trafficking vessel went wrong, and for about four seconds he stopped being a measuring instrument. Eleven died. Nine were the crew, and two were people he had come to get out. He reported himself to the chapter hold of Sanctum Lux in writing that evening, before anyone asked. The finding was lawful use of force in defence of persons. He asked four times for its review.

For forty-five years he took testimony onto his own chest, holding both of the speaker's hands, and it left him with the barotrauma of a deep diver and a blue cast at the lips. Twice, at fifty-one and at sixty-three, he carried another's cost further than the working would bear, and the damage stayed. At fifty-eight he read his own testimony at full depth. It told him he had built a doctrine to keep himself from ever being wrong again. He lay insensible for nine days.

At his investiture he read aloud the lowest figure for force ever entered against a Palatine's name, and asked that it be minuted. He never sat in the Palatine's chair. He never sent the parish the instruction that four Palatines before him had sent, and the fishermen still say Osric. He held that the body which measures must never be the body which decides. Deacon Ilse Barrow called that an elegant permanent excuse in open session. He had no answer. He extended her appointment the following spring. He had two of the chapel windows that carried his likeness taken out.

At Vaeloris the provision came due. The parish deacon there had asked the Court for a chapter detachment, a Sealwright and forty men. Verinus deferred it in writing to wait for corroboration. In the valley he knelt with his palm on the ground and counted the dead back aloud, one at a time, in his father's dialect. Six hundred and six. The roll said seven hundred and forty-one. He counted a second time because he could not stand up. He had the deferral filed beside the return. Barrow told him it would end him, and he answered that it would end the provision.

### Where They Stand

In the lower chapel he blessed the four-month-old son of Charles Lambert. The child weighed on his floor before making a sound. At the blessing the boy's eyes went gold. Verinus told the bishop that the boy would not be written down. Then he sent Charles north with the Kusharan party.

On the fourteenth day he came down into the cellar beneath the Grand Church in the Palatine's gold, with chalk on his fingers. Aurelian had hung on a frame there for fourteen days, for four Attestants and a room of books. Verinus declared the surety his own.

"Do you consent," said Verinus.

"No."

"Entered."

The wounds crossed the room into him in the order they had been made. The four had already gone out through the low cloister. Corwin Sesk knew, and said nothing.

At the root of the wound he sealed what he could of Kujo, and the seal took pieces and left the man. He could have saved perhaps four thousand at the price of the rite. He did the sum and let thirty-five in every hundred go. He became the wall. He had taken Aurelian for its anchor without asking. When the draw turned down the line toward the cellar he tried to take the line back, and the rite would not permit the amendment. The Judger's hand lifted the Custodia out of the world, still standing, and set him down in Utopia with his cap gone and his hands empty. He put his palm flat on the floor. It held him and did not sink.

He is still Palatine. An empty chair still stands at every session of the Weighing, and he has never found Meren. The line to Aurelian stays tied. It has no release valve, and Aurelian has not been told what he holds. Sefa Bream of the Steps has an appointment on the twenty-third, and Barrow has said she will keep it whether or not he is standing. He has told three people, and written nowhere, that Darius will come back. The word Altherion uses for him when it thinks he is out of the room, he has neither claimed nor put down.

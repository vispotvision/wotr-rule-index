# c24 · Babyl and the Praetorian March

A king of stone and faith, a praefect and an admiral who oppose each other on one imperial periphery, and three people that ground made or took in: an adjudicator born to the king's nobility, a deserter who guards a Wellspring on his frontier, and a veteran of the March who lives on the same frontier. Four cards say Withering Era. Juno's and Valen's say nothing. No archived scene holds any of the six. Canon order holds throughout: Isaac's direction, then the card, then the archived scenes, then the wiki, then this record.

**The ground is under ruling.** Babyl, the Babylian Imperium and Karn-Bael are struck (WORLD.md §3), and Varuun's, Juno's and Seren's cards each flag the realm for Isaac's ruling on re-homing or striking. The Praetorian March is struck on Valen's card, and "The Praetoria re-home-or-strike flag stays open" (RULINGS.md, 2026-09-12). This record quotes those names where the cards use them. In the shared history it writes Varuun's kingdom, the March and the frontier, and places nothing nearer than the cards do, so that a ruling can move the events with the realms.

## 1 Who is here

**Varuun Daskorr · The Unbent Crown.** King Varuun Daskorr, called the Unbent Crown, "Mortal King. Wellspring-Bound Sovereign. Living Anchor." His card makes him reigning monarch of the Kingdom of Babyl, "an Inner World dominion of stone and faith", ruling from a citadel over the worked Core of Coagula, and flags the whole frame as a struck polity for Isaac's ruling. He signed the Guild Accord and "often delays decrees citing "Temporal Weight Clauses"". His line, the Daskorr, traces to the Lawbearers of Coagula; its ancestors served the Dawn Guard as oath-smiths, and it is rumoured to carry Atlazon's Strain, a gravitational aura in every heir. He is towering and aging, white-maned, in red and sable robes worked with golden script, and his sword, the Kneeling Sun, is also his sceptre. He speaks his edicts in the Throne Hall, where they show as glyphs on the floor until they are fulfilled. He cannot decree at a distance or in haste. The rumour is that his Crystal is fusing into the Core under the city and that one day nothing of him will stand apart from the law. His people "remember him as the king who did not bow to gods, yet never broke faith with them." His card holds Eresse as an ally, the Guild Accord as neutral, and the March as tense. It leaves open whether "The Unbent Crown" is a description or a prophecy.

**Juno Petros Marien · Praefect of the Outer Colonies.** Called the Veil-Warden of Babyl, he is Praefect of the Outer Colonies, "Sovereign Arbitrator of the Bound Marches. Diplomat, strategist, and executor of imperial law." His card binds him to the Babylian Imperium's Outer Colonial Authority, to the House of Civil Flame and to the Imperial Concord of Doctrine & Peace, and flags all of it as dead canon; it names the Iron Mandate of Undaar-Keth and the Holy Sea of Alabaster as places a ruling might move him. Of the Imperial Senate of Babyl: "Half revere him, half fear him. None control him." The Colonials and Dustborn see him as "their only voice in the imperial machine." He is the last living heir of House Marien, "of name, not pride." Emotion, compulsion and falsehood do not take on him, his presence steadies any land he governs, and words he speaks under solemn oath become law in frontier courts. He reads old clauses into rooms that would rather not obey them. It works on institutions and fails on a single man who refuses. He has never struck anyone. He spared the rebel general Cael Thurn publicly. His card lists a trial he presided over in the Cradle-Marches, the Veil Proclamation to the warring Houses, a bloodless surrender at Balth Mere won with a sealed letter, and a final judgment before he vanished into the Eastern Range. Rumour says he dissolved into light mid-sentence.

**Valen Therosian · The Gilded Commandant.** High Admiral of the Praetorian March and "Imperial Arbiter of Crown Preservation", of the Empire of Praetoria's Naval High Command, its Council of Sovereigns and the Loyalist Circle of the Crown-Forged. The March is struck and held for Isaac's ruling. The office stands, ruled 2026-09-12. He is the Crown's enforcer: "He preserves its will, not its mercy." He is loyal to the fact of a monarchy. He cannot be mentally dominated or spiritually shamed except by an Archon. His voice compels any audience of five or more, and his Culling Doctrine falls hardest on those an Imperial edict names traitor. His Signature turns an assembly's doubts against the doubters and does nothing to the certain, and every use thins the ranks of people willing to stand near him uncertain. He can move a fleet and cannot read a room or hold a friend. His one named relation is Juno, "Ideological foil and frequent opponent on council." His card lists four chapters: "The Fires That Do Not Beg Forgiveness", "The Gathering of Sovereigns", "When Dust Demands Blood" and "The Executioner's Voice".

**Seren Valenne · The Grey Adjudicator.** A Senior Adjudicator of the Guild Accord's Arbitration Division, assigned to the Neutral Chambers of the Inner World: "Mediator of divine, political and Wellspring-related disputes. A living instrument of Concord Law." Their card calls them they. Mortal, of the Withering Era, born in Babyl, which the card flags as struck. They carry "The quiet refinement of Babylian nobility tempered by years in the Accord's Neutral Chambers": silver-white hair braided past the waist, the Division's mark of neutrality, and robes of grey and deep umber. Peers know them for never raising their voice or drawing a weapon, and for quelling conflicts that once became interrealm wars. They carry the Oath Scale, the Vow-Seal Mantle and the Seal of the Grey Court. The Scale shivers at falsehood. Near them lies feel heavy and honesty light, and two parties in conflict are brought to an equilibrium neither chose. Their card says of it: "It equalises. It does not decide." It fails in a crowded chamber and on anyone who says nothing. Every hearing sends them home exhausted. Their card records the Caedor and Babyl Arbitration, a Wellspring boundary dispute after the Eighth Fracture of Faith, where both sides held silence for an hour, and, taken from Kaelrith's card, the Arbitration of Anguz. It leaves open what the mirror has absorbed and never returned. It also leaves open whether the hour was the field or the Adjudicator's exhaustion reaching the room.

**Kaelrith Dorne · The Iron Wound.** Veteran of the Withering Rebellions, former Guild Accord auxiliary in the Enforcement Division, now a Free Lance, "serving no flag but his own." He was born in Anguz. He deserted at the Siege of Cindralith "after watching his entire unit dissolve under corrupted Aether rain." He is tall and gaunt, his hair silver that was once "the pale blond of Anguzian lineages", with cross-pattern scars on his face and neck, "the ritual mark of an aborted Temperance binding", and the Enforcement insignia worn inside-out on his half-mantle. He still consults on Concord clean-ups and keeps an independent watch on a Wellspring near the Babyl frontier. His Chain of Oathglass reads "Never again under command." He carries Unrest, half a Guild greatsword broken at Cindralith, and drags it across old battlefields so that no corrupted echo rises. Each wound he takes anchors him further. Near him weapons refuse to shatter, corpses refuse to rise and corrupted Wellsprings lower their hum. Unwounded, he is a tired man with half a sword. Younger Accord soldiers fear him. "Don't call it courage. It's what's left when the fear burns out." His card leaves open what the aborted binding was meant to bind and who held the other end.

**Rheon Kaeth · The Emberline.** A retired, discharged Accord soldier on the Guild Accord's Inactive Reserve, called the Emberline among old comrades, whose realm of origin is the Praetorian March, struck and carried as his card gives it. At the Firefront Collapse his regiment was annihilated "when a Flame-aspected Wellspring imploded under corrupted pressure." Half his unit burned into glass. He pulled three dying men out of molten air and walked out carrying light under his skin. One side of his face bears the molten scar. One eye is amber and the other dulled from the blast. He never removes his gloves, and his plain clothes are always freshly cleaned. He lives on the fringe of the Babyl frontier offering quiet labour, maintains Guild outposts and trains young recruits in emergency containment. Fires die near him and explosions mute, and when he bleeds the air grows hot again. His Guild saber stays wrapped in linen, undrawn since the Collapse. A charm of fused battlefield glass pulses when danger nears, and the March brand on his skin, once a badge of honour, is faint under the scars. Children trust him, and veterans salute him in silence. "His guilt is the fear that if he forgets the war, it might return." His card leaves open what the heat near his home on storm nights is; the reports say the Wellspring is reawakening.

## 3 The shared history

### The glass field at the Firefront

**When.** Withering Era. The Collapse is the one event Rheon's card gives, and his Catalyst Event. The clean-up is new and falls after it, and after Kaelrith deserted at the Siege of Cindralith, since his card has him consult on Concord clean-ups as a Free Lance. No card dates the Collapse against Varuun's reign. His accusation is made in his reign.

**Where.** The Firefront ground, at a Flame-aspected Wellspring on the frontier where Varuun's kingdom meets the March (new; Kaelrith's and Rheon's cards name the frontier, and Varuun's card names the March across it). Rheon's house stands on the fringe below the same Wellspring, and it is the Wellspring Kaelrith guards (new). The cards put the kingdom in the Inner World (Varuun, I; Seren, I; WORLD §3); its name is struck and its re-homing is Isaac's open ruling.

**Who.** Rheon Kaeth, Kaelrith Dorne, Varuun Daskorr.

**What happened.** A Flame-aspected Wellspring on the frontier imploded under corrupted pressure, and Rheon's regiment was annihilated. Half his unit burned into glass. He pulled three dying men out of molten air and walked out with light under his skin, one side of his face scarred and one eye dulled from the blast. He has not drawn his saber since. When the ground had cooled, the Accord sent a Concord clean-up. Kaelrith came to it as a consultant. He had deserted the Enforcement Division at Cindralith and still took its clean-up work. He walked the glass with the broken half of Unrest dragging behind him, so that no corrupted echo of the regiment would rise, and the Wellspring's hum lowered as he came near. Rheon had been discharged by then. He came up from the frontier and walked the field beside Kaelrith and showed him where the men had stood. Neither spoke of his own unit. Rheon took a piece of the fused glass off the field. It is the charm he wears. When the clean-up closed, Kaelrith stayed on as the Wellspring's guard, hired by nobody who could command him. Rheon settled on the fringe below it among the frontier's civilians and took up the outposts and the recruits. On storm nights the instruments read heat near his house. The reports say the Wellspring is reawakening. Varuun accuses the March of weaponizing Wellspring fire, and the Firefront is among the fires he names. Rheon's card gives the implosion as corrupted pressure. No record says who corrupted it.

**What each took.** Rheon took three men carried out of the fire, a piece of the glass, and a house below the Wellspring that burned his regiment, with a man on watch above it who never asks about the glow. Kaelrith took one more field kept quiet, a post on the frontier that answers to no one, and the fear of the recruits Rheon trains, as younger Accord soldiers fear him everywhere. Varuun took a fire on his own frontier to name against the March.

### The Arbitration of Anguz

**When.** Withering Era. After Kaelrith's desertion, since he came as a Free Lance, and while Seren sat as a Senior Adjudicator of the Arbitration Division. No card orders it against the Firefront.

**Where.** Anguz, Inner World, North (WORLD §3; the Inner World page, ruled 2026-09-12), where the guilds set the coil and price Aethersteel and Bloodsilver on it. It is Kaelrith's realm of origin (his card).

**Who.** Seren Valenne, Kaelrith Dorne.

**What happened.** The Arbitration of Anguz was a trial under Concord Law, heard at Anguz. The Division's record of it names no Free Lance. Unofficial reports say Seren invoked Kaelrith's aid. He came as a Free Lance, on the invocation alone, and his Chain of Oathglass still read "Never again under command." He came back to the realm he was born in with the pale blond of its lineages bleached to silver and the Enforcement insignia worn inside-out on his mantle. Through the trial he stood in the chamber with half a sword. Seren sat in grey and umber with the Oath Scale before them and did not raise their voice. Neither spoke to the other throughout the trial. Where Kaelrith stood, the room did not panic. Nobody drew a weapon. When the trial closed, both walked out alive, and the reports count that as peace enough.

**What each took.** Seren took a trial that closed with everyone in the chamber alive, and an aid no Division record carries. Kaelrith took a return to Anguz on his own terms with his vow whole, and his name beside a Senior Adjudicator's in unofficial reports.

### The hour of silence

**When.** Withering Era, after the Eighth Fracture of Faith, which Seren's card names and no page describes. New: in Varuun's reign.

**Where.** The Neutral Chambers of the Inner World, where Seren is assigned (their card). No page places the Chambers (c12, section 7). The parties were Caedor, on the New World's chartered arc (WORLD §3, §5), and Varuun's kingdom, which the cards put in the Inner World.

**Who.** Seren Valenne, Varuun Daskorr.

**What happened.** After the Eighth Fracture of Faith, Caedor and Varuun's kingdom contested a Wellspring boundary, and the dispute came before the Arbitration Division. Seren heard it. They were born in the kingdom, and they carry its nobility's bearing under the grey of the Neutral Chambers, with the Division's braid down their back. Varuun did not come. He decrees only in his Throne Hall. He did not leave it. The kingdom's case came by its advocates, and Caedor's by its own. Seren sat between the parties with the Oath Scale and did not raise their voice. Both sides fell silent. They held that silence for an hour. When the hearing closed, Seren wrote terms, and they went to both parties as the Accord's. In the kingdom they reached the Throne Hall. Varuun signed the Accord. He has delayed the terms under the Temporal Weight Clauses, as he delays its decrees.

**What each took.** Seren took the hour, the exhaustion their card says a mirror carries out of every hearing, and terms for the kingdom of their birth that its king has not carried out. Varuun took the Accord's terms from an adjudicator born in his own kingdom, and answered them as he answers the Accord, under the Temporal Weight Clauses.

### The sparing of Cael Thurn

**When.** Undated. Neither Juno's card nor Valen's states an era. Juno's card places his first recorded trial in the Cradle-Marches, in Book II, Chapter V, before the Veil Proclamation and the siege of Balth Mere. This record sets the sparing at that trial (new).

**Where.** The Cradle-Marches, where Juno's card sets that trial, on the imperial periphery that Valen's card says the two men administer. No page places the Cradle-Marches (Juno, X), and no place in WORLD.md holds the periphery while both realms wait on Isaac's ruling.

**Who.** Juno Petros Marien, Valen Therosian.

**What happened.** General Cael Thurn had been loyal and turned rebel. An edict named him traitor. Before the trial Valen argued on the council that the edict be carried out, since his Culling Doctrine falls on those an Imperial edict names traitor. Juno presided at the trial in the Cradle-Marches. He heard the case and spared the general in open court, where everyone present could hear it. His card says he "now commands his silence in every battlefield." Valen opposed the sparing on the council. The two have opposed each other there ever since.

**What each took.** Juno took a rebel general's silence on every battlefield, held by a sparing the whole court saw, and a council opponent who had asked for the edict. Valen took a traitor under edict spared in open court over his word, and the man his card names as his foil.

## 8 Left open

These stay open in every Lore written from this record, and no backstory settles them.

- Isaac's re-home-or-strike rulings on Babyl (Varuun, Juno, Seren) and on Praetoria (Valen, and Rheon's origin), and with them where the frontier, the Cradle-Marches and the periphery lie.
- Whether the Kingdom of Babyl and the Babylian Imperium are one realm; whose periphery Juno and Valen share; which council they sit on.
- Varuun: whether "The Unbent Crown" is a description or a prophecy; the rumours that his Crystal is sintering into the Core and that his line carries Atlazon's Strain.
- Juno: what happens when the correction finally lifts; his final judgment, the Eastern Range and the rumour that he dissolved into light; the Wy, Ci and Ma glyph check; the House of Civil Flame, the Imperial Concord of Doctrine & Peace, the Cradle-Marches and Balth Mere, which appear only in legacy material.
- Valen: Valorion, Purganeth and Theros, which are not among the sixty.
- Seren: what the mirror has absorbed and never returned purified, and whether the hour of silence was the field or the Adjudicator's exhaustion; the Grey Court, named nowhere else on their card; the Eighth Fracture of Faith.
- Kaelrith: what the aborted Temperance binding was meant to bind, and who held the other end; where Cindralith and the Withering Rebellions lie.
- Rheon: what the heat near his home on storm nights is, the Wellspring or his own Crystal; whether the three men lived.
- Who corrupted the Firefront Wellspring, and whether Varuun's accusation is true.
- What was tried at the Arbitration of Anguz, and what the terms of the Caedor hearing say.

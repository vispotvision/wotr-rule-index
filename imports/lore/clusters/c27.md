# c27 · Ash and the red thread

The Ashveil desert blade and the death-speaker who shares his name, the ash-duelist, the butcher, the sanguine assassin and the sealed-ash exile: a pyrokinetic mercenary who stopped being a caravan guard, the Herald of Auren who adjudicates contested deaths, a ronin who left the Ash Warden Corps, a grafted killer bound to a blood-seed Asura, an assassin who left the Scarlet Accord, and a half-infernal elf the Pyre Courts exiled. Only Ilyrion's card states an era, and no archived scene holds any of the six. Canon order holds throughout: Isaac's direction, then the card, then the archived scenes, then the wiki, then this record.

**Sources read.** The six cards in full, in the Volume I character-card folder of `wiki/`. The neighbouring cards where they name a member or bear on one: Ymir Rhok, Hyo Miran, Yukizumi Yukari, Orokh Sable-Forge, Khan Kirv, Morphaene the Gloam-Brewer, Drevath Mourne-Kal, Adalric Vladimer Valen, Beatrix Vaelthorn, Leon Vaerenthal and Kaien Rhuven. **Era.** Ilyrion's card gives the Withering Era. The other five cards give none. Vaerath's card puts her before the Arbitration Division's charter, which The Concordance of Ages dates to the Voyager Era, while c06 and c16 set Yukizumi Yukari and Orokh Sable-Forge, who called her in and forged her scythe, in the Withering Era; her length of life is held open (section 7). ## 1 Who is here

**Zahir Ashveil · Desert's Fang.** He is called Emberstride, the Sandflame and Desert's Fang: "Independent Mercenary. Desert Wellspring Raider. Pyrokinetic Duelist." He was once with the Sunwatch Caravan Guard, which no page attests, and he is "now a contracted blade-for-hire across the Inner World and Outer World borders." His card says a caravan guard is taught a burn that stops. He worked out a burn that waits, alone, and it is the reason he stopped being a guard: the Scorchbrand, a burn that stays in a wound and reignites when he decides. His card makes the Catalyst and the change of trade one event. He walks heated sand and molten glass unharmed, breathes short bursts of superheated air that light whatever his ash has left, and takes the ground out from under an opponent. His curved blade carries his draw into the cut, and his card has it from Ymir Rhok, the Emberhand. He talks in maxims and names what he does. His output peaks in the first exchanges and he has almost nothing to sustain it, so the fights he wins he wins early. "Wet stone, a cold night and a closed room take away all three of his Wellsprings' preferred conditions at once." Ash kept below its ignition point never lights again, "which is precisely why Kaerith Vireth keeps winning." Caldris Vaelthorn, who has no card, fought beside him against Outer World raiders. His card leaves open why he left the Sunwatch, and whether the caravan he guarded is one of the ones that burned. His words: "burn fast, or be buried slow."

**Vaerath Ashveil · The Pale Reaping.** Her card gives her true name as Vaerath, her field name as the Pale Reaping and her epithet as She Who Carries The Last Breath: "Herald of Auren. Death-Speaker. Voice of Auren's Silence." She is an "Independent Arbiter under Auren's direct sanction", loosely adjacent to Vocatia (the Pale Pact) and the Accord's Arbitration Division. She "predates the Division's formal charter by three decades" and answers to Auren. She was born on the threshold of death, half-mortal and half-Remnant, caught by Auren's Domain reaching through the Veil before she followed her mother into it. The wound she made load-bearing is her own continued existence, and the office she holds is made of it. A living seal sits at her throat, "shaped like a crescent that does not quite close", and the false thread in Rot constructs, Mechanica undead and soul-anchor violations frays near it. Her mantle carries skull-fragments of souls she adjudicated, fused by consent: she offered passage, and each echo chose to stay as witness and armour. She walks the membrane between the living world and Auren's Domain. Her scythe, Vernaculum, was forged by Orokh Sable-Forge from a rib out of the Thanaris Hollow, and its edge subtracts light. It cuts the threads that bind a thing to continued existence. It cannot touch the living who have not broken death-law. Every grand Edict leaves her fingertips translucent for days, and "She has not told anyone how far it has crept." She refuses contact with Verdantia, whose life makes her tether ache. Yukizumi Yukari called her in on the Niran Audit-Scar Incident. She watched two cycles, laid a palm on the anchor and spoke one word, and "It apologized. Then it stopped." At Eldervale she came three days after the battle and adjudicated every contested death across six silent hours. Her card leaves open why an empire it strikes let her walk free after she tore a consumed army out of a Rot Host general's keeping. Her words: "Auren does not mourn. He waits. I am his impatience."

**Renjiro Kaithen · The Pale Fang.** He is the Ash-Warden of Kyru: "Ronin Duelist. Ashen Wellspring Swordsman. Keeper of the Bone-Sealed Vows." He was of the Senoth Kingdom's Ash Warden Corps and is now a wandering sword-for-hire. His Catalyst was the first thing he made out of his own understanding that held. The folk call that coming into the road, and "Renjiro left the Corps and took to it literally." His ash finds a weapon, a plate or a construct, and a few exchanges later the thing that was blocking him is failing on its own. It works on made things, and flesh is not improved by it. He breathes out burning ash that blinds and chokes, and he wears the Warden's Mantle, armour inherited from the Corps. It repels a direct Wellspring discharge for three seconds before it ruptures. Kyru's Memory, a spectral katana spirit, whispers to him in battle, warns him of killing intent and parries strikes he does not see. His Bone-Sealed Vows bar him from striking unarmed children or anyone he owes a debt to, and breaking them bleeds Essence from his Crystal. So a favour disarms him. A rescue or a gift accepted does the same, and any of them "has disarmed him without touching a blade". He has sparred with Ymir Rhok many times. His card leaves open who Kyru was. His words: "Steel cuts flesh, ash rots steel, but memory… memory rots the soul."

**Spoil · The Butcher.** Spoil, formerly Tanya, is called the Blood-Seed Marauder and the Red-Thread Butcher: "Independent Killer. Blood-Asura Binder. Dual-Blade Executioner." They are nomadic and unaffiliated, "Known to take contracts from Outer World warlords and Concord dissidents." Originally female, their head was joined to a male body by forbidden graft-binding rites, which gives them an unnatural physical synergy and resilience to decapitation. The join should still be open, and it holds. Raktabija, a blood-seed Asura, is bound to them in permanent servitude as companion and summoned weapon. That binding is the bond their Flourishing required, and their card "does not pretend that is the same thing as being loved." Their Ram-Dao saber, Raktabija's Finger, leaks blood through its wrappings, and what it spills does not fall: blood taken from the field becomes Raktabija's constructs, and Raktabija does not tire. Against a bloodless opponent they have two blades. Tattoos across their back, violent and sexual, ward them against certain prophetic divinations. They wear ancient golden spiked shackles as ornament, and the shackles leave Essence wounds in whatever they strike. Hyo Miran fences their relics. Their words: "Blood's just memory made warm again. I like to remind people of what they've forgotten."

**Rathien Veythar · The Fang.** He is called the Crimson Whisper, the Black-Threaded Fang, Shadow-Step Duelist and Visceral Cutter, and "The Silent Hand of the Sanguine Tally." He is "Independent, ex-Scarlet Accord", and he "Operates as a highly selective contractor." He is an assassin of speed and silence who works vital fluids as a medium and lays one effect over another for a clean kill nobody can trace, "viewing excessive force as a flaw in method." His Catalyst was a conclusion he reached alone: rigid control of his own visceral impulses, and speed and silence over destructive output. Light goes a soft grey close around him, and the air drags a little. His eyes keep finding the lamps. He works at contact or a few steps off, and he has no ranged option at all. He drives his force into a point smaller than a coin, so the wound is worse than the strength behind it. Overuse costs him. It ruptures his capillaries and takes his fine motor control for a time. On principle he takes up no current beyond his three. Anyone faster than he is beats him outright, and he chooses every contract with that in mind. His card names nobody. His words: "They won't hear me. They won't see me. They'll just fall."

**Ilyrion Vantheir · The Veiled Horn.** He is called the Ember-Touched Exile, Quiet Calamity and the Veiled Horn, and He Who Burns Without Flame: "Half-Infernal Elf Exile. Sealed Ash-Wellspring Ascetic. Restraint as a discipline of combat." His card sets him in the Withering Era, exiled from the Pyre Courts, which no page attests, and "Unaffiliated, wandering the frontier territories." His Catalyst: "Containment failed once, on someone else's ground, and he was standing close enough to be blamed for it." He built the seal that morning and has not taken it off. "The Pyre Courts called it exile." He holds a dormant infernal resonance close to his body, and heat distortion and shadow bleed are its only ordinary signs. A room he has sat in for an hour reads a few degrees wrong. His seal runes damp his emotional volatility, and emotional spikes are the one input the seal was never built to filter. He steps a short way in an instant and leaves a heat mirage behind. What another practitioner would waste becomes his recovery, and he does not tire the way a fight suggests he should. Dormant Wellsprings near where he stays begin to lean toward his resonance: "He has never done this on purpose and has stopped staying anywhere for long because of it." He wears a Sealing Cloak, a Horn Sigil that gives him his byname, and bands of plain iron at the wrists, "chosen because plain iron cannot fail in a way that surprises him." His Crimson Fracture breaks the seal in part, and a second break in one fight is a decision about whether he still gets to be the same person tomorrow. He commands nobody. Ruling other people is something "he tried that already." Frontier settlements know him by reputation, "and nobody has tested that reputation twice." He has never once tried to explain his side of the exile to anyone who could carry it back. His card leaves open how close the seal already is to failing on its own. His words: "If I cared enough to try, this would already be over."

## 3 The shared history

### The morning of the seal

**When.** The Withering Era, undated. It is Ilyrion's Catalyst Event as his card gives it: a containment that failed on someone else's ground, the seal built that morning, and the exile. The town, its dead and Vaerath's coming are new. It comes before the other three events, since Ilyrion walks the frontier after it.

**Where.** A walled town of the Free March of Saren, the confederation of fortified towns founded by refugees from dragon-conquered land, among the Kingdoms of the Five Rivers in the Outer World (WORLD §3; the Five Rivers row of the Outer World page). It was not the Pyre Courts' ground. No page places the Pyre Courts, and this record does not place them.

**Who.** Ilyrion Vantheir, Vaerath Ashveil.

**What happened.** Ilyrion came to the town from the Pyre Courts, on business no card records. While he was there a containment failed. What it held, and whose it was, the record does not say. He was standing close enough to be blamed for it. People of the town died in the street. By morning he had built the seal, and it was on him when the town came out to count its dead. The town blamed the Courts. The Courts' people blamed the town. Contested deaths are Vaerath's work, and she came some days later. She walked the street with the scythe, read each death in turn and gave her accounting to Auren. Ilyrion was still there. He sat apart with the seal on him. She did not ask him anything, and he did not explain himself to her. One echo from that street took her offer of passage and chose to stay, and it rides in her mantle as a witness. Neither the town nor the Courts asked her what she had found, and she told neither. The Pyre Courts called it exile.

**What each took.** Ilyrion took the seal, which he has not taken off, and the exile. He took the dead of a town that was not his, and the accounting of them is in the keeping of a Herald who answers to Auren alone. He has explained none of it. Vaerath took the dead of that street into her accounting and one of them into her mantle. She also took the sight of a man sitting among the deaths he was blamed for, who did not flinch.

### The Kethra night

**When.** The Withering Era, undated, and new. It falls after Spoil's Flourishing, since Raktabija is bound to them, and after Rathien's Catalyst. It comes before the fog on the Glass Coast.

**Where.** Kethra, one of the port cities of the Eastern Sun Coast of the Outer World (WORLD §3 and §5; the Outer World page lists it among the coast's cities). A merchant's house in the city.

**Who.** Rathien Veythar, Spoil.

**What happened.** A merchant of Kethra was marked by two contracts in one season, and neither contractor knew of the other. Rathien held one. He watched the house for several nights and learned its lamps. On the night he chose he went in inside his grey, came within reach of the merchant and killed him. The man died with no mark anyone could find and his blood still in him. Rathien was out before the house knew. Spoil held the other contract, from an Outer World warlord, and came in later the same night. They found the man already dead and nothing spilled. Raktabija's Finger needs blood to work, and the house guards gave it. Spoil cut out through them with both blades, and what left the guards did not fall: it stood up as Raktabija's constructs. Rathien watched the fight from the dark across the street. In the morning the house counted its master among the Butcher's dead and put a price on Spoil's head. The price came to Rathien through the brokers, as the contracts he takes come to him. He declined it. Spoil is faster than he is, and he does not take a contract on anyone faster. The warlord paid Spoil for the merchant.

**What each took.** Rathien took a kill nobody traced to him, entered under another's name. He declined the price on the one who carries it. He also took a close sight of the Butcher at work, and of blood that does not fall. Spoil took pay for a death they did not make. The price on their head is for that death and for the guards. They also took a dead man whose blood had not moved: the work of a blood-worker they never saw and cannot name.

### Fog on the Glass Coast

**When.** The Withering Era, undated, and new. It falls after the Kethra night. It is one of the Outer World raids Spoil's and Renjiro's cards give them together, one of the Outer World hunts in which Spoil's card has Kaerith try for Raktabija, and one of the frequent clashes Zahir's card gives him with Kaerith. It comes after Zahir left the Sunwatch and after Renjiro left the Corps.

**Where.** The Glass Coast of the Aurelian Dust Coast, the mineral desert between the Drakhal Spine and the sea in the Outer World (WORLD §5). The Outer World page lists the Glass Coast among the Dust Coast's features, puts copper first among what comes out of it, and says fog rolls in from the sea. A copper enclave on the shore.

**Who.** Spoil, Renjiro Kaithen, Zahir Ashveil; and Kaerith Vireth, who has no card.

**What happened.** An Outer World warlord sent a raid against a copper enclave on the Glass Coast, and Spoil and Renjiro rode with it as hired blades. The Kethra price on Spoil had gone out, and two hunters came after it: Zahir, and Kaerith Vireth. Zahir reached the raid first, in the last heat of the day, walking the glass unharmed. He went for Spoil. Renjiro met him on the glass, and for a few exchanges two men who work the same ash worked it at each other. Then the sea fog came in over the coast with the dark. Kaerith came out of it. Kaerith and Zahir wanted the same head, and they fell on each other first, as they had before. Zahir put ash into Kaerith with the curved blade and called it. The night was wet and cold, and Kaerith's frost kept it colder. The ash would not light. Kaerith made the fight last until Zahir had spent all he opens with, and Zahir went down on the glass. Then Kaerith went at the raiders. The frost went into Raktabija to freeze it and shatter it; the blood froze on the Asura, and it did not shatter. Kaerith tried to claim Kyru's Memory for the Concord archives, and Renjiro kept it. The spirit parried what came at him out of the fog. Once, when the frost came at Renjiro from a side he had not covered, Raktabija's blood stood in its way. Then Renjiro owed Spoil, and his vows bar him from striking anyone he owes. Before the fog lifted he had taken a strike of Kaerith's frost on the Warden's Mantle to cover Spoil in turn, and he counted the debt square. By morning the raid had gone north up the coast with the enclave's copper. Kaerith had taken neither the Asura, nor the blade-spirit, nor the price. Zahir walked off the glass alone.

**What each took.** Spoil took Raktabija back whole from the frost and the price still on their head. They also took the sight of a man paying back a rescue within the same night, which left him free to strike them. Renjiro took Kyru's Memory out of the fog, still his, and a debt to Spoil paid before dawn. Their cards record deep distrust. The Glass Coast gave each of them a reason for it. Zahir took another loss to Kaerith on the ground he fights best on, made cold. The price went uncollected.

### The Well in the Dead Wells

**When.** The Withering Era, undated, and new. It falls in Ilyrion's exile, after the morning of the seal, and after the fog on the Glass Coast.

**Where.** The Dead Wells of the Aurelian Dust Coast in the Outer World (WORLD §5). The Outer World page lists the Dead Wells among the Dust Coast's features, and its account of the continent's ground calls empty works of this kind Hollow Wellsprings that "occasionally answer weakly". A mining enclave's ground there, on the frontier Ilyrion walks.

**Who.** Ilyrion Vantheir, Zahir Ashveil.

**What happened.** Ilyrion stopped for a time near a mining enclave by the Dead Wells. One of the Hollow Wellsprings there began to answer, and what answered leaned toward his resonance. He had not meant it. The enclave did not know why the Well had woken. Its people knew the exile by reputation and kept away from him. Word of a Dead Well answering went along the coast, and Zahir came to raid it. At the Well's rim he found a stranger sitting who did not get up, and he took the stillness for nothing. He opened fast, as he always does. The first cut went in, and the ash went with it. Ilyrion stepped. The next cut went through heat mirage where he had been. Zahir called the ash in the first cut, and it did not answer him. The man he had cut did not tire. By the time Zahir had spent what he opens with, Ilyrion had not raised a hand. Zahir left the Well. Ilyrion moved on within days, and the Well stopped answering when he had gone.

**What each took.** Zahir took a cut whose ash would not answer, a Well he did not take, and the one reputation on the frontier he has tested. He has not tested it twice. Ilyrion took a Well on ground that was not his, leaning toward him without his leave, and a raider it had drawn to him. He has not stayed anywhere long since.

## 6 Same person?

No two cards here are one person.

- **Zahir Ashveil and Vaerath Ashveil.** He is a pyrokinetic mercenary governed by Cinerion, the Ash Veil, whose surname is that current's name. She is the Herald of Auren, born half-Remnant on the threshold of death, whose card carries no Cinerion and gives her true name as Vaerath alone. Neither card names the other. They share a word.
- **The fangs.** Renjiro Kaithen is "The Pale Fang"; Lyris Myndross is "The Pale Fang of Ruin" (c17); Rathien is "The Fang" and Zahir "Desert's Fang". Four people, and nothing on any card joins them.
- **Near names outside.** Zahir Ashveil is a different man from Zahiren Vahl, the Horizon-Eyed, a Wellspring cartographer of the Arbitration Division, and from Zahur Vane, the Silt Walker, both of c19. Ilyrion, the Veiled Horn, and Raivhar Drakonis, the Ashen Horn (c10), are two men. This record gives Caldris Vaelthorn, who has no card, no kinship with Beatrix Vaelthorn (section 7).

## 7 Where the cards disagree

1. **Who commissioned the blade.** Zahir's card, in its Artifacts: "Commissioned from Ymir Rhok, the Emberhand." and "Ymir's own sheet records the commission from the smith's side; the two agree." Zahir's card, in its Relationships: "Once commissioned Zahir to forge a flame-channeling curved blade; they still trade rare desert ores." Ymir's card: "Zahir Ashveil · Once commissioned him to forge a flame-channeling curved blade." Two lines make Zahir the client, and one makes Ymir the client and Zahir the smith. c16 records the same (item 3).
2. **The lowest Stage in Volume IV.** Rathien's card: "He is the lowest-Stage combatant in Volume IV and lethal anyway". Zahir's card counts Spoil's, Renjiro's and its own among "three Volume IV sheets", and Zahir's and Renjiro's cards each give "Stage III, Ascension", the Stage Rathien's card gives him.
3. **Caldris Vaelthorn.** Zahir's card: "Unattested; no sheet, and the name appears nowhere else in the mirror." The surname stands on another card, `Beatrix Vaelthorn · The Root-Sworn.md`, which names no Caldris. This record makes no kinship.
4. **Vaerath's span of life (card against wiki).** Her card: "she predates the Division's formal charter by three decades". The Concordance of Ages: "The Guild Accord, the Concord Codex, the Lattice, the Divisions, the Tiered Path, and the first nine Concord Gates all date to this seventy-year window", the Voyager Era. c06 sets Yukizumi Yukari, who "Called her in on the Niran Audit-Scar Incident", in the Withering Era, and c16 sets Orokh Sable-Forge's century at the anvil there. Read together, her life runs from before the Divisions into the Withering Era, and WORLD §6 allows a life across eras only where the card says so. Her card gives no era and no age. This record sets her one event in the Withering Era, where those records already have her, and states no length of life.
5. **Vocatia as a body (cards against wiki).** Vaerath's card: "Operates loosely adjacent to Vocatia (the Pale Pact)". Yukizumi Yukari's card: "Arbitration Division, special charter under Vocatia (Pale Pact)." The Lexicon of Magic page and The Spirit Summoning Arts make Vocatia a Category of working: "Invocation by recognition. Never domination." No page has a Pale Pact.
6. **Senoth (card against ruling).** Renjiro's card: "Formerly of the Senoth Kingdom's Ash Warden Corps". `RULINGS.md`, 2026-09-12: Senoth stays under "Names That No Longer Attach to Ground", the list in The Bearing and the Holding of names no survey can find. The Atla'zon and Gaia page has "the Priests of the Axis in the Senoth Kingdom", and the Master Material Ledger places Senoth's hills "in the western quarter on trade-route evidence only." No page has the Ash Warden Corps.
7. **The Empire of Bluvest.** Vaerath's card: "struck; card gives no such empire in current canon". Drevath Mourne-Kal's and Adalric Vladimer Valen's cards carry it live: "Why the Empire of Bluvest let him leave." and "Former High Lord of the Empire of Bluvest". c11 (item 8) and c17 (item 9) record the same.
8. **A half-infernal elf and the Pyre Courts (card against wiki).** Ilyrion's card: "Half-Infernal Elf Exile." and "unattested in current canon; kept because the exile the card describes has nowhere else to have come from". No peoples page names an infernal kindred (WORLD §6), and no page places the Pyre Courts.

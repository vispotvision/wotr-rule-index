# c29 · The Withering courts

The people who move crowns through rooms, and two whose work takes them into those rooms: Cyranthia Valez, the Gilded Marionette, a broker to whom every capital owes a favour; Elowen Mireille, the Porcelain Archivist, placed in households by the Ivory Ledger Society; Marceline Vireaux, the Crimson Mourner of the Sable Rose Consortium; Aurelian Veymont, the Lion of the Dawn, sovereign and High Marshal of the Ember Crown; Seri Zeliro-Vanthe, the Lantern-Fox, a thread-reader of House Zeliro-Vanthe in the Guild Accord's service; and Rovhen Talvasciel, the Prettier, once court enchanter to the Queen of Talveris.

**Era.** Four cards say the Withering Era, each in its affiliation line: Cyranthia's, Elowen's, Marceline's and Aurelian's. Seri's and Rovhen's cards state none. The Withering is the era of the western quarter's falling density (WORLD §1; CA, "The Withering Era"). This record sets all four of its events in that era and in the western quarter, and it names no year. Seri and Rovhen are placed in the Withering Era by this record alone. Nothing on either card forbids it, and the Guild Accord that employs one and hunts the other stands through the whole Imperial Age.

Her Catalyst: "She gave up the right to be believed for her own sake." The card reads what that left her: "Her networks are not ambition. They are the shape trust takes once you can no longer spend it directly." She works rooms. "Groups in her presence align toward her conclusions without being told to"; a target's decision "is reconstructed in memory as self-derived"; and "Statements she makes read as emotionally truthful regardless of content". Rooms she has worked "read as calmer than they are". Her Salon is "a claimed space (furniture, lighting, who sits where)". She wears the Signet of Valez, which "Binds a verbal agreement metaphysically once both parties have spoken it aloud in her presence", and a crimson tassel earring, and she keeps a Ledger of Promises. The counter is to stay out of her rooms: "Refuse the Salon, refuse the tea, refuse the treaty dinner". ## 3 · The shared history

Four events. None is in the archive. Each is new and fitted to every card it touches.

The ground is the western quarter, the Old World (WORLD §3; §5, West). The Withering is its condition: "The great houses buy Essence supply the way an army buys grain" (CA). The Value page carries that into the ledgers. An old-rate supply contract "is now an inheritance", and "Houses are being bought for their supply books with the ore thrown in" (`Value, Coin and Trade.md`, the Withering). The Accord reaches this quarter least: "Ancient polities with law older than the Accord and no interest in being harmonised", and "Every district the Withering cuts is a district the Accord quietly stops governing" (ME, II). Where the wire ends, "A district cut off is deaf" (`News, Rumour and the Speed of Information.md`, I), and news goes by post and by mouth. Those are the rooms the three brokers work. The Old World page gives the look of the country. The western kingdoms "build in pale stone", "Spoil heaps stand at the head of every valley", and "The rivers run orange below the old adits".

Three of the events stand in districts WORLD names: the coal foreland, the Greaves districts and the slate belts. The fourth stands at Talveris, which Rovhen's card names and no page places. This record sets Talveris among the western courts and names no district for it. The Ember Crown Dominion, the Rose Courts, the Ivory Ledger Society and the Sable Rose Consortium stay unplaced, and the kingdom the Ember Crown fought stays unnamed. No new name is coined. The tin house of the second event never failed on delivery, and it is none of the houses the Value page says did.

### The peace in the Ember Crown's field

- **When.** After Aurelian's coronation trials, when the Ember Crown first answered him (his card, I), and after the Catalysts on Cyranthia's and Marceline's cards.
- **Where.** The coal foreland of the Old World (WORLD §5, West). The capital of a western kingdom, where Cyranthia kept her Salon through the talks, and the field below it, where the Ember Crown's pavilion stood. No page places the Ember Crown Dominion, and this record does not.
- **Who.** Aurelian Veymont, Cyranthia Valez, Marceline Vireaux.
- **What happened.** The Ember Crown's campaign against the kingdom ended in the field. The kingdom's king was carried off the last engagement and died of his wounds some days later. Marceline Vireaux came to his widow within the week, in her mourning colours, and stayed at her side through the season. She arranged the burial. The king went into the ground in velvet.

  The terms were made in the capital, in Cyranthia Valez's Salon. For weeks the kingdom's council sat there with the Ember Crown's envoys, at tables she had placed, under lamps she had set. Aurelian never came up to the capital. He sent word that the terms would be spoken in his field.

  The widow came down to the pavilion with her council. Marceline walked beside her, and Cyranthia came with them. The council's officers had ridden down hard in the face. At the table, seated beside Marceline, they eased. Aurelian read the Ember Crown's terms aloud himself and swore them on his Imperial Signet. The widow spoke the kingdom's terms aloud. Cyranthia sat at the table while both of them spoke. The kingdom knelt, and the Ember Crown's banners stood over the field.
- **What each took.**
  - Aurelian: a peace sworn on his own ground and his own signet. His envoys ran up a favour in Cyranthia's Salon, and the Ember Crown owes it. He never sat there himself.
  - Cyranthia: a favour owed in each capital, and neither called. Terms she had shaped, sworn on ground she did not hold by the one man at the table who had never entered her rooms. Both parties had spoken the peace aloud in her presence, so her Signet of Valez holds it too.
  - Marceline: a king she buried in velvet, and the widow's household in her circle. In the seasons after, that house's wealth moved toward the Consortium's networks.

### The supply book of a Greaves tin house

- **When.** The Withering Era, when old-rate supply contracts had become inheritances (the Value page). After the Catalysts on all three cards.
- **Where.** The Greaves districts, the western tin districts of the Old World (WORLD §5, West; `The Old World · The Western Wearing.md`, Greaves Tin): the hall of a tin house, the widow's parlour inside it, and Cyranthia's Salon in the district town. The district was off the wire.
- **Who.** Cyranthia Valez, Marceline Vireaux, Elowen Mireille.
- **What happened.** In the winter the head of a tin house died. His shafts ran on pumps, and the pumps ran on supply his house held under contracts his father had written at the old rates. The supply book was worth more than the shafts.

  The Ivory Ledger Society had placed Elowen Mireille in his household before he died. Every afternoon she poured the tea in the widow's parlour, from the same cup and with the same pour.

  Marceline Vireaux came within the week. She sat with the widow and the heirs through the weeks after the burial. In those weeks the heirs came to remember a promise the old man had made, years before, to a house of the Sable Rose Consortium. None of them had spoken of it since. Before the thaw they wrote it into her Ledger of Wills, and the supply book was bound to the Consortium's house.

  Through the same weeks the tin house's creditors had been sitting in Cyranthia Valez's Salon. Cyranthia called once on the widow to condole. While she sat in the parlour, the widow and the chief creditor agreed terms aloud. In the spring the house's credit failed inside a week. No creditor accused the house of anything. Each called his paper separately, in turn. The marriage promised to the elder heir was broken off in the same week. The creditors took the shafts in settlement, and with them the pumps that had to run every hour. The supply book to feed the pumps was already bound, and it stayed with the Consortium.

  Elowen poured through all of it. She marked the afternoon the heirs spoke their father's promise, and the afternoon the widow agreed terms in Cyranthia's presence. She could read the heirs, the widow and the creditor. She found nothing out of true in anything Cyranthia said. Under Marceline's mourning hat she could read nothing at all.
- **What each took.**
  - Cyranthia: a house brought down, and creditors she cannot spend a second time. The supply book her creditors needed, bound in another woman's ledger. It was her first sight of Marceline's hand.
  - Marceline: the supply book, for the Consortium. And a rival whose collapse took the house down around the book and left her holding the one thing in it worth having.

### The exile from Talveris

- **When.** After Rovhen's Catalyst had turned outward (his card, I), while he held the office of court enchanter. Before Seri's assignment to the Quiet Route, while her handler was still her partner in the field (her card, IX: "Former partner and instructor").
- **Where.** The palace of the Queen of Talveris. Rovhen's card names Talveris and no page places it. This record sets it among the western courts of the Old World (WORLD §3; §5, West) and names no district.
- **Who.** Rovhen Talvasciel, Cyranthia Valez, Seri Zeliro-Vanthe.
- **What happened.** Rovhen was court enchanter to the Queen of Talveris. She sat for him in his workroom, and he "redesigned" her entire face (his card, I).

  The queen's council met that night and sat until morning. Several of its members had sat in Cyranthia Valez's Salon in seasons past, and she was sent for before midnight. By morning the council had agreed, member by member, that there would be no trial. The court enchanter would leave Talveris and would not come back. He left that day with the crimson-gold wires of the Needlewing Halo coiled at his back.

  Some days later the Guild Accord sent Seri Zeliro-Vanthe and her partner to read the palace. In the workroom her lantern's blue flame burned brightest beside the chair where the queen had sat. The council chamber read calm. Her flame stood as high there as it had in the workroom. The palace servants gave her the name of the woman the council had sent for. She wrote both rooms up for the file the Accord keeps on him.
- **What each took.**
  - Rovhen: exile, alive and untried, and the loss of his court. An Accord file with a thread-reader's account of his workroom in it. Nothing records that he knows who settled his exile.
  - Cyranthia: the favour of Talveris, uncalled.
  - Seri: the chair beside which her flame burned highest. A quarry the Accord hunts. And a room that read calmer than her flame, with Cyranthia Valez's name attached to it.

### Tea on the Quiet Route

- **When.** During the Quiet Route, Seri's current long-form assignment (her card, IX). The latest of the four events.
- **Where.** A merchant household in the slate belts of the Old World (WORLD §5, West), where the caravans of the Quiet Route halt.
- **Who.** Seri Zeliro-Vanthe, Elowen Mireille.
- **What happened.** Seri came to the household as a guest of the caravan, which was her cover on the Route. The Ivory Ledger Society had placed Elowen Mireille in the same household, and at the afternoon table Elowen poured. Every cup was the same cup and every pour the same pour. Seri saw that before she saw anything else.

  The table talked more freely than a merchant's table talks with a caravan guest at it. Seri felt the ease reach her as well, and she said less. Her lantern stood in the hall with its blue flame. At the edge of her sight, at each pour, the woman with the teapot was deciding something. Across the table Elowen watched a guest with a gold-clasped tome, whose posture showed she was listening harder than she spoke. Seri said nothing of what she was investigating while Elowen sat at the table. Neither woman spent anything that afternoon.
- **What each took.**
  - Seri: an entry in the tome, on a companion who poured every cup alike and decided something at each pour. Like every entry, it cost her some of her distance. And a gatherer she could not read to the bottom.
  - Elowen: a marked afternoon in which the guest said nothing she could ever expose. The guest was the first person she had poured for who never said the thing where she was sitting.

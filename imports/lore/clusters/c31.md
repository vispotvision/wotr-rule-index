# c31 · Old World roads and chains

Six cards: the Thunn-Gorr of Gorr-Keth, a chain-fighter, his wife and their son; a merchant lord who owns the safe places on the Iron-to-Salt Corridor; and a courier and a scout who walk the frontier march with the caravans. The three Thunn-Gorr cards name one another. The other three cards name no member, and no card outside the family names a Thunn-Gorr. The roster lists no scene for any of the six. A search of the archive finds two: the son, spelled Kroathar, on the ridge above the Scourge of Hell in `The_Path_of_Sorrow.md`, and Torven Greis in the Nalūn passage of `the_revolution_of_the_inner_world.md`. ## 1 Who is here

**Krothar Thunn-Gorr · The Old Chain** (krothar-thunn-gorr-the-old-chain). A Crimson Order Hobgoblin of the Kha-Urrum blood-branch, born on Gorr-Keth in the Hobgoblin Expanse and living there now, on the family holdings on the caldera's western shelf. His card calls him retired from contract work. His card gives him about a hundred and forty years, middle age for his kind. The Old World circuits named him the Gorr-Keth Reaver. The guild circuits call him Chain-Father, never to his face, since his son's reputation spread. He spent his first sixteen years under the Vow of Silence, never fully left the habit, and speaks in short declaratives. At sixteen he watched a visiting Ironblood warband chief break a Bugbear family's fishing dock in half and walk away, because nobody on the shelf could stop him. He did not fight the Orc. He picked up the snapped dock chain, wound it round his fist and carried it home, and he trained with chains from that day. He left the Expanse at twenty-two and fought on contract in the Old World's western quarter, the New World's eastern arc, the Inner World frontier and the Outer World's edge-territories, a Guild Accord contractor who broke no contract, abandoned none and left no job unfinished. The Accord's records call him "consistent". He fights with two fifteen-foot chains carried coiled over his shoulders, can find the weak link in a long chain by running it through his hands, and plants his feet so that standing still near him is more dangerous than running. Freda made his first olive scarf. He is on the sixth. He trained his son with chains and later forged the suppressor chains the son wears. He does not know where the son's black light came from. He has theories, and he keeps them.

**Freda Thunn-Gorr · The Caldera Wife** (freda-thunn-gorr-the-caldera-wife). Born Freda Vaal-Kess on the Vaal-Kess shelf-island holding, of an old civilian line that never entered the Crimson Order: warm brown skin, gold eyes, no Legion Etchings, black hair worn loose. Her card gives her about a hundred and thirty-five years. She met Krothar on the shelf docks at fourteen, when he had not yet spoken outside the Vow. The courtship lasted six years, and one aunt called it scandalous. She is no practitioner and has never fought. She runs the Thunn-Gorr constituency seat in the Keth-Gorrum as its administrative anchor: correspondence, constituent records, census work. She also keeps the papers of a household whose earner fights across the world, the pay schedules, guild insurance, contract terms and repatriation clauses that bring a mercenary's pay home. The Keth-Gorrum's records division has tried to hire her twice. She wears a bone-white ear-drop of shelf-island volcanic glass that Krothar carved, and neither of them will say its shape. She raised their son on Gorr-Keth while his father was on contract, watched him train with his father's chains in the caldera yard, and watched him leave. He does not write or visit, and he goes by a name that is not hers. She keeps his room. She dusts the boyhood chains on the caldera-yard wall, which do not need it. She wants him home long enough to use his name.

**Krothar Veylshroud · The Chain Without a Master** (krothar-veylshroud-the-chain-without-a-master). He is Krothar Thunn-Gorr, called Veylshroud, whom Saiya alone calls Krothy, and his card gives him about fifty years, young for a Hobgoblin. He was born on Gorr-Keth and does not say so. His skin is forest green, the green of lichen on wet stone, where the Order runs red-ochre, and the Order noticed. He left the Expanse with his father's chain technique. He works as an unaffiliated mercenary. Silver chains answer him out of the Soul Plane unsummoned, and a black light, Death's Lance, comes only with his suppressors off. His father forged the suppressor chains at his wrists, forearms and throat after looking at his son's unmanaged output, and the son accepted them. After Death's Lance he asked for them back himself. Saiya, a Khajiit mercenary partner, holds the sealing rite. The chains were made for a lesser practitioner than he has become, and hairline fractures now show in the metal. He has not contacted the man who made them. He is in the Scourge of Hell on contracted bounty work, where he met Sodoku Moto, read him in one pass and filed him as unfinished business. He wants to finish the work and go somewhere quiet, and his card names the place: Gorr-Keth.

**Torven Greis · The Merchant Lord** (torven-greis-the-merchant-lord). A human of the Concord, forty-nine, born on the Inner World frontier to a family that crossed from the Old World's western coast in the late Long Reckoning. At seventeen, a carter's runner on a convoy taking structural iron south from the Ring smelters to the port, he watched the lead wagon go into a thaw-swollen gorge when the ward on a bridge footing thinned past holding. Nobody had paid for the inspection. The convoy master, running the route on a Nalūn factor's credit, lost everything. Torven bought his first way-station with borrowed salt before he was nineteen. The Greis Trading House keeps its head office at Chainward Landing, factor-offices at Ironlink Hold and at two Ring smelter camps, and eleven way-stations on the Iron-to-Salt Corridor. Nalūn traders call him the Grey Comptoir, and his title is Lord of the Chainward Road. He does not fight; he hires people who do. His Accord rank is collateral, and the Measurewright who certified him wrote in the margin that the instrument was working. He can feel whether a Nalūn-sealed contract is sound. He remembers every road he has travelled and inspects his own stations. His wife Dalla ran the Landing office while he ran the roads, and died of a fever the ward on her well-stone should have caught. Within the year he rebuilt the wards of every station on the network. Their son Edsen, twenty-three, is apprenticed to a shipwright at the Landing. In `the_revolution_of_the_inner_world.md` the empire buys his eleven stations in a single morning, above market, at a valuation Edward Lambert set himself, and the Greis name goes onto the northern line's freight contracts.

**Aren Vallestride · Stone-Runner** (aren-vallestride-stone-runner). A human of Old World borderland farmstead stock, an apprentice road courier and monster-scout. He rides on probation with a minor caravan-company, on a courier line chartered by the Guild Accord for low-risk routes along the frontier march. His village was abandoned in a minor Withering surge when a nearby Well went hostile, and there was nothing left to guard when he came of age to guard it. Nobody taught him to hook his weight into the earth. He found it the first time a wagon-raid nearly killed him. He carries a chipped short sword that belonged to a dead outrider, on the promise of returning it once he "earns the right to carry it", and finding the outrider's family is his standing debt. The march trusts him past his rank for two runs. In the Hollow Ford Run he ran through the night on failing legs to warn a village of a nest under its bridge, and his anchors collapsed the ford in the fight. In the Black Cart Ambush he tripped a raider charge; three wagons were lost and every civilian lived. Nobody knows whether the anchor is his own or an echo of the dead outrider's decades of road-duty in the sword. He will explain the anchors to anyone who asks.

**Garu Luneward · Winter Hound of the March** (garu-luneward-winter-hound-of-the-march). A human and Lupine hybrid of an Old World northern strain, born without a tail and without the Pack-Soul most Lupine share past infancy, which reads to his own kind as quietly wrong. He is a forward scout of the Hunter's Guild, working the frontier march for the Caravan Families. He reports to the Guild and belongs to no one there. He holds no pack and answers to no house, and the caravans are the nearest thing he has to either. The Families trust his tracking absolutely and understand almost nothing else about him. He can separate single heartbeats in a crowd and moves near-silently in armour. His bond formed with the act of tracking, at the moment he began to treat a trail as a promise someone else's fear had made him. He tracked a missing sled convoy through a three-day whiteout on fear alone and found survivors in a ravine others had missed. He hunted a Withering-touched direwolf pack alone for a week and turned it on itself with false scent. He wears the Blue Cloak of the Northern Watch. He answers questions about his methods with the fewest words that will close the conversation.

## 3 The shared history

Five events. The first two are on the Thunn-Gorr cards and this record only gathers them. The other three are new, and each is built to fit every card it touches.

### The caldera yard, the son's seventeenth year

**When.** The son's seventeenth year, which Freda's card gives, before he left the Expanse. The father was home.

**Where.** Gorr-Keth, the caldera yard of the Thunn-Gorr holdings on the caldera's western shelf.

**Who.** krothar-thunn-gorr-the-old-chain, freda-thunn-gorr-the-caldera-wife, krothar-veylshroud-the-chain-without-a-master.

**What happened.** The boy had trained in the yard with his father's chains since he could lift them, and Binding Lash was the first thing he learned. In his seventeenth year he passed his father's ceiling while his father and his mother watched. Neither spoke of it to the other, and neither has since. His skin had already told every Crimson Order Hobgoblin who saw him that he was not wholly one of theirs. The Order noticed, and the son left.

**What each took.** The father took the gap his card names, between what he taught and what grew on top of it, and the words he would say later: "I did not teach him what comes after." The mother took a subject she and her husband have never opened, and a room to keep. The son took his father's chain technique, his mother's patience, and a skin the Order could read at a glance. The chains he trained with stayed on the yard wall.

### The year of the suppressor chains

**When.** After the son left the Expanse and before the Scourge of Hell contract, where the scene finds him already chained.

**Where.** Gorr-Keth, where both parents' lines of that year were spoken. Neither card places the forge where the chains were made or the ground where they were put on him.

**Who.** krothar-thunn-gorr-the-old-chain, krothar-veylshroud-the-chain-without-a-master, freda-thunn-gorr-the-caldera-wife.

**What happened.** The father looked at his son's unmanaged output and made a decision. He had the craft to forge sigil shackles inscribed for the stone-blood, and he forged them for the son's wrists, forearms and throat. The son accepted them. Freda was not there. Her card has her watch from a distance she cannot close as whatever grew in him "required chains of a different kind". Afterward the father went back to the same forge and re-forged his own fighting chains with the same inscriptions, and it took him the better part of a season. That year he told Freda what he had and had not taught. She said her own line in the caldera yard, to no one, and has never repeated his.

**What each took.** The father took a fracture his card records as accommodated and unhealed, and fighting chains re-forged at the forge that made his son's shackles. The son took the leash he agreed to, which he still accepts, and which he asked for back himself after Death's Lance. Saiya now holds the rite that reseals it, and the chains are fracturing around him. He has not contacted his father. The mother took the chains as the whole of the conversation the family has had about him.

### The ford in the melt

**When.** New. After the son left the Expanse and before the year of the suppressor chains. It fell in Torven's early years on the corridor, while he still loaded his own wagons, which his card says he did for the first fifteen years of his career. The two men were close in age, and both were young.

**Where.** A ford on the Iron-to-Salt Corridor, the Greis road between the Ring smelter camps of the Upper Ring and Ironlink Hold. Which ford is not recorded.

**Who.** torven-greis-the-merchant-lord, krothar-veylshroud-the-chain-without-a-master.

**What happened.** Torven hired a young Hobgoblin chain-fighter out of the Expanse to ride escort on an iron run. The fighter gave a name and no island. On the way down to the salt the run came to a ford at the melt, and the river rose while the lead wagon was in it. The wagon began to go. The Hobgoblin threw a weighted chain round the wagon's tongue, planted on the bank and held it against the water, and the teamsters cut the team free and dragged the load back up the bank. No iron went into the river. Torven walked the ford himself the next morning.

**What each took.** Torven took a line in his supply book: the ford goes into it as one that goes out in the melt, and no Greis wagon has crossed it in the melt since. He paid the contract as written and walked that crossing himself every spring after. He did not stop expecting the next bridge to fail. The son took the contract's pay and work on a road, and his name went into a merchant's book. He sent nothing home.

### The Old Chain on the frontier march

**When.** New. In the Old Chain's contract years, long before Aren or Garu rode the march. The telling reached each of them on the march in their own time.

**Where.** The frontier march of Aren's and Garu's cards. Both cards give their stock as the Old World; no card or page places the march. The Old Chain's circuits reached the Old World's western quarter and the Inner World frontier, so the march lies within his range wherever it runs.

**Who.** krothar-thunn-gorr-the-old-chain, garu-luneward-winter-hound-of-the-march, aren-vallestride-stone-runner.

**What happened.** The Caravan Families hired the Old Chain to escort their trains on the march, and every contract ran to its term, as all of his did. The Families' old hands still tell of the Hobgoblin who did not chase. When a raid came he planted with his chains in the ground and let it come to him, and when it broke he let it go. He spoke seldom and explained nothing. The Families trusted his work and understood little else of him. His pay went home to Gorr-Keth on the papers Freda kept.

**What each took.** The Old Chain took finished contracts and his pay, and he left a name on the march that is still told there; he has no way of knowing it. Garu took the Families' measure of an outsider. They speak of the Old Chain and of their packless scout in the same terms, men whose work they trust and whose selves they cannot read. Aren heard the story from old hands in his probationary year, after the march had seen him hold ground. They said he stood the way the Old Chain had stood. He took the story and the name.

### The Hollow Ford Run

**When.** Aren's probationary term on the courier line, in the Withering Era. The run is on Aren's card; Garu's part is new.

**Where.** A village on the frontier march, its bridge and its ford. The village's name is not recorded.

**Who.** aren-vallestride-stone-runner, garu-luneward-winter-hound-of-the-march.

**What happened.** Garu was scouting ahead of a Caravan Families train that Aren's caravan-company travelled with, and downwind of a village he caught the scent of a Withering-spawned nest under its bridge. Had he left the nest he would have lost it. He stayed on it. Aren ran the warning through the night on failing legs and reached the village before the nest moved. When it moved, the fight came to the ford. Aren's anchors collapsed the crossing and cut the creatures off before they could swarm inland.

**What each took.** Aren took the trust of the guards and civilians who were there, which his card says outruns his rank, and a scout who tells him first. Garu took a runner. When a warning has to go back faster than the train can move, he stays on the trail and sends it by Aren.

## 6 Same person?

No two cards here are one person. The father and the son share a name: the son's card gives "Krothar Thunn-Gorr, called Veylshroud", and the father's card calls him "Krothar Thunn-Gorr (son), called Veylshroud". They are two lives. The father's card gives about a hundred and forty years and the son's about fifty, each card names the other, and Freda's names both. ## 7 Where the cards disagree

Between the cards:

- **Whether the Old Chain still fights.** His card: "Retired from active mercenary/guild contract work." Freda's card: "a household whose primary earner is a mercenary on contract across the world".

## 1 · Who is here

**Wystan Ashmore · Late Bell.** Officer of Attribution on the Night Register, the Guild Accord Arbitration Division's night desk on Cutler Row. His card files him "Northern register, chartered family, third son", and the byname Late Bell is the Division's. He holds the rank of Master, addressed as Warden of Duty. "Nobody in the districts he works uses it." He has been eleven years on the desk. At nineteen, clerking assay tins in a Measurewright's roll-room, he opened a sealed tin without gloves and received the last minute of a man nine years dead. "He filed the tin, went outside, was sick in the yard, then went back in and finished the shelf." Bare contact with a worked object gives him the operator's last minute, and the minute stays; his card counts forty-one. One is Osgood Keld's, the officer who trained him, who lies in the Division's medical house with his identity overwritten. His hands are stained brown with residue and shake in the mornings. He refuses "To sign an attribution he cannot derive." He believes naming who did a thing is the only justice left when the thing cannot be undone, and he chased a chartered house's purse, filed it accurately, and was moved to nights. Thirty-eight, tall and narrow, in black oiled wool, he holds everything by the edge with two fingers, even a cup.

**Qiu Yinzhi.** Twenty-three, capped eight months, of a Measurewright lineage and an artificer's apprentice; she carries coils for the Timberline chapter of the Night Watch Society. She wears her black hair in the double coil of the Measurewright lineages, pinned with plain iron. A regulation bracelet of three brass bands sits against her left wrist, and her thumb works its badly struck proof-mark when she thinks. The bracelet pulls toward a suppressed practitioner. Her master died two years before the scene, "after anamnetic contact off a coil housing pulled from a foundry fire, taken against advice"; he was sixty-one, and he spent his last eleven months correcting people who called him by the wrong name. He was right about half the time. She sat five days on a clean return she believed she was not good enough to have read. Then she came out in the rain to find the one man who might look. She brought two coils. At the close of the scene she is the assay of record on a Night Register exhibit, and her full name heads its docket.

**Nol Tally.** Forester and deer-counter of the Timberline chapter, North Ferriby walk, filed as "Tally, N." He walks forty miles of timber every week. His card calls him "The sort of man who measures." On the ninth he found eleven hinds and a stag dead where they stood inside a quarter mile of the Ferriby cut, measured the grey-green mass to nine inches at the tallest, and cut a section with his knife. On the tenth he walked north to the Ferriby stones and checked the clean ground past the parish marker twice. He counted four ravens that would not come down. On the fourteenth he filed his insert in person at the bulletin office, which he had never done, and asked whether the Night Register was a person. He has not walked his line since the eleventh. He carries a knife and a rule.

**Lu Sanjing.** A scholar, cited and never seen. In his studio, at the end of a long and distinguished career, he wrote the honest position on why Wellsprings surface, wander and return: "we do not know, and the instrument does not care that we do not know." His card files him in the lineage-hall register. It does not say whether he lives. Wystan holds his line against the eleven competing accounts and the one the Accord enforces.

**Edwyn Brack.** Classifier in the Lattice Classification Bureau's northern office, of the Northern stratum. His signature is on half the classifications that come out of it. He returned the Ferriby insert inside two days as "Wellspring bleed, Verdantia, tertiary expression, no operator indicated". His note put it down to a vein surfacing through a fault in the drift geology that would exhaust by autumn, and recommended no further action and no file. Wystan judged him correct off a true measurement. His card closes on one line: "The classification is exactly what Malphas's method is built to produce."

## 3 · The shared history

Four events. The first is new. The other three are the scene's, told as it tells them. Nothing here passes the close of the scene, where Yinzhi takes the pen off the bench and writes her name.

### The Timberline bench

- **When.** The archive's present, before the ninth. Yinzhi already carried the chapter's coils, and Tally already walked the North Ferriby walk. No card says when either began.

- **Where.** The Timberline chapter's bench, and the walks the chapter keeps. The scene gives the bench a standard to calibrate against and a morning hour to do it (259). No page places the chapter.

- **Who.** Nol Tally and Qiu Yinzhi; the chapter's walkers.

- **What happened.** New. Tally walked his forty miles each week and sent his counts in by other hands. He never carried one to the bulletin office himself. What he brought out of the timber came measured, by count and by rule, with the place given by the nearest stone. The chapter calibrated its coils at the bench, and Yinzhi carried them. When a walker's find wanted sealing, she went out to the ground with a tin. What came back she assayed at the bench. In that way she came to know his walk and his way of filing. Neither of them had any dealing with Cutler Row.

- **What each took.**
  - Tally: a coil-carrier of his own chapter who would come out to the ground and seal what he cut.
  - Yinzhi: a walker's habits she could read by. On the fourteenth she knew what "in person" meant for him.

### The Ferriby cut

- **When.** The ninth and tenth of the month the scene opens in. No month or year is named. By the night at Cutler Row, Vaeloris "had reformed after the void took it" (37).

- **Where.** The Ferriby cut on the North Ferriby walk; the Ferriby stones and a parish boundary marker to the north; the Timberline chapter's bench. The scene's country keeps counties and parishes, the Swale walks, timber towns in the east and the Marches. No page places any of it in a polity of WORLD §3. This record places none of it.

- **Who.** Nol Tally and Qiu Yinzhi. Four ravens in the birch.

- **What happened.** On the morning of the ninth Tally came down the Ferriby cut and found eleven hinds and a stag inside a quarter mile, all of them dead where they stood and all of them "carrying". A pale grey-green mass had come up through the hide along the spine and the flanks, fibrous at the base and soft at the crown. He measured it. Nine inches at the tallest. He cut a section with his knife, and the tissue under the mass had not decayed. Yinzhi sealed the section inside the hour, standing in the cut. The tin was Fixatio-anchored. Four ravens sat in the birch across the cut the whole time he worked. None came down. On the tenth he walked the line north to the Ferriby stones and found the ground clean. He went back and found the last carcass twenty-two paces short of the parish boundary marker, with clean ground on the far side of it. He checked the far side twice. That morning Yinzhi calibrated a coil at the chapter bench at the eighth hour and loaded the sample dry. She ran four passes, the last with the gate open. All four came back clean, and the fourth burned the coil.

- **What each took.**
  - Tally: a count, a height and a boundary checked twice, all written down. He did not walk his line after the eleventh. No page says why.
  - Yinzhi: a return with no author in it, run four times, and a burned coil. She did not file it. For five days she told herself she was not good enough to have read it.

### The return and the fourteenth

- **When.** After the ninth. The Bureau's return came "inside two days", and Tally carried his insert to the bulletin office on the fourteenth. The scene orders the two no more closely than that.

- **Where.** The Lattice Classification Bureau's northern office and the Night Watch Society's bulletin office. No page places either.

- **Who.** Edwyn Brack and Nol Tally; two of the Arbitration day-desk; the bulletin office clerk.

- **What happened.** The Ferriby insert reached the Bureau's northern office, and Brack returned it inside two days: Wellspring bleed, Verdantia, tertiary expression, no operator indicated. His note said the vein had probably surfaced through a fault in the drift geology and would exhaust itself by autumn. It recommended no further action and no file. Two of the day-desk agreed before they had finished reading. Neither Brack nor the day-desk mentioned the boundary. On the fourteenth Tally carried his insert to the bulletin office himself. He asked the clerk what happens to a filing after the Bureau closes it. The clerk said it goes to the Night Register. Tally asked whether the Night Register was a person, and the clerk said it was. The office printed the insert on its cheapest rag and nearly cut the last paragraph for length. It went up to Cutler Row in a bundle of nine.

- **What each took.**
  - Brack: one more return under his signature, correct off the measurement it was given.
  - Tally: the clerk's word that the Register was a person.

### Forty-two

- **When.** A night after the fourteenth. The main under Cutler Row had been off since the eleventh division. Yinzhi knocked at the third hour and a half, and the bell for the fourth division went while Wystan stood at the sink.

- **Where.** Cutler Row: the Night Register's desk, and the back room behind it, a scullery before the Division took the building, with the drain in the floor and the long stone sink under the window.

- **Who.** Wystan Ashmore and Qiu Yinzhi. Tally by his insert, Brack by his classification, Lu Sanjing by his line.

- **What happened.** Wystan had read nine inserts and one classification. He read Tally's insert twice and its last paragraph four times. In the margin he wrote *edges*, and under it *ask Tally who set the marker*. A bleed has no edge; a boundary means a hand, and a hand means Article XV and a purse behind it. Under that ran Lu Sanjing's line. Yinzhi came up the stair with a chit and the Ferriby tin, its seal unbroken and the chapter's wax across the join. She told him of Tally's line and his question at the bulletin office, and then of her clean return. He put on the gloves he always forgot. "Break the wax," he said. In the back room she read the ambient, low. Under the third lamp the wedge had grown, about a quarter inch, inside the sealed tin. He wrote it in the day book and moved on. Then he put a second question to the tin: "What is carrying the signature" (285). He had her open the gate and sweep below the Bureau's floor. She held the coil through the noise, and at a minute and forty the wire went over hard. The residue was signed by the hind. The deer had run the working on its own Lattice, so Brack's classification was correct. Wystan took off his gloves. Yinzhi told him about her master and would not stand in front of the bench. She asked him to count aloud afterward. He put his bare fingers on the wedge. He received the hind's last minute: warm, unafraid, standing while her legs came apart under her, the ravens arriving in the birch and not coming down. He was sick at the stone sink. Then he counted. Forty-two. He called it a design: pain makes an animal run, and whoever built this needed them to stay where they were put. He told her to write to Tally and have him keep walking and measuring. He wanted a fresh tin, the wax struck with his brass. From that night the wedge was a Register exhibit. Last he laid a blank docket in front of her and asked for her full name. She wrote it out.

- **What each took.**
  - Wystan: a forty-second minute, a hind "who was not frightened, and did not suffer", which he called "the worst thing in the tin" (373). A Register exhibit and an assay of record. A purse to chase under the only jurisdiction his desk has, "the first honest reason I have had in four years to be awake at this hour" (331). No hand anywhere in the cut.
  - Yinzhi: the hind's signature, found below the floor. Her name at the head of a Register docket. His warning that at a hearing they will come for her first. A letter to write to Tally.
  - Tally, absent: a request for a second dataset from the same observer. Wystan's read that he is of no interest to a man "who has arranged never to be within a mile of his own work" (387).
  - Brack, absent: the verdict of the officer who checked his return, that he "is not a fool and is not corrupt" (317).
  - Lu Sanjing, absent: his line, kept against eleven accounts at the third hour.

## 6 · Same person?

No. Five cards give five bodies and five offices, and nothing on any card folds one into another.

- Yinzhi's dead master and Lu Sanjing. Both belong to the lineage-hall register. The master was an artificer of a Measurewright line who took anamnetic contact against advice and died two years before the scene. Lu Sanjing wrote on why Wellsprings move, at the end of "a long and distinguished career". Nothing on either card makes them one man, and this record does not.

- Wystan Ashmore and House Ashmore. House Ashmore is the breadbasket among Kharven's six Banner Houses, seated at Ashbury (`The Banner Houses of Kharven.md`). Edric Ashmore speaks for its grain in part 5 of the Kharven manuscript. Pietro Ashmore, "of a branch house" (`UNCARDED.md`), and Leonardo and Valdís Ashmore appear in later parts. None of them is carded. Wystan's card: "Northern register, chartered family, third son." The archive note on part 5 asks for "Continuity to check: Edric Ashmore's house against Wystan Ashmore's card". No kinship is made.

- Osgood Keld and House Keld. Keld is the Banner House of the Polar Reach, and Haren Keld stands in the hall in parts 5 and 6 of the Kharven manuscript. Osgood Keld is an Arbitration officer on Wystan's card. No kinship is made.

- Nol Tally and the Dawi Tally. The Dawi keep a count of their own, the Tally, by entry number (WORLD §1). Nothing ties his name to it. Whether his name is a frozen surname or a byname from his counting, his card leaves open.

## 7 · Where the cards disagree

Quoted and left open.

1. **When Wystan went to nights.** Wystan's card: "Eleven years on the desk, four of them after the demotion" (I); "He chased a purse belonging to a chartered house two years ago, filed it accurately, and was moved to nights the following month" (XIV); "he has taken no soul-cost event in four years" (XVI). The scene: "He had held the title Warden of Duty for four years" (41), and "the first honest reason I have had in four years to be awake at this hour" (331).

2. **The dead at Ferriby.** Tally's card: "On the ninth he found eleven hinds and a stag dead where they stood inside a quarter mile of the Ferriby cut" (XIV). The scene, in Wystan's mouth at the bench: "Nine hinds and a stag are dead in a cut in the north" (345). At Greyshaft Nine: "Nine hinds hung on the rail." (105).

3. **The wedge's days.** Yinzhi's card: "Sat five days on a clean return" (XIV). The scene has her seal the tin "on the ninth" and assay it "on the tenth", then carry it "around for five days" (85, 89). It has the wedge "cut with a knife eleven days ago and sealed inside the hour" (273), and the day book reads "crown +¼ in sealed, 11d" (279). Tally filed on the fourteenth. The counts do not meet on one night.

4. **What Article XV reaches.** Wystan's card: "He can name who performed and never who paid" (X); "Article XV supplies the rest, since liability falls on the payer and his gift returns only the hand" (Notes). The scene: "a hand means Article XV and a purse behind it" (31); "Article Fifteen says the liability follows the payer" (177). The Articles page gives Article XV as *De voce*, the speaking: "Liability for a spoken working falls on the person who paid for it", and "A working that was spoken has no author in its own grammar." The Ferriby working was a formulation put into an animal (117 to 161). No line calls it spoken.

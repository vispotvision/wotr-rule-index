## Summary card
**Effect** · A trained listener reads a running working by its tone, names its weakest member, and answers with one small counter-note that pins or unravels it mid-cast. Turned the other way, a choir lifts a primary's working past what one caster could hold.
**Cost** · Half a percent of full reserve for a read, 3 percent for an Interference Lock, 1 percent a turn for a supporting voice or ritual stabilisation. A lock that goes wrong is paid twice.
**Limit** · It wins by timing, never by force: a finished working, a silent one, or a broadband working with no sharp line gives it nothing to grip.
**Counter** · Close the working in short bursts, stiffen the Crystal with Basilithe, cast loose and broadband, fill the room with noise, or hide the working below hearing with Tenebra or Luminalis.
**What nobody knows** · Whether a working's sand-plate figure is its true form, as the Figure school teaches, or only a shape that unrelated workings happen to share.

## Codex line
**Wellspring** · None of its own; it listens to the target working's Wellspring. Nihiloth, Basilithe and Aurevane describe what it does to other workings.
**Family** · None of its own; it reads whatever Family the target working draws on.
**Physics Domain** · Forced oscillation and superposition: a small, correctly timed wave in antiphase cancels a large one.
**Category** · Harmonia (Spirit and Attraction), with Aetherica supplying the clean baseline a reading is compared against.
**Craft** · Spellcraft, most often used against another Spellcraft or Runecraft working already in progress.
**Stage floor** · Glory
**Grade required** · A-Grade for perception; S-Grade for Interference Lock; SS-Grade for ritual stabilisation.
**Path gate** · Attraction and Spirit Paths for Attunement past C; without them it caps at C and cannot hold a frequency.

## Design Chain
**Trigger** · The Harmonist goes still, listens about a second to a working inside its casting window, and sings one short note, aloud below Refraction and silent above it.
**Function** · Pins one phase angle at one frequency on the weakest member of the target's chain, so the hostile working's boundary never closes.
**Mechanism** · Frequency laid under Knot. Every running working hums a line for each moving member, and a small wave in antiphase sums against a large one without having to overpower it.
**Numerical Effect** · A counter a thousandth the target's size locks a sharply tuned working in about a thousand cycles; a tenth-size counter takes about ten. A master reads, analyses and counters in under two seconds, and a choir of ten in phase gives a hundred times the intensity.
**Target Response** · The hostile working keeps casting, drawing and costing, and arrives nowhere; its caster feels a correct working producing nothing, and pushing harder only spends reserve.
**Consequence** · The locked working is pinned or unravelled, and its trace carries two frequency sets, which is how casework tells a countered working from a botched one.
**Limitation** · Reads diagnose drafts, rune arrays and enchantments without touching them; the lock needs the casting window and must finish before the cast; stabilisation lasts only while the phase holds.
**Weakness** · Echo Fracture: a counter on the wrong harmonic, or half a cycle out, amplifies the hostile working instead. The lead singer's Crystal Fracture risk rises with each supporting voice.
**Cost** · Half a percent of full reserve per read, 3 percent per lock, 1 percent a turn per supporting voice or stabilisation; thirty locks before Starvation.
**Counterplay** · Force your own Seal early, pre-claim your phase with Lock, present two workings with one figure, or work in a Thick Veil zone. A contested lock turns on the Harmonist's Harmonics Attunement against the caster's Harmonics Stability.

## Origin
**Origin** · Parunic monks of the Second Epoch, decoding glyph chants, found that Parunic syntax is a harmonic language. Thalos of Ira codified the branch through Chord Theory, and Aurevia the Echowright made it a combat art with Interference Lock.
**Practitioners** · Thalos of Ira; Aurevia the Echowright; the Cantic Order, whose choirs teach "break the song, do not block the spell"; the Sonochoral Conclave's chanters; Genn Arkelos, the guild Frequencywright who diagnosed leyline anomalies during the early Imperial Convergences.

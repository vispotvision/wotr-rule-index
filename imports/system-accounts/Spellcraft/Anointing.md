# System account · Anointing

## 1 · Physical account (physics)

**The phenomenon in plain words.** A mark is placed on something, and the mark
does no work of its own. What it does is change what the thing is recognised as,
so that everything downstream begins treating it differently — and the mark
outlasts whoever placed it.

**The real phenomenon.** **High-affinity binding, measured by a dissociation
constant**, and **covalent post-translational modification**, which is what a
standing mark actually is in a system made of molecules.

*The affinity figure.* The strength of a biological bond is not a mood. It is a
number: the **dissociation constant K_d**, the concentration at which half the
sites are occupied, and a low K_d means the complex, once formed, spends almost
all of its time formed. A good antibody binds its antigen at **10⁻⁹ M**. The
biotin–streptavidin pair, one of the strongest non-covalent bonds known, binds at
about **10⁻¹⁴ M**, with an off-rate of order **10⁻⁵ s⁻¹** — a residence time
measured in **hours to days** for a bond held together by nothing but shape and
electrostatics. This is Benediction's own register, stated as a law: *"The
strength of a biological bond is a measurable physical quantity … This is the
mechanical reason an oath sworn under Benediction carries system weight: **it has
an affinity figure, and the Continuum reads it.**"*

*The mark that is not the function.* A phosphate group placed on a serine
residue does nothing. It has no activity, breaks no bond, supplies no energy. It
is **a tag**, and what follows from it is entirely a matter of what else in the
cell is built to recognise a phosphoserine at that position: a kinase writes it,
a phosphatase erases it, and a dozen downstream proteins change what they will
bind because of it. **The modification alters what the molecule is *willing to
carry*, and nothing else.** That is the page's central claim with the vocabulary
swapped: *"the marking is not decorative: it alters what the subject's Soul
Crystal is willing to carry."*

*And the false mark, which is the page's whole history.* Two failure modes are
available and the corpus uses both. A mark can be placed **at the wrong residue**
— chemically identical, indistinguishable to any assay that only asks *is the tag
present*, and recognised by nothing. Or a mark can be **displaced**: Benediction's
Failure is *"Competitive inhibition. A rival ligand with higher affinity displaces
the existing bond, and the displacement is **silent, complete, and not felt until
the bond is called on**."* Both produce exactly what the page describes as the
False Anointing: *"a mark indistinguishable to an untrained eye from the real
thing and mechanically empty."*

**The governing law.** **Mass action at a binding site**, K_d = k_off/k_on, with
the residence time 1/k_off deciding whether a mark is a ceremony or a structure.
And **recognition rather than activity**: the mark's consequences are the
behaviour of everything that reads it.

**Measurable quantities, SI.** The page states a Stage and a Grade and nothing
else (`SA-GAP-ANOINTING-NO-NUMBERS`). What the law supplies as checkable
consequence:

- **Durability is a residence time and is therefore a number.** The difference
  between a rite that holds for an afternoon and one that holds for a Temperance
  path is a factor in k_off, and the corpus's own high-affinity comparison —
  hours to days for the best non-covalent bond in biochemistry — says that
  anything lasting a **career** must be covalent, which is Fixatio's register
  exactly: *"A thermoset, once cured, has formed covalent bridges across its whole
  network and will char before it flows."* **The page's two secondary Wellsprings
  are the two answers to 'how long', and it chose the right one.**
- **A mark's effect is a property of the reader, not of the mark.** Which means
  an Anointing recognised by nobody is mechanically empty even if it is
  metaphysically genuine, and an Anointing recognised by everybody is mechanically
  live even if — see below — it is not.
- **Detection requires an assay, not an inspection.** Judicium's spectroscopy is
  the assay and the page assigns it correctly: *"Judicium, the Wellspring of
  Truth, of Fulguria, for the discernment an officiant needs to recognise a
  genuine calling rather than manufacture a false one."* No substance can present
  another's spectrum; a mark with no affinity figure reads as a mark with no
  affinity figure, **to an instrument.** To an eye it reads as a mark.

**The energy budget, and where the joules were.** Negligible, and permanently so.
A recognition tag costs the energy to place it and nothing thereafter; everything
that happens downstream is paid for by the systems doing the recognising. This is
`Two Sets of Books` §III at its most extreme — the practitioner supplies a
boundary condition of almost no energetic content and the consequences are
carried entirely by others — and it is why a working that can *"awaken a latent
Wellspring alignment"* and *"unseal glyphs that were otherwise dormant"* sits at a
Stage VI floor rather than somewhere frightening.

**Precedent we are not copying.** The blessing that makes you the chosen one is
the oldest device in the genre and is almost always a power-up with a ceremony
attached. Two things make this one different and both are institutional. First,
**it confers recognition and explicitly not dominion** — *"the card is right to
distinguish it from Coronation, which grants dominion, since Anointing grants
recognition instead"* — so the thing it hands over is a **claim**, which someone
else has to honour. Second, **the forgery is perfect and the consequence of the
forgery is not.** A false Anointing is undetectable by eye, mechanically empty,
and *"cannot be undone casually"*, so *"a false Anointing left standing can
misdirect a subject's Crystal for the rest of their Temperance path if nobody
catches it."* A power-up you may have been lied about, for decades, is a much
better object than a power-up.

## 2 · Stratal account (metaphysics)

**Aether stratum.** Draw is a single ceremonial expenditure. **Residue:** the
mark itself, and it is the point rather than a by-product — *"a standing,
Traitus-category mark visible to anyone with sufficient Aetheric perception."*
The Core Vocabulary's *"Aetheric Residue … A permanent, if often subtle, change
to the location where the working occurred. Always readable by someone patient
enough"* here attaches to a person rather than a place. **Saturation:** not a
factor.

**Wellspring stratum.** Three, and the page's division of labour is exact.

> **Benediction · *The Wellspring of Grace*** *(Vitalia)* — *"The strength of a
> biological bond is a measurable physical quantity, and a low dissociation
> constant means the complex, once formed, spends almost all of its time formed.
> Benediction gives bonds on the Plane of Fate that same measurability. This is
> the mechanical reason an oath sworn under Benediction carries system weight: it
> has an affinity figure, and the Continuum reads it."* Stat Effect: *"Strengthens
> Harmonics Empathy, Axis, Resilience Oath. Bonds formed under Benediction carry
> mechanical weight in the system."* Failure: *"**Competitive inhibition.** A
> rival ligand with higher affinity displaces the existing bond, and the
> displacement is silent, complete, and not felt until the bond is called on."*
> (`wiki/The Eight Families & the Sixty Wellsprings/Vitalia — Biochemistry.md`)

The page cites this law almost verbatim as its justification — *"Mechanically
this runs through Benediction, whose documented law is that bonds formed under it
carry mechanical weight rather than merely social or narrative weight"* — and
then does not carry the Failure, which is the single most important thing the
register says about this working. **Competitive inhibition means an Anointing can
be *displaced* by a higher-affinity claim, silently, with the subject learning of
it only when they call on it.** A whole genre of betrayal sits in that sentence
and the page does not know it is there (`SA-PHYS-ANOINTING-COMPETITIVE-INHIBITION`).

> **Fixatio · *The Binding Flame*** *(Materia)* — *"What it holds is not held by
> force but by the fact that the alternative configuration no longer exists as a
> reachable state."* Failure: *"Irreversibility. The cure is not undone by wanting
> it undone, and a Fixatio binding placed in error is a permanent feature of
> whatever it was placed on."*

This is why the mark is durable and why *"it cannot be undone casually"*, and the
Failure is the page's own worst case stated in the register: **a binding placed
in error is a permanent feature of whatever it was placed on.** The False
Anointing is a Fixatio binding placed in error.

> **Judicium · *The Wellspring of Truth*** *(Fulguria)* — *"no substance can
> present another's spectrum … Eddy-current inspection finds the crack under the
> paint without removing the paint."* Failure: *"Spectral overload."*

The assay, and the officiant's actual skill. The page assigns it to
*"the discernment an officiant needs to recognise a genuine calling rather than
manufacture a false one"*, which is correct and is also the counter to the whole
False Anointing tradition — **the forgery is undetectable by eye and detectable
by instrument.**

*Four Theories.* **The Consent Doctrine**, enforced, and unusually visibly. *"The
Wellspring recognises a lawful boundary condition and behaves accordingly … This
is the position that permits licensing, because a process can be licensed and a
relationship cannot"* (`Two Sets of Books` §IV). Anointing is the working that
makes the Accord's enforcement decision awkward, because the rite's own doctrine
is that a genuine Anointing is a **relationship** — a subject chosen *"by a
Wellspring current, an Archon, or the officiating order's own standing
authority"* — and a relationship cannot be licensed. The Order of Benediction
licenses it anyway. That tension is the page's whole political content and the
Registers' footnote covers it: the enforcement *"is a licensing decision rather
than a finding."*

**Essence stratum.** Active layers: the **Attraction Layer**, which *"holds
bonds, Domains, and Wellspring harmonizations"* and is where a recognition is
held; the **Essence Core**, which is where the resulting **Traitus** mark sits,
since a Trait is *"a standing law held in the Essence Core"*; and the officiant's
own Core, which is the thing they are putting on the line. Developmental Tier at
the Stage VI floor: **Harmonic** (Part Twenty-One). Crystal State: not stated.
**The invoice:** the page states a cost in kind and it is a good one — *"Glory's
own Catalyst, willingly accepting genuine soul-cost to preserve something valued
above one's own structural integrity, is the correct floor for a rite that asks
an officiant to vouch, with their own Crystal, for a claim about someone else's
purpose; an officiant below that Stage has nothing of their own on the line to
lend the rite, **which is the mechanical reason a false or unearned Anointing is
detectable at all.**"* That is the best-reasoned Stage floor in the section: the
floor is set by what the rite requires the officiant to *stake*, and the stake is
what makes forgery detectable. No EU figure exists (`SA-GAP-ANOINTING-NO-NUMBERS`).

**The lens: the indelible character.** Augustine against the Donatists —
*Contra epistolam Parmeniani* II.13.28 — and its systematisation in Aquinas,
*Summa Theologiae* III q.63. The question was practical and bloody: are the
sacraments administered by a bishop who handed over the scriptures under
persecution valid? The Donatists said no; an unworthy minister confers nothing,
and those baptised by traitors must be rebaptised. Augustine said yes, and
founded the answer on the **character indelebilis**: baptism, confirmation and
orders imprint on the soul a mark that is permanent, unrepeatable, and
independent of the worth of either the minister or the recipient. The sacrament
works *ex opere operato*, by the work worked. **That is Anointing's mechanism,
down to the vocabulary** — a permanent Traitus mark, *"it cannot be undone
casually, since a standing Traitus mark does not lapse the way a sustained
working does"*, conferring recognition rather than power. Where the lens
misleads is the exact point on which Augustine won the historical argument and
would be wrong here. *Ex opere operato* says the correctly performed rite is
valid regardless of the officiant, and a school holding it **will not test an
Anointing**, because on its doctrine there is nothing to test: form is validity.
The page says the opposite in plain words — *"A rite performed with the correct
words and no genuine current behind it produces a mark indistinguishable to an
untrained eye from the real thing and mechanically empty"* — so an Augustinian
order is, by its own theology, the perfect victim of the False Anointing
tradition, and the page's own crisis is what that looks like at scale. And the
symmetry is worth having: **the Donatist error is available too**, and it is
rejecting genuine marks because the officiant was unworthy, which in a system
where the officiant stakes their own Crystal is not obviously wrong either. Both
schools argue about worth. Neither runs the assay, and Judicium is sitting right
there. Offered as a school's error, not as canon.

## 3 · Mechanism (the effect)

**Glyph (uncertain, and the page says so).** The page names three from its source
card — **Lo**, **Wy**, **Ka** — and reports that two of them are attested
elsewhere with different readings and the third is not attested at all:
*"Wy and Ka are attested elsewhere in current canon in the Identity and Force
strata respectively, as Truth and Cycle, not as the readings the card gives; Lo
does not appear in any glyph fragment available to this conversion."* None of the
three appears in the **thirty mirrored forms** of
`wiki/The Magic System/The Master Glyph Index — 136 Attested Forms.md`, which
carries thirty of one hundred and thirty-six entries, so this account cannot
check them either (`SA-GAP-GLYPH-MIRROR`). The page's own recommendation stands:
*"The chain as given should be checked against the Master Glyph Index before
being treated as final."* **This account proposes no substitute**, because the
page has named glyphs and a proposal would be a third reading rather than a
check. `SA-UNATT-ANOINTING-GLYPHS`.

**What boundary moves.** **What the subject's Crystal counts as its own.**
Ordinarily an Attraction-Layer recognition is a thing the Crystal has built by
living; the rite establishes one from outside, at a site the Crystal will
thereafter treat as belonging to it. Nothing is added to the subject's capacity.
A site is made recognisable.

**What the law then does on its own.** It binds, and then everything that reads
the site behaves accordingly. Benediction supplies the affinity figure, so the
recognition is not a claim about the subject but a **measurable property of the
subject** that the Continuum can read; Fixatio cures it, so the alternative
configuration — the subject as unmarked — stops being a reachable state; and
after that the consequences are simply what a recognised thing gets. The
Crystal *"begins answering to the recognition"*, dormant glyphs that were waiting
on an authorisation receive one, and a latent Wellspring alignment the subject had
not yet accessed becomes accessible — not because the rite supplied access, but
because **access was gated on a recognition the subject now has.**

And the empty rite fails for the same reason, in reverse. *"A rite performed
without that harmonisation, using only ceremony and no genuine current behind it,
produces exactly the failure the card's own history names: a mark that looks like
the real thing and carries none of its weight."* A tag with no affinity figure is
a tag nothing reads. It is present. It is visible. It is not a site.

**Law V check.** The floor is **Stage VI, Glory**, below Refraction, so an
external anchor is required (`The Four Crafts`, Law V). A Ritus supplies one by
construction — a rite is performed, with words, hands and usually oil — and the
page's Craft line is consistent: *"Spellcraft, consistent with the destination
shelf and with Ritus's standing durability rule."* **The gate closes.** But the
durability clause the page adds in the same sentence does not sit inside
Spellcraft's rule: *"the mark the rite leaves is a Traitus-category standing
effect rather than a sustained working, so it persists after the officiant's
attention moves on, unlike most Spellcraft outputs."* That is the same tension
`SA-CROSS-EDICTUM-SPELLCRAFT-DURABILITY` records for Edictum, Imprinta and
Kurotana — **four pages in this section produce durable objects under a Craft
whose rule is that it lapses** — and the page is at least honest that it is an
exception.

**Failure mode — Boundary fault, and it is a forgery rather than an accident.**
*"The condition was established imprecisely and the law ran somewhere
unintended"* (`Two Sets of Books` §VII). Here the condition is *this subject has
been chosen*, and the fault is that the condition can be **stated without being
true**, in a form no inspection distinguishes from the true one. The law then
runs nowhere at all, which is a rarer and nastier kind of Boundary fault than the
usual burn: nothing unintended happens, and the absence of anything happening is
itself invisible until the mark is called on.

Two further faults from the registers and not on the page. **Benediction's
competitive inhibition** — a higher-affinity claim displaces the Anointing
*"silently, completely, and not felt until the bond is called on"*, which is a
genuine Anointing becoming an empty one without anybody performing a rite.
**Fixatio's irreversibility** — *"a Fixatio binding placed in error is a permanent
feature of whatever it was placed on"*, which is why a false Anointing is not
merely useless but **load-bearing in the wrong direction for the rest of a
Temperance path.**

**What the target sees and feels.** A rite, performed properly, by someone with
standing — and then a mark that other people can see and they mostly cannot.
What changes is not felt as power. It is felt as **permission**: things that were
not available become available, and the subject has no experience of having
acquired anything. A Wellspring alignment they never reached answers. A glyph
that had been inert since birth is not inert. Their own Crystal, which had been
declining to carry something, carries it. And the thing that makes this working
frightening rather than pleasant is that **the counterfeit is subjectively
identical at the moment of the rite** — the words are the words, the officiant is
the officiant, the mark is visible — and the difference only appears at the
moment of use, possibly years later, possibly in the one moment that mattered.
The page's own worst case is the ordinary case: *"a false Anointing left standing
can misdirect a subject's Crystal for the rest of their Temperance path if nobody
catches it."*

**What bleeds, at the stated efficiency.** No practitioner has a card
(`SA-GAP-ANOINTING-NO-CARD`). At the Stage VI floor the Tier of Standing is
**5 · Expert**, η **0.60–0.70** (Part Nineteen as corrected by **R44-4**):
**30 to 40 percent** of the
officiant's expenditure leaves as heat, sound and structural bleed. The
expenditure being unquantified, the bleed is too — but the page's own reasoning
about the Stage floor supplies the interesting version anyway: what the officiant
spends is **their own standing**, staked on a claim about somebody else, and the
efficiency of *that* is not measured in η.

**The arithmetic, and what it turns up.** One derivation and one gate.

*The derivation.* The system's own strongest non-covalent bond analogue — a K_d
of 10⁻¹⁴ M, the best in biochemistry — holds for **hours to days**. An Anointing
holds for a **Temperance path**. So under Benediction alone the mark would lapse
within a week, and the page's inclusion of **Fixatio** is not decorative: it is
the difference between an affinity-held mark and a cured one, and it is the
reason the page can claim permanence. **The page's Wellspring stack is load-
bearing and correct, and the register supplies the number that proves it.**

*The gate.* The page says *"Path gate"* nowhere at all, and Part Seven gates all
three of Benediction's Stat Effects on the **Attraction Path**, two of them at
Stage IV to exceed B — below the rite's own A-Grade requirement. So an Anointing
cannot be performed by an officiant without an Attraction Path, and nothing says
so. That is the fifth instance of the same omission in this section
(`SA-CROSS-EDICTUM-PATH-GATE` and its kin).

**Does the physics close against the stratal account?** **Yes, and the closure is
the best argument for the page's own design.** A recognition tag costs almost
nothing to place and is paid for downstream by everything that reads it, which is
exactly why a rite conferring access to dormant Wellspring alignments and sealed
glyphs sits at a modest Stage floor with no stated EU cost — **there is very
little to bill.** The stratal account bills the one thing that is genuinely
scarce, which is the officiant's standing, and the page's reasoning about why
Glory is the floor is a stratal argument rather than a numerical one and is
sound. The two books agree that this working is cheap in energy and expensive in
credit.

**What does not close is the Failure.** Benediction's competitive inhibition is
not on the page, and it is the mechanism by which a *genuine* Anointing becomes
an empty one with no rite performed and no warning given. A working whose entire
subject is the reliability of a mark, whose Wellspring's documented failure is
the silent displacement of marks, should carry it.
`SA-PHYS-ANOINTING-COMPETITIVE-INHIBITION`.

## 5 · Counterplay and the challenge

**The fairness check** (`.claude/skills/wotr-write/references/fair-play.md`).

| Test | Verdict | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Costs something that hurts | **pass on reasoning, fail on accounting** | The stake is exactly right and the page's argument for it is the best in this section: an officiant *"vouches, with their own Crystal, for a claim about someone else's purpose"*, and *"an officiant below that Stage has nothing of their own on the line to lend the rite."* A cost that makes forgery detectable is a cost doing real structural work. But it is stated in kind and never in quantity — no EU, no Sub-Stat, no threshold, no count — so an officiant can perform an unlimited number of Anointings and nothing tracks it. |
| Stated limits | **pass** | *"It cannot confer legitimacy from ceremony alone"*; *"it cannot grant dominion or rule, which remains Coronation's separate function"*; *"it cannot be undone casually."* Three limits, all of them consequential, and the middle one draws a genuine line between two rites the corpus could easily have collapsed. |
| Something beats it | **pass, and the best route is not on the page** | Below. Assay it, outbid it, or exploit the fact that a mark is only worth what its readers say. |
| It has a tell | **pass, and the tell is the problem** | *"A standing, Traitus-category mark visible to anyone with sufficient Aetheric perception."* The mark is highly visible — and **the false mark is equally visible**, which turns a good tell into a liability: the thing everyone can see is the thing that tells them nothing. |
| Numbers in band | **cannot be checked** | Stage VI ⇒ Max Grade A (276–400), ceiling 400, Tier 5 (Part Five), consistent with the page's A-Grade. No EU, no duration, no range, no carded officiant, and three glyphs whose readings the page itself flags as contested. |

**The Counterplay routes that work**
(`wiki/The Magic System/Counterplay What Beats a Practitioner.md`).

- **Break the boundary — run the assay, which is the counter the page names and
  does not follow through.** Judicium is on the page as the officiant's
  discernment, and it is equally the **inspector's**: *"no substance can present
  another's spectrum … Eddy-current inspection finds the crack under the paint
  without removing the paint."* A mark with no affinity figure is detectable to an
  instrument and invisible to an eye, so the entire False Anointing tradition is
  beaten by a competent Judicium practitioner and by nothing else. **A player who
  reads the Fulguria register knows to bring a Measurewright to a coronation.**
- **Break the boundary — outbid it, and the page has no idea this is possible.**
  Benediction's own Failure: *"**Competitive inhibition.** A rival ligand with
  higher affinity displaces the existing bond, and the displacement is silent,
  complete, and not felt until the bond is called on."* A higher-affinity claim on
  the same site **displaces an Anointing without unmaking it**, and the subject
  does not find out until they call on it. This is the single best counter in this
  account, it is nowhere on the technique page, and it is one line down in the
  Vitalia register.
- **Break the man — refuse to read the mark.** A tag's effect is a property of
  its readers. An Anointing confers *recognition*, and recognition is a claim that
  somebody else has to honour; a faction, an order or a Realm that declines to
  recognise it has not attacked anything and has removed most of what it does.
  The page's own boundary is the hinge: *"it cannot grant dominion or rule, which
  remains Coronation's separate function."* **Recognition without dominion is
  political, and politics is answerable politically.**
- **Break the boundary — Rescind, if consent was never given.** The
  `Precept` page in this same section names the relevant effect: *"Rescind, the
  withdrawal-of-consent effect attested elsewhere in the corpus, is the clearest
  documented counter to a soul-bound Precept, and only when the Precept was never
  lawfully consented to in the first place."* An Anointing performed on an
  unwilling or deceived subject is a soul-bound mark without lawful consent, which
  is the condition Rescind is documented for. Worth a ruling
  (`SA-GAP-ANOINTING-RESCIND`).
- **Break the man — the officiant staked their own Crystal.** Every Anointing an
  officiant performs is a claim they have vouched for with their own standing, and
  `Counterplay` IV's *"Coherence drift"* is what happens when a person's lived
  behaviour and stated law come apart. **An officiant who has knowingly performed
  a false Anointing has staked their Crystal on a lie**, and the register's advice
  is to notice and wait.

**The tell, stated plainly.** The mark, which everyone can see and which proves
nothing. The real tell is second-order and archival: **an Anointing has an
officiant, an order, a date and a claim**, and all four are checkable by someone
who cares to check. The forgery survives an eye and does not survive a record.

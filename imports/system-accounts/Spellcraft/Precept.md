# System account · Precept

## 1 · Physical account (physics)

**The phenomenon in plain words.** A region is given a structure, and the
structure removes a possibility rather than opposing one. Afterwards nothing
resists anything: whatever tries to take the removed shape simply finds there is
no such shape available there, and the region is not doing any work to make that
so.

**The real phenomenon.** Two laws, and the page has reached for both without
naming either.

*The constraint that does no work.* In Lagrangian mechanics a **holonomic
constraint** is not a force. A bead on a wire does not feel the wire pushing it
along the wire; the wire has **deleted a degree of freedom**, and the constraint
forces that appear in the accounting satisfy d'Alembert's condition — the virtual
work they do is exactly zero:

**Σ Rᵢ · δrᵢ = 0**

That equation is the page's own best sentence written in mechanics: *"the law is
not enforced from outside afterward. It is load-bearing from the moment it is
sealed."* A constraint is load-bearing and costs nothing to hold, which is
precisely why a Precept can outlive its author. **Nothing has to keep enforcing
it, because enforcement was never the mechanism.**

*The forbidden band, which is what the removal looks like from inside.* Give a
medium a periodic structure and its dispersion relation acquires **gaps**:
frequency ranges in which no propagating solution exists. A wave at a gap
frequency is not attenuated and is not reflected off a barrier; there is no mode
for it to be. A quarter-wave stack of two ordinary dielectrics, n₁ = 1.5 and
n₂ = 2.3, has a gap of fractional width

**Δω/ω₀ = (4/π)·arcsin[(n₂ − n₁)/(n₂ + n₁)] = (4/π)·arcsin(0.211) = 0.27**

— twenty-seven percent of the centre frequency, from nothing but alternating two
transparent materials. Inside that band the structure is not strong. It is
**empty of the option**, and the page's hardest claim is the physically correct
one: *"other workings, other oaths, and in the right circumstance other people,
do not defy a Precept so much as discover that the space it named will not hold
the shape they were trying to give it."*

*And the way a band gap is actually beaten, which is the counter and is not on the
page.* You do not overpower a gap and you do not argue with it. You **break the
periodicity in one place.** A single defect in an otherwise perfect lattice
creates exactly one **localized state inside the gap** — a narrow allowed mode at
a specific frequency, at a specific site, where a moment ago there was nothing.
This is how every photonic-crystal waveguide and every defect-mode laser is built.
The page's *"a Precept is unmade by a correction to the law, not by breaking the
stone it is cut into"* is right, and the physics sharpens it into something a
player can use: the correction does not destroy the rule. **It opens one narrow,
precisely-placed exception inside a rule that otherwise still stands**, and the
rule's own regularity is what localizes it.

**The governing law.** **Constraint rather than force** — d'Alembert's principle,
constraint forces doing zero virtual work — with **band structure** (Bloch's
theorem in a periodic medium) supplying what the constrained region looks like to
anything inside it, and **defect-state localization** supplying the only route out.

**Measurable quantities, SI.** The page states no Stage, no Grade, no cost, no
duration and no glyphs (`SA-GAP-PRECEPT-NO-STAGE`). What the law supplies as
checkable consequence:

- **Upkeep is exactly zero and that is a theorem, not a convenience.** Constraint
  forces do no work. So the page's *"It can outlast its author, the way an
  inscription outlasts the mason"* is not a property the working was given; it is a
  property it could not have avoided. A Precept that cost anything to maintain
  would not be a constraint, it would be an enforcement, and everything else on the
  page would be false.
- **Strength scales with periodicity, not with the author.** Reflectivity at a gap
  frequency rises exponentially with the number of periods in the stack, which means
  the sharpness of a Precept is a function of **how regularly the clause was laid
  down** rather than of how strong the person laying it was. This is the mechanical
  version of the page's *"Fixatio does not care who is still standing."*
- **The gap has edges, and outside them the medium is ordinary.** A band gap
  forbids one frequency range and is completely transparent elsewhere. That is the
  page's *"It cannot compel what falls outside the clause it names. A Precept
  written for one sphere has no purchase past its stated boundary"* — correct, and
  the physical reason is that a constraint deletes the degrees of freedom it names
  and no others.
- **One defect, one state.** The number of allowed modes opened inside a gap is set
  by the number of defects, which means a lawful correction is **countable**. A
  single exception does not soften a Precept generally; it makes one thing possible
  at one place. That is a rule a table can adjudicate and it is not stated anywhere.

**The energy budget, and where the joules were.** **In the substrate, paid once,
and none of it in the holding.** The inscription costs what laying down a periodic
structure costs; the constraint costs nothing thereafter, because constraint forces
do no work. `Two Sets of Books` §III — *"The practitioner does not supply the
joules. They supply the boundary condition"* — is not merely satisfied here, it is
the working's entire content: **a Precept is a boundary condition and nothing
else.** Which is also why its counter is the counter to any inscription: the
substrate is where the whole cost is sitting, and a substrate can be chiselled.

**Precedent we are not copying.** Binding law-magic is a staple: the geas of Celtic
material and its descendants, *Fate*'s Geas Scrolls, *Hunter × Hunter*'s Nen
conditions, the D&D *Geas* spell. All of those are **enforcement** — a penalty
clause, a punishment that fires when the subject transgresses, which means the
subject *can* transgress. This one has no penalty clause anywhere in it, because
nothing is being punished and nothing can transgress: the shape is not available.
The divergence that matters for scenes: **there is no moment of defiance.** The
interesting hour is a clerk with a straight edge deciding how wide to write the
clause, and the interesting fight, years later, is over where the one defect goes.

## 2 · Stratal account (metaphysics)

**Aether stratum.** Draw is a single front-loaded cost and then nothing at all,
which makes this the only working in this section with **zero standing draw**.
**Residue:** heavy, located and *intended* — the inscription is the residue, it is
the point, and it is the thing an opponent can find and touch. **Saturation:** not
a hazard, and the reason is worth stating because it inverts the section's usual
problem: a working with no sustained expenditure deposits nothing continuously, so
a Precept can be filed in a place people have to live in. That is why the guilds
use it for codes and the Ritus Orders do not use Fallacy for anything.

**Wellspring stratum.** **Fixatio · *The Binding Flame***, Family **Materia**. Its
law, verbatim:

> **Analogue** · *"Covalent cross-linking. Thermoset cure."*
> **Mechanism** · *"A thermoplastic can be melted and reformed indefinitely. A
> thermoset, once cured, has formed covalent bridges across its whole network and
> will char before it flows. Fixatio performs the cure. What it holds is not held
> by force but by the fact that the alternative configuration no longer exists as a
> reachable state."*
> **Stat Effect** · *"Strengthens Vitality Fortitude, Resilience Anchoring. What is
> held by Fixatio stays held."*
> **Failure** · *"Irreversibility. The cure is not undone by wanting it undone, and
> a Fixatio binding placed in error is a permanent feature of whatever it was
> placed on."*
> (`wiki/The Eight Families & the Sixty Wellsprings/Materia — Material Science.md`:60)

**The register's Mechanism is the band gap, word for word, and it was not written
for this page.** *"What it holds is not held by force but by the fact that the
alternative configuration no longer exists as a reachable state"* is the definition
of a forbidden band and the definition of a holonomic constraint at the same time,
and it is the sentence that makes every claim on the Precept page defensible. The
two books agree so exactly here that the physical account had almost nothing left
to add.

**And the Failure is the page's missing cost.** *"Irreversibility. The cure is not
undone by wanting it undone, and a Fixatio binding placed in error is a permanent
feature of whatever it was placed on."* The page gestures at this — *"it cannot be
argued out of existence by force alone"* — as though it were a strength. It is the
bill. A Precept written a little too wide is a permanent feature of whatever it was
written on, it costs nothing to keep being wrong, and the clerk who wrote it will be
dead long before anyone notices which cases it caught. Nothing on the page carries
that as a cost (`SA-FAIR-PRECEPT-NO-COST`).

**Judicium · *The Wellspring of Truth*** *(Fulguria)* is the second current and it
does the half Fixatio cannot:

> **Mechanism** · *"Every element absorbs and emits at wavelengths fixed by its own
> electron structure, and no substance can present another's spectrum. Judicium
> tunes Gnosis to that principle: the practitioner reads the characteristic lines of
> a Crystal, a technique, or a lie, and identifies composition from signature
> alone."*
> **Failure** · *"Spectral overload. Every object in view reports its composition
> simultaneously and the practitioner loses the ability to prioritise any of it."*
> (`wiki/The Eight Families & the Sixty Wellsprings/Fulguria — Electromagnetism.md`:19)

Fixatio sets the gap; **Judicium sets its edges** — which cases fall inside the
clause, which is the whole craft of drafting one. And Judicium's Failure is the
characteristic failure of a Precept written badly: *every object in view reports
simultaneously*, which for a clause means every case reads as caught by it, and a
law that catches everything has stopped discriminating. The page's own limit —
*"A Precept written for one sphere has no purchase past its stated boundary"* — is
Judicium working. The absence of that limit is Judicium overloading.

*Four Theories.* **Consent Doctrine**, and it is doing more here than anywhere else
in this section — which is why the page's one named counter is a consent counter.
The page: *"a geasa that binds a named party rather than the world at large"*, and
*"Rescind, the withdrawal-of-consent effect attested elsewhere in the corpus, is the
clearest documented counter to a soul-bound Precept, and only when the Precept was
never lawfully consented to in the first place."* A Precept filed over a space needs
no consent; a Precept filed over a person is void without it, and the remedy exists
precisely because the Doctrine does.

**Essence stratum.** Active layers: for a Precept filed over a sphere, none of the
subject's — the working is in the substrate, which is what makes it Runecraft. For a
soul-bound Precept, the **Essence Core**, since a standing law held in the Core is
what the system means by a Trait, and this is why the Votia tag and the Rescind
counter both attach to that case and not to the other. Developmental Tier and
Crystal State: `null`, no Stage stated. **The invoice:** `null` in every currency
the system has, on a working the page says can found a dynasty's law.

**The lens: the *Grundnorm*.** Hans Kelsen, *Reine Rechtslehre* (1934). Kelsen's
Pure Theory removes morality, force and sociology from the account of law and asks
only what makes a norm **valid**, and his answer is that a norm is valid because it
was created in accordance with a higher norm — a hierarchy of authorisations
terminating in a *Grundnorm*, a basic norm that is not posited by anybody but
**presupposed**, the way a coordinate origin is presupposed. Validity is not
efficacy: for Kelsen, a norm nobody enforces is still valid, and a command everybody
obeys is not a norm unless it was correctly authorised. That is the Precept page's
own doctrine with a Viennese jurist's rigour behind it — *"The practitioner does
not carve an effect into the world so much as file an instruction with it, in the
Continuum's own legible grammar"* — and it is why the page can say, correctly, that
a Precept is neither persuasion nor threat. Kelsen would have recognised
**Glyphica's Authorization and Sealing roles** immediately; they are his hierarchy
with glyphs in it.

Where the lens misleads, and where it beggars the school that holds it, is in
**which direction the remedy lies.** Kelsen's hierarchy means every norm is
challengeable **upward**: show that a norm conflicts with the higher norm that
authorised it and it was never valid, and the whole edifice above the defect comes
down with it. A school reading Precepts through Kelsen therefore becomes a school of
**appeal**. It trains advocates. It files against the clause, cites the Accord
above the guild and the Archon above the Accord, and it is very good at it — and the
space still will not hold the shape, for the whole duration of the litigation,
because the page has already said the thing they are refusing to believe: *"the law
is not enforced from outside afterward."* A band gap has no superior to whom the
wave may appeal. There is no mode. **And the actual remedy is in the opposite
direction: downward and local** — one defect in the periodicity, at one site, which
opens exactly one narrow allowed state and leaves the rest of the law standing. The
school that spent three generations litigating upward was beaten by a mason with a
chisel and a very good idea about where to put the notch. Offered as a school's
error, not as canon.

## 3 · Mechanism (the effect)

**Glyph (proposed; the page names none).** `[RenB]` **Boundary** as the limit that
moves, laid under `[Ho]` **Order** for the register the clause is filed in. Both
from the thirty unclassified forms, and the Index's reason applies: *"Each names a
kind of limit, applicable to any law at all"* — which is what a working that files
one clause against workings, oaths and people at once requires.

**One note, and it is a real question rather than a flourish.** The nearest form in
the register to what a Precept actually *does* is `[Lk]` **Lock**, and the
Counterplay register defines it in the Precept page's own terms: *"Lock occupies a
limit so no other limit can be set there."* That is a standing prohibition
described exactly. But the Index files Lock as one of **two Counter forms** —
*"The only two Counter forms in the index, and they do not oppose a law, because
laws cannot be opposed. They condition the conditioning"* — and a Precept is not a
counter to anything; it is a first move. Whether Lock is available as a **standing**
form rather than only as a reply is not stated anywhere, and it decides which glyph
this working is built on. `SA-GAP-PRECEPT-LOCK-AS-STANDING-FORM`.
*Filing decision, not canon — Isaac's ruling (`SA-GAP-GLYPH-MIRROR`).*

**What boundary moves.** **The set of configurations reachable inside a named
sphere.** Not the strength of anything, not anyone's willingness, not what is
permitted — what is *available*. Fixatio performs the cure and the alternative
configuration stops being a reachable state; Judicium decides the clause's edges,
which is to say where the gap begins and ends. Everything after that is a structure
sitting in a substrate.

**What the law then does on its own.** Nothing, continuously, and *nothing* is the
correct description rather than a dismissive one. A constraint does no work. The
region is not watching, not testing, not deciding; it has fewer degrees of freedom
than it had, and every process running inside it runs correctly within what remains.
A working that would have taken the forbidden shape does not fail — it **completes,
in the nearest available configuration**, which is a different and stranger thing.
An oath that cannot be sworn in the forbidden terms gets sworn in terms that are
available. A rank that cannot be claimed gets claimed in the form the clause leaves
open. This is why the page says *"a guild seal outlives the clerk who struck it"*
and why Precepts are the instrument of institutions rather than of duellists:
**what a Precept produces is not obedience but a shape in everything downstream of
it**, visible for generations in what a place's workings all have in common.

**Law V check.** **Cannot be run, and the reason is the entry's largest filing
problem.** Law V is a Stage gate and the page states no Stage. Worse, the page
states a **Craft** — *"Craft · Runecraft. Per the current Craft/durability split,
Spellcraft holds only while attention holds and Runecraft is fixed and degrades
like any inscription; a Precept that is meant to found a guild code or a dynasty's
law cannot be an art that lapses when the caster looks away"* — and that reasoning
is exactly right and puts the page on the wrong shelf. It sits in `wiki/Spellcraft/`
and says in its own words that it is not Spellcraft.
`SA-CROSS-PRECEPT-RUNECRAFT-ON-SPELLCRAFT-SHELF`. The consequence is not cosmetic:
Runecraft is beaten by damaging a substrate and Spellcraft by interrupting a chain,
and those are different fights.

**Failure mode — Boundary fault, with the fault built into the drafting rather than
the casting.** *"The condition was established imprecisely and the law ran somewhere
unintended"* (`Two Sets of Books` §VII). A Precept's imprecision is a clause edge in
the wrong place, and its consequence is Judicium's **spectral overload** on an
institutional scale: a clause a little too wide catches cases nobody intended, costs
nothing to keep catching them, and cannot be undone by wanting it undone — Fixatio's
**irreversibility**, arriving as a legal fact rather than a magical one. **The
characteristic disaster of this working is not a backlash. It is a century of
correct rulings nobody wanted.**

A second fault: **Coherence fault, on the institution rather than on a person.**
*"Lived behaviour and stated law drifted apart."* A Precept holds its clause
whatever the guild beneath it has become, so a house running a two-hundred-year-old
Precept is running a stated law its lived behaviour may have left long ago — and
`Counterplay` IV's *"Coherence drift"* becomes something an opponent can read off the
archive rather than off a man.

**What the target sees and feels.** If the Precept is filed over a sphere: nothing,
ever, and that is the working. There is no pressure, no resistance, no moment of
being stopped. A practitioner reaches for the forbidden shape and their working
comes out in the nearest available one, and unless they had read the clause they
will experience this as **their own preference**. The page's *"do not defy a Precept
so much as discover that the space it named will not hold the shape they were trying
to give it"* is exact, and the word *discover* is doing the work: the discovery is
usually made by someone reading the archive afterwards and noticing that every
working of a certain kind, in a certain city, for a certain number of centuries, has
the same peculiar shape.

If the Precept is soul-bound, it is worse, and the page does not describe it. A
standing law in the Essence Core is what the system calls a Trait, and the Core
Vocabulary's **Rigidity Effect** applies — *"The more absolute a Trait's law, the
less the soul can safely contradict it."* A person carrying a Precept does not feel
constrained. They feel like themselves, and the shape of what they can want has an
edge in it that was put there by a document.

**What bleeds, at the stated efficiency.** `null`, and for once the answer is
interesting rather than merely missing. Efficiency is read from the Tier of
Standing, the Tier of Standing from the Stage, and the page states no Stage
(`SA-GAP-PRECEPT-NO-STAGE`). But the physical account says the **standing** bleed is
genuinely zero regardless: constraint forces do no work, so whatever η costs the
practitioner at the moment of sealing, it costs nothing thereafter. **This is the
only working in this section whose bleed is zero by theorem rather than unknown by
omission**, and the page has not noticed that it owns the section's one free lunch.

**The arithmetic, and what it turns up.** Three lines, and the first is a result
rather than a gap.

*First, the zero is real and it is the working's whole architecture.* Of the twenty

*Second, every other ledger row is null and they are all null for one reason.* Stage

*Third, the counter is countable and the page nearly says so.* A lawful correction

unmade by a correction to the law"* from a principle into a procedure with a number
in it: a party seeking an exception is not seeking to weaken the clause and cannot
weaken it; they are seeking **one notch, in one place**, and the negotiation is over
where. Everything interesting about Precepts politically is in that sentence and
none of it is on the page.

**Does the physics close against the stratal account?** **Yes — more completely than
any other entry in this section, and the two accounts were plainly written

**The Counterplay routes that work**
(`wiki/The Magic System/Counterplay What Beats a Practitioner.md`).

- **Break the boundary — the substrate, because this is Runecraft and the page says
  so.** *"Wards. Runecraft standing on a substrate, and therefore beaten the way
  Runecraft is beaten: damage the substrate. A ward is not argued with. It is
  chiselled off, or the wall it sits on is brought down, or the third layer is
  scraped through by somebody who knows which layer to scrape."* The whole of a
  Precept's cost is sitting in one physical object, paid once, with nothing being
  spent to defend it — which makes the object the fight. Note the register's aside,
  which is a scenario on its own: *"An inscription with a defective Sealing role has
  been failing continuously for two centuries and is a liability to whoever owns it,
  not a defence."*
- **Break the boundary — the defect, which is the lawful route and the one the page
  points at without describing.** The page: *"a Precept is unmade by a correction to
  the law, not by breaking the stone it is cut into."* The physics says what a
  correction *is*: **one defect in the periodicity opens exactly one localized state
  inside the gap, at one site, and leaves the rest of the law intact.** So the route
  is neither destruction nor appeal — it is obtaining a single, narrow, precisely
  placed exception, in the same register the clause was written in. Whoever controls
  where the notch goes controls the Precept, and the Precept's own regularity is what
  makes the notch work.
- **Break the boundary — Rescind, but only against a soul-bound Precept without
  consent.** The page's own: *"Rescind, the withdrawal-of-consent effect attested
  elsewhere in the corpus, is the clearest documented counter to a soul-bound
  Precept, and only when the Precept was never lawfully consented to in the first
  place."* The limit is doing real work: a Precept filed over a sphere is not
  touched by it, and a Precept lawfully consented to is not either. Whether Rescind
  reaches a standing Traitus mark generally is the open question logged at
  `SA-GAP-ANOINTING-RESCIND`, filed from the Anointing page in this same section.
- **Break the boundary — Countersigning, and the page's Sealing role invites it.**
  *"Some Seals end when countersigned in writing, and some end when the sum is
  spoken back correctly by another. A working that carries either of those closing
  conditions can be ended by an opponent who is literate enough, which is why Accord
  field practice avoids both and why they persist in the old inscriptions
  everywhere."* The page files this working under Glyphica's **Authorization and
  Sealing** roles and says a Precept founds dynastic and guild law — which is to say,
  the Precepts most worth beating are precisely the **old inscriptions** the register
  says still carry closing conditions.
- **Read the clause before it seals, which is the only pre-emptive route.** The
  page: *"a working that reads the Precept's shape before it seals can in principle
  decline to be caught by it."* This is the sealing-window counter, it requires
  Judicium or its equivalent, and it exists for exactly as long as the drafting does.

**The tell, stated plainly.** There is no cast, no chant, no light and no pressure,
and there is nothing to notice in the moment — a Precept's whole nature is that
nobody experiences being stopped. What there is, is an **inscription somewhere and a
pattern in the record**: a sphere where every working of one kind has come out the
same odd shape for a suspiciously long time, and, at the bottom of it, a substrate
with a clause on it and a Sealing role that may or may not have been drafted by
somebody competent. Of the twenty workings in this section, this is the one most
likely to be found by a reader rather than by a witness.

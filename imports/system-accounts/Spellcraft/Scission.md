# System account · Scission

## 1 · Physical account (physics)

**The phenomenon in plain words.** Two things that have been bound together are
put into a condition in which being apart is the cheaper arrangement, and then
held there, without force, until they come apart by themselves.

**The real phenomenon.** **Solvation**, and the two quantities that govern it:
**free energy**, which decides whether separation happens at all, and
**activation energy**, which decides whether it happens *today*.

*The free-energy criterion.* A process runs spontaneously when its Gibbs free
energy change is negative: **ΔG = ΔH − TΔS < 0.** Dissolution is the standard
illustration because it can run *uphill in heat* and still proceed — ammonium
nitrate dissolving in water absorbs heat and cools the beaker, and it dissolves
anyway, because the entropy of mixing is large enough that TΔS beats ΔH. The
solvent is not attacking the solid. The solid is going into solution because the
dissolved arrangement has the lower free energy, which is the register's own
sentence: *"A solid dissolves not because the solvent attacks it but because the
mixed state has higher entropy than the separated one, and the process therefore
runs on its own once initiated."*

*The activation barrier.* A spontaneous process is not a fast one. A
supersaturated solution can sit for hours holding more solute than equilibrium
permits, and will surrender it all in seconds the moment a nucleation site
appears. What the site supplies is not driving force — the driving force was
there the whole time — but a **route**: a place where the first few molecules can
reorganise without paying the full surface energy of a new interface. **The rite
supplies the nucleation site. It does not supply the separation.** This is what
the page means, correctly, by *"holds that boundary open long enough for the two
halves to separate on their own"* and *"does not need to be forced the whole way
through."*

*The numbers that make the page's own Weakness true.* Entropy of mixing for an
equimolar pair is **ΔS = R ln 2 = 5.76 J·K⁻¹·mol⁻¹**. At body temperature,
310 K, that is **TΔS ≈ 1.8 kJ·mol⁻¹**. A covalent bond runs **300–400
kJ·mol⁻¹**. So the entropic driving force available to a dissolution-type
separation is **two hundred times too small to break a covalent bond**, and the
only way such a separation runs is if the enthalpy term is doing the work —
which it does for ionic and van der Waals bonds and does not for cross-linked
ones. **Fixatio is covalent cross-linking**, *"a thermoset [that], once cured, has
formed covalent bridges across its whole network and will char before it flows"*,
and the page's Weakness says a Fixatio-stabilised bond *"resists the entropy
gradient the rite depends on."* **That is not flavour. It is a factor of two
hundred**, and it is checkable by anyone who reads the Materia register beside a
thermodynamics table.

**The governing law.** **The second law as a free-energy criterion**, ΔG < 0,
governing whether the separated state is preferred, with **nucleation theory**
governing the induction time. Both are needed and the page uses both.

**Measurable quantities, SI.** The page gives EU, a duration and a Stage. What
the law supplies as checkable consequence:

- **Induction time is exponential in the barrier and does not scale with the
  caster.** Nucleation rate goes as exp(−ΔG*/kT), so a bond with a slightly
  higher interfacial barrier takes disproportionately longer, and a bond with a
  much higher one does not separate in any available time at all. The page's
  *"roughly six to twelve minutes"* is an **induction time**, and its factor-of-two
  spread across *"how tightly the two components had fused"* is exactly the shape
  an exponential-in-barrier process shows when the barrier varies modestly.
- **Once past the barrier, completion is fast and unstoppable.** A supersaturated
  solution crashes out in seconds once seeded. This is the physical reason the
  page's Counterplay has the window it does: *"Read the bond's shape **before the
  Seal step completes** and refuse the boundary condition — this is the one window
  where the separation can be declined rather than merely survived."* After
  nucleation there is no declining anything.
- **Separation does not destroy.** Solvation produces a solute and a solvent,
  both intact, neither annihilated. *"One thing becomes two. Neither is
  destroyed."* The page is right and the physics is why.

**The energy budget, and where the joules were.** In the bound configuration,
mostly, and in the medium. The rite pays for the **barrier**, not for the
separation — the activation, the nucleation site, the seven steps of apparatus —
and the free energy released by the separation itself was already stored in the
arrangement. This is `Two Sets of Books` §III correctly instantiated: *"The
practitioner does not supply the joules. They supply the boundary condition."*
The caster supplies a site and a condition; thermodynamics supplies the rest and
supplies it for free, which is why a rite of this consequence can cost six
minutes and 40,000 EU rather than a war.

**Precedent we are not copying.** Curse-removal is universal and is almost
always a contest: Bleach's Kidō, exorcism as a battle of wills, the priest
shouting at the demon. This is **a chemistry bench with a seven-step protocol**,
performed on an unconscious patient by people who have read the casework, and it
is famous for its *outcomes* rather than its drama: *"What comes back is
fragmentary, amnesiac, or resonant-altered more often than not."* The divergence
is that Scission is **surgery with a known complication rate**, and the interesting
scene is not the rite but the consent conversation before it and the person
afterwards who is not quite the person.

## 2 · Stratal account (metaphysics)

**Aether stratum.** Draw is sustained across six to twelve minutes of ritual
time, which makes this the longest working in the batch by two orders of
magnitude and the only one with a genuine *procedure*. **Residue:** heavy and
located — a rite performed in a circle, with an offering and a catalyst, leaves a
worked site, which is why Concord casework on Scission exists at all and why
*"three centuries of Concord casework"* has a documentary trail. **Saturation:**
relevant. A rite that runs for ten minutes in a fixed circle is depositing into
one small volume continuously, and the Core Vocabulary's saturation state —
*"excessive Essence floods an area faster than it can disperse"* — is a live
concern for a rite that cannot be moved once begun. Neither page mentions it.

**Wellspring stratum.** **Dissolution · *The Unbinding Stream***, Family
**Limina**. Its law, verbatim:

> **Analogue** · *"Solvation. Dissolution driven by the entropy of mixing."*
> **Mechanism** · *"A solid dissolves not because the solvent attacks it but
> because the mixed state has higher entropy than the separated one, and the
> process therefore runs on its own once initiated. Dissolution gives the Crystal
> that thermodynamic preference toward the unbound state, which makes shedding
> what no longer serves nearly effortless and makes retaining anything a matter of
> active work."*
> **Stat Effect** · *"Strengthens Resilience Severance, Tempering Processing."*
> **Failure** · *"Indiscriminate solvency. Entropy does not consult the
> practitioner about which structures were load-bearing."*
> (`wiki/The Eight Families & the Sixty Wellsprings/Limina — Entropy, Void and Mind.md`)

**The Failure line is the page's own Consequence, arriving on schedule.** *"What
comes back is fragmentary, amnesiac, or resonant-altered more often than not"* is
*indiscriminate solvency* with a clinical vocabulary on it. Entropy does not know
which of the patient's memories were load-bearing, and the rite's whole method is
to make the unbound state preferred — which it does for everything at that
interface, not only for the thing being removed. The two books agree here without
either having been told.

The page names three further currents as *"the law/permanence/memory triad the
rite reads against"*, and each is doing identifiable work:

- **Judicium** *(Fulguria)* — *"the practitioner reads the characteristic lines
  of a Crystal, a technique, or a lie, and identifies composition from signature
  alone"* — decides **where the interface is**, which is the whole of the
  diagnostic half and the reason the page's secondary Primary is Gnosis Analysis.
- **Fixatio** *(Materia)* — *"What it holds is not held by force but by the fact
  that the alternative configuration no longer exists as a reachable state"* —
  sets **the barrier height**, and is therefore both the rite's reference for how
  fused a bond is and its stated Weakness.
- **Anamnesis** *(Limina)* — supplies **what the record says the bond used to
  be**, which is how a practitioner knows what a clean parting would have looked
  like.

*Four Theories.* **The Consent Doctrine**, enforced, and unusually literally: a
Ritus is *performed* with *"someone with standing to invoke the rite"*, and the
Wellspring is not asked for anything beyond recognising a lawful condition.
*"A process can be licensed and a relationship cannot"* (`Two Sets of Books`
§IV) — and Scission is licensed, with three centuries of casework, which is what
the Doctrine exists to make possible.

**Essence stratum.** Active layers: all three, which is rare. The **Essence
Core** is where a Trait or a memory sits and is therefore what is being cut from;
the **Attraction Layer** holds *"bonds, Domains, and Wellspring harmonizations"*
and is what a Wellspring-from-host cut operates on; the **Aether Shell** takes
the backlash — the page charges **Resilience Ward**, a Shell-side Sub-Stat, and
charges it *"proportional to how forced (versus found) the cut was."*
Developmental Tier at the Stage VI floor: **Harmonic** — *"the three layers have
found a recurring rhythm the Continuum can read consistently"* (Part
Twenty-One), which is the minimum a practitioner needs in order to operate on
another Crystal's layer structure without losing track of their own. Crystal
State: not stated. **The invoice:** well drawn. EU per the Numerical Effect line,
a **Resilience Ward** toll scaled to how much the cut had to be forced, and the
full ritual apparatus — circle, offering, catalyst — which is a material cost the
rest of this batch does not carry.

**The lens: Love and Strife.** Empedocles of Acragas, the fragments
(DK 31 B17, B35). Nothing in the cosmos comes to be or perishes; there are four
roots and two forces, **Philia** and **Neikos**, Love which mingles and Strife
which separates, and every change that appears to be a birth or a death is in
fact a mixing or an unmixing of what was already there. *"There is no birth of
any mortal thing, nor any end in accursed death, but only mingling and
interchange of what is mingled."* That is the Scission page's Consequence
restated by a Sicilian in the fifth century BC: **one thing becomes two, neither
is destroyed.** The lens fits the working so exactly that the working's own
Category — Ritus, a *performed* thing — reads as a liturgy of Strife. Where the
lens misleads, and where it kills people, is in what Strife is supposed to
produce. On Empedocles' cosmic cycle, Strife acting to completion drives the
roots apart into **four pure masses**: complete separation yields purity. A guild
school reading Scission through Empedocles therefore expects that a properly
completed cut yields two clean components, and that a fragmentary,
amnesiac outcome means **the cut did not go far enough** — so the remedy is to
cut again, and harder. The page's own record says the opposite: *"neither is
exactly what it was; the separated piece carries fragmentation, amnesia, or
altered resonance more often than a clean parting."* Cutting further does not
purify. It is Dissolution's **indiscriminate solvency** applied a second time to
a structure already short of load-bearing members, and it is the mechanism by
which the page's *"mass, forbidden severance at one extreme"* end of the
discipline gets there — not by cruelty, but by a school that believed the second
cut would fix the first. Offered as a school's error, not as canon.

## 3 · Mechanism (the effect)

**Glyph (proposed; the page names none).** `[Al]` **Edge** · Root · Thalen for
the interface, laid under `[Me]` **Measure** · Synonym · Urion for the
diagnostic that locates it — the rite's two operations, in that order, and the
page's own order: *"Locates the interface where the two components are joined and
imposes a boundary condition across it."* Both from the thirty unclassified
forms, and the Index's reason applies: *"Each names a kind of limit, applicable
to any law at all"*, which is what a rite that cuts Trait from soul, Essence from
body, memory from identity or Wellspring from host requires — **the same glyph
against four different laws.**
*Filing decision, not canon — Isaac's ruling (`SA-GAP-GLYPH-MIRROR`).*

**What boundary moves.** Not the bond. **The free energy of the bound
configuration relative to the separated one, at one interface.** The rite does
not pull the two halves apart; it changes which arrangement is cheaper, and then
supplies a nucleation site so the cheaper arrangement is reachable. Everything
after that is thermodynamics doing what it does.

**What the law then does on its own.** It separates, at its own pace, and the
pace is the induction time. The seven steps of the rite — *"Intention → Space →
Circle → Offering → Invocation → Catalyst → Seal"* — read as an apparatus
protocol and behave as one: the Space and the Circle are the vessel, the Offering
and the Catalyst are the reagents, the Invocation states the condition, and the
**Seal** closes the operation against the warrant that opened it. Somewhere
inside the six to twelve minutes the barrier is crossed, and at that point *"the
process completes on its own and does not need to be forced the whole way
through."*

Which is also why the window closes where it does. Before nucleation the
separated state is merely *preferred*; after it, the interface is gone. A working
that *"reads the target bond's shape before the cut completes"* can decline the
condition, because a condition not yet acted on is still a proposal — and one
second after nucleation there is nothing to decline.

**Law V check.** The floor is **Stage VI, Glory**, below Refraction, so an
external anchor is required (`The Four Crafts`, Law V), and the page supplies
more anchor than any other entry in the batch: a circle, an offering, a catalyst,
a spoken invocation, and a Seal. **The gate closes emphatically.** The page's own
Limitation states the consequence better than the Codex does: *"Ritus is
performed, not built — it cannot be improvised mid-combat, and the full apparatus
must be assembled before the cut is attempted or the rite fails outright rather
than partially."* An anchor that elaborate is a working that can be beaten by
breaking any one piece of furniture.

**Failure mode — Boundary fault, and it is the Wellspring's own.** *"The
condition was established imprecisely and the law ran somewhere unintended"*
(`Two Sets of Books` §VII). Here it is **indiscriminate solvency**: the condition
is *make the unbound state preferred at this interface*, and an interface in a
Soul Crystal is not a clean surface. Whatever else crosses it becomes cheaper
apart too. That is the whole account of *"fragmentation, amnesia, or altered
resonance"*, and it is why the page's open question — *"Why some cuts leave a
Trait Echo behind and others leave nothing at all"* — has an instrument-level
candidate worth putting rather than asserting: **residual nucleation.** Solvation
rarely goes to completion; what remains is whatever sat at a site where the
barrier happened to be higher. A Trait Echo would then be **the fraction of the
cut structure whose local barrier the rite never reached**, which would make the
variable neither caster's Stage nor patient's — the two the guild registers have
already eliminated — but the *geometry of the specific bond*, which nobody is
measuring. Offered as a candidate, not as canon.

A second fault: **Structural fault on the caster**, which the page bills
honestly. *"The caster's own Resilience Ward takes the first backlash if the cut
is forced rather than found"*, and against a Fixatio-anchored bond, *"forcing it
anyway multiplies the backlash the caster eats."* Forcing a barrier the
thermodynamics does not support means supplying the difference yourself, and the
difference against a covalent bond is the factor of two hundred computed in §1.

**What the target sees and feels.** Not pain, and the casework is specific about
it: *"The host feels the cut as a structural absence rather than pain — Concord
casework logs it as 'the wrong kind of quiet.'"* For six to twelve minutes they
lie inside a circle while five people work, and the thing being removed does not
struggle, because nothing is pulling on it. What they notice is that something
that had been making a sound has stopped. **Whichever half is more load-bearing
to the host's identity destabilises first**, which means the patient usually
discovers, during the rite, which of the two things they actually were. Then it
is over, and one thing has become two, and neither is destroyed — and neither is
quite what it was. The piece that comes back comes back *"fragmentary, amnesiac,
or resonant-altered more often than not"*, and the host is left with a Trait
Echo, or with nothing, and nobody in three centuries has been able to say which
they will get.

**What bleeds, at the stated efficiency.** No practitioner has a card
(`SA-GAP-SCISSION-NO-CARD`). At the Stage VI floor the Tier of Standing is
**5 · Expert**, η **0.60–0.70** (Part Nineteen as corrected by **R44-4**):
**30 to 40 percent of the
expenditure leaves as heat, sound and structural bleed.** At the 60,000 EU
ceiling that is **18,000 to 24,000 EU of waste**, spread across six to twelve
minutes rather than dumped in a second — so unlike Edictum's instantaneous
discharge this is **25 to 67 EU of bleed per second, sustained, into a fixed
circle**, which is the profile that makes a Scission chamber a warm and noisy
place and is the reason the rite has a Space step at all.

**The arithmetic, and what it turns up.** Three lines.

*First, the Fixatio Weakness is quantitatively true.* The entropy of mixing
supplies about **1.8 kJ·mol⁻¹** of driving force at body temperature; a covalent
cross-link costs **300–400 kJ·mol⁻¹** to break. The available driving force is
short by a factor of roughly **two hundred**, and a practitioner who *"forces it
anyway"* is supplying that difference personally. **This is the rare case in this
batch where the real physics does not embarrass the canon but confirms it**, and
confirms it with a number the page never claimed.

*Second, the reserve floor against the corpus.* A ceiling-cost Scission needs
**66,667 EU** in reserve to be performed once from full. Of the five attested
reserves: Karo Venrik (Stage V, 1,600 EU) — **no**, by a factor of forty; Dougou
(Stage XIII, 92,000 EU) — **one rite**, with 25,000 EU left standing; Niran
(Stage VII, 485,000 EU) — seven; Muken (Stage X, 1,850,000 EU) — twenty-seven;
Gimbzo — unbounded. A Stage XIII practitioner managing a single Stage VI rite is
the same `SA-CROSS-EU-STAGE-LADDER` finding logged on the Edictum account,
arriving for the third time.

*Third, the bleed rate is the reason for the circle.* Unlike every instantaneous
working in this batch, Scission's waste is delivered at **33 to 83 EU per second
for up to twelve minutes into one small fixed volume**. The Core Vocabulary's
**Aetheric Saturation** — *"excessive Essence floods an area faster than it can
disperse … produces Crystal Fracture Events in everyone exposed, including the
person who caused it"* — is the obvious hazard, and neither page names it as one.
`SA-GAP-SCISSION-SATURATION`.

**Does the physics close against the stratal account?** **Almost, and the one
failure is a sentence that runs backwards.**

The mechanism, the cost, the duration, the window and the Weakness all close —
this is one of the best-built pages in the batch, and the Wellspring's Failure
and the page's Consequence are the same statement. But the page's own
thermodynamics is inverted. It says the rite *"raises the entropy of the **mixed,
bound** configuration above the entropy of the two components held apart"*, and
then concludes *"once the **separated** state is thermodynamically preferred."*
Those do not follow: if the bound configuration has the higher entropy then the
**bound** state is preferred and nothing separates. The sentence the mechanism
needs is the opposite one — the rite must raise the free energy of the bound
configuration relative to the separated pair, or equivalently lower the
separated pair's, which is what supplying a solvent does. Dissolution's own
register is consistent (*"the mixed state has higher entropy than the separated
one"* — which is why a **solid dissolves into a solvent**), and the page has
carried the register's sentence across while reversing what counts as "mixed."
Logged `SA-PHYS-SCISSION-ENTROPY-INVERTED`, with options, and not resolved.

**The Counterplay routes that work**
(`wiki/The Magic System/Counterplay What Beats a Practitioner.md`).

- **Break the boundary — anchor the bond with Fixatio, and the thermodynamics
  refuses.** The page's own Weakness: *"A bond stabilized by Fixatio resists the
  entropy gradient the rite depends on."* The Materia register says why, and says
  it in a sentence that is a counter all by itself: *"What it holds is not held by
  force but by the fact that **the alternative configuration no longer exists as a
  reachable state**."* A separation that is not a reachable state does not have an
  induction time; it does not happen. This is the strongest counter in the batch,
  it is bought in advance, it requires no Stage advantage, and the physics puts a
  number on it (§4: a factor of two hundred). **Fixatio's own Failure is the
  price:** *"Irreversibility. The cure is not undone by wanting it undone, and a
  Fixatio binding placed in error is a permanent feature of whatever it was placed
  on."* You can make yourself uncuttable. You cannot change your mind.
- **Break the boundary — interrupt the chain, which here means the furniture.**
  *"Below Refraction a working requires an external anchor: voice, hand, ink or
  blood. Take the anchor and the sentence does not complete."* Stage VI is below
  Refraction and the anchor is a **seven-step apparatus in a fixed location over
  twelve minutes.** Scuff the circle. Take the catalyst. Interrupt the invocation.
  The page is explicit that a partial rite *"fails outright rather than
  partially"*, so there is no half-cut patient to worry about.
- **Break the boundary — refuse the condition before the Seal.** The page's own
  Counterplay: *"Read the bond's shape before the Seal step completes and refuse
  the boundary condition — this is the one window where the separation can be
  declined rather than merely survived."* And note what the register says about
  Seals generally, which cuts the other way and is worth knowing:
  *"**Countersigning.** Some Seals end when countersigned in writing, and some end
  when the sum is spoken back correctly by another. A working that carries either
  of those closing conditions **can be ended by an opponent who is literate
  enough**."* A rite with a named Seal step is a rite with a closing condition,
  and three centuries of Concord casework means the protocol is **documented**.
- **Break the man — the casework is public.** *"Taught techniques are documented
  and counterable by anyone who studied."* The page's Origin says this outright:
  *"As a documented rite rather than a self-derived technique, it is counterable by
  anyone who has studied the Concord's own casework on it."* Scission is the most
  thoroughly published working in this batch and is correspondingly the least
  surprising.
- **Deny the field — Silence, and it is a mercy here.** A Silence Realm forbids
  lawful transformation outright, so a rite cannot be authorised inside one. Note
  that this cuts both ways in a way that matters for scenes: a patient who **wants**
  a corrupted Wellspring cut out cannot have it cut out in a Silence, which makes
  the location of a Scission chamber a logistical fact worth fighting over.

**The tell, stated plainly.** Five people, a circle, an offering, a catalyst, a
spoken invocation, and somewhere between six and twelve minutes. This working
cannot be performed on someone who does not know it is being performed, and it
cannot be performed at all by anyone who has not spent the afternoon setting up.
Of the twenty workings in this section, it is the one an opponent is most likely
to walk in on.

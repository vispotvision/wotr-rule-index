## SABA NO ROSA — THE SEA OF ROSES AND THORNS

_Bara Moto's bequest, taught once and only once, to Muken on the seventy-third day of instruction beneath the lower lattice of the Plane of Fate._
_Of all the Works Bara has carried, this is the one he warns against teaching to anyone who has not first learned to grieve correctly._

**Type:**
Apex Fate Discipline – A drowning garden of lawful unmaking; the practitioner raises a sea of roses whose thorns dilute the target's past until the present can no longer be sustained by it.

**Primary Wellsprings:**

- **Anamnesis** – Reads the target's causal ledger backward and surfaces the deposits upon which their present coordinate is currently drawing.
- **Mortalis** – Governs the threshold between what is still lawfully present and what has been released; the thorns work along this threshold rather than against the body.
- **Fractura** – Identifies the seams in the target's history that have been bearing load and widens those seams without snapping them, the way a slow tide widens a hairline crack in a stone pier.
- **Contrition** – Surfaces the unpaid weight the target has carried into the present, drawing it up to the rose-line so that the dilution can occur lawfully rather than as theft.
- **Aether Sangui** – Provides the medium of the sea itself; the roses are not flowers but lawful blooms of dilute Aether-blood, carrying the cumulative load-bearing of every prior holding the target has drawn upon.
- **Monlithion** – Ratifies the dilution as causal sentence so that the target's timeline cannot simply refile the lost deposits as still present.

You can think of Saba no Rosa as a composite discipline that leans hardest on **Anamnesis + Mortalis + Aether Sangui**, then layers Fractura, Contrition, and Monlithion on top.

---

## Summary

Saba no Rosa is the lawful raising of a sea of roses and thorns around a target, in which the roses are not flowers and the sea is not water; each bloom is a lawful instance of the target's own past brought to the surface, and each thorn is the unpaid debt of that instance made present at the threshold. The sea does not drown the body. It drowns the inheritance the body has been standing on. At its core, Saba no Rosa is the art of taking from a man what he has already spent, until the present coordinate he occupies can no longer be paid for.

---

## Core Principle

Saba no Rosa is built on three linked ideas:

- **A present coordinate is paid for by past deposits.** Every living being stands on a stack of lawful holdings — relationships honored, debts paid, inheritances carried correctly, oaths kept. Anamnesis reads the stack as a ledger, not as memory. The Work targets the ledger, not the body.
- **The thorns dilute, they do not destroy.** Saba no Rosa does not erase the past. It dilutes the present's claim upon it. The deposit remains in the cosmos; it simply no longer answers to the target's coordinate. Mortalis governs the threshold across which the answering is lawfully withdrawn.
- **The sea must be raised lawfully.** Aether Sangui provides the medium, drawn from the practitioner's own load-bearing reserves, so that the dilution is paid for at the source rather than stolen from the target without ratification. The roses bloom because Bara's discipline has chosen to spend itself, not because the target has been violated.

The Wellspring backbone for this is:

- **Anamnesis** for surfacing the target's load-bearing past as accessible ledger.
- **Mortalis** for working along the threshold between what is still answering and what has been released.
- **Fractura** for widening the seams of the ledger so that the dilution can enter without snapping the timeline.
- **Contrition** for raising the unpaid weight to the rose-line so that the work is lawful rather than predatory.
- **Aether Sangui** for providing the medium of the sea itself, paid for from the practitioner's own reserves.
- **Monlithion** for ratifying the dilution as causal sentence the target's timeline cannot quietly refile.

Taken together, the six Wellsprings behave as a single drowning garden: a lawful sea in which every bloom is a holding being withdrawn, every thorn is a debt being surfaced, and every breath the target takes is an attempt to stand on a ledger that is no longer his.

## Saba no Rosa in Practice

A practitioner of Saba no Rosa does not ask, _"How shall I defeat this man?"_
They ask, _"Which of his past deposits is paying for the coordinate he is currently standing on, and is that deposit one I am lawfully entitled to dilute?"_

Examples:

- Against a single duelist whose stance is being held together by a particular early-life teacher's discipline, the practitioner raises a low sea of three or four roses at the duelist's feet, each rose blooming as a lawful instance of that teacher's earlier corrections. Contrition surfaces the unpaid weight of those lessons. The duelist's stance, no longer lawfully drawing on the teacher's earlier deposit, slowly degrades over the next exchanges into the stance he held before the teacher ever corrected him — usually a far poorer thing.
- Against a king whose throne is being sustained by an old oath sworn to a now-dead predecessor, the practitioner raises the sea around the throne-room itself. The thorns surface the unpaid portion of the oath, and Mortalis withdraws the lawful answering of the oath from the present king's coordinate. The king does not fall from the throne. The throne simply stops recognizing him as its lawful occupant, and the room's substrate, having read the change, begins to host the king as an ordinary man rather than as a sovereign.
- Against a battlefield army drawing morale from a single charismatic commander's prior victories, the practitioner raises a wide low sea across the field. The roses bloom along the lines where each soldier's individual deposit in the commander's prior victories has been paying for their current willingness to die. The army does not break. The army simply ceases to be able to remember why it was willing to charge.
- Against a god-aspected mortal whose divine corridor is being paid for by an ancestral covenant, the practitioner raises a tight, dense sea at the corridor-seat. Aether Sangui carries the medium up the corridor itself. The covenant remains intact in the divine record; it simply stops answering on behalf of the present practitioner. The divine corridor closes, lawfully, without offending the god at the other end of it.

Because each rose is a single lawfully surfaced deposit and each thorn is the unpaid debt against it, Saba no Rosa excels at **drowning by inheritance withdrawal**: the target is not struck, not cut, not unmade — he is simply, lawfully, separated from the past he had been standing on.

## Synergies

Saba no Rosa reaches its most dangerous expression when combined with the **Shingan inheritance** of the Moto bloodline.

The Shingan provides:

- Direct lawful comprehension of which past deposits are currently load-bearing for the target's present coordinate, so that the practitioner does not have to raise a wasteful sea across the target's entire ledger; the eye sees the exact deposit to dilute.
- A revised decision-loop in which the user does not ask _"how much of this man must I drown"_ but _"which single rose, if surfaced, will leave him standing on nothing"_ — converting an apocalyptic Work into a surgical one.

Saba no Rosa then:

- Uses **Anamnesis** to confirm the load-bearing deposit named by the Shingan.
- Uses **Contrition** and **Fractura** to surface the deposit and widen its seam without snapping the timeline.
- Uses **Mortalis** and **Aether Sangui** to lawfully withdraw the deposit's answering across the threshold.
- Uses **Monlithion** to ratify the withdrawal so the target's timeline cannot quietly refile.

A practitioner with both Saba no Rosa and the Shingan does not simply attack; they **withdraw** the target from his own past, leaving the body intact and the coordinate unsupported.

## Stacking and Composition

Saba no Rosa treats a "total drowning" as the end product, not the starting point. The sea begins small. The sea expands only as the practitioner's own load-bearing reserves permit. The discipline is composable rose by rose, thorn by thorn, deposit by deposit.

Process, in Wellspring terms:

1. Use **Anamnesis** to sketch the target's ledger and identify the load-bearing deposits sustaining the present coordinate.
2. Use **Fractura** to anchor the work along the existing seams in the ledger, widening them only as much as the timeline can absorb without rupture.
3. Use **Contrition** to define the local change, surfacing the unpaid weight of each deposit so that the dilution occurs lawfully.
4. Use **Mortalis** to declare the law of the withdrawal, ratifying the threshold across which the deposit's answering is lawfully removed.
5. Use **Aether Sangui** and **Monlithion** to externalize and keep it running — the sea as visible bloom, the dilution as sustained sentence, both paid for from the practitioner's own reserves rather than stolen from the target without ratification.

Each rose does its job: **surfaces, widens, withdraws, ratifies, drowns.**
Together they behave as a custom, adaptive **drowning garden** that can be reconfigured by adding new roses, retiring spent ones, and toggling individual deposits as the exchange demands.

## Why Saba no Rosa Is Dangerous

Saba no Rosa is dangerous because:

- **It scales by the target's history, not the practitioner's strength.** The richer the target's past, the more roses there are to raise. Kings, gods, and ancient practitioners are the easiest targets, not the hardest, because their present coordinates are paid for by enormous stacks of past deposits each of which can be diluted.
- **The Work is invisible as violence.** No blade is drawn. No body is struck. The target does not see the attack arrive. He simply, over the course of the engagement, loses the ability to stand on the ground he has been standing on. By the time he understands what is happening, the roses are already at his waist.
- **The Shingan synergy converts the apocalyptic into the surgical.** The discipline does not need to drown a man entirely. It needs only the one correct rose. A single lawfully surfaced deposit, correctly identified, can withdraw a tyrant from his tyranny without ever touching his body.
- **It is lawful.** The Work cannot be answered by appeal to the cosmos. Every withdrawal is paid for. Every dilution is ratified. The target cannot accuse the practitioner of theft, because Aether Sangui has paid the medium-cost from the practitioner's own reserves, and Contrition has surfaced only what was already unpaid.

Where conventional unmaking-arts wrestle with the moral weight of erasure, a true Saba no Rosa user appears **terribly, patiently merciful**: he does not erase. He merely withdraws. From the outside, fights with him look like a man gently lowering an opponent into still water, who, by the time the water closes over the opponent's head, has been drowned not by the practitioner but by every promise the opponent had been keeping for himself.

## Philosophical Tension

By leaning on Wellsprings like **Anamnesis, Contrition, and Mortalis**, Saba no Rosa carries a worldview:

- It sees the past not as memory but as load-bearing structure. Every present coordinate is paid for. The Work treats every standing thing as standing because the past has agreed to hold it up.
- It interprets failure as the practitioner spending what is not his to spend. The roses must bloom from the practitioner's own Aether Sangui reserves; a Saba no Rosa user who tries to draw the medium from the target himself, or from the surrounding field, finds the sea turning brackish in the next exchange and the thorns turning inward.
- It tempts the user toward applying the Work where it should not be applied. The discipline can dilute a tyrant. The discipline can also dilute a husband, a teacher, a friend whose only fault was being remembered. Bara has carried the Work for a long time, and the warning he gave Muken on the seventy-third day was not about strength. It was about discernment.

Used with discipline, Saba no Rosa is **the lawful drowning of every tyranny that has been paying for itself with unpaid debts** — a sea that rises only against those whose presents are sustained by ledgers they have refused to balance.

Used without restraint, it becomes **a garden of quiet murders**: a slow-blooming sea raised against men and women whose only crime was a past the practitioner could not forgive, every rose a deposit unjustly withdrawn, every thorn a debt that was never owed to the practitioner in the first place, until the day the practitioner's own Aether Sangui reserves, having paid for too many wrongful seas, fail him at the threshold — and the ledger he stands on dilutes beneath his own feet, the garden finally turning to face the gardener.

# Bulwark

*The Ashgate road, within sight of the Kharven gates.*

---

The sky over the north went to work at the ninth hour and did not consult anybody.

It began as a thickening. The cloud base did not move and did not break, and through it, from horizon to horizon, purple came up in strings. They were not lights in the sky. They hung at a height the eye could not agree on twice, and they thrummed, and each thrum arrived in the chest a full second before it arrived in the ear, and the strings drew together across the whole vault of the north into folds and the folds moved, and it was an aurora, and there is no season in Kharven when that colour belongs to that sky.

For the length of one breath, everything inside a thousand miles weighed less.

Frost lifted off the road in a sheet and hung. Blood standing in the ruts came up out of them in beads and went sideways. Every loose thing in the country, ash and grit and chaff and the teeth of the dead, rose an inch and travelled, all of it in one direction, and every horse in six hundred screamed at once because a horse knows what down is and had been told otherwise.

Then it came back.

Everything that had risen went to a single point on the Ashgate road, and reached it, and stopped, and the sky's purple came down out of the folds and went to the same place, and the aurora emptied itself into one man over the course of four seconds and did not leave a residue anywhere.

---

Wren Greymane had his hand up in front of his face and did not remember putting it there.

Kaalabad's eyes had gone the same colour.

He brought the draconic horse to a halt in the middle of the road and swung down off it, which he had not done once in three days of riding, and he put his boots in the frozen mud, and the mud under both of them went down and cracked outward in a ring eleven feet across.

Then he put his right hand out into the air beside him and pushed it through.

Space parted for the arm and did not close around it. There was a seam, a hand's width and about four feet long, hanging at chest height with nothing on the other side of it, and Kaalabad's arm was in it to the shoulder, and Wren watched the wrongness gather in a ring at the join where the man stopped and the seam began, and the ring turned slowly, and the light in it came from nowhere.

He drew it out.

The old halberd, the dirty one with the marks, had never been the thing. Wren understood that the moment the new one cleared the seam. It came out head first, eleven feet of it, and the head was a spike and a fluke and a blade the length of a forearm, and it was black through, and along the shaft ran a hairline of the same purple the sky had just given up, and the road under the head began to sag toward it as it came.

Zarakal.

Wren had never heard the name and had never needed to. Some objects introduce themselves.

*Now.*

He got his hand up and made the sign and the sign went down the line.

Six hundred men turned around.

It happened along the whole length of the column at once and it took four seconds. Cass Holloway put his spear through the back of the Tenrai rider he had shared bread with for eleven weeks. The Amagiri light horse on the left flank, who had been told nothing and had suspected everything and had done nothing about it, came apart before they got their heads around. Two of Wren's men in the fourth rank went for the same officer and got in each other's way and one of them died for it.

It was ugly. Sixteen months of work and it came out ugly, and it did not need to come out any other way, because the whole of it had been designed to happen in a place with a gate behind it and no room to reform.

Wren had walked the ground in the summer under a false name with a hired cart and a load of salt fish, and had counted the road's width at the narrows, and had gone home and drawn it on the back of a psalter. Two hundred and ten feet from the ditch on the left to the drop on the right, and a column of six hundred strung out along it three abreast, and the officers riding in the middle third because officers always ride in the middle third.

Kill the middle third and the head cannot turn round and the tail cannot get past.

The noise of the column changed key. That was the sound Wren would keep.

He came down off the horse.

He hit the road and slapped the animal away and it went, and he reached back over his right shoulder and brought the shield off his back and onto his arm and got the enarmes seated with two shakes.

It was a heater, four feet, oak core and hide and a rim of iron gone silver with wear, and the boss had a crack in it that had been rebound twice, and it was the same shield, and there was no version of this morning in which he carried a different one.

Behind him the column was already doing what a column does when it has no officers left. The front third had begun to run for the gates, which were shut, and the rear third had begun to run for the ditch, which was full of men who had gone into it first.

Kaalabad looked at him for the first time in sixteen months.

"You are the traitor's traitor," he said.

His voice was flat and did not travel. He had said perhaps forty words since the muster and he was spending nine of them here.

"Aye," said Wren.

Kaalabad closed the space.

He did not throw anything and he did not swing. He simply brought the centre in, and Wren, forty feet away, went to one knee.

*It has no shape.*

The weight came from above and from every other direction at the same instant, and it was not wind and it was not force in any arrangement his body had ever been asked to receive. His shoulders went down. His neck could not hold his head at the angle he wanted it. The mail on his back gained about four times its own weight and the padding under it compressed and his ribs found the limit of where they could go, and the road under both knees cracked in a circle, and forty feet away the draconic horse folded its legs and lay down.

Every loose thing between the two of them slid inward across the mud.

Wren got his left foot flat.

That was all he could do and it took him two full seconds and both of them were free.

The horizontal came in at the height of his throat.

It came without any wind-up and that was the part his eye refused: eleven feet of Zarakal travelling flat across the road at a speed the shaft should not have permitted, with a man's whole standing effort behind it and the pull of that centre added on top of the standing effort, and it was going to take his head and the shield and the arm and about three feet of the road behind him.

"Bulwark."

The shield went blue.

It came up through the hide from the boss out, a cold clean blue the colour of ice with light under it, and it ran to the rim and reached the iron and stopped there.

Zarakal hit it.

There was no crack. That was the thing that would frighten anyone who watched it and understood shields. A shield struck like that gives one sharp report and a splitting sound and then it is firewood. This one made a low sustained note, a foot deep, and held it, and the blue went out of it from the point of contact outward in a spreading front and left grey behind.

The force went across the face of the shield instead of into the boss, every square inch taking an identical share of it, and Wren felt the whole four feet of it push against his forearm with an even pressure like a hand laid flat instead of a hammer laid on a nail. His arm held. His shoulder held. His feet did not.

He went back eleven feet with both heels ploughing two furrows through frozen mud, and the furrows stayed.

He came to a stop against something soft that had been a man an hour ago.

Grey to the rim.

Something under the hide had gone. He could feel it through the enarmes as a slackness that had not been there, and the boss's old crack had opened past its second binding, and there was a long split running up from it toward the top edge that he could not see and did not need to see.

*That is one.*

*It takes three. It has always taken three and it took three at the ridge and it took three in the Stormfold and there is no reason on this earth it should take three from that.*

He got his shoulder under the shield's top edge and drove it up.

There was no technique in it. A big man and an angle, and that was all of it. The head of Zarakal was still in contact and still pushing and Wren put the rim under the fluke and stood up out of his knees, and the shaft went up and over his right shoulder and past his ear, and Kaalabad's hands went with it, and for about a third of a second the whole of the man was open from the hip to the jaw.

Wren did not go for the opening. He knew what was in there and he had nothing that would reach it.

He went to his belt.

The charge was iron, the size of a duck's egg, with a wax seal and a brass ring, and there were four of them on the belt and the smith who made them had died in the Stormfold and had never told anyone how.

There were four on the belt and Wren had carried them for eleven months and had used two in that time, one of which had done nothing at all, and the smith had been unable to tell him why beforehand and was in no position to tell him afterward.

He pulled the ring and threw it underhand and flat.

"Compact."

It went out about nine feet and opened.

Aetheric material came out of a duck's egg in a hard sphere twenty feet across, white, dense, arriving all at once with a face on it like a wall coming forward, and it took Kaalabad across the chest and the raised arms and put him off the ground.

He went up. Sixteen feet, and back, with the shaft still in both hands and his heels above his head for a moment, and the sphere continued to expand behind him with the sound of a mountain clearing its throat.

Then the sphere bent.

Wren was watching for the landing and saw the whole thing instead. The white material reached the edge of what surrounded that man and stopped going straight. It curved. It came round him in a shell and went outward past him on both sides and closed again behind, and none of it touched him after the first instant, and the expansion that should have crushed a house went round a man and out into the field on the far side and knocked forty Tenrai foot off their feet at ninety yards.

He landed on his boots. The road took him and cracked, and the crack ran out from under him toward the ditch and split a cart in half on the way past.

*There.*

*There it is, and I have paid two exchanges for it, and I could have paid more.*

*It is not the weapon. The weapon is a stick. He has a middle, and everything that comes near it stops choosing its own direction, and the shaft is only there to tell the middle where to be. That is why the sky came down into him and that is why the horse lay down and that is why my own blood went up my face instead of off my chin.*

*And it works on the ground under him as much as on me. He cracks the road every time he lands. He is doing that to himself.*

He had bought that read with two exchanges and both of them were gone now and the arm he had held the shield with had begun to shake below the elbow, and fear was no part of it.

The shield was grey to the rim and the grey had begun to dull.

Around him, the whole road was coming apart. Six hundred against six hundred at the gates of Kharven, with the Tenrai officers dead in the first four seconds and nobody left in that column senior enough to reform it, and Wren could hear Cass Holloway's voice somewhere behind him going hoarse, and the Kharven gates were opening, and a white line was coming down the middle of the road out of them at a speed nothing on legs makes.

Kaalabad rolled his shoulders once and settled the shaft.

The purple came up around him.

It came out of the skin and out of the plate and out of the ground he was standing on, all at the same moment, a fever of it, a dense violet corona that did not illuminate anything and moved on its own account, and the air inside it went to a shimmer, and the frost on the road for thirty feet in every direction went to water and then to nothing without passing through steam.

Every man on the road within sixty feet who was still standing stopped being able to.

They went down where they were, on both sides, Tenrai and Greymane together, hands on the mud, and one of them was sick into the frozen ruts and could not lift his head out of it. The draconic horse got up and moved away.

Wren Greymane stayed on his feet.

He did it by putting the rim of a grey shield into the road and leaning his whole weight onto the boss and locking his knee, and his nose had begun to run blood down over his mouth and chin and it was going upward across his cheek instead, in a thin line, toward the man in front of him.

He got his teeth together, and set his left foot, and brought the shield up off the ground and onto his arm again.

## Notes (author-facing, not canon)

**One fix.** Your paste has the space closing around *Bram*. Bram is at the southern reach with three hundred men. I read it as Wren and wrote Wren.

**Register split, per Pack Twelve.** The aurora is Mystic: the sky does a thing, nobody explains it, no causal account anywhere. The duel is Technical: exhaustive, tactical, the read on the page. The two never sit in the same passage.

**Wren's read is the spine of the fight, and it costs him two exchanges to buy.** *It is not the weapon. The weapon is a stick. He has a middle, and everything that comes near it stops choosing its own direction, and the shaft is only there to tell the middle where to be.* The evidence is planted three times before he says it: the sky emptying into one man, the draconic horse lying down, his own blood running up his cheek instead of off his chin. He also gets the corollary that will matter later, which is that the pull works on the ground under Kaalabad as much as on anyone else, and that the man cracks every surface he lands on without meaning to.

**Wren's kit, originated, pending ruling.**

*Bulwark.* **Effect** — the shield's whole face takes an identical share of an impact instead of the boss taking it. Blue at the boss, spreading to the rim, leaving grey behind. **Cost** — the grey is spent capacity and does not come back inside a fight. Three deliveries is the historical number and this one took a third of the shield in a single exchange. **Limit** — it moves force across the shield, and the shield still transmits into an arm, and the arm still goes back eleven feet. **Counter** — go round it, or hit it four times. **What nobody knows** — why the grey never returns to blue on this particular shield and does on others.

*Compact.* **Effect** — an iron charge the size of a duck's egg opens into a hard sphere of aetheric material twenty feet across, arriving all at once. **Cost** — four on the belt, no more coming, and the smith who made them died in the Stormfold without telling anyone how. **Limit** — it expands, and an expansion can be bent by anything that governs direction. **Counter** — demonstrated on the page. **What nobody knows** — one of the four did nothing at all when he used it and neither he nor the smith could say why.

**Zarakal.** Drawn out of a seam in space, eleven feet, black through, with a hairline of the sky's purple down the shaft, and the road sagging toward the head as it comes. The dirty old halberd was never the weapon. Nine words spoken and one exchange later the reader understands the previous three days differently.

**The betrayal is ugly and I kept it ugly.** Cass Holloway puts his spear through a man he shared bread with for eleven weeks. Two of Wren's own go for the same officer and one dies for it. And the terrain is the plan: two hundred and ten feet at the narrows, counted in summer under a false name with a cart of salt fish, drawn on the back of a psalter, officers riding the middle third because officers always do. Kill the middle third and the head cannot turn and the tail cannot get past.

**Wren does not win and I have not left him a route to.** He is on his feet at the close because he put the rim in the road and locked his knee, with a split shield, an arm that will not stop shaking, and his own blood running upward across his face. Everyone else inside sixty feet is on the ground. The white line is coming down the road out of the Kharven gates and will arrive after this.

**Open.** Whether the shield survives a third. What the purple fever costs Kaalabad, because nothing on this board has been free yet and he has now spent an aurora. Whether Seiji was on the road at all when the column turned.

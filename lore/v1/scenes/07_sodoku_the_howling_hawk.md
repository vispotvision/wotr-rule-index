# The Howling Hawk

*The narrows. The hand on his shoulder.*

---

He had been here before.

That was the first thing Sodoku Moto understood and he understood it with a flatness that surprised even him, because the emptiness arriving behind him was being offered as a revelation and he had already had it. Twice. Once at thirty-two in the four months the record has him missing, and once in the water, and neither of those had been administered by anybody. They had simply been where he was for a while.

*He thinks this is new.*

*He has done this to a great many people and every one of them has met it for the first time, and he has built his entire method on the shape of a man's face the first time, and I am not going to be able to give him that.*

*He is going to find out. And when he finds out he will change what he is doing, and what he does second will be worse, and I would rather have the second thing arrive while I am still standing than while I am on my knees pretending.*

There were four hundred paces of road behind him with eleven hundred men on it who had not finished moving south, and a girl under a cistern, and a schoolmaster in the ice at the far end doing work that was not his, and every one of those was a reason to keep a mouth shut.

He said nothing.

He wanted to. There was a sentence in his chest with weight in it and he could feel the shape of the whole thing, and he let it stay there and did not spend it, because that had been the arrangement he made with himself at nineteen and had kept in every room since. The Ice had called it coldness. Yoko had once told him, accurately and without malice, that a man who never shows the tide is a man who has decided the tide is a weapon. Freya had known the whole of it in about a week and had never once mentioned it.

The feelings moved anyway. They always did. In and out, twice an hour, the whole eleven years, a river running under a floor.

His grandmother had had a word for it and had used it about him when he was nine, in front of his mother, and had refused to take it back. A boy who holds the tide, she said, grows into a man the tide holds. He had thought at the time that it was a compliment and had been pleased with himself for a week.

The hand on his shoulder had no weight in it.

That was the wrongness he could not stop reading. It rested there with the ordinary friction of a hand and the ordinary warmth of a hand and it displaced nothing whatsoever, and where the fingers lay across the pauldron the metal reported pressure and his shoulder underneath it reported none, and he had never in his life encountered a contact that arrived in one layer and stopped.

And the cold came out of him.

Outward, from the middle. That was the diagnostic and he made it in about a second and a half, because he had put men into cold water and pulled them out again and knew every stage of it. Cold from a room arrives at the fingers and the ears and the nose and works inward. This one started behind his sternum and went outward through the chest wall and down both arms and into the palms, closing the small vessels as it went, a wave of shutting-down travelling from the middle of him toward the edges.

His own body doing it to itself. No part of it came from the field or the hand. His own architecture reading the thing standing behind it and beginning, on its own authority and without consulting him, to prepare for a wound.

*Even my blood knows. It has gone to the middle and put a wall around the heart and the lungs and it is spending my hands to do it, and it did not ask, and it is correct.*

There would be an accounting for it afterward. The vessels in the hands do not reopen politely. Whatever this cost him would arrive in about four hours in the fingers and the ears, along with everything the ice had already done to two thousand other men, and he put that where he put such things.

He set his feet anyway.

---

The wind arrived from four directions and one of them was above.

It came into the narrows without a front. There was no gust and no build and nothing that moved the ice hanging in the air, and yet every loose thing on that road began to lean, and the lean was toward a point, and the point was eleven feet in front of Sodoku Moto and it was where an old man was standing.

He had not walked there.

Sodoku's whole trained apparatus went at him at once and came back with nothing. No projection, no working, no called thing. He stood on the road with the road under him and the ice hanging around him and moved it out of his way when he shifted his weight, and he was as present as any man Sodoku had ever stood in front of, and he was standing in front of both of them and around both of them at the same time.

Old. Seventy at least and hard with it, the way a man is hard when the work never stopped: bones showing through the face at the temple and the jaw, a mouth that had lost the shape of a mouth some years ago, white hair cropped to the skull with the scalp brown through it. Small. That was the surprise. He came up to Sodoku's collarbone. Wearing a working deel gone the colour of nothing at all, a hide belt with three knives on it and none of them dress knives, and boots that had been resoled by their owner more than once and badly.

His hands were enormous and out of proportion to the rest of him, and the tendons stood on the backs of them, and the last two fingers of the left one were gone at the second joint and had been for a very long time.

The eyes were the Shingan and they were open and they were not looking at Sodoku.

There was a hawk's feather through the left ear. Old, brown, the shaft worn shiny at the quill from being touched, and it was the only thing on him that had been chosen instead of used.

*Bōfū Fūkoku Moto.*

*I have known that name since I could read and I have never once expected to use it about a man standing on a road.*

*The Howling Hawk. Eleven mentions in the family literature and nine of them are the same three sentences copied forward, and the tenth is a marginal note in a hand nobody has identified, and the eleventh is the record of what was done at the Kurenai hall in the fourth year of the second Concord, which the Bench sealed and which my grandmother described to me once, in a doorway, with her hands in a bowl, and then never again.*

*He is nine hundred years dead. He was born before the Kōkan Line had a name and he held the eastern hall for forty-one years and refused the seat twice in writing, and the second refusal is the oldest document the family still holds and it is four words long.*

*He has two names because he was named at birth and named again afterward by the Plane, and the family literature keeps both because dropping either was considered dangerous. And he is called a hawk and not a wind, and I have wondered about that since I was eleven years old, and nobody at the Seat has ever been able to tell me why.*

Behind him, the hand on his shoulder did not move.

But something in the road changed, in the pressure and in the black and in the quality of the attention arriving over Sodoku's left ear, and it changed in a specific way that Sodoku had felt exactly once before, in a hall, when a man he had been negotiating with for two hours looked past him and saw somebody he had not expected to see alive.

*He knows him.*

*That thing is old enough to have met my ancestor and to remember what he looked like from the front.*

The old man grinned.

It was an awful expression on a mouth in that condition and it had a great deal of pleasure in it and none of the pleasure was kind.

"Well," said Bōfū Fūkoku Moto.

That was all he said for a while. He looked past Sodoku's shoulder at what was standing behind it and he did not hurry, and the wind kept leaning inward, and the ice that had been hanging in the air across four hundred paces began, all of it, to turn.

"They told me you would come back around," he said. "Sixty of us sat in the hall and worked it out and none of us liked the arithmetic and we did it anyway, and I have been in the wind nine hundred years waiting to find out whether the sixty were right, and here you are, and you have brought a hand and put it on my great-grandson's shoulder."

He wiped his mouth with the back of the ruined hand.

"They will tell you the sixty burned," he said, and he was still not looking at Sodoku. "They did. We burned the floor and every one of us went into the wind, and what came out the other side was not sixty men and was not one man either, and the Bench sealed the record because the Bench could not decide what to call it. I have been the wind over this country since before your family kept a ledger. I have watched eleven of you die badly and I did not come out for any of them."

He turned his head then, and looked at Sodoku for the first time, and the Shingan in the old eyes had a structure in it that no living Moto carried.

"You went quiet," he said. "That is all it ever wanted. Nine hundred years and not one of you would put the tools down."

He faced front again.

"Embrace despair."

The wind stopped leaning and started running.

---

There is a way a wind ends and Sodoku had never seen it done.

It came in on the point from every direction at once and it did not slow down as it converged, and that was the part his body refused before his mind caught up. A thing running inward across a shrinking circle should thin out and lose itself. This gathered. The circle closed and the speed went up, and the closer it came to the point the faster it came, and the rate was not something anybody had chosen. It was set by the medium and by the closing itself, and the old man was not driving it and had not been driving it since the first second. He had opened it and stepped back.

The condition was the silence.

Sodoku understood that at the last instant and understood that he had provided it. A converging thing of that kind can only run into ground that is holding nothing. It has to invade a quiet, and there had been a quiet in the narrows since he turned his back on the thing with his own face, and there had been a quiet in him since the same moment, the Ledger gone irrelevant and the Shingan gone irrelevant and the whole four-hundred-pace valley emptied of everything that could have pushed back.

The old man had walked into a silence that his descendant had made about ninety seconds earlier, and the silence had been the door, and refusing the thing with his own face had been the key, and nobody had told him and nobody was going to.

*He waited for it. Nine hundred years and he waited for the one man in the line who would go quiet in front of the right thing.*

The wind arrived.

Everything the Ashgate valley had in it went to that point and reached it together. Ice, the whole hanging suspension of it, four hundred paces of the stuff, drawn inward and down to a volume the size of a fist. Frozen mud. The wreck of the road. The letters that had come off nine spectral arms an hour ago and gone out. Frost off two thousand men. All of it converging, and going faster as it converged, and where it converged there was no space left to hold it and no time left to hold it in.

The speed at the centre went past what a number can be asked to do.

Then the point.

Everything that had come in came in at once and stopped being anywhere, and for a length of time that had no length the middle of the Ashgate road was a place where the whole valley existed at one address and the pressure at that address had no ceiling and the heat at that address had no ceiling and nothing that has ever been built could stand at the middle of it.

And it did not stay.

That was the thing Sodoku had not been told and had no way to know. A collapse of that kind does not end at the bottom. It arrives, and it turns, and it comes back out, and what comes back out is the same wind with every one of those four hundred paces still in it, expanding now, running outward at the speed it had going in.

He had time to understand that and no time at all to do anything with it.

The reflection hit.

It went through the narrows in a ring at head height and it went through the ice and the wreck and the ridges and the dead, and it took the black on the road and moved it, and for the first time since the ninth hour something that was not the black decided where things went.

Sodoku Moto was inside it and standing.

The wind went past both sides of him without touching him, which was a fact about the working and not a kindness, and his deel cracked flat against his back and his hair went forward across his face and his eyes ran and he did not close them.

He got his weight onto his front foot and turned his head to look at what was standing at his shoulder, and the hand was still there, and he brought the sword up out of the low guard for the first time that morning.

## Notes (author-facing, not canon)

**Kujo unwritten.** No new line, gesture or interiority. He is a hand with no weight in it, a change in the quality of attention over Sodoku's left ear, and one recognition that Sodoku reads off the road rather than off a face. His question stands unanswered until the last thing Bōfū says answers it for him.

**The line you wanted lands as a reversal.** *Embrace despair* comes back at him, and I gave it to the old man rather than to Sodoku, because Sodoku's whole scene is that he says nothing. Sodoku holds the sentence in his chest and does not spend it, which is the character note you set up. Tell me if you want it in his mouth instead; it is a two-line change.

**Bōfū Fūkoku Moto, built.** Small, seventy-hard, hands out of proportion, two fingers gone at the second joint, boots he resoled himself and badly, a hawk's feather through the left ear that is the only thing on him chosen instead of used. Named twice, at birth and afterward by the Plane, and the literature keeps both because dropping either was thought dangerous. Eleven mentions, nine of them the same three sentences copied forward. Refused the seat twice in writing and the second refusal is four words long and the oldest document the family holds.

**The sixty.** Your ancestor needed a reason to still be here, so: sixty of them sat in the Kurenai hall in the fourth year of the second Concord, did the arithmetic, disliked it, and burned the floor together. That is **Gisei performed collectively**, and what came out was not sixty men and not one man, and the Bench sealed the record because it could not decide what to call it. He has been the wind over that country since. He has watched eleven Moto die badly and came out for none of them. Originated and pending your ruling, and it is the largest thing in the file.

**Why he came out now, and it is not sentiment.** The technique requires a silence to run into. Sodoku made one ninety seconds earlier by turning his back on the Unburied and dropping into Asami, and *nine hundred years and not one of you would put the tools down* is the whole of the old man's reason. The previous scene is load-bearing for this one.

**The physics, researched: Guderley's converging shock.** A shock running inward on a point does not thin out. It accelerates as the radius closes, and the rate is not chosen by anyone; the similarity exponent falls out of the medium as an eigenvalue. At the moment of collapse the velocity, pressure and temperature at the centre all go unbounded. And it does not stop there: it reflects instantly and comes back out as an expanding shock carrying everything that went in. That is your line about the winds collapsing into the real world at one point, and it gives the technique a second half you did not have.

The precondition is the good part and it is also real. For the convergence to be exactly self-similar the region ahead of the shock has to be quiescent, with the sound speed vanishing identically. The wind has to invade a silence. So the working is not something Bōfū fires. It is something he opens into a quiet, and then steps back from, and the valley does the rest.

**The cold is Sodoku's own body, correctly diagnosed.** Environmental cold arrives at the fingers, ears and nose and works inward. This starts behind the sternum and travels outward, closing the small vessels as it goes, which is catecholamine-driven peripheral vasoconstriction: the body walling the core and spending the hands to do it. He knows the difference because he has pulled men out of cold water. And he prices it: whatever it costs him arrives in about four hours in the fingers and the ears, alongside everything the ice has already done to two thousand other men.

**The coyness given a source.** His grandmother named it when he was nine, in front of his mother, and would not take it back. *A boy who holds the tide grows into a man the tide holds.* He thought it was a compliment and was pleased with himself for a week.

**Research consulted, per §7.** Guderley self-similar converging shock: acceleration toward the origin, similarity exponent as eigenvalue rather than chosen parameter, unbounded velocity and pressure at collapse, instantaneous reflection into an outgoing shock, and the vanishing-sound-speed condition in the quiescent region ahead. Catecholamine-mediated peripheral vasoconstriction from standing knowledge, flagged as such.

**Rulings.**
1. *Bōfū Fūkoku Moto* entered on your name. The two wind-words stacked read as a doubled name, so I made that the point: birth-name and Plane-name, both kept. The hawk is the epithet and the reason for it is withheld, and Sodoku has wondered about it since he was eleven and nobody at the Seat can answer.
2. *The Kurenai hall, the sixty, the fourth year of the second Concord, and the sealed Bench record* are originated. Note the Gisei document already quotes an unattributed line scratched into the wall of the Kurenai meditation hall, date unknown, and I have written this as the event behind it.
3. *Kujo recognising him* is on your line and Sodoku reads it off the road, not the face. It tells Sodoku that the thing behind him is old enough to have met a man nine hundred years dead, and I have let him have that and nothing else.
4. *Eleven Moto watched to die badly* is a number that implies a list. Yours to fill.

**Open.** What the reflected shock does to the black, and to Kaalabad and Mu-jin at the far end of it. What the old man wants, since he has been explicit that he did not come out for the previous eleven. What four words are in the second refusal.

# Two Men Who Would Not Take the Seat

*Kharven. The Vaeloris quarter. The tenth hour.*

---

"I have to go."

He said it without turning round, because turning round would have cost him the four seconds he had, and because he had learned at twenty-six that a man who looks at somebody while refusing them is asking for help with the refusal.

"You cannot follow me into this. Not for any reason you are about to give me, and I have heard all of them and two of them are correct."

He went.

The Fourth Work is order and nothing else and it is the least impressive of the seven and it is the one he used now, and eleven hundred yards of ruined Vaeloris quarter passed under him in a way that had no running in it. His coat did not move. That was the difference nine months had made and he noticed it in passing and put it away: everything he had done before the ash had cost him drag, and drag is what happens to a body that has to negotiate with the air, and he had stopped negotiating.

The Hammer came with him and weighed what a smith's hammer weighs and was refusing, continuously, in his right hand.

He arrived over the boy and put himself between.

---

Asante Zettari was on the rubble with the body.

Darius took the whole of it in one pass because he had no choice about that any more. A man of fifty-six with the sixth Fist in him reads a scene the way a physician reads a chest, and the scene said: the child is nine or ten, the man in his arms is Zuberi Zettari, Dawn Guard, sovereign of Kushara, and he has been dead for somewhere between forty and ninety seconds. Entry at the sternum, no exit, and nothing about it that a surgeon would call a wound. There was no cavitation. A puncture at that velocity should have carried a temporary cavity out through the back and it had carried nothing, which meant nothing had passed through him in the ordinary sense and something had simply stopped being present along a line. The tissue around the track had gone the colour of absence and was going on doing it, outward through the pericardium and into the lung roots and the great vessels, at a rate that had not slowed since the heart stopped.

Void does not kill by trauma. It kills by removing the conditions under which a structure is permitted to continue, and the removal propagates along whatever the structure was joined to, which in a chest is everything.

Black in the mouth. Black at the corners of both eyes.

And the Shield of Kush, dropped, with a perfectly round hole through its centre.

*He dropped it.*

*He had it up and it failed and he dropped it and he turned round, and the turning is what killed him, and any officer in four worlds would tell you the turn was the wrong call. And I have read the Zettari succession for thirty years and I would have bet the seat that Zuberi Zettari would make exactly that call.*

The Obsession Force was standing over the whole district.

Crimson-black, in strands and chains, running out of a ten-year-old across four streets and killing things wherever it touched them without the child directing any of it. It came off him in a column and the column had a Name at the top of it and the Name was no longer being spoken, because the boy had stopped screaming and had gone to holding.

Aetheric Density in that district had no business being what it was. There is no Well under the Vaeloris quarter and the ward was cut and every standpipe on that street had been dry since the ninth hour, and the boy was producing it out of his own Crystal at a rate that would have put a Grade Six practitioner on the flags inside a minute. He had been at it for four.

*He does not know he is spending. That is the thing about Obsession and it is why the Accord will not license it. It sustains on refusal, and refusal is free at the point of use, and the bill arrives all at once and somewhere else.*

Darius stood in front of him and did the hardest thing he had done since the fourth month.

He held the Trait back.

*Every strand of this comes apart if I let go. All of it, in about a second and a half, across the entire district. It is refusal-sustained and I am what removes the conditions of refusal, and there is no aiming it and no partial application, and I have never once in twenty-one years had to hold it in.*

*And it is the only thing standing between that boy and the next thing to come down this street. And it is also the only thing he has left of what he feels about the man in his arms, because that is what Obsession is, and if I take it he will find out in about a second and a half that his grief has been quietly filed as a working and that I filed it.*

*So he keeps it. He keeps every strand of it. I will stand here with my hand shut and let a child hold the worst thing in his life in both arms, because it is his.*

It cost him more than the fall had. Purity is not a working and cannot be modulated and has no dial on it; it is a property of the Lattice and holding it in is holding a structural fact in abeyance, and the strain of that went into the same bones the Hammer was already standing on.

---

"Finally back."

The voice came up the street behind him and Darius knew it before he had finished hearing the first word, and there was one man in four worlds whose voice arrived that way and it had been arriving that way since Darius was nineteen.

He turned his head.

Aurelian Prudentius came up the rubble at a walk with his hands loose at his sides and no vestment on him worth the word and his feet leaving prints in the ash that filled with black water behind him. Young. That was the first thing and it went into Darius like a splinter, and the second thing was that the man cast a shadow and threw no Residue, and after six hours in a city on fire there was not one mark on him that an instrument would have read. The hands were young and there was no chalk and no ink on them, the thigh under the ruined black was whole, the jaw was somebody else's, and the eyes were the same silver they had always been.

"Darius." A small, warm, entirely successful smile. "I suppose we can fight this Void Regent together."

"You look well."

"I am told it is disconcerting."

"It is." Darius did not lower the Hammer. "Who paid for it."

Aurelian's smile did not falter at any point, and the ground under him went down the width of a coin and stayed down.

"Verinus."

"Where is he."

"Gone. The crossing shows no arrival, which means it took him entirely." He said it pleasantly, in a flat plain voice with no drop at the end of the sentence. "I looked for him among the traffic. He is not in it."

*He is wrong.*

*He is wrong about that and I know he is wrong about that because I have felt the wall standing since the ninth hour and I know what is holding it, and I am not going to tell him in this street.*

*And the wrongness is not the thing I have noticed.*

Darius looked at him properly for the first time.

"How long since you came up out of the ground."

"Six hours. A little less."

"And how much of what you are saying to me now would you have said six hours ago."

Aurelian considered that with visible interest.

"None of it," he said. "You have got there faster than the archbishop did and he has known me since he was fifteen."

"Charles is dead."

He put it down between them flat and without a run-up, because there was no arrangement of it that was kind and because he had been carrying it for eleven minutes with nobody to hand it to.

"I know," said Aurelian.

"How."

"He came apart on the Ashgate road at about the ninth hour, and I felt the office go, and it went in a way I had not been told to expect, and I have been thinking about it since." He put his hands behind his back. "You are the other half. I put the chain over his head in the lower chapel and you signed second and I have wondered for ten years which of you would go first."

"You watched him."

Aurelian said nothing.

"That is not a question," Darius said. "There is a man in that column with a wax tablet who was on the wall at the Seat this morning and he has already told three people. The halberdier came for Charles and you were four yards away with two fingers and you did not move, and you found it interesting."

"I did."

"Say the rest of it."

"I held twelve futures in the front of my head." The smile had gone somewhere, and what was underneath it was worse, because it was pleasant too. "In nine of eleven interventions the archbishop lives. I chose the one where I did nothing, and I chose it in the time it takes to notice a smell, and I did not experience the choosing as a decision. I have gone back over it four times since. It comes out the same way each time and it does not bother me, and the fact that it does not bother me is the only part I have been able to identify as a problem."

Darius stood in a ruined street with a hammer that could not be put down and a boy behind him holding a body.

"You are not the man who ordained me."

"No."

"The man who ordained me was frightened every day of his life and got up and did it anyway, and that was the entire content of him, and it was worth more than my Trait and my name and the whole of the apparatus I built for that Order." Darius did not raise his voice for this part. "I have read forty years of your correspondence. There are eleven letters in it that I keep in my own quarters and I have never told anybody which eleven. Not one of them could have been written by whatever is standing in this street."

"I have read them too." Aurelian's head came round, mildly. "Since this morning. I went and looked, because I could not remember why I had written the fourth one, and the file told me what I had done and could not tell me what it had been like. That is the condition. It is not that I have lost the record. The record is intact. It is that the record is now the whole of it."

"Then you know what you are."

"I know what I am no longer." He said it without any weight at all. "I have not decided whether the difference matters, and I notice that I do not appear to be in a hurry to find out, and I am telling you that plainly because you are the only man alive whose office obliges him to weigh me and I would rather you had the true reading than a flattering one."

"Then why are you here."

"Because I am still the Primate, and because I have seen every fate in four worlds at once and I have looked at what happens to this city and at what happens to me, and I am not afraid of any of it." Aurelian brought his hands round to the front again. "You have spent eleven years telling that Curia the office does not require the man to be good. I have not come to argue with you. I have come to be the instrument, which is what you told them the office was."

"I said that at thirty-five and I was showing off."

"You were correct."

"I have been correct about a great many things and it has never once helped anybody." Darius turned back toward the black. "Stay on my left. I want no part of you there and I want you there anyway, because whatever is left of you is a very great deal, and I would rather have it where I can see it, and I am too old to pretend that is not what I meant."

"That is the most honest thing anyone has said to me in six hours."

"It is the tenth hour of the worst day in the history of the North and you have made a joke."

"I have made four since I came up out of the ground. Nobody has laughed and I have kept count." A pause. "That is a thing I would not have done either."

"Verinus made one at his investiture." Aurelian looked out at the abyss standing over the palace. "Nobody minuted it. I have thought about that more than I have thought about most things."

---

Darius put the Hammer out at arm's length toward the black.

"I am going to enjoy this."

The filaments came up off the Lattice along both arms and stood out in the light and the light was still coming down through the gradient he had opened in the overcast four minutes ago.

"You thought you were rid of me. Did you not. You put me inside Fate and you shut it and you went about your business, and every account you have of me since is nine months old and every one of them is wrong."

His voice had gone up and he let it. He had not raised it in front of another living soul since the year he was twenty-six and had regarded that as a discipline, and he had spent nine months in an ash pit learning that some things are supposed to be said at volume by a man who means them.

"I have been waiting. Eagerly. In the dark, in a place with no hours in it, with an old man taking me apart one Work at a time and putting me back together wrong, and I have thought about this specific street and this specific minute every day of it."

The Hammer refused in his hand, continuously, as it had since it was made.

"I am going to kill you."

Beside him, Aurelian Prudentius put both hands on his sides.

Then he took them off and put them behind his back, which is the posture, and which he had been taught at nineteen in a room with four men in it and had used at eleven hundred offices of the dead.

He smiled at the black.

"It is nice to meet you again," said the Primate of the Holy Sea.

The ground under him went down another coin's width.

"You are the creature. The strong one. The reason behind the whole of it, and I have read the file and there is nobody else in it." He tilted his head very slightly. "I am going to enjoy giving you the word of the Judger."

Behind them a ten-year-old boy held a dead man's head against his chest and the Obsession Force went on standing over four streets, and Darius Ignatius Sanctus Sanctorum kept his hand shut and did not let go of the one thing he had that could have stopped it, and started walking.

## Notes (author-facing, not canon)

**Nobody restricted is written.** Haruki gets no line and no reaction; Darius refuses her without turning round, and the refusal is the whole of her presence. Kujo is the black at the end of the street and nothing else. Asante is rendered strictly from outside, and I have given him no interiority, because he is a grieving child in another author's beat and the scene does not need me inside him.

### The spine I built it on

**Darius's Trait is the one thing that could unmake what the boy is holding.** Obsession is refusal-sustained. Purity removes the conditions under which refusing is possible. So the man who arrives to protect Asante is carrying the only weapon on that street that would take the child's power apart, across four streets, in about a second and a half, with no aiming and no partial application.

So he holds it in, and it costs him more than the fall did. And his reason is not tactical:

*It is the only thing he has left of what he feels about the man in his arms, because that is what Obsession is, and if I take it he will find out that his grief has been quietly filed as a working and that I filed it.*

That is the hardest thing he does in the scene and nobody in it knows he is doing it.

### The two of them

You asked for more between them and this is where the scene lives. What they actually have: Aurelian put the chain over Charles's head in the lower chapel and Darius signed second, and both of them have spent ten years wondering which half would go first, and Darius has just found out by paying the bill.

**Darius reads Aurelian in four exchanges.** *Who paid for it. How long since you came up out of the ground. And how much of what you are saying to me now would you have said six hours ago.* Aurelian answers all three accurately, because he has no reason left not to.

**Aurelian's own account of himself is the worst thing in the file.** He went and read his own letters this morning because he could not remember why he had written the fourth one, and the file told him what he had done and could not tell him what it had been like. *It is not that I have lost the record. The record is intact. It is that the record is now the whole of it.*

And he volunteers the twelve futures without being pushed, and states that he does not experience the choosing as a decision, and that the only part he can identify as a problem is that it does not bother him.

**Aurelian is wrong about Verinus and Darius does not correct him in the street.** The crossing shows no arrival, Aurelian reads that as *taken entirely*, and Darius has felt the wall standing since the ninth hour and knows what is holding it.

**And Darius keeps him on his left anyway.** *I want no part of you there and I want you there anyway, because whatever is left of you is a very great deal, and I would rather have it where I can see it.*

### The body

Zuberi's wound gets its mechanism. Entry at the sternum, no exit, **no cavitation** — a puncture at that velocity should carry a temporary cavity out through the back, and it carried nothing, which means nothing passed through him in the ordinary sense and something simply stopped being present along a line. Void does not kill by trauma. It removes the conditions under which a structure is permitted to continue, and the removal propagates along whatever the structure was joined to, which in a chest is everything.

And the Shield of Kush is on the ground with a perfectly round hole through the middle of it, and he dropped it and turned, and any officer in four worlds would call the turn the wrong call, and Darius would have bet the seat that Zuberi Zettari would make exactly that call.

### Rulings

1. *Asante is spending out of his own Crystal* at a rate that would put a Grade Six on the flags in a minute, and has been at it four. Obsession sustains on refusal, refusal is free at the point of use, and the bill arrives all at once and somewhere else. That bill is unwritten and it is coming.
2. *Aurelian throws no Residue and casts a shadow.* Six hours in a burning city and not one mark an instrument would read. Originated, and it is the physical tell for what he now is.
3. *Eleven letters of Aurelian's that Darius keeps in his own quarters* and has never told anybody which eleven. Originated.
4. *Darius has not raised his voice in front of another living soul since twenty-six*, and does it here on purpose, and says why.
5. *Kujo trapped Darius inside Fate* on your line, which reframes the ash at Sum-gol as an imprisonment that became a forge, and means Gimbzo was either the jailer or the thing already in the cell. Large, and yours.
6. **Zuberi Zettari is dead.** Mizuyi is married into that house. Somebody is going to have to carry that south.

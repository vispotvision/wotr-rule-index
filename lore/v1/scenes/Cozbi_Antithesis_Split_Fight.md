"No. This won't do." Cozbi's voice carried across the burning clearing with the particular disappointment of a man reviewing a performance he'd expected more from. "I expect more than this. I want more. I want the actual urge of battle, the blood and the sweat and whatever's left of the tears once the fear finishes wringing them out of you."

He came down out of the air on the last standing limb of Impharous's ruined frame, using the construct's final mandible as a step rather than a landing, his cloak snapping wide around him in the heat still rolling off the scorched grass. His black hair whipped loose across his shoulders in the same wind, and he laughed, low and genuinely entertained, before he'd fully found his footing.

"Your Iron Bison is weak next to your father's." He said it plainly, no cruelty performed in the delivery, which somehow made it land worse than cruelty would have. "If you carried Dougou's ceiling, I might actually be in some trouble tonight. But you didn't inherit his gift for raw output, only his patience, and you brought this failed scrap of Valorin brilliance along with you for company." His gaze slid to Dhaerin, unhurried, cataloguing. "Never quite matched his siblings for skill, so he leaned entirely on cleverness instead. I have some unfortunate news for the two of you. He'll have to die tonight. I've no interest in keeping him around to finish whatever formula he's been chewing on."

Dhaerin's jaw tightened, though nothing else in his posture moved. "You think that's going to be easy. Two against one."

Cozbi clapped his hands together once, delighted. "Two against one, yes, but let's be precise about the arithmetic, since precision seems to matter so much to you. Two children against one adult. I'll need to spend some real effort tonight, I'll grant you that much." His hands came together and locked, fingers interlacing, and the air between his palms began to fold inward on itself, Essence drafts gathering there in layered colors, pulled whole from souls he'd harvested across years he didn't bother counting anymore. "But I don't owe either of you an explanation. You wouldn't understand it if I gave one. So instead I'll simply have to show you how thoroughly useless this is."

Rengai did not wait for the summoning to finish.

He came forward low and fast, forearms still faintly smoking at the wrist from the cost of the first exchange, and drove a jab toward the gathering light between Cozbi's hands, aiming to break the working before it could close.

Cozbi's body split down the center the instant before contact.

It did not tear. It parted the way a reflection parts when a hand passes through still water, one Cozbi remaining rooted at the mandible-step while a second peeled away from him at an angle no anatomy should have allowed, already moving to intercept. The shadow half caught Rengai's jab on a forearm that gave slightly, absorbing rather than blocking, and answered with a palm strike compressed to a point no wider than a coin, driving a shockwave directly into Rengai's abdomen that folded him half a step backward before his own heat-hardened muscle caught the recoil and held.

*Antithesis,* Rengai thought, teeth gritted around the pain blooming through his ribs. *Of course that's what he calls it, even when he doesn't say the word. Everything about him is the mirror of what should be standing there instead.*

*They fight like children who believe conviction is the same thing as sufficiency,* Cozbi thought, watching both halves of himself work without needing to direct either consciously, the way a man doesn't need to remind his own hands how to hold a cup. *I will enjoy watching that belief fail them both. I have watched it fail better men than these.*

The second Cozbi, whole and unhurried, turned his attention toward Dhaerin without bothering to finish the summoning working his first hands had started. Three spikes of raw earth answered before he'd taken a full stride, breaching upward out of the scorched ground at ankle height, aimed to catch his footing rather than his body.

Dhaerin read the geometry of it before his eyes finished confirming the shape, his Scripted Soul Lattice surfacing along his throat in fine gold light, and he stepped through the gap between the second and third spike rather than around them, a margin of clearance no wider than a hand's width. His grimoire fell open against his palm without him consciously willing it, page already turned to the working he needed.

"Fixatio." The word came out clipped, economical, none of the ceremony he'd given the larger casting earlier. A binding-chain construct uncoiled from the open page and lashed toward the earthen spikes still rising, script-forged links wrapping each one and locking its growth mid-formation, freezing three columns of raw stone in place like teeth caught in the act of biting down.

Cozbi's clone did not slow for the interruption. It came through the frozen spikes at an angle, close enough now that Dhaerin could see the specific stillness in its face, the particular calm of something that had never once needed to hurry toward killing a person it didn't consider a person at all.

*He isn't holding anything back because he doesn't believe I'm capable of costing him anything,* Dhaerin thought, and the thought did not arrive as fear so much as correction, the specific clarity of a man revising a hypothesis mid-experiment. *Then I'll have to be the variable he didn't account for.*

He raised the wand and did not chant this time. Chanting cost him seconds he no longer had spare, and the binding on his belt had been sitting half-prepared since the fight's opening exchange for exactly this reason.

"Vincula Tenent."

Script-chains erupted from the ground beneath Cozbi's advancing clone, the same tensile lattice he'd used against the earth-spikes now aimed directly at legs and wrists, closing fast enough that the clone's own forward momentum worked against it, each attempted stride tightening the net a fraction further rather than breaking free of it.

Across the clearing, Rengai straightened out of the fold the shockwave had driven into him and closed the distance again before the pain in his ribs had finished registering fully, heat building once more along both forearms in a second, hungrier flare. The clone facing him raised a hand to answer in kind, gathering another compressed strike, and for the length of one held breath both fighters stood close enough to read the exact shape of what the other intended before either one committed to throwing it.

Rengai moved first.

He drove his forearm up and through the clone's gathering strike rather than around it, meeting the compressed shockwave at its point of origin before it had finished condensing, and the two forces met with a concussion that cracked the ground beneath both men outward in a spreading web of fractures. The clone's hand lost cohesion at the wrist, unraveling into the same fine grey drift that had taken Impharous apart, and Rengai's own arm went numb to the elbow from the recoil, heat and impact both driving deep enough into muscle and bone that his fingers refused, for one long second, to close into a fist at all.

He forced them closed anyway, and turned to see how much of the fight was still standing.

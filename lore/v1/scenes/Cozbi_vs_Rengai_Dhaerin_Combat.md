Ash rose off Rengai's skin in a ring before he'd finished deciding to call it, particulate and grit and old carbon lifting off the ground around his boots and gathering into a shape that bent, unmistakably, toward the horned silhouette of a bison's skull. Each grain struck the next hard enough to spark, a dry, metallic clinking filling the clearing the way hail fills a tin roof, friction from a thousand collisions per second building heat past the point where the surrounding air could carry the charge away safely. The grass beneath his boots blackened and curled inward. The deer carcass Impharous had abandoned lost its shape by degrees, hide and muscle sublimating into fine grey powder that lifted off the ground and joined the swirling ring around him, one more fuel source pulled into a furnace that hadn't asked permission to be fed.

Rengai breathed once, slow, and pulled the whole ring inward through his crown and down through the root of him in a single motion, a full lungful drawn down past the place where most bodies would refuse it.

"Iron Bison."

He said the name the way his father said it, flat, unornamented, a fact rather than a boast, and his skin lit from beneath with a dull, banked glow that ran down both forearms and gathered at his knuckles in coils of compressed heat. The ash he'd pulled inward didn't vanish. It became current, aetheric pathways carrying it through muscle and bone until his whole body ran hot enough that the air directly around him shimmered.

He was gone from where he'd been standing before the shimmer finished settling.

Impharous's foreleg parted at the joint with a sound closer to a forge quench than a cut, chitin flash-heating past its structural threshold and failing all at once rather than resisting, and Rengai's hand was already through the second leg before the first one finished falling. He drove his palm flat into the underside of the creature's abdomen next, past the softer plate where Dhaerin's read had told him to aim, and the impact carried enough concentrated heat that the wound didn't bleed so much as combust, souls Cozbi had bound into the construct's architecture tearing loose in a burst of fire that took the whole abdomen with it in a single collapsing detonation.

Cozbi was already three body-lengths into the air by the time the fire caught, his own Essence wrapped tight around him in a translucent shell that turned the fall of ash and burning chitin harmless against his skin. He did not look surprised. He looked, if anything, mildly pleased, a man watching a wager he'd half-hoped to lose pay out in his favor instead.

Four hands descended from above him, each easily the size of a cart, formed in the space of a single breath from souls carried as casually as coin, translated without effort from spirit into something the material world could strike with.

Below, on the scorched ground, electricity cracked between Dhaerin's raised wand and the grimoire open at his hip, the sound arriving a half-beat after the light did, current finding the shortest path between two points and complaining loudly about the trip.

"Invoco caelum." Dhaerin's voice carried none of the theater most casters gave a chant, only the flat cadence of a man reading a proof aloud. "Da mihi vim, da mihi potestatem, ut terror hostis mei fiat calor, fiat fulgur."

The wand came up. A pillar of raw current answered him, arcing upward in branching, fractal paths, current searching a hundred directions at once before committing to the one that worked. Aether wound around the pillar's core in a slow, visible spiral, distorting the air the way heat distorts a horizon, and the pillar carried a second property beneath the first, a pull, subtle and total, the same law that kept a man's feet on the ground applied now with intention rather than habit.

"Tellus Magna."

The runes it left behind hung in the air, gold-black script rotating slowly, incomplete sentences waiting for the rest of their grammar.

"Via Draconis."

The runes folded into each other, seams closing, structure resolving out of structure, a jaw assembling itself out of scattered teeth, until a maw wide enough to swallow a cart hung suspended in the space between Dhaerin and the falling shapes of Cozbi's hands, jaws already parting to close on whatever crossed its threshold.

Two of the four hands came down together and struck the pillar broadside, Cozbi's construct absorbing the current directly into its own borrowed architecture and scattering it sideways in a hiss of steam and burnt ozone, the electricity dying against palms built from material that had stopped answering to the world's ordinary rules the moment its former owners stopped needing it.

The third hand moved differently. Its palm split along a seam that hadn't existed a moment before, and out of the opening came a blade of compressed spiritual pressure shot forward with the exact economy of a stiletto driven underhand, no wasted motion, no flourish, aimed directly at the widening jaw of the Via Draconis construct. It entered the maw's structure at the hinge point where the runes' grammar was weakest and tore through in a single motion, the dragon's jaw losing its closing force the instant the sentence holding it together came apart mid-word.

Dhaerin's grimoire snapped shut against his hip on its own, feedback rippling back up the wand and into his arm hard enough to buckle his knees by an inch before he caught himself.

Rengai landed in the ash and cooling embers where Impharous's body had been, chest heaving, both forearms still smoking faintly at the wrist where the technique's cost had settled into muscle rather than passing through clean, and looked up at the four remaining hands circling above him with the flat, unhurried patience of a man taking inventory of exactly how much fight was left standing between him and the thing he'd come to kill.

The nearest hand came down first, fingers splayed wide enough to bracket a horse, and Rengai didn't try to outrun it. He drove both palms into the heel of it instead, meeting mass with heat rather than force, and the point of contact flared white before the material began to shed itself in burning flakes, the same slow combustion that had taken Impharous's abdomen apart now working through borrowed spirit-flesh a finger's width at a time. His shoulders screamed under the impact regardless. Heat wasn't the same as weightlessness, and a hand the size of a cart still carried enough mass to drive him three feet backward through churned earth before the fingers gave out entirely and scattered into drifting cinders.

A second hand swept low while he was still skidding, aimed at the joint of his knee rather than his center, and Rengai felt the read of it a half-second before his eyes confirmed it, some old Cinerion instinct reading the air pressure ahead of the strike the same way his father's had for forty years before him. He rolled beneath it, ash smoking off his shoulder where the passing edge grazed close enough to blister cloth, and came up already reaching for the flask still half-empty at his hip.

Dhaerin had not stopped moving through any of it. His wand hand trembled faintly from the feedback that had buckled his knees, and he forced the tremor into stillness through sheer discipline rather than any recovery the moment had given him time for.

"Tellus Magna." He said it again, lower this time, the correction of a man refining a formula rather than repeating a failed one. The gravity current he called this time did not aim at the hands at all. It bent the ground itself beneath the two still descending toward Rengai, a localized well opening in the scorched earth just deep enough to pull their trajectory half a body-length off its line.

It was enough. The third hand's strike meant for Rengai's spine caught only air and the edge of the crater Dhaerin's working had opened, driving itself elbow-deep into dirt instead of flesh, and Cozbi, still airborne, watched the miss with the same unbothered patience he'd shown from the very start of this, as though the fight were a formula he'd already solved and was only now confirming the arithmetic aloud.

"Persistent." Cozbi's voice carried easily across the burning clearing. "Both of you. I'll give you that much before this finishes."

Rengai came up out of the roll with the flask already open, heat radiating off both forearms in visible waves, and did not answer him with anything but the look of a man who had stopped needing conversation the moment the fighting started.

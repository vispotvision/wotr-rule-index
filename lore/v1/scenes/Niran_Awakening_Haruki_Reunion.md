"I am..."

Reborn Niran held the pause the way his old body used to hold a breath before a dive, the last still second before something changed permanently. He looked down at his own hands, the fingers longer than he remembered them, the knuckles unmarked by any of the scars the last seven years should have carved into them and hadn't, because this body had never earned a single one of its own yet.

"Returned."

He closed both hands into fists and pushed himself upright. His hair fell well past his waist now, black and unbound, and some old, unbidden part of him noted the resemblance before the rest of him caught up to why the resemblance mattered. He looked the way Naiser had looked at this age, the same black hair, the same set through the jaw, a dead man's youth handed forward into a son who had just outlived him by dying first.

The thought lasted less than a second before his legs betrayed him. His composure buckled all at once, and he dropped to one knee hard enough that the impact should have hurt and mostly didn't, his new nerves still learning which signals deserved his attention. He had rushed the reconstruction. He knew it the moment his knee hit the ground, the specific knowledge of a man who understands exactly which corner he cut and exactly what it is going to cost him.

He breathed in, and the air around him answered the breath wrong.

Reality did not so much bend as concede, a slow and total unraveling in the space immediately around him, matter and law both losing their grip on the specific shape they were supposed to be holding. It was not a technique. It was overflow, seven years of gathered authority pouring out of a vessel that had not yet learned where its own edges were, and it took the ground beneath him with it in slow, widening rings of erasure that no one had asked for and nothing present had the strength to refuse.

He pressed one hand flat against his own chest, over the place where a heartbeat should have been settling into a rhythm and instead kept restarting itself in fits.

"All these innocent souls." His voice climbed before he could stop it, cracking somewhere in the middle of the sentence. "All these innocents. Being terrified." He caught the word in his own throat and forced it back down, and it came out again anyway, louder, nearly a scream. "Terrified!"

He pressed his palm hard against his own face, dragging it down across his eyes as though he could physically scrape the sight of the burning horizon out of his own skull. He looked, in that moment, exactly like a man coming apart, and some distant, cataloguing part of him noted that fact too, filed it beside everything else his new mind refused to stop filing even now.

"No." He shook his head, once, violent. "This wasn't my doing. It carries my essence. My architecture is written all through it, I can feel that much. But I would never." His voice broke again, quieter this time, more dangerous for the quiet. "I would never do this."

He looked up at the sky, at the smoke standing in for stars, and something in his face went very still.

"This thing." The words came out flat now, stripped of everything but certainty. "Is the nightmare."

*Someone has taken what I am and worn it as a mask,* he thought, and the thought arrived with the cold, total clarity his new mind produced for anything it had finally finished refusing to doubt. *And every soul screaming on that horizon is screaming because they mistook the mask for my face.*

Her footsteps were hurried, not with fear but of urgency. She could feel it before she could see it, the way the world shifts and stirs underneath the new pressure. The way the wind current changes once it passes. The air became still, not embodied by the weight, but the law that filled the gaps of where others couldn't fill.

She stopped before she could speak, her breath light and lacking. Suffocating with the revelation of what stood before her. For many cycles she had witnessed it, the death of her younger cousin Niran, but when the reality set in, the truth that what she had predicted had become true, she couldn't help but tremble. Her eyes shifted as she grew focused.

"Is this… truly you? After all this time… you still live?" She asked, as if needing the validation, to understand what she sees is not a phantom of her past, but the truth manifested as sight.

"You… You died…. Or at least that is what I was told to believe… but then you remain… how?" She said, her hand shaking a bit but not out of fear, just shock. Her composure collected as she stepped forward, not presenting the notion of violence, but the comforting of knowing they still live on.

"We thought we lost you, but you live on." She said, almost breathless as she approached him, disregarding the warnings that Cozbi had given her. The pain was too much for her to bare, a weight lifted off her for a moment. She found peace in knowing this, her cousin lived.

Niran smacked the touch away before he had decided to, the motion arriving out of some deep, animal register that had not yet learned the difference between comfort offered and a hand raised against him. He regretted it in the same instant, though the regret did not undo the strike any more than seven years undid the seven years.

"Haruki." Her name left him rough, unpracticed, a voice that had not shaped a single word aloud in longer than either of them had a clean way to measure. "I am not a phantom. I am not what did this." He gestured, one shaking hand, toward the burning line of the horizon without looking away from her face. "Something wearing what I used to be has done this in my name, and I intend to make it very sorry for the borrowing."

He tried to stand fully upright and barely held his own balance, his legs still relearning what they were for after seven years spent as nothing but architecture and intention. He had not occupied a physical body in all that time, and the body remembered how to fail before it remembered how to stand.

"Stay back." His voice steadied on the second try, the Septarch settling into the words even as the boy underneath them still fought to keep his footing. "Whatever that thing is out there wearing my face, it does not get to finish what it started."

He forced his legs to hold, planted his feet against the shuddering ground, and began walking toward the fires.

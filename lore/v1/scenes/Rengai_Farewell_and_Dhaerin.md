Rengai had gone to see his son before anything else, because a man who put a war before his own child had already lost something no victory would give back.

The house smelled of banked coals and the particular sweetness of the dried apple Lyria kept in the pantry for exactly this hour of the evening, and underneath both sat the fainter mineral trace that followed Rengai everywhere he went, ash settled so deep into the wood of the doorframe over the years that the house itself had started to carry his signature. He stopped in the doorway, one hand braced against the frame, and looked at his son across the low firelight.

Asante was small for six, all elbows and a stubborn chin he had not earned from his mother, and he looked up from the floor where he'd been arranging carved wooden birds into some private formation only he understood the rules of.

Rengai crossed the room and knelt, his knee cracking against the floorboards the way it always did lately, and placed his hand against his son's cheek. The ember-red eye held steady on the boy's face while the dark one drifted, briefly, toward the window and whatever lay past it.

"Little man." His voice came out rougher than he meant it to. "You have to be strong for me. Understood?"

He had a second sentence ready behind that one, and he swallowed it before it could leave him. He did not know if he was coming back. He did not know it with any certainty at all, and Rengai had spent his whole life refusing to speak in certainties he hadn't earned, not to enemies, not to allies, and least of all to a six-year-old who would remember every word his father said to him tonight for the rest of his life. So he gave Asante a pat on the head instead, firm and brief, and stood.

Lyria was already watching him from across the room, arms loosely folded, her eyes soft in the particular way they always went soft when she was choosing not to argue with something she disagreed with. There was a smile on her face too, though it didn't reach all the way up, and beneath both the softness and the smile Rengai could see the thing she'd actually come to look at. Not his leaving. His conviction. She had learned, over twelve years of marriage, exactly which parts of him bent and which parts had never once bent for anyone, and she was reading the second kind now in the set of his shoulders.

"I have to go, Lyria. I have to see this through." He kept his voice low, level, the register he used when a thing was true and did not need volume to make it truer. "I might be the only one capable of standing in front of Cozbi and coming out the other side of it. That's probably exactly what he wants, if I'm honest with you. He wants me alone. He wants to be the one who takes me." He let a beat pass, and something dry and humorless moved at the corner of his mouth. "Don't worry. I don't plan on giving him the satisfaction."

Lyria's jaw tightened, and for a moment he watched her decide not to shake her head at him, not to tell him no, because she already knew what his answer would be if she did. She could not ask him to sit idle in this house while children were dying somewhere past the ridge. She had never once been able to ask that of him, and some nights she resented him for it and other nights it was the exact reason she'd married him in the first place.

"There's the dragon first," Rengai said, quieter now. "That's its own problem, and a worse one than Cozbi, if I'm being honest about the order of things." He reached out and rested two fingers briefly against her jaw, an echo of the gesture he'd just given his son. "But know this. I will come back. I will return."

He looked down at Asante again, who had gone still on the floor, one wooden bird still clutched in his fist.

"Take care of your mother while I'm gone." Rengai's voice softened all the way down, the register he used only in this house, for these two people. "She's going to need every bit of support you can give her. That's your work now. Understood?"

Asante nodded, solemn in the specific way small children are solemn when they've decided an instruction matters more than they have words to say so.

Rengai gathered his cloak from the hook by the door and the flask of pressed ash from the shelf beside it, settled both into place with the ease of a body that had done this exact sequence more times than he wanted to count, and walked out into the evening with his eyes already lifted toward the sky.

Dhaerin was waiting for him at the edge of the path, cloak dark against the last of the light, the faint gold geometry at his temple catching what little remained of it.

"I'd like to come with you."

Rengai stopped and looked at him, the confusion plain on his face before he'd decided to hide it. "You're certain about this. I can't promise either of us comes back from where I'm walking."

"I am certain." Dhaerin said it the way he said most things, with the flat, unhurried precision of a man who had already read the whole page before speaking any part of it aloud. "Xhem will manage Vaeloris in my absence. He has managed harder things with less warning. And I find I would rather try to save the people I can reach than finish cataloguing the reasons I couldn't." A faint, self-aware weariness crossed his face, there and gone. "I have spent a great deal of my life finishing things that mattered only to me. I would like, for once, to finish something that matters to someone else."

Rengai studied him a moment longer, the way he studied most things before deciding whether they'd hold weight, and found nothing in the man's face that read as doubt.

"If you're certain," he said, "I can't very well stop you."

They had made it no further than the second bend in the path when a thin line of pale script peeled itself out of the dusk ahead of them and folded, mid-air, into the shape of a small geometric bird, its wings built from sentences still rearranging their own letters as it hovered.

Rengai's hand went instinctively to the flask at his hip before he recognized the make of the thing, Vaeloris craft through and through, and let his hand fall back to his side.

The bird's wings caught the last light as it spoke in Xhem's own measured cadence, words already fixed into its shape before it had ever left the warden's hand. Cozbi had spent years preparing for powerful men to arrive angry, it told him, and going immediately was not the same as taking the initiative. It was arriving at the hour the enemy had already chosen. It told him that Niran's new senses might trace back to the dragon, to the soured Wellsprings, to whatever had touched him beyond the Veil, and that until they knew which, chasing the loudest trail risked exposing everyone still bound to Niran through the Plane of Fate. It told him, if he still meant to go, to come back to Vaeloris first. Not for permission. For information.

Then, last, quieter than the rest, it told him that Lyria would prefer that Asante continue having a father.

Rengai stood very still on the path, the ash flask cool against his palm, the bird's wings folding closed as the message finished delivering itself into the space between the two men.

He exhaled through his nose, slow, and something in his shoulders that had been locked tight since he'd left the containment corridor eased by one degree, no more.

"Insufferable old man," he muttered, though there was no heat left in it, only the specific fondness reserved for people who were usually right and never let you forget it.

He turned back toward the village he'd just left, Dhaerin falling into step beside him without a word, and the two of them walked back through the dark to gather whatever facts Xhem intended to hand them before either man set out to face the thing burning the Inner World down to its foundations.

# A Name Held In Common

*Part One: the coast road, after the cut.*
*Part Two: the Warden's house above Ollavaeth Ridge, nine days later.*
*POV: Ruvaen Aloreth, then Ilthára Korvaeth.*
*Dova'Kan, Gorgi, Xmere and Baraxes are not written; their beats stay open.*

---

# PART ONE: THE ROAD

## I. WHAT HE SAID WITHOUT TURNING ROUND

He had got eleven paces before the correction reached him and he stopped where he was and did not turn round.

"You are right and I heard you say it at the time.

"Retrieve. Not execute. I took the word you used and I took the space in it, and I did it on purpose and about four seconds after you said it, and I am not going to stand on this road and tell a dragon I misunderstood plain Common.

"Enter it however you like. Mine will enter it as I did it, with your word and my word side by side on the same line, so that a man reading it in forty years can measure the gap I walked through."

The sea worked at the shingle on the left. Ten thousand men behind him had still not made one sound between them.

"I will have my vengeance and I have not finished having it.

"I hope the two of us are never on opposite sides of a field, and I am going to be honest about why, which is not that I would lose. It is that there is a crueller version of me than the one you have met, and you have not seen it, and I would rather you did not.

"I have no hatred for your people. I had none this morning and I have none now and nothing on this road has changed it. If you are wise you will forget the whole of this ever happened and go home across your own water.

"I will kill the people responsible for this. I need no help with it and I want none, and I would refuse it in writing and have the refusal copied to your own archive, so that nobody could later say I had been assisted."

---

## II. THE STAIN

"My own people will call this a stain.

"They will be correct. There is a form for a commander taken on a field with no weapon in his hands, and it is old, and the whole of it exists so that the thing I have just done cannot be done, and every gallery in Velthaeir will have a version of this inside the month with my name at the top of it.

"I do not care.

"I have never done any of this for glory and I have never done one hour of it for the chair. I do not want the chair. I said so on a quarry stair yesterday morning and it was true then and it is true standing in this.

"It is the principle and nothing else.

"No one will ever hold these lands as emperor again while I am breathing. Not her. Not me. Nobody. The elven nations are going to come out of this split, and they are going to stay split, and I am going to make certain of it, and I will tell you what that costs so nobody can say I did it in ignorance.

"We were one people once and we made it a virtue, and what a virtue does in a house full of proud men is turn into a rule about who is properly inside the house. Nine hundred years of that. Every war we have ever had, we had with a cousin. Unity did not keep us alive. Unity is the thing we have been killing each other over, because a people who are supposed to be one thing cannot ever stop auditing which of them is the real one.

"So let there be four of us, and five, and eleven, and let them hate each other across borders like ordinary nations, which is cheaper than what we do now.

"Perhaps in some age a long way from here they come back together and do it better. I doubt it. It will not be in my life and it will not be by my hand and I am not going to pretend I would recognise it if it happened."

---

## III. THE THAELUN

"And it is not what they did to my sister.

"I want that understood. I have said her name once in eleven years and I said it two minutes ago over that body and it did nothing, and it is not the reason.

"They had her on the eleventh day of the withheld season and they did it inside an hour and it was not slow. That is more than most people in this war got.

"It is the Thaelun."

*Say it. It is nine days from the boards to Rovann and this is the thing I have never once allowed onto a board.*

"Nine women. Her sworn shield, and they held a name in common, which is a thing our law allows and allows perhaps four times in a century. One name, validated once, in front of a convened assembly, held jointly by all nine at the same time. Not nine names. One, in nine bodies.

"They took the house in the second hour. The nine were alive when the fighting stopped and they were not alive at dawn and I have read what was done to them in the interval and I am not going to say it on a road in front of ten thousand people. It is in a sealed entry at Velthaeir and any Reckoner who wants it may have it and none of them have ever asked twice.

"Here is the part that is law and not grief.

"A name held in common passes to the survivors. That is the entire mechanism and it is why the form exists, because a name in common cannot die while one holder is standing. They knew that. Somebody in that house knew our law well enough to know it, and they went through all nine, and they did not stop at eight.

"So the name is gone. Not vacant. Not withheld. Gone, out of the world, with no instrument in the four quarters able to make it again, because a name in common requires a living holder to pass it and there is not one and there never will be.

"They killed nine women and they extinguished a thing that had been continuously held since before the Vessel Coast had a charter.

"I will kill all of them for it. Every hand and every man who gave an order about it and every clerk who filed the return. It is the one piece of this I consider an honour and I have never once been ashamed of it.

"I do not care about the story. I have never cared about the story.

"I only ever wanted this."

---

## IV. WHAT HIS FACE DOES

He had been told about his eyes twice.

Once by Ithuran Melloran, in the fourth year, in the lower yard, who had put it in about nine words and had then found something urgent to do with a chart. Once by Corvienne Sorreth, in form, as part of an entry, in a sentence she had struck out herself before the fair copy and had left legible in the draft.

They both said the same thing. That the grey went out of them. That it stopped being a colour and went flat, the way water goes flat, or the way the light goes flat on a headland about forty minutes before weather.

*I have never seen it. There is no mirror in a war and nobody has ever held one up.*

*What I know is what happens in me, which is that the thing I call Iesera stops threading and starts attending, and I stop being alone behind my own face, and the whole of the world at that moment reads to me the way a chart reads. Flat, and legible, and considerably smaller.*

*And when it goes, I have a headache, and I have lost four to eleven minutes, and the people in the room do not want to discuss it.*

He did not know it was happening now. He never did.

Ruvaen Aloreth stood on a coast road with a dead drow behind him and his sister's name still lying in the air where he had put it, and went on down the line at a walk, and the men in the fourth rank got out of his way without being told and did not look at him as he passed.

# PART TWO: THE RIDGE

## V. NINE DAYS LATER

The field book came up the ridge at the eleventh hour with a Vhaeloth's seal on it and the runner's hands still shaking.

Ilthára Korvaeth read it in the chair, in the raw light, with the shutters open and the ache arriving behind her eyes exactly on schedule.

She read it twice.

Her brother was dead on a coast road. The head taken in one cut by Aloreth. Four thousand men taken or dispersed, six caster frames destroyed, ninety-one hooks lost with the carts. Two foreign nationals of draconic house on the field, one of whom had stopped the air.

She put the book down on the arm of the chair.

*Four thousand.*

*I sent him with four thousand. I have sixty in my own and twenty in each of four houses, and the Bench would put every one of them on that coast road tomorrow if I said the word out loud in the right room, and I sent him with four thousand because four thousand was what the argument was worth.*

*He knew that when he went. He did the arithmetic on the road out and it came out the same as mine and he never once asked me for another man.*

She did not move for some while.

Then she picked the book up again and read the third page, which was the one the runner had been frightened of, and which was Sennuvar Vhaeloth's record of what the Lord Validate had said in the open in front of six hundred men about the animals in the passes.

*He is supposed to have said that he enjoyed it.*

*That he felt the shudder come up the haft into his wrists and liked it. That they butchered the carcasses at the third fire and he took the first cut himself because a commander who will not eat what he has killed is a commander his men stop believing. That it is sweeter than horse and does not take salt and the young one is better than the old.*

*Vhaeloth has him saying that five separate times in under an hour, each one a little more detailed than the last.*

She read the passage a fourth time and then set the book flat on her knee and looked out of the long window at eleven miles of ridge.

*My brother could not lie about food.*

*He could lie about anything else. He lied to a Bench at sixty-four and held it for nine years. He has lied to me perhaps four times and I caught two of them and let one go. But he could never do it about a meal, not once, not since he was a child, because he did not care enough about food to bother constructing anything, and so he told the truth about it by default and always had.*

*That paragraph has been built. Every clause in it is doing work. It is written the way a man writes when he intends a stranger to read it and reach a conclusion.*

*He was protecting something, and he spent his name to do it, and he knew exactly what a name costs.*

She did not write any of that down.

There was no instrument. There was no Bench she could take it to, no petition she could raise, no entry she could contest, and the only validated name in her family had gone into the grit on a coast road nine days ago with its mouth open.

*So it stands. It stands in every register in the world and I am the one person alive who can read it correctly and I have no standing to say so.*

*And if I ever did say so, out loud, in a hall, the first question would be what he was protecting, and the second would be from whom.*

She closed the book.

---

## VI. THE LEGION

"My Grace."

Dessa Oth-Karreth had been standing at the door for some time and had come in only when the book was shut.

"My Grace, the houses are asking. When do we march."

"We do not."

"Grace."

"They are coming to us." Ilthára Korvaeth put both hands flat on the arms of the chair. "Aloreth has taken four thousand men off a road, killed a commander in front of ten thousand witnesses and done it in a form his own Court is going to call a stain. He cannot stop now. A man who does a thing like that and then sits still has admitted it was a crime, so he will come up the coast road, and he will come inside the season, and he will come whether or not he has the men for it.

"And he will not be alone. There were two of them on that field and one of them stopped the air over four thousand people, and Vhaeloth has him refusing to kill my brother on principle in front of both armies, which means he is not a mercenary and he is not for hire and he has some idea of himself.

"So it is not a succession any more. Begin the preparations. And the legionaries."

The pen stopped.

"Grace."

"Say it."

"Those have not been tested." Dessa Oth-Karreth did not look up from the board, which was a Karreth habit and a wise one. "Not properly. We have run eleven and we have lost three of the eleven at the seating, and the housings crack at the collar when the charge goes, and nobody at Rovann can tell us whether that is the alloy or the pattern or the powder. We do not know what the powder does to any of it. The Bench has been asking for another season since the spring, and the Bench is right, and I will say that in front of you and in front of anybody who asks me."

"I am aware of what the Bench has been asking for, and for how long, and of whose name is at the bottom of each request."

"Grace, if one of those opens up inside a formation."

Ilthára Korvaeth stood up out of the chair.

She did it slowly, because she was fifty-one and had been sitting for three hours and had never once spent anything on appearing otherwise, and she went to the long window and put her hand on the frame where the glass had gone in the fourth wave and nobody had replaced it.

"Then it will open up inside a formation," she said, "and we will write down what it did and we will build the next one differently.

"Do you know what is on the other side of not doing it.

"I have one brother and I had one, and he is in a road, and the Sunroot Court has a man who has stopped pretending there is a form for anything. There is no arrangement left to reach. There has not been one since the eleventh day of the withheld season and I knew that at the time and I did it anyway and I have never once said otherwise in this room.

"It is the legionaries or it is the four of us on a beach in nine years explaining ourselves to a jurist.

"Begin. And send for whoever drew the housings, not the man who cast them. I want the hand that drew them standing in front of me by the eighth day."

*And I want to know who put that pattern in front of my brother in the spring, and where it came from, and why nobody in this house can tell me.*

Dessa Oth-Karreth wrote it down and went out.

Ilthára Korvaeth stood at the open window in the raw light with the ache behind her eyes and looked west along eleven miles of ridge she had taken eleven times and held none, and did not close the shutters, and did not sit down.

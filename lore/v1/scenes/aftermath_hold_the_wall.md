# Aftermath: Hold the Wall

## Geturo

The calico went round his chest twice and over his left shoulder, and the infirmarian tied it off at the collarbone with a knot that pinched, and the dead arm was a parcel against his ribs.

"Don't lift it," said the infirmarian. "Don't reach with it. Don't sleep on it. Don't be clever with it." He pushed his spectacles up his nose with one knuckle and left a smear of Geturo's blood on the lens. "You'll want to be clever with it. Don't."

Geturo sat on the sand and let himself be parcelled. The cold was still in him where the borrowed Essence had been, a hollow in the middle of his chest the size of a fist, and his own Essence moved round it slowly and did not fill it. Everything hurt in a way that had stopped being separate hurts and become weather. He was very aware of how much he wanted to lie back down.

What had been said to him was still in his ears. He turned it over the way he would turn a sample under a lens.

*Eleven months of homework.* That was fair. That was more than fair.

"One day you'll be past anything I can put in a bottle," Geturo said. His voice came out rough, and he had to stop and spit to the side and try again. "I should be glad I caught you while you're still learning."

He meant it. It was also not all of what he meant. The rest of it sat under the words where nobody could see it, and it was simpler: he was not going to stop either. Not for the arm, and not for the eleven months soaked into the sand, and not for anybody. He found, sitting there in the calico, that he was curious. Honestly curious, the way he got about a reagent that did something in the pan he had not expected. Where he would be in a few months. Where any of them would. It was going to be fun to find out.

The old summoning lecturer had come across the sand while the infirmarian worked, holding her gown up out of the grit, and she stopped a pace off and looked down at him. Close to, her eyes were very black and very bright, and the bottle on her chain had a crack in the wax that had been mended with a thread of red silk.

"Kwon Geturo," she said. She said the whole name the way a clerk reads it off a ledger. "Third bell, tomorrow. My room. Bring the notes."

"I don't have notes," Geturo said.

"Then bring the eleven months," said the old woman, "and we'll write some." She turned and went back across the sand without waiting for him to agree, and did not seem to think he might not.

His father had let go of the dome at last. The draw-inspector was standing under the white seam on his folding stool with a brass instrument held up to it, and Mu-jin was flexing his left hand open and shut, open and shut, the nails gone the colour of candle wax. He did not look at Geturo yet. That would come later, at the table, with the door closed. Geturo knew that the way he knew how gypsum set.

The Dawi orderly held out the stretcher poles.

"Lie," he said.

"No," said Geturo.

The Dawi considered this for a while. Then he nodded once, as if that were also a reasonable answer to a question, and stood the stretcher on its end.

Geturo put his left hand flat on the sand and pushed.

The thigh screamed. The shoulder told him things in the calico. His vision went grey at the edges and stayed grey for three long breaths while he got one boot under him, then the other, and came up. He had spent everything the match had asked of him and then some it had not asked for. He stood anyway. He swayed there, bleeding a little from the wrist, until the sand stopped tilting under him and was only sand.

## Lance

The tunnel was cold after the arena, and it smelled of wet stone and lamp oil and the rope-and-tar smell of the service ducts overhead, and Lance Greymane had got as far as the second lamp before his sister caught his sleeve.

She was a head shorter than him and built narrow like all of them, shoulder blades like folded wings under the grey coat. She had their mother's hair. It was straight and fine and so pale a brown it went silver under the lamps, cut blunt at the jaw. Her face was a small sharp triangle with a pointed chin. Freckles ran across the bridge of her nose, and her eyes were the Greymane grey, the colour of rain on slate. Her coat was issue grey, not tailored, gone thin at the elbows and let down twice at the hem. The silver cord at her collar was bright and new. There was chalk on her cuff and ink on the side of her right hand from the heel to the little finger, where it dragged across whatever she was writing. The card was still in her fingers, both sides full.

She did not let go of his sleeve.

"Do you still think you can beat one of them?" she said.

He looked down at her. Behind them, out on the sand, somebody was calling for more calico, and the draw-hum in the conduit overhead climbed slowly back toward its proper note as the Guild man worked on the seam.

"I don't know," Lance said.

It cost him something to say it. She saw that. She always did.

"The first two, maybe." He turned his right hand over and looked at the old straight scar across its knuckles, the way he did when he was thinking and did not know he was doing it. "If I'm ready for them. If I know what's coming. The archer spends himself like a man who's never had to count. The Mahuo boy fights like the ground owes him something." He closed the hand. "Those I can plan for."

He looked back once, down the length of the tunnel, to the bright square of the arena mouth at the end of it. A tall figure was getting up off the sand in a sling.

"I see why we didn't make the cut," he said. "I didn't, before. I thought it was the examiners." His mouth moved, not quite into anything. "There's nothing normal about any of them. Not one. Not even the one who's spent all week looking like he was."

His sister waited. She was good at that too.

"That doesn't mean I stop," said Lance Greymane.

He took her hand off his sleeve, not unkindly, and held it a moment before he let it go.

"I'll get strong enough to hold the wall."

He turned and walked on down the tunnel toward the next lamp, and after a moment she put the card inside her coat and followed him.

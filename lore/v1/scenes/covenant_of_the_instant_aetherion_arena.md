# Covenant of the Instant — Aetherion Arena

*Naori Yukari · the arena floor, inside the typhoon*

---

His essence holds the skin of him together, and the burns come through anyway.

They find the seams. The shield he keeps close to his body took the worst of the explosions. He felt them break against it one after another, a string of hard blunt knocks across his ribs and shoulders and the backs of his thighs like a man beating a rug, and the shield flexed under each one and gave a little and held. The blood held differently. The summon's blood got under it. It is on his face and his neck and the backs of both hands and it has soaked through the charcoal wool of his jacket in a long dark splash across the chest, and it is still working. He can feel it working. It does not sit on the skin the way an acid would, burning a crust and then stopping at the crust. It goes down. It eats the way lye eats, turning the fat in the skin to soap and the soap to slurry and the slurry out of the way, so that every layer it opens is a fresh layer for it to reach, and there is no scab to stop it because it dissolves the scab as fast as the body can lay one down. Along his left jaw it has gone through the outer skin entirely. The pain there is white and enormous and very simple. Across his chest under the soaked wool it is still deciding how deep to go.

It feels like being painted with something that has just come out of a forge. It feels like that and keeps feeling like that.

And around all of it, the wind.

The typhoon has him in its fist, and it turns around him in a hard, hot, roaring wall, so loud he can no longer hear the crowd or his own breath, only the shriek of air moving faster than air should move, and the bright metallic hiss of a thousand iron feathers riding it. They go past him in a blur at the edge of sight, glowing at their leading edges where plasma clings to the iron, every one of them a knife thrown flat and spinning. The ones on the outer edge of the storm move slowest. The ones near him move fastest, whipping round so tight that he can feel their passage as a pressure on the skin before he hears them. He understands the shape of it without needing to. A whirlpool spins fastest near its heart and slowest at its rim, and its heart is a stillness. He is not in the heart. He is a pace off it, in the place where the wind is worst, and the wind has been trying to drag him round with it for three full heartbeats.

The heat makes it worse. It is a dry, cooking heat. It is not all hers, and he knows it is not all hers, because it has the taste of the earlier bouts in it, the leftover heat of four other fights rising off the sand and caught up in her storm and driven at him. His throat has gone to leather. His eyes stream and the streams dry on his cheeks before they reach his jaw, salt crusting in the lashes. Every breath he takes is an oven door opened in his face.

Four feathers have found him. He knows each one exactly. One is through the meat of his left shoulder, just under the point of it, the iron grating on bone when he moves the arm. One has opened his right flank along the ribs, a long shallow cut with the feather still lodged at the end of it like a splinter of slate. One is in the outside of his left thigh. One has gone through the sleeve of his sword arm and stayed in the forearm, between the two bones, close enough to the tendons that his fingers have a new and stupid weakness in them. Blood runs from all four. It soaks the grey under-robe and the jacket over it, warm in the cloth and then cooling, dripping off his trouser hem onto the sand where the wind snatches it away in red threads.

He is still standing. That is the first fact. He takes it and builds on it.

---

His essence refracts.

He did not choose that word. Elder Tae chose it, in a soil lecture Naori spent the whole of glaring at a knot in a cedar beam, and it stuck in him the way the elder's words always stuck, against his will. *Light goes into water and bends,* the old man said, drawing it on a slate. *It comes out the other side as itself, going a new way. The water does not destroy it. It changes the road.*

The Shell around his body is doing that now. It began the moment the storm closed on him, the way it always does, because it is the same habit that made Awai: gates turned inward, tuned to the room. The room is the typhoon now. Every hot roaring gust that hits him carries Aether in it, loose and violent, her working's fuel thrown outward in the spin, and where the gusts break against his Shell some of that Aether goes in instead of glancing off. It goes in bent. It enters as the storm's and comes out on the inside as his own: Aether becoming Essence, the world's loose power becoming the shaped power that lives in a Crystal and answers to one person. The road changes and the power is kept.

It is slow, always. Turning Aether into Essence is like warming a cold stone in your hands; the change comes on fast at first and then crawls, every moment closer to done and never quite done, the last part always taking longest. In a storm this violent most of what hits him glances off before his Shell can bend it.

But the Inzensho sees the storm's gusts before they come.

He can read the wind the way he reads anything, forward, the threads of each gust lifting off the spin and running ahead of it. The typhoon has a beat. Every wall of wind that comes around has a crest and a trough, a hardest part and a slackest part, and the crests come at a rhythm. So he opens his Shell to the crests. He shuts it to the troughs. He takes the storm only at the top of each gust, where the Aether is thickest, the way a child on a swing pumps her legs only at the top of the arc and goes higher every pass on the same small effort. Each gust he takes is a gust taken at its peak. The slow warming of the stone in his hands becomes something faster, because he is only ever holding the stone when it is hottest.

It has a name in his head. **Kussetsu**, the Bending. He has never said it aloud.

He breathes in.

The air is an oven and he takes it anyway, down to the bottom of his lungs, slow, his ribs opening against the cut along his flank so the cut pulls and leaks. He breathes out the same way, slow. In again. The Essence he has bent out of the storm runs into him on the breath. He feels it go into his channels, the deep lines of the Crystal's reach that run out through the body the way roots run out from a tree, and it does not go easily. The channels are hot and torn from the explosions. The fresh Essence goes into them and jerks back, recoiling the way a hand recoils from a stove, and goes in again, and jerks back, and the third time it stays.

The feathers in him feel it.

He can hear them. Each piece of iron lodged in his body is a thing with a voice, and the Cymorath has been hearing them since they went in: four small thin notes under the roar, each a different pitch, set by the length and thickness of the iron and how deep it sits in him. The one in his shoulder rings lowest, because it is the biggest and the bone holds it. The one in his forearm sings highest. They are her iron, shaped by her working, and they are in him now, and what is in him is in the reach of his Crystal.

He finds each note and sings it back.

It is a ward-carver's trick, the first thing his father ever taught him to do with a sound instead of only hear it. Every solid thing has a note it would ring at if you struck it. Drive it at that note, gently, again and again, and each push adds to the last the way pushes add on a swing, until the thing is ringing harder than it was ever made to ring and something in its grain gives way. He has watched his father crack a flawed ward-stone in half that way without touching it, with only his palm laid flat on the rock beside it and his eyes closed. Naori is nothing like as good. But the iron in him is thin, and hot, and full of the plasma still clinging to it, and his voice in the Cymorath is right up against it.

He calls the trick **Kurogane-nari**, the Iron Sings, and the four notes climb.

He feels them climb through the meat of him, a buzzing that goes from an itch to an ache to a high whining vibration in the bone of his shoulder that sets his teeth on edge, and then, one after another, they break. The forearm first, with a feeling like a struck nerve, a white flash all the way down into his fingertips. The thigh. The flank. The shoulder last, with a crack he hears inside his own skull. The feathers come apart along their grain into slivers and then into grit, iron filings and flakes of black scale and the dying glow of the plasma that rode them, and what was a blade lodged in him is suddenly a crumble of dark sand in the wound.

The Crystal takes the rest. The plasma's heat, the working that shaped the feathers, the Essence she spent to throw them: it breaks out of the shattered iron the way warmth breaks out of a coal that has been knocked apart, and his channels are open and drinking, and it goes in. Raw material. Her storm, turned to his fuel. The iron grit runs out of the wounds with his blood and stains it nearly black.

It hurts more than the feathers did. He accepts that, the way he accepted the oak at eight.

---

He turns the katana in his hand and stands it upright.

The blade comes up in front of his face, edge toward her, point to the sky, the long pale line of the temper glowing in the storm-light, and his right arm shakes with it because of the broken feather in the forearm and the slack it has left in his grip. He tightens his fingers anyway. He holds it there. And the blood that has been running down his right arm from the forearm wound, warm, thick, darkened with iron, runs over the back of his hand and onto the guard, through the openwork crow, and onto the steel.

It runs down the blade toward the guard in a thin line. Then it stops running down.

It begins to run out.

His blood lifts off the steel in fine lines. They draw outward from the blade into the air, thin and dark and perfectly straight, as if the steel were the centre of a drawing and someone were ruling lines away from it with a straightedge. They go out through the storm and the storm cannot touch them. The wind tears past them and they do not bend. Three of them. They go out at three angles, low, hissing faintly where the heat dries their edges, and where each one ends, above the sand at a distance of ten paces from him, the air thickens. He feels the pressure set at the three points. It sets the way a nail sets in a beam, one blow and then held, three nails, three corners, and the air between them is pulled taut.

A triangle. It is the only shape that cannot be bent out of true without breaking one of its sides. A square leans when you push it. A triangle holds. His father taught him that on a roof, at nine years old, nailing braces into the ridge-pole of the drying shed: *Three is the first number that stands up by itself.*

He calls it **Chi-suji**, the blood lines. It is ward-craft, the thing he once swore he would only learn because the body asked it of him, and it turned out the body was right. His own blood for ink. His own blade for the straightedge. Three corners pinned in the air to hold whatever he is going to put inside them.

The burns on his chest are still going down. He can feel the lye-heat pressing into the muscle now under the soaked wool, a slow terrible push, deeper every heartbeat. The wound along his jaw has gone quiet in the way that is worse than pain.

He does not have time.

So he makes some.

"That was well made," he says.

The words come out of a throat cooked dry. The storm takes most of them, and it does not matter whether they reach her. He says it because it is true and because his father taught him to name good work when he sees it even when the work is on his own skin. Then he lowers his eyes to the blade, and a single drop of his blood slides off the lower edge of the crow on the guard and falls toward the sand.

He watches it fall. The Inzensho shows him where it lands before it lands. It shows him the instant of the landing, one single instant, the drop meeting the grains of sand and flattening and breaking, the clearest single moment in a world made of blur and wind.

"Covenant of the Instant," he says.

It is a Root Invocation. It is not his. It belongs to Valen, the Archon of Time, the Clock of Ages, the one whose edict cut in the old stones reads *Every moment is a covenant*, and it has been a Root Invocation since before there was a Ketsuen to read the stones. Two glyphs make it. **[VaM]**, Moment, the glyph that cuts a single instant loose from the drift of cause into cause. And **[Cro]**, Cycle, his since boyhood. They are written into the Wellspring Anamnesis, which keeps what was and when it was, and its sister Lethegraal holds the cup beneath. The invocation asks for one thing before it will open: a moment that is truly single. Not a span. Not a while. A decision, a word, an impact: a drop of blood meeting the sand.

He has given it one.

The two glyphs unwind out of him. He feels them leave the Crystal and go out along the blood lines to the three corners of the triangle, **[VaM]** and **[Cro]** spiralling away from each other along the lines like thread paying off two spools at once, and they go through his Essence rather than around it. Through the gates he keeps turned inward. They run under his Shell, down where the tone of a working stays in the body instead of crying out into the air, and so the chain sends out almost nothing for anyone to hear. From outside it has no voice. From outside, the Covenant has a stranger face than that.

From outside, nothing happens at all.

That is the law of it, and he knows it the way he knows the Telling. Everything beyond the pocket of the held instant sees no time pass. The boy in the storm does not move. He stands with his blade upright and his blood ruled out into the air in three straight lines, and the drop falls from his guard and strikes the sand, and in that same instant, all at once, without any space between, whatever he does inside the moment is simply done.

Inside, he has time.

Inside, the drop hangs.

---

The storm stops.

Nothing in it dies, and everything in it stands. Every iron feather in the typhoon hangs where it was in the air, glowing, frozen in its spin, a thousand of them in a great slow-seeming wheel around him that is not slow at all, only held. The roar is gone. In its place is a sound like the inside of a seashell, a soft vast hush with something ringing far down in it. The heat is still on his face. The pain is still in his jaw and chest and shoulder. And the drop of his blood hangs a finger's width above the sand under his guard, round and dark and perfect, catching the storm-light on its curve.

He has perhaps three breaths of the world's time. Inside them, if the old texts are right, he has nine or ten times that, his own heart running at the pocket's pace while the world's heart has stopped. Thirty heartbeats, perhaps. Perhaps less. The texts say the Covenant never holds past three of the world's seconds, however high a man climbs. And they say what it costs. His own body runs at pocket time. His own lungs and heart and blood keep their own pace inside the held moment, so that three of the world's seconds will cost him the better part of a minute of his own: a minute's labour, a minute's breath, a minute's ache, all spent in what the world will count as no time at all.

And a minute of the lye in his skin.

That is the first thing. The burns run at pocket time too. The thing eating down into his chest will eat at his pace here, not the world's, and he has bought himself a minute of being burned.

He begins with it.

"Paru," he says, into the hush. "Fifty-eight."

There is no pause for the chain to assemble. It does not need assembling. A ward-carver who has built the same chain a thousand times stops building it; it goes down into him and stays there, whole, the way a word you have known since childhood no longer needs to be spelled. The Lexicon calls that an implicit chain. The Yukari carvers call the place where such chains are kept the Chain Space, and they keep their chains by number, the way his father keeps his fourteen layers of ward by number, because a number is faster to reach for than a name in a fight. Naori has fifty-eight. The fifty-eighth is three glyphs long, and he built it in the second winter after he took up the blade, alone, at the stone above the gate, because he had seen in the threads a day very like this one.

He has a name for it as well as the number. *Shizume no Ma.* The Room That Stills.

The first glyph goes into his wounds.

**[Stg]**, Stagnate. It is a compound glyph and an ugly one, under two masters. Auren of the Still Gate is the first, and Uurgath, whom the old texts name with distaste, is the second. Its meaning in the Codex is plain and unkind: it hides contamination as serene illusion. It does not heal. It has never healed anything in the long history of its use. What it does is stop a process where it stands and lay stillness over it, a calm surface on a poisoned well.

He lays it on the burns.

He feels it go over his chest under the wet wool like cold water poured on a hot stone, and the lye-heat stops. It stops dead. The push down into the muscle halts where it is, the reaction frozen mid-step, the slurry of ruined skin locked in place on the flesh like wax going hard. The pain does not leave. It stops changing, and that is a mercy so large he nearly sways. Along his jaw the glyph closes over the place where the skin is gone, a smooth grey stillness, and the raw meat under it stops weeping. In the shoulder and the flank and the thigh and the forearm, around the iron grit his blood has been washing out, it closes the torn edges. It does not knit them. It seals them, holds them shut, stills the bleeding by stilling the blood at the edges of the wound so that it stops being a thing that flows.

Underneath, nothing has been mended. He knows that. The contamination is still in him, hidden under its serene face, the summon's blood held in his chest and waiting. The day this glyph lets go, it will start again exactly where it stopped. *Stagnate* is a debt, signed with his eyes open.

He is not attuned to the Wellspring that usually carries **[Stg]**. The Codex lists its homes: Tenebra, Rebirthine, Somnalis, and among them Luminalis, the Wellspring of light. And the Essence in his channels right now, the Essence he bent out of her storm and broke out of her feathers a moment ago, is not yet entirely his. It still remembers where it came from. Essence made from another's working keeps that working's dialect for a little while, the way a bell keeps ringing after the hand comes off it, and her working sang in fire and in light. Exuroth's forge-roar. That high glassy note he could not name, which he now thinks must be Luminalis, the Wellspring of light, singing in steps like dropped coins. So for a few breaths of pocket time, Naori is speaking with a borrowed voice. He carries **[Stg]** on the light she poured into her storm.

He has a name for that too. **Karine**, the borrowed note.

The second glyph goes into the storm.

**[Rk]**, Rupture. Uurgath's again, and it belongs to Fractura, the Wellspring of breaking, and to the law of how things bear load and fail. The Codex says it tears repeating patterns by breaking the nodes they repeat around. A typhoon is a repeating pattern. He can see it now that it is held still: the feathers are not scattered at random through the wind. They ride it in ranks, in rings, each ring a set distance from the next, each feather a set distance from its neighbours, a great wheeling lattice laid out around the stillness at the storm's heart like the spokes and rim of a cartwheel repeated over and over. Every lattice has places where its lines cross and hold each other up. Nodes. Knots in the net. Break the knots and the net is only string.

He reads them with the Inzensho, forward, the places where the storm's pattern would hold itself together a moment from now. He finds four of them close around him. He sends **[Rk]** into them along the light still singing in his borrowed Essence, and the knots break.

He hears them break in the hush, four sharp clean notes like ice cracking on a pond.

And now the triangle is not enough.

The three blood lines hold the Covenant's corners, the held instant pinned in the air on its three nails. But a held instant is not a trap. A trap needs walls, and walls need a shape that will close. He sends a fourth line of blood out off the blade, down and out to the place where the fourth broken knot of the storm sits, and pins it there. Four corners. The triangle becomes a square.

A square leans. It always leans when it is pushed. The storm will push it the moment the instant ends. A carpenter braces a square frame by nailing a diagonal across it. Naori braces his with stillness. The **[Stg]** he laid in his wounds goes out along the four lines as well and fills the square, a calm that hides what is inside it and holds the frame from racking out of true, and inside the square the air goes dead. The Aether in it stops circulating. It cannot leave. It is trapped there in a box of stillness twenty paces on a side, with Naori at its heart, and he can feel it hanging in the square around him as thick and motionless as water in a cistern.

The third glyph fills the box.

**[OniV]**, Oneiric Veil. It is a root of Kaetra's, the Dream-Weaver, the Veil-Singer, whose edict reads *Truth wears the mask that love or fear demands*. It is written into Oneirion, the Wellspring of dream, and its law in the Codex is the law of signals and noise: how much can be known from what reaches the eye, and how easily that knowing is drowned. It saturates a space with dream-emotional confusion. It takes every true signal in the space and gives it company. And the Codex lists Exuroth among its homes, fire, the forge-roar he heard in her chain from the moment he walked out of the gate. Her fire is in his channels too. He carries the Veil on it.

It goes into the still air of the square like ink into water.

And the square fills with him.

They stand up out of the hush in the four corners first, on the four blood-pinned nails, four figures in charcoal wool with black hair tied at the nape and crimson eyes and katanas held upright before their faces. Then more. Between the corners, along the walls, in the open air of the box: Naori, and Naori, and Naori, a dozen, two dozen, each one standing exactly as he stands, each one bleeding where he bleeds, each one carrying the long dark splash across the chest and the grey stillness along the jaw. Some of them are a fraction of a second behind him: what he was a breath ago. Some are a fraction ahead: what he will be a breath from now. They are made of nothing but the confusion the Veil pours into the space, the dream-stuff of the eye mistaking one thing for many, and he can see the threads of every one of them. They have no threads of their own. They borrow his.

The cost reaches him then, all at once, the way he knew it would.

His heart hammers. His lungs are dragging at air that is still an oven. He has been running at pocket time for what his own body counts as half a minute and more, a half minute of breathing and bleeding and burning and building three glyphs and four blood lines and one borrowed voice, and it is all landing on him now as fatigue: a heaviness in the arms, a grey crowding at the edges of his sight, the slack in his sword hand getting slacker. The Threadburn is back behind his left eye, the hot needle along the bone, worse than at the start of the fight. Something warm is running from his nose again.

The texts warn about this too. Anyone who knows the Covenant knows its counter: **[Uur]**, Disruption, breaking the pocket open from outside and snapping the caster back into the world's time mid-motion, wherever he is, whatever he is doing. A man caught that way halfway through a step can tear his own knee apart. He has finished every motion. He is standing still.

The drop of blood touches the sand.

---

The world comes back with a roar.

The storm comes back. All at once the wind is screaming again, the iron feathers are wheeling, the heat slams into his face and the noise slams into his ears, a thousand feathers spinning, the whole vast wheel of the typhoon moving the way it was moving three breaths ago.

But not here.

Here there is a square cut out of the hurricane, twenty paces on a side, and inside it nothing moves. The typhoon tears past its walls the way a river tears past the stone pier of a bridge, piling up and boiling and splitting and sliding by. At the four corners where the knots were broken, the lattice of the storm has come apart, and the feathers that rode those knots tumble loose and fall out of the spin and drop onto the sand outside the square, clattering, glowing, dying. Inside the walls, the air is dead still. The heat in it hangs, trapped, shimmering in the stillness. The Aether in it hangs. Four lines of dark blood run from the upright blade in his hand out to four points above the sand, and between those points the light of the arena sits on the air like light on the surface of a pond, flat and calm and serene.

And standing in the stillness, in every corner and along every wall and scattered through the open air of the box, two dozen bleeding figures with crimson eyes hold two dozen blades upright before their faces, and all of them, at the same instant, turn their heads.

### Abilities invented in this post

**KUSSETSU · "The Bending"** [Passive, grown from Awai]. His Shell, gates turned inward and tuned to the room, bends loose Aether that breaks against it into his own Essence, the way water bends light without destroying it. On its own the change is slow: it comes on fast and then crawls, the last part always taking longest. The Inzensho speeds it by reading the gusts ahead. He opens only to the crest of each gust, where the Aether is thickest, and shuts to the trough, like pumping a swing only at the top of its arc. *Cost:* nothing to start; holding it while hit keeps the channels hot and torn, and fresh Essence recoils twice before it seats. *Limit:* it needs a working throwing Aether at him (a storm, a field, a blast); against a cold blade it gives nothing. It takes perhaps a third of what hits him, and only while he stands his ground. *Counter:* before, attack without spilling Aether (steel, stone, a pure strike). During, throw your gusts out of rhythm so there is no crest to read. After, the Essence he took is still the caster's in dialect for a few breaths, which is a door both ways.

**KUROGANE-NARI · "The Iron Sings"** [Technique, Cymorath]. He finds the note a solid object lodged in his body (or touching it) would ring at, and drives it at that note, each push adding to the last, until it rings harder than it was made to and breaks along its grain. That is resonant fracture, the singer and the wine glass. What the object held (heat, a working's Essence, plasma) breaks out of it and his Crystal takes it in. *Cost:* about a twentieth of his reserve for each object, and the breaking happens inside his own wounds, so it hurts more than the object did. *Limit:* thin or flawed things only: feathers, needles, splinters, flawed stone. Thick steel or a worked blade has too deep a note for him. Contact or embedding only. *Counter:* before, make shrapnel that does not ring (soft metal, glass, organic matter). During, bind the shrapnel with your own working so its note is yours to hold steady. After, there is no undoing a broken feather, but the grit stays in the wound and will need cleaning out.

**CHI-SUJI · "The Blood Lines"** [Inscription, Yukari ward-craft]. Blood off the blade, ruled out in perfectly straight lines that the wind cannot bend, each line ending in a pinned point of pressure in the air. Three points make a triangle, the one figure that cannot be pushed out of true without breaking a side, and it holds a working's boundary rigid. A fourth point makes a square, which leans unless it is braced. *Cost:* the blood itself (he must already be bleeding, or cut himself), and about a tenth of his reserve to throw and pin four lines. *Limit:* about ten paces per line; the points stay where they are pinned and do not follow him. *Counter:* cut a line before its point sets; or push a square before it is braced, and it racks into a rhombus and the working inside spills.

**COVENANT OF THE INSTANT** [Root Invocation, canon, Valen]. From the Master Codex as written: **[VaM]** Moment and **[Cro]** Cycle, Anamnesis primary, Lethegraal secondary, Limina family, Abstract/Entropic Systems, Temperance minimum Refraction. It needs a genuinely single moment as its trigger (a decision point, an impact, a single spoken word), never a span. It isolates that instant from causal drift: the caster acts inside a held moment at roughly eight to twelve times normal, capped at three real seconds however high he climbs. Everything outside sees no time pass, as if the caster never moved. *Cost:* his own metabolism and Shell run at pocket time, so three seconds outside costs him 24 to 36 seconds of internal fatigue, and anything already acting on his body (here, the lye burns) acts at pocket time too. *Counter:* **[Uur]** Disruption breaks the pocket's isolation from outside and snaps the caster back into normal time, often in mid-motion, which is dangerous. Naori's trigger is the drop of blood meeting the sand, an impact the Inzensho shows him before it happens.

**CHAIN SPACE · PARU NO. 58, "SHIZUME NO MA" (The Room That Stills)** [Implicit chain, his own]. A three-glyph chain stored whole in the Chain Space, the Yukari carvers' name for the place an internalised chain lives once it no longer needs assembling (the Lexicon's "implicit" glyph). It is called by number, carvers' habit, because a number is faster to reach for than a name. **[Stg] → [Rk] → [OniV].** He built it alone in the second winter after he took up the blade.

### The physics, real and named

- **Lye versus acid burns.** Acid burns cause coagulative necrosis: they burn a hard crust (an eschar) that slows them. Alkali burns cause liquefactive necrosis: they turn fat to soap and keep going down. That is the post's "like the base of an acid", and it is why the burns are still deepening and why Naori needs **[Stg]** first.
- **The vortex.** A typhoon spins fastest a little way out from its still eye and slower toward its rim (a Rankine vortex). He is caught in the worst band, just off the eye.
- **Dry heat.** Hot, dry, moving air pulls moisture from the eyes and throat faster than the body replaces it.
- **Relaxation, and pumping a swing.** Changing one state into another comes on fast and then crawls. Taking energy in only at the crest of each cycle is resonant pumping.
- **Resonant fracture.** A thin object driven at its natural frequency rings harder each push until it fails along its grain.
- **Triangles and squares.** A triangle is the one rigid figure, which is why roof trusses are made of them. A square racks into a rhombus unless something braces it across the diagonal.
- **Signal and noise** (the law behind **[OniV]**). Adding false copies of a signal drowns the true one; the more copies, the less any glance can be trusted.

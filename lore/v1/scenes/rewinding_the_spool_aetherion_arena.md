# Rewinding the Spool — Aetherion Arena

*Naori Yukari · the arena floor, the second gate*

---

The gate is still groaning open when Naori Yukari walks out of the dark.

The tunnel behind him smells of lamp oil and wet stone and the iron of the winch chains, and then the light takes him full in the face, white off the sand and white off the tiers, so bright after the tunnel that for three strides the arena is only heat and noise. The crowd is a wall of it. It comes down off the stands in a long rolling surf with no words in it, the stamp of a thousand feet travelling down through the timber frames and into the ground, where the Cymorath catches it through the soles of his boots the way it has caught every floor he has ever stood on. He lets it pass through him. Under the roar there are smaller things. The gate chain still paying out over its drum behind him. A loose peg ticking. A woman somewhere high up laughing at a joke he will never hear.

The name the girl across the sand has just given the arena is still coming back off the far wall.

He stops ten paces out of the gate and lets his eyes settle. The sand is raked smooth, pale gold where the sun has dried it and darker in a long smear near the centre line where the last bout bled and the rakes have only turned it over. Heat stands off the floor in a low shimmer, and his shadow lies short and black at his feet. He is tall for thirteen, Yukari tall, the growth coming into his shoulders and forearms first. The black hair he has never cut much is tied back at the nape and falls between his shoulder blades, and in the Aether-heavy air of an arena that has held four bouts since morning the thread-shimmer is on it, that colour that is not quite iridescent and not quite black. He wears Ketsuen dark. A close jacket of charcoal wool over a grey under-robe, wide trousers gathered at the shin, the cloth plain and well kept and worn pale at the knees. The katana rides at his left hip in a black lacquered scabbard gone matte along the spine where his forearm has rubbed it through a thousand drills. He keeps his hands loose at his sides.

He does not fold them behind his back. He made himself stop that, in the end.

His eyes are fully crimson, and in the red field the thread-patterns are turning, slow and constant, always forward.

He hears the girl's working before he sees it clearly.

Every working hums. His father taught him that before he taught him the sword: every glyph chain sings at a pitch set by its phonemes and the Wellspring it calls, and a Yukari ear that has been trained on stone can learn to hear the song under any sound in the world. What stands behind her sings loud. There is the summon first, the great winged thing hanging in the air at her back, and it broadcasts the way every called construct broadcasts, its authorizing glyph and its summoner's tone braided together into one bright drone that he could pick out of a battlefield. Under it, lower and hotter, is the chain that feeds it. He knows the root before he names it. **[Ra]**, Flame. The heat-dialect of Exuroth: a rough, rising note like air pulled through a forge throat, the sound of thermodynamics being told what to do, every link of the chain heating the Essence that passes through it and handing it on hotter. Riding over that is a second tone, thin and very high, glassy, the singing of light being pushed off something at a rate that makes his back teeth ache. Fire and light braided together. He has never heard that braid before. It is beautiful, in the way a well-carved ward is beautiful, and he notes the beauty and sets it aside.

The light tone puzzles him. He guesses at it the way he guesses at anything that did not come out of the valley's stone: the fire is driving the light, heat pushing brightness off whatever it touches the way a smith's hammer drives sparks off struck iron. He may be wrong. He suspects he is wrong in some way that matters, because the high tone does not trail behind the heat the way sparks trail a hammer. It comes off in steps, in small counted pieces, like coins dropped one at a time onto a stone floor rather than water poured from a jug, and the pieces come faster when the light is bluer and not when the heat is greater. He has no word for that. He files the wrongness away where he keeps the things he does not yet understand, and it is a crowded place.

The field is spreading.

He can see it now that his eyes have stopped watering. A skin of light is unrolling across the sand from the place where she stands, a pale gold sheet laid an inch above the grains, crackling at its leading edge like fat on a pan. The heat comes off it in a dry wave against his face and hands. Where its edge passes, the sand under it brightens and the shimmer above it bends, the light folding around itself as if the air had thickened into glass. It is reaching outward in every direction. It is reaching toward him.

The Inzensho runs ahead of it without asking him.

It always does. The threads lift off the spreading light the way his father's carvers' shavings lift off a block, and they run out ahead of the field across the next second, and the next, and he sees where they are going. He sees a dozen endings at once, the way he always sees them, as possibilities with bodies. The field closing over the whole floor from wall to wall. The field locking into the sand and hardening into a floor of light that answers to her and not to him. Edges of brightness standing up out of it. His own shape at the end of some of those threads, on the ground, the sand burning under his cheek.

*See the end,* his father said, in a frozen yard, with his hand on Naori's shoulder. *Let it show you. Then come back.*

He comes back.

He comes back to his feet on the sand and the heat on his face and the hum of the chain in his ears, and from there, standing in the present, he listens down the length of the thread for its middle.

It is there. Every working has one. A glyph chain is a sequence, link after link after link, and Essence moves through it the way a pulse moves down a rope, each link turning it a little further toward what the caster wants. The field is the far end of that rope. The girl is the near end. And somewhere between them is the place where the chain stops being a thing that is happening and starts being a thing that has happened, the instant the threads tighten and commit and no longer can be talked out of anything. His father reads that instant sideways, across the room, as a lock closing. Naori reads it forward, as a place on the road ahead where all the roads run together. It is close. Less than half a breath away. And this side of it, a hair this side, is the middle.

He opens his mouth and speaks two sounds very quietly, under the roar, so that nobody in the stands could possibly hear them.

"Cro." A breath. "Vor."

They are not words. They are glyphs, written the oldest way, by saying them. **[Cro]** first, Cycle, a root of Valen's, the Archon the old texts call the Clock of Ages. It is the glyph that encodes repetition with variation, the law of things that go out and come back. It sits in the Codex under Lethegraal, but it is attested to Cymorath, and through Cymorath he can carry it, because Cymorath is vibration and a cycle is only a vibration that has been given a name. **[Vor]** second, Return, a root of Auren's, the Still Gate. In the burial rites it is the glyph that recycles Essence and keeps the dead from wandering. It is attested to Cymorath too. Spoken together, in that order, at the right pitch, they make a chain of two links that does one thing.

It turns a wave around.

He learned what it does before he understood why. He was twelve, down by the terrace where the carvers' boys raced each other on summer evenings, and he watched the old carver Ikuro clap his hands halfway through one of their races for no reason but his own amusement. The rule was that a clap meant turn around. So the boys turned. The fast ones had got far ahead and the slow ones were barely past the second step, and every one of them turned at the clap and ran back the way he had come, at the same speed he had been running out. The fast ones had the longest way to come home. The slow ones had the shortest. And they all arrived back at the top step at the same moment, tangled together and laughing, as if the race had been run backwards.

Naori had stood on the wall and watched the threads of that race fold up on themselves, and something in his Crystal had gone very quiet and very interested.

A glyph chain is a race. The Essence leaves the caster in one tight bundle and spreads as it travels, each part running at its own pace through each link, the parts drifting out of step with each other the way ripples widen on water. That spreading is how the working opens into the world. The field across the sand is the race at its loosest, every part of it run far out from the start. And if, at the exact middle, someone claps, every part turns. The drift that was carrying them apart carries them home. The chain gives back what it was given, in reverse, link by link, until the whole of it arrives at the place where it started, all at once, together.

His father's word for that place in a cut was *the middle*. The carvers' word for it was halfway. Naori, alone, the winter after the race, gave it a name of his own.

**Makimodoshi.** Rewinding the spool.

The two glyphs leave his mouth as a sound so low it is almost below hearing, and the Cymorath takes them and throws them across the sand at the exact pitch of her chain's middle, inverted. He feels it go out of him. It goes from the Crystal behind his breastbone, up through the throat, out along the vibration of the air, and the cost of it lands at once and lands where it always lands: behind his eyes.

The light stops.

Its leading edge halts on the sand six paces from his boots, crackling, and holds there for the space of a heartbeat, quivering, a sheet of brightness trembling against something it cannot see. Then it begins to run backward.

The crackle goes first. He hears it unsnap: the sound of fat spitting on a pan played the other way, a series of little implosions, pops folding in on themselves, swallowed. The heat on his face pulls away in a long dry breath. The edge of the field slides back across the sand toward its source, faster than it came, the brightened grains going dull again in a wake behind it. The bent air above it straightens. The glassy high tone of the light climbs, and wavers, and folds back down into itself, and the forge-roar of the **[Ra]** chain stutters on its rising note and drops a third, then a fifth, sliding down the scale the way a struck bell's note bends when a hand is laid on it.

He keeps the chain where he put it. That is the work. The two glyphs have to hold their pitch for exactly as long as her chain ran before the clap, no longer and no shorter, or the race does not fold home. He holds it. The time between the start of her working and the middle of it runs back through him like a rope drawn through his fingers, and he counts it with the part of himself that has counted the threads of things since he could walk.

It is harder than it sounds. A chain does not run backward willingly. Every link of it was made to pass Essence one way, the way the channels in a valley ward are cut to carry the draw downhill, and turning the flow back up it is like turning a mill race back up its flume. The **[Cro]** does the turning, but the turn has to be fed for as long as it lasts, and what feeds it is him. His reserve goes out through his throat in a thin pour, grain from a split sack. He can feel the exact moment it will be enough, because the Inzensho has already shown him the thread of his own working arriving at its end. That is the one mercy in the blood: he never has to guess how long to hold, only bear the holding.

And at the links where the Return bit hardest, where the spool turned back under the heaviest load, the chain comes apart.

He hears it break. It is a small sound against everything else, three or four bright clean notes in quick succession, the sound a carving makes when it cracks along a glyph that was cut against the grain. The flow of the working, its order, its direction, the willed sequence that held one link to the next, has been run backward over itself at the instant it was about to commit, and the commitment does not happen. What the chain was made to do and what it is now doing are no longer the same thing, and a chain cannot bear that. The links undo. He hears the disorder spread down the length of it from the middle outward in both directions, every tone of it going out of true together, dissonant, and he has heard enough failing wards in the enclave to know the sound of a working whose own logic has been turned against it.

The summon's drone does not break. He did not strike the summon. It hangs in the air at her back still singing its bright braided note, broadcasting glyph and summoner as it has from the moment it came, and he leaves it alone. It is the chain that was feeding the field that comes apart, and the field that was spreading toward him that goes home.

And the Essence goes home with it. **[Vor]** does not destroy. It returns. Whatever was poured into those links runs back up them, all of it, arriving together at the place it left, and Naori knows exactly how it feels to have a working come home that fast. He has felt it in practice, from the other side, the first time his father turned one of his own small wards back on him. It feels like catching a thrown bucket of water in both arms.

He does not watch to see how it is caught. That is not his to know yet.

His eyes are spinning.

He can feel them do it. The crimson field behind them has gone from its slow constant turning to something faster and wrong, the thread-patterns twisting against each other in a slow grinding spiral like two millstones that have been set a hair out of true, and the pain comes in after it: a long hot needle laid in behind the left eye, along the bone, out toward the temple. It is the Threadburn, the price the blood takes for running the Inzensho and the Cymorath together at full depth in the same instant. Anyone standing on her side of the sand can see what it looks like from outside. They would see two crimson eyes in a young face, the patterns in them turning and twisting over themselves, reading something nobody else in the arena can see. He lets them look.

Something warm runs from his left nostril and stops at his lip. He tastes copper. He does not wipe it.

The crowd has gone strange. He can hear that too. The roar has broken up into pieces, a thousand separate voices asking a thousand separate questions, a murmur rolling down the tiers the way wind rolls down a hillside of grass. Somewhere high up a man is shouting the same word over and over. Nobody in the stands heard two glyphs spoken. They saw a field of light stop on the sand and go backward, and they have no name for it.

Naori lets his breath out, slowly, and the white needle behind his eye dulls by half.

His right hand comes up and settles on the hilt of his katana.

The grip is wrapped in black cord over white ray-skin, the cord worn glossy at the place his forefinger rests and dark with years of his hand. The guard under his thumb is plain iron, round, filed smooth at the edge, with a single crow cut through it in openwork, wings half spread. His father's mark. His father did not give it to him. He had it cut himself, the spring he chose the blade, and when his father saw it he said nothing for a long while and then put his hand on Naori's head, and that was the whole conversation. Under his left hand the scabbard is warm from the sun. He sets his thumb against the guard and feels the blade seated in the throat of the scabbard, the steel resting on its collar, the whole weight of it waiting.

He looks across the sand at her for the first time as a person, past the summon and past the field that is no longer there.

"No hard feelings," he says.

His voice is quiet. It does not need to be loud. It is the same register his father uses in council, and it carries the way his father's carries, because the arena has gone quiet enough to let it. There is no apology in it, and no mockery either. It is information, delivered plainly, the way a carver tells an apprentice the stone was cracked before he touched it.

Then he moves, and the arena moves with him.

It begins in his chest. The Crystal under his breastbone has been running hot since the glyphs left him, the way a forge runs hot after the bellows have worked it, and he does what his father taught him to do with heat in a Crystal: he gives it somewhere to go. Most practitioners open outward, broadcasting their tone into the room like a shout. He opens inward. He turns the gates of his Shell around so that they receive instead of send, and he tunes them, the whole of himself, to the pitch of the air around him.

The arena air is full. Four bouts since the morning bell have left it ringing. Every working that was thrown on this sand today has left its leftover behind, residue still humming in the ground the way a bell hums after the clapper has stopped, and an arena is the worst place in the world to listen for exactly that reason: the floor is so loud with old workings that a fresh one drowns in it. The girl's broken chain has just added its scatter, and Naori has been standing in all of it since he walked out of the gate. Now he stops standing in it, and starts drinking it.

He tunes himself to the room the way a string is tuned to another string, until when one sounds, the other answers. A string that matches the note in the air takes that note out of the air and into itself. That is all resonance is. The stronger the match, the more of the sound comes into the string and the less is left in the room. His Shell matches the arena's ring, and the arena's ring comes into him.

He feels it arrive. Loose Aether, warm and dirty, carrying the shape of every working that left it there. It comes in through the skin of his hands and the soles of his feet and the hollow at the base of his throat, the leftover heat of someone's fire working from the second bout, the cold brittle remainder of a ward someone broke in the third, the scatter of the girl's field, all of it pulled down the slope of the match and into his space. Into his Crystal. Into the part of him that is only himself.

It changes what the air looks like around him. The heat shimmer that was standing off the sand leans toward him from all sides, bending in, a ring of wavering air drawn toward his body like water toward a drain. The fine grains of sand on the surface of the floor stir and creep, a hair's breadth at a time, inward across the pale gold in thin spiral lines that all end at his boots. Along the wall the caged lamp flames lean toward him, every one. A dropped ribbon somewhere on the sand, some favour thrown from the stands in an earlier bout, lifts at one end and turns over and points at him like a needle finding north.

And as the room's noise pours into him, the room goes quiet.

That is the half of it nobody outside him can see. The ring in the ground drops away. The ambient hum of four bouts' worth of leftover workings thins and thins and falls toward nothing, because it is all in him now, and in the space around him the floor of sound that hides everything falls away like a tide going out from a beach. He can hear clearly for the first time since he walked out of the gate. He can hear the summon's braided note in every overtone. He can hear the dissonance still running along the broken links of her chain. He can hear, very faintly, the girl's own Crystal, the chord of it, the way a Harmonist hears it: a sound that is entirely her own, and that he does not reach for, and does not read.

He could. That is the thing he keeps behind his teeth. In this quiet, with the floor drained, the Inzensho could run out along her chord the way it ran along her chain and show him where she is going. His father has a name for that, a worked read of a person, and a rule to go with it: you do not do it to anyone who has not given you leave. Not in a tournament. Not anywhere. Naori leaves her chord where it is. He listens to the working. The person belongs to herself.

What the stands feel is the other half. His own tone, unbroadcast, contained, full to the edge with the room he has drunk, becomes a pressure. It makes no sound and has weight all the same. It is his identity made into weather: everything he is pulled tight around him and given weight. It sits in the air around him the way the air sits before a storm comes down the valley, close and heavy and charged. In the front tier a dog someone has brought on a leash flattens its ears and pushes its belly against the boards. A boy leaning over the rail leans back without knowing he has done it.

It is some far cousin of his father's Murder of Crows, smaller and stranger, and it is his own. It is a boy who learned to stand in the middle of a frozen yard, standing in the middle of a room until the room becomes him. He has called it, privately, in the only name he ever gives anything, **Awai**: the in-between. The place where one thing stops and the next has not yet begun.

*The end belongs to the threads.*

He steps forward, into the middle.

The pressure steps with him. The sand creeps after his heel. The leaning flames turn on their wicks to follow.

His left hand closes on the scabbard and his left thumb pushes the guard forward out of the scabbard's throat, a finger's width, a soft click of iron leaving lacquer, and the blade is unseated. The first inch of steel shows white in the sun, the edge-line catching it, a pale wavering band running down the hardened edge like frost on the inside of a window.

Then the draw.

It is the first thing his father taught him that was not about the eyes. A curved blade does not leave the scabbard by being pulled from it. Both hands work. The right hand draws the hilt forward and the left hand draws the scabbard back, each moving half the length of the blade, so that the steel clears in half the time it would take either hand alone. The curve of the blade does the rest. Its own bend carries it out of the scabbard's throat on an arc, so that the cut has already begun before the steel is in the air. His hips turn under it. His back foot drives. The weight comes forward off his left heel and into the ball of his right foot and up through his trunk into his shoulder, and the shoulder carries the arm, and the arm carries the steel, and the steel leaves the scabbard already moving, already curving, a long low bright arc travelling forward out of the dark lacquer and across the space between them at the height of her midsection.

The power of it comes up out of the floor. It always does. The ground pushes back against his driving foot exactly as hard as the foot pushes it. That push climbs through ankle and knee and hip, each joint turning a little later than the one beneath it and a little faster, the way the crack of a whip travels from the handle to the tip. The speed began as a heel shoving sand. At the last hand's breadth of the blade it is many times what his arm alone could make. His father made him learn it with a rope tied to a stick, a whole winter, before he ever let him swing the oak at anything. *The arm is the last thing that moves,* Niran said. *And the least.*

It is a true cut. Every part of him says so.

His hips are committed. His breath has gone out of him in the short hard push that goes with a real strike. His shoulder has opened behind the arm. Anyone watching his body, anyone trained to read a body, sees a man who has decided to cut through the middle of whoever is in front of him. And anyone who reads the threads the way his father reads them, sideways, looking for the instant a strike locks and can no longer be called back, sees the threads around that blade drawing tight, tighter, tightening toward the lock.

But Naori reads forward.

He sees the lock coming the way he sees everything coming, a place on the road ahead where all the threads run together. He has drilled this ten thousand times, in a frozen yard and in hot ones, with the oak and then with the steel: to run the cut right up to the edge of that place, a hair's breadth short, and hold it there. Nine parts of the commitment made and the tenth held back. The threads of the cut drawn so tight they sing, and the last one not yet pulled.

It is a feint. It is a feint built to look exactly like the thing it is not.

He has a name for that too. His father, who has no patience for names, would only call it a feint and leave it there. Naori calls it **Kara-ito**: the empty thread.

The bright arc of the steel crosses the sand toward the middle of her body, low and curving and very fast, the edge turned to cut and the pressure of Awai riding in behind it like a wave behind a prow, and in the red field behind his eyes, under the spinning, the patterns are already looking past this cut. They are looking at where the real one will go.

### Abilities, invented in this post

**MAKIMODOSHI · "Rewinding the Spool"** [Signature, Inzensho with Cymorath]. Two spoken glyphs, **[Cro]** Cycle and then **[Vor]** Return, thrown at a working's pitch, turned upside down, at the exact middle of its casting, so that its flow runs backward link by link and comes home to its caster. A glyph chain moves Essence the way a pulse moves down a rope, and the parts of that pulse drift out of step as it spreads into the world. Struck at the middle with the inverted tone, the drift carries the parts back together, and the working arrives at its source all at once instead of at its target. If the turn lands just short of the moment the chain commits, its own sequence is run backward over itself and the links come apart. The Inzensho shows him where the middle is by reading forward to the commitment; the Cymorath hears the chain's pitch and carries the glyphs. Spoken glyphs need no ink and no hands. *Cost:* about a ninth of his full reserve each time, plus Threadburn: a hot needle behind the left eye, the patterns in his eyes grinding against each other, a nosebleed. Twice within a minute and the second throw takes a quarter of his reserve and his left eye's sight goes white for a breath. *Limit:* only against a chain still in flow, never a finished construct, which is why the summon stands. One chain at a time. Within about thirty paces, and less where the ground is loud with leftover workings. He has to hold the reversal for exactly as long as the chain had run before the turn, and a chain that has run for several seconds before he finds its middle is beyond him. **[Vor]** returns and does not destroy: the Essence goes back to its caster whole, so the price is time and a hard catch, not reserve. *Counter:* before contact, a caster with a quiet Shell gives him nothing to hear and nothing to throw at. During, casting with a changing tempo (speeding and slowing the chain so it has no fixed middle), or laying a second chain out of step with the first, means the turn folds home on the wrong one. After, the caster simply recasts, with the returned Essence in hand. Speed beats it too: a chain that commits faster than he can say two syllables never gives him a middle.

**AWAI · "The In-Between"** [Aura, emergent]. He turns the gates of his Shell from broadcasting to receiving and tunes them to the ringing left in the ground by earlier workings, so that the room's leftover Aether comes into his Crystal the way a note in the air comes into a string tuned to match it. Two things follow. The leftover ring around him falls toward nothing, so the noise that hides every other working drains away and he can hear clearly where no one else can. And his own tone, held in and filled to the edge, sits around him as pressure rather than sound: heat shimmer leaning toward him, sand creeping in spiral lines to his boots, lamp flames turning to follow him, animals and the unwoken in the front rows pulling back without knowing why. *Cost:* the Aether he drinks is used and dirty, carrying the shape of the workings that left it, and holding it is a strain on the Crystal that grows the longer he holds. Clean draw it is not, and it cannot be cast as fuel for his own workings. It only fills his space and quiets the room. *Limit:* it needs leftover workings to drink, so in a clean room it gives almost nothing and an arena late in the day gives most. It holds steady while he moves at a walk or a fighting step and slips at a full sprint, because the tuning shakes loose. *Counter:* before, fight him in a clean room. During, flood the ground with fresh noise faster than he can drink it, or break his tuning with a loud discordant working thrown close. After, there is nothing to undo: the moment he lets go, the room's quiet is simply gone and the pressure lifts.

**KARA-ITO · "The Empty Thread"** [Sword form, Yukari blade]. A cut made true in every part of the body (hips, breath, shoulder, the draw), carried right up to the instant before it would commit, and held there. The Inzensho shows him the commitment ahead of him, so he can stop a hair short of it. To anyone reading his body it is a real cut, and to anyone reading threads sideways it is a strike tightening toward its lock. Nine parts of the decision made and the tenth kept back. *Cost:* the stop is paid in his own forearms and wrist, muscles that drove the blade now braking it, a strain that builds over a long fight. *Limit:* it works only on an opponent who reads, and shown twice it is read at the third. *Counter:* ignore the first movement and watch the back foot, which carries the real cut; or attack into the feint before its tenth part is decided.

### Mechanism, the three strata

- **Aether (the room).** An arena after four bouts is thick with leftover workings still ringing in the ground (the Ambient Floor, from The Harmonic Arts). That ring is the reason Makimodoshi is hard, and the reason Awai helps him.
- **Wellspring (the law).** Cymorath, vibration and resonance. **[Cro]**: root, Valen, primary Lethegraal, attested to Cymorath, Abstract/Entropic Systems ("repetition with variation; governs cycles and returns"). **[Vor]**: root, Auren, primary Somnalis, attested to Cymorath, Fluid Dynamics ("recycles Essence"). Her **[Ra]** Flame: root, Veyra, primary Exuroth, Caloria, Thermodynamics, also attested to Cymorath, which is why he can hear it in its own dialect.
- **Essence (the body).** The Crystal running hot, the Shell gates turned to receive, Threadburn behind the eyes.

### The physics, real and named

- **Spin echo (Hahn, 1950).** Spins in a magnetic field drift out of phase after a pulse. A refocusing pulse at time τ makes them come back into phase at 2τ, as though the drift had run backward. Old Ikuro's terrace race is the classic way of explaining it (runners who turn on a signal all arrive back together). Makimodoshi is this, worked on a glyph chain, struck at the middle.
- **Time-reversal acoustics (Fink's time-reversal mirror).** A recorded wave played back reversed refocuses at its source. That is **[Vor]** sending the working home.
- **Resonance and impedance matching.** A string or antenna tuned to a source draws the most energy from it. That is Awai drinking the room.

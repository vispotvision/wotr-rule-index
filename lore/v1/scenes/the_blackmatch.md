# The Blackmatch

*A frontier holding on the Eresse landward march. Day's end.*
*POV: Sennuvar Vhaeloth, Under-House officer. Explaining voices: the POV's own read, and Aeldros.*

---

## I. AFTER

The smell came in three layers and the top one was the wrong one.

Burnt thatch first, which was ordinary. Under it the stockyard, opened up, six cattle and a pen of pigs done with the rest of it, and that was ordinary too and Sennuvar Vhaeloth had smelled it on nine fields. The third layer was cooked hair and cooked bone with no smoke in it at all, which is not a thing that happens, because meat that cooks makes smoke, and this had none, and it came off the eastern row where there were no flames anybody could see and the ground was still putting heat up through the soles of his boots.

He did not walk down the eastern row. Nobody did.

The holding had been called something. He had heard it twice on the approach and had not kept it, because he was a Vhaeloth and Vhaeloth officers keep bearings and counts and not names, and the count was what he had. Two hundred and six of theirs. Eleven of ours, and the eleven was a lie, because four of those had gone down on the eastern row after it was over and nobody had entered them yet.

His ears hurt. That was the other thing. Sixteen days of being under a sky was sixteen days of light coming at him sideways off every wet surface in the world, and his eyes had given up around the fourth day and handed the work to his ears, and his ears had been pulling at it ever since and were sore the way a shoulder is sore.

*The boys from the Fourth have stopped squinting. Which means they have stopped being able to see properly in the dark, and it is permanent, and nobody has told them.*

The high elves lay where they had made their stand, which had been the mill wall, and they had made it well and for a long time. Eressean dead in the Sunroot green with the mail under the surcoat cut through at the neck and at the armpit. Wood elves scattered further out in twos and threes the way skirmishers die. Mixed among them the Principality's levy in no colours at all.

Aeldros Korvaeth was standing at the head of the common with his back to every bit of it.

---

## II. WHAT THEY HAD KILLED IN THE PASSES

They had brought the wyverns down off the high road on drags and laid them along the north side of the common, which was a Korvaeth order with a reason behind it that nobody had been given.

Nine. Six dead. Three that were not dead and were not going anywhere, hamstrung at the wing root with the long hooks the Fourth had been issued in the second week, which had been the first sign anybody had that this campaign was going to include something other than elves.

The smallest one was still making a sound. It had been making it for about two hours.

*Storm Talons, out of a country they have no business being in, coming over the pass at us in fours. Not hunting. Not nesting. Every one of them going south and west, and we were in the way.*

*Nine days and nobody has asked why. Not once.*

He crouched by the third carcass, because it was the one that had bothered him since the drag teams brought it in.

The others were straightforward. Hook wounds at the wing root, two with crossbow work through the throat, one burned along the whole flank in the manner things got burned on this campaign. This one had something else on the shoulder. A stripe about seven feet long running back from the base of the neck, and the plate along it had not been broken or peeled or melted. It had gone thin. Full thickness down to nothing across four inches, evenly, and it had done it well before the Fourth ever got a hook into the animal, because the margin was old and the flesh under it had already crusted over.

He put two fingers on the thin part.

The air over it was moving the wrong way.

He took his hand back and stood up and did not know what he had touched. There was no word for it in Rovann and none in Common, and he had been reading ground and wounds and weather for a hundred and sixty years.

*Do not write it down. A Vhaeloth who enters a thing he cannot name on a Korvaeth campaign has volunteered to explain it.*

---

## III. THE SPEAKING

"Officers to the common," said Oth Karreth, coming past at a jog with his helm under his arm and his hair stuck flat to his skull with somebody else's blood. "All of them. He is going to speak."

*Not tonight. Let the man be tired for one evening like the rest of us.*

They came in from the four quarters of the holding and stood in the open with the light going orange and horizontal behind the mill, six hundred drow in the black-lacquered scale of four Under-Houses, every one of them squinting into it, every one of them exhausted past the point where exhaustion feels like anything at all.

Aeldros Korvaeth turned round.

He had not washed. He had not pulled the surcoat straight. The blood on him was not a spatter and not a spray. It was a coat, the front of the chest and both forearms to the elbow and the whole left side of his jaw, going tacky and brown at the centre and still wet and bright at the edges where it had landed last, and there was elf in it out of at least four houses.

He was not a large man. That was the thing that always undid the new ones. Sennuvar had watched three intakes of boys arrive expecting a giant and get a compact grey-skinned man of middling height with a bad leg, and had watched all three intakes work out inside a day what the bad leg meant.

He came down the line of them at that limp, hard on the right, swinging the left through from the hip.

"You want to serve the true Empress of the Empire of Eresse."

Not shouted. He never opened with volume. The voice went out flat across six hundred men with nothing behind it and every one of them heard it, and Sennuvar had never worked out how that was done and had stopped believing it was a trick.

"How much are you willing to give, men?"

He let it sit. Twelve feet off the front rank, turning as he walked, taking faces one at a time and holding each about a second and a half.

"You look like you want to go home."

*Because we do. There are four of the Fourth on the east row nobody has entered, my eyes are ruined, and I would give this holding and both my hands for forty feet of deep road and no sky over it.*

"You look like you want to return to her with failure and injuries." The limp carried him three more steps along the rank. "Are you truly drow?"

He stopped.

"Or are you nothing more than our misguided brethren."

That was the one that did it. Sennuvar felt it go through the rank beside him as a physical event, a shift of weight, four hundred men adjusting their feet at once, because *brethren* means the ones who went up and stayed up. The ones who forgot the roads and married into the light and became Eressean in everything except the colour of them. There is no worse word in Rovann. It is not an insult. It is a category, and a man can be moved into it and never moved back.

He had put it on them in the interrogative, which meant nobody could answer it, which meant it stayed.

*He never accuses anybody of anything. He asks, and then he walks away, and you spend the night proving the answer to yourself.*

---

## IV. THE COLOUR GOING OUT OF THE FIRE

The fires took the rest of the holding while he was talking.

They had been ordinary fires. Thatch and rafter and the oil store behind the mill, burning orange, throwing the shadows of six hundred men out long across the common toward the east.

The shadows went first. Sennuvar noticed that before he noticed anything else, that the shadows stopped moving with the flames and then stopped being there at all, and when he looked up the fire on the mill roof had gone the colour of wet coal.

Not smaller. Not banked. The same body of flame in the same shape licking the same way off the ridge beam, with the light simply gone out of it.

Everybody who had campaigned with Korvaeth for more than a season knew the mechanism, and it was the least comforting piece of knowledge Sennuvar Vhaeloth owned.

A fire sheds most of what it makes as radiance. That is where the light comes from, and it is a substantial share of the heat leaving the burn. Refuse to let that share leave, and the fire does not go out. It goes dark and it gets hotter, because everything that would have left as light stays in as heat and goes out through the only two doors remaining, which are the air and whatever is touching it.

The eastern row had been burning at something near three times what a thatch fire has any business reaching, for two hours, with nothing to see there but shimmer.

Four of the Fourth had walked into it after the fighting stopped. Because there was nothing to see.

*He does not put them out. He has never once put one out. He lights them and he walks away and they burn until the fuel is gone, and the deep-road name for him is the name of a cord that carries a flame slowly to the thing it was aimed at, and not one of them thinks about that name long enough.*

The cost was standing in front of them and had been standing in front of them for eleven years, and Sennuvar had worked out what it was in the fourth year and had never said it aloud to a living soul.

The radiant share does not vanish. It has to sit somewhere until the burn is finished, and it sits in the man holding it.

Aeldros Korvaeth did not sweat on the left side of his body. Had not since Ollavaeth. The skin from hip to shoulder on that side was the wrong texture and the wrong colour even for a drow, and it did not tan and it did not blister and it did not do anything at all, and the leg under it dragged because the tissue had thickened the way tissue thickens when it has been cooked slowly a thousand separate times and told to go on working.

A drow is an underground animal with no tolerance for heat whatsoever.

He had been carrying his sister's fires inside his own body for eleven years. The fires were dark, and he was the reason they were dark, and he was going to run out of body a long while before Eresse ran out of villages.

---

## V. WHAT IT MEANS

Then the laugh started, and that was the part Sennuvar Vhaeloth had never been able to stand.

It came up out of him on the back of nothing. He had been speaking level and he stopped mid-line and it came up wet and broken and went on too long, and his shoulders went with it, and the blood on his jaw cracked and flaked off in a piece the size of a thumbnail.

*Blood-drunk. He has been in it since the second hour, he has not eaten, and he is bleeding from somewhere under that surcoat he has not mentioned. This is what that looks like in a man with no ordinary appetites left in him.*

*Somebody should get him off his feet. Nobody is going to.*

"Don't you UNDERSTAND what this means."

The voice had gone. Not gone quiet. Gone the other way, up out of the register he used on men and into something that came off the mill wall and back across the common and hit them a second time.

"IT MEANS EVERYTHING TO ME."

Six hundred men stood in the orange light with the dark fire behind them and did not move.

"IF I CANNOT SERVE MY EMPRESS WITH MY LIFE, WHAT USE IS MY LIFE TO HER."

There it was, and Sennuvar heard the whole of it and understood none of it, because he was hearing a soldier's oath and it was not a soldier's oath at all.

It was an account being settled.

Aeldros Korvaeth had a name. Full form, entered by the yard, validated before a convened assembly with the count called and the witnesses paid, and they had given it to him fourteen days after they refused his sister for the third and final time. He had not asked. He had been sixty-one years old and standing in a gallery in a coat that did not fit him, and they had given it to him anyway, in front of her, and she had clapped.

Rovann's grammar has nothing for a debt like that. The Bench holds no instrument that measures a thing given to the wrong sibling, so it cannot be tallied, and a debt that cannot be tallied cannot be paid, and a debt that cannot be paid does not stop accruing. The only currency he had ever found the Continuum would accept against it was his own body, applied to her enemies, at whatever rate the body would bear.

That was not lust for blood. Sennuvar Vhaeloth would go to his grave believing it was. He was not a stupid man. He had simply never been handed the one fact that makes the rest of it read.

The sun went down behind the mill and took the orange with it, and the common went to the colour the dark fires left, which was no colour at all.

---

## VI. THE MARROVEL BOY

They brought the defectors up at the end, because he always did it at the end, and the sound six hundred men make when defectors are brought up is no sound.

Four of them. Three Under-House infantry out of a levy that had gone over at the ford on the sixth day. One who was not infantry.

Sennuvar knew the belt before he knew the face. Rovann work, three-plate buckle with the tongue set off centre and the deep-road stamp at the back of it, out of a shop under the fourth gallery that had made belts for nine generations of Under-House officers and had shut thirty years ago when the old man died without a son who wanted it. There are perhaps two hundred of those buckles in the world and there will never be another one, and there was one on a boy kneeling in the mud of an elven holding at the wrong end of a campaign.

*Iveth Marrovel. Marrovel refused her. The whole house refused her, in form, on the record, and they are still seated and still breathing and nobody has laid a hand on them. And this one went the other way.*

Aeldros Korvaeth came down the line to him at that limp and stopped.

"Marrovel."

The boy did not answer. He was perhaps ninety and his mouth was going and his eyes were steady, which was more than most of them managed.

"Your house declined my sister and I have never held it against them for an hour." Level again. The voice had come all the way back down inside four breaths, which was the other thing about him. "A house is entitled to decline. That is the whole architecture. They stood in the yard and said no and they are drinking in their own hall tonight with their name intact and their seat under them."

He crouched, on the good leg, the bad one out sideways.

"You did not decline. You joined the other side of it." He put his left hand up and took the boy's chin between finger and thumb, almost gently, and tipped the head back. "There is no entry for that. I have looked. I have had the Bench look."

*He is ninety. Do it and stop talking.*

"So I am going to make one."

He brought the knife across.

It was done properly, which is to say deep and from the far side and drawn the whole way through, and the boy went over sideways into the mud with the belt buckle turning up to the sky. Aeldros Korvaeth stayed crouched in the spray of it with his eyes open and let it come across the left side of his face, over the top of what was already there, and did not blink and did not turn away and did not wipe it off.

He knelt a moment. The dark fires made no light behind him and the common held six hundred men who had stopped breathing at more or less the same time.

Then he stood up, hard on the right, swinging the left through from the hip, and turned to face them with the new blood running down into the old.

"Bury the wyverns," he said. "All nine. Deep, and out of the wind, and with the heads pointed south."

He limped away up the cart track toward the Principality with his back to every one of them, and Sennuvar Vhaeloth stood in the dark and counted, because counting is the thing his house does, and made it two hundred and ten.

# The Draught

*The coast road. Inside the column.*
*POV: Aeldros Korvaeth. Dova'Kan, Xmere, Gorgi and Baraxes are not written; their beats stay open.*

---

## I. WHAT A FIRE MAKES WHEN IT CANNOT MAKE LIGHT

The trench was still burning and that was the only reason he was still alive, and he had known it since he came down.

A man with nothing in the tank cannot seed. Seeding is the expensive half, and the expensive half is finished, and he had spent the last of himself opening forty feet of road and would spend no more of it today or in any of the days after.

What a man with nothing can still do is steer what is already burning.

*It is making soot. It has been making soot since the first second and it is making more of it than any fire that is allowed its light.*

That is a consequence and not a trick, and the physics of it are not complicated. A burn denied its radiant channel runs hotter at the surface and dirtier in the column, because the carbon in the fuel goes up faster than the air can finish it. The trench had eleven centuries of broken shell and clay and old road tar in it and it was throwing black particulate off the whole four feet of its length in a rope that the sea wind was bending inland.

He did not have to make that. He had only to put his dead hand into the column and close what was left of it.

The broadsword came round and the short blade came up the inside of his sword arm, and between the two of them, along the line where they were going to meet, the column folded.

It went from a rope of drifting black to a slab about nine inches thick and the length of a forearm, and it did it in under a quarter of a second, and it was no longer smoke at that point. Compacted particulate is not stone and it is not metal. It is closer to a fired brick made of nothing but char, hard and glassy on the face and brittle all the way through, and it will take one strike and only one, because what it does with the force is come apart.

Rovann steel bent outward off it.

Not turned. Bent, four or five degrees along the flat where the load went through unevenly, and the slab exploded into black grit that went across the road and stuck to everything wet on it, and Aeldros Korvaeth got the broadsword back with a blade that would never be straight again and an arm that had gone numb to the elbow.

*One. There is enough column for perhaps three more and every one of them costs me a straighter sword.*

---

## II. WHAT HE SHOUTED

He came out of it backward, on the bad leg, with the grit stuck to the blood on his throat, and he raised his voice for the first time on that road.

Not at the man in front of him. Past him, through him, out at six hundred drow and four thousand spears and the whole of a coast road, because there were witnesses and witnesses are the only architecture he has ever had.

"Beings."

The word came out of him with more in it than he intended.

"Beings that can be destroyed. Beings that can be killed. You have come here to teach me the word and I will grant you the word and it changes nothing that happened in that pass, so let us have the rest of it out where everybody can hear it.

"You want me to say we killed them because we thought ourselves above them. I will not say it, because it is not true and it is the comfortable version and you came a long way for the comfortable version.

"We killed them because they were in the way.

"Two of them put themselves between my line and a holding at Harrowbeck. Not a nest. Not a brood. A holding full of high elves who had never seen a wyvern before that morning and would have put a crossbow through either of them by noon, and they stood in front of it anyway, and I have known since the second day what that means and I have not said it to one living soul."

*And there it is, out loud, in front of six hundred men. That is the true thing and I have just spent it as cover for the lie underneath it.*

"They chose. They chose a side and the side they chose was not mine, and my men went at them with hooks because I had given my men hooks, and they fought back, and anybody who lifts a hand against the Empress of this land gets no mercy from her army and that is the whole of the law we operate under.

"Now the other half."

He put the bent sword out at arm's length and swept it across the four thousand and the six hundred and the shingle and the sea.

"WHAT AUTHORITY.

"You walked out of an ocean onto a continent that has been killing itself for nine hundred years and you have stood on my road and told me what may and may not be done in it. By what instrument. Under what article. Petitioned by whom, convened where, witnessed by whom, entered in whose register.

"You have none of it. You have wings you left behind a hill, and a name nobody on this coast has ever been obliged to recognise, and a very great deal of feeling.

"WHO DO YOU THINK YOU ARE."

---

## III. THE HYPOCRITE

He brought the sword back in and went to guard on the right hand alone, because the left had not answered an order since the trench opened.

"And the last of it, and it is the piece you are going to pretend you did not hear.

"You ride them."

*Say it. He is inside my reach and he is faster and he will open me in the next four exchanges, and I would rather go with this said than with it not.*

"Ten thousand of them. You breed the lines. You raise them from the shell and you choose which ones are bred to which and you decide which of them will carry a man and which will not, and you have been doing it for how many generations, and at the end of it you put the ones you kept into somebody's war.

"You will tell me they consent.

"I am sure they do. I am sure every one of them walks up to the harness willingly and I am sure you can tell me which hatchlings refuse meat for three days and which mothers go for anything near an egg, and I believe you, and I think you love them, and none of that is the question.

"The question is who made the animal that consents.

"You did. Over generations. You selected for it the way a man selects for shoulder or for wind, and then you called the result a choice and built a House on the difference between your harness and my hook.

"There is a difference. I am not going to stand here and pretend there is not one and I would rather be one of yours than one of mine. But you did not find that difference. You manufactured it, slowly, over a great many lifetimes, and then you crossed an ocean to be outraged at a man who did the same arithmetic faster and worse and wrote it down.

"You want sympathy off me."

He spat, and it came out black.

"Find religion. If you dragons have anyone left to look up to, go and put it in front of them, because you will not get it here. I will take a great deal today and I have earned most of it.

"I will not be threatened by a hypocrite."

---

## IV. THE LEFT SIDE

Then he gave him the left side, because the left side is the only thing he has left that has ever worked.

It looks like an opening and it has looked like an opening to every man who has come at him since Ollavaeth, and every one of them has taken it, and that is the point and has been the point for eleven years.

*There is no sensation in the hollow under that collarbone. There has not been since the fourth wave. The tissue over it went to something that is nearer to boiled hide than to skin and it is a quarter of an inch thick and it does not bleed properly and it does not hurt.*

*And the arm above it is half fused at the elbow and cannot be opened once it is shut.*

The blade came in under the collarbone exactly where it was invited.

It went through the hide and into him about two inches and stopped on bone, and Aeldros Korvaeth felt none of it and did not react to it, which is the entire trick, because a man expects a body to answer a wound and a body that does not answer buys half a second.

He shut the dead arm.

It came down across the blade and locked at the elbow with the whole weight of a side that no longer bends, and it was not a grip and could not be released, and for that half second there was four and a half feet of Rovann steel in his right hand and a man attached to a trapped edge in front of him.

He did not swing. There was no room for it.

He dropped the point, took the blade short by the ricasso in the one hand that still worked, and drove the cross-guard up into the underside of the jaw with everything the bad leg had left, the way a Rovann gallery man ends a thing in a corridor four feet wide where nobody can swing anything.

Something went in that face. He felt it through the steel and up his forearm.

Then the dead arm gave, because half a second is half a second and boiled hide over bone only holds an edge for so long, and the blade came out of him with a sound and took a piece of the hollow with it, and Aeldros Korvaeth went backward on the bad leg toward the lip of his own trench and did not fall into it.

---

## V. THE DRAUGHT

He gave the column what it wanted.

A fire does not need help to move air. Four feet of open trench at that temperature pulls at the base and lifts at the middle and dumps at the top, and it had been doing that for two minutes and building a circulation across the whole width of the road. All he had to do was bend it.

He put the last of the working into the shape of the draught and not into the fire at all, which cost him almost nothing, because a man steering a thing that is already moving is not paying for the motion.

*Down the trench line, out at the lip, and let the sea wind fold it back over both of us.*

The column came off the burn and stopped going up.

It came out sideways along the road at about the height of a man's chest, black and greasy and moving faster than it looked, and it went over the two of them and over eleven feet of glassed road in either direction, and the light on the coast road went from bad to nothing at all.

Then the air went thin.

That is not a working and it is not theatre and it is the second most reliable way a fire kills a man indoors. A burn that size eats what it needs out of everything within reach and gives back the products of eating, and the products are heavier and sit lower and do not support life, and a trench making its own inflow at the base produces a band above it where there is simply not enough left in the air to be worth breathing.

He had walked men into that four times in eleven years and had never once stood in it himself.

The first of it arrived at the back of the throat as a sweetness, which is the part nobody writes down. It does not taste of burning. It tastes faintly of something cooked and sugared, and the body does not read it as a warning of any kind, and that is precisely why gallery men die sitting up with their tools still in their hands and no expression on their faces at all.

He took it in and held it and let it out and took the next one.

*Four times. Ussen's crew at the second gallery, and the storehouse at Vhaelmoor, and twice in the passes.*

*I remember the names of two of the four and I have never once gone looking for the other two, and that is a choice I made on purpose about eleven years ago and have renewed every year since without ever saying it to myself in words.*

*Eleven or twelve breaths before the hands go. Perhaps thirty before the legs. I do not know because I have never done it and the men I did it to were not able to tell me afterward.*

*And I cannot see, and he cannot see, and there is a difference between the two of us, which is that I have been half deaf on this side for four days and I have spent those four days learning to fight without it.*

He put his back to the heat off the trench, because heat is a direction and a direction is worth more than sight.

Then he listened.

Not for the man. A man moving in smoke makes very little and what he makes is unreliable, and the right ear had four days of practice at doing the work of two and had learned in that time that the useful sound is never the footfall. The useful sound is the grit.

Eleven feet of glassed road on either side of the trench, and every inch of it under an eighth of an inch of black carbon that had come out of the air in the last two minutes and settled on everything, and it is fine and it is dry and it is loud, and nothing crosses it quietly. A boot on that surface makes a sound like a hand closing on dry sand.

He got the bent sword up in the one hand and stood in his own smoke with the blood coming down the front of him and the black grit stuck to all of it.

His chest had already started to work harder than it should.

"Come on, then," said Aeldros Korvaeth, into the dark.

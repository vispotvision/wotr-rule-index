# The Full Match

*The coast road. After the sword breaks.*
*POV: Aeldros Korvaeth. Dova'Kan, Xmere, Gorgi and Baraxes are not written; their beats stay open.*

---

## I. THE THIRD ENDING

The upper half of the broadsword went spinning off into the grit and lay there, and Aeldros Korvaeth looked at it and understood that he had lost.

Not the fight. He had never been going to win the fight and had said so out loud twice. He had lost the other thing, the one that mattered, the one he had been building since the moment a foreign voice declared a foreign law over elven ground in front of four thousand Eressean spears.

*He read it.*

*He read the whole of it, in about ninety seconds, while standing in air that is killing me, and he has taken it apart in front of every board on this road and then refused the ending.*

*You live. You explain.*

*He has no idea what he has just asked me for.*

The pressure came off the armies. He heard that happen too, four thousand men and six hundred getting their lungs back in one long ragged sound, and it did not come off him, which was correct and which he would have done the same way.

And then the other thing arrived, and it was worse than the sword.

*Xmere Dovaryn is going north-east. To Harrowbeck. To the pen behind the burnt mill.*

*Where there are four housings lying smashed on the far side of a wall with the keys still in two of them, and a gate standing open, and three animals with the pins out and the web margins gone the colour of meat, and my boot prints in the clay all round them.*

*He will be there before dark.*

*Everything I have said on this road for the last hour has a life of about nine hours and then a man with wings lands in a stock pen and reads the floor.*

He started to laugh again and stopped it, because there was not enough left in the chest to spend on it.

---

## II. WHY HE WAS STILL STANDING

There is an honest account of it and he had been running it in his own head for six minutes, because a practitioner who does not know why his body is still answering is a practitioner about to be surprised.

None of it was courage.

The first part was the air. What comes off a burn like that one does not announce itself and it does not hurt. It goes into the blood and it sits on the places the blood uses to carry what a man needs, and it does not leave, and the body has no sense organ anywhere that reports it. What it produces on the way through is a lightness behind the eyes and a great confidence and an absence of concern about outcomes.

*I am not brave. I am poisoned, and this particular poisoning feels from the inside exactly like being brave, and I have known that since the second gallery and I am doing it anyway.*

The second part was the left side, which had been dead since Ollavaeth. Tissue that has been cooked slowly for eleven years and told to keep working does not bleed the way living tissue bleeds. It has no vessels left worth the name and it closes on itself. The blade that went into that hollow would have emptied any other man onto the road inside two minutes, and it had put perhaps a cupful on the grit and then stopped.

*I have been carrying a wound that will not kill me in a place that cannot feel it, and that is the only reason I am upright, and it is also the reason I am going to die today.*

The third part was the working, and the third part was the one nobody at Rovann had ever written down properly.

A man with nothing in the tank cannot seed and cannot store. That is settled and he had been living inside it since the pen. What nobody had ever put in the register is that the tank is not the only fuel on the field.

*The column came out of the road. The armour came out of the column. The set came out of taking heat away instead of adding it.*

*And every one of those was free because I was never the fuel.*

*There is one more and it is not free, and the reason there is no entry for it in any register in the four quarters is that the people who have done it were not available afterward to file one.*

---

## III. THE FULL MATCH

A blackmatch is a cord.

That is the whole of the object and the whole of the name. You light one end and it carries flame along its length at a walking pace, slowly, for as long as the cord lasts, and it does not consume itself all at once, and a man can hold the unlit end and carry the fire to where it is wanted. Every gallery in Rovann has a hundred of them in a box by the door.

There is a failure state. It happens when a cord is badly made or has been kept wet and dried again, and what it does is go off along its entire length at the same moment instead of travelling. There is no flame front. There is no travel. There is one cord and then there is a line of heat where the cord was and a man standing there with nothing in his hand.

The gallerymen call it a full match, and they say it as a warning, and the theorists at Velthaeir who have never been under stone in their lives took the phrase off a Rovann porter forty years ago and wrote a paper about the terminal case and called it *the Full Match* with the capital letters, and there is not one man alive who has read that paper and also done the thing.

*The refusal is the same refusal. It is always the same refusal. I take the radiant channel away from a burn and the heat that cannot leave as light goes into whatever is touching.*

*The only variable in eleven years has been what I put in front of it.*

*The fuel has always been the road, or a roof, or an oil store, or a village.*

*It does not have to be.*

---

## IV. WHAT HE HAD TO WORK WITH

He had carbon.

Eleven feet of glassed road in every direction under an eighth of an inch of it, and the same eighth of an inch on the front of his own surcoat and in the creases of his one working hand and packed into the split of his forearm and drying tacky in the blood on his throat. Four minutes of a starved column dumping particulate onto everything wet had coated the whole of him.

It was on the other man too. It was on everything within eleven feet. That is what a column does.

He had a hand that opened and closed without instruction, because the flexors had been cut across and the muscle had not finished being told, and he had the wrist, and he had about four inches of forearm below the cut that still answered.

And he had the last of the draw. Not a reserve. Not a reservoir. The thing at the very bottom of a man that he is not supposed to be able to reach and that a Rovann surgeon will tell you is structural rather than spendable, and which is spendable exactly once.

He put the three together.

*Compress the carbon on the hand into a shell. Not for armour. For fuel, and for a face that will hold contact.*

*Drive it into him. Contact, not range. Range is the whole of the problem with every version of this I have ever run and contact solves it.*

*And at the moment of contact, refuse the channel entirely. All of it. Not a fire's worth. Everything that is in the shell and everything that is in the arm and everything that is in me, released at once with the only door shut.*

*A blackmatch carries fire because it is the cord and the cord survives the carrying.*

*A full match is the cord going all at once.*

*I have been the cord for eleven years.*

---

## V. THE PUNCH

He did not announce it.

He stepped in on the good leg with his shoulder behind it and put the hand into the centre of the other man's chest, and the shell formed on the way, in about a fifth of a second, compacted particulate closing over the knuckles and the back of the hand and up over the wrist in a grey-black casing a half inch thick and hard and glassy on the outside face.

It cut him going on. That was the first thing and he had not accounted for it and would not have changed it. Compressed particulate at that density has an edge everywhere it has a face, and it closed over living skin and went into it, and the whole back of his hand opened along four lines at once before the shell had finished setting.

Then contact.

And then he shut the door.

There was no flash, because there is never a flash, and that is the entire signature and it is why nobody on either army understood what they had watched.

What there was, was a shell of carbon and a forearm and the last structural draw of a practitioner all going at once with no radiant channel available, and every part of it having exactly two exits, which were the air and the thing it was touching.

The air went first and went strangely. At that density the gap between two bodies stops behaving like air and starts behaving like a conductor, and the discharge does not spread, it finds paths, and the paths were arcs. Black arcs. Filaments of carbon-loaded air an inch across running between his shell and the other man's chest plate in a splayed hand of four or five, throwing no light at all, and the sound of them was not thunder and was not a crack. It was a tearing. Like sailcloth going along a seam, four times over, at the same instant.

And the rest went into the other man.

*Into.*

*Not through.*

He had put a shaped hand of pure conducted heat into the centre of a dragon at contact range and it had gone into him the way water goes into a wall.

*It is like striking the middle of a star.*

*There is no give in it. There is nothing in there that is compressible and nothing that wants to move and no volume anywhere for the energy to occupy, and it has to go somewhere, and it does not have somewhere, so it comes back.*

---

## VI. WHAT CAME BACK

The arm went.

Not burned. That is the word every witness used afterward and every one of them was wrong, because a burn leaves a thing behind and there was nothing left between the elbow and the wrist to leave. The tissue along the conduction path was the path, and it went the way the cord goes, all at once and along its whole length, and what came off the end of him was a fine hot grey nothing that the sea wind took inland across the shingle.

The shell went with it. The four cuts stopped being cuts, because there was no longer a back of a hand for them to be in.

He felt the whole of it and felt none of the pain. He understood exactly why, and that frightened him a great deal more than the pain would have.

*Above the elbow. It has taken it above the elbow and it has cauterised on the way, so I am not going to bleed out, which is not the mercy it sounds like.*

*And I have one arm dead at the side and one arm gone and there is nothing left in me at all, not a reserve, not the structural draw, not one seeded point, and I am going to be conscious for a while yet because the air will not let me be anything else.*

He went back three steps. Recoil, not decision. He stayed up because the good leg was still the good leg and for no other reason at all.

Six hundred men behind him had watched the whole of it. Not one had seen a thing. That was what he would have wanted entered, if there had been anything left in his hand to write with, and there was not, and there never would be again.

No flash. No report. Four black arcs nobody's eye could hold, a sound like sailcloth going along a seam, and a commander with one fewer arm than he had started the sentence with.

*They will say I was struck. Every one of them. It goes into the book as a blow received and it stays there four hundred years.*

*And the only man on this road who knows otherwise is the one I have just failed to kill.*

*Which is the third time today that the record has come out the opposite of the fact, and I put it that way myself on the first two, and on this one I did not have to.*

Then Aeldros Korvaeth stood on a coast road in front of four thousand spears and six hundred of his own with nothing above his right elbow and his left arm hanging dead and the grey of himself going out across the shingle on the wind, and looked up at the man he had just failed to hurt.

"You said I would live," he said. "And explain.

"I have removed the explaining. That was the last thing I owned.

"Now kill me or carry me.

"Both of those are entries. Both of them will be read out in a gallery you will never see, in a language you do not have, by a woman with no name, and she will weigh them against each other the way she weighs everything, and she will be right about the arithmetic.

"Choose whichever one you can live beside. I have run out of preferences."

He stood there. The wind off the sea went through the space where his arm had been, and he could feel it doing that, which he had not expected and did not have a word for.

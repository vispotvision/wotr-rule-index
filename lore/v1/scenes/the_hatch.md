# The Hatch

*Coldwater common. Late morning, the second day.*
*POV: Ruvaen Aloreth. RP reply. Dova'Kan, Gorgi and Xmere are not written; their beats stay open.*

---

## I. THE HATCH

The bottom of the quarry stair let out beside the leat, and the leat had a hatch on it, and the hatch was shut.

Brannoch Skell had built it eleven years ago to take the beck across the face for the wet drilling, and it was a good piece of work, oak frame and a lifting board and an iron rack with a pawl that clacked. It had been shut since the village went over the ridge, because Wenna Tull had shut it herself on the way out, and she had shut it because Ruvaen Aloreth had told her to leave the water alone and she had understood him to mean leave it in the beck.

He put both hands on the rack and wound it up.

Nine turns. The pawl clacked nine times and the board came up out of its seat with a suck and the beck went over into the leat and down the stone channel along the western edge of the common, filling the cattle trough, then the overflow, then the long stone drinking course behind the mill that had been cut for a hundred and forty beasts and had not held forty in a generation.

It made a great deal of noise. He had wanted it to.

*Nobody has drunk from that course since the cattle went. Eighty feet of cut stone and six inches of standing water and it is the only thing in this village that is any use to anything out there.*

He left the hatch up and walked out onto the common.

---

## II. THE CROSSING

He closed himself before he crossed the line of the first scorch star, and that was the part that mattered and the part almost nobody would see.

It took him four steps. Eleven hours open on the quarry lip and four full breaths of it thrown across eleven miles of forest at the end, and now the whole of it drawn back in and compressed down and held, the way he had held it since he was sixty-one, so that a man walking across a common at a walking pace was simply a man walking across a common. There would be no pressure off him from here. Not a pound of it, not a foot of it, and if he was killed in the next two minutes he would be killed with his hands empty and his weight ordinary.

Anyone in that clearing who could read at all would know he had done it deliberately, and would know what it had cost, and would know it was addressed to them.

His right hand hung four inches off the hilt.

Not on it. Four inches, which is the Landward measure, and any officer of any company in the four quarters would read it the same way. It says *I am not drawing.* It also says, without apology, exactly how long it would take him, and a man who conceals that from a thing that can already smell his pulse is a man wasting everybody's morning.

He crossed the common at a walk with the water running loud on his left.

---

The dragon bent the field before the field arrived.

Ruvaen had told Hesh Orovain that a wyvern sits in the Aether and this thing did not, and standing in the middle of the common he understood how badly he had undersold it. There was no windward side to it. There was no shape of a disturbance with an inside and an outside. The current for two hundred yards in every direction was already arranged, had been arranged since before he came down the stair, and would go on being arranged after he left, and at no point had anything spent anything to arrange it.

*I have read one thing in my life that was there whether or not it was doing anything, and I met her yesterday, and she is not in the same order of magnitude as this and she knows it.*

*Every wyvern on this common has bowed to it at least once. Kethyrros has not stopped watching it and she is not watching it the way you watch a threat.*

Beside the great one, closer in and lower down, there was a man.

Ruvaen put the pressure of them side by side and drew the obvious conclusion, which was that the dragon had come and had brought someone.

That was wrong. He had no means of finding out it was wrong, and would go on holding it until somebody told him otherwise, and it would colour every word he chose for the next quarter of an hour.

---

## III. THE SEAL

He got close enough to see the juvenile's shoulder and stopped where he was, because what had been done to it stopped him.

Yesterday that wound had been a gradient. Full plate going to thin plate going to nothing, evenly, over four inches, and the flesh under it following the same curve into a place where there was no longer any flesh to speak of, and the flow over the whole of it going in and coming back out nowhere. He had put his read on it for less than a second and had shut it so fast his own Shell had rung.

Now there was an edge.

Crushed mineral packed along the surviving margin, chalk and iron-stone and something dark and glassy, worked into the damp and pressed flat against intact scale and not into the hollow. It sat on the living side of the line and made a wall out of ordinary matter, and the absence beyond it went on being an absence, and the absence could no longer reach past the wall to the next inch of a living animal.

The clear weeping had stopped.

*Essence goes into that and does not come out. Everyone who has looked at this has established that in the first four seconds and then stopped, myself included, twice.*

*Matter goes into it and stays matter. Chalk sits on the margin and is still chalk. Whoever did this stopped asking the wound what it wanted and started asking what it could not have, and the answer was a boundary, and a boundary does not have to be alive.*

*I do not know why that works. Nothing in any register at Velthaeir tells me why that works.*

He had been on a wall for eleven years and had never once seen anyone counter this, because nobody had ever seen this, and here was a rough grey paste pressed into a shoulder with somebody's thumbs.

There was a cane lying in the grass about nine yards behind, where it had gone in two turns and stopped.

He looked at it for exactly as long as it took to understand what throwing it had meant, and then did not look at it again, and did not mention it, because there are things a man does in front of company that are not addressed to the company.

---

Kethyrros knew him.

Her head came round as he came level with her flank, and the crest stayed down, and the thundercloud eye tracked him the whole length of his walk and did not follow him after he had passed, which is not the same as being watched and he understood the difference.

She said one thing, low, and it was three syllables and he had none of them, and she did not repeat it.

*Yesterday she talked to me for four minutes on the inhale in a body a day and a half past empty, and I only worked out what that meant in the last twenty seconds of it.*

*She is still doing it. She has been doing it since the moment I came down the stair, and there is now something on this common that could put a village under the horizon without changing expression, and she is still spending air she does not have so that forty men on a rise behind me go home.*

*Nobody at Velthaeir is going to believe a word of this and I am going to make them enter it anyway.*

He put his right hand flat on his own chest as he went past her, which was the form he had used yesterday for a name received and not returned, and she had not known the form then and had read it anyway.

---

## IV. WHAT HE SAID

The wyverns had found the drinking course.

Four of them had come in over the north ridge during the night, drawn down out of the migration by whatever had told the rest of them where to go, and they had been standing at the top of the common since dawn doing nothing in particular. When the leat went over they had all four turned their heads at the sound, and one of them had walked the length of the stone course with its wings half up and put its head down into six inches of standing water and stayed there.

None of them moved away from him as he passed.

That is the entire vocabulary. A wyvern that is not moving away from you is saying the only thing a wyvern has to say on the subject, and he had read enough of them in the last day and a half to know it was not tameness and was not trust and was closer to a very cold acknowledgement of accounts.

He came to a stop at a distance that left Kethyrros her reach and left the great one everything, and inclined his head to the dragon first and to the man beside it second, which was the order his reading of them required, and was wrong.

"I hope my people didn't cause you any trouble."

Common. He said it in Common because his Valorin was three broken sentences deep and he was not going to insult something that old by performing.

He let it stand a moment, because there were four scorch stars walking across the common between him and them, and any creature with eyes could count them and follow the direction they walked in.

"Coldwater is mine," he said. "The powder is quarry stock, four barrels, and the man who fired it is alive and over that ridge and I have not punished him and I am not going to. He had a daughter behind him in the powder house and a thing larger than his village lying against the chalk. He did the stupidest possible thing four separate times and walked it in thirty feet closer each time, and he did it because he was terrified, and terrified is the only crime on that common."

He moved his right hand very slightly further from the hilt. Five inches. Six.

"The holding is cleared. Eighty-one souls went over the far ridge yesterday and they took the pigs and left the water, on my order, and the hatch is up now and the drinking course behind the mill holds about six inches and runs as long as the beck does, which is always, because it comes out of the face." He nodded very slightly toward the four at the top of the common. "There is grazing on the eastern slope and nothing on it and nobody is coming back to it. You may have all of it for as long as you want it and I will put that in writing in any hand you like, and if the Sunroot Court objects they may come here and explain the objection to you personally."

Then he looked past both of them at the juvenile's shoulder and the grey mineral packing and the place where the weeping had stopped.

"And I would be in your debt," he said, "for whatever you are willing to tell me about what you have just done to that animal. Because I have a hundred and forty miles of frontier, and I have seen that wound three times now, and until this morning I did not believe there was anything to be done about it at all."

---

## V. WHAT HE HAD TO TRADE

He gave them the rest of it standing where he was, in the same flat voice, because the alternative was to make them ask.

"Three times," he said. "The first was eleven days ago, four leagues off my southern water. Not a wound, that one. Something high and moving south with a front and no tail, and the current over four leagues bending toward it instead of round it. Every gull on my headland went inland in the dark that night and did not come back."

The water ran along the western edge of the common. Behind him, forty spears stood at the rise and had been standing there since the sixth bell.

"The second is on that shoulder there.

"The third is a hundred and forty miles north and east of here, in the Korvaeth camp, and it is on a dead one. Nine of them came over the high road at that army in fours. Six are dead and three were hamstrung at the wing root with hooks that were made for the purpose and issued before this campaign had seen a single wyvern, which means somebody at Rovann knew there would be wyverns before anybody here did. One of those six carries a stripe about seven feet long at the shoulder, and the plate along it has gone thin across four inches without breaking, and it was old before the hooks ever touched the animal."

He let that sit.

"They are burying them today. Deep, out of the wind, heads pointed south. The man who gave that order has not explained it to anybody and I doubt he can."

*And I read him last night from four hundred feet of chalk and eleven hours in the dark, and I concluded he was driving them, and I have been certain of it since about the fourth hour.*

*I am going to keep being certain of it. There is nobody on this continent in a position to correct me.*

"I have no theory," Ruvaen said. "I have been reading the Aether since I was forty and I have nothing. I can tell you the three of them are the same phenomenon at three sizes, and I can tell you nobody on either side of this war has a word for it in any language they own, which I know because I have read both sides looking for one. My own Archive has four entries on your kind and three of them are poetry, so you may judge for yourself what my Archive is worth on a subject it has never met."

He lifted his chin very slightly toward the north ridge, and the four drinking, and the eleven miles of green beyond them.

"There are three hundred of them on the river south-south-east of here. I counted them from the quarry lip last night and I could not tell you why I know the number and I am not going to pretend otherwise. They did not come to this country because it is rich." The grey eyes came back down and settled. "They came to it because it was the only thing left that was rich enough."

He said nothing else.

He stood on a burnt common in the middle of his own frontier with his hand six inches off a sword he had not touched, and the beck running loud behind him into eighty feet of cut stone that had held nothing for a generation, and waited to be answered by whichever of them had the standing to answer.

Ancient battles flickered in miniature across the metal: riders claiming monsters, armies breaking war-beasts, kings dragging conquered standards through ruined cities. The Chains of War did not understand ownership as paperwork or bloodline. They understood the oldest claim ever recognized on a battlefield.

I faced you. You resisted. I remained.

Sonzai wrapped one chain around his forearm and pulled.

“Enough.”

Calamity rolled outward.

The pressure was not Winter. It was not sovereignty in Sodoku’s sense. It was the atmosphere of catastrophe held behind human skin, dark crimson force spilling around Sonzai as eleven years of anger, battle, exile, hatred, and the restrained desire for war entered the links at once. The Droval’s struggle changed immediately. What had been physical resistance became recognition of something else—a creature of violence encountering a man whose soul carried more violence than his body should have survived.

Sonzai stepped forward instead of pulling backward.

One pace.

Then another.

Every time the Droval resisted, the Chains recorded it. Every time Sonzai advanced, they shortened. The contest stopped being about strength and became about who would surrender distance first. Sonzai’s expression remained hard, crimson hair whipping around his face while the sword at his side stayed untouched. He had no intention of killing this creature. Killing was easy. Claiming something powerful enough to kill you and making it decide not to was harder.

The Droval snapped its jaws less than a yard from him.

Sonzai did not blink.

“Try again.”

The beast growled. Sonzai stepped closer.

Selvara stared at him from behind, and for the first time since waking she understood why people might give this man the name Blood Hound. It was not because he chased. It was because once Sonzai fixed upon something, retreat ceased to exist as a concept. He simply kept coming until whatever he had chosen to pursue either broke, surrendered, or learned to walk beside him.

The Droval’s growl slowly diminished. Its enormous head lowered. The chains immediately loosened.

Sonzai felt the change travel through every link. No enchantment told him he had succeeded. No voice announced submission. The Chains of War simply stopped treating the Droval as an enemy. Their molten inscriptions cooled from violent red into a low ember glow before one final mark formed upon the link nearest Sonzai’s wrist: the silhouette of a great white beast beneath a broken circle.

Sonzai stared at it.

Then at the Droval.

“Apparently you are coming with us.”

The Droval exhaled over him, hot breath turning instantly into fog.

Selvara stepped closer. “You just claimed it.”

“Yes.”

“A Titan Droval.”

“Yes.”

“With chains.”

Sonzai glanced at her. “You saw it happen.”

She looked up at the enormous creature, then back toward him. “I wanted to make sure you understood how unreasonable that was.”

“I have been informed.”

The Droval lowered itself enough that mounting became possible, though even crouched its back stood higher than most horses. Sonzai gathered the Chains of War and returned most of their length to his hip, leaving a single loose run around the beast’s neck, not a strangling collar, but a visible acknowledgment of the connection they had just established. He climbed onto its back first, red-and-black armor striking sharply against the blue-white fur.

Then he looked down at Selvara.

She looked back expectantly.

“No.”

“I did not say anything.”

“You were about to.”

“I was going to ask how I am supposed to keep up.”

“Walk.”

“I nearly died five days ago.”

“You have reminded me.”

“Sonzai.”

“No.”

The Droval shifted beneath him. Selvara folded her arms and looked at the immense distance between herself and the creature’s back before turning those pale eyes on him again. Sonzai held out for perhaps five seconds.

Then his irritation won.

“Fine.”

He extended an armored hand.

Selvara took it immediately, and Sonzai pulled her onto the Droval behind him with enough ease that she barely touched its flank during the ascent. The instant she settled against his back, her arms instinctively circled his waist. Her cheek came near the black fur at his shoulder, and the warmth radiating beneath the crimson cuirass reached her immediately.

Selvara relaxed.

Sonzai became rigid.

“Do not.”

“I have to hold something.”

“The fur exists.”

“The fur does not have a waist.”

“That is precisely why I recommended it.”

She tightened her arms slightly when the Droval rose.

Sonzai stared forward with an expression that suggested he would rather fight a third Titan.

Then he felt it again.

That subtle response.

Selvara had been weak when she climbed aboard. Now, pressed against him while the aftermath of Calamity still smoldered through his body, her breathing steadied almost instantly. The weakness disappeared from her grip. Her body warmed despite the polar wind.

Sonzai said nothing.

The Blood Hound had caught the scent again.

His hand dropped casually toward the Chains of War at his hip while the newly claimed Droval began walking north beside its companion. He did not confront Selvara. Not yet. Accusations frightened secrets into hiding, and Sonzai wanted hers comfortable enough to keep making mistakes.

Selvara rested closer against him, believing his silence meant acceptance.

Sonzai looked toward Kharven.

“Outer World,” he murmured beneath the wind.

Selvara lifted her head. “What?”

“Nothing.”

But his purple eyes had already hardened.

She could stay. She could ride with him. She could even keep occupying his damned igloo.

Until Sonzai learned exactly what Selvara Kusho was, keeping her close was no longer an inconvenience.

It was the hunt.

The Droval moved first.

The claw came from the right, a sweeping lateral strike that covered the distance between the creature's resting forelimb and Sodoku's standing position in a single motion, and the motion was not fast in the way that combat was fast. The motion was fast in the way that geology was fast when geology decided to stop being patient, the specific explosive speed of a mass that had been still for so long that the stillness had become indistinguishable from permanence and that the breaking-of-permanence carried the accumulated force of every moment the mass had spent being still. The claw was black and hooked and the size of a man's forearm, five of them sweeping through the polar air in a formation that would have opened Sodoku from hip to collarbone if the opening had been the thing the claw was designed to do.

It was not.

The Glacier Eye read the strike before the strike arrived. Five ring-lines contracted toward the pupil, the outer iris producing the pale frost-luminescence of Vohrin-integrated perception, and the reading was instantaneous and the reading said: the strike is not aimed at the kill-line. The strike is aimed at the response-line. The Droval is not trying to open the man in front of it. The Droval is trying to see what the man in front of it does when something the size of the man's torso moves toward the man's torso at a speed the man's body should not be able to process.

The claw was the question.

Sodoku's body was the answer.

He did not step back. Stepping back was retreat, and retreat was the thing the Droval was testing for, because retreat was the response of prey, and prey was the category the test was designed to confirm or deny. He did not block. Blocking was the response of a fighter, and a fighter was a smaller category than the one being contested, because fighters fought and sovereigns governed, and the contest was governance, not combat.

He moved laterally. One step. The step was the Edict Step's smallest expression, a compression of distance that relocated his body eighteen inches to the left, the glacier-crystal greaves grinding against the ice surface as the Coagulatio-compressed movement deposited him outside the claw's arc with the specific economy of a body that had not wasted a single calorie of motion on anything the motion did not require. The claw passed through the space he had occupied. The wind off the passing ruffled the fur trim of the Verdict Plate's collar. The claws were close enough that the Glacier Eye read the individual scratches on each talon's surface, and the reading was filed, and the filing was the answer's preamble.

Sodoku's eyes closed.

The closing was voluntary. The closing was the thing that made the crew stop breathing, because a man closing his eyes in front of a Titan Droval was either a man who had decided to die or a man who did not need his eyes for what he was about to do, and the not-needing was the sovereignty, and the sovereignty was the thing the Droval was testing, and the test required the answer to be delivered without the diagnostic's assistance, because the diagnostic was the tool and the sovereignty was the man, and the man needed to prove he was the man without the tool.

The cold that lingered on his breath became visible. Not the fog of warm exhalation meeting cold air. The inverse. The specific phenomenon of breath that was colder than the ambient temperature, breath that produced frost rather than fog, the exhalation crystallizing the moisture in the air in front of his face rather than the air crystallizing the moisture in his exhalation. The breath was the Vohrin integration expressing itself through the body's most fundamental rhythm, and the expression was the announcement, and the announcement was: the cold is not something I am channeling. The cold is something I am.

"Tōken."

The word left his mouth as frost.

Robin Ice, standing thirty paces back with his knife in his hand and his runner's body producing the specific stillness that the runner's body produced when the runner's body had decided that running was not currently an option, felt the word arrive in the air the way a change in pressure arrived, not as a sound but as a condition. The word changed the air's composition. The word told the air what the air was now subject to, and the air obeyed, because the air was cold, and the cold was the jurisdiction, and the jurisdiction recognized the word.

The Droval felt it.

The creature's vitality lurched. Not drained. Not extracted. Disrupted at the interface, the specific phenomenon that Cold Ken produced when the Cold Ken was delivered not through a fist but through the Frost Sovereignty Field itself, the word Tōken spoken as an invocation that carried the Ken's diagnostic-strike architecture through the cold rather than through physical contact. The Droval's stat-to-body interfaces, the biological junctions where the creature's millennia-old Winter resonance connected to the creature's nervous system, experienced a simultaneous freeze at every junction point. The freeze was not damage. The freeze was governance. The cold was telling the creature's body: your vitality is not yours right now. Your vitality is inside my jurisdiction. The jurisdiction is temporary. The jurisdiction is the test's answer.

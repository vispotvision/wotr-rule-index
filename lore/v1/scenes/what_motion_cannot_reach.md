# What Motion Cannot Reach

*The coast road. The end of it.*
*Part One POV: Aeldros Korvaeth. Part Two POV: Ruvaen Aloreth.*
*Dova'Kan, Xmere, Gorgi and Baraxes are not written; their beats stay open.*

---

# PART ONE: THE LIMIT

## I. UNMOVED

He was not fine and everybody on that road could see he was not fine, and none of it had moved him one inch, which is a different thing and the only thing he had left.

*Stopped. Not countered, not absorbed, not shielded. Stopped.*

*Velocity removed from a claimed volume. Translation first, then rotation, then oscillation, then the transfer itself, and the arcs hung in the air between us like ink in glass and did not finish.*

*I have read every register in Rovann and four of Velthaeir's and there is no entry anywhere for that, and I have just watched it performed twice in front of eleven boards and forty runners and six hundred of my own men.*

He stood at the angle his balance had been arrested in and did not fall over when the field let him, which took a certain amount of doing on one leg and no arms.

"Well," he said. "Thank you."

He got it out in the voice, which cost more than it had an hour ago.

"You have just published."

---

## II. THE BOUNDARY

"Understand what you have done here, because I do not think you have troubled to.

"Every working has a hinge. Mine is a refusal, and any man who watches me put a fire up and sees no light on it knows within four seconds what I refuse and can build against it, and three of them have, and one of them nearly finished the job. That is the cost of using a thing in front of people. You spend the secret to spend the power and there is no version where you keep both.

"I have spent mine eleven times in eleven years and I have been careful about the audience every single time.

"You have spent yours today, twice, on a public road, in front of scribes.

"So here it is out loud where the boards can get it.

"You do not stop *things*. You stop *motion*. Translation, rotation, vibration, transfer, expansion, recoil. Every one of those is a body going from where it was to where it was not, and you take away the permission for the going.

"Which means you are the finest defence in the four quarters against anything that has to *arrive*.

"And you are nothing at all against a thing that does not."

*Say it in the order a jurist would say it, because half the men writing this down are clerks and clerks copy structure.*

"Essence does not travel to reach you. It is not a projectile and it has no path and there is no moment where it is between one place and another with a velocity you could remove. A working performed on the material of a man is not a thing thrown at him.

"Presence does not travel. You have stood on four thousand people this morning with your presence and not one grain of anything moved from you to them, and you know that, because you did it.

"A Wellspring answering an appetite does not travel.

"Pressure in the Aether does not travel.

"Coherence does not travel.

"Rot does not travel. Nor does thirst, nor cold that is taken rather than given, nor anything at all that works by removal instead of delivery."

He got his weight back over the good leg and did not manage it well.

"You are a wall against the whole of the physical and you have no roof.

"And you told me so yourself, in a demonstration, in daylight, with the Sunroot Court's own clerks eleven yards behind you, because you wanted the satisfaction of explaining the trick to a dying man."

---

## III. THE LAST OF IT

"I have already sent it home."

*Which is true, and it is the only piece of soldiering today that will outlive the day.*

"A runner went west at the first caster and a second went at the parley and a third went the moment you put your weight on this road. Ordinary men on ordinary horses. You cannot stop what has already arrived and you are not going to catch a boy who left ninety minutes ago.

"Rovann has it. She has it. Dragons are nettling in an affair that is not theirs, on our ground, in our war, and there is a name attached and a description and two demonstrations of a limit, and it is in a Vhaeloth hand.

"You think your little stillness will hold against her.

"She has never needed a working and she has never been given a name and she has held four Under-Houses in one doctrine for thirty years with nothing but a voice and an arithmetic, and I am the small end of her, and I have never in my life been able to tell you what the big end does.

"She will enjoy it. That is the part I would sit with, if I were you and had the evening. She does nothing in anger, and she will enjoy this one.

"If you think I am the problem, wait."

Then he turned his head, because the man on the white horse had come down off it forty feet away and was standing on the road in the black grit, and had been standing there for some while.

"And you," said Aeldros Korvaeth. "You are not going to make Vaelmarr.

"I will finish with you here. This road, this morning. Before your causeway sees one spear of mine, and I will not need an arm to do it.

"Come and try it, Aloreth. Eleven years you have walked at this, and here it is, standing in a road with nothing in its hands."

*That is a lie and everybody on this road knows it is a lie.*

*It is also a request, and there is exactly one man here with the standing to grant it.*

# PART TWO: THE OPENING

## IV. WHAT RUVAEN WAS READING

Ruvaen Aloreth had come down off the horse at the second demonstration and had walked forty feet up the road and had not said one word in eleven minutes.

He had been reading the whole of it and his range was four hundred yards and at forty feet he had everything there was.

*Nothing.*

*There is nothing in him. Not low and not narrow and not hard, which is what he has been every day for eleven years. There is a body standing in the road and the reach is gone out of it entirely and I have read four thousand men and never once seen that in anything still upright.*

*He is not going to do anything to anybody ever again. That is not an opinion. That is the read, and my read has been wrong twice this fortnight and it is not wrong about this.*

Which meant the threat was theatre.

Which meant the man was asking.

*And he is asking the dragon, and the dragon has just said, in front of both armies, that he will neither kill him nor carry him, and that he remains an elven commander on an elven battlefield, and that his own people may retrieve him.*

*I am the other elven commander on this battlefield.*

*He handed him to me. He did it on principle, out loud, and he will work out what he did about four seconds after it is done, and he will not be able to say it was not what he said.*

Eleven years.

Ollavaeth eleven times. Four hundred horses in a pit with no lime. Nine of the Hales and a boy who ran messages because he was fast, and the pear stock that will never take on that ridge again, and a barge coming up a causeway under a covering with the gulls screaming on the stacks.

*Now.*

---

## V. THE CUT

He came in on the left.

Not the front and not the sword side, which no longer existed. The left, because the man had told an entire coast road four days ago that he had lost the hearing on that side and had spent four days learning to fight without it, and because he was standing at the angle his balance had been arrested in and had not got it back.

Eleven feet. Seven. The grit made the sound it makes and Aeldros Korvaeth did not turn.

The collar of the surcoat was already gone. Xmere had taken the top two inches off it at the parley with the first cut of the day and the mail beneath it ended at the same line, because Rovann harness is built for a man who will be underground and nobody armours a throat properly for a corridor four feet wide.

So there was four inches of bare neck above a burnt collar, on a body that was not moving, at seven feet, in front of ten thousand witnesses.

It is not an easy cut and every man who has ever tried it on a field knows it. The neck is not a neck. It is seven bones in a column with a great deal of muscle laid over them and the muscle is the problem, and a blade that arrives even slightly off the edge line will wedge in it and stop and you will be standing there sawing at a man in front of your own company. Most of the men who have done it cleanly did it to somebody kneeling, over a block, with time.

Ruvaen Aloreth had a three and a half foot blade, a hundred and forty years, a target that was not moving, and a line of approach that let him come across the front of the throat instead of down onto the back of it.

He stepped through off the rear foot, left past right, hips arriving before the hands, and brought it up and across in one long rising arc with the edge true the whole way.

It went through above the fourth bone and it did not stop anywhere.

The body stood.

That is the thing nobody writes and everybody who has seen it remembers. It stood, square on the good leg, for something between one and two seconds, with the wind off the sea going through the space where the right arm had been, and then the knees went and it came down forward onto the glassed road and lay there.

The head went the other way. It rolled about four feet into the black grit and stopped on its side facing up the road, and the eyes were open, and they went on being open.

Nobody on either side made any sound at all.

That went on longer than it should have. Four thousand spears and six hundred of the black-lacquered scale, ten thousand men on a coast road with the sea working away on the left, and not one of them cheered and not one of them shouted and not one of them broke.

Ruvaen had been told all his life what an army does when a commander goes down in front of it.

What this one did was stand there and look at the grit.

*They do not know what they have watched. Not the drow and not mine. They have seen a fire with no light in it and a man stopped in the air and an arm turn to nothing in front of them, and now a head in the road, and there is not one man in ten thousand who could give a Reckoner an account of the last twenty minutes that would survive being read twice.*

*Including me.*

---

## VI. THE NAME

Ruvaen Aloreth stood over him with the blade down and the blood going off the point into the carbon.

*I have been waiting eleven years for this and I am standing in it and there is nothing here.*

*I expected something. Not joy. I am not a fool and I never expected joy. I expected the thing to close. There is a shape in a man that a debt makes and I have been carrying that shape since the eleventh day of the withheld season and I assumed that when the debt came off the shape would go.*

*It has not gone. It is exactly where it was.*

He looked at the head in the grit, and at the stump above the elbow that had cauterised itself, and at the four black lines still hanging in the air eleven feet away where nothing had been permitted to finish.

*And the other thing has stopped.*

Iesera had been threading since the second day of the march. Not at the man. Past him, through him, at something behind the drow line that Ruvaen had never once been able to resolve, and it had been threading all morning under the smoke and the pressure and the shouting.

It was not threading now.

It had gone quiet at the moment the cut landed, the way a room goes quiet when the person who was about to speak decides not to, and it had not gone away, and he could still feel it sitting there entirely still and attending.

*Something was interested in him.*

*It is not interested in him any more.*

He put his hand flat on his own chest, which is the form for a name received and not returned, and held it there over a dead drow on a coast road in front of ten thousand people, and could not have explained to any of them why.

Then he said it.

"Onawa."

Out loud. In full form, at the volume the yard requires, with the count uncalled and no witnesses paid and eleven years of a Reckoner's entry standing behind it.

It went out over the black grit and the flattened smoke and four thousand spears and did not do anything at all. The sea went on. The wind came back across the shingle and took a sheet of carbon off the road and put it into the water.

He had thought the saying of it would be the hard part.

*Eleven years. Four Reckoners. A private note beside the eleventh entry that I have never read and never will.*

*And it is only a word. It has been a word the whole time. I could have said it in the chapel on the first morning and I could have said it in council on any day since and it would have done exactly as much as it has just done, which is nothing, and nobody would have died differently.*

*Corvienne Sorreth will have to strike the withholding now. She will do it tonight, in form, with the entry re-opened and the reason given, and it will be the eleventh thing she has entered about my sister and the first one that is not about my silence.*

*She will be pleased. She has been waiting eleven years to close that page and she has never once asked me for it.*

Ruvaen Aloreth wiped the blade down the outside of his thigh, twice, and turned and walked back down the road toward his own line without looking at anything.

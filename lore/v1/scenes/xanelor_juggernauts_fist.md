# Xanelor — Juggernaut's Fist

Xanelor's palm arrived.

It came through the last hand's width with everything the rising tide had put behind it, the whole weight of his body turning in off the back foot and the pressure still streaming off his shoulders in hard broken planes, and whatever it met would have come apart. A rib cage would have caved and the breastbone gone in with it. The coat on Hiromi's chest was already tearing open before the palm got there, the cloth splitting along its seams under the air shoved ahead of the blow.

It did not meet a rib cage.

Hiromi had not answered the feint. He had done something else with the half-breath it bought him, and done it without moving his hands at all. Out at the edges of the floor, the constellation points Xane had never reached flared at once and pulled inward along their lines. They had been drafted. Now they drafted in turn. A second generation of form came up out of the first in the one place it was needed and set itself in the narrow space between the masked boy's palm and Hiromi's body.

A warden. Broad, faceless, armoured in something pale and translucent, and holding a shield the height of a man.

The palm hit the shield dead centre.

The blow landed on one point of the face and was simply not allowed to stay there. It ran outward, spreading across the whole surface of the shield in rings that raced from centre to rim, so the force that should have gone through one boy's chest went around the edge of a disc, resolving itself across every inch of the face at once. The shield dented inward to the depth of a hand. Its rim buckled. The warden's heels ploughed two long furrows backward through the raked sand.

And it was still not enough.

Xanelor had not only hit it with a hand. The essence that had come up out of him was still pouring down his arm in one long streamlined run, and it went through the shield where the force could not, and it hit the warden and the boy behind the warden together. Hiromi's body was flung back like a ragdoll. The warden went with him, both of them thrown across the ring in a spray of grit, the torn front of his coat flapping open across his chest.

Hiromi reached out without looking and jolted aether out of the air itself.

It came to him from everywhere, pulled in off the sand and the broken wards and the gallery's warm breath, and it caught under his back in a sheet of cold blue-white flame that took his weight a yard above the floor. He stopped falling. He lay there on it, floating on his back, torn coat hanging down through the flame, and looked up at the dome.

"How fun...."

His nose was bleeding properly now, both nostrils, a steady run across his lip and into the corner of his grin.

At the rail, Mu-jin had the bell rope in his fist.

The ward was still cracking. It had crossed a third of the dome and was feeling its way further, bright hairline branches reaching and splitting and reaching again, and a ring with a broken shell was not a ring anybody should be fighting in. He looked at the rope, then at his son lying on a bed of borrowed fire.

He let go of the rope. He laid his open hand flat on the oiled rail and left it there.

Two tiers up, Naori felt the whole room settle. The hairline cracks in the dome stopped spreading. They did not close. They simply stopped, frozen mid-branch, and stayed that way.

Nobody in the gallery could have said who had done it.

Mu-jin's attention was already back on the floor. A second-generation form. The boy had drafted a draft, and every link of that chain had been paid for out of him whether he authorised it or not, and each link carried less holding than the one before it, which was the only reason a single palm had crumpled a shield that size. A third generation would have come up with almost nothing in it.

*Where did you learn that. Nobody taught you that either.*

Xanelor stood where the blow had ended, right arm still out.

The tide was going out of him. He could feel it draining back down whatever deep place it had come up from, and it left a hollow behind it the size of his whole chest, and a shake in both legs that had not been there before. The heel of his right palm had gone raw where it met the shield. The old burn on his left was open again under the split glove.

And he had started it. The Way did not start fights. It waited to be told what shape the fight was, and then it answered the shape, and he had just crossed the ring and put his hand into a boy's chest without being told anything at all. Drogo had said a great many things about that, on a great many mornings.

He did not look up at where the bow had gone.

"Do you concede?" The shell took the edge off his voice and left it level.

On his bed of flame, Hiromi turned his head and looked at him. Then he shifted his form upward, the flame folding under him as his feet found the sand, and he stood.

"No..."

He was upright and swaying slightly. Behind him the dented warden turned translucent, then thinner, and then it came apart into light and ran back into him, poured in through his back and shoulders. He flinched as it arrived. Something in his face went grey and stayed grey. Then he brought his hands together in front of his chest, palm against palm, and aether began to mend around his wrists, closing itself in rings, one ring and then another and another, turning and settling and locking into place like the bands of a cooper's barrel.

Xanelor came forward.

He threw the right hook high and wide at the boy's jaw, a big honest shape of a punch, and it was never going to land and was never meant to. It was meant to lift Hiromi's hands. Behind it he was already stepping in, weight sinking onto the left leg, and the right knee came up and drove forward straight from the hip, aimed at the soft place under the ribs where a body folds.

Hiromi's hands did not go up. His body answered the knee instead, on its own, before he could have decided anything, a twist through the hips that swung him out of its line so the knee drove through air a finger's width from his coat.

It left Xanelor on one leg, his right side open from the hip to the armpit.

"Juggernaut's Fist."

The rings around Hiromi's wrists broke open and mended again into something larger. Essence poured along them and set in a new shape, heavy and plated and spectral, closing over both his hands and forearms to the elbow. They were the gauntlets of a behemoth. Each knuckle-plate was as broad as a man's palm, dark and scarred and pitted along the edges, and when they closed into fists the air around them sagged.

Up at the rail, Mu-jin read it as another borrowed pattern, and read the fault in it at once. A thing that big carried its size. Every fist the boy threw in those would come slower than the one before and leave slower still. Frithia would have told him to bait it out and step around it.

Hiromi drove the right fist forward at the open side, up and under, at the place below the last floating rib where the liver sits.

Xanelor twisted on his standing foot and brought his right forearm down across his body to parry, the only arm he had left that would take a blow, and at the same time he tried to get the other foot back under him and find his footing in the churned sand.

Gauntlet met forearm.

The parry held. The fist was knocked off its line and went past his ribs by a hand's width, and he had kept it off the liver.

Then his forearm got heavier.

It hung from the elbow as if somebody had strapped a flatiron to it, a dead drag pulling the whole arm down toward the sand, and he could not tell whether it was the blow or something the blow had left behind.

Hiromi grinned at him with blood in his teeth.

"Do you wanna know the effect of Juggernaut's Fist? The more strikes I land on you the heavier the aetheric pressure around you grows."

High above them both, the bow came back down out of the dark under the dome, turning end over end as it fell.

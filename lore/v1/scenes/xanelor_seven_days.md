# Xanelor — Seven Days

The scriptia finished arranging itself and left the bracket hanging there in pale lines, empty positions spreading nearly the full width of the slate, the lettering across the top big enough to read from the corridor. Chalk dust settled out of the air where the board had ignited. At the front someone had already begun copying the whole thing into a notebook, nib going fast and badly, and two rows back a pair of students were arguing about seeding in the tone people use when neither of them knows anything yet.

Hiromi rubbed his cheek with the flat of two fingers.

Seven days of training. The word landed in his head wearing the face it always wore, which was the face of a thing that ate mornings and gave nothing back. He had a list of what he would rather do with the week and it was long and every item on it was better.

He kept looking at the board anyway.

Losing was the part he could not get comfortable with. He had no particular opinion about being hurt and less than that about rankings. What sat wrong was the picture of a warded arena, everyone he had grown up around in the gallery, and somebody taking off him the one thing that had never in his life had to be defended.

*Never worked a day for any of it. Going to be a public accounting, that.*

His grin came back, slower than usual, and he let his head tip against the chair.

At the back row, far left, Xanelor had not moved since the board lit.

The recurve bow stood against the wall inside the reach of his left hand, and he had put two fingers on the limb without deciding to. Behind the shell, his jaw worked once. He would need a showing. That much was arithmetic and he had done it in the first four seconds, before the Headmaster finished the sentence.

The trouble sat in what a showing cost. The Sage Arts opened whole in a first match meant six days of thirty prodigies taking them apart at leisure, and they were young and clever and bored, and they would take them apart. A quiet first match meant something worse. It meant the masked one with no Crystal, the one who could not do what any of them did, marked down as the soft bracket by every student who drew him and by every student who did not.

His thumb went over the worn place on the cheek-plane, the way it had gone ten thousand times before.

*Drogo would say the first thing they see is the thing they hunt.*

In the middle rows, Naori had already stopped listening to the room.

He sat the way he sat, upright, shoulders square, the crimson steady in his eyes and giving nothing away about what was underneath it. His father's name was the oldest thing he owned. Niran had not asked him for results, not once, which was the most expensive way a man can ask for them, and Naori intended to hand them over anyway.

Then he turned in his seat toward his sister and the whole calculation changed shape.

Akira's feet had stopped swinging. The pale clouded jade was aimed at the board she could not see, chin lifted, following the room's noise to the place the news had come from.

Seven days. Their mother had trained her in the yard at home, on grass, at a pace their mother controlled, with nobody watching and nobody trying. A warded arena with faculty in the gallery was not that. Somebody was going to hit her on purpose for the first time in her life, and there was no version of the week where Naori talked her out of entering.

He did not let himself finish the other thought, and then he finished it anyway.

She was better than him, and it was not close, and nobody in that family had ever once said it out loud.

"Am I really in it too?" Akira's hands found the edge of her desk. "The whole thing? All of it?"

"You're in it." Naori kept his voice level. "We start tomorrow, first bell. Bring the wrap for your hands."

Her feet started again at roughly twice the rate.

Somewhere behind them a chair complained about a weight it had not been designed for.

Rikudoku's head came up off the desk one degree at a time. Six foot three of thirteen-year-old unfolding out of furniture built for somebody else, white hair flattened on one side and falling black-tipped past his shoulders, eyes opening slowly and blinking slowly and getting no further open than halfway.

"Huh..."

He scratched the back of his head and looked at the board with the expression of a man reading a notice about a road closure.

A tournament sounded like a fight. Fighting had never been the hard part; the hard part came afterward, in the specific quiet that followed a thing going too far. He did not want to hurt anyone in this room. Most of them had not even said their names to him yet.

"If I have to." Rikudoku said this out loud, to nobody, and put his cheek back down on his forearm.

Dabney sat at the back with his hands folded on the desk in front of him.

He had not spoken since the introductions and nothing about him reached past the edge of his own chair, which was seven years of practice and the only lesson Darius had ever repeated. A tournament. That could be very interesting.

*Never show anyone your true power.* Said on the training floor in the Palatinate, said in the corridor, said over meals until it stopped being instruction and became the shape of how he sat in a room. Show it and they would not see a boy who was good. They would start the other conversation, the one with the word Paragon in it, and after that there would be no version of him that anybody at this Academy could look at normally again.

Half, then. Half of what he had would put him deep into the bracket and would look like a talented thirteen-year-old with good instruction behind him, which was the entire point.

He turned his eyes up to the empty positions on the slate and counted along them until he reached the middle, and stopped there, and started again from the top.

# Xanelor — The Dorms

The last sparks went out on the flagstones near the front bench, and the room got its ordinary sound back. Conduit hum behind the granite, the note the whole place sits on. Somebody two rows over had not stopped scratching a nib across paper since the fire column went up. Chalk dust still turned in the window light where the heat had shoved it.

Hiromi had his feet on the floor and nothing else about him upright.

White hair to the collar of his open coat, loose waves the colour of forge-ash before it cools, parted rough down the centre so the two long strands hung forward past his jaw like the legs of a compass. Red eyes, half-lidded and moving anyway, running the room the way a man runs a market stall he already owns. Hands in his pockets. The grin had been sitting in his face since before the headmaster arrived and had not been asked to leave.

Three seats down, Lily's pen stopped. Geturo did not look up from his notebook, which was its own kind of looking.

Hiromi turned his head and looked over to Verona.

"Nah I am good." He let it sit a beat, easy, no heat in it at all. "I'd rather have my own peace, and quiet. I also don't need anyone spreading any weird rumors."

His sister was in this room. His brother was in this room. Somewhere up the gallery there was a cousin he had not finished being surprised about. A fourth pair of eyes behind his own door was not a shortage he was suffering from.

*She'd have my whole week written down by the third night. Worse, she'd be right about most of it.*

At the back row, far left, in the seat by the service corridor, Xanelor looked forward.

He had not turned for the fire and did not turn now. Grey canvas longcoat, heavy, the green cross over the heart stitched by hands that had been shaking when they did it and never restitched since. Deep brown skin. Black-umber hair fallen forward past the temple, which was deliberate, and which covered what ran from there to the jaw. The mask was down over his face, carved shell, the brow-line shaped to suggest a mountain ridge and the cheek-planes worn smooth by a thumb that had gone over them a great many times. A recurve bow leaned against the wall within reach of his left hand. A small copper hammer hung at his throat on a cord.

"I'd also like my own private dorm." The shell flattened his voice and took the edges off it. "So I don't have to wear this mask all the time."

Naori, in the middle rows, heard the sentence arrive in a room with thirty people in it and did not hear anything follow it. He filed that. He could not read it and he did not ask.

Beside him, Akira had been swinging her feet against the desk leg in a rhythm that belonged to no conversation anyone else was having. They stopped.

Pale clouded jade where the pupils should have been, aimed at nothing and confident about it. Her chin came up and around toward her brother's voice the way a compass needle finds north, without hunting for it, because she had known where he was since they sat down and had never once needed to look.

"Big Brother! we can share a dorm......... together..."

The last word went quiet and kept going after the sound of it ran out. Both her hands had found his sleeve.

Naori did not move for a moment. Upright in the seat, shoulders square, the dormant corona sitting at the edge of his dark irises the way it always sat, the least hurried person in a room of people trying hard to be interesting. His father had put her in his hands at the gate. Not in the old register, not formally, not with anything behind it that a Yukari would have called binding, which was somehow worse. He had simply said it and expected it to hold.

Easier, then, if she was behind his door instead of somebody else's.

"Yes I think that'd be fine."

Akira let go of his sleeve, sat back, and began swinging her feet again at roughly twice the previous rate. Something in Naori's jaw did a thing. He was aware that it did a thing.

Down at the front, Mu-jin turned from the board with chalk on the sides of three fingers.

Thirty-eight, and carrying it in the hands rather than the face. Dark hair tied back off a jaw that had spent the better part of twenty-four years being told the writ behind his name had arrived too early. Coat sleeves pushed up past the wrist for the chalk. There was a cup on the bench that had gone cold during the introductions and he had drunk out of it twice since without noticing.

He scratched the back of his head.

"I am a teacher... I don't attend the academy anymore, but I am willing to teach any of you."

He set the chalk down on the rail and clasped his hands behind his back, and the voice went where it went when he meant it, steadier and warmer and slower.

"Be clear about what that is worth, though. I can't spar. I've never been able to spar. Put a blade in my hand and I am the least dangerous person in this building, and Class S would not need a second exchange to establish it." A small shrug, one shoulder. "What I can do is look at a thing you built and tell you which part of it is going to kill you, and roughly when. Most of you will find that less exciting than a duel. A few of you will still be alive in ten years."

He looked along the rows, unhurried, the way a man reads a formula he has read before and is checking anyway.

"Class S, Class X. Nobody has ever been saved by a designation. Come and find me after, and bring whatever you are already working on, and I will start from there."

He picked the chalk back up and turned to the board and wrote a single word across the top of it, large enough for the back row.

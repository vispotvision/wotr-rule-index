*The Ring at Stannvaard, a loft above Ostler's wire-drawing works. Deep winter, the second bell of the night.*

[narrating, low and gritty] The stair smelled like somebody had set fire to a library and put it out with vinegar.

Jem Haskett went up it one tread at a time with his back to the wall, the way you go up any stair in the Ring, and breathed through his mouth. Chemical burn, sharp enough to sting the back of the throat. Hot copper. Scorched paper. Under all of it the wire-drawing works below them thumped through the boards, the big die-bench taking another pull of copper rod through its holes, and the draw-hum came up the stair with it and sat in his teeth. Snow had come in under the street door behind them and was melting on the treads in grey lumps from Sigrid's boots.

He had the paper in his coat. He did not need to look at it. *Wanted, for theft from Halberd and Sons, factors, of the Ring: one goblin, tinker, female, called Grisk Vreshen. The property taken: one master key to a pressure-lock of her own make. Five silver marks on delivery of the key. Ten with the goblin.*

Ten silver was the rent on the room in Coldharbour Lane until the thaw, with something over for coal. He had done the sum on the walk.

At the top of the stair was a door. Somebody had painted a sign on it in Common, small, very neat: *KNOCK. THEN WAIT. THEN GO AWAY.*

Sigrid came up beside him with her breath smoking. She was taller than him by a hand and wider by more, and she wore the double-coat of the north over Accord wool, sealskin over felt, fastened at the left shoulder so the right arm hung free. Hair like old rope, cropped short under a felt cap. A broad flat face, wind-burned across the cheekbones, the nose broken flat at some point and left. She carried a short-hafted axe through a loop at her belt and an iron bar in her hand. She had brought the bar for the door.

"[weathered Northern English accent, low] Smells like a fire waiting," she said.

"[rough London working-class accent, low] Smells like money waiting." [narrating, low and gritty] He knocked.

Nothing. Then a sound on the far side, very close to the wood, a dry little rattle like a dice cup.

"[quick, clipped Hungarian accent] Two kha-urrim on my stair." The voice came through the door at the height of his belt. Fast and thin, every r gone soft. "One smells of seal fat and one smells of the Lane. Read the door."

"[rough London working-class accent] I read it. Open up, Grisk, Halberd's want their key back and they've sent me to fetch it."

"[quick, clipped Hungarian accent] Halberd's want." [narrating, low and gritty] The voice laughed, a short dry crack. "[quick, clipped Hungarian accent] Halberd's always want. Halberd's took a lock from me, twelve weeks' cutting, paid for nine. Took the lock. Took the door it went on. Took the three weeks." A pause. "[quick, clipped Hungarian accent, slower] Nakh. You know nakh, Lane-man?"

"[rough London working-class accent] I know ten silver."

"[quick, clipped Hungarian accent, quiet] Then you know nothing at all."

[narrating, low and gritty] Sigrid caught his eye and shook her head once. She had worked the Kharven roads before the Accord paid better, and she said goblins on the north road went quiet before they went for you. This one had gone quiet.

*Bluffing,* Jem thought. *Little thing behind a door, talking big. Everyone talks big behind a door.*

"[weathered Northern English accent] Come out, lass," Sigrid said. "We'll walk you down nice and slow, and nobody needs hurting for a key."

"[quick, clipped Hungarian accent] Nurr."

[narrating, low and gritty] Jem did not know the word. He knew the shape of it.

Sigrid set the iron bar into the gap by the lock and leaned.

[door splintering] The door went in on the second heave, and the first thing that came through it was a wire.

It was strung at shin height across the frame, so fine he never saw it. Sigrid's boot took it. Somewhere in the dark a weight dropped, and a shelf came off the wall in a long tumbling crash of tins and jars and coils of wire, [metal clattering] and something glass broke and the vinegar smell doubled, and Sigrid went down on one knee in it, swearing in her own tongue.

[narrating, fast and tense] The loft was long and low under the roof-beams, lit by one red brazier at the far end. Benches down both sides. Things on every bench, half made: brass housings, springs, glass bulbs strung on wire, a jointed arm with no body. Shadows everywhere. Jem came through the door low with his fists up, left forward, chin down, the way old Mother Tallis had taught him behind the Pelican for a copper a lesson.

He did not see her, only heard her: bare feet on boards, quick, to his left.

Something came out of the dark at his face and he slipped it by instinct, head off the line, and it went past his ear and struck the doorpost with a flat chunk. A wooden mallet, thrown hard. He turned toward where it came from and she was already gone.

"[quick, clipped Hungarian accent, from the dark] Big Lane-man. Slow Lane-man. Weightless."

[narrating, fast and tense] There. A shape low along the right-hand bench, small as a child and nothing like one. She came up into the brazier's red for half a breath, and he saw her.

Three and a half feet of her, maybe, built crooked. The left side was narrow and quick. The right arm was thicker than the left from shoulder to wrist and ridged with raised scar-lines in a pattern, cut-runes worked into the skin and healed over shiny, the hand on the end of it broad as a man's. Skin the grey-green of verdigris on an old roof. A big head for the body, bald but for a tight black knot of hair at the crown stuck through with a brass pin. Ears long and swept back and notched at the edges. A leather apron gone stiff and black with burns, a tool-belt crossed over it hung with pliers and an awl and a little hammer. And the eyes. They caught the brazier's light in a dozen small pieces each, like a fly's, amber split into facets, and he could not tell where they were looking.

She had a soldering pot in her big right hand, the iron ladle kind, and it was smoking.

"[weathered Northern English accent, sharp] Jem, the pot!"

[narrating, fast and tense] Grisk threw it underhand along the bench, and the molten tin came out of it in a bright sheet.

Jem got his head out of the way. Sigrid, coming up off her knee behind him with her arm raised against the light, did not. The tin hit her forearm. It spattered up the sleeve, and the felt of the double-coat took most of it and smoked. What came through at the cuff took the skin off the back of the forearm above the wrist bone.

"[weathered Northern English accent, pained] [grunts] Frozen piss!"

[narrating, fast and tense] She did not drop the bar. She shook the arm once, hard, and the hot metal flew off in silver beads and hissed on the floor, and Jem smelled cooked wool and something under it that was worse.

He went down the right-hand bench after the goblin.

She let him come. That was the first thing he read, too late. She backed along the bench at his pace, not faster, and when he threw the jab she was not there, she had dropped flat under it and gone through between the bench legs like a cat through a fence, and come up on the other side with the awl out of her belt. The awl went into the back of his left hand where it rested on the bench as he leaned, between the second and third metacarpals. Not deep. It grated on bone and stopped and she pulled it out again before he felt it.

Then he felt it.

"[rough London working-class accent] [gasps] Bitch!"

"[quick, clipped Hungarian accent] Kha-ur. Hand of kha." [narrating, fast and tense] She was already moving again. "[quick, clipped Hungarian accent] Weightless hand."

[narrating, fast and tense] Blood ran warm between his knuckles. He shook it out and put the hand back up because there was nothing else to do with it, and he watched her this time instead of chasing.

She went left, as she always went left. Every move she had made she had made off the right foot, pushing off the heavy side and turning on the light one, so she turned to her left and came round. It was the arm. The arm was too heavy for the frame and she had built her whole way of moving around carrying it.

*Left,* he thought. *Next time she goes, she goes left.*

At the far end of the loft she stopped by the brazier, and picked something up off the bench beside it, and held it out at arm's length in the big right hand.

[humming] [narrating, slower, tense] It was a glass jar the size of a man's head, stoppered with brass and bound in copper wire, and something moved inside it, pale and slow, like milk poured into water that would not settle. It hummed, a low steady note under the draw-hum, and the note was rising.

"[quick, clipped Hungarian accent, quiet, slow] Drakh," Grisk said. "You know drakh, Lane-man? Ground moving under a standing thing." She did not raise her voice, but every word had got slower and longer, held a beat past where it should end. "Pressure jar, my make. Twelve weeks I cut a lock for Halberd's and this I cut for me. You break it, the floor goes, the works go, the Lane-man goes." She tilted her big head. "Seal-fat goes."

"[rough London working-class accent] You're lying."

"[quick, clipped Hungarian accent, quiet] Goblins don't lie, kha-ur. We got no words soft enough."

[narrating, slower, tense] He did not know if that was true. It sounded like the kind of thing a liar would say. The jar hummed. Her faceted eyes held the brazier in fifty red pieces and gave nothing back.

Behind him he heard Sigrid moving away from the goblin, toward the end wall, where the brazier's light did not reach, where a pair of wooden shutters stood closed over a window.

"[weathered Northern English accent, quiet, to Jem] Keep her talking."

[narrating, fast and tense] *Keep her talking.* He could do that. He had kept drunks and debtors and two murderers talking in his time.

"[rough London working-class accent] Twelve weeks, you said. What's a lock take twelve weeks for?"

"[quick, clipped Hungarian accent] Pressure-lock. Opens on the main. Shuts when the main drops. Can't be picked, can't be forced, can't be shot." [narrating, slower, tense] The jar hummed higher. Her right arm, holding it out, had begun to shake, the fine fast shake of a muscle held too long. "[quick, clipped Hungarian accent, slow] Best lock in the Ring. Halberd's put it on their strong-room. Halberd's took the key off my bench while I slept, Lane-man, and wrote I stole it." She held the vowel. "Stoooole."

"[rough London working-class accent] That's not what the paper says."

"[quick, clipped Hungarian accent, very quiet] Paper." [narrating, slower, tense] She said it the way you would say *dung*.

[wind howling] [narrating, fast and tense] Sigrid threw the shutters open.

The winter came in all at once. A white blast of it, snow and wind off the roofs of the Ring, the air on the far side of the window cold enough to burn the inside of the nose. It went down the loft in one long breath and the brazier's coals flared red and then went dull, and every jar and bulb on the benches frosted at the edges, and the cold hit Jem across the back of the neck like a slap.

Grisk flinched from it. And then she was slow.

Anybody would have seen it; it was like watching someone through water. She turned her head toward the window and the head came round late, a fraction behind where it should have been, and her mouth opened to say something and the word came late too, and the heavy right arm with the jar in it sagged a finger's width and came back up late, all of her a beat behind her own will.

*Stone-blood,* Sigrid had told him once on the north road, when they had watched an Accord sergeant beat a hobgoblin in a frozen yard. *It runs slow in the deep cold. They're all there. They just get there after you.*

He went in.

He went for the jar. He came across the last three yards with his right hand open and his left fist low, and she saw him coming and moved the jar away from him, left, of course left, and the move came late, and he was already where the jar was going. He got his open right hand under it and closed it round the copper wire and held it.

[narrating, fast and tense] It was warm. It was humming in his palm like a hive.

Grisk's big right hand stayed on the jar. Her other hand, the light quick one, came up with the awl. And he hit her with the left. A short hook, all hip, the hand that she had already put a hole in, and it landed on her temple just above the swept-back ear, on the thin bone there, with a sound like a mallet on a cask, and the edge of the ear split against his knuckles. [heavy thud]

Her knees went. The faceted eyes went out of focus in fifty directions and the awl fell and rang on the boards. She did not let go of the jar. She folded down onto the floor with her big hand still wrapped round it and his hand still under it, and for a moment the two of them knelt there in the cold with the thing between them humming, and he could feel through the glass that her hand was shaking, and the humming was climbing with the shaking.

"[weathered Northern English accent, urgent] Jem, don't. Let her be."

"[rough London working-class accent] I've got it."

"[weathered Northern English accent, urgent] You've got nothing. Look at her hands, and then look at the jar."

[narrating, slower, tense] He looked. The pale stuff in the jar had stopped drifting. It was going round now, slowly, in a ring, and the ring was tightening every time the goblin's hand shook, and her hand was shaking harder now, not from the cold. Her chest was going in and out fast and shallow. Sweat stood out on the bald grey scalp in beads despite the cold, and the long notched ears had gone flat back against her skull.

*It's her,* he thought. *She's the one doing it.*

He did not know if that was true either. But he took his hand away from the glass, slowly, and set it on his knee where she could see it.

"[rough London working-class accent, quieter] All right," he said. "All right, Grisk, nobody's touching it."

[narrating, quieter] The ring in the jar went round, and round, and slowed.

Sigrid came across the loft and shut the shutters halfway, so the cold came in a thinner blade. Then she crouched on the goblin's other side, keeping her burned arm against her chest, and pulled the iron bar across her knees and did nothing with it.

"[weathered Northern English accent, gentle] Breathe, lass," she said. "By the fire, you're all right. Breathe."

[narrating, quieter] It took a long time. The brazier crept back toward red. The draw-hum came up through the floor from the works and steadied. And in the goblin's hand the jar went down from a hum to a mutter to a sound like someone thinking, and the milk inside it lay down at the bottom and was still.

Grisk set it on the boards in front of her knees, very carefully, with both hands.

Then she put the narrow hand to the split ear, looked at the blood on her fingers, and said something in her own tongue that was all r and no vowels, and spat.

"[rough London working-class accent, tired] I'll want the key," Jem said.

"[quick, clipped Hungarian accent, flat] Nurr."

"[rough London working-class accent] I'll want it anyway."

[narrating, quieter] She looked at him, or he thought she did; with those eyes you could not tell.

Then she reached behind her on the bench, without looking, the way you reach for a thing that has lived in the same place for years, and brought down a piece of board the length of her forearm. Pale wood, planed smooth. Along one edge ran a column of cuts, each a little stem with nicks to one side or the other, and some of the stems had a cut straight across them, and some did not.

"[quick, clipped Hungarian accent, slower] Read," she said, and held it out to him.

"[rough London working-class accent] I can't read that."

"[weathered Northern English accent] I can." [narrating, quieter] Sigrid took the board in her good hand and turned it to the brazier. Jem watched her face. She had grown up in a country where nobody wrote anything down and every woodpile was counted by the notch, and she read the cuts the way she read a tally stick, with her thumb, one at a time.

"[weathered Northern English accent, slow] It's a count," she said. "Weeks, I think. Twelve stems, each with its mark." Her thumb went down the edge. "Nine crossed through, and crossed is paid, I'd say, same as ours." Her thumb stopped. "Three not crossed."

"[quick, clipped Hungarian accent] Three weeks." [narrating, quieter] Grisk held up three fingers of the big hand. "Three weeks I cut for them and they did not brakh. Nakh. They took." She put the hand flat on the board. "Their paper says I stole and this says they did. Mine is cut, kha-ur. Yours is ink, and ink washes."

[narrating, quieter] Jem sat back on his heels. The hole in his hand had begun to throb in time with his heart. Ten silver. Coldharbour Lane until the thaw, and coal. And on the other side of the reckoning, a goblin with a split ear and a board with three cuts on it that nobody in the Accord could read, and a factor's paper that every magistrate in the Ring would read the moment he set it on the table.

He knew how that went. He had carried a hundred papers into that room.

*She's lying,* he thought, and he did not believe it, and he tried to anyway, for ten silver, and could not make it stick.

"[weathered Northern English accent, quiet] Jem."

"[rough London working-class accent] I know."

"[weathered Northern English accent] My arm's cooked."

"[rough London working-class accent] [sighs] I know, Sig."

[narrating, quieter] He looked at the goblin.

"[rough London working-class accent] Here's what I've got," he said. "I've got a paper that says you're a thief, and a magistrate who'll believe it, and five silver for the key if I bring it, and ten if I bring you. And I've got a partner with half her arm cooked, and a hole in my hand, and the rent owing." He held up the bleeding hand. "I'm taking the key. If I don't take it, Halberd's send someone who'll take the key and you both, and he won't sit on the floor and talk about it."

"[quick, clipped Hungarian accent, quiet] Nakh," said Grisk.

"[rough London working-class accent] No." [narrating, quieter] He reached into his coat, past the paper, to the purse. There were four coppers in it and the price of a meal. He took out all four and laid them on her board, on the three uncut stems, one by one. [coins clinking] "[rough London working-class accent] Brakh. That's the word? Bearing the cost?"

[narrating, quieter] The faceted eyes moved. Down to the coppers, he thought, and back up.

"[quick, clipped Hungarian accent, slow] Four copper," she said, "for three weeks."

"[rough London working-class accent] It's what there is. The rest when Halberd's pay me." He heard himself say it and knew it was the stupidest thing he had said all winter. "I'm good for it."

"[quick, clipped Hungarian accent] Kha-urrim are never good for it."

"[rough London working-class accent] Then cut it on your board and come and find me."

[narrating, low and gritty] Grisk looked at him for a long moment with her head on one side. Then she reached into the tool-belt and brought out a little knife, a short blade worn thin with honing, and held the board flat on her knee with the heavy hand.

She cut a new stem under the twelve. One straight stroke, then two nicks to the right of it, then one to the left, quick and certain, the blade biting clean into the pale wood. She did not cross it through.

"[quick, clipped Hungarian accent, quiet] Lane-man," she said, and blew the shavings off it, and held it up so he could see his debt, cut into the wood where no ink could wash it. Then she reached under the bench and dropped the brass key into his bleeding hand.

## Author notes

- **New in this piece (originated, pending Isaac's word):** Jem Haskett, thief-taker of the Ring (Accord commons, boxer taught "behind the Pelican"); Sigrid, Kharven former road-rider working for the Accord; Grisk Vreshen, Tinker goblin locksmith; Halberd and Sons, factors; Ostler's wire-drawing works; Coldharbour Lane; the pressure-lock and the pressure jar.
- **Canon carried:** Old Vaross: *kha-urrim* (humans, the weightless ones), *nakh* and *brakh* as one concept split in half, *drakh* (fear, ground moving under a standing thing), *nurr* (standalone refusal); emphasis by holding the vowel; rage gets quieter and slower; the Goblin dialect rapid and contracted with the syllabic r often lost; no adjectives, so every insult is an accusation ("hand of kha"); Vaross writing cut into objects with a single blade, a document being a thing and not a page. Tinker Goblins: small, asymmetric, a reinforced limb with spliced runes, faceted eyes, the smell of chemical burn, solder and scorched paper; fragile; the Emotional Detonation risk, here shown as the jar answering her panic. Stone-Blood running a beat late in deep cold, used as the counter.
- **POV and irony:** Jem, close third, Accord commons. He reads the jar as a bluff; Sigrid reads it as real; the scene never settles which, and the jar only quiets when Grisk does.
- **Combat audit:** boxing school for Jem (jab, slip, hook), plain body language for the rest. The deciding read is shown twice before it pays: Grisk always turns left off the heavy right arm. The cold is planted in the weather and paid off by the shutters.
- **Wounds:** Sigrid, molten solder through the cuff of the double-coat onto the forearm, a deep partial-thickness burn needing dressing. Jem, an awl puncture through the back of the left hand between the second and third metacarpals, grating on bone, bleeding but not disabling; he still throws the hook with it. Grisk, a hook to the temple with the ear split against the knuckles, brief loss of focus; her panic shows as sweat and flattened ears.
- **Lie:** Halberd and Sons' paper claims theft; Grisk's board shows three weeks unpaid. Her line "goblins don't lie, we got no words soft enough" is her own reading of her language, not a narrator's claim.
- **Open thread:** Jem owes Grisk the balance of three weeks' pay, cut on her board; Halberd and Sons still hold the theft claim.

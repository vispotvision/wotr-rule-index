*Naori Yukari · the arena floor, inside the incandescent field*

---

It is just as he expected.

He saw it in the threads before the square went up. That is the part nobody in the stands will ever know. He laid the Room That Stills across the storm with the Inzensho already running ahead of it, and at the end of half the threads it showed him, the square came apart, well made as it was, because she would break it, and because breaking a held thing does what breaking always does: it lets go. A held storm released does not fall inward. It goes out. And a thing that goes out is a wave, and a wave is the one thing in the world he has spent his life learning to turn around.

So he let her break it.

He pays for that now.

The light is on him. It came up out of the sand in a pillar and bent down over him and pressed, and it is pressing still, white and enormous, the purified light of her Thirty-Fifth chain burning on every surface of his body at once. It does not feel like fire. Fire has edges, a tongue that licks and pulls back. This has no edges. It is a weight made of brightness, and it lies on him the way deep water lies on a diver, from every side, so that his ribs creak under it and two of them on the left have already given with a sound he felt in his teeth. Every breath is a short sharp stab under the arm. He is still on his feet because the light presses from above as hard as from the sides, and there is nowhere for his knees to fold to.

His eyes are full of it. The world is white, white with a blue edge, and the afterimage of the pillar's first flash is burned across the middle of his sight in a long purple bar that will not go away. He cannot see the sand. He cannot see her. He does not need to. The Inzensho does not look with the eyes the way the eyes look; it reads the threads through them, and the threads run on behind the white like fish under a frozen pond, dark shapes moving forward under the glare.

And his wounds are open again.

He felt **[Stg]** burn. Her disruption came through the stillness like a hot iron through wax, and the Stagnate he had laid over the lye in his chest went up in the same breath as the Cycle and the Return, and the debt he signed came due exactly where he left it. The summon's blood is eating again. He can feel it go down into the muscle over his breastbone with the same slow terrible push as before, because for the burn no time has passed at all. Along his jaw the grey stillness has cracked off and the raw meat underneath is weeping into the collar of his jacket. The four feather wounds are open. The iron grit in them is hot from the light. His blood runs from all of them and boils a little where it beads on his skin, small angry hisses he can hear even under the hum.

The hum is the worst of it. That surprises him, a little, in some far back room of himself that is still taking notes. Worse than the light. Worse than the ribs.

It is her disruption, left standing. The static of it fills the field around him like a swarm of bees inside a bell, a dense crackling drone at a pitch just off his own. Every time he reaches into the Cymorath it is there, jangling against the note he tries to hold, shaking it out of true the way a hand on a struck string shakes a chord into noise. It is built for exactly that. She has learned his voice in one exchange and built a cage for it. He is, as the last line of her working said in every tone it made, right back where he started: standing in a field of incandescent light, with nothing in his voice that will hold.

*Paru, Thirty-Five.* He heard her say it before the pillar rose. He heard it through the roar of the breaking storm the way a man hears his own name across a crowded market, because it was nearly his own words in another mouth. The carvers' habit. Chains kept by number, reached for by number, because a number is faster than a name. Somewhere under the burns something in him almost smiles, and the almost is the whole of it. His father would have liked that. His father would have said that a person who numbers her chains has built each of them more than once, and must be fought as if she has already seen every answer you have.

"Thirty-five," he says. His voice comes out of a cooked throat as a rasp, and the light swallows it. "You count yours too."

He listens to the hum anyway.

*See the end.*

He lets the threads run. He sees the light finish what it began. He sees himself on the sand with his chest burned through and the stands silent. He sees, at the end of another thread, a boy with crimson eyes thrown backward out of the white like a doll. He lets them show him.

Then he comes back, to the middle, and listens.

---

The hum is not one sound.

It took him a breath to hear it, and a breath is long in this light, but it is true. Her disruption is a drone made of two tones lying very close together, two pitches a hair apart, and two notes that close never sit still against each other. They beat. He learned that as a boy, before he learned to read a single glyph: Elder Tae tuning the temple's two bronze bowls before the lantern festival, striking both and listening, and the sound swelling and fading, swelling and fading, *wah, wah, wah,* as the two pitches drifted into step and out of it. When they were in step they made one loud note. When they were out of step they cancelled, crest on trough, and for a moment there was silence, real silence, in the middle of two ringing bowls.

The hum has silences in it.

They are short. They come at a rhythm, a hundred times in a breath perhaps, a ticking stillness running under the drone where her two tones fall out of step and cancel each other to nothing. The static is not a wall. It is a wall with gaps in it, and the gaps come at a beat.

Naori has been reading beats since he could walk.

He lets the Inzensho find the next gap before it opens, and the next, and the one after that, the way it found the crests of her storm. He does not try to hold a note through the hum. He speaks into the silences. One glyph, broken into a hundred small pieces, each piece set down into a gap where her disruption has cancelled itself out and there is nothing to shake it. A carver laying inscription points between the cracks in a flawed stone.

The glyph is **[Cro]**. Cycle. The same root of Valen's she burned out of him a breath ago. A burned working is broken, but the glyph itself is not; it is a word, and a word can always be said again. He says it into the gaps, and the Cymorath carries it on the light that is crushing him.

That light is not his. He knows that. It is hers, purified in her own chain, poured out of her own Crystal and her summon's, and it is the loudest thing in the arena. But it is in him now, the way her storm was in him and her feathers were in him: he is drowning in it, and his Shell is still turned inward, and Kussetsu is still bending whatever breaks against him. He bends the light. It goes into him searing and it comes out on the inside as Essence, and it still remembers where it came from. It still sings in her dialect, glass and fire, Luminalis and Exuroth. Karine, the borrowed note: he carries the Cycle on her light.

And then he turns her light around.

---

Her Thirty-Fifth chain did something simple and enormous. It took everything the collapse of her storm released, the entropy, the broken Essence, the shattered feathers, the rupture and the disruption and the light, and it gathered all of it into a single point, and then it let it go. Upward. Outward. A pillar of light, and a blast of it spreading from that pillar over him and across the field. Everything that went into that point came out of it as an outgoing wave, expanding, spreading, every part of it running away from where it started.

He has turned an outgoing wave around before. He did it to her field on the first exchange. Makimodoshi: catch the race at its middle, clap, and every runner turns and comes home.

This is that, and it is the opposite of that.

At the first exchange he turned her chain back along its own length to its caster. Now he does not send the wave home along the road it came. He folds it in on itself. The light pouring out of the place where her chain gathered everything is an expanding sphere, a shell of brightness swelling outward like a soap bubble blown from a pipe, and he does not reverse the shell's travel. He reverses its sign. Its pressure. Where it pushes out, he makes it pull in. The Cycle goes into the silences of her hum and into the shell of her light with it, and every outward push in that light becomes an inward pull, all at once, everywhere on the shell, the way a soap bubble would fall in on itself if every bit of its skin suddenly wanted to be at its centre.

His father would call it turning a thing inside out. The carvers in the valley have a word for the way a badly fired pot sometimes collapses inward in the kiln instead of bursting. *Uragaeshi.* Inside out. That is what he names it.

The light caves in.

He hears it. It is the strangest sound he has ever made happen. The roar of the outgoing blast drops, all at once, into a long rising whistle, like air rushing into a jar when the stopper is pulled, except that the whistle is made of light and the jar is the size of the field, and every edge of the brightness around him is suddenly rushing past him the other way. The pressure on his body reverses. The weight that was crushing his ribs into his lungs lifts and becomes a pull, a hard sucking drag toward a point a dozen paces in front of him, so strong that his heels plough two short furrows in the scorched sand before he sets them. His jacket snaps forward. The loose black strands of his hair stream ahead of his face. The light slides off him, off his burned skin and his open wounds and his bloody sleeves, and goes rushing away from every side toward the place it came from.

The place where she gathered it. Her singularity. The point at the heart of her Thirty-Fifth chain.

All of the light goes there. The whole expanding shell of it, every ray of it that was spreading across the field, folds back in on that point together, faster and faster as it falls, the way water falls faster the nearer it comes to a drain. And as it falls inward it piles up on itself, denser at every step, the brightness of a whole sky squeezed into the space of a room and then a cart and then a barrel and then a man.

It stops there. A man's height across. A sphere.

He named that too, long before he ever saw it, from a thing he watched once as a child in a bowl of water. A bubble trapped in the water by the sound of a ringing bowl will swell and shrink in time with the ringing, and on the shrink, if the sound is hard enough, it falls in on itself so fast that for an instant it shines. A tiny cold flash of blue in the bottom of a bowl. Sound becoming light. He called it a crying bubble, then, being six. He calls this one **Hibiki-awa**, the Ringing Bubble.

It hangs above the sand a dozen paces in front of him, a sphere of light so dense it has stopped looking like light. It looks like glass. It looks like a pearl the size of a man, white and blue and very faintly gold, turning on itself in slow ripples, and it rings. He can hear it under everything, a deep clean note, his own Cycle sounding at the heart of all her light, steady, a pulse.

And around it, time thickens.

It is only a little. It is barely anything. But he can feel it with the part of himself that has counted the threads of things since he could walk: inside the bubble's reach the threads move at the Cycle's beat and not quite at the world's. A fraction slower. A twentieth, perhaps. The heat-shimmer rising from the sand near it moves as if the air had turned to oil. Two feathers of her iron, still tumbling loose from the broken storm, drift through the edge of the bubble's ring and slow there, visibly, their spin dragging before they pass out the other side and quicken again. It is nothing like the Covenant. The Covenant held an instant. This only leans on time, the way a heavy man leans on a door. But in a fight decided by the width of a hair, a twentieth is a great deal of hair.

Everything she poured out is in that bubble now. All of it. The entropy, the Essence, the light, the rupture, packed into a man's height of ringing glass.

He has a heartbeat, perhaps two, before it starts to come apart.

---

He moves.

The wounds tear as he goes. He feels the flank open wider, the shoulder grind, the two broken ribs shift against each other with a wet crunch like boots on rotten ice. The burns on his chest pull and split. None of it is the point. He goes forward off his right foot so hard that the sand under it sprays behind him in a fan, and the second step comes before the first has finished landing, and the third, low, fast, leaning so far into the run that he is nearly falling and only his feet arriving under him again and again keep him off the ground. Three strides. Four. The dozen paces close in less than a breath. It is the oldest thing in a swordsman's body, older than any glyph, and his father drilled it into him on the terrace with a rope tied round his waist and an apprentice hauling back on the other end: *Go before you have decided to go. If you decide, you are late.*

He goes into the bubble's ring and the world slows by its twentieth and he does not.

That is the gift of it. He set the beat. The Cycle at the heart of the bubble is his own voice, and inside its reach he moves on his own beat while everything else moves on the bubble's, so that for the two strides he crosses inside the ring, the world is a hair late and he is not.

From the stands it must look like a trick of the eye. A boy who was standing burned and bent in a field of white light is suddenly a dozen paces nearer the heart of the arena, and the eye cannot find the strides in between, because inside the bubble's ring those strides were taken on a beat the eye was not keeping. Somewhere high in the tiers a man shouts. A child laughs, sharp and frightened, the way children laugh at a conjurer who has done something they did not want to be possible.

The katana comes up off his hip in both hands, the right hand weak where the forearm is torn, the left hand doing the work, the long edge turned toward the bubble.

And he cuts into it.

He does not cut it apart. A cut that split the bubble would only let it all go, everywhere, at once, like stabbing a waterskin. He cuts *into* it, the point first, the blade turning in his hands as it goes, twisting. The steel turns over on its long axis like a key in a lock, once, twice, and then the whole sword is spinning in a tight hard circle at the end of his arms, the point drawing small fast rings in the heart of the light, and the light comes with it.

It winds onto the blade the way thread winds onto a spindle.

He feels it go. The light of the bubble begins to turn around the steel, slowly at first and then faster, a spiral of brightness wrapping itself round the blade from the point toward the guard, coil on coil, twisting as it winds. It is not only turning. The light itself is twisting along its own length, the way a rope twists when it is laid, every ray of it turning a corkscrew as it travels, so that what wraps round his sword is not a glow but a drill. A helix of light. Her light, wound in on itself, spinning on the steel, hissing where it touches the blood still on the blade and boiling that blood to a fine red smoke.

He learned that from his mother, too, of all things, watching her spin thread at the kitchen window when he was small. *If you only pull it, it breaks,* Mira said. *Twist it as you pull, and it holds anything.* He calls this one **Ito-yori**. Thread-twisting.

The bubble shrinks as he winds it. It shrinks to a cart's width, a barrel's, a man's head, the brightness of it pouring onto the sword in a long screaming spiral, and the sword grows heavier with every turn, not in his arms but in his Crystal, as if he were winding the weight of a whole storm onto a single spool. His arms shake. His Threadburn comes back so hard behind his left eye that for a moment the white of the light goes red at the edges. Blood runs from his nose down over his lip and into his mouth. He keeps turning the blade.

The last of the bubble goes onto the steel.

And the katana in his hands is a bar of twisting light as long as his arm and shoulder together, screaming, ringing, too bright to look at, every scrap of what her Thirty-Fifth chain poured out wound tight around it.

---

He has her light. Now he gives it back.

A blade of light that size thrown at a body would only be a weapon, and weapons can be met by weapons, blocked, dodged, turned. So he aims past the body, at the place the light came from.

Every working has a source. Her light did not come out of the air. It came out of a Crystal, out of the Essence Core at the heart of her and out through the channels that run from that core through the body like roots through soil, the way his own do. Every scrap of that light still carries her dialect, and every ray of it still knows the road it came by. The light wound round his sword is, in the oldest sense, hers. It belongs to the place it left. And there is a glyph whose whole work is to send a thing back to the place it belongs.

He reaches the last stride and plants his foot and speaks it into a gap in her hum.

"Vor."

**[Vor]**. Return. Auren's root, the Still Gate's own word, the glyph the burial rites use to send Essence back where it came from so that nothing wanders. Its law in the Codex is the law of moving water. He has always thought of it that way: as a river let back into its own bed.

He turns the twisting light around on his sword and sends it home.

It leaves the blade.

It does not fly. It goes the way water goes when a sluice is opened upstream: all at once, in one direction, down the only road it knows. The light comes off the steel in a single violent unwinding and runs back along every thread it ever travelled, back along the line of her Thirty-Fifth chain, back toward the hands that shaped it and the Crystal that poured it, and it goes back not as light but as Essence returning. All of it. In one surge.

A river let back into its bed slowly fills it. A river let back all at once, through a narrow channel, at the speed of a sluice, does something else. Every miller in the valley knows what it does. When the mill race is slammed shut too fast, the water already running in the flume has nowhere to go and stops all at once, and the stopping throws a hammer-blow of pressure back up the flume that can split oak staves and burst iron bands. They call it the race's kick. The bigger the flow and the faster it stops, the harder the kick.

Everything she poured out of herself in one chain is coming back down her channels toward her core in less time than it took to leave.

He feels it leave through his own hands. That is the part the rites never mention. Returning water passes through whatever lies between it and its bed, and the first thing it passes through is the one who opened the sluice. It goes out of the blade and up his arms and through his own channels on its way home, every scrap of her light dragging at the roots of him as it passes, and for one long moment he is a pipe with a river in it. Every old crack in him sings. The burns on his chest flare white. The broken ribs grind. The torn forearm opens a finger's width further and his weak right hand comes off the grip entirely, and he holds the blade up through the last of it with his left alone.

That is what he has sent. The working is not built to cut skin or break bone. It goes past skin and bone the way a reflection goes past glass. It is built to run up the lines of a Crystal, along the channels her own Essence opened, and to strike the Essence Core at the end of them all at once with everything that core gave away. A working built like that, aimed at a Crystal, can split one along its lines. Whether it does is not something his eyes will tell him yet. Whether it reaches her core at all, whether she has a gate to throw open or a summon to bleed it into or some answer he has not seen, is hers.

He named it a long time ago, the way he names everything, alone. **Kaeri-mizu.** Returning water.

He says one more thing, quietly, into the last gap in her hum, as the light goes.

"It's yours," Naori says. "I'm only giving it back."

---

What does not go home goes into the ground.

No surge of that size runs clean. The Return takes what knows its road, and what is left over, the broken entropy of the collapse, the light too scattered to remember where it came from, the rupture that tore loose in the winding, has nowhere to go but down. It goes into the arena floor under the place where the bubble hung, and the arena floor stops being sand.

It melts.

He sees the sand go dark first, then red, then a bright sullen orange, spreading out from under the spot in a ring, and then it slumps and runs and is not sand at all but a pool of liquid stone, glowing, smoking, the grains of a thousand rakings flowing together into one shining skin. The heat comes off it in a wall. Sand melts at a heat that turns iron to soup. To turn a pace-wide pit of it to glass a hand's depth down takes a great deal of power, and there is a great deal of power in the ground: more than a carved ward would hold in a year of standing. The smell is of hot slag and scorched stone and, under it, sharp and clean, the smell of lightning.

And then the pressure comes up out of it.

The heat dumped into the floor heats the air above it all at once, and air heated that fast does not rise. It explodes. A shockwave rolls out of the melting sand in a ring, a wall of hammered air, visible as a ripple running out across the arena floor, throwing up a skin of sand ahead of it in a long brown curling crest. It goes out in every direction. It slams into Naori from behind and shoves him a stride forward, his jacket cracking like a sail. It rolls on past him. The lamp flames along the arena wall go out all at once in a long row. The timber of the near stands groans. A sound like a great door slammed somewhere under the world comes up through the soles of his boots.

It rolls on toward where she stands.

It is a wall of air and sand with the weight of a falling house behind it, driving outward toward the arena wall, hard enough and fast enough to pick a body off the ground and carry it, end over end, out across the sand, over the barrier, and out of the ring.

### Abilities invented in this post

**URAGAESHI · "Inside Out"** [Signature, grown from Makimodoshi]. Where Makimodoshi sends a working back along its own road to its caster, Uragaeshi reverses an expanding release's sign rather than its direction. He speaks **[Cro]** into the outgoing shell of a blast so that every outward push in it becomes an inward pull at once, and the shell caves in on its own point of origin. It runs on Cymorath carrying borrowed Essence, Karine again, so the working's own dialect carries the glyph. Against a disruption field he speaks in pieces into the field's beat silences, the instants where two close tones cancel each other. *Cost:* about a sixth of his reserve, more against a strong disruption. Threadburn behind the left eye; the pull drags his own body toward the point of collapse. *Limit:* only against an expanding release that came out of a single point (a burst, a blast, a pillar spreading from its base). It cannot turn a beam, a steady stream or a solid working, and it needs the release's origin within about fifteen paces. *Counter:* before, release from many points at once, so there is no single centre to fall back on. During, fill the beat silences: a disruption made of one pure tone, or three tones out of step, leaves no gaps to speak into. After, the caved-in energy sits in one place, and anything that bursts the bubble early scatters it all at once, his blade included.

**HIBIKI-AWA · "The Ringing Bubble"** [Technique, the product of Uragaeshi]. The caved-in energy stops at the size of a man and hangs as a dense ringing sphere, held by the Cycle sounding at its heart. It is the arena-sized cousin of a bubble collapsing under sound in water until it flashes (sonoluminescence). Inside its ring the threads keep the Cycle's beat rather than the world's, so time runs about a twentieth slower there: drifting feathers drag, heat shimmer moves like oil. Because the beat is his own, he moves inside it at his own pace. *Cost:* holding it is part of Uragaeshi's price, but every heartbeat it hangs it strains toward bursting. *Limit:* a heartbeat or two before it fails. The slowing is marginal (a twentieth, nowhere near the Covenant's held instant) and reaches only about four paces from the sphere's skin. *Counter:* before, don't release into one point. During, strike the sphere and it bursts outward with everything in it, which is as dangerous to him as to anyone. Or speak **[Uur]** Disruption at its heart, and the Cycle that holds the beat breaks.

**ITO-YORI · "Thread-Twisting"** [Sword form, Yukari blade]. He drives the point into a body of light or loose energy and spins the blade on its long axis, so the energy winds onto the steel like thread onto a spindle. The light twists along its own length as it winds, a helix rather than a glow (in real optics, an optical vortex: light carrying orbital angular momentum). It turns a cloud of power into a single tight winding that can be carried and aimed. His mother's lesson is the principle: pulled thread breaks, twisted thread holds anything. *Cost:* the wound energy sits heavy on his Crystal while it's on the blade, and the strain comes out as Threadburn. Holding it more than a few heartbeats burns the blood on the steel to smoke and then starts on his hands. *Limit:* only loose or gathered energy (a bubble, a cloud, a field, a held glow), never a working still bound to its caster's will. Winding takes two or three full turns of the blade, so it needs the beat or two that Hibiki-awa buys. *Counter:* before, keep your energy bound and moving, so there is nothing loose to wind. During, a single blow to the blade while it's winding spills the coil. After, see Kaeri-mizu.

**KAERI-MIZU · "Returning Water"** [Signature, non-physical]. Energy that still carries a caster's dialect still knows the road it came by, and **[Vor]** Return (Auren's root; Fluid Dynamics) sends it home all at once. It runs back along the caster's chain and channels to the Essence Core that poured it. It goes past skin and bone the way a reflection goes past glass. What makes it a weapon is the speed: a flow stopped or reversed too fast throws a pressure blow back up its pipe (water hammer, the millers' "race's kick"). A Crystal hit by everything it gave away, returning in a fraction of the time it took to leave, takes that blow along its channels and at its core. A working built this way can split a Crystal along its lines. *Cost:* about a seventh of his reserve to throw, on top of whatever winding the energy cost him. It also needs the energy to be hers, so he has to have taken it from her first. *Limit:* it only carries Essence that still holds the target's own dialect, which fades within a few breaths of the working leaving her. Most of the scattered, broken energy doesn't remember its road and won't go, and dumps itself into whatever is nearest instead (here, the floor). One target per throw. *Counter:* before, cut your link to a working the moment it leaves you, so its Essence stops knowing its road. During, open a gate and vent the surge outward as fast as it comes (spending it, not catching it). Or bleed it into a bound summon, which shares your road. After, a Crystal that took the blow can be tempered back along its lines by a Crystal-smith (Borin Ironheart's quench-temper art is the canon precedent).

**The shockwave and the melt** [the remainder, not a technique]. Whatever the Return won't carry goes into the ground. That is why the arena melts and a blast wave rolls out: a side effect of the reversal, unaimed. He chose to make the throw anyway, knowing it would.

### Mechanism, the three strata

- **Aether (the room).** Her incandescent field; the pillar's spreading light; the standing disruption hum; the broken storm's loose feathers and leftover heat.
- **Wellsprings (the law).** Cymorath, for the standing wave, the beat silences and the ringing bubble. Luminalis and Exuroth, borrowed through Karine, carrying the glyphs on her light. **[Cro]** Cycle: Valen, Abstract/Entropic, attested to Cymorath. **[Vor]** Return: Auren, Fluid Dynamics, attested to Cymorath.
- **Essence (the body).** His Shell still turned inward (Kussetsu); the lye burns resumed; two broken ribs; flash blindness; Threadburn and a nosebleed; his own channels straining under the wound energy on the blade.

### The physics, real and named

- **Beats and destructive interference.** Two tones close in pitch drift in and out of step, swelling and cancelling. In the cancelled moments there is real silence. Elder Tae's two temple bowls show it.
- **Converging waves and implosion.** An expanding shell turned to an inward pull piles energy onto its centre, denser and denser.
- **Sonoluminescence.** A bubble in water driven by strong sound collapses so violently that it emits a flash of light. That is the "crying bubble" in the bowl, and Hibiki-awa's model.
- **Optical vortex.** Light can carry orbital angular momentum and travel as a twisting helix. That is Ito-yori.

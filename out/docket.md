## R19-2-BUILT_AROUND — Prose is built around fixed dialogue, not the reverse

*Pack Nineteen §2, blocks: dialogue, scene-structure*

> The prose is built around the dialogue, never over it. Isaac's lines are the fixed points and the scene is constructed to make them land.

## R19-2-FIXED_TEXT — Isaac's dialogue is fixed text

*Pack Nineteen §2, blocks: dialogue, prose-law*

> Set it. Do not rewrite it.

Extends Pack Sixteen's vocabulary-conversion principle (§1) from vocabulary to speech generally; not a supersession of Pack Sixteen, which is not named as struck or replaced anywhere in this pack.

## R19-2-NEVER_ALTERED — What may never be altered in Isaac's dialogue

*Pack Nineteen §2, blocks: dialogue, prose-law*

> May never be altered: - Register, diction or formality level - Rhythm, sentence length, or clause structure - Repetition, including repetition that looks accidental - Fragments, run-ons, missing conjunctions - Profanity, crudeness, or bluntness of any kind - Capitals, stretched vowels, ellipses, and every other prosodic mark (see §3) - The order in which a character says things - Anything at all on the grounds that it reads better the other way

## R19-2-PERMITTED_CORRECTIONS — The narrow set of silent corrections to Isaac's dialogue

*Pack Nineteen §2, blocks: dialogue*

> May be corrected silently: - Typographical slips and dropped words (wsaas → was) - Verb tense and agreement where the intent is unambiguous - Punctuation required for the line to parse - A canon term used in a stale or incorrect form (Khavern → Kharven, kurotesu → Kurosetsu)

## R19-2-WORLD_BREAK_FLAG — Procedure when a fixed line breaks canon

*Pack Nineteen §2, blocks: dialogue, verification*

> Where a line genuinely breaks the world — a term that contradicts canon, a name that has been retired, a claim that cannot be true — Natalie flags it in one sentence in the author notes and leaves the line standing unless Isaac rules otherwise. The draft is not touched.

## R19-3-PROSODY_VERBATIM — Prosodic marks are reproduced verbatim, never normalised

*Pack Nineteen §3, blocks: dialogue, prose-law*

> Natalie does not convert stretched vowels into a narrative tag (he tore it out of himself), does not reduce capitals to italics, and does not tidy ellipses into em dashes or commas.

## R19-3-TAG_ALONGSIDE — A descriptive tag may accompany prosody, never replace it

*Pack Nineteen §3, blocks: dialogue*

> A tag may be added alongside prosody. It may never be added instead of it.

## R19-4-COMPOSURE_COST — Staying composed under stress must cost visibly

*Pack Nineteen §4, blocks: dialogue*

> The inverse is a character note and it must cost. A character who does stay composed under extreme stress is exercising an expensive discipline, and the scene shows the price.

## R19-4-ICEBERG_DIALOGUE — The iceberg rule applies to dialogue

*Pack Nineteen §4, blocks: dialogue, pov*

> And nobody explains anything they both already know. The iceberg rule is a dialogue rule first.

## R19-4-REALISTIC_SPEECH — Originated dialogue must show real-time speech production

*Pack Nineteen §4, blocks: dialogue, prose-law*

> Speech is not prose. It is produced in real time by a person who has not finished thinking.

Source lists eight specific artefacts (false starts/self-repair, interruption, repetition, non-answers, talking past each other, trailing off, physical business mid-line, saying the wrong thing) under one craft-law header; kept as one row rather than eight since the source treats them as one checklist, not eight separately verified rules.

## R19-4-REGISTER_UNDER_STRESS — Register under stress shortens and simplifies

*Pack Nineteen §4, blocks: dialogue, register*

> Register moves with state, and it moves the same way in every human being. Under stress, speech gets shorter, more concrete, more repetitive, and loses subordinate clauses.

## R19-4-WORLD_ANCHORED_SPEECH — Speech idiom is anchored to the speaker's own life

*Pack Nineteen §4, blocks: dialogue, worldbuilding, standing-inventory*

> Speech is world-anchored. People reach for the objects and idioms their life has given them.

## R19-5-COMPOSURE_BAN — The composure ban

*Pack Nineteen §5, blocks: dialogue, prose-law*

> No character speaks in balanced clauses under duress, and Natalie's habit of writing everyone that way is a tell.

Source itemises five specific bars under this header (antithesis under duress, three-part lists from bleeding/frightened/grieving characters, subordinate clauses over ~11 words in shouted speech, perfect syntax from characters established as inarticulate/young/exhausted/foreign, and uniform sentence length across a scene's speakers); kept as one row since they are one named ban, not five independently checked ones.

## R19-5-SORT_BY_SPEAKER_TEST — The cover-the-tags test is now a Pack Nineteen failure

*Pack Nineteen §5, blocks: dialogue, verification*

> Cover the dialogue tags and read the lines. If a stranger could not sort them by speaker, the scene failed, and the failure is now a Pack Nineteen failure and not merely a voice-differentiation warning.

Escalates the severity of the existing voice-differentiation test (compare R15-1-VOICE_DIFFERENTIATION) rather than replacing it; no supersedes link, since nothing is struck.

## 6. The Moto Bloodline — Kharven Crown-Line and the Seven Great Lines

This is the pass answering `R21-5-MATERIAL_CULTURE_PENDING`. It builds out six proposed elements — red enamel over dark steel, a beast-face boss, a knotwork belt, a fur mantle, a robe-over-harness silhouette, and a court-versus-campaign split — into real material culture, grounded in what Kharven and the Moto already are on record: a bloodline of foreign conquerors (Mongolian-Inuit in naming and spiritual architecture) ruling a native northern population (Nordic in pragmatism and honor-code), a hybrid that "carries the severity of both and the softness of neither." Moto dress should read that same way: an outsider's formal law laid over a native people's practical toughness, never fully assimilating into either.

**General silhouette.** The base shape is a fitted war-harness — leather and articulated steel, cut close and low-profile the way a functional soldier's kit has to be — worn *underneath* a looser outer layer rather than replaced by one. That outer layer is the robe-over-harness silhouette: a robe or long over-coat, open enough at the front and sides to show the harness beneath it, not concealing the wearer's readiness for violence so much as declaring that violence is not the only thing being worn. This isn't decorative confusion; it's a genuine statement about what a Moto *is*. Their techniques are described as administrative rather than explosive — a Moto strike is as likely to rank, condemn, or stabilize as it is to simply hit harder — and the robe is the visual translation of "judge" layered over "soldier." A Moto in full dress should read as magistrate and combatant at once, because both are true of them simultaneously and neither one is a costume over the other.

**Materials and palette — red enamel over dark steel.** *Enamel*, for reference, is a glassy, colored coating fused permanently onto metal by firing — it doesn't chip or fade the way paint does, and it's expensive and slow to apply, which is exactly why it reads as rank rather than as decoration. Dark steel here means steel deliberately blackened past the ordinary quenched-dark finish that's already the Concord baseline for worked metal (see §4) — a further, costlier step that turns "iron the world has worn dark" into "iron someone chose to make black." Deep red fired over near-black steel is the crown-line's pattern, and it isn't invented from nothing: the Ashen Crown itself — Kharven's actual crown of state — is "ironwork set with garnet," a deep-red stone set into black-worked iron, forged by a Dawi smith whose descendants still hold the commission. The crown-line's personal dress simply repeats, at human scale, the palette the kingdom's central object of authority already wears. It also happens to sit naturally against Sodoku Moto's own coloring — near-black hair carrying "a Kōkan violet-cast in direct light," amber-gold eyes — dark ground, one hot accent color, the rest kept quiet.

**The beast-face boss.** A *boss* is the raised, often sculpted knob or medallion set at the center of a shield, a belt buckle, or a shoulder-clasp — the part of the fitting that's allowed to be a picture rather than just hardware. For the Moto this boss is cast or worked into the shape of a beast's face, and which beast is not arbitrary: each of the Seven Great Lines carries its own set of animal epithets (the Kōkan crown-line alone claims the Lion, the Dragon, the Raven King, the Crowned Serpent), so a beast-face boss is a legible marker of lineage before anyone reads a badge or hears a name. On crown-line dress it should default to the lion or the dragon — worked in the same near-black steel as the rest of the harness, so the face reads as carved shadow with maybe a single garnet or red-enamel eye, rather than a bright decorative medallion. It sits at the belt clasp in daily and court dress, and migrates to the shoulder or a pauldron rivet in campaign dress, where a belt ornament would catch on gear.

**The knotwork belt.** *Knotwork* is a woven or carved pattern of interlacing cord-like lines with no visible beginning or end — the kind of ornament northern and Nordic-coded material cultures use on belts, sword-fittings, and grave-markers, and one of the clearest visual fingerprints of the *native* Kharven clans rather than the Moto themselves. Putting it on Moto formal dress is the single most useful signal in this whole pass: it's the one element that isn't Moto in origin at all. A crown-line belt in knotwork is the ruling house visibly wearing a piece of the people it governs and never assimilated into — an inherited, worn-leather or tooled-metal belt, never gaudy, sitting low at the hip beneath the robe's opening where the boss also sits. It should always read as *earned or married-in* rather than appropriated wholesale: a specific belt tied to a specific alliance, oath, or northern-born mother, not generic "northern flavor" set-dressing.

**The fur mantle.** A *mantle*, for reference, is a cloak-like outer layer, usually sleeveless and open down the front, draped over the shoulders rather than fitted to the arms — Muken Moto's own "Crown Mantle of Kharven," a reinforced royal mantle that "spreads impact across the torso," is already on record, and Sodoku's "Field Coat" already carries "a wide fur collar" as part of its description. The fur mantle in this pass generalizes that: heavy northern fur (the same climate that makes House Stark's Dire Icebears and the kingdom's whole cold-survival economy necessary), worn over the robe in court and reduced to a collar or lining in the field. Fur here does real climate work first — Kharven has an eight-month heating season — and status work second: quality and completeness of the fur (unbroken pelt, even color, no patchwork) marks rank the same way the Concord baseline marks a lord's coat by wholeness rather than embroidery.

### The court-versus-campaign split

This is the axis the whole pass should organize around, and it should never collapse into "one outfit with accessories added or removed."

**At court**, the full robe-over-harness silhouette is worn as intended: robe open enough to show the harness's quality (proof of readiness) but heavy with fur, dark steel, and red enamel at throat, belt, and shoulder. The knotwork belt is tooled and clean. The beast-face boss is polished enough to be read across a hall. This is Kharven performing what the Ashen Crown itself performs — a sovereign authority that is inscribed, not merely decorative — and every piece of it should be read by the room as a claim being made, the way §5's Concord baseline treats ornamentation as something that always costs the wearer narratively.

**On campaign**, the robe comes off or is reduced to a weathered field version — exactly the precedent already on record in Artemis Amagiri Moto's Passbreaker Mantle, an "indigo over-robe woven with Balance-thread and *weathered campaign sigils*," meaning the over-robe survives into the field but loses its court polish: mud-hemmed, iron-thread seams doing structural work rather than decorative ones, the fur reduced to a collar for warmth rather than a mantle for display, exactly as Sodoku's own Field Coat is described — "heavy, dark, wide fur collar, iron-thread seams, replaced twice." The beast-face boss moves off the belt and onto a shoulder rivet or pauldron, out of the way of gear; the knotwork belt, if worn at all, is the plain working version rather than the tooled court piece. Nothing about campaign dress should look like court dress with the shine turned down — it should look like a completely different priority set (survival and function) wearing the same lineage markers out of necessity rather than choice.

### Line variations

The crown-line pattern above belongs specifically to Kōkan — the ruling line, and the only one of the Seven Great Lines still holding a seat (Kharven). The other six lines are landless since the western seat's fall, which itself should read in their dress: their material culture is portable, worn-on-the-body wealth rather than the fixed, architecturally-reinforced authority Kōkan expresses through a literal crown and a literal seat.

- **Kōkan** (crown-line) — red enamel over near-black steel, gold or violet-toned metallic accents echoing the line's own confirmed metallic hair-sheen, lion or dragon boss. The only line entitled to the full court presentation described above without it reading as a claim on the throne.
- **Shirogane** *(Silver)* — the line's own in-text gloss. Trim, buckles, and boss worked in bright, hard silver rather than blackened steel; harder-edged and less warm than the crown-line's palette, consistent with Shirogane's reputation for "merciless structural punishment."
- **Akagane** *(Copper)* — copper and red-bronze fittings rather than enamel; the line's technique names run to blood and heart imagery ("Crimson Reprieve," "Heart-Warrant"), so their metal should read as a warmer, rawer red than Kōkan's fired enamel — closer to exposed metal than to a jeweler's finish.
- **Kurenai** — the name means crimson, but the line's own techniques are named in ash, mourning, and black ("Ashen Catechism," "Black Testament," "Widow's Sentence"). The dress should carry that contradiction on purpose: a bloodline named for red that dresses in unrelieved black and ash-grey, the crimson kept to a single hidden accent (a lining, a thread) rather than shown — a visual pun on a line whose whole function is punitive soul-law and execution.
- **Byakuya** — the line's technique names run to glass, veils, and halos ("Glass Cathedral Sight," "White Verdict"); pale, near-white materials and a clean, almost ecclesiastical cut fit better here than heavy fur or dark steel.
- **Tenrai** — war-command and banner imagery in their technique names ("Banner of the Next Turn," "Warroom Edict") suggest gold or imperial-toned trim and actual banner-heraldry rather than a boss — fitting a line whose ambition for the throne is already on record as the kingdom's founding wound. Flagged as the most speculative line in this pass; nothing in the source material fixes an actual palette for them.
- **Amagiri** *(Mist)* — already partially on record: Artemis Amagiri Moto's Passbreaker Mantle is indigo. Indigo and mist-grey, lighter and less armored-looking than the other lines, fitting a line built around route-reading and escape rather than frontal command.

> A note on what this pass does *not* touch: the birth-markers already on record for the whole bloodline — metallic skin-sheen at brow, clavicle, wrist, and temple; sigilic ring-lines and shard-patterns in the iris — are the body's own markers, not clothing, and they already follow this guide's magic-grammar rule (§3) by costing something and reading as physical rather than as special-effect ("pale fracture lines glowing amber under Hataraki strain" is strain-triggered, not decorative). This pass is about what a Moto chooses to put on over that body, and should be written as separate from it.

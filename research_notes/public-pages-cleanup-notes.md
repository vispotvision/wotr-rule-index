## Geography & the Four Quarters/News, Rumour and the Speed of Information.md

**Three standing rules for the table.**

**One.** Ask whether the destination is on the line *before* asking how far it is. On the few roads with a line, distance is secondary now, and that is the age's whole quarrel. Everywhere else it is everything it ever was.

**Two.** When a Front advances, decide the true version once, then decide separately what the line carried, what the herald cried, what the song says and what the tap-room says. They will differ, and a character's decisions should be made on whichever one reached him.

**Three.** The Guild reads everything. Anything a person genuinely needs kept is sent by hand, which costs days, and the choice between speed and secrecy is one of the few clean decisions the age offers.

## Geography & the Four Quarters/News, Rumour and the Speed of Information.md

## V. Hooks Sitting Here Unused

**The deaf district.** A quarter's ground thins, or its tower wardens walk. The line goes with it. Nobody in the capital notices for eleven days because the absence of news reads as the absence of events.

**The nine-word ambiguity.** A message arrives correct in every word and wrong in its meaning, because the stop was omitted to save a charge. Somebody acts on it. The pursuivant, the clerk and the copyist are all blameless and the man is still dead.

**The boy who reads faces.** A beacon boy is the only person in a street who knows which houses have had bad news this week, and somebody has worked out that this is worth money.

**Two hours in one ledger.** A hold clerk keeps Guild hour and hold hour in parallel columns, and a Tally entry falls on different sides of a boundary depending on the column, and a Cask-Oath's term turns on which one governs.

**The herald's deposit.** A story the Guild dislikes is never refused. It is simply capped shorter this week, and the house that could have cried it in full cannot afford a second pursuivant.

**The bill still on the door.** A bounty posted by a lord nine years dead is still paying out, in the sense that people keep trying to collect it, and the man it names has just come home.

**The price that moved.** The odds on a siege shift hard at noon with no news in town. Somebody knows something, the bookie will not say who, and half the street is already selling up.

**The song that will not die.** A minstrel's mistake has made a respectable family into murderers, in four hundred taverns, and the only person who can sing it right is the one who was paid to sing it wrong.

## The War Cycle/The Army/The Fisc — Paying for a Siege.md

## V. Running It At The Table

**Do not compute a budget.** Track three things and let them tell you what happens.
1. **Turns of pay in hand**, both sides. This is the clock.
1. **Whether small coin exists.** Silver in the chest is not pay.
1. **Whether the Seat has signed anything.**
**Every fiscal decision is a scene.** The levy is a purveyor at a door. The forced loan is one room and eleven households. Debasement is an assay house finding the fineness and deciding what to do. Accord credit is a factor's clerk, polite, patient, with the instrument already drawn, waiting.

**And the Front advances on money.** When you advance the Kharven Front, ask what was paid for and who has not been paid, before asking who moved.

## The War Cycle/The Army/The Fisc — Paying for a Siege.md

## VI. Hooks

**The captain who has been paying his own company.** Six weeks. He has told nobody. He is about to run out, and the first person to find out will be a sutler.

**The copper famine.** The Seat has silver and cannot pay. Somebody proposes melting the temple fittings, which are bronze, which is worth nothing, and the proposal reveals that the man making it does not understand money and is nonetheless in the room.

**The mint rolls.** The mintage fees say aggressive. An assay house has the figures. Posting them on a door is treason and keeping quiet is complicity, and the assayer has a family in the city.

**The presentation.** Fifth turn. The formation is in the yard, correct, silent, waiting. Nobody has drawn anything. The scene is entirely about who speaks first.

**The instrument on the table.** The factor's clerk has waited three days and is in no hurry. The security named is the Wellspring beneath the Kharven-Seat itself. Everyone in the room understands that signing ends something and that not signing ends something else.

**The exemption.** A household bought its way out of the levy. The household beside it did not, gave up its stack, and is now the household with the low stack, and the greeting has already changed.

## Factions, Bloodlines & Institutions/Powers and Polities/The Guild Accord/Structure and Rank/The Guild Accord in the Dark Ages — Divisions, Companies, Academies and Offices.md

## VIII. Hooks

**The eighth and ninth places.** The Chandlers and the Saddlers, two of the Twelve Great, in a precedence dispute nobody can resolve, because the original ruling is ambiguous and both readings hold. It matters at every civic occasion and has cost lives twice.

**Enforcement's ledger.** A Division funded by forfeiture. Somebody in Arbitration has the numbers and is deciding whether raising them ends the practice or ends them.

**The three directions.** A Divisional Head wants a charter revoked. He does not ask. He arranges for the Concordant to hear about the problem from three sources over a season. Somebody notices the pattern.

**Redemption.** A man bought his freedom of the company and has been elected to the Court, and the Court is deciding whether to let it stand, and the vote will be close, and everyone will pretend afterward it was about something else.

**The tables.** The Adventurers' underwriting tables say what a Containment on a live Wellspring site actually costs in lives. A crew has just been shown them by a clerk who should not have.

**The still line.** The beacons out of the Citadel go dark for two days.

**The fee strike.** The Sitting of Halls has declined the hall-fee for a second quarter, and this time the Legion's pay is in it.

**A silver problem.** Every token out of one small hall on the salt road has been reading a little light on the beam. Either the hall has been cutting its silver, or somebody very good has been striking copies and using its mark, and there is a crew at T5 right now who believe they are led by a Master.

## Materials, Alchemy & Trade/Work, Trade and Measure/Weight, Measure and the Standard.md

## X. Hooks

**The dull note.** A searcher taps a weight in a market where he is outnumbered, and it does not ring.

**The filed strike.** Every weight in a district carries a six-pointed mark filed halfway out. The district has been trading on struck weights for nine years. Somebody has just been sent to inspect it.

**Falling density, same bill.** A quarter is billed for draughts it did not receive, because the keeper's book counts bore and hours. The arithmetic is correct. The Guild's position is that the arithmetic is correct.

**Western grain.** A northern clerk can demonstrate that the Accord's grain is a Western seed and that every weight in the four quarters is therefore calibrated to one soil. He is right. He has no forum, and the demonstration takes two days and a set of scales nobody will lend him.

**The strike-board.** A slow roller is hired for a season's buying. The difference across the harvest is a fortune. It is not illegal and cannot be made illegal without describing a hand movement in a statute.

## Races & Peoples/The Tongues of the Realms/Naming and Usage/Element Inventories — Mahuo, Yukari, the Elven Branches, Beastkin.md

## Provenance

**Mahuo.** Grounded in the Mahuo character cards (Kwon Mu-jin, Ara Min Mahuo, Cozbi Mahuo; all three carry a recurring Ledger motif: "the Ledger-Keeper," "The Ledger Nobody Kept," "the Ledger-Prince") and the Vermilion Dynasty page (clan seat Sum-gol, "Breath Hollow"; Anima Spirare soul-breath theology). The structural facts are settled: lineage name, two-syllable given name with a shared generational syllable, a "hollow-seat."

**Yukari.** Grounded in The Yukari Bloodline (Senri, the Ketsumyōgan, "The Telling": the name is chosen at a coming-of-age rite; signature imagery of silver-threading-through-dark, still water before a stone hits it, the crow motif) and the Silver Cedars page's attested etymology: Ketsuen = Ketsu (thread-bound) + En (connection, bond). The amendment's own text glosses Jinmu = divine + dream. Full life-stage slots are attested: name-taboo on the living, renaming at promotion, vow or allegiance.

**The Elven branches.** The best-documented of the four: the Vey-Elarin grammar is already a full set of real roots, particles and affixes, functionally most of an element inventory that had not been formatted as one. The Elven Peoples page confirms five branches, each theologically distinct, and calls for names "recognizably related but structurally different," exactly what shared roots plus branch-specific affixes deliver.

**Beastkin.** The Five Beastkin Lineages documents Name-Keeping concretely, but specifically for the Fox-Spirit/Mishiro lineage ("the last thing that cannot be physically removed... speaking one's name aloud in the presence of a witness"), not the other four (Catkin, Lupine, Bearkin, Serpentkin); the amendment generalizes it to "Beastkin" broadly, and this inventory follows that lead.

**Format.** Mahuo and Yukari match the amendment's Marrowkin (30 elements, two-element compounds) and Satulagi/Moto (25 elements, open-syllable) inventories. The elven and Beastkin inventories are restructured where the culture's own anchor does not fit that shape.

**A note on Undaar-Keth and Undaar-Gorr.** Under the compounding rule the second element governs, so *Undaar-Keth* is a seal of the standing kind and *Undaar-Gorr* is a mantle-weight of the standing kind. They are two different places, or one place renamed by somebody making a claim, and never spelling variants of one name.

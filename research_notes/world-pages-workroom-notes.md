## News, Rumour and the Speed of Information

## Author Notes

**The beacon line** is built on real fire chains. The Byzantine beacon line of the ninth century, credited to Leo the Mathematician, ran some 450 miles across Asia Minor from the fortress of Loulon on the Cilician frontier to the palace lighthouse at Constantinople, and modern experiment suggests a signal could cross it in about an hour. Identical water clocks at the two ends assigned a different message to each of twelve hours, so the hour a fire was lit said what had happened. Later chroniclers claim Michael III shut it down so that news of an invasion would not spoil his chariot racing, which scholars usually treat as propaganda and this page treats as an idea for a hook. The Mamluk post in Syria and Egypt ran fire signals and carrier pigeons beside its relay horses, and Greek writers describe older hilltop chains. The real chains sent a few agreed signals. Sending words by shuttered color is this setting's own step past them, and the price per word, the heralds' rate, the deposit and the night watch all follow from that step.

**The distortion mechanic** is the plain arithmetic of any short signal sold by the word, and it has a cousin in the merchants' newsletters of the Italian towns, where news went from hand to hand and each writer summarized the last.

**The bag** is built on the relay systems: the Mongol yam, whose stations stood about 20 to 40 miles apart with spare horses, food and shelter, and the Persian royal road before it, for the riders; and for everything else the way medieval letters really moved, in the care of whoever was going that way, with the Florentine merchants' own courier service of the fourteenth century as the model for the merchant houses' riders.

The substitution of wardened fire-towers for the plain beacon is originated, and so is everything downstream of it, including the Withering-era contraction of the line and the four peoples' different responses to Guild hour.

## Accent — Placement, Prestige and the Shibboleth

## Author Notes

Real-world substrate, named per the research rule.

**Style-shifting and hypercorrection.** Labov's 1966 New York department-store study: prestige forms stratified by store rank and rising with attention to speech, with hypercorrection concentrated in the group immediately below the one being imitated.

**Covert prestige.** Trudgill's Norwich work, with the standard critique attached: that it can romanticize constraint, since vernacular forms are sometimes maintained for want of access to the standard rather than from positive valuation.

**Accommodation.** Giles's communication accommodation theory, including the upward and downward axis, over-accommodation as perceived condescension, and objective convergence carrying divergent intent, illustrated by the Catalan and Castilian norm.

**Indexicality and linguistic capital.** Silverstein and Bourdieu, and the third-wave move associated with Eckert that treats variation as deliberate stylistic practice rather than a passive readout of social category. This is the license for characters who *use* their accents rather than merely having them, and it is what keeps this page from being a table of stereotypes.

**Shibboleths.** The Sicilian Vespers of 1282, the Matins of Bruges of 1302, Kraków in 1312, and the eighteenth-century runaway advertisements identifying absconders by regional accent. The historicity of the medieval anecdotes is disputed in each case, which does not matter for the craft point: what recurs is the shape, an ordinary word chosen because it cannot be faked.

## The Fisc — Paying for a Siege

## Author Notes

**The pay conditions.** Military payment had to be organized across great distances, **with sufficient regularity to prevent mutiny, and in denominations small enough to be used by soldiers in local transactions.** The life thread of army finance was short-term credit: unpaid bills to suppliers, payments advanced by officials and officers, temporary loans contracted by intermediaries, and bills of exchange used to transfer funds.

**Mutiny.** Desertion and mutiny were frequent among troops paid late or not at all, and unpaid soldiers have marched leaderless on a city to obtain what they were owed and sacked it.

**Debasement** was an effective aggressive fiscal policy for financing war rather than merely a monetary accident, and the diagnostic separating aggressive from defensive debasement lies in the mint accounts: **mintage fees run high on aggressive debasements and low on defensive ones.**

**Purveyance**, the compulsory taking of goods for military use, generated corruption and abuse severe enough to force nationwide investigation and the removal of most purveyors from office; it was surrendered for military purposes under sustained pressure and revived at the next war with instructions to be fair, to avoid church property, and to pay a fair price.

**Other instruments** in the record include emergency borrowing from temple treasuries, borrowing from factor houses and money-changers with the attendant risk that a crown simply will not pay, and the fact that representative institutions grew out of rulers needing authority to tax for war.

**Setting canon used.** The western silver mark as the accounting standard; copper as the money of ordinary life and the only metal money most people hold; the copper famine, in which array windings are worth more by weight than struck coin and small coin is melted within three years of any great array; salt bars of fixed weight circulating at a mark and edible, which no other money is; the Sky faith's treatment of a low stack as a moral condition; and contribution and sack from the existing war-cycle material.

The jurisdiction trap, the dissolution schedule and the presentation are originated.

## Outer World, reconciliation note

> **Reconciliation note: the test the Archives asked for, run against the Old World's.**
>
> **What matches, and matches closely.** The defining condition is identical in both: Aether-exhausted ground, practitioners running on their own reserves, no array work, and the whole political order shaped by who holds the few places where Essence still gathers. The rainless nitrate coast is in both: *Palebed Nitre, caliche in basins that have not seen rain in living memory*, set against the Aurelian Dust Coast's nitrate and lithium salts under multi-year drought. Flood basalt is in both. Bone-beds and fossil wealth are in both. So is a cold fractured southern extremity of fjord and island. **Hollow Wellsprings and Dead Zones are the same idea the Crevice belongs to.**
>
> **What does not match.** The ledger describes almost no water and almost no wood; the document puts the largest rainforest in the world in the same quarter. The ledger names no mountain chain; the document is spined by a volcanic cordillera running its full length. And the ledger's polities, Braqth, Dorrosfenn and Epprenea, do not appear in the document at all, which is held four parts in five by dragons the ledger never mentions.
>
> **A fourth resolution, available here and possibly upstream.** The ledger is an **Accord document about ground Accord surveyors can reach.** Everything it records for this quarter is dry, low, treeless and westward-facing, which is precisely the Dust Coast, the Varakh Drylands and the Ashen South. If eighty percent of the quarter is draconic and issues no survey permits, then the Measurewrights never wrote the wet half **because they were never let in**, and the ledger is not wrong so much as it is a partial survey wearing the confidence of a complete one. *That is diegetically better than any of the three exits offered on the Old World page, and it costs no canon.*
>
> **Whether it rescues the Old World too is a separate question and my answer is no.** Coal foreland, karst, coppiced woodland on eleven-hundred-year rotation and orange acid drainage are not an unsurveyed corner of an African-register country; they are different ground entirely. The partial-survey reading works here and does not work there, which is itself evidence the two documents are misfiled differently.

# The Vacancy

*The Korvaeth War. Being an account of the naming, the fracture, and the first year.*

---

## I. THE RECKONER

Maren Vaeleth had counted six executions in forty-one years and this was the first one where the ledger came out before the blade.

She stood at the foot of the yard steps with the book braced on her forearm and her right hand free, and she had been standing there since the fourth bell, and the callus at the first joint of her forefinger had begun the specific dull ache it produced after the second hour of any long entry. Three hundred and six sending. Four abstaining, forfeits paid, entered. Two refusing, unpaid, entered as unpaid instead of absent, because absent is a fact and unpaid is a position and the record should say which it was.

The count was closed. She had closed it herself two days ago in the black drown chamber and had put the ledger in the vault and had taken it out again this morning, and it had stopped being hers the moment it went in and had not become hers again on the way out.

That is the whole of a Reckoner. You are not a witness. You are the instrument that makes witnessing legible, and an instrument does not have opinions about what it measures.

Maren Vaeleth had opinions.

She kept them where she kept the Vosk woman's granddaughter, who was nine, and who was standing in the fourth rank of the yard with her grandmother's hand shut around her wrist because the count does not stop for age.

---

The Empress of Eresse came out of the eastern door at the sixth bell with two of her own household behind her and neither of them touching her.

Onawa had asked for that in writing, in full form, eleven days ago, and Korvaeth had granted it because there was no reason on any ledger in four quarters to refuse it. She walked the length of that yard by herself in the cold with three hundred and twelve compensated pairs of eyes on her, and she walked it at the pace of a woman crossing her own hall, and Maren Vaeleth watched forty years of Reckoning fail to give her a word for what that was.

She had put her hair up herself. Anyone who had ever seen a coronation would have known it, because the imperial coil is done in nine passes by two attendants and cannot be done in fewer, and what was on Onawa's head that morning was four passes and a plain pin, and it was holding, and it was going to hold for exactly as long as it needed to.

Grey riding wool. No jewellery. Boots that had been resoled.

*She has dressed as a woman with no estate. She has dressed herself as* -ama.

*Humility is no part of it. She is telling three hundred and six paid witnesses that she arrives holding nothing, and if she arrives holding nothing then nothing is being taken from her here, and if nothing is being taken then this is not a transfer.*

*She is arguing. She is going to argue all the way down and she is going to do it in what she is wearing.*

Maren Vaeleth opened the ledger to the leaf she had ruled for it and set her nib.

---

Ilthára Korvaeth came out after her and did not walk the yard.

She came down the steps and stopped at the third one and stood there, which put her a head above the block and two heads above the front rank, and it was correct and it was cold and it was the first thing that morning Maren approved of professionally. A Reckoner needs a sightline. Three hundred and six people needed a sightline. The woman had thought about the geometry of her own atrocity and had thought about it well.

Thirty-four. Five feet eleven. The Korvaeth build, which is the deep build, shoulders narrow and squared and the hands too large for the arms. Skin the grey-violet of a four-day bruise, hair the flat unlit white, bound back in a single black cord. A scar through the left eyebrow that pulled the lid a fraction so that half her face read as considering and the other half did not.

She wore the coat with the salt-black facings. Cut from the same drown alder groves as the panelling in her chamber, boiled and pressed into cloth-backing by a workshop in Vessel that had gone under two winters ago, and there were eleven coats like it in the world and she had all eleven.

*She is dressed as an estate.*

*Onawa arrived holding nothing and Ilthára has come out dressed as a holding, and one of them is making an argument about property and the other one is making an argument about property, and they are the same argument from opposite ends, and they have been having it since they were fourteen years old.*

Aeldros stood at the top of the steps with his hands behind his back.

He was six years younger and a hand larger and he had the family hands and none of the family stillness, and Maren, who had watched him grow, counted him shifting his weight nine times before the blade came out. He was called the Validated. His name had gone through full assembly at nineteen with the witnesses called and compensated and the record sealed, in a ceremony his sister had been refused, and he had never once corrected anybody who said the byname as a compliment.

He was not looking at the block.

He was looking at his sister, and Maren Vaeleth understood, watching him, that Aeldros Korvaeth had spent eleven days being afraid his sister would not do it, and had arrived that morning at a new and worse fear, and had not yet worked out what it was.

---

The Empress knelt without being asked and put her own hands behind her.

The yard made a sound with no word in it. Three hundred and twelve people taking the same breath at slightly different times, which in a cold enclosed space produces a noise like water withdrawing off shingle, and Maren had heard it at four of the six and had never once got used to it.

Onawa turned her head and said something to the woman with the blade, and the woman with the blade nodded, and that exchange is not in the record because a Reckoner enters what is said in form and nothing else.

*She asked how it is done. She asked so she would know where to put her chin. There is no version of that woman that arrives anywhere unbriefed.*

Then Onawa spoke in full form, and Maren's nib went down onto the leaf.

"Elar-uren Onawa. Ie-sae." A pause, exactly the length a pause is supposed to be in the Ureth register, no longer. "Sae-ureth."

*My name, and the assembly stood for it. Granted in season.*

*The witnessing-office window stands open.*

Maren Vaeleth's hand stopped.

*She has opened it.*

*She is the Empress and she holds the calendar and there is no question anywhere in four quarters about her standing to open a window, and she has opened the witnessing window in the yard where she is about to be killed.*

*She has convened an assembly.*

Three hundred and six compensated witnesses, called and paid and entered, standing in a yard with the window open over them. Every one of them had been compensated to witness a killing and every one of them was now, by the same fact, a witness of the assembly, because the compensation does not specify what is witnessed. It specifies that the witness was paid.

The Empress of Eresse had turned her own execution into a session.

*And she cannot transfer anything into it, because she offered the transfer eleven days ago in full form and it was refused, and a refused grant cannot be re-offered inside the same term. That door is shut and she shut it herself by offering early.*

*So what is she convening it for.*

Onawa said the last of it with her chin already down.

"Elar-ith."

*A by-name. A small name, neutrally so.*

Maren wrote it and did not understand it and entered it verbatim, which is the office.

Then Onawa said, in Common, so that the fourth rank and the Vosk child could hear it:

"Say my name, Ilthára. In full. You know the form."

---

There it is, Maren Vaeleth thought, and her whole body went cold from the wrists inward.

She had been Reckoner of Korvaeth for eleven years and a Reckoner in the Vessel courts for thirty before that, and she had entered perhaps nine hundred namings, and she saw the shape of it inside a second and a half because seeing the shape of it is the only skill the office has.

*A full-form naming, spoken by the person who will hold the vacancy, before an assembly that has been convened by a party with standing to convene it, with every witness compensated and entered.*

*That is a validated utterance.*

*It is not a courtesy and it is not a farewell. It is an act of assembly, performed in the Ureth register, in front of the count, and the moment it leaves Ilthára Korvaeth's mouth the record shows the house of Korvaeth performing Eressean procedure.*

*And if Eressean procedure governs at Korvaeth then it governs everywhere Korvaeth's writ runs, and every Varrisak holding in the north is a contingently held name with nobody standing for it, and the first of them is Aeldros, whose validated name was validated by an assembly that Varrisak doctrine says was never necessary and Eressean doctrine says was fine.*

*No. Not Aeldros first.*

*Her. Ilthára has no assembly. Ilthára has never had an assembly. Ilthára was refused one at seventeen and has built thirty years and a war and a doctrine on the position that she did not need one.*

*If she says the name, she concedes the doctrine that says she has no name, in front of three hundred and six people, in the same breath as taking an empire.*

Maren Vaeleth looked up from the ledger for the first time that morning.

Onawa's eyes were closed. She was not going to argue for it and she was not going to explain it. She had put the trap on the ground between them in the plainest Common she owned and had then given the woman about to kill her the dignity of being clever enough to see it.

*She has offered her an out. Refuse and you keep the north. Refuse, and I die unnamed, and a vacant name is a silence in a chord, and you will have to carry that instead.*

*She has made the whole of it into a choice between a legal position and a friend.*

---

Ilthára Korvaeth came down the last two steps.

She stopped a yard short of the block and stood there, and Maren watched her hands, because a Reckoner watches hands. They were open. They had been open since the eastern door.

The yard was completely silent. Three hundred and twelve people who had been standing in the cold since the fourth bell had all, at the same moment, understood that something was happening that they did not have the training to follow, and the sound of that many people deciding to be quiet has nothing in common with the sound of them being told to be.

*She has seen it. Whatever else she is, she has seen it. She has read four Eons of Velthaeir procedure and she has spent eleven days building this yard and she has seen it inside the same second and a half that I did.*

*She is going to refuse it.*

*She is going to leave her unnamed, and it will be the correct move, and every jurist in Velthaeir will write it up in forty years as the moment the north was won.*

Ilthára Korvaeth put her hand on Onawa's head, on the four-pass coil with the plain pin in it, and left it there.

"Elar-uren Onawa," she said.

Full form. Ureth register. Verb-initial, contours realised, the falling tone landing on the syllable a Velthaeir jurist would have put it on. Every word of it correct.

"Ie-sae. Ureth zo. Vey-en Eresse-en, elar-uren, uren, uren."

*Granted in season. The witnessing, restated. Of the kin, of the estate of Eresse: the validated name, witnessed, witnessed.*

She said the witness word three times.

Maren Vaeleth wrote all of it because that is the office, and her hand was steady because it has been steady for forty-one years, and she was aware while she was writing that she was entering the death of everything her employer had ever wanted.

The blade came down eleven seconds later and by then it was almost beside the point.

---

The yard emptied over about an hour.

Maren Vaeleth stayed where she was with the ledger open until the last of the four abstentions had come past her table and put their thumb in the wax, and then she ruled off the leaf and turned it and carried the book up the yard steps herself, because the vault key is the Reckoner's and the walk is the Reckoner's and it has been that way since before there was a Korvaeth.

Aeldros was waiting at the top.

He had not moved during the naming. She had seen him from the corner of her eye, standing with his hands behind his back and the weight going off one foot onto the other and then stopping, all at once, on the fourth word, and staying stopped.

"Reckoner."

"My lord."

"What did she just do."

Maren Vaeleth held the ledger against her chest with both arms, which is a thing she had never done in forty-one years and which she noticed herself doing.

"She named an Empress in full form before a convened assembly of three hundred and six compensated witnesses," she said. "And I have entered it. And the entry closes in nine days, and after nine days it is in the sealed register at Velthaeir and it is a matter of record in four quarters and there is no instrument in any court that can take it out again."

"And what does the record say."

"It says the house of Korvaeth performed Eressean procedure."

Aeldros Korvaeth stood there in the cold with his hands behind his back and looked out over the emptying yard.

"Say the rest of it."

"My lord."

"You have been in my family's employ since before I could walk and you are going to make me ask for it twice. Say the rest of it."

Maren Vaeleth had entered forty-one years of other people's positions without ever once taking one, and she took one now, and she has thought since that she took it because the Vosk child was nine and had been made to stand in that yard for three hours to watch a woman die for a reason nobody in the fourth rank could have named.

"It says your sister has no name, my lord," she said. "It says it in her own voice, in the correct register, in front of the count. Everything the north has held for thirty years, it has held on the position that a holding validates itself when assembly is impossible. She has just performed an assembly. She has demonstrated, personally, that assembly was possible at Korvaeth on the eleventh day of the withheld season. And a doctrine that says assembly is impossible dies the moment somebody holds one."

"And mine."

"Yours was validated, my lord. Yours is the only one in this house that survives."

Aeldros Korvaeth made a sound she had never heard him make.

"That is the worst thing anybody has ever said to me," he said, and went inside.

---

## II. THE BROTHER

The chamber still smelled of salt.

It came out of the walls and had never stopped, eleven years after the panelling went up, a dry mineral edge under the tannin and the wax and the old smoke from a chimney that had never drawn properly. Black drown alder, from the groves below the Vessel Coast where Eresse opened the sea gates in the third year of the war and let the salt in over four thousand hectares of standing timber. The trees had taken nine years to die. They had come out of it dense and black and beautiful and worth more dead than the grove had ever been worth alive, and Ilthára had gone through the delivery plank by plank and rejected forty-one boards for being cut from the northern edge where the salt had not reached deep enough to blacken the grain.

She was sitting at the long table with her coat over the chair-back and her hands flat on the wood when her brother came in.

"You said it."

"I did."

"In full form."

"In full form."

"Before the count."

"Before the count, Aeldros, and I said the witness word three times, and I said it in Ureth because Common would not have carried and I wanted it carried." She did not look up. "Sit down. You are going to say all of it and then you are going to sit down, so you may as well do it in the other order and save us both the standing."

He did not sit down.

"Thirty years." His voice had gone somewhere Maren Vaeleth would not have recognised. "Thirty years of the Under-Houses, and the black wards, and the deep roads, and every one of them holding on the position that assembly is impossible in the north. Thirty years of me telling six houses that their names are as good as mine, and me knowing it was a lie, and telling them anyway because you asked me to and because I believed the doctrine even while I knew what the doctrine was covering."

"You believed the doctrine."

"I believed the *cause*." He put both hands on the table. "You have just told three hundred and six people that a Korvaeth can convene an assembly in a yard on a Tuesday. Every house that has been holding without one is now holding without one *by choice*. That is what you did. You did not lose them the argument, you took the argument away."

"Yes."

"Yes." He said it back at her. "Yes. That is the whole of what you have. Yes."

Ilthára Korvaeth looked up.

"Do you want to know why."

"I want to know whether you saw it."

"I saw it before you did." She said it without heat and without pride. "I saw it while she was still speaking. She opened the window on *sae-ureth* and I heard what she had done to the compensation and I had it complete before her chin was down. I had a second and a half and I used about a third of it."

"Then you *chose*."

"I chose."

Aeldros Korvaeth pulled a chair out and sat down in it hard, which was the first uncontrolled thing he had done in eleven days.

"Then tell me it was a calculation," he said. "Tell me there is a reading where this wins us something and I have not found it, because I have been going through it since the fourth word and there is nothing at the bottom of it. Tell me it was for the empire. Tell me it was for the record, or for the Under-Houses, or for the war. Give me a reason with a number in it and I will go and sell it to six houses tonight and I will make them believe it."

"There is no number in it."

"Ilthára."

"She was fourteen," said Ilthára Korvaeth.

Her brother stopped.

"She was fourteen and I was fourteen and we were in the Vosk orchard, and a boy had told me an hour earlier that I did not have a name, and I had gone to my father with it, and my father had confirmed it and gone back to what he was doing without putting down what was in his hands." She turned one palm over on the black wood and looked at it. "And I was crying, and she could not bear it, and she said a thing to make me stop. She said the holding validates itself. She said it because I was crying, Aeldros, and it worked, and she went home.

"And I built a doctrine on it. And then a faction. And then thirty years and six houses and a war, and by the time she understood what she had made she could not pull it out, because the whole north was standing on it by then and pulling it out would have brought the house down on the people leaning against it."

"She told you this."

"Eleven days ago. In form. Along with the offer." Ilthára closed the hand. "She told me the only honest thing that anybody has ever said to me about my own name, which is that a thing said carelessly by a child can still be correct, and that neither of us can prove it either way, and that this is the condition every doctrine that has ever existed has always been in."

Aeldros sat back.

"And you named her anyway."

"I named her *because*." For the first time her voice moved. "She gave me the trap in Common so the fourth rank could hear it. She could have said it in Ureth and taken the empire out from under me and been the cleverest woman in four quarters, and she said it in the register a nine-year-old could follow, because she wanted the child in the fourth rank to understand that she was being offered something.

"She spent her last sentence making sure I had a real choice. And she went down on her knees in a yard I built to kill her in, in resoled boots, with her hair up in four passes, and she asked me for the one thing I could give her that cost her nothing and cost me everything."

"And you gave it."

"I gave it."

"Why."

Ilthára Korvaeth looked at her brother across a table made out of a drowned forest.

"Because a vacant name is a silence in a chord," she said. "And I have been a silence in a chord since I was seventeen years old, and I have spent thirty years finding out what that does to a person, and I was not going to do it to her."

---

Aeldros put his face in his hands, and left it there, and spoke through it.

"They are going to call you mad."

"They already do."

"They are going to call you mad *and be right*, and that is the part I cannot get around. Ilthára, I have defended you in eleven halls. I have stood up in front of men who called you the Mad Queen of Korvaeth and I have told them, every time, that they were confusing a woman who thinks four moves out with a woman who is not thinking. And I believed it. I believed it this morning."

"And now."

"Now you have handed me the first argument I cannot answer." He took his hands down. "Because there is no defence. There is no clever reading. You had an empire in your hand and you gave it away for a courtesy to a woman you had just killed, and when they ask me why I will have to tell them the truth, which is that my sister did it because they were fourteen in an orchard."

"Then tell them."

"They will not hear it as love." His voice cracked on the last word and he let it. "They will hear it as a woman who cannot tell the difference between an empire and an afternoon. That is what mad *is*, out there. It has no raving in it and no blood. It is a ruler who spent the wrong thing on the wrong thing and could not explain the exchange rate afterward."

Ilthára Korvaeth said nothing at all for a while.

"I know," she said.

"Then why."

"Because it was worth it."

"To whom."

"To me." She stood up and picked her coat off the chair-back. "That is the only ledger I have ever been permitted to keep, Aeldros. Every other one in this house has my name in the contingent form. I have exactly one column that nobody gets to audit, and this morning I spent an empire out of it, and the entry balances."

She got to the door and stopped.

"Send for the Under-Houses," she said. "All eleven. No summons. Ask them."

"They will not come."

"Six of them will not come. Ask anyway, and ask in form, and pay the messengers so the refusal has to be entered as a refusal." She had her hand on the frame. "If we are going to lose them I would like it in the record why."

---

## III. THE FINDING

Velthaeir does not publish.

The Floating Archive-City has never issued a statement about anything in four Eons of existence, and the reason is doctrinal and is written into the founding instrument: a body that keeps the record cannot also be a party to what the record says. What Velthaeir does instead is enter findings in the sealed register, and the sealed register is not secret and is merely sealed, and any party with standing may request a reading, and the reading is performed by two jurists in alternation and costs eleven days and a fee.

Sylvenn Thaeren was second jurist and had been for thirty-one years, and the whole of the office is to catch what the first one lets past.

The Korvaeth entry came up the Vessel road in a Reckoner's case with a Reckoner's seal on it and reached the reading-floor on the ninth day, and Aeren Lyth read it first and Sylvenn read it second, and they got four lines in before Aeren stopped.

"Read that again."

"You read it. I read after you."

"Sylvenn."

He read it again.

*Elar-uren Onawa. Ie-sae. Ureth zo. Vey-en Eresse-en, elar-uren, uren, uren.*

The two of them stood on either side of a lectern in a floating city with eleven Eons of law under their feet and did nothing at all for a length of time that the floor-clerk later entered as *considerable*.

"She said the witness word three times," said Aeren Lyth.

"Yes."

"Once is the form. Twice is emphasis. Three times is."

"Three times is the Elareth construction," said Sylvenn Thaeren. "It is in the rite calendars. It is the closing of a consecration and it is not used in the Ureth register and it has not been used outside a rite in four Eons because there has been no occasion."

"So what is it doing in a yard."

"It is making it permanent."

Aeren Lyth sat down on the reading-floor bench, which is not permitted.

---

The finding took nine days to draft and eleven words to state, and Sylvenn Thaeren wrote the second half of it, and the argument in the drafting chamber went on for six of the nine days and was the worst professional experience of his life.

The problem was that everything in the entry was correct.

The window was opened by a party with unquestioned standing to open it. The assembly was convened. The witnesses were compensated, called, and entered by a Reckoner of forty-one years' standing whose hand no court in four quarters has ever impeached. The utterance was made in the correct register with the contours realised, by the party who would hold the vacancy, and it was made freely, and there is no instrument anywhere in Eressean law that permits a validated utterance to be voided on the grounds that the speaker regretted it afterward. There is not even a form for the request.

"It is valid," said the Master of the Floor on the fourth day. "That is the difficulty. It is valid, and beyond valid: it is the cleanest validation this floor has read in my tenure. If a student produced it as an exercise I would mark it full."

"And the speaker has no name."

"The speaker has no name. Which does not signify. A person with no name may perform a validated utterance about somebody else's name. There is no standing requirement on the *speaker* in a naming, only on the party convening the window, and the party convening the window was the Empress, and the Empress could convene anything she liked, including this."

"Then Korvaeth is now Eressean."

"Korvaeth has *performed as* Eressean," said the Master of the Floor, "in front of the count, on the record, in a matter that cannot be reopened. Which is not the same thing and is going to be treated as the same thing by everybody who is not in this room."

That was the sentence the whole war came out of, and it was said by a seventy-year-old archivist on the fourth day of a drafting argument in a room with nine people in it, and none of the nine ever repeated it outside.

---

The finding was entered on the twentieth day.

*Ie-sae. Uren. Elarama Ilthára Korvaeth. Thaeren zo lyth.*

*Granted in season. Witnessed. A contingently held name, Ilthára Korvaeth. The archive restates: silence.*

Eleven words. The first two confirm the transfer. The third and fourth confirm the witnessing. The fifth through seventh state, in the plainest form the language has, that the woman who performed the utterance holds her own name contingently and that nobody has ever stood for it. And the last three are the archive's own position, which is that Velthaeir has restated the matter and will say nothing further, and that the restatement is the whole of the finding.

*Thaeren zo lyth.* The archive restates: silence.

Sylvenn Thaeren wrote *zo lyth* and put the pen down and went out onto the eastern gallery and stood in the wind for a while.

*We have not taken a side. That is the finding and it is honest and I drafted half of it and I would draft it again.*

*And every court in four quarters is going to read it as a side, because we have confirmed her taking and denied her standing in the same eleven words, and there is no way to hold those two things at once except by being an archive, and nobody out there is an archive.*

*We have given the Under-Houses a document that says their queen has no name. We have given the Sunroot Court a document that says the empire lawfully changed hands. And we have given the Sylvaaren a document that says Eressean procedure ran at Korvaeth, which is the one thing four Eons of Varrisak doctrine has insisted was impossible in the north.*

*All three of them are correct readings.*

*That is what a record is for and it is the first time in thirty-one years I have wished the office did something else.*

---

## IV. ELEVEN ASKED

Ilthára Korvaeth asked in form and paid the messengers, and the refusals had to be entered as refusals.

That is the whole of what she did, and it was the single most damaging administrative decision of the war, and Aeldros told her so on the night she gave the order and told her so again eleven weeks later when the ledger came back.

An Under-House that ignores a summons has done nothing. An Under-House that ignores a *paid* request has produced a refusal, and a refusal is a position, and a position has to be defended, and six houses who might have sat the year out in comfortable ambiguity were now on a page in a Reckoner's hand with a reason next to each of them.

Five came.

**House Thenn-Ashal** came first and came whole, because Vessa Thenn-Ashal had been at the yard in the second rank and had heard the naming and had understood roughly a third of it and had decided the rest did not matter. She was sixty-one, wide, deaf in the left ear from a ward-collapse forty years back, and she ran eleven miles of deep road and four black wards and had buried two sons in them. Her position, given in the black drown chamber with her hand flat on the table, is entered as follows.

*"I do not care what she said. I care that Eresse has spent four Eons writing my houses into an archive as a failure that happened to them, and a Korvaeth put an Empress on her knees in a yard, and I was there. Enter me as sending."*

**House Vhelor** came for money. **House Aarenn** came because Vhelor came. **House Serath-Lo** came because their charter runs through Korvaeth's own vault and they had no other option, and their delegate said so out loud, in form, which everybody in the room respected and nobody forgot.

**House Mirroven** came last and came conditional, and the condition was Aeldros.

Corlan Mirroven was thirty and had been head of his house for nine months and was frightened of everybody in that chamber including his own retainers, and what he said is the most quoted sentence of the first year.

*"We send under the Validated, and under no queen. Enter it that way or do not enter us."*

Aeldros Korvaeth accepted it in front of his sister without looking at her.

That is the moment the north acquired two commanders, and neither of them wanted it, and the entry is four words long.

---

Six refused, and the six refusals are worth reading in order, because between them they contain the whole political argument of the war and no two of them agree about anything except the outcome.

**House Marrovel** refused on doctrine and refused cleanly.

*"Elar-ama. Elar-ama vey-en."*

*A contingently held name. A contingently held name, of that kin.*

Ten words in the conservative morphology, thrown back at a woman who had abandoned it in a yard, and the whole of the drow argument sits inside them: she performed Eressean assembly, so by her own demonstration assembly was possible, so every unassembled name in the north is contingent by choice, **including hers**, and Marrovel had the grace and the cruelty to say so in the register she had grown up speaking.

**House Ordhaen** refused on arithmetic. Their delegate read the Velthaeir finding aloud, all eleven words, and then said that a house cannot fight a war for a claimant whose claim the archive has entered as contingent, because when the war ends somebody will have to hold the ground under law, and there will be no law under it.

**House Ess-Vaelen** refused on the granddaughter.

That one nobody had predicted and it did more damage than the other five together. Their delegate was a woman named Thela Ess-Vaelen who had no standing in any court and had been sent because nobody senior would come, and what she said was not in form at all and was entered anyway because the Reckoner ruled that a refusal is a refusal in whatever register it arrives in.

*"You made a nine-year-old stand in a yard for three hours in the cold to watch a woman die, and then you named the woman, and the child went home not knowing what she had seen. My house has counted the cost of that and cannot make it come out. Enter us as refusing and enter the reason, all of it, including this sentence."*

Maren Vaeleth entered all of it, including that sentence.

**House Korrindal** refused because Ess-Vaelen refused.

**House Thal-Ann** refused and gave no reason, which under the debt-marker system means their obligation is *lo*, past its term, and they are entered as having defaulted instead of declined. That is worse for them than for Korvaeth and they knew it and did it anyway, and nobody in four quarters has ever got a straight answer about why.

**And House Vosk refused last.**

Korrin Vosk was forty-eight and ran a fruit estate and eleven hundred tenants and had been a boy in an orchard once, and he came to Korvaeth in person, which none of the other five did, and he asked for the chamber and got it, and he stood in front of Ilthára Korvaeth in a room panelled in drowned wood and said it to her face.

*"I told you you had no name. I was eleven. I had heard my father say it at a table and I repeated it to hurt you and it worked better than I understood. I have thought about it perhaps four hundred times and I have never once found a way to take it back that would not have made it worse."*

*"And you have spent thirty years proving me wrong, and I would have followed you into any of it. Any of it. Up to and including this morning."*

*"But you did not prove me wrong. You went into a yard and you proved me right in your own mouth, in front of the count, in a register I do not speak well enough to argue with. And I am not going to send eleven hundred people to die for a doctrine that its own author retracted at a block."*

*"Enter us as refusing. And write down that I said this and that I am sorry, because I want both halves in the record and I do not care which one they use."*

He bowed properly on the way out and Ilthára Korvaeth did not say a single word to him from the moment he entered the room to the moment he left it.

Aeldros, afterward, asked her why.

"Because he was right," she said. "And because he was eleven."

## V. THE COURTS ANSWER

The finding went out on the twentieth day and the branches answered over four weeks and the New World broke along the seams the language had been marking for four Eons.

**The Sunroot Court did nothing for nineteen days and then did the only intelligent thing available.**

They did not contest the transfer. They could not: the entry was clean, the Master of the Floor had said so on the fourth day and it had leaked by the ninth. What the Court did instead was convene, in full form, with the count called and the witnesses compensated, and vote a **regency**, on the stated ground that the Empress's name had been transferred and the *office* had not, because a name and an office are separate objects in Eressean law and always have been, and Onawa had transferred one and said nothing about the other.

It is the thinnest argument in the record of the war and it held for eleven years.

**Ovaeren, styled Regent-by-Assembly**, took the chair at Vaelmarr on the fortieth day with a validated name and no blood claim whatsoever, and the Court had chosen him precisely because he had no blood claim, since a regent with a claim is a usurper waiting for a bad winter.

He was sixty-eight, careful, personally decent, an administrator of forty years' standing, and he understood on the first morning that he had been given an office specifically because nobody would follow him anywhere, and he served eleven years and never once complained about it in writing.

**The Eladrin answered with a count.**

The Winter Court sent no letter. Loreth Ann of the northern envoy stood in the Vaelmarr chamber, was asked directly for Queen Seralyth's answer, and held a silence of nine counts, and then held it past nine.

Every Concord observer present transcribed the exchange as having had nothing said in it.

A four-count is assent. A nine-count is a refusal that will not be argued. A silence held past a nine-count has moved out of language and into judgement, and the Winter Court had just passed sentence on a sovereign claimant in front of witnesses without entering a word anybody could answer. There is no reply to it. There is no grammar for a reply to it.

Ilthára Korvaeth, told of it, is recorded as having asked one question.

*"How far past?"*

The answer was eleven.

She is recorded as having said nothing further, and Aeldros, who was in the room, wrote afterward that it was the only time in the entire first year that he saw his sister look afraid.

**The Echo Elf confederation split, and split cleanly, because their grammar permits nothing else.**

An agreement that has not been *zo*-restated by every party has not been entered. Tenn Ollaren put the question to the nineteen delegates at Ollamarr and restated the Korvaeth transfer himself, and eleven restated after him, and eight did not, and the eight walked out of that hall inside the hour.

There was no argument. There was nothing to argue about. In Echo Elf there is no half-agreement, no ambiguity to litigate later, and no mechanism by which a party who declined to restate can be said to have partially agreed. Eleven houses to Korvaeth. Eight to the coast and thence to the Regency.

They have never refought it. In eleven years of war, on nine fronts, the Echo Elf houses have never once had to re-establish which side they were on, and both sides have found this so restful that neither has ever tried to turn one.

**The Sylvaaren refused to be asked, and their refusal is the one that will outlive all of this.**

Aardhae of the Dream Groves came south under a truce-bough at the request of the Regency, listened to the whole recitation of the finding, and gave a reply that the recording jurist entered verbatim and marked *unanswerable*.

*Elarieur. Sylvaar ven-dhae. Sylvaar ur.*

*Self-witnessed name. The old growth, seen-and-dreamed. The old growth stood for it.*

*I hold my name and the forest witnessed it. You were not asked because you were not there.*

Which says, to anybody who speaks the tongue, that the Groves witnessed themselves into their own sovereignty four Eons ago and have no interest whatsoever in whose name is written on a chair in a city, and that the Varrisak consider the entire war a dispute between two branches of a filing system.

It is also, and Aardhae knew it, the single most useful thing anybody said to Ilthára Korvaeth that year, because *elarieur* is the Varrisak self-witnessed form, and if the self-witnessed form is valid then Ilthára has a name after all.

She did not take it.

Aeldros begged her to take it, in the chamber, on the record, in front of Vessa Thenn-Ashal and Corlan Mirroven, and she refused in eleven words that are now carved above the eastern door at Korvaeth.

*"I will not hold my name from a forest I have never stood in."*

Vessa Thenn-Ashal is entered as having said, afterward, to nobody in particular, that she had followed the woman for a year before she understood that she was mad, and had gone on following her for eleven more after she did.

## VI. THE FIRST YEAR

The war did not start with a battle. It started with a road and an argument about who was allowed to use it.

**The Vessel road** runs from the drowned groves up through Korrindal ground to the Vaelmarr terraces and it carries the salt, the timber and the winter grain, and it had been open under an eleven-house compact since before either claimant was born. Korrindal refused Korvaeth and therefore closed it, and Korvaeth needed it and therefore opened it, and the opening took four days and two hundred and eleven lives and is entered in the Regency's own record as *the Vessel Assize*, because the Regency has never once called anything in this war a battle.

It was Aeldros who opened it, with Mirroven under him, and he did it competently and cheaply and hated every hour of it.

He was not a soldier. He had been trained as a jurist because his name was validated and the family's legal work fell to the one member of it who could hold an estate, and he had spent nineteen years reading procedure and eleven months learning to move eleven hundred men down a road in winter. What he turned out to have was the thing nobody expects: an absolute, unhurried patience with logistics, and a jurist's memory for what he had promised whom.

Corlan Mirroven, who was frightened of everybody, was not frightened of Aeldros, and said so, and that sentence went round the northern camps inside a month and did more for the war effort than the road did.

**The Queen took the field twice in the first year and won both.**

Sunfall Ford in the fourth month, against a Regency column under an officer who assumed she would fight the way a doctrine fights. She did not. She put Thenn-Ashal's ward-carriers into the water at night, upstream, and let the ford silt itself for eleven hours, and took the column on ground that had been a crossing that morning and was a bog by noon, and lost forty men and killed nine hundred.

**And Ess-Vaelen Green in the eighth**, which was worse and which is the one people mean when they say her name.

Thela Ess-Vaelen, who had refused her in a chamber and gone home and been made head of her house for it, held the Green with four thousand and a good position and no reason to expect a winter assault. Ilthára Korvaeth came over the northern hedge line in the ninth hour of the coldest night of the year and took the position in under three hours and took Thela Ess-Vaelen alive.

She did not execute her.

She convened, in the field, in form, with a Reckoner and a count and the witnesses compensated out of the Korvaeth chest, and she named Thela Ess-Vaelen in full form before eleven hundred paid witnesses, and thereby validated the name of a woman who had refused her, and entered it, and let her go.

*"Elar-uren Thela Ess-Vaelen. Ie-sae. Ureth zo. Uren, uren, uren."*

The three-fold witness word again. The Elareth closing. The consecration form, in a frozen field, over a prisoner.

Nobody has ever satisfactorily explained it.

The Regency's own jurists spent six weeks on it and concluded that it accomplished nothing legally, cost Korvaeth eleven hundred witness-fees, and gave a hostile house a validated name it could now use against her in any court in four quarters. Aeldros called it the worst thing she had ever done, in writing, in a letter he did not send and which survives.

Thela Ess-Vaelen went home to a house that now held its name under Eressean validation, performed by the Queen of the north, and spent the next four years being unable to fight her, and knew exactly what had been done to her, and has never forgiven it.

*"She did not spare me,"* is her entered position. *"She named me. There is a difference and I am the only person alive who has had it done to her, and I would rather she had used the blade."*

---

**By the end of the first year the New World held five things and none of them was a border.**

The Regency at Vaelmarr, holding the office and not the name, under a sixty-eight-year-old administrator nobody would follow.

The Queen at Korvaeth, holding the name in the contingent form by the archive's own finding, with five Under-Houses, eleven Echo Elf houses, the deep roads and the black wards.

Six refusing houses who were not loyalists and not rebels and who had between them the fruit, the grain, the Vessel charter and eleven hundred miles of ground both sides had to cross to reach each other. Both sides courted them for eleven years. Neither ever took them.

Velthaeir, saying nothing, entering everything, hated by every party for the same eleven words.

And the Groves, who considered the entire matter a dispute between two branches of a filing system and had stopped attending.

**And there was a sixth thing, and nobody counted it for four years.**

The Tsohanto Reach had gone quiet.

It had gone quiet without going hostile and without declaring. Every barrow-ground east of the Vessel line had acquired, over the nine days after the yard, the specific stillness of a place already grieved for, and it held that stillness with nobody maintaining it, and no Tsohanto delegation came to any hall of either party for eleven years.

The Reach listens. Something in the tree line always notices first. Whatever it noticed on the eleventh day of the withheld season, it has not yet said.

## VII. THE MAD QUEEN

The byname was not new and it was not earned in that yard.

They had called her the Mad Queen of Korvaeth since she was twenty-six, and it had meant, at the time, a woman with no name who behaved as though she had one, which is a thing the world finds funny for about a decade and then finds frightening. Aeldros had defended her in eleven halls against it and had believed the defence.

What changed after the yard was that the word acquired evidence.

And the evidence is the problem, and it is why the byname will outlive every other fact in this account, because the evidence is not raving and it is not blood and there is nothing in it that a physician could look at. The evidence is a ledger.

She had an empire in her hand and she gave it away for a courtesy to a woman she had just killed.

She was offered a valid name by the Dream Groves and refused it on the grounds that she had never stood in the forest.

She took Thela Ess-Vaelen alive at Ess-Vaelen Green and named her, at a cost of eleven hundred witness-fees, to no legal purpose anybody has ever identified, and let her go.

Every one of those is a decision. Every one of them was made by a woman who saw the consequence clearly beforehand and said so afterward and has never once claimed she was confused. And that is precisely what makes it unanswerable, because a ruler who did not understand what she was doing can be pitied and a ruler who understood perfectly and did it anyway cannot be filed under anything else.

Aeldros Korvaeth put it into a letter in the third year, to Vessa Thenn-Ashal, and the letter survives because Vessa kept everything.

*"They will not hear it as love. They will hear it as a woman who cannot tell the difference between an empire and an afternoon. That is what mad is, out there. It has no raving in it and no blood. It is a ruler who spent the wrong thing on the wrong thing and could not explain the exchange rate afterward."*

*"And the terrible part, Vessa, is that she can explain it. She explained it to me in eleven words on the night of the yard and I have not been able to put it down since. She said it was worth it, and I asked to whom, and she said: to me. She said it is the only ledger in this house that nobody gets to audit."*

*"I have spent three years trying to find the flaw in that and I cannot. It is either the sanest sentence anybody in this family has ever produced or it is the exact centre of the illness, and I have stopped being certain which, and I am not sure the two are different."*

---

Maren Vaeleth retired in the fourth year at sixty-eight, having entered every position of the first three, and did one thing on her way out that was not in the office.

She went to the vault and took out the yard ledger and read the leaf again, all of it, the three hundred and six and the four abstaining and the two unpaid, and the naming in her own hand with the witness word entered three times.

Then she took out a second book, which had no seal on it and no standing anywhere, and which she had kept for forty-one years and had never shown to a living soul, and she made her last entry in it.

*The Vosk woman asked me twice whether she could bring her granddaughter. She is nine. I said the count does not stop for age.*

*The child stood in the fourth rank for three hours in the cold and watched a woman die and then watched the woman who killed her put a hand on her hair and give her back her name in a register the child does not speak.*

*I have entered the legal effect of that morning eleven times in three different instruments and every entry was correct.*

*Not one of them says what the child saw.*

*I do not know what to do about this and I am going to be dead before anybody solves it. Let the record say that the Reckoner of Korvaeth knew there was a thing the record could not hold, and entered that too, in a book nobody will read, because entering it was the only thing left in the office that I still believed in.*

She closed the book and put it back on the shelf where she had kept it, and it is still there, and Velthaeir has never asked for it.

---

Ilthára Korvaeth is fifty-one now and holds nine hundred miles and has never once been called Empress by anybody with standing to call her one.

She signs with the name nobody gave her. She has signed with it since she was seventeen years old, on every instrument, in the contingent form, in her own hand, and there are eleven thousand documents in four quarters bearing a signature that says, in the plainest form the language has: *this is the name I am holding, and you are entitled to ask by what right.*

She has never been asked.

That is the thing Aeldros wrote about in the eleventh year and never resolved. In three decades of war, on nine fronts, across four quarters, with every jurist in Velthaeir holding a finding that says her name is contingent, **not one person has ever stood up in a hall and asked her by what right.**

The form is there. It has always been there. It is two words long and any commoner in any market in the north could say it to her face without breaking a single law.

Nobody has.

And Aeldros Korvaeth, who is called the Validated and has heard it as an accusation every day since he was nineteen, has come to believe that this is the entire answer to his sister, and that he worked it out too late to say it to her, and that she would not have accepted it anyway.

They will not ask her because they know what she would answer.

She would tell them about an orchard.

### The one decision I made, and I want it ruled

**She said the name.**

You left it open as the hinge and I could not build an arc without turning it, so I turned it and built everything on the turn. If you want it the other way it is one movement rewritten and the rest still stands, because the fracture works from either side — it just fractures along different lines.

Here is why I chose it.

**Mechanically it is the catastrophe.** Onawa opens the window herself on *sae-ureth*, which she has unquestioned standing to do, and thereby converts three hundred and six compensated execution-witnesses into a convened assembly, because compensation does not specify what is witnessed, only that the witness was paid. A full-form naming by the party who will hold the vacancy, inside that window, is a validated utterance and therefore an act of assembly. So the moment Ilthára says it, the record shows **Korvaeth performing Eressean procedure** — which proves assembly was possible in the north on an ordinary Tuesday, which kills the Varrisak doctrine that assembly is impossible, which voids every unassembled holding in the north, **starting with her own**.

She wins an empire and voids herself in the same breath, and she sees it coming and does it anyway.

**Dramatically it is the byname.** *Mad* out there does not mean raving. It means a ruler who spent the wrong thing on the wrong thing and cannot explain the exchange rate.

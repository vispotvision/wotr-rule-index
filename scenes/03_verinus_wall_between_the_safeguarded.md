# The Wall Between the Safeguarded

*The root of the wound. Continuous.*

---

*From the Rubric, on Entombment. The leaf is sewn in at the back of the press with a linen tab, and the tab is the only one in the volume that is grey.*

Entombment does not kill and does not imprison. It takes the subject out of the flow: out of fate, out of memory, out of the reach of the realm. What is entombed stops being a thing the world can be about.

The bill of goods: a threshold with a lintel, a mirror or a still water, the mark of binding, the mark of the seal, the mark of finality, the mark of reverence, and a vessel.

The vessel is the wall. The rite maintains itself out of the celebrant's own substance and does not stop drawing while the seal stands. Most who perform Entombment do not come back. The remainder are sealed alongside the thing they sealed, which the register lists as the successful outcome.

Reserve the rite for what cannot be permitted to continue. This is a clause of the Covenant and it has been read narrowly four times and broadly once.

*Recorded failure. Nightglass. The seal was closed nine days early on an assessment that four guilds had signed and none had checked. Planar stability ruptured along the join and the soul-feedback ran back down every line that touched the working. The Inner Circles struck the rite from the lawful register in the following spring and have not restored it. The four guilds are entered by name. Two of the names have no members.*

*Ideh Genesis, called the Bright, set this seal between the two worlds and did not set it around anything. He put it along the join and became the join. The register does not record his death and does not record him afterward, and the Accord has twice declined to say which of those two entries is missing.*

---

The lines began to go.

He felt them in the chest, where the Song was, because they came down the same road. One, and then eleven, and then a number that stopped being a number and became a texture, a fraying at the front of him, and Verinus stood at the root of the wound under a sky he had carried in with him and understood what was happening across the Inner World from the inside of the Plane of Fate before any bell in any tower had begun to ring.

Ash was still on his tongue. Beeswax and lime and the sweet dead smell of a cellar four hundred years old, and it stayed, and under it now came something with no smell at all.

Bonds terminating. That was the flat word for it and he reached for the flat word because the alternative was to feel it accurately.

A household in the salt cities losing three of five and the two left standing in a doorway with the light behind them. A ward-keeper in a Kharven valley going out mid-breath and the ward going out four hours later, which nobody would connect. A woman who had known the winter planting pattern for a district of nine thousand people and had taught it to nobody, because she was sixty-one and had assumed she had time.

The physic garden.

He knew that one. It came up the line with a shape he recognised and he had walked in it nine days ago with poppy heads in his hand and a letter he had already decided to send, and the boy who kept it had swept the path in front of him without looking up, and Verinus had not learned his name and had meant to.

He did not learn it now.

The Song went up a degree and told him what to do with it.

It did not use words. It had never used words. It arrived as the Scale arrived, weight in a place where weight is information, and what it gave him was this: the cries are the material. The pain is the material. Let them go down into the ground and let the ground hold them, because a Paragon is not born out of a good year. He is assembled out of what a generation could not carry, and the greater the load the sooner the thing arrives that can lift it, and every one of these deaths is a stone in a foundation that a child is going to stand on.

Verinus, who had spent nineteen years refusing to let his Court spend a single person it could not account for, heard the instruction and did not argue with it.

*I could stop perhaps four thousand of them. I have the reach for it now, and spending the reach spends the rite, and there would be no rite left afterward. Four thousand against a seal that holds for an age. The sum is sound. I have done a sound sum on a person before and I know exactly how it reads from the other side, and I have checked this one twice in the time available, which was none.*

*And I want it entered that I did not hesitate, because a man who hesitates gets to keep the hesitation afterward and call it conscience, and I am not going to be permitted anything afterward, and I would rather the record was clean.*

He let them go.

Thirty-five in every hundred, across the Inner World, out of a black sky with no argument attached, and the man appointed to weigh stood at the root of it and permitted the number because he had been told it was fuel.

Something in him that had held since he was fifty-two went over quietly and did not right itself.

"How little that is going to mean," Verinus said.

His voice went out flat and level and the Song carried it, and it did not shake, and that was the worst thing about it.

"You have made a number. Congratulations on the number. I want you to understand what you have actually spent it on, because I have the only instrument in four worlds that can read the receipt and I am going to read it to you once."

He put both hands behind his back, which was the posture, and the posture was ridiculous here and he took it anyway.

"There is a child. He is alive at this moment. He is not in the Inner World and I am not going to tell you a great deal more about him than that, because the single most useful thing I have ever done for him was to keep him out of a day-book." The wings held steady off his shoulders and the gold in them had stopped spreading. "The Church has an apparatus for finding him. Men give their whole working lives to the reading of it. I have refused that apparatus for nineteen years on the grounds that a thing declared divine stops being a thing anyone will argue with, and I have been right about that, and I remain right about that."

The fraying at the front of him went on and he made himself keep speaking through it.

"And you have just built him."

A stream that had been black over a kingdom somewhere ceased to have a kingdom under it.

"Every household that comes apart tonight is a person that child is going to meet. Every ward that fails in the third week because nobody knew it needed feeding. Every judge gone with the precedent still in his head and every bridge with no master and every congregation that kept its priest and lost its people. That is the ground he grows in. You have laid it. You have laid it thick and you have laid it fast, and you have made him eager, which is the part that should frighten you, because a Paragon assembled out of a slow century is a magistrate and a Paragon assembled in an afternoon is something else, and I do not have a word for what you have just commissioned."

The Song shifted under the sentence and did not contradict it.

"You have said that judgment and destruction differ because one requires a reason. That is correct and it is the only correct sentence in your speech, and you have drawn the wrong conclusion from it, which I have watched clever men do at four tables in this life." His hands stayed behind his back. "A reason is a constraint. A constraint is what keeps a course of action from generating the next one. You have removed the constraint, so nothing you do from this hour will be answerable to anything you did in the last one, and you will therefore make a sequence of decisions that each look correct standing alone. That is how ends arrive. A string of them, laid end to end, each one defensible, by a man who had stopped checking."

The fracture on his forearm opened another finger's width and the gold in it had gone to a deeper colour.

"And behind the speech, and behind the annihilation, and behind the laughing." Verinus let a breath out. "There is a hand in you. I can see the shape of the fingers. You have taken a thing out of a Titan and you are wearing it and you have been careful to call it yours, and it is still doing what it was made to do, and it is doing it through you, and you have organised an entire cosmology around not noticing."

He straightened his back, which no longer complained about anything.

"You are a puppet, titan. You will be reminded of your standing when the One True Judger rises, and he will not be an argument, and he will not be a doctrine, and he will not require your agreement."

The bell rang.

It came from above the sky he had brought with him. One stroke, enormous, so far down in the register that the front of his ribs took it before his ears did, and the old wrong stars shook in the gold and steadied. Verinus had heard the small version of that bell through a cellar wall three days ago and had assumed it was an orchard bell and had assumed the shears with it were pruning, and he had been unable to interpret either, and he had written down that he could not.

He read this one immediately.

*Almost. That is the courtesy stroke. That is a man being told the hour so he can put his affairs in order, and I have perhaps as long as it takes to say the thing properly, and I have never in my life needed longer than that.*

He did not ask who was ringing. He would have given the rest of it to know.

"Monstrous is the whole of what you are going to amount to," Verinus said. "A monstrosity of unusual size, filed as one, and filed in the appendix." He held the gold steady off his shoulders. "Sovereigns go in the body of the register. You go after the weak because the totality of the strong will not fit inside you, and I have met that in men who ran a parish and I am unimpressed to find it running a Void."

The wings came all the way out.

"So allow this weak man to leave you with one gift."

Verinus put his hands in front of him and turned the palms up, which is the oldest posture in the Rubric and means the celebrant is holding nothing and intends to be holding nothing at the close.

"Findantur caeli."

Let the heavens be split. They split. The gold went open from horizon to horizon of a place with no horizon, and behind the gold, the second thing, the join, the seam where the Void gave onto the Material World, exposed along its whole length and turned to the light.

"Claudatur ostium inter vacuum et mundum."

Let the door between the Void and the world be shut.

"Sepeliantur in veritate."

Let them be entombed in the truth.

"Ligetur hoc vas intemporali, incredibili."

Let this vessel be bound to the timeless, to the unbelievable.

He heard the last word leave him and go, and the Song took it and held it, and the four marks came up in the gold overhead where nothing had drawn them: binding, seal, threshold, reverence. The fourth was the one he had never understood until this moment. Reverence. The rite requires the celebrant to honour the thing he is sealing. The Rubric gives no reason and the Court has never asked for one and Verinus, at twenty-six years old, in a body that had spent seventy-one, found that he could produce it, and produced it, and it cost him more than the knee had.

"Fiam murus inter servata et hoc."

Let me become the wall between the safeguarded and this.

"Fiam fluxus fati. Fiam puritas vera."

Let me become the flow of fate. Let me become the true purity.

"Ritus sigilli," said Verinus. "Custodia aeterna."

The realm answered.

It came from underneath, which he had not expected and should have. The world itself took hold. Nothing of his own strength was anywhere in it, because Entombment has never run on the celebrant's power, and every practitioner who assumed otherwise is entered at Nightglass. It runs on the office. It runs on the Archon whose whole function is that everything which exists has an edge, and it asks one question of the thing in front of it, and the question is whether the thing is here.

Verinus had put that argument to a Curia at fifty-two and had been told it was elegant and inapplicable.

*A thing that exists has a boundary. A thing in a body has a further boundary, and the second is stricter, and a construct raised by an Archon and given requirements carries the requirements stamped through it like a coin. And the Void has no boundary and the Titan of nothing cannot be measured and every scholar who has looked at this has stopped at that sentence and filed the matter. They stopped one step early. I am not sealing a thing. I am sealing a doing. The skill is the limit. A performance has an edge because it is being performed, and I do not have to find the edge of him, I only have to find the edge of what he is doing with his hands.*

The flow began.

It came up through the join in a slow seismic turn, the whole substance of the realm shifting weight with the deliberation of ground about to slide, and it reached past Verinus without acknowledging him and took hold of the calamitous thing at the root of the wound.

It did not take all of it.

He felt the seal find its purchase and he felt exactly how much purchase it had found, and it was pieces. The borrowed grammar. The parts that were a doing and no part of a being: the propagation, the reach that let collapse travel through structures the Void had never been able to touch, the ancient interval between standing and failing that had come out of a Titan and had been carried here in somebody else's chest. Fate-threads went taut all across the wound and the seismic flow took them and turned them down into the join, and the gold marks overhead came together into one, and the seam began, along its full length, to close.

The rest of him stayed where it was. The seal had no grip on it. Verinus had known that would be the case since the third clause and had said the rite anyway, because half of a monstrous thing is a thing that can be fought by people who cannot fight the whole of it, and that was the gift, and he had never claimed it was generous.

Then the wall was made and he was the material.

It went into him from the hands. His substance began to go out of him and into the seam, laid along the join, spread thin over a length that had no length, and the Song went with it and became the mortar. He felt the years empty out. Nineteen at the Court, forty-five in the order, a boy on a slope with chalk on his fingers, all of it going out of him along the seam in a direction that was flat and endless and had a wall in it now with his name in the material.

And the anchor line went taut.

He felt it snap out from the middle of him and run down and back, out of the Void, through the join, into a cellar under Altherion, into a man standing on flags in the wrong skin with no teeth in his lower jaw who believed that four people were alive and had no idea in the world that he was load-bearing. It went tight. It would stay tight. Whatever the seal drew from now on, it would draw down that line as well, on the far end, into a soul that had been made a fixed point at dawn by a man who did not ask.

Aurelian would hold. That was the sum, and it was a good sum, and there had been no time to check it at all.

The bell rang the second stroke.

Verinus felt the last of himself go out along the join, and the seam took his shape, and the gold above him closed over the old wrong stars one arrangement at a time. He got his right hand up while there was still a hand to get up. He set the palm flat against the seam he had become, the way a man puts his weight on a door he intends to hold, and pushed.

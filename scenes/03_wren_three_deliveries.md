# Three Deliveries

*The narrows, the Ashgate road. Continuous.*

---

Inside the corona the air had a grain to it.

Wren Greymane learned that in the first second of standing up in it, and he learned it through his teeth, because the violet did not press on him and did not burn him and did not do any of the things a man's body has language for. It made every breath arrive at the back of his throat about a quarter-second after he had asked for it. It made his own blood go up his face. It made the frozen mud under his boots stop being a surface that pushed back and start being a surface that agreed with him, provisionally, and could be persuaded otherwise.

The road tilted.

It did not tilt. He checked it twice, with the shield rim planted, and both times the road was level and both times his own inner ear reported a slope running away toward the man in front of him, and he stopped checking on the third and decided to fight on a hill.

That decision was the only reason he lived the next forty seconds.

Kaalabad came at a walk.

He did not close in a rush and did not need to. He came the length of eleven paces with Zarakal held low on the right in a hanging guard that no manual in the North had a name for, and every pace put a crack in the road behind him, and behind the cracks the road sagged half an inch and stayed sagged.

*He is doing that to himself and he does not care.*

*It is not free. Nothing is free. He has been on that road since the ninth hour and he cracks it every time he lands and the sky came down into him and none of that is free, and I have to live long enough to find out what it costs.*

The second delivery came underhand from the right hip, rising, aimed to take him from the groin to the collarbone.

Wren dropped the shield's bottom edge and turned the face out and got his weight forward and down and let the road's imaginary slope help him, which it did.

"Bulwark."

Grey went blue for about a third of the shield's face and no further.

That told him more than anything else in the fight had. Three deliveries had always meant three whole ones, boss to rim, and what came up this time was a third of a shield's worth and it came up sluggish, and the rest of the face stayed grey and dead under his arm like a board.

Zarakal hit the live third.

The note was there and it was shorter. The distribution held across the whole face regardless, and Wren felt it as the same even flat pressure on his forearm, an entire mountain coming through a hand laid open instead of a nail. His arm did not break. The oak did.

It went along the grain from the boss to the top edge in a single split that opened four fingers wide and let daylight through it, and the hide on the front went with it, and the second binding on the boss let go and the rivets came out of the rim on the right side and hung.

He went back seven feet and stayed on his feet by the width of a decision.

*Two.*

*And it was not a whole one. That was a third and it took the shield and there is not a third left in it.*

The corona around Kaalabad had thickened at the wrists.

Wren saw that on the recovery and did not know what he was seeing. The violet had gathered at both cuffs, denser than the rest of it, moving in a slow turn, and under the right cuff the plate had begun to stand off the arm by about a finger's width where it had sat flush ten minutes ago.

He put it away to look at later, and there would be no later, and some part of him knew that and filed it anyway because that was how he had been built.

He went to the belt.

Second charge. Ring, throw, and this time he did not throw it at the man.

He threw it low and long, past Kaalabad's left boot, twenty feet up the road behind him, into the sag.

"Compact."

The sphere opened in the cracked road instead of in the air.

It went into the fissures first because that was where the space was, and it filled them, and then it kept expanding and the road had nowhere to put it. Forty feet of the Ashgate road came up. Nothing threw it. It lifted, in slabs, frozen mud and gravel and the buried corduroy of split logs some Kharven road gang had laid in the reign before last, all of it standing up on end at once with a sound that went through the whole narrows and stopped two hundred men fighting.

Kaalabad's footing went out from under him.

He came down onto one knee in the wreck of it, and the pull round him caught the falling slabs and drew them inward, and they arrived at him from four directions and stacked against him and pinned his own weapon between his chest and about a ton of the road he had been standing on.

Wren was already moving.

He came off the imaginary slope with the ruined shield up and the arming sword in his right hand, and he did not go for the head and did not go for the chest, and he went for the one place on that man that had ever shown him anything, which was the scarred band around the throat above the gorget.

He got the point in.

Two inches, maybe three, into the side of the neck under the ear, through scar tissue that felt like driving into a barrel stave, and he twisted the wrist and put his shoulder behind it and drove.

The blade stopped.

There was nothing in there for it to go into. Wren had killed thirty men with that sword and he knew the whole vocabulary of what a body does around a point, the give and the grate and the awful ease of it, and there was none of that. The steel went two inches into a man's neck and met something that had no interest in being cut, and the last four inches of the blade bent instead, slowly, in his hand, and then went.

He got a look at Kaalabad's face from eight inches away.

*He is going to want it recorded that he did this.*

*Forty years and men do not step toward me and this one has stepped toward me twice and put a road on my back and a sword in my neck, and it is going to be a Greymane, and the Greymanes are going to be extinct by the spring, and nobody is ever going to be told.*

The slabs came off him.

They did not fall away. They went out, all of it, forty feet of a road going outward in every direction at once from a man getting up off one knee, and Wren took the whole leading edge of that across the chest and the ruined shield and went backward through the air for a distance he did not measure.

He landed on the frozen ditch bank on his back with the shield still strapped on and his left shoulder out of its socket.

The third delivery took eleven seconds to arrive.

He used them. He got the arm back in by rolling onto it against the bank, which he had done twice before in his life and which made him black out for a portion of a second, and he got up onto one knee, and he got the shield in front of him with a dead arm and a split face and four fingers of daylight through the middle of it.

"Bulwark," said Wren Greymane.

Nothing happened.

He looked at it. Grey, and split, and the boss hanging off the second binding, and the iron rim gone from the right side, and the crack he had put in it at the ridge sixteen months ago running up through the whole of the rest.

Zarakal came down.

The fluke went through the shield and through the arm behind it and through the meat of his left shoulder and into the frozen ditch bank underneath, and it did all of that in one motion and pinned him to Kharven.

The pain arrived late and enormous. Wren heard himself make a noise. He had time to be embarrassed by it.

He got his right hand up and took hold of the shaft, which was a thing to do, and he did it because a man's hands go where they go.

Kaalabad set his boot on the shield.

*He is looking at my face. He wants the face.*

*Then have it. I have had sixteen months of men spitting in it and it is the only thing I have left that is mine to spend.*

Wren Greymane looked up at him and did not close his eyes.

The spike came down through the collarbone and the top of the chest and the whole architecture under it, and it went into the bank, and everything below Wren's throat stopped being a place he lived.

He had about nine seconds.

He spent four of them getting his right hand off the shaft and down to the belt, and the fingers had nothing left in them and had to be dragged, and he got two of them under the ring of the third charge and could not pull it.

So he brought it up to his mouth.

*Bram.*

*The book is in the left saddlebag under the oilcloth and the horse went south when I slapped it and somebody will find the horse and nobody will open the book, and you will go to your grave believing it, and there is no arrangement of this in which I get to fix that.*

*Sixteen months and I never once got word to you. Every route ran through somebody, and every somebody was a way in, and it was the correct decision. I would make it again. I have made it every single morning since the ridge and I made it again this morning.*

*I am sorry about the smiling. I have never been able to account for it.*

He pulled the ring with his teeth.

"Comp."

It went off in his own hand.

Twenty feet of aetheric material opened at the level of Wren Greymane's chest and took his arm off at the elbow and put Kaalabad backward off the ditch bank and into the road, and the shaft came out of the bank with him, and out of the shield and the arm and the shoulder and the chest, and Wren was already gone by then and did not have to feel any of it.

The sphere bent around the man as he went, as it had the first time, and closed behind him, and travelled on.

It went up the narrows and through the press.

Sixty-odd men were still on the ground in the corona's radius where the fever had put them, Greymane and Tenrai together, and the wave came through under them and picked them up and rolled them forty feet toward the Kharven end of the road, and it broke three legs and a jaw and it did not kill any of them, and every one of them who could stand afterward was standing on the far side of the man they had been lying in front of.

They ran. All of them, both colours, in the same direction, and nothing came after them, because the thing that would have come after them was face down in the road with a road on top of it.

Cass Holloway got up out of that and ran with the rest and did not know whose wave it had been.

---

Kaalabad came out of the mud in his own time.

He put the shaft across his knees and knelt on the broken road for a moment and did nothing at all, which was a thing nobody had seen him do.

Then he stood, and shook the right hand once, and looked at it.

The gauntlet would not come off. The plate had stood off the arm by a finger's width at the wrist and now it had stood off further and the leather under it had gone tight, and the whole hand from the knuckles to the forearm had begun to swell against the steel from the inside, purple under the nail beds, the skin between the fingers gone shiny and full.

He looked at it for a while.

Then he put the shaft in the left hand, and walked back down the narrows toward the Kharven gates, and did not look at the body on the ditch bank, and did not look at the shield either.

Four hundred paces up the road, on foot, alone, coming the other way, a man in Moto harness with a sword low along his right leg had stopped walking and was watching him come.

## THE ORCHARD ROAD, SUM-GOL, THE SAME MORNING

The snow at Sum-gol was eleven days old and had a crust on it that took a man's weight for one step in three.

Kwon Mu-jin came up the orchard road with his right arm hanging.

It had been hanging for six weeks. There was no wound in it and there had never been a wound in it, and the fingers were warm, and the nails grew, and the physician at the school had spent four days on it and had written *no finding* in a hand that got smaller toward the end of the report.

He had stopped trying to lift it in the second week. Geuk-hon had told him to stop, in a room with a table in it and two cups on the table, and had said it in the voice of a man describing something he had already watched happen once.

*You are still holding that arm the same way you held the roster.*

*You want it back the way it was, annotated and gradeable, and you want the annotation to say that it is yours. It is not going to say that again. Put it down. What you have left is enough, and it has always been enough, and the reason you never found that out is that nobody in four hundred years has taken enough away from you in one afternoon to make you count what was left.*

He had counted.

The orchard came up on his left, thirty-one trees, eleven of them down at the root since the summer and none of them cleared, because nobody at the school had been willing to be the one who cleared them. The wall that had stood since before the family kept a ledger was still open at the place where a live pine had gone through it end over end.

Raga came out from under the trees.

Three-limbed, and the left forequarter rebuilt out of somebody's cartwheel and a length of mill shaft and two hundredweight of the wall, and the socket where the green eye had been packed with orchard clay and river stone and burning, faintly, from the inside. The compact had held. The animal had never once asked him why the arrangement had changed.

It put its head down beside him and walked.

There was smoke on the western sky where no town was, and there had been since the ninth hour, and under Mu-jin's boots the ground had been carrying a note through the crust since before dawn, low and steady and wrong, and he had walked eleven miles toward it instead of away.

He stopped at the top of the orchard road.

Below him the Ashgate valley lay open all the way to the Kharven narrows, and there was a violet standing in it, and a road that had come apart, and something on a ditch bank with a shield pinned to it.

Mu-jin looked at that for a long moment.

Then he lifted his left hand, which was the only one he had now, and drew the circle in the air in front of him, and it took under three seconds, and it opened.

## Notes (author-facing, not canon)

**Wren dies and he earns every second of it.** Three Bulwarks were always the number and he does not get three. The second comes up a third of a shield and takes the oak, the third does not come up at all, and he says the word over a dead board and nothing happens. That silence is the moment he dies, four beats before the spike.

**What he wins.** Not the fight. The third Compact goes off in his own hand and takes his arm off, and the wave rolls sixty men who were flat in the corona forty feet toward the Kharven end of the road, breaks three legs and a jaw, kills none of them, and puts every one of them on the far side of the man they were lying in front of. They run and nothing follows. Cass Holloway is in that and does not know whose wave it was. That is the whole of Wren Greymane's victory and nobody will ever attribute it to him.

**The book.** Left saddlebag, under the oilcloth, on a horse he slapped away himself. Somebody will find the horse and nobody will open the book, and Bram goes to his grave believing it. Wren's last coherent thought is that there is no arrangement of this in which he gets to fix that, followed by *I am sorry about the smiling. I have never been able to account for it.*

**Kaalabad's cost is canon, not invented.** The swelling hand from the Sum-gol fight. It starts at the wrists inside the corona, the plate stands off the arm by a finger's width, and by the end the gauntlet will not come off and the hand is swelling against the steel from the inside. Wren sees it mid-fight, files it, and dies without learning what it means. That is his ignorance beat and it is the reader's information.

**The read paid off and still lost.** He worked out that the pull governs the ground under Kaalabad as much as anything else, so the second Compact goes into the road behind him instead of at him, and forty feet of the Ashgate road comes up and the pull itself stacks the slabs onto the man who made it. He gets a sword three inches into the scarred band on the throat, and it stops, and the last four inches bend in his hand. Correct read, correct execution, insufficient.

**Kaalabad's one italic** is the second half of his line from the precinct: forty years and men do not step toward him, and this one has done it twice, and it is going to be a Greymane, and the Greymanes will be extinct by spring, and nobody is ever going to be told.

**Mu-jin.** Glimpse only, per your ask, and it stops on the circle opening. The arm is still dead six weeks on, no wound, no mark, *no finding* in a physician's hand that gets smaller toward the end of the report. Geuk-hon's lesson is the one that had to be there: he is still holding the arm the way he held the roster, wanting it annotated and gradeable and marked as his, and nobody in four hundred years had taken enough away from him in one afternoon to make him count what was left. Raga is three-limbed, rebuilt out of a cartwheel and mill shaft and the wall, socket packed with orchard clay and burning faintly from inside, and has never asked him why the arrangement changed. He draws with the left hand because it is the only one he has now.

**Canon and rulings.**
1. *Sodoku is four hundred paces up the road* at the close, on foot, watching Kaalabad walk toward him. Mu-jin is coming down the orchard road from the other side. Both arrive into the same valley and neither knows about the other. Your call which contact happens first.
2. *The corona's cost.* I have kept the swelling as the only visible price and have not priced the aurora itself. Whatever the sky spent into him has not been paid for yet and that is deliberate.
3. *Wren's kit is spent.* One charge left on a belt on a corpse in a ditch, and the smith is dead.
4. *Geuk-hon's dialogue* is originated. He is Mu-jin's own prior soul, so it is not another creator's character, but say the word if the voice is wrong.

**Open.** Who finds the body. Whether the horse goes south far enough. What the arm is, since six weeks and a physician have produced nothing.

# What the Sky Does Not Ask

*The Ashgate road, from the Kharven side. The ninth hour.*

---

Cold coming up off the road through the soles. Grease and old leather where the gorget sat under his jaw. The wind out of the northwest carrying woodsmoke from a village that had no business burning at this hour, and under the smoke the sourness of two thousand men who had slept in harness.

Then the sound of the column ahead of him changing.

Sodoku Moto sat on the Titan Droval a quarter mile short of the narrows and watched the Greymanes turn.

It went along the enemy column from the middle outward, which is where it had to start, and it was over inside the time a man needs to fill his lungs. Six hundred spears reversing on the six hundred they had marched beside. The Tenrai officers were already down. He counted eleven of them off the horses before he stopped counting, and he had been in enough of these to know what it meant that the eleven went first and went together.

Wren Greymane had built this.

That was the second thing Sodoku understood and it arrived a half-beat behind the first, and there was no pleasure anywhere in it.

Sixteen months of it. The defection at the ridge. The smile across two hundred paces of dead men that had gone into every mouth in the North and had been repeated to Sodoku three times by three separate people who each thought they were the first to tell him. Every village between here and the Stormfold saying the word traitor about a man who had been standing in the enemy's saddle waiting for a road narrow enough.

*Sonzai.*

*He put a man inside. That is his hand and nobody else's, and I have known him since we were both small enough to fit under the same table, and it did not occur to me once.*

*Nineteen years and it did not occur to me once. I have been thinking about walls. Whether they hold, whether they can be held, how many men on the wall and how long the stack lasts. Everything I have built since I came out of the water has been a thing that stands still and takes a blow.*

*A man who thinks in walls does not think of the door being opened from inside. That is the whole of the fault and it is mine and it has cost us nothing today, which is the only reason I am able to look at it.*

Breath went out of him and hung in front of his face and stayed there longer than it should have, because the Droval under him kept the air around them at a temperature that made a man's own breath into a small standing cloud.

Down at the near end of the narrows a Tenrai serjeant had got about forty men into a block facing the wrong way and was trying to turn them, and a Greymane spear went into him under the arm from behind while he was still shouting, and the forty came apart in the time it takes to notice they have.

He put that away and looked at the Seat behind him.

Kharven-Seat stood on its shoulder of rock with the light coming up behind it and every stack in the outer town smoking, because the outer town had been told to burn its winter wood this morning and had done it without argument, which was a thing Sodoku had asked for and had expected to have to ask for twice. Four hundred households putting a season on the fire in one day. If the road held, they would be through the Thin Weeks on borrowed heat and short rations and the death-house would take whatever the cold took, and that was the arrangement, and the town had made it in about an hour.

Hild was inside. Yoko with her, and Osric with both, and the order had been given in the yard before light and Osric had repeated it back word for word without being asked, which was Osric. Under the main keep, in the cut chamber below the old cistern, which was stone on six sides and had one way in. Hold the walls of the inner court. Hold the gatehouse. Do not sortie for anything, including him.

*He will do it. He will do exactly it, and he will do it while she argues with him, and she will argue with him, and he will not move.*

*And if the wall comes down he will put himself in the doorway, and that is a thing I ordered and did not say out loud.*

He swung down off the Droval.

His boots went into the frozen ruts and took his weight and the cold came up through the plate at the ankle. He put his hand flat on Tōga's flank and held it there.

The hide under his palm had frost standing on it in a nap, and beneath the frost the animal's warmth ran the wrong way, inward, so that touching it was touching the outside of a very deep cold that had decided to be still for a while. The great head came round. The eye found him and held.

"Go and help them," Sodoku said.

He patted the flank twice.

Tōga looked at him a moment longer.

There had been no promise made when the two of them came to terms on the ice and there was none now. The creature had ten thousand years of winter in it and had chosen, and choosing is a thing that can be done twice, and Sodoku had never once let himself forget which of them was the smaller party to the arrangement.

The Titan Droval went up the road.

It did not run. Nothing that size runs and nothing that size needs to. It went at a long rolling pace with its head low and the frost coming off it in a wake, and the ruts froze solid ahead of it in a line, and the water in them cracked as it went over, and the men of the broken column who could see it coming stopped doing whatever they had been doing.

The temperature at the narrows dropped eleven degrees in under a minute. Every Tenrai man in that press felt his fingers report late. Every Greymane man in that press felt the same thing and had grown up in it and knew what to do about it, which was to keep swinging and let the cold take the other side's hands first.

That was the whole of what Sodoku had sent, and it would be enough for the foot, and it would be nothing at all for the thing standing on the road with the purple around it.

He was on foot now. He had put his mount into the fight and his mount was the reason anyone within a mile of him took a step back, and he had done it without weighing it, the way a man puts his hand out to catch something falling off a shelf.

He turned his sight.

---

The road went out.

Reigan does not add anything to the world. It takes the world away and leaves what is under it, and what is under it is traffic, and the traffic has weight and direction and health, and a man who opens that eye stops having the ordinary one for as long as he keeps it open.

Sodoku Moto stood in the ruts with his hand still half-raised from the flank of an animal that was no longer there and could see nothing whatsoever of the field in front of him.

He found the two of them at once. That was the trouble.

He had gone looking for the road. For the six on it and the shape of the press and whether the Droval had opened anything worth putting men into, and the eye had taken him past all of that in the first instant and put him four hundred miles away without being asked, because Reigan goes to weight and weight is not always where a man wants it.

His brother came up first, north and west, at a distance he could not have put in miles, and the signature was wrong in the way that a note is wrong when the string has gone slack. It was there. It was still moving and still fighting and still absolutely itself, which was the part that told him how bad it was, because a man does not put out that much of himself unless the alternative has already been considered and rejected.

Zuberi was beside him and the reading was the same.

Two of them. Together, which meant it had come to them at the same time and in the same place, and both of them running at an output that no living body sustains, and the load coming off them was going into something that Sodoku could feel the shape of and could not measure at all.

He put the eye further out and found the edge of the thing they were standing in front of.

It had no edge.

*That is the Work.*

*The one who took the Crevice. He is out there, and he has both of them at once, and neither of them is winning and neither of them will stop.*

*And I am here.*

The read cost him eleven seconds.

He knew the number because he counted while he was blind, which is a habit and had been a habit since he was fifteen, and at nine he heard the horse.

He let go of the sight.

The world came back in a rush that put his stomach into his throat and greyed the top and sides of everything for a moment, and there was a rider forty feet away and coming, and the sword was already out of Sodoku's scabbard and low along his right leg with the edge turned out.

The horse went past.

Robin Ice hauled it round in a spray of frozen mud and got it stopped and half fell out of the saddle, and stood there holding the cheekpiece with both hands and breathing in a way that was not going to be sustainable for much longer.

Seventeen. A face still deciding what it would be, thin at the cheek and heavy at the brow, black hair cut by somebody in a hurry, ice in it. A courier's coat two sizes too big and cinched at the waist with a belt that had gone round nearly twice. Blood on his left ear that was not his. He had ridden through the narrows to get here, which nobody had told him to do and nobody would have permitted.

"My lord."

Sodoku waited.

He had learned that off Muken, who had learned it off their father, and it was the only piece of the whole inheritance he had kept without arguing. A man arriving with news will give it in his own order if you leave the silence alone, and the order he gives it in is half the message.

*Say it in the order. He told you once, in the hall, in front of everyone, say it in the order and do not say anything else, and I have got four things and I have been rehearsing the order for six miles and I have already lost one of them.*

"The southern reach is still standing. Bram had the breach at the ninth hour and was holding it and I saw him on foot. The Seat is closed and the inner court is manned. Lord Osric has the princess and the lady below the cistern and has ordered the gatehouse shut behind him." The boy got a breath in. "And Lord Lambert went out the postern before the gates opened. Six riders. No banner. He said five days."

"Wren Greymane."

"On the road. Standing." Robin Ice's jaw moved. "In front of the one with the halberd."

Sodoku looked past him at the narrows.

Lambert going out the postern before the gates opened meant Lambert had left with the road still open and would have been on the Stormfold before the aurora came up, which was either the best timing of the man's life or the worst, and there was no way to know which for five days.

Four hundred paces of road, choked end to end with two columns that had turned on each other and had nowhere to go, a ditch on the left full of men who had gone in first, a drop on the right. Somewhere in the middle of it the Droval was making a hole. And past the hole, at the far end, a violet corona standing on the road with nothing left upright around it inside sixty feet.

"Go back," Sodoku said. "Tell Osric the road is closed and I am not coming back through it. Tell him he has the gate until I do."

"My lord, the ditch is full and the narrows is."

"Then go round the ditch."

The boy stood there for a second, because he was seventeen and because the thing he wanted to say was *come with me*, and he did not say it, and that was the beginning of him being useful.

He got back onto the horse and went, and he took the long way round the ditch without being told twice, and Sodoku watched him do it and put the boy's name into the part of his head where he kept the ones who came back.

Sodoku Moto set the sword in his right hand and started up the road on foot.

The wind came round out of the north and put smoke across the whole width of the narrows and took it away again, and somewhere in the press a horn went and was cut off in the middle of the note, and the cold coming back down the road from where the Droval had gone reached him and went through the mail and found the sweat under it.

Wet wood burns eventually.

His grandmother had said it about men who could not be relied on and about the fires they made. She had said it in a doorway in a house that no longer stood, with her hands in a bowl, and she had been dead forty years, and it came up out of him now with no invitation and in her voice.

*Wet wood burns eventually. A liar never dries.*

Wren Greymane had been the wettest wood in the North for sixteen months and every hearth in the country had said so, and he was standing on that road in front of the halberd with a shield on his arm and would be dead inside the quarter hour.

*The sky does not ask whether you are ready.*

He put his shoulder into the wind and walked into the narrows.

## Notes (author-facing, not canon)

**Read and taken.** I had been rendering Sodoku externally out of caution over the PC rule. You handed me his interiority directly, so this runs in his POV throughout. Say the word if you want him back outside the camera.

**Moto POV register, first build, pending ruling.** Paratactic. Concrete nouns. Verb before reason, because he moves before he decides to, and that is a prose principle before it is a character note. Sky-referencing. Proverbs arriving uninvited and in someone else's voice. This is the highest-priority cultural gap on the docket and this scene is my proposal for it. If it reads right, I will write it up as a guide amendment.

**He is wrong about Sonzai and I have left him wrong.** The reader watched Wren build this over sixteen months, alone, with a book in his saddlebag and a road counted in summer under a false name. Sodoku credits it to his brother inside four seconds and then spends a paragraph on the fault it exposes in his own thinking, which is the more interesting error: a man who thinks in walls does not think of the door being opened from inside. He is right about the fault and wrong about the man. Osric spent three years inside the Tenrai system and nobody in this scene mentions him either.

**The read costs.** Reigan takes the world away and leaves the traffic, so he is blind for the eleven seconds he holds it, and he counts while he is blind because he has counted since he was fifteen, and at nine he hears a horse and the sword is out before he has finished coming back. It also does not go where he wanted. He opened it on the road and it took him four hundred miles because weight is not always where a man wants it, and now he knows about his brother and Zuberi and can do nothing at all with the knowing.

**He is on foot.** He put his mount into the fight to help other men's soldiers and did it without weighing it, and it costs him the only thing he had that could reach the far end of that road.

**Kharven Inventory, two drawn.** The outer town burning its winter wood in one day, which puts four hundred households into the Thin Weeks on borrowed heat with the death-house taking whatever the cold takes. And *wet wood burns eventually, a liar never dries* in his grandmother's voice, landing on Wren, who has been the wettest wood in the North for sixteen months.

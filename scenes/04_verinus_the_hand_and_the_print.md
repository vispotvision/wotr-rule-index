# The Hand and the Print

*The root of the wound. Then the Plane of Fate.*

---

*From the Rubric, the last leaf, on what the Court calls the Withdrawal. There is no bill of goods. The leaf is here because the volume had to put it somewhere.*

An Archon is not summoned, invited, or reached. The office is addressed, the office answers, and what answers is a weight in the place where weight is information.

Nothing is asked of it and nothing is offered to it. A man who believes he has bargained has misfiled the encounter.

Where an Archon takes hold of the world, the world afterward has a shape it did not have. This is the whole of the mechanism the Court is prepared to state, and Sanctum Lux has been asked eleven times to state more and has eleven times declined in writing.

*Recorded observation, and the register calls it an observation because it declines to call it a doctrine: a rite in the office's own function, performed to completion by a soul spoken for, has three times been taken up. Taken up is the register's word. In none of the three cases did the celebrant remain where he had been standing. In two of the three, the seal that was made continued to stand afterward and was no longer made of the man.*

*The third instance is entered without particulars. The clerk's hand is steady for the first line and is not steady for the second.*

---

The paying went on and the wall stopped arriving.

He knew the fault the moment it opened, because he had been reading exchanges for forty-five years and the shape of a bad one is the same at every scale. Cost leaving on schedule. Value coming back short and then shorter. His years went out of him along the seam at the full rate the Rubric demanded, forty-five in the order and nineteen at the Court and a boy on a slope with chalk on his fingers, and what they became at the far end was mortar that had stopped setting.

Something had been put into the handover. Between the going out and the becoming, at the exact joint where one turns into the other, a thing had been seated, and Verinus could feel it there and could not reach it, because to reach it he would have had to stop, and the rite has no clause for stopping.

*Elegant. God help me, that is elegant. He has left every term of the contract standing and taken the conversion out of the middle of it, and I am now a man shovelling coal into a firebox with no boiler on the far side, and the office will not let me put the shovel down, because the office is not aware that anything has gone wrong. It is only aware that the payment is holy.*

The seal compensated. That was the design and the design was correct in every case the Rubric had ever contemplated. Where the wall came up thin, the rite drew harder to thicken it, and where the drawing found the vessel already spent, it went to the fixed point, because a fixed point is what a rite reaches for when its own substance runs out.

The line went hot.

Verinus felt it happen from the middle of himself outward and he had perhaps a quarter of a second of understanding before the load reversed and started down. Every span of wall that had eaten a cost and produced nothing. Every contradiction between what had been paid and what stood. All of it found the soul the rite had named as responsible for holding steady, and turned, and went, out of the Void and through the join and down into a cellar under Altherion where a man in the wrong skin with no teeth in his lower jaw was standing on flags in a puddle of his own blood believing that four people were alive.

"No," said Verinus.

He put his weight against it. There was nothing left of him to put and he put it anyway, and he tried to take the line back into himself, and the office would not permit the amendment, because the anchor had been declared at the third clause and a declaration made inside a rite is a fact about the world.

He had done that. At dawn, in a half-second, without asking, and he had known exactly what he was doing and had written it in his own head as good arithmetic, and here was the invoice arriving at the address he had put on it.

*I told the Bishop to spend the objection where the cost is measurable. This is measurable. It is measured. It has my name on the bottom of it and there is no clerk in four worlds who will file the appeal.*

The load went down the line and did not arrive.

It stopped. Mid-flight, at the full weight of it, with the whole unfinished catastrophe of the wall behind it and nothing in front, and it hung in the seam and held there, and the seam held, and Verinus, who had spent his life on the proposition that everything in the world has an edge that can be found, understood that something very large had just put a finger on the scale and stopped it swinging.

Then the sky came down.

The gold he had carried in with him, the dilution, the old wrong stars in their first arrangement, all of it drew together across the whole width of a place with no width, and the stars did not go out. They moved. They came in toward one another and went down into a shape, and the shape took them the way a body takes blood, and Verinus watched constellations that had stood over the Okuchi before Malakar broke it settle into the mass of a thing that was arriving and become the light inside it.

It had a name. He heard the name before he saw the hand, and the hearing was not a sound.

The One True Judger.

He had said that phrase from a lectern for forty-five years and had struck it from two minutes as improper and had told a Curia at fifty-two that the office does not feel and cannot be addressed as a person, and he stood in the ruin of his own working with the name going through him and had no correction to make to it.

The hand came out of the fold.

It came the way a scale-pan comes to rest, without hurry and without any possibility of being interrupted, and it was enormous past the point where enormous is a description, and the Void did not part for it. The Void stopped being the thing the hand had to move through. Reality drew back from every place the mass of it interjected, shrouding, folding away along the edges of the arriving weight, and under the fingers everything that had been at the root of the wound went to grains on a beach with the tide coming.

The gaze went past him.

It did not go to him. He was under it, in the sense that everything was under it, and it went over him and past and settled somewhere behind and above the root of the wound and stayed there.

One second. Perhaps two.

The whole Mortal World shook. He felt it through the seam, through the join, through eleven hundred years of stone at Altherion and every white shelf of the Holy Sea and the salt of the Nalūn basin and the frozen ground of the north, a shudder that went into houses that had never heard the name and cracked plaster in rooms where nobody could have said why. Bells rang that nobody was ringing. In four worlds, every scale-pan hanging on every chain came to level at once and stayed level for the length of the gaze and afterward would not agree with itself for a season.

There were no words. The office does not use them and never has and Verinus had been correct about that at fifty-two and had been correct about it his whole life and took no pleasure in it now.

What arrived instead was a finding, and it came in the only grammar the office has, which is weight.

*Unworthy. That is the whole of the reading and it is not an insult, it is a measurement, and the thing measured has been put on the pan and has come up light. He believes what he has taken makes him the heaviest object in the room and the office has looked at him for one second and gone back to what it was doing, and the chase is the flaw. The reaching is the flaw. The root that will bring it down is the root itself, and he will not be told, because nothing here is going to speak to him.*

The gaze came off.

And the hand closed on the seal.

It took the whole of the Custodia up out of the root of the wound, the golden wall and the seam and the join and all of it, and Verinus felt himself go with it, because he was the material, and the material went where the wall went. He saw the thin black line as it lifted. A hair's width of fault running through the gold, the thing that had been seated in his handover, and it did not fall out and did not close, and it went up with the rest.

The seal did not stop standing.

That was the thing he understood last and would have most liked to write down. It came out of the world and kept working, and the Custodia was no longer made of Verinus, and it was a door now, and what it stood between was the safeguarded and this, exactly as the rite had asked, and what would come through it in either direction was not going to be a matter for the Alabaster Court.

The line to Aurelian came up with the hand and stayed tied.

He tried one more time. He got a hand to it, and there was still, somehow, a hand.

Then the palm closed around him and the pressure of it took his years and set them down somewhere with the care of a man laying a plate on a shelf he can reach, and the gold went dark, and Verinus felt himself being carried, and the carrying had a direction and the direction was up and inward and toward the three sub-realms where the Lawbell hangs.

He did not die that day.

He was set down in Utopia, in the law-architecture, in a quiet the like of which he had not experienced since he was nineteen years old, and he lay in it with his eyes open and his cap gone and both hands empty, and he put his palm flat on the floor under him to find out whether it would take weight, and it did, and it did not sink.

---

Deeper in, where the Plane stops being a place a soul travels and becomes the thing the soul is made of, an old man opened his hand and found the print of an axe head in his palm, in the flesh, seated there since the third month and no longer sore.

Darius Ignatius Sanctus Sanctorum had been ankle-deep in the ash for a stretch of time he had stopped trying to number.

The ash had come up over the welt of both boots on the first day and had closed, and it had not let go since, and somewhere in the fourth or the ninth or the fortieth month he had stopped experiencing that as a restraint. He was seated. There was a difference and it had taken him most of a life to find it and he had found it here, standing still, at sixty-odd years of age, with a coat that had gone to felt and a beard he had given up on.

He put his fist out.

The air in front of it did not move, and the ash did not move, and three hundred feet up the branches that stopped being branches and became directions did not move, and the whole column of grey bark that ran down the nearest trunk in a hand no living scholar could read went, for the length of one breath, entirely legible.

Ruin first. The fist that finds the ending already on its way and makes it arrive correctly. Then Purity, which is the labour of what has to remain. Spirit, which is the traffic. Temporality, which is order and nothing else and is the hardest of the seven because it refuses to be impressive. Knowledge, which he had come in believing he already had and which had taken him longer than any of them. Balance, which was his, which had come to him the way a language comes back to a man who spoke it as a child, and which he had needed a year to stop over-reaching with.

And the seventh, which is the six held at once and the Plane delivering the finding.

He let the hand open.

He had not learned seven techniques. He had spent eleven hundred years of his order's doctrine reading a single string and calling it the choir, and the thing he had been given here was the whole instrument: the matrix, the Works standing in relation to each other, function itself, and what it means that a Work is a labour and a labour is performed and a performance can be done well or badly by a man with a body. Work magic. He had used the phrase in three hundred writs. He had signed his name under it. He had not known what it meant until the ash closed over his boots.

"You've got it," Gimbzo said.

He was sitting on nothing, which he did, with Tsuiji laid across his knees and the linen unwound to the beard of the blade. Square shoulders. At that mass, and with the shoulders square, he stopped resembling a man who happened to be enormous.

"I have it," Darius said.

"Say what you've got. Don't be modest, you're bad at it and it wastes my afternoon."

"All seven. The relation between them. And the reason the order has been standing on Balance for eleven centuries without knowing what it was standing on, which is that Balance is not one of the seven. It is the seven agreeing to hold still long enough to be looked at."

"HOO." Gimbzo scratched the side of his jaw with one thumb. "Four times I've said that sentence to a man and this is the first time one's said it back at me."

"Then I would like the gate."

"Aye. You would."

Kizami came down the column with too many joints going at once and landed on the ash without disturbing it and put his chisel-jaw sideways to read the trunk.

*Sixty-one arrivals and eleven gates opened and nine walked out, and now this one wants the twelfth on a completed column with the ink still wet, and there is a subsection. There is always a subsection. Nobody reads the subsection until it is standing in front of them in a coat.*

"There's a subsection," said Kizami.

Darius looked at Gimbzo.

Gimbzo did not look away from him, which he had done at every other difficult moment in the last however many months, and the not looking away was the answer before the words arrived.

"One more," Gimbzo said. "I'd have told you at the start and you'd have walked. I've watched two men take the seventh and go home the same afternoon, and both of them went back with the whole matrix in their hands and neither of them could put it down on the world, because what you've learned here is how the Works stand. You've not once been asked what it costs to stand them somewhere that doesn't want them." He turned the axe over on his knees. "That's the trial. It's not a fight and I'm not going to tell you what it is, because the last thing a man in your position needs is a fortnight to prepare a position."

"And afterward I go back."

"Afterward you go back."

"To what."

"Don't ask me that."

The ash held. Darius looked down at his boots, and at the two curls of grey bark that had lain against the toe of the left one since the first hour and had not moved, and at the print of the axe head in the packed ground beneath them, which was clean and had never filled.

He worked his right foot out of the ash for the first time since he had put it there, and set it down flat in the print, and put his weight on it.

## Notes (author-facing, not canon)

**Kujo untouched.** His counter arrives inside Verinus as a diagnosis: cost leaving on schedule, value coming back short, something seated in the handover that cannot be reached because reaching it means stopping and the rite has no clause for stopping. No line, no gesture, no interiority. Urion's gaze goes past him and the finding is rendered as weight, in Verinus's read, never as anything Kujo does about it.

**Urion does not speak.** Your instinct was right and I held it hard. The office has one grammar and it is weight. The finding comes through as *unworthy*, and Verinus reads it as a measurement and not an insult: the thing was put on the pan and came up light, the reaching is the flaw, the root that brings it down is the root itself, and nothing here is going to tell it so. Two seconds of gaze, then off. That is the most contemptuous thing in the scene and no one says a word.

**The anchor.** Kujo's reversal reaches Aurelian and stops mid-flight when the hand closes. The line comes up with the seal and stays tied. So the man in the cellar is now anchored to a standing Custodia held by an Archon, and Verinus got a hand to it at the end and was not permitted the amendment, because a declaration made inside a rite is a fact about the world. He does not get to fix it. That is his last cost and I would not give it back to him.

**The seal survived and is no longer made of him.** It came out of the world and kept working, exactly as your line about the gateway for the Paragons requires. The hairline Kujo left went up with it and did not close. Whatever comes through that door in either direction is now outside the Alabaster Court's jurisdiction, which is the political bomb.

**Verinus rests in Utopia,** in the law-architecture near the Lawbell, per the Celestial Host canon. Alive, cap gone, hands empty. First thing he does is test whether the floor takes weight. It does.

**Darius.** Held in his POV, ash-seated, Gimbzo and Kizami from the existing scenes with their voices intact. The seventh is the six held at once, and the payload I gave his realisation is the same doctrinal knife Verinus swallowed in the Void: eleven hundred years of reading one string and calling it the choir. He and Verinus have arrived at the Unsingled Crown from opposite ends and neither knows the other did. The trial is unnamed on purpose and Gimbzo says why.

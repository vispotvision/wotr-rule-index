# The Primate Under the Wrong Stars

*Altherion. The precinct of the Grand Church, where the Cloister Ward used to be. Before dawn.*

---

He came up into open air through a hole that had been a stair.

Cold on the new skin of his thighs, which had no hair on it and no history in it and reported temperature at a volume the old skin had never used. Wet ash to the ankle, sucking at each foot, giving up smoke where it broke. The smell was lime and burnt beeswax and, under those, the flat mineral nothing of a place where four hundred years of stone had stopped being stone in the course of an afternoon.

No hum anywhere. The ward was cut and the standpipes had emptied and the gauge-housings in the sealed conduit hung open and dead, and Altherion at the fourth hour was the quietest it had been since the city was a shrine and a well.

Aurelian Prudentius put his hands down at his sides and let them hang.

The dead were still going.

They came past the precinct in a slow spiral, gathering from every direction across the Inner World and turning down through the same aperture, and the aperture had not been built for this volume. He could feel the crowding in his chest where the crowding was. Souls in traffic. Dread coming off them in a sheet, and pain, most of it the pain of the last four minutes still being carried because there had been no time to put it down.

A woman going past with the shape of a task still on her, both arms held as though a basket were in them.

A man who kept stopping, which is the worst thing they do.

Children who had no idea they were moving.

They were suffering as they went in, and there was a bottleneck at the crossing, and the bottleneck was the same fact from the other side that the Measurewrights would spend a season failing to explain about their scale-pans.

His eyes changed.

He felt the moment it happened as a pressure behind the orbits and a cooling across both corneas, and the world came apart into its actual grain. The silver went out of them. What replaced it went black from the pupil outward, and around the black a corona stood in the iris, a deep violet with a light behind it, and it did not come from the sky and the sky had nothing to give it.

He saw all of it.

Every fate in the precinct, and then every fate in the city, and then the lines going out past the coast, and past that. Thirty-one years he had done this one thread at a time, at cost, with a headache after. Here was the whole cloth. Every branch that came off every choice and every branch off those, backward into what had already been decided and forward into what had not, and all the pain distributed along all of it, at once, held in front of him at a size that had no business fitting behind a man's face.

He looked at it with interest.

That was the part he noticed. Somewhere under the flood a small piece of Aurelian Prudentius stood up and observed, in the tone of a clerk finding an error in a column, that a man shown this much should be on the ground and screaming, and that he had a warm and pleasant feeling in his chest instead, and that this was worth entering somewhere.

He did not enter it.

The corona thinned and the black withdrew into the pupil and the silver came back, and the world went ordinary and small, and his hands were still at his sides and had not moved.

Boots in the ash. Several people, running badly on bad footing, and one of them well ahead of the others.

"Your holiness."

Charles Lambert went down onto one knee in the wet ash without checking what was under it and bowed his head, and the hat came off and went into both hands, and stayed there.

Forty-one, and looked older tonight. A wide, plain, north-coast face, cheeks gone red with cold and with running, a jaw that had been square before the last two years put a softness under it. Hair the brown of a wet rope, cut short and grown out of the cut, plastered flat across the forehead. Big shoulders and a big chest and the beginnings of a stomach, and the archbishop's black over all of it, sodden to mid-thigh, with the pectoral cross gone round under his arm and hanging at his ribs where he had not noticed it. Ink on the first two fingers of the right hand. A thin white line through the left eyebrow, old, the kind a child gets off a stone step.

Behind him, four of the guard, stopping at a distance nobody had told them to stop at.

The stars came out from behind the last of the ash-cloud and put light on the precinct, and Aurelian turned his face up into it, and it lay on him and reflected off him the way light lies on water and off it.

They were in the wrong places.

Every one of them. The whole vault over Altherion was an arrangement nobody living had seen, hanging there since the sky had been diluted and the hand had closed, and it had not gone back. Three of the guard behind Charles were looking up at it. One of them had been looking up at it since he arrived and had forgotten to lower his halberd.

"Your holiness, you are here." Charles's voice had gone thin at the top. "We were worried for your health."

Aurelian smiled at him.

It was warm. He put the whole of himself behind it, and it worked, because his face had always been good at that, and the four molars that used to sit in the lower left of it were somewhere in the Void in another man's jaw and the gum had closed over smooth. He kept the smile small on that side out of habit.

"You had no reason to fear, Charles Lambert."

He came forward through the ash. The wet ash took his weight and let it go and the ground under him went down a coin's width and stayed down and he did not look at it.

"And you have no reason to fear for the safety of Dabney Lambert. My eyes have confirmed both things."

Charles's head came up.

*He said the name.*

*Nineteen years the old man would not put that name in a room with more than two people in it. He would not put it in a day-book. He sat me on a cold step and told me why and I have not slept properly since, and here it is said out loud in a burned precinct with the guard standing at ten feet and the sky in the wrong order over all of us, and he said it kindly. He said it to comfort me.*

Charles got up out of the ash. He did it slowly.

"Your holiness. Forgive me. We must get you to safety. The Ward is cut, the lower cloister is gone, there is no roof on the chapter house and the Curia has been sitting since the second hour without the Palatine and without any word of him, and there are men in this city tonight who will want you where they can see you."

Aurelian shook his head. He put his hand on Charles Lambert's shoulder, and the hand was young, and Charles looked at it.

"Mm. You have no reason to worry, Archbishop."

"With respect, holiness, I have every."

"I have seen these events." The hand stayed where it was. "Seen, Charles, with the eyes. All of them, in the order they will arrive and in three other orders they will not, and I have looked at what happens to this city and at what happens to me, and I find that I am not afraid of any of it." He let a breath out through his nose. "I am no longer afraid, Charles. Of anything. That is a new condition and I am still becoming familiar with it."

Charles Lambert did not answer.

*I have known this man twenty-six years. He was afraid of the Curia. He was afraid of the vote. He was afraid of the Seventh Objection and afraid of what would happen if he ever moved on it, and he was afraid every single day, and every single day he got up and put the vestment on and went and did it anyway, and that was the whole of what made him worth kneeling to. Fear was the instrument. He was the man who felt the weight.*

*They have taken him underground for a fortnight and given him back to me weighing nothing.*

"There is only one path to peace now, Charles," said Aurelian.

He took the hand off the shoulder.

"War."

The sky opened.

It came as a ripple first, a long slow warping of the star-field to the north-east of the precinct, and the wrong constellations bent through it and came back wrong in a different way, and then the ripple went to a mouth, and the mouth was the near end of a tunnel with nothing on the walls and no light in the length of it, and it ran back further than the sky was deep.

Kaalabad came down it.

He came head-first and fast, out of the mouth at eighty feet with the halberd already up and back over the right shoulder in the loaded position that no living halberdier used because no living halberdier could recover from it, and the plate across his shoulders sat correct on a frame that had since changed its mind about being a frame, and the helm sat a few degrees off true over a skull that helms are not made for.

Aurelian saw the whole strike before it left the top of the arc.

The read cost him nothing. That was the second thing worth entering that he did not enter. The strike was Percussion at its plainest, no Sweep and no Return, a Settling delivered in one piece from a height with the whole mass of him behind it and the tunnel's exit velocity added to the top of that, and the halberd head coming down was old, and dirty, and the marks on it were old, and it was going to arrive at the crown of Aurelian's skull.

He lifted two fingers.

Time in a sphere perhaps four yards across came apart.

It did not stop. It went to grain. The interval between one instant and the next opened up and became a thing with a width to it, and inside that width the halberd head continued to descend and did so through a substance that had begun charging by the inch. The air in the sphere went visibly thick. Ash hanging in it stopped falling and did not hover, and instead came apart into its own separate motions, individual flakes each keeping a different hour.

The halberd head reached Aurelian's two fingers and touched them.

Nothing entered him.

Every last unit of what had been coming down that shaft, the mass and the drop and the tunnel's gift and the whole standing effort of a body built at the Zenith to deliver exactly this, arrived at a point four inches wide and was not absorbed and was not turned. It was put out.

It went sideways.

The kinetic wave came out of the sphere in a flat ring at knee height and crossed the precinct in every direction at once, and the wet ash went off the ground in a wall and kept going, and the four guardsmen behind Charles Lambert left their feet and travelled and landed and slid. Charles went down on his back with his hat still in his hand. Every window remaining on the south face of the chapter house went out of its frame in one movement, glass and lead and all. The precinct wall, which had stood through the fire, took the ring at its base and shed its top three courses.

Out at the edges the ring stopped being force. It came apart into something that had no push in it, a pressure across the skin like the moment before a decision, and it went through the standing men and through the ash and out into the streets beyond and every dog in the Cloister quarter began to scream at once.

Aurelian's arm had not moved. His shoulder had not moved. His feet were where they had been.

The ground under him went down another coin's width.

Somewhere else, a great way from Altherion, along a seam that a young man in a gold robe had become and had then been lifted out of, a hairline fault the width of nothing widened by the width of slightly more than nothing.

He did not feel it. There was no mechanism by which he could have felt it. He had not been told he was load-bearing.

Kaalabad hung above him in the thick air with both hands still on the haft.

He came down onto the ash and landed heavily and stayed in the crouch, and the ground took it, and the halberd stayed in contact with the two fingers, and he set his back and his hips and his heels and put the entire architecture of himself into a shaft that had stopped being able to go anywhere.

*It did not go in.*

*Forty years. It has gone into a gate, and a mountain shoulder, and the neck plate of a thing that had no business having a neck, and it has never in forty years failed to go in. It is going in now. Every part of me is doing what it does and the arithmetic is correct and the head is not moving and there is a man on the other end of it with his hands at his sides.*

Aurelian looked up at him with mild interest.

Then he flicked the first finger.

The halberd went out of Kaalabad's grip and across the precinct and through the standing remains of the north colonnade and out the other side, and it took two columns going through and buried itself to the beard in the cathedral's west footing at a distance of ninety yards, and stood there humming.

The thick air let go all at once. Ash resumed falling. All of it landed together.

Kaalabad straightened out of the crouch. Both hands were open and neither had anything in it, and he looked at them, and then at the colonnade, and then down at the man in front of him, and the scarred band around his throat above the gorget moved as he swallowed.

"Hopefully," said Aurelian pleasantly, "I can excuse you this one."

Kaalabad's head came up.

"WHO THE HELL TOLD YOU YOU COULD LEAVE."

It went off the cathedral face and came back off the chapter house and rolled out over the quarter, and Charles Lambert, flat on his back in the ash twenty feet away, flinched at the volume of it, and one of the guardsmen made a small sound.

Aurelian let the silence sit for a moment.

He looked at Kaalabad, and the look went on past the point where a look is a look, and the silver in his eyes did not change and did not need to, and the ground between the two of them settled a third time.

"You are far too weak to give me any enjoyment," he said. "It would be a disgrace to the grace of the Judger to kill something this pathetic."

He turned his back on the halberdier.

He walked over to where Charles Lambert was getting up out of the ash on one elbow with his hat crushed in his fist, and he crouched, and he put his hand under the archbishop's arm and lifted him onto his feet with no effort at all.

## Notes (author-facing, not canon)

**The horror is that he is fine.** Aurelian sees every fate in four worlds at once, notices in a clerk's tone that a man shown this much should be on the ground screaming, notes that he has a warm and pleasant feeling instead, decides it is worth entering somewhere, and does not enter it. Everything after that is a man being extremely kind with nothing behind it.

**The name.** He says *Dabney Lambert* out loud, warmly, to comfort a frightened father, in a burned precinct with the guard at ten feet. Verinus would not put that name in a room with three people in it and refused to say it to Kujo's face when saying it was the only card he had left. Charles registers what just happened and Aurelian does not. Nobody in the scene knows what it cost. That is the whole of the beat and I have not underlined it.

**Charles's read is the scene's thesis.** Fear was the instrument. He was the man who felt the weight, and he got up every day and did it afraid, and that was what made him worth kneeling to. They took him underground for a fortnight and gave him back weighing nothing. That is his italic and I would not trade it.

**Where the two fingers get their power.** Nothing comes out of Aurelian. It comes down the anchor line, out of the Custodia, and the hairline fault Kujo seated in the seam widens by slightly more than nothing. He does not feel it, there is no mechanism by which he could, and he has not been told he is load-bearing. Every impossible thing he does from here thins that wall. That is the cost and the misreading in one object, and it is the engine I would run the rest of this arc on.

**Kaalabad.** Percussion at its plainest, a Settling delivered in one piece from height with the tunnel's exit velocity on top, no Sweep and no Return, because he did not think he needed them. The force is expunged rather than absorbed or turned, per your line, and the ring goes out at knee height and takes the precinct apart. His italic is the point: forty years and it has never failed to go in. And the man who does not speak, who dropped Sesk to his knees with his neck alone, *shouts*. That is him rattled, and everyone present who knows him will understand it.

**Canon and rulings.**
1. *Spelling.* Your message says Kaalabad; the two existing prose files say Kalaabad. I used yours. One of them needs to lose.
2. *The wrong stars* did not go back after the hand withdrew. The sky over Altherion is now an arrangement nobody living has seen, permanently, and three guardsmen are staring at it. Originated, and it is the public evidence problem.
3. *The eyes.* Silver at rest, black with a violet corona when open. I have kept it unexplained and put no system vocabulary near it. What is looking, and whether it is his faculty at all any more, is left open on purpose.
4. *The crossing is choked.* Thirty-five percent of the Inner World arriving at once, with a bottleneck at the aperture. This is the same fact the Measurewrights will fail to explain about their scale-pans, from the other side. Ruling needed on whether the Soul Kingdom's Threshold can be overrun, because that is a large door to open.
5. *Charles Lambert* physically inventoried for the first time. Age forty-one is originated.

**Open.** Whether Kaalabad leaves or escalates. Who sent him, and what "who told you you could leave" means about who was holding the cell. Whether anyone in Altherion is going to ask the Primate why the ground sinks when he speaks.

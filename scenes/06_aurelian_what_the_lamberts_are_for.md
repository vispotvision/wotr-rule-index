# What the Lamberts Are For

*Altherion. The precinct, still. Four minutes later.*

---

Kaalabad did not leave.

He stood in the ash with his hands empty and his halberd ninety yards away in the cathedral footing, and he looked at Aurelian's back for the length of three breaths, and then he turned his head twelve degrees and looked at the archbishop instead.

Aurelian saw the decision arrive. He saw it in the fate before he saw it in the body, and then he saw it in the body, which was the hips going first, which is always the hips going first.

He watched with interest.

There was a version of the next four seconds in which he put his hand out and the halberdier stopped existing in the ordinary sense, and he could see that version, and he could see the eleven that came off it, and in nine of the eleven the archbishop lived and in two he did not and in all eleven something in the north went differently in a way Aurelian could follow for a considerable distance. He held all twelve futures side by side in the front of his head with no effort whatsoever.

He chose the one where he did nothing, and he chose it in the time it takes to notice a smell, and he did not experience the choosing as a decision.

Charles Lambert had got to his feet by then, hat in the left hand, black soaked to mid-thigh, and he was still turned toward Aurelian and mid-sentence when the ground behind him made the sound of an enormous man beginning.

He did not look round. That was the first thing.

He stepped off the line before he turned, on the left foot, a short passing step out and forward toward where the attack was coming from, and the arm that came through the space he had been standing in was a knuckle too long and went past his shoulder blade at a distance the eye could not resolve into a distance.

*He is inside it.*

*Forty years and men do not step toward me, and this one has no reach, no weapon, no Stage, no shell, nothing in him that reads at all, and he has stepped toward me and put himself where my elbow lives.*

Charles turned as he stepped, which put his hip into the ground, and his boots took hold of the wet ash where a running man's would have gone out from under him, because he had put weight on the outside edge of the foot before he committed. That was a thing a body learns on a wet deck at fourteen. Aurelian watched him do it twice more in four seconds.

The second attempt came down and across, a backhand off the same shoulder, and Charles was under it and out on the other side with his chin tucked and both hands up in front of his sternum and open. He did not strike anything. He had nothing to strike with and he knew it and he did not waste the motion.

The open hands were the tell, for anyone watching who knew what a tell was. A frightened man puts his hands up closed. Charles Lambert kept them open and kept them low and kept them where they could feel a shaft coming before his eyes could confirm one, and every third of a second he shifted the line of his hips by a degree or two, keeping the great body in front of him strung out along one axis where only one limb at a time could reach him. It is the oldest solution to the polearm and it is taught nowhere in the Holy Sea, because in the Holy Sea a man who is going to be attacked by a polearm is expected to be carrying something.

The third came low, at the knee, to take the base out.

He turned his shin out of it and let the whole weight of his upper body fall in the same direction, and went down onto one hand in the ash and back up, and there was ash in his mouth and he did not spit it out.

He was not fast. That was the thing Aurelian found himself examining. A forty-one-year-old diocesan administrator in soaked wool with a stomach on him is not fast in any register that means anything, and none of the four evasions had been quick. They had been early. Each one had begun before the delivery it answered, by a margin so slight that a spectator would have called it luck and a practitioner would have called it a read, and Charles Lambert had no faculty with which to read anything.

Aurelian stood four yards away with his hands at his sides and did not move.

*How interesting.*

*There is nothing in him. I have read this man for twenty-six years and there has never been anything in him and there is nothing in him now, and he has just voided three deliveries from something at the Zenith, and he did the third one with his eyes on the wrong hand, which means the eyes were never the instrument.*

*So this is what the Lamberts are. Nobody ever told me. Edward is a clerk and Charles keeps the diocese and the whole line reads as furniture in every register the Church holds, and this is what is under it, and they have been in our building for two hundred years.*

He wondered, mildly, how many of them there were, and whether the Church had ever counted, and what the counting would have been for.

Twenty-six years of vestry meetings. A man who took the minutes and brought his own pen and never once sat where the light was good.

Charles evaded the fourth by an inch and a half.

Then Kaalabad stopped swinging and simply closed the distance, which is what all of this had been for, and brought the right knee up into the archbishop's stomach.

It went in under the floating ribs on the left side and lifted him.

The sound was not loud. It was flat and wet and short, and it was made by a diaphragm being driven up into a chest cavity that had no room to receive it, and Charles Lambert's whole frame folded over the joint with his heels a foot off the ground and his hat still shut in his fist.

Kaalabad flung him.

The arm came across and put him out sideways and he travelled twenty-two feet through the air and landed on his shoulder and his ear and rolled through the ash and stopped face down.

He did not get up.

His back moved in a fast shallow flutter that was his body attempting a breath and being refused by a diaphragm that had gone into spasm and would not answer, and his hands were somewhere under him, and after four seconds he made a sound that was air going into a man in the wrong order.

The tunnel opened above the precinct without any warping this time.

Kaalabad went up into it. He did it slowly, which was worse than speed, and he did not look back at Aurelian, and the last thing that went into the dark was the empty right hand, and then there was no mouth in the star-field and the wrong constellations closed over where it had been and did not appear to have been disturbed.

The halberd came out of the cathedral footing by itself and went after him and was gone. It took the last of the west buttress with it on the way, and the masonry came down in the dark across the transept steps in a long tumbling run, and the dust of it went up and stood in the air over the cathedral and stayed there because there was no wind in Altherion that night.

Aurelian looked up at the place where the tunnel had been for a while.

"That man is the threat," he said.

There was nobody in a condition to answer. He said it anyway, in the flat plain voice he had begun using since the cellar, the one that had no drop in it at the end of a sentence.

"The halberdier is a tool with a good edge and somebody's hand on it, and I could put him down without standing up. I am speaking of the other one. I saw him from the inside of the crossing when my eyes were open, and he is considerably more dangerous than anything I have ever been asked to weigh, and I no longer wonder that Verinus was defeated."

He turned that over.

"I do not think that at my current best I would defeat him."

Four guardsmen stood at the edge of the ash and heard the Primate of the Holy Sea say that out loud, in the open, at the fourth hour, and one of them would repeat it in a tavern in the Cooper's quarter inside nine days and would be believed by nobody, and the not being believed would save his life for a period of about two months.

He said it the way a man reads a number off a gauge. Not defiantly and not with fear, because there was no fear anywhere in the precinct to be had, and the sentence went out into the ash and lay there.

*Verinus is dead. That is what the crossing shows and the crossing does not lie about arrivals. He went out of the world sometime in the last hour and there is a shape missing where he was, and I have looked for him among the traffic and he is not in the traffic, which means it took him entirely, and the last thing that man did in his life was buy four people who left through the low cloister nine days before he signed for them.*

*I will not tell Charles. There is no version of that which helps.*

He was wrong about all of it, in every particular, and there was nothing in four worlds positioned to correct him.

"Kharven," he said.

The word settled. Somewhere under his sternum a thread went taut and then slack again, the way a line does when the far end of it takes weight, and he did not notice it, because there was no instrument in him that had ever been calibrated to notice it.

"Nalūn will need to be rebuilt. That will not begin until the end of the war and it should not be attempted before, because you cannot lay a foundation in ground that is still being argued over, and there is going to be a great deal of arguing."

He said Nalūn without any particular weight, in the tone of an item on a schedule, and the salt basin at that hour had chain-bridges hanging at their stops and every sealed instrument in the lower vaults sitting at a reading no watchman could account for, and eleven of the black stones on the floor of the Deepvein station where they had come through the roof.

Charles Lambert got a whole breath.

It was audible across twenty feet and it sounded like a man being hauled up out of water. He got a second one, worse, and rolled onto his side, and then onto his back, and lay in the wet ash with his knees drawn up and both hands flat on his ribs and his mouth open.

Aurelian walked over.

He crouched, and looked down at him with the calm interest of a man reading a parish return, and the stars in their wrong arrangement stood over the two of them and put light on his face.

"Are you all right, Archbishop."

The inflection did not go up at the end.

Charles turned his head and looked at him. Ash on the whole left side of his face and in his hair and packed into his ear. Blood at the corner of his mouth from a bitten tongue. The white line through his eyebrow had opened again and was leaking down into the socket. He was breathing in a way that used his shoulders.

He did not answer for a moment.

*You were four yards away.*

*You put a Zenith's own strike out of the world with two fingers and threw his weapon through a colonnade, and then he came for me, and you stood there. You watched me. I could see you watching me. I have known you since I was fifteen years old and you have never once in your life let a man be hit in front of you if you could reach him, and it was the reaching that ruined you, and it is the only reason any of us ever loved you.*

*And you found it interesting.*

Aurelian held his hand out.

Charles looked at the hand. It was a young hand with no chalk on it and no ink and no callus anywhere. He took it.

He came up off the ground with no effort at all involved, which was its own indignity, and stood swaying with one arm across his middle and the other on Aurelian's forearm, and could not straighten fully, and gave up trying.

"We should continue your journey to Kharven," Aurelian said. "I will be accompanying you now."

Charles nodded. It cost him something to do it.

He got the hat off the ground, which took him a while, because bending was a project now and would be for six weeks, and he turned it round in both hands and looked at it. The brim had gone at the back. He put it on anyway, and the ash came off the crown and down the side of his face, and he did not brush it away.

"If you are certain, holiness." He stopped and took another breath and waited for it. "People will find it strange. That you position yourself in Moto territory. At this hour, with the Curia sitting, and the Palatine's seat empty, and the Ward cut. They will draw a line from you to them and they will not draw it kindly."

Aurelian blinked.

It was the first ordinary thing his face had done since he came up out of the hole.

"Find what strange."

"Holiness." Charles got his weight onto the other foot and something on his left side objected in a way he did not let reach his face. "Forgive me. I have carried your correspondence into the north for nine years and I have never once done it under your name, on your instruction, or with you in the party. Every man on that road will notice which of those three has changed."

"The Moto are the most important piece on this board. Anyone with sufficient knowledge of the game understands that, and anyone who does not is being played by somebody who does." He said it without heat and without emphasis, which was somehow the worst way it could have been said. "There is a war beginning that will be decided in the north and every party to it knows where the north keeps its weight. The Church has spent two hundred years pretending otherwise in writing. I am no longer required to pretend and I find I have no appetite for it."

He turned and started walking east across the precinct toward the gate, through ash that took his weight and let it go, and the ground under each footfall went down a coin's width and stayed down, and behind him four guardsmen who had not moved since the ring went out watched the prints filling with black water.

Charles Lambert put one hand on the broken top course of the precinct wall, and held there a moment with his ribs grinding, and followed him.

## Notes (author-facing, not canon)

**One sequencing change.** Your paste puts Aurelian's assessment before the Charles fight. I ran the fight first, because the assessment lands on a man face down in the ash unable to breathe, and because "Are you all right, Archbishop" and the offered hand only work after. Every line you wrote is present. Say the word and I will flip it back.

**He watched.** Before Kaalabad turns on Charles, Aurelian holds twelve futures side by side in the front of his head, sees that in nine of eleven interventions the archbishop lives, chooses the one where he does nothing, chooses it in the time it takes to notice a smell, and does not experience the choosing as a decision. Charles's italic is the bill for that: *you have never once in your life let a man be hit in front of you if you could reach him, and it was the reaching that ruined you, and it is the only reason any of us ever loved you.* Neither of them says a word about it and they walk east together anyway.

**The Lambert skill, originated, pending ruling.** No talent, no Stage, nothing that reads on any register. What he has is a read with no faculty behind it. He is not fast. He is early. Open hands kept low where they can feel a shaft before the eyes confirm one, hips shifting a degree or two every third of a second to keep the great body strung out on one axis where only one limb at a time can reach. Weight on the outside edge of the foot before committing, which is a thing a body learns on a wet deck at fourteen. The oldest solution to the polearm, taught nowhere in the Holy Sea because a man in the Holy Sea who expects a polearm is expected to be carrying something. Four evasions, then the knee, because that is what all of it was for. Aurelian has read this man for twenty-six years and never once saw it, and now wonders how many Lamberts there are and whether anyone ever counted.

**The injury is real.** Floating ribs on the left, diaphragm driven up and into spasm, four seconds of a body attempting a breath and being refused, bitten tongue, the old eyebrow scar reopened. Bending is a project for six weeks. He does not get to be fine because the scene needs him walking.

**Aurelian is wrong about everything and nobody can correct him.** He believes Verinus is dead, because the crossing shows no arrival and he reads that as *taken entirely*. He believes he is not load-bearing. He believes Kharven is a schedule item. The thread under his sternum goes taut and slack when he says the word and he has no instrument calibrated to notice.

**Canon and rulings.**
1. *Moto.* Your line puts the Moto in the north as live canon and I have written it that way. This is now the third source pulling against the Büri amendment in the standing instructions, alongside the Moto Reversion Ledger and the Inner World Naming Amendment. It needs one ruling and then a sweep, and I would rather do it once than keep flagging it.

3. *Charles's nine years carrying correspondence into the north under no name* is originated and gives his objection teeth.
4. The guardsman who repeats what the Primate said, is believed by nobody, and is kept alive about two months by the disbelief, is originated. Cut it if you want the precinct silent.

# The Shallow-Seen Unburied

*Somewhere off the Ashgate road. No time passes.*

---

He stood apart from the darkness and the darkness let him.

That was the first wrong thing. There was a boundary and he was outside it, and the black at the end of the valley went on being the black at the end of the valley and did not come to meet him, and Sodoku Moto stood in a place with no ground in it and found that he could see.

Backward. Only backward.

---

A room he had never entered.

Stone. A drain in the floor with a grating over it, and the grating had been lifted out and set against the wall, which told him what the room was for before anything else in it did. Three lamps. A chair that had been made for the purpose, with the arms cut short so the wrists came past the ends of them.

Freya was in the chair.

He watched all of it. That was the arrangement and nobody had explained the arrangement to him and there was no refusing any part of it. He watched them start at the hands because you start at the hands when you want a person to keep speaking, and he watched the second day, and the third, and he watched a man he had eaten with at his own table in the year of the ice come into the room on the fourth morning with his sleeves pinned back and take the chair opposite hers and speak to her pleasantly for eleven minutes before he touched anything.

Seiji Tenrai Moto had wanted one thing.

Where is he.

That was all of it. Four days and a drain in the floor for one sentence, which she did not have, which she had never had, because Sodoku had made certain that she did not have it and had told himself at the time that he was protecting her. She had no answer to give on the first morning and none on the fourth. The whole of what was done to her was done to extract a thing that had been removed from her in advance by her husband, for her own safety, with her agreement, in a kitchen, over a bowl.

He watched her die.

It took a long time and it was not clean and there was nothing in it that any account would call dignified, and the thing that broke him was none of that.

At the end of it she was still holding him.

He could see it. He was in the Plane and it was the one place a thing like that is visible, and the bond at her Attraction Layer was intact, undegraded, unstained, and four days in that room had put nothing into it. It was the same shape it had been in a kitchen nineteen years earlier when neither of them had a title. Whatever they had done to her body, they had not reached that, and they had not known it was there to reach, and it went out of the world still burning at full strength and unbent.

That was the most beautiful thing Sodoku Moto had ever been shown.

He would have given every year he had left to unsee it.

And the only remaining piece of it in the world was asleep under a cistern with a broken circlet she had put down and forgotten, in a stone chamber with one way in and an old man standing in the doorway.

---

*How much are you willing to give.*

The question came back carrying the picture with it, and he understood at last that it had never once been rhetorical.

There is a state the Moto record calls Gisei. It has no activation and no training path. What it has is a price list. The Plane accepts memories and it accepts capacities and it accepts crystallised Traits, and it converts them at a rate set by what the loss costs the man making it, and it cannot be deceived, because the grief itself is what does the conversion.

And it accepts bonds.

No abandonment in it anywhere. Severance at the Attraction Layer. The anchor comes out of the Crystal and the person goes on existing and the practitioner goes on remembering them accurately, every detail, the whole history, and cannot feel any of it ever again. And what that bond was worth to him while it lived becomes density available immediately.

He had one bond of that magnitude left in the world.

*Authorship. That is the word for what is on the other side of this. I have been telling myself I want enough Ardency to put that thing in the ground, and it is a lie, and I have been telling it since the ninth hour.*

*Authority over the real. To open my chest and let it go outward until it is the size of an ocean and everything in it obeys the shape of what I have decided. That is what I want. I have wanted it since a kitchen, since a bowl, since somebody came to my house while I was elsewhere.*

*And it is available. That is the obscene part. It is not a fantasy. It is a documented state with a documented price and I have read the price list and I know exactly which item on it is worth the most.*

The shallow wall of darkness around his heart began to come apart.

It unwound. He felt it go, one turn at a time, something he had not known was there and now could not think of as having ever been absent, and what had been inside it stepped out and stood a little way off from his soul and looked at him.

It had his face.

The face from the wedding portrait that had burned with the house, thinner through the cheek, the nose still straight because the nose went in the second year of the ice.

---

It was not a demon and it did not pretend to be. It was younger. That was the thing that landed hardest and the thing he had least expected. Thirty-two, thirty-three, before the ice and before the Crossing and before the water, with the jaw he used to have and the hands he used to have, and a mandible of the void running down one side of it where the shape had not been finished.

It did not gloat. It sounded tired.

"Really," it said. "This is what is left."

Sodoku said nothing.

"Look at yourself. Walking up a road on foot because you gave your mount away to help another house's soldiers. Standing in a dead patch of your own grief doing arithmetic about a girl." It put its head on one side. "You have become a weak, disposable thing. And the terrible part is that you have made it a virtue. You have found a way to call this restraint."

"Say it."

"Where is the anger."

The voice did not rise. It never had. That was the whole of what made it his.

"Where is it. You watched four days in a stone room with a drain in the floor and the man who did it is eight miles from you at this moment with six hundred men and I could tell you the exact number of paces. Where is the obsession. You had it. You had it for years and it was the only thing that ever made you worth anything, and you put it down somewhere around the fourth year and you have been telling yourself since then that putting it down was growth."

*It is going to be right about the next thing. I can feel the shape of it coming and I already know it is going to be right.*

"You could kill that man in the Void." The thing came a half-step closer, which was the first movement it had made. "The one at the root of it, and you know which one I mean. Everything necessary is inside you at this moment and it has been inside you since the water and the only thing between you and it is a price you have decided not to look at. You could end the entire war by tonight. You. Alone. And instead you have arranged it so that Wren Greymane died on a ditch bank, and Bram is standing in a breach at the southern reach with three hundred men, and Edward Lambert is fifty-three years old and covered in someone else's blood in the mouth of a sally gate, and a schoolmaster with one arm is doing your work in the narrows because you would not."

"They chose that."

"They chose it because you were not available." It let that sit. "Every one of them is spending their life on a gap you left. You know it. That is why you have not looked at any of them since the whistle."

"And your correction."

"Give me the girl."

The thing said it in the flattest voice he had ever heard come out of his own mouth.

"Sever it. It is one act and it takes less than a second and she does not die and she does not even find out. She grows up. She rules. She is fed and guarded and taught, and she never learns that the man who kissed her hair this morning stopped being able to feel her somewhere between the gate and the narrows. Nobody is harmed anywhere. That is what makes it perfect, and it is why you will refuse it, and your refusal will be about you."

"Say the reason."

"You want to keep feeling it," said the thing with his face. "That is all. You have confused the feeling for the love. The love goes on working whether or not you can feel it, exactly as her love went on working in that room where nothing else survived, and what you are protecting when you refuse me is not your daughter. It is your access to your own grief. You would rather hold the ache than end the man who caused it."

The dark went very quiet.

*It is not wrong.*

*It has not said one thing that is untrue and it is not going to, because it is made out of me and it has had nineteen years of me to work with, and everything it has is something I gave it. That is what it is for.*

*And that is the answer.*

---

"How long have you been in there," Sodoku said.

The thing did not respond.

"You are not older than me. You are younger. You stopped at thirty-two, which is the year of the room, and you have not aged a day since, and I have been standing here trying to work out why a stronger version of a man would be a younger one." He looked at it. "You are not the strong part. You are the part that could not look at it."

"I am the part that would have burned Vaeloris to the footings."

"You are the part that took the rage off me so that I could get out of bed. Somebody had to hold it. I could not hold it and stand up, so I gave it to you, and I built a wall around you and I have been feeding you through it for nineteen years, and you have kept your side of the arrangement, and you have been the only reason I have functioned." He found his voice had not moved at all. "So thank you. I mean that. But you have never once been in the room with what happened, because holding the rage is how you avoid being in the room, and that is the whole of your function, and you cannot advise me on a thing you have spent your entire existence not looking at."

"You are afraid."

"Of course."

The thing stopped.

"Say that again."

"I have been afraid since the water. I was afraid on the boat and I was afraid in the hall and I was afraid this morning in the yard with my hand on her head, and I have been afraid every single day that I would find out what happened in that room, and now I have found out and I am still afraid, and none of it has ever once stopped me putting my boots on." He looked at it steadily. "You thought fear was the accusation. It is the condition. Every man on that road is in it. The only question anybody has ever been asked is what they did while it was true."

"They can all see it," the thing said, and there was heat in it now, which there had never been. "Every one of them. Your daughter can see it. She has been able to see it since she was four years old and it is why she does not ask you for anything."

Sodoku Moto took that.

He took it all the way in, without argument, and stood there in a place with no ground in it and let it be true, and the mandible of the void running down the side of the thing's face flexed once and did not know what to do.

"Yes," he said.

And then he turned his back on it.

That was all it took. There was no working in it and no expenditure and nothing he could afterward have described to a Bench. He stopped looking inward, which was the only ground the thing had ever stood on, and he looked at what was actually in front of him, which was a road and ice and men, and the noise in him that had been running since he was fifteen years old, the Ledger reading every room, the Shingan sorting every surface into what it hid and what it owed, the ranking and the assigning, all of it went quiet.

None of it suppressed. All of it irrelevant, the way a tool stops mattering when the hand carrying it has nothing left to hold.

He had always been this.

Behind him the thing with his face said his name, twice, and then said something about the price and about how much was still available and about the arithmetic of a single second, and it went on talking for some time, and Sodoku Moto did not turn around and did not answer and could no longer entirely hear it.

He was thinking about a bowl in a kitchen.

She had been holding it against her hip with her forearm and stirring with the other hand, and she had said the thing about the whereabouts without looking up, and he had agreed to it because it was sensible, and neither of them had said anything else about it ever again.

He was thinking that the bond in that room had been intact at the end, and that nothing anyone did to her had reached it, and that this was a fact about how the world was built and had been true before he learned it and would be true after him.

The dark ahead of him thinned.

There was a road under his boots again, and ice in the air, and the sound of two thousand men, and somebody a long way off shouting a name that was not his.

Sodoku Moto set his sword low along his right leg with the edge turned out, and walked on into the narrows.

## Notes (author-facing, not canon)

**Your corrupted self has a canon name and I used it.** *The Two States of the Moto* already documents this exactly: the Shallow-Seen Unburied, the Obsession Force inversion of Asami, which the Moto scholars refused to formally name because they considered naming it too dangerous. The stated difference is the one your scene needed: **Asami looks outward at what is actually here; the Unburied looks inward, at what it has always been denied, at the gap it is finally free to fill.** So the whole confrontation runs on that axis, and Sodoku's answer is a direction of attention rather than an act of will.

**Gisei is the question, and it has a price list.** *How much are you willing to give* is not rhetorical, because the Plane accepts memories, capacities, Traits, and **bonds severed at the Attraction Layer**, at a rate set by what the loss costs the man making it, and it cannot be deceived because the grief itself does the conversion. Sodoku has one bond of that magnitude left in the world. The Unburied names her, and names her as the perfect offering precisely because nobody is harmed.

**The psychology, researched.**

*Moral injury*, which is the correct frame for him and is clinically distinct from PTSD. PTSD is fear-based and centres on threat. Moral injury is identity-based and centres on self-referential processing; it runs on guilt, shame, self-condemnation and betrayal, and its governing question is whether you are still a good person. The literature separates perpetration-based, witnessing-based, and **failure-to-act** and **betrayal-based** events. Sodoku's is the last two at once: he was elsewhere, and the man who did it had eaten at his table.

*The inner persecutor.* Ferenczi's identification-with-the-aggressor model, carried into modern dissociative-structure work: the persecutor self-state carries the rage and contempt so the main self can go on functioning and stay attached, and it works by keeping the traumatic memory from ever being assimilated. So the Unburied's contempt is a protection racket, and its function is avoidance. That gives Sodoku the only reply available: **you are not the strong part, you are the part that could not look at it.** It stopped at thirty-two because thirty-two is the year of the room, and it has never once been in that room, because holding the rage is how it avoids being in there.

*The fear exchange.* Shame says I am bad; the Unburied is running shame and calling it an accusation. Sodoku takes it entirely, says yes, and it is the only thing in the scene the Unburied has no answer to.

**The bond survived and that is the beautiful, unbearable thing.** He watches four days and what breaks him is none of it. At the end her Attraction Layer anchor is intact, undegraded, unstained, the same shape it was in a kitchen nineteen years earlier, and they never knew it was there to reach. That is the most beautiful thing he has ever been shown and he would give every year he has left to unsee it.

**And the reason for the four days is the worst detail in the file.** Seiji wanted his whereabouts. She did not have them, because Sodoku had removed them from her in advance, for her own safety, with her agreement, in a kitchen, over a bowl. Originated and pending your ruling, and I will pull it if it goes against what you have planned.

**Kujo unwritten, and the irony is free.** He has just said aloud that resistance and expenditure are what pay for Phase Three. The Unburied spends the whole scene urging Sodoku to spend everything he has. The reader can hear both. Sodoku cannot, and I have not hinted at it in his voice.

**Rulings.**
1. *Freya* is not in project knowledge anywhere. Entered here as canon on your line.
2. *The Unburied appearing at thirty-two with the pre-ice face and an unfinished mandible of the void down one side* is originated, and the age is doing real work.
3. *Sodoku reaches Asami at the end of this scene.* The record's account is exactly what I wrote: the Ledger and the Shingan going irrelevant rather than suppressed, and the recognition that he has always been this. He does not enter Gisei and does not sacrifice anything. That is a large canon event and it is your ruling.
4. *Nineteen years* used throughout for the gap; confirm against Hild's age.

**Open.** What the Unburied does now that it is outside the wall and has been refused. Whether Asami holds when he reaches the narrows. Whether anyone at the Seat notices what walked back onto that road.

# The Imperceptible District

*Kharven. The narrows. Charles Lambert's last testament.*

---

He got the chain over his head with two fingers and a thumb because the other two would not close.

Ash in his mouth. The cold of the ice-field on the ruined side of his face where the eyebrow had opened again, and under both of those the flat animal smell of a road that had two thousand men on it and had been at this since the ninth hour. His ribs on the left side had been broken for four hours and had settled into a thing he could work around by breathing shallow and not turning.

The amulet came out from under the collar and hung.

A scale. Two pans on a beam, worked in silver gone black in the recesses, no bigger than a thumbnail, and around the rim and across the face of both pans the glyph-work ran continuous and unbroken, the Urion hand, the old one, the version the Bench stopped teaching in the year Charles was born because it was too easy to get wrong. Eleven glyphs to the inch. He had counted them once, in a cold room, on the night they gave it to him, because counting was what he did with his hands when he was frightened.

Ten years ago.

They had done it in the lower chapel at Altherion with four men present and the door barred, and it had taken eleven minutes, and nobody had said a word that was not in the form. Aurelian had put the chain over his head. Darius Ignatius Sanctus Sanctorum had stood on the other side of the table with his hands behind his back and had not looked at him once, and had signed second, and afterward the two of them had been walked out of that chapel through different doors and had not been in a room together since.

The office is not held by a man. It is held between two, and it is called the God Hand in the family literature and in nothing else, and Charles had never learned why, and had asked once and been told that the reason was in the sealed part.

One to carry and one to spend. That much he had worked out for himself inside a year, from the shape of the form and from which of them had signed first. The carrier does not know when. The spender does not know where. Neither is told the other's condition, ever, on the grounds that a man who can check on his other half will eventually check instead of act.

The other half of it had been in the ash at Sum-gol for six weeks. He knew that the way he knew the weather.

*I have carried this for ten years and I have never once been able to tell my wife what it was, and she is dead, and I have never once been able to tell my son, and he is four months old and asleep on a keel somewhere north of here with a woman I paid.*

*Aurelian said it would be a thing I would know when to do. He said it in that chapel with his hand still on the chain and he was afraid when he said it, and he was afraid every day of his life, and this morning he stood four yards away and watched a man knee me in the stomach and found it interesting.*

*So there is nobody left to ask.*

---

He looked up the road.

Four hundred paces of it, and the ice, and the ridges, and at the far end of it the black with the gold in it, and inside that a shape with its hand somewhere it should not have been.

He could not see the eyes. He knew they were there and he could not perceive them and had never been able to perceive anything of that order, and that was the whole of Charles Lambert, fifty-three assessments deep in the Accord's file at the bottom of the register, no output, no faculty, no working worth the name.

Which was the reason.

Thirty-eight years in the Church and the only thing about himself he had ever been proud of was the handwriting.

*They did not give it to a practitioner. Everyone at that table understood why and nobody said it out loud. A man with a faculty is a man the register has a great deal of information about, and information is the thing this destroys, and it would have taken him apart before he finished the second line.*

*They gave it to the emptiest man in the building.*

He put his thumb on the beam of the little scale and pressed until it turned.

---

The first thing was that he could see.

Nothing of the eyes and nothing of the black. The road, and everything on the road, and everything that had not happened on the road, all of it arriving with the same weight and the same solidity and the same claim on being the case.

The Imperceptible District. That was the Bench's term for it and Charles had filed correspondence about it for nineteen years without ever once understanding that it was a place. Everything the register cannot weigh, because nothing witnessed it, because it left no mark, because it did not come out. The road not taken and the blow not landed and the word not said. Filed under the same heading and kept, and the Church's position for four hundred years had been that the District is real and cannot be entered and that the two facts together are the reason the Scale is the Church's sign.

It came up into his hand and became as real as the mud.

Every man on that road, and beside him the eleven of him that had gone the other way at some point in the last four hours, all standing, all as solid as each other. Wren Greymane on the ditch bank, dead, and beside him Wren Greymane not dead, and Wren Greymane never a traitor at all, and every one of them with the same claim. The ridge that came up under the Stormfold road at the fourth mile and the ridge that did not. Kasumi in a courtyard at Vaeloris in the fourth hour of the ninth of Tenth-month, and Kasumi walking out of that courtyard, and Charles Lambert did not look at that one and did not permit himself to look at that one and knew that he was going to look at it before the end.

The whole world stopped being one thing.

*This is what it is. Nobody told me and I understand it now with the thing in my hand and I have about a minute.*

*A thing is only definite because the world kept a record of it. That is all of it. Nothing about it happened harder than the others. It left marks in enough places that the alternatives can no longer reach each other, and once they cannot reach each other, one of them is the case and the rest are gone.*

*And that creature at the end of the road works on the record. It finds a completed thing and asks whether the completion still belongs to it. It needs the marks. Everything it has ever done, it has done to a finished sequence.*

*So do not fight the finished sequence.*

*Take the record away and there is nothing finished anywhere for it to hold.*

There would be no second attempt. That was in the form too, and it was the only sentence in the whole eleven minutes that had been spoken in the common tongue, and it had been spoken by Darius, and it was four words long.

He walked forward eleven paces and stopped in the middle of the road with his ribs grinding and the scale turning in his fist and the beam of it hot enough now to be a difficulty.

He began.

"Creatura infecta."

Unfinished creature.

The word went out and the ice took it. He said it in the Church Latin he had used at eleven hundred offices of the dead, in the reading voice, because it was the only voice he had ever used for anything that mattered, and the road under him began to lose its edges.

"Te vinciat Planities Fati. Te liget."

Let the Plane of Fate shackle you. Let it bind you.

"Arcus graduum tibi parcant."

Let the arcs of degrees spare you.

And then the two that were the working, which Charles Lambert had known by heart for ten years and had said aloud exactly once, in a cold chapel, with his hand shaking.

"Sit omne quod fieri potuit."

Let everything that could have happened, be.

"Nihil perficiatur."

Let nothing be completed.

---

The cold hand came out of him.

That was the honest description and there was no other. It came out through the front of his chest and it was not his and it had never been his, and the arm of it went up the road four hundred paces in the length of one heartbeat, and it took hold.

Charles could not see what it took hold of. He could see the effect: an arm somewhere at the far end of the narrows going upright, wrist first, lifted the way an official lifts a man's hand off a document he is not entitled to sign.

And there was a figure standing at the end of the arm, and it had not walked there, and the ice under it was undisturbed.

White. A white that was not light and did not illuminate the ice or the mud or anything else, standing in a corona that moved on its own account and hid everything inside it, so that the shape had a height and a set of shoulders and an outline and nothing else at all, and Charles Lambert, who had been in the Church for thirty-eight years and had heard every account, looked directly at it and could not have said afterward whether it had a face.

*I did not summon that. I opened a door and something that was already standing behind it stepped out, and the four men in that chapel knew and did not tell me, and I would have taken the chain anyway.*

The Void near it came apart.

It unspooled. That was the only word for what it did: the black at the near edge of it stopped being a mass and became a length, and the length ran out and out and went on running, unwinding from the far end of the narrows in a line that got thinner as it went and did not stop getting thinner.

And time fractured.

Charles felt it in the ribs first, which was absurd and was also the truth: the broken ends of two ribs on his left side grated in four different ways in the same instant, because there were four different ways he had been standing, and all four had become the case. Around him the road did the same. Every unfinished possibility in the Ashgate valley came up off the floor of the District together and broke against the ones that had been standing on top of them, and what had been a fixed thing with one outcome became a relative thing with a great many, and none of them completed.

Somewhere up the road the black with the gold in it was made to twist, and the twisting came from the Plane of Fate directly and had nothing local in it at all, and Charles could not see that either and knew it from the way the ice went.

Down on the road to his right, at fifty feet, a man in Moto harness with a sword low along his right leg went down onto both knees in the ice and stayed there.

*Good.*

*Stay down. Whatever you are, whatever you have become in the last hour, stay on the ground until this stops, because I do not think that a man standing upright inside this keeps the shape he came in with.*

---

The scale had gone through the skin of his palm.

He did not remember when. It was in the meat of his hand now, both pans and the beam, and it was drawing, and what it was drawing was the part of him the register held.

He understood the price about four seconds before it finished, which was three and a half seconds later than he would have liked and exactly as long as the four men in that chapel had intended.

Records. That was the currency and it had always been the currency and it was written in the sealed part, and to take the marks off the world you have to put marks in, and the only marks available were the ones that said Charles Lambert had been here.

It took the vestry minutes first. He felt nineteen years of them go, every meeting he had ever taken down in a hand somebody would have recognised. Then the ordination roll. Then the marriage line in the parish book at Vaeloris, and the courtyard report, and the four hundred readings of it, and then the report itself, and Charles Lambert made a sound he had never made in his life and let it go and let it go and let it all go.

Kasumi went last of the writing.

The record of her, which was all the thing in his hand could reach. What was left afterward was the thing that had been under the record the whole time, unfiled, unweighed, unentered anywhere, in the District where it had always actually lived, and it was intact.

He had that for about a second and a half and it was enough.

He got his mouth open.

What came out of it was not entirely his voice. It came out doubled, layered under itself, his own and a second one over the top that was lower and drier and belonged to a man standing in ash eleven hundred miles away who had spent six weeks learning how the Works stand and had just been handed the trial he was not told about in advance.

"What did I tell you, kid."

It went up the narrows. It went past the ice and the ridges and the black and it did not go to any of them.

"You are not allowed to die on my watch."

---

Afterward, in the accounts, there would be a hole.

Sixty depositions taken by the Bench and every one of them describing a white figure and a cold hand and an arm being lifted, and not one of them able to say who had opened it. Eleven witnesses within forty feet. The Seat's own gate-roll for that morning. The Alabaster Court's correspondence file, the ordination roll, the parish book at Vaeloris.

Nothing in any of them. Nowhere. Not a line.

A four-month-old boy on a keel north of the Sound would grow up in a household that could not produce a father for him on paper, and the Church would tell him what it had told the last one, which was that the sealed part is sealed.

Charles Lambert went down onto his side in the ice with the scale burned into his palm and both hands still up in front of his chest in the posture of the reading, and the last thing he did was turn his head to see whether the man in the Moto harness had stayed on his knees.

## Notes (author-facing, not canon)

**Nobody I am not allowed to write appears.** Kujo is an arm being lifted at four hundred paces and a twist Charles cannot perceive. Haruki does not appear at all; the doubled voice goes up the narrows and I do not render anyone receiving it. Sonzai is absent. POV is Charles start to finish, which is what a testament wants.

**The mechanism, researched, and it is the exact counter to what Kujo has just described about himself.**

He said it himself: he works on **completed sequences**. He finds a finished thing and renegotiates whether the completion still belongs to him, and he cannot erase what never occurred. So he needs the record.

The physics that gives you that: a thing is definite because the environment kept **records** of it. Decoherence is the environment monitoring a system and destroying the phase relations between the alternatives, so the branches stop being able to interfere and one of them becomes the case. Einselection is that process picking which states get to be definite. The determining factor is redundancy of the record, not the event being somehow more real.

So Charles does not attack the finished sequence. He takes the records away, restores interference between everything that could have happened, and leaves the valley with nothing completed anywhere for the Unwinding to hold. *Sit omne quod fieri potuit. Nihil perficiatur.* Let everything that could have happened, be. Let nothing be completed.

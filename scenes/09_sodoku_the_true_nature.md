# The True Nature

*The narrows. The road to the Seat. Continuous.*

---

He came up out of the steam into warmth.

Nothing from the mud and nothing off the melt. The warmth that had arrived at the one layer that had not gone anywhere, the hand on his arm, the thing that had come in from outside when nothing else in four worlds was coming in from outside. It went through the middle of him and out along the crystallised avenues to the last joint of every finger, and it kept going after it reached them.

He stood up in it.

The monster stood up with him and did not run the show.

That was the thing he had not expected and had no framework for. Everything he had been between the corridor and the water was still there, undiminished, the whole Scourge, the road out of the Tenrai settlement and the villages on it and the specific quiet of the third one. It had crystallised with the rest. It had gone into the lattice at the same moment as the grief and the daughter and the kitchen and the warmth, and what came out on the far side of the front held all of it in one structure, and none of it was in charge.

Sodoku Moto looked at Yoko Mishiro.

She had her hand where it had been. She had not moved it, and she was still kneeling on the leg with the spearhead in it, and she was looking up at him with her ears flat back against her skull, and she did not let go.

The tears came then.

They came out of both eyes at once and went down his face and off his jaw, and he did not put a hand up to them and did not turn his head away and did not stop looking at her. He had not wept at the corridor. He had not wept at his mother, or at the water, or in nineteen months of a boat. He wept now, on a road, in front of the one person left, and the crying did not take anything out of him and did not fold him and did not reduce the thing standing there by a single degree.

He was still here.

*She stayed.*

*Her nose has told her there is nothing under her hand and she has kept the hand there for the length of it, and she is the only living thing in four hundred paces that has not moved away from me, and the reason is that she was chained once and knows what it is to be the thing nobody comes back for.*

The air began to turn.

---

It gathered on him the way a charge gathers, from the crystallised centre outward, and every strand that had come down through the fractured sky and gone into his shoulders and his crown and the backs of his hands was feeding it. The field around his body climbed. Aetheric Density in that four-hundred-pace valley had been the highest any practitioner on the road would see in a lifetime an hour ago, and it stopped being the relevant number, because what mattered now was the gradient across four feet of him.

He put his hand out to them.

That was all. An open hand, down and forward, offered to a woman on one knee in melted ice, and it was the first ordinary thing he had done since the whistle. There was no working in the gesture and nothing behind it but the gesture, and the whole valley bent toward it anyway, because a hand offered by a thing at that gradient is an event whether or not the man means it to be.

And the Plane stopped.

---

The threads of Fate are not objects. That is the first thing a Moto is taught and the last thing any of them understands, and Sodoku had recited it at fifteen and had believed he knew what it meant.

They arrive and they pay themselves back. Every one of them is a small unique nothing, seething at every scale, coming into being with its opposite and going out again inside an interval too short for anything to happen in. They are the bookkeeping the world does under itself. They have no weight. Nothing has ever been cut by one and nothing ever could be, because before a thread could reach an edge it has already returned what it borrowed.

Unless the field is strong enough.

Above a certain gradient the pair does not get back. It is pulled apart faster than it can close, and the separation goes past the distance at which return is available, and the borrowing is never repaid because the debt has been settled from outside.

The virtual becomes real.

It is not an amplification of the thing that was already happening. It is a qualitative breakdown of the state that had been holding, and above the threshold the vacuum stops being a vacuum and starts producing, and what it produces has mass, and a thing with mass has an edge.

The Plane of Fate actualised in a four-hundred-pace valley in Kharven.

It did not come down and it did not arrive from anywhere. It had been there the whole time, under everything, the way it is under everything, and it stopped being under and became present, and it came into the narrows with the entire weight of a stratum that had never once had weight in the Material.

Everything on that road held still while it happened, and afterward the sixty depositions the Bench collected would agree on almost nothing except that there had been a pause, and that the pause had not been in the men.

Behind Sodoku Moto, four feet off his left shoulder, something that had been standing there ceased to be able to occupy the position.

He did not turn around. What he read, through the eye that had refused to shut all morning, was a term going real. There had been a carried thing at that spot, an unpaid relationship held forward out of a completed sequence, virtual in the exact sense the word has, weightless, uncuttable, present only as an entry. The stratum actualised across it. The entry acquired mass in the middle of being an entry.

And a mass cannot occupy a place that has already been actualised by something heavier.

It came apart along the seam where the carrying had been joined, cleanly, along no edge that any blade has ever had, and the two halves went out sideways into the dark and were not connected to each other any more.

---

The lance came out of his chest.

Violet, collimated, four feet across at the root and holding four feet across at four hundred paces, and it left him without recoil because the thing pushing it had never been momentum. It went up the narrows on a flat line at the height of a man's sternum and it took the black with it.

Nothing burned. The dark it passed through stopped having anything joined to anything. Where the beam went, the road and the ice and the ridges and the standing black were all reduced to their constituents at the same instant and none of it got hot, because the field crossed the binding energy of the bonds before it crossed anything a thermometer would have noticed, and matter that has its bonds taken faster than heat can move through it does not burn or melt or char.

It leaves. As what it was made of, in a straight line, at speed.

There is a threshold for that too and it sits well under the one the Plane had just crossed. Molecular bonds hold at a few units of the field. The gradient standing off his sternum was past that by more orders of magnitude than any instrument in Kharven could have counted, which is why the corridor the lance opened had walls to it, and why the walls were clean, and why nothing along four hundred paces of it caught fire.

Four hundred paces of the Ashgate road opened from Sodoku Moto to the far end of the valley and the black came off it in a corridor with edges.

And in the cleared space, on the ditch bank, he saw the body.

---

Charles Lambert had been going for some minutes.

Sodoku had never met him and did not know the name and would learn it eleven days later from a woman on a keel. What he saw, through the actualised stratum with everything visible at once, was a man on his back in the ice with both hands still up in front of his chest in the posture of the reading, and a small scale burned through the meat of one palm, and no file on him anywhere.

Nothing. No record, no entry, no line in any book in four worlds. Sodoku's own trained apparatus went at him and came back with the same nothing it had come back with from an old man in a deel, and this was a different nothing, and Sodoku understood the difference in the moment he saw it.

The body was decaying and going up.

No rot in it. The strands of him were unbinding out of the bundle and going into the general Plane, and they were doing it fast, because there was nothing left holding them and no record anywhere to say which had been which. Every one of them into the continuum, free to take any value, unfiled.

It was going badly.

A dissolution with no boundary condition on it comes apart in every direction at once and takes whatever is nearest with it, and there were eleven hundred men on that road, and there was a woman on one knee behind him.

Sodoku put the actualised weight across it.

He did not stop the man's going and did not want to. What he did was give it a shape: a clean edge, a direction, a rate, an ending with a form to it, in the way a mason gives a collapse a line to fall along so it goes into the yard instead of into the street. The going stopped being a scattering and became a departure, and it went up out of the Ashgate road in one piece, whole and orderly and gone.

He authored the last four seconds of a stranger, and the authoring cost him something he did not price and would not find missing for two months.

"Rest," said Sodoku Moto.

---

The wings came out of his back.

They came out along the same crystallised lines the front had run down, out of the shoulder blades where the strands had gone in, and they were the same violet as everything else because everything else was now the same violet. No feather in them and no membrane. Actualised Plane, standing off him in two sheets, and where they moved the air behind them did not close, because the air behind them had been taken apart and had to be replaced from further out.

This is what a Moto is under the Work.

The eyes are apparatus. So is the Ledger, and the sixteen Stages of installed friction, and every one of the four disciplines the family has spent eleven hundred years arguing the order of. Those are the tools a man carries so that he does not have to be the thing that carries them.

The Plane is not something a Moto reaches into.

It is what a Moto is made of, and the entire architecture of the bloodline exists to keep that fact from ever becoming load-bearing, because a man who finds it out has stopped being a man in the way that word gets used at a table.

He found it out on a road with a dead girl four miles behind him, which is the order these things arrive in and has been the order since Bara, and the family literature has eleven words to say about that and has been saying them for eleven hundred years.

---

He moved.

The first stride took him eleven hundred feet and the second took him further, and there was no flight in it and no working under his feet, and what happened under each foot was that the ground stopped being bonded.

It went to its constituents in a circle nine feet across and it did so without heat, and the circles stayed behind him down the whole length of the road out of the narrows and up the shoulder of rock, eleven of them, each one a perfect disc of what had been stone and frozen mud and was now a fine hanging dust that would still be settling at nightfall.

Kharven-Seat came up in front of him.

The outer wall was open. The gate was open and had been opened from inside. The yard he had walked out of at first light with eleven hundred men behind him had things standing in it that had come up out of the black, and there were a great many of them, and they turned toward him together.

He went through the doors.

He did not open them. He arrived at them at the speed he had been travelling and the doors and the frame and eleven feet of the wall on either side went to dust in front of him and he was through, and the dust hung in the doorway behind him in the shape the doorway had been.

There were, at a count nobody made, somewhere between three and four hundred of them in that yard.

The voidlings died on the way past.

All of them, in the length of the crossing, without a stroke being aimed at any of them. They came into the field around him and their bonds went where every other set of bonds in his path had gone, and they came apart into what they had been assembled from, and none of them made a sound because sound requires something to still be joined together.

The dust of them went up and hung, and it hung at the same height as the dust of the doors, and by the small hours it would all have come down together onto the flags in a layer the depth of a fingernail, and men would still be sweeping it out of the corners at Ice-out.

The inner court went quiet.

Sodoku Moto stopped in the middle of it, in the ruin of a yard where a girl had stood at the foot of a stair that morning with a broken circlet in her hand, and he drew.

Kurosetsu came off his back and out.

He held the Black Blade at arm's length in front of him and looked at it. The violet stood off the steel in a corona a hand deep, and it was not sitting on the blade and was not being carried by it, and what the eye showed him was the edge and the stratum agreeing about where the edge was.

The whole of the Plane, resonating along four feet of iron.

Sodoku Moto turned his wrist once, and set his feet in the ash of his own courtyard, and started up the inner stair.

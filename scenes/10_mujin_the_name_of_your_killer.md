# The Name of Your Killer

*The narrows, the Ashgate road. Immediately after.*

---

## I

The fighting stopped in pieces.

Sodoku Moto stood four hundred paces up the road and watched it go out along the whole length of the narrows the way a fire goes out of wet straw, in patches, unevenly, with men in the middle of it still swinging at nothing for a second or two after the men beside them had lowered their arms.

It started at the ditch bank.

A Greymane serjeant with half a helmet got up onto the bank and looked down at what was pinned there and made a noise that had a word inside it.

"Wren."

It went up the road. Nobody organised it and nobody made a chant of it. It went from mouth to mouth in both colours, because half of the men on that road had spent sixteen months hating that name and the other half had spent sixteen months carrying it, and every one of them turned to look at the same place at the same time.

*They are going to break.*

*Both sides at once, in a defile, with a ditch on one flank and a drop on the other and four hundred paces of dead men between them and any ground worth standing on. Two thousand men are about to find out that there is nowhere to run to, and the ones at the back are going to push, and it will be the ditch that kills most of them.*

He got his hand up and put two fingers in his mouth and whistled, twice, long, and the sound went down the road ahead of him.

Then the second figure came onto the field.

It arrived at the far end of the narrows, past Kaalabad, past the wreck of forty feet of lifted road, and it did not walk up out of anywhere and it did not step through any seam. It resolved. A man's shape stood there at about half the density of a man, gleaming, edged in a light that had no source and cast nothing, and the ground under it was undisturbed and the snow on the ground under it was undisturbed, and for perhaps three seconds Sodoku could see the broken cart on the far side of the road straight through the middle of its chest.

He had spent thirty years being told what that was and had seen it done properly twice.

*The figure comes first and the body follows. That is a working walking out ahead of its own owner, and it means the man himself is somewhere behind it and closing, and it means he has enough to spare that he can afford to arrive twice.*

Nobody on the road knew the shape. Sodoku watched two thousand men fail to recognise it in the same instant, Tenrai and Greymane together, and something went out of the press that belonged to neither army.

Down at the ditch bank, the corona thrummed.

It went through the ground and came up the road four hundred paces and into the soles of Sodoku's boots and up the long bones of his legs, and every horse still standing in the narrows put its head down.

The sword went back into the scabbard.

*Leave it. Whatever that is, and whatever the other one is, I have four hundred paces of open road and no mount and one arm's worth of anything that would matter, and there are eleven hundred men on this road who can still be got off it.*

He turned his back on both of them and started shouting names.

---

## II

The road smelled of opened men and frozen mud and the particular hot-slate smell that comes off a working that has spent everything it had.

Kwon Mu-jin came the rest of the way in his own body four seconds later and the shape that had been standing there went into him without any ceremony at all.

His right arm hung at his side. Snow on the shoulder of the coat. Raga had stopped at the top of the orchard road because he had told it to and because the animal had understood which part of this was not for it.

"So you have returned."

The purple around Kaalabad had thickened at the wrists and gone dark at the right one, and the gauntlet on that hand no longer resembled a gauntlet, and he was holding Zarakal in the left with the right laid along the shaft for balance and taking no weight.

"Kwon Mu-jin." The head came up. "YOU HAVE RETURNED TO DIE."

Mu-jin did not answer him.

He had come eleven miles toward a sound in the ground and had spent the last of those eleven miles building four sentences, and every one of them had gone out of his head the moment he saw the ditch bank, and what he had instead was a man he had never met.

Big. Forty-odd. A shield split along the grain from boss to top edge, still strapped to the arm, and the arm gone below the elbow and the stump cauterised by whatever had taken it. The chest opened from the collarbone down through the middle by something with a spike on it. A face turned up at the sky with the eyes open.

He knew about the grey wolf on the surcoat. Everybody between here and Sum-gol knew about the grey wolf and what had been said about the man wearing it.

The soul was still coming away.

That was the thing that stopped Mu-jin where he stood. It had begun the parting and had not finished it, and the parting had a shape, and the shape was slow and orderly and going up in a way that souls on a battlefield almost never do, and there was no fear anywhere in it and no confusion in it and a great deal of something that Mu-jin had no name for and had spent forty years being unable to name.

His eye took it in.

The left one. It had been an ordinary brown eye six weeks ago in a room with a table and two cups on it. The white of it had gone violet, all of it, corner to corner, and the pupil at the centre of that had gone a deep dense white with no reflection in it whatsoever, and the physician at the school had spent four days on the arm and had not been shown the eye.

He walked over to the ditch bank.

The men on both sides of the road watched him do it and none of them moved.

He got down on one knee beside the body in his own snow-wet coat and put his left hand out and drew the lids down with two fingers, and held them a moment until they stayed.

"Rest in peace," said Kwon Mu-jin.

"PEACE."

The corona came up four feet.

"There will be no peace where he is going. There is hell and there are labours and there is nothing else on the far side of that road, and that is the destiny of every traitor who ever drew breath, and he will be a long time finding it out."

Mu-jin stood up.

Nothing about it was fast. He came up off the knee and got his boots square and stood there on the ditch bank with his back to the corpse, and the calm man who had run a school for eleven years and had been described in four separate Accord assessments as *temperamentally unsuited to field deployment* was somewhere else entirely.

The valley began to shake.

It came up out of the ground under the whole of the narrows at once. The lifted slabs of the road ground against each other and settled. Frost came off the ditch bank in sheets. Every loose stone in four hundred paces began to travel in small hops, and the men who had been standing went to their knees, and the men who had been on their knees went flat, and somewhere behind Mu-jin a supply cart came apart at every joint at the same moment and dropped its load into the mud.

It was leaking.

He knew that with the flat certainty of a man who has lived inside an arrangement his whole life and can feel when a seam has opened. Small, dense pieces of the thing he carried were coming out of him in a way that had nothing deliberate about it, and they were going into the ground and into the air and into the cold, and they were very small, and there were a great many of them.

The shaking went out past the narrows.

At the Kharven gates the bar in its iron cradle jumped a quarter inch and came back down. Eight miles south at the reach, in a breach twenty-two feet wide, three hundred men and two thousand two hundred felt the wall move against their shoulders and did not know why. Further out, in a salt basin, chain-bridges hanging at their stops for eleven hours swung four degrees and came back.

*Stop.*

*You will crack the world open across four hundred miles for one man you never met, and he would not have wanted it, and you do not even know his name.*

Mu-jin closed his left hand.

The shaking came down over about six seconds, as a bell comes down, and the ground went quiet and stayed quiet, and every man on the road inside a hundred feet of him stayed exactly where he had fallen.

Kaalabad started laughing.

"HAHAA." It went off the drop on the right and came back. "There you are. THERE you are. I put a spike through your bear's eye and took your arm off at the shoulder without touching it and you stood in an orchard and cried, and I have wondered all summer what it would take, and it was a stranger. A stranger on a ditch bank."

He set the shaft.

"GEUK-HON."

Mu-jin looked at him.

"That is not my name."

He said it in the voice he used for a boy who had put the wrong glyph in a Draft-circle for the third time in a term, and every man on the road who could hear it heard something in it that made them wish they had not.

"You should get the name of your killer properly. It is the one thing a man in your position can still get right, and it will be the only lesson I give you today." He stepped down off the ditch bank onto the road. "I am Kwon Mu-jin."

The arms came out of him.

They came out of the right side, out of the dead one, along the line the shoulder had stopped being able to move six weeks ago, and there were nine of them and then there were more than nine. Each was longer than a man and made of the same half-density light the figure had been made of, and every inch of every one of them was covered in glyphs.

Nothing was laid on the surface. The arms were made out of the glyphs, wrist to elbow to knuckle, Parunic in the hand the Accord used and letters in three other hands under it, and the letters moved and rearranged and settled, and any Measurewright alive would have recognised what they were doing, because that is what a grading mark does. Every called thing that answers him arrives annotated. It arrives readable and correctable, which is how he has been read and corrected since he was fourteen.

They closed into fists.

Kaalabad brought the pull in.

Mu-jin felt it arrive and felt what it was doing, and it was a good working and it was elegantly made and it had a centre and everything near the centre stopped choosing its own direction, and the arms took hold of it.

They did not break it. They bound it, and then they turned it, and the sphere the man had been holding in perfect balance for eleven hours went into a spiral with nobody driving it. The weight in the road had stopped being his. It had belonged to Mu-jin from the moment he stood up off that knee, and the pull had no way of knowing and went on pulling, and what it pulled on was a mass it could not lift, and the working came apart along its own axis with a sound that made two thousand men put their hands over their ears.

The fists landed together.

All of them, in the same instant, from nine directions, on a man at the Zenith who had killed everything that had ever come at him in forty years. Plate went in at the chest and the hip and both shoulders. Kaalabad left the ground and went a hundred and ten feet back up the road, and he went through the wreck of the slabs and through a cart and out the far side, and he did not tumble, because he was still holding on to the shaft.

He came down on his boots and skidded eleven yards and stayed up.

*It bound me.*

*Nothing binds me. The office does not bind me and the seal did not bind me and the wall in the Void did not bind me, and a schoolmaster with one arm has just turned my own centre inside out and hit me nine times before I finished the thought.*

He brought Zarakal round in the left hand and cut.

The Ruinic edge went through four of the arms and they came apart into their letters, and the letters hung in the air for a moment in the shape the arm had been, and then went out.

Mu-jin walked forward.

He did nothing else. He did not replace them and did not answer the cut and did not raise the remaining arms, and he came down the middle of the Ashgate road at the pace of a man crossing his own yard, and behind him the ditch bank and the shield and the body got further away, and in front of him the corona came up to meet him.

Kaalabad threw the arrows.

The purple came off him in bolts, forty of them, then eighty, and each one duplicated as it flew and each duplicate came with the weight of the original poured into it, so that what left his hand as forty arrived as several hundred and every one of them carried the whole of the first.

Half of them never got there.

They reached the air around Mu-jin and bent, wide, all in the same direction, and went out into the ditch and the drop and the frozen fields and opened forty holes in the Ashgate road that would still be there in the spring.

The rest went into his eye.

They did not strike it. They travelled toward the left side of his face and their line shortened and their line went on shortening past the point where anything should have been left to travel, and they entered the violet and the white at the centre of it and were gone, all of them, in the order they arrived, with no light and no sound and no report of any kind.

Kwon Mu-jin did not slow down.

He put his left hand out into the air in front of him and drew the circle.

## Notes (author-facing, not canon)

**Two POVs, split at the section break.** Sodoku holds the arrival, because your beat opens on him looking and because the projected form landing needs a witness who knows what a projected form is and is still frightened by it. Then it hands to Mu-jin for the confrontation. Neither of them registers the other, and Sodoku turns his back on both of them to get eleven hundred men off a road with a ditch on one flank and a drop on the other. That is a king's decision and it costs him the fight he walked four hundred paces to reach.

**Projected form, per Pack Ten.** The figure resolves at half density with the broken cart visible through its chest, the ground under it undisturbed, and the body arrives four seconds later and absorbs it without ceremony. Sodoku's read is the one that matters: a man who can afford to arrive twice.

**The arms come out of the dead side.** Nine and then more than nine, out of the right shoulder along the line that has not moved in six weeks. That is the whole of Geuk-hon's lesson made physical, and I did not have anyone say so. They are made *of* glyphs rather than marked with them, Parunic over three other hands, and the letters move and rearrange and settle, which is what a grading mark does. Every called thing that answers him arrives annotated, correctable, the same way he has been since he was fourteen. When Zarakal cuts four of them they come apart into their letters and the letters hang in the shape of the arm for a moment before going out.

**The binding, and why it works.** Kaalabad's centre is elegant and it is not broken. It is bound and then turned, and the sphere goes into a spiral with nobody driving it, because the weight in that road stopped being his the moment Mu-jin stood up. The pull has no way of knowing and goes on pulling on a mass it cannot lift, and the working comes apart along its own axis. That is your line about the magneto sphere, run through the read Wren died buying.

**The leak is the scene's danger and Mu-jin is the one who stops it.** The shaking reaches the Kharven gate bar, the breach at the southern reach where Bram is still standing, and the chain-bridges in the Nalūn basin. He closes his left hand and it comes down over six seconds. Nobody made him.

**The eye is unexplained and stays unexplained.** Violet where the white should be, dense white at the centre, no reflection. The physician had four days on the arm and was never shown it. Half the arrows bend wide and open forty holes in the road that will be there in the spring. The rest travel toward the left side of his face and their line goes on shortening past the point where anything should be left to travel, and they are gone with no light, no sound, and no report. What it is, and where the several hundred bolts went, is left open on purpose.

**Kaalabad's continuity.** The right gauntlet no longer resembles a gauntlet and he is fighting Zarakal one-handed in the left with the right laid along the shaft taking no weight. His italic is the point: nothing binds him, the office did not, the seal in the Void did not, and a schoolmaster with one arm has just turned his own centre inside out and hit him nine times before he finished the thought.

**Canon and rulings.**
1. *Mu-jin's known limit is three sustained forms before strain*, four or five under Full Accounting at severe cost. He is well past that here and I have priced none of it yet. Whatever this costs should land in the next beat, and the Slip's currency is memory.
2. *He does not know Wren's name.* He closes the eyes of a stranger, says four words, and the whole rest of the scene comes out of that. Nobody on that road will ever tell him who it was.
3. *Ruinic blade* used as your term for Zarakal's edge.
4. *"Temperamentally unsuited to field deployment"* in four Accord assessments is originated.
5. *Geuk-hon* named only in Kaalabad's mouth and refused. Mu-jin's correction is the scene's title beat and I gave it the schoolmaster's register on purpose.

**Open.** What the eye did with several hundred bolts. What the leak cost, four hundred miles wide. Whether Sodoku gets his men clear before the second exchange.

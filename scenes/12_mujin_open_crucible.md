# Open Crucible

*The narrows, the far end. Continuous.*

---

They had been trading weight for eleven minutes and neither of them had landed anything that was not paid for.

That was the honest shape of it and Kwon Mu-jin had known it since the fourth exchange. There was no technique left in the fight. What was happening on that road was two Soul Crystals putting their entire Coherence against each other in turn to find out whose Shell buckled first, and buckling had been running about even, and every blow either of them landed came off a reserve with a floor under it.

His was in sight of the floor now.

Fervor is the word a document uses. In a body it is the thing that decides whether the next demand on the Crystal gets answered at all, and for the first four hours the answer had been immediate, and for the last eleven minutes it had been arriving late, and twice in the last forty seconds it had not arrived.

Every practitioner has a figure for it and every practitioner learns the same lesson about the figure, which is that it does not warn you. Harmonics falls off against Aetheric Density in a curve that stays almost flat and then does not, and a man reading his own draw feels comfortable right up until the moment he is on the steep part with nothing under him.

Kaalabad's plate was black with him.

Forearms to gorget, the geometric wrongness of it slicked over, the buckle-lines filled. Mu-jin's blood, in quantity, and none of it Kaalabad's, and there was a professional insult in that which he was too far gone to feel properly.

Zarakal was through his chest.

The head had gone in at the right side under the third rib and out at the back below the scapula and the shaft was still occupying the space. Right lung down. He knew the sequence from the inside because he had watched it in other people. Air into the pleural space through a wound that would not close and a lung folding away from the chest wall, and haemothorax filling the space behind it at a rate he estimated once and then declined to estimate again. Class three haemorrhage, going on class four. The intercostal vessels are the ones that do it and they do not clot against a shaft that is still occupying the hole, and the shaft was the only reason he was not already finished. The tell was that his own hearing had gone flat and far off and that he had stopped feeling cold in a valley that was still killing men by temperature.

He was working on one lung, with one arm, half a hand of usable fingers, Vitality spent past the point where a wound closes on its own, and an eye that took things in and did not report what it did with them.

"So this is what you have been hiding."

Kaalabad's voice came down the shaft.

"All this. All morning, and in the orchard, and every year before that in whatever room they kept you in. You had this the entire time."

Mu-jin did not answer. Answering costs air.

*He is wrong, and it is going to kill him, and I do not have the breath to correct him.*

*I hid nothing. There was nothing to hide. What is happening today is not a reserve I kept back. It is that a man took my arm and my bear's eye and my standing and left me with an inventory short enough to finally count, and counting is the only thing I have ever been good at.*

---

The purple went out of him.

It did not fade and it did not fail. It darkened, all at once, through the last violet and into a black that had no shine on it anywhere, and the corona stopped being a corona and became an absence with an outline.

Then it began to turn.

That was the change and Mu-jin's eye caught it about a quarter-second before his body did. The centre that had been a static pull all morning acquired rotation, and the space immediately around it went with it, dragged into corotation, the whole local structure of the road turning with a thing at the middle of it that was not visibly turning at all. The same arrangement a spinning magnetised body imposes on everything inside its own field, and the same failure mode waiting at the edge of it.

And a dragged region has a radius.

Out at the distance where the space could no longer keep up, the corotation failed. It had to. Nothing that has to travel with a centre can keep travelling with it past the point where keeping up would cost more than exists, and at that radius the structure did not simply stop turning.

It opened.

Junctures. Eleven of them at first and then more than eleven, standing in the air at the failure radius in a rough ring, each one a place where the bent space had come apart along its own strain and had failed to close behind itself. They spindled as they formed, narrowing down and inward with a slow twist, and through every one of them the field that had been trapped inside the corotating region got out.

It came through as a pulse.

Nothing thermal in any part of it. Mu-jin had spent the previous exchange being killed by thermodynamics and this was a different law wearing the same violence, and he registered the difference in the half-second he had, because the ice hanging in the air did not melt as it arrived. The crystals aligned. Every plate and column and six-armed thing across forty feet turned on its own axis at once and stood parallel, and then they stopped being crystals and started being needles, elongated along the field, drawn out of the shape water makes and into the shape a field makes when the gradient runs stronger than the bonds holding the lattice square.

What came out along those open lines was the entire pressure the corotating region had been holding, released in the only direction still available to it, and it came out as a beam because an opening that narrow cannot produce anything else.

The pulse came through the ring of junctures on eleven curved paths and arrived at him.

Mu-jin barely saw it. That is the exact truth of it: the eye took the whole event in, all eleven arrivals with their geometry and their gradient and their timing, and the man behind the eye got perhaps the first two of them before the arithmetic outran him.

The other nine went in.

---

His body did not fall down.

It should have. He was standing on a road with a polearm through his chest and one lung and a right arm that had been dead six weeks and had frozen solid an hour ago, and eleven curved arrivals had just gone through the whole architecture, and the correct behaviour of that body was to stop.

It stayed up.

He was not doing it. That was the part he would think about afterward, on the two occasions he was able to think about it at all. There was no signal going down from him into the legs, because there was very little of him left up there to send one, and the legs held the road anyway and the spine held the legs and something adjusted the left foot four inches to keep the line under the load.

The vessel was moving itself.

*I am cargo.*

*Whatever this is, it has been carrying me since the ditch bank and I have been taking credit for it, and it is not obeying me and it never was, and I am going to be dead in about ninety seconds and I would very much like to have found out what it is first.*

He got the left hand up.

It took most of what remained. The shoulder answered late and the elbow answered later and the hand came across his chest and stopped at the sternum, and there was nothing on the right side to bring to meet it, and he brought the dead arm up with the living one, hooked under the wrist, and set the two palms together.

Blood came out of his mouth.

It left him warm and it did not stay warm. It went from liquid to solid in the air between his lips and his hands with no interval in the middle, straight through, no drops, and what landed on the backs of his fingers was a scatter of small bright crystals that had been the inside of him a quarter-second earlier.

He set his teeth in what was left and said it.

"Domain Expansion."

The valley took a breath.

"Open Crucible."

---

A Domain seals.

That is the whole of what a Domain is and every practitioner alive learns it the same way: you draw a boundary and inside the boundary your terms are the terms, and the strength of the thing is the strength of the wall, and everything that matters about it is exclusion. A Domain keeps the world out. That is why the great ones are described by their walls and why the counter to every one of them that has ever been catalogued is to break the wall.

His had no wall.

It went out from his joined hands to a radius of about two hundred feet and it did not exclude one thing. Everything on that road at that moment came inside it: the ice and the needles and the wreck and the dead, the eleven junctures still spindling in their ring, the pulse still arriving along its curved paths, the whole standing black and the thing at the centre of it, and Kwon Mu-jin, and Zarakal, and every drop of Mu-jin's blood on another man's plate.

An open crucible accepts the charge. All of it. That is the first step of an assay and there is no assay without it, and any assayer will tell you that the sample you exclude is the sample you have already decided the answer for.

Then it began to work.

Assay is a separation by differential affinity and nothing else. You bring the whole charge up together and you supply the one condition, and under that condition the base content reacts and the noble content does not, and that is the entire principle and it has not changed since the Bronze Age. What reacts becomes fusible and drops its surface tension and gets drawn into the porous wall by capillary action. What does not react stays on the surface, and coalesces as the rest is taken away, and at the end there is a bead.

The condition Mu-jin supplied was ownership.

Everything in that Domain was assayed against the question of whether it belonged to the thing carrying it.

The borrowed content went first.

Across two hundred feet the field began to lose the material that had been drawn into it out of somewhere else, and there was a very great deal of that, because the Ashgate valley had been the richest and most polluted Aether in four worlds since the ninth hour. Aetheric Residue off the aurora. Off a shield spending itself grey. From three charges out of a dead smith's belt. From four hundred paces of men dying with workings half made. From a leak out of Mu-jin's own seams that he had been ashamed of for an hour.

All of it went to the walls.

And the black at the centre of the ring began to come off the man wearing it.

Nothing stripped it and nothing tore it. It oxidised, in the exact sense: it met the condition, it reacted, and having reacted it stopped being able to hold surface tension against the thing it had been sitting on, and it slid. Mu-jin watched a working that had drunk an aurora slide off its bearer in sheets and go outward into the walls of a Domain, and the sliding had a play of colour on it at the trailing edge as the last of it went, and no assayer who has ever stood at a muffle would have failed to recognise the sight.

What remained on him after that was smaller and it was entirely his.

*There. That is the reading, and it is the first honest one anybody has taken off him in forty years. That is his own Ardency, at his own Depth, on his own Wellspring, without the district he has been drinking, and it is not small and it is not nothing and it would kill nine tenths of everybody breathing.*

*But it is not what he was thirty seconds ago, and he has never in forty years had to fight anybody with it.*

---

The walls of a crucible have to be porous and they have to be made of something that will not react with what they take.

Cupels are made of bone ash.

Mu-jin found that out at the same moment as everybody else, which is to say at the moment the absorbed material reached the boundary of his Domain and had to go somewhere, and the boundary of his Domain was not in the air.

It was in him.

Every long bone, and the Lattice through them. The femurs first because they are the largest reservoir, then the humerus on both sides including the dead one, then the pelvis and the ribs and the vault of the skull, and the sensation was not pain and had no relation to pain. It was fullness. It was the specific unbearable fullness of a vessel taking on more than it was fired to hold, and it arrived from the inside of the marrow outward, and it did not stop, because the charge was two hundred feet across and the walls were one man.

Bone ash is calcium phosphate, and calcium phosphate is chosen for a cupel because it is chemically inert to what it absorbs and porous enough to take it, and a man's skeleton is the same material in a different arrangement. The Lattice through it makes it the most absorbent structure in a body and always has, which is why every practitioner's Resilience is read off the bone and never off the flesh.

He held it open anyway.

*Nine tenths of an assay is the temperature. Too low and the litharge crusts and seals the bead in and you have learned nothing and destroyed the sample. Too high and you lose part of what you were trying to weigh.*

*I have no thermometer. I have never had one. I have the same instrument I have always had, which is that I can tell what a thing is worth by looking at it, and I have four seconds of usable judgement left in me.*

The Domain held.

And in the middle of it, on the cleared road, with the borrowed sheeting away into the bones of a schoolmaster and the ring of junctures still turning above them both, the last of the base content went off the bead.

The play of colours ran across it and finished.

Kwon Mu-jin took his hands apart, and dragged air into the one lung he had, and looked at what was standing in front of him with an eye that had gone entirely white from lid to lid.

**His has no wall.** Two hundred feet, and it excludes nothing. **Then it assays.** Cupellation is separation by differential affinity: bring the charge up together, supply one condition, and what reacts becomes fusible, drops its surface tension, and is drawn into the porous wall by capillary action, while what does not react stays on the surface and coalesces into a bead. Unchanged since the Bronze Age.

This is the payoff of the read Wren died buying and Mu-jin refined two scenes ago: *he was borrowing, and in this field he stopped borrowing.* The Domain takes the field away.

The hunk of steel left the ground without sound, no grunt of effort, no scrape of metal finding purchase, because nothing inside it needed purchase to begin with. It simply stopped being subject to the floor. Aether wrapped itself around the armor's frame in a slow, dense coil that had nothing in common with the Aether the field had carried all night. It behaved like appetite.

Dust near the plate stopped drifting and began falling sideways, in toward the breastplate's center, as though the air itself had developed a preference. A heartbeat later the preference sharpened. Grains of stone dust that should have taken a full second to settle instead vanished mid-fall, not burned, not scattered, simply absent, their matter unmade at the scale where matter stops being rock and starts being agreement between particles that have decided to remain particles. The construct's core did not glow. It only pulled, patient and total, a held breath the lungs have stopped asking to release.

It had no Domain. It had no Name.

That should have been a mercy and was not. A named thing could be read, argued with, cut along the grain of its own history. This had no grain. It was two ruined lives pressed into a single architecture by a hand that did not care whether the material cooperated, the seams never allowed to fully close, alloy being the kinder word for what should have stayed a wound. How many souls had it taken to build the first attempt. How many worlds Cozbi had emptied learning the ratio that finally held. The construct did not know the answer. It was not built with the part of a mind that keeps count.

It knew only two states. Contained, and not yet contained enough.

The armor crossed the courtyard's ruined width at a rate no eye trained on human motion should have been able to track, and yet it was not fast in the way a blade is fast. A blade earns speed through torque and hip rotation and the long discipline of a body learning to waste nothing. This earned nothing. It simply arrived, because between one instant and the next the material composing the space it needed to cross had agreed, under sufficient appetite, to stop existing there and exist somewhere closer to the target instead. The ground beneath its passage did not crater from impact. It came apart at the scale of atoms deciding they preferred to be elsewhere, a scorched, fine-grained wound left behind that would still be sterile a decade later, nothing green surviving in the print of it.

Heat arrived as an afterthought, born of compression rather than any flame, the specific heat of a thing crushed too fast to complain. It ran liquid down the construct's forearms, gathering at the wrist in beads too bright to look at directly. Where the beads touched the broken paving they did not scorch a mark. They erased one.

Matter answered the appetite's call the way iron answers a lodestone, and the construct closed its fist around what gathered there until the fist held something shaped like a spear only because a spear was the simplest shape violence had ever needed. The second followed a half-breath later, condensed out of the same converging debris, a black length with an edge that had never been ground, only agreed into existence already sharp.

The first spear crossed the remaining distance toward the space a body had occupied a heartbeat before, aimed with the flat, uncomplicated logic of something that understood centerlines without understanding why a centerline mattered to the thing it belonged to. It found only the memory of where a heart used to be, air still faintly warm from a presence that had already stopped standing in it.

The construct did not register the miss as failure. It had no register for failure. It simply completed the second half of what it had already begun.

The other spear went down instead of forward, driven into the shattered paving at an angle that turned the ground itself into an accomplice. Energy poured into the strike point and had nowhere honest to go, so it went up, a pillar of compressed light and grinding pressure erupting out of the courtyard floor in a column wide enough to swallow a doorway, meant not to kill outright but to close the only directions a body could have used to slide clear of the first attack. It was not a strike. It was a wall built out of the one half-second a slower creature would have needed to breathe.

The pillar held its shape for three full seconds before the ground beneath it gave up trying to remain ground, sinking inward at the edges in a slow, grinding collapse, stone folding on itself rather than shattering all at once.

The strike's failure earned no reaction. Whatever passed for the armor's interior held no capacity for triumph, and none for its opposite either. Somewhere underneath the black iron, past the runework and the borrowed heartbeat keeping the whole architecture from remembering it had once been two separate and terrified things, something that had been a person once made a sound that did not carry any words in it anymore, only the raw shape of having been asked to do this for longer than any single mind should have to survive being asked anything.

The pillar's collapse threw broken paving outward in a low, grinding wave, and the construct used it rather than waited for it to settle. Debris that would have crippled a slower body simply became more material to spend. A slab the size of a door lifted off the buckling ground and folded inward on itself midair, matter and its contradiction meeting at the exact center of the stone the way two hands meet to crush something held between them, and what remained was compressed into a third shape, blunt, low, and already moving before the first two attacks had finished failing. It did not swing the new weight overhead. It drove it forward at knee height, low enough that a body already committed to clearing the pillar's reach would have nowhere left above it to go.

The construct's faceless head did not track the miss the way a hunting animal tracks a scent it has lost. It had no scent to lose. It simply understood, the moment contact failed to register through whatever passed for its senses, that the exercise was not yet finished, and it began the next motion with the same total absence of urgency a falling rock brings to the last few feet of its fall.

The construct turned its faceless head toward the settling dust where its targets had stood a moment before, patient in the specific, empty way only something with no fear of its own ending could afford to be patient, and began, without hesitation, without hurry, and without a single thought behind the decision, to close the distance again.

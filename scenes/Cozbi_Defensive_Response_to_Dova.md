Cozbi felt the knot close a full second before Dova's fingers actually closed around it, and he tried, in that single stolen second, to make the lattice ungraspable.

He drove Antithesis into the Pneumosphera nearest the grip, forcing Matter and its contradiction to occupy the coordinating point at once, hoping the resulting collapse would fold the whole knot into something too unstable to hold. The filaments answered wrong. Instead of folding inward they spiraled off his spiritual forearms in tight, uneven zigzags, the reversion refusing to invert cleanly, and where the pattern should have closed into a blade it opened instead, tearing back through his own conduit before it ever reached Dova's palm. The half-formed scissor-shapes he'd meant to drive between the Dragon God's fingers broke apart against the purple-white flame already climbing Dova's wrist, unraveling into the same scattered light the rest of the lattice was already shedding.

Something in the failure told him exactly how badly the arithmetic had turned against him. A working of that class did not simply fail. It failed *specifically*, refusing him at the one moment refusal cost the most, and Cozbi understood, with the cold clarity of a man watching his own ledger audited in real time, that Dova's grip had not merely resisted the reversion. It had read the reversion's shape before Cozbi finished casting it, and simply declined to let the shape exist.

The knot came apart in Dova's hand a moment later, and every Pneumosphera fed by it lost its borrowed unity in the same breath. Cozbi felt each one go, not as pain, but as a hundred small accountings closing at once, ledger lines he had spent years keeping open now settled without his signature anywhere on the final page.

He straightened inside the wreck of his own architecture, black armor scale sloughing off him in the same slow, papery sheets it always shed when a working this large finally ran out of road, and found his voice before he found his composure.

"That was elegant." He said it too quickly, the compliment arriving half a beat ahead of anything steadier he might have reached for instead. "I'll grant you that much. Elegant, and considerably more expensive than you're currently showing me."

*He is not bluffing about the shred,* Cozbi thought, and the thought carried none of his usual patience, only the flat, unpracticed alarm of a man recalculating a fight he had already told himself he'd won. *I built this facade to survive Rescind. I did not build it to survive something that reads a working before I finish casting it.*

"You call it theft." His voice found more of its old footing, though not all of it, the words coming faster than his usual unhurried cadence allowed. "I call it correction. Every soul in this lattice was already going to die uselessly, in wars that never once asked their permission either. I only asked what they were dying for. You're welcome to disagree with my answer. You are not entitled to pretend the question was never worth asking."

He took one step back across the crater floor, and did not try to disguise that it was a retreat rather than a repositioning.

"Begin with the shred, then." He lifted both hands, black scale still smoking faintly at the knuckles where the failed reversion had cost him skin as well as architecture. "I've built worse things out of less than what you'll leave me."

Cozbi had turned his attention to his other shadows, the mirrors of himself seeded across the different veil connections that stitched the spiritual extensions of each realm together, and for close to two years the work produced nothing anyone above the surface would have called news. He preferred it that way. Silence was the sound of a plan that had not yet been interrupted.

The first thing to surface as truth was plague, though it was not a plague of flesh. It moved through the Wellsprings themselves, in the Inner World first, then the Old World, then reaching the furthest edges of the Outer World in threads too thin for most practitioners to notice until the drawing was already done. A Wellspring touched during those years did not sicken the way a body sickens. It soured. Essence drawn from an infected source came back wrong in the hand, curdled, resentful, as though something had used the spring before the practitioner arrived and left an opinion behind in the water. No priest, sage, or scholar of the Material Force could name what had gone through first. Cozbi had gone through first, and Cozbi did not leave a name where a question served him better.

He had spent that silence building something else entirely. Essence drafts, siphoned patiently from a hundred unwilling sources, folded and refolded until they stopped being drafts and became spiritual axes bent entirely around the shape of his will. He held one such axis now, wrapped in a body two years old, asleep in his arms in a nursery that had never once needed daylight.

The child had golden Vaeloris eyes set into a face carrying the soft, unmistakable beauty of the Mahuo line, an inheritance that should never have shared a bloodline and did so now only because Cozbi had insisted the two lines cooperate. He was the Paragon of the Archon of Form, though he did not know it yet, would not know it for years, might never know it at all depending on how the arithmetic resolved.

"Oh, my beautiful boy." Cozbi's projected thumb traced the child's temple, gentle in the specific way a man is gentle with something he intends to spend completely. "You don't yet understand how well your blood is being put to use. My precious son, after all, and the form of this world. Not merely its physical shape. Its spiritual one, which is where the true power has always lived. Control the invisible and you control the visible along with it. Control the invisible and you hold the leash on fate and destiny both."

The child did not stir. Cozbi had made certain of that months ago.

Amalgamation designation forty-five wobbled into the doorway on legs that had never learned to agree with one another, folding into its approximation of a bow before it dared speak. "M-Master. I have news to r-report. Approximately s-six more hours before the God Hand's re-emergence is expected. Th-thirty-two and thirty-six report the Dragon of Calamity is r-ready for release. Upon your word."

"Six hours." Cozbi said it the way another man might repeat a grocery list, without weight, because the number had already been accounted for in every plan built beneath it. "Then we have time enough to finish properly. Tell thirty-six the chains stay sealed until I arrive myself. I will not have my finest work loosed by anyone's hand but mine."

"Y-yes, Master." Forty-five did not move to leave. Its cobbled joints shifted, an approximation of hesitation borrowed from whichever salvaged body had once known how to feel it. "M-Master, if I m-may. Thirty-two asked wh-whether the boy would be... p-present for the release."

Cozbi's expression did not change, though something behind it settled, the particular stillness of a man deciding a question was beneath needing an answer at all. "The boy is two years old. He will be present for a great many things before he understands any of them. That is the privilege of raising him myself rather than leaving the raising to someone sentimental." He looked toward the crib, toward the small chest rising and falling in its patient rhythm. "Tell thirty-two his curiosity is noted and unnecessary. Go."

"Y-yes, Master." Forty-five withdrew, its retreat as unsteady as its arrival, though it did not look toward the crib on its way out. Something in its assembled face had learned, over the long practice of serving him, precisely which things were safer left unseen.

Cozbi laid the child back into the crib, unhurried, and drew a small silver needle from within his sleeve before he left, drawing a measure of the boy's blood into a vial no larger than a finger. The child's face did not so much as crease at the intrusion. "Mmh. There is one more thing I require, to secure loyalty absolute." He said it to the empty nursery, a man narrating his own competence to no one because the habit had long since outlived any need for an audience.

The chamber beneath the reliquary held the dragon, and the dragon announced Cozbi's arrival the way it announced everything, with a roar that came less from anger than from the accumulated grief of every soul pressed into its making. It was not one dragon's rage. It was the harvested hatred of an entire kind, dragonkind's darkest instincts and oldest grudges folded together into a single body large enough to remember all of them at once, chained to a floor built to hold something that should never have been assembled in the first place.

Cozbi approached the bars unhurried, patience worn as easily as everything else about him. "You are to be my loyal subject." His voice carried none of the warmth he had used on the child, only the flat patience of ownership. "I cannot afford your will to break from mine. You will be my ultimate pawn against all who oppose me, unmatched even by those immortal brats who think their bloodlines make them permanent. My perfect creation." He smiled, and the smile did not reach anything behind his eyes. "My magnum opus."

The dragon's chains tightened at the sound of his voice, iron singing under strain, and somewhere beneath the roar a second sound moved, quieter, less animal, something that might once have been a hundred separate voices asking to be released from a body that had stopped being any of theirs individually a long time ago.

Cozbi opened the vial and let the boy's blood fall onto the dragon's exposed foreleg, one drop, then three, working it into the scale with two fingers, patient and deliberate. The dragon convulsed, chains screaming against the stone, its roar climbing into something closer to a scream before dropping into silence entirely, its enormous head lowering by degrees until the great skull rested against the floor in an approximation of submission that had cost it something Cozbi did not care to name.

He drew his own blade next, a thin ceremonial edge kept for exactly this purpose, and opened a shallow line along the dragon's foreleg. He caught what came from the wound in a shallow dish, dark red gone nearly black at the edges, and did not hesitate before he drank it.

The reaction arrived immediately and without mercy. Cozbi's projected body doubled over the instant the blood crossed his throat, and he vomited violently onto the chamber floor, blood mixed with something darker than blood, his hand slamming against the stone to keep himself upright through the second and third wave that followed the first. The spiritual pressure behind the draught was not meant for a body, projected or otherwise, to absorb in a single swallow. So much hatred concentrated into so small a measure. So much power carried on the back of grief that had never been permitted to finish grieving. It overwhelmed him even as it fed him, pressing him through the threshold at level five hundred and one full step closer to whatever he was building toward beyond it.

Heat came off him in visible waves, his skin smoldering at the edges the way a coal smolders after the flame has already moved on, his own nature shifting to accommodate what he had just taken in, the Archon of Form's architecture folding itself around the dragon's blood until the two stopped behaving like separate things. Cozbi felt the bond settle into him and understood immediately what it was built from. Not affection. Not even respect. Hatred, pure and load-bearing, the only material the dragon had left to offer after everything else had been harvested out of it.

This did not mean the dragon's death would be his own. He had built the binding too carefully for that kind of carelessness. But should the dragon fall, every soul it had ever taken and every soul that died beneath its wings from this hour forward would funnel directly into him, harvested and delivered without any further ritual required. It was elegant in the specific way corruption always is when it has been engineered rather than merely committed. Complete and total dominion over the dead. Mastery over necromancy that no living practitioner before him had achieved at this scale.

Cozbi began to laugh, low at first and then without restraint, the sound climbing out of him at the same rate the power climbed inside him, until laughing was the only response his body had left that made any sense at all. He gave the order before the laugh had finished. Loose the beast.

The sixth hour struck as the chains fell away.

The skies over the mortal world dimmed as though something had reached up and pressed a thumb against every star at once, black bleeding outward from a point no one on the ground could name, and the spiritual worlds beyond the Veil felt the calamity arrive before the mortal ones ever saw its shape. The dragon rose from the horizon at a scale that made mountains look temporary, wings unfolding across a distance that swallowed whole provinces in shadow, and it began, without pause, without mercy, and without a single soul left inside it capable of choosing otherwise, to ravage the Inner World from the ground up.

Cozbi watched from the reliquary's high window and laughed again, quieter now, savoring rather than celebrating. He enjoyed every part of it. He always did.

At the same hour the world's clock struck dawn on the far side of the Veil, and dawn was the hour the God Hand had waited seven years to claim.

Ashura's voice arrived to Niran the way warmth arrives before a fire is visible, felt in the chest before it could be located anywhere in the surrounding dark. "Things have changed," she said, and there was no urgency in the saying, only the calm of someone who had watched the change coming from very far away and had simply waited for the world to catch up to what she already knew. "The world is stricken and it needs a Septarch. It needs a guide to cleanse what has just been let loose upon it. You must become that thing, Niran."

*I have not agreed to anything yet,* he thought, and the thought carried the weight of testimony rather than protest, the specific discipline of a man who had spent six years learning to examine a claim before he accepted or refused it. *You are telling me what the world needs. You have not yet told me what you need it for.*

"You always did require the second sentence before you trusted the first." Her voice held something close to fondness, the particular fondness of someone watching a habit they had predicted arrive exactly on schedule. "Very well. What I need is simple. A hand upon the living world that will not flinch from what it is asked to hold. Every Titan bargains for patience. Every Archon bargains for law. I am asking for something rarer. I am asking for someone who will feel the full weight of what has just been done to the Inner World and use that feeling as a blade rather than a wound."

*A hand can be lent,* he thought, *or it can be taken. You have not said which this is.*

"Lent," she said, and the single word arrived with a precision he recognized, the register of a promise being made rather than a demand being issued. "I do not sever what I claim, little crow. I do not need to. An arrow lent to the right bow does not resent the lending. It resents only the archer who never lifts it to the string."

*Then lift me,* he thought, and did not soften the thought into a request. *But understand that what returns through the Veil will not be only what you built. It will be what I choose to carry alongside it. You are welcome to the hand. You are not owed the whole of what moves it.*

Her laugh came warm and unbothered, the laugh of someone who had already accounted for exactly this condition and found it acceptable, perhaps even necessary. "That," she said, "is precisely why the arithmetic favored you over every other name the Plane considered."

The dark around him thinned by degrees, dawn light finding its way through the Veil's last resistance in long, patient fingers, and somewhere below the world he had not yet returned to a dragon the size of a mountain range was already teaching an entire continent what it meant to be small. Niran felt the shape of that devastation arrive in him before he had fully finished becoming whatever he was becoming, a weight settling into the same place his grief had always lived, and he did not turn away from it.

He opened his eyes into the returning light, and he began, without hesitation, to rise toward the world that was already burning.

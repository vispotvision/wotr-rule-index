Kurama's body rode the black flame the whole distance into the mountainside, and the mountain lost an entire face of rock to the impact, granite exploding outward in a spray of dust and molten scree that rained down across the bowl for a full ten seconds after the initial concussion had already passed. He came to rest half-buried in the rubble, the hole through his chest visible even at that distance, breath moving through him in shallow, ragged pulls that suggested his body had not yet decided whether it intended to keep going.

The dragon's vast shape began to fold inward on itself.

Scale by scale, the massive form collapsed downward into something the world could recognize as a body, muscle and bone and borrowed authority all compressing toward a single point until what stood in the crater where the dragon had been was a man's shape wearing the last of the creature's shed scales like a coat it hadn't finished discarding. The face beneath them was Cozbi's, patient and pleased, watching the six of them the way a teacher watches students who have just failed an exam he'd already known the results of before it was administered.

"This form is too large to be useful for a conversation," he said, mostly to himself, and started walking toward Kurama.

He crossed the rubble unhurried, the remaining draconic scale sloughing off his shoulders in dry, papery sheets with every step, until he reached the crater where Kurama lay and dropped into a low squat beside him. He took the young man's chin between two fingers, tilting his head up with something that might have passed for gentleness in a man who meant it.

"You could have been so much more." Cozbi's voice carried genuine, unhurried regret, the specific regret of a collector examining a piece he'd damaged through his own carelessness. "More purposeful. More complete. A shame. It really is a shame."

"SŌMA, DON'T!"

Kasei's warning arrived a half-second too late.

Sōma was already moving, rage riding him past every discipline the Danshi had ever taught him to hold, Ruin's black flame spreading out from his boots and eating through the crater floor toward Cozbi's borrowed feet in a scorched, widening line. He closed the distance in three strides, blade already reforming from the haft in his grip.

Cozbi turned at the last possible instant, and his eyes lit a deep, arterial red.

A spear of raw spiritual pressure lanced out from his open palm and caught Sōma clean through the forearm, the impact spinning him a full rotation before it threw him backward across the crater floor hard enough to bounce once on landing. He hit the rubble and did not immediately rise, the wound in his forearm smoking faintly where the spear's passage had cauterized as much as it had torn.

"You will all learn true fear before this ends." Cozbi's voice carried no heat behind the words, only patient certainty, the tone of a man stating a schedule rather than a threat. "True pain. It is, I promise you, a considerably better teacher than anything your Septarch has offered any of you so far."

Rui stared at him, and her knees gave out beneath her before she'd decided to kneel, the strength simply leaving her legs at once.

"No." Her voice came out thin, disbelieving. "It can't be. You've merged with the beast, but this isn't actually you. Weren't you sealed? By Dhaerin?"

Cozbi clapped his hands together once, delighted, the particular delight of a teacher whose slowest student has finally asked the one question worth answering fully.

"Yes." He said it warmly, without a trace of defensiveness. "Dhaerin did seal me. That much is entirely true, and I'll grant the boy his due, it was a genuinely elegant working for someone who died executing it. His Rescind stripped me of my authority over the spirits I'd claimed. Every soul I'd conscripted, every draft I'd bound into service, the world simply stopped agreeing that I had any right to hold them, and I felt every last one of them slip loose in the same instant. A masterful piece of Manifestus craft, honestly. I don't say that to flatter a corpse. I say it because it's true, and because understanding exactly what it did is the only way any of you will understand why it didn't finish the job."

He straightened out of his squat, leaving Kurama where he lay, and turned his full attention toward the four of them still standing.

"Rescind withdraws consent. It tells the Continuum that a claim once recognized is no longer recognized, and the claim simply stops being real, the way a debt stops being owed the instant the ledger recording it is declared never to have existed." He spread both hands, palms up, an educator's gesture. "But consent only matters where a claim requires it in the first place. I never claimed authority over this dragon as I claimed authority over harvested souls. I built the dragon's authority directly into its architecture, at the level of its own nature, the same register a Divine Beast's dominion occupies without ever needing anyone's permission to be true. Dhaerin's Rescind stripped the borrowed crown off my head. It never touched the throne I built underneath it, because the throne was never borrowed from anyone to begin with."

*He's not lying,* Kasei thought, the Zenshi confirming the shape of the explanation even as every part of her resisted accepting it. *The authority reads as native rather than conscripted. Dhaerin closed one door and left an entire wing of the house standing.*

"And fear." Cozbi's smile widened, something almost fond in it now. "Fear turns out to be a remarkably renewable material, once you understand it correctly. Not borrowed from any single soul's consent. Generated fresh, in real time, by every living thing that watches what's about to happen to the people it loves. What has more authority than a dragon wearing a god's own shed scales? Only one thing." He gestured, almost apologetic, toward the horizon where the fighting had already cost so much. "Death itself. And death, unlike the souls I once conscripted, was never anyone's to withhold consent for. The dead don't get a vote in whether they're dead. That's rather the entire point of the category."

His gaze drifted, briefly, toward the mountainside where Kurama's blood was still spreading across broken stone.

"The souls of the dead are being tortured even now, somewhere behind all of this, screaming in a register your Ketsumyōgan can't quite reach yet." He said it almost gently, the cruelty riding entirely on the calm of the delivery rather than anything harsh in the tone. "I find that a remarkably efficient use of a resource everyone else insists on wasting through burial."

He turned back toward Kurama, crouching once more beside him, and rested one hand flat against the young man's ruined chest.

"Let's see how much fear the rest of you still have left to spend."

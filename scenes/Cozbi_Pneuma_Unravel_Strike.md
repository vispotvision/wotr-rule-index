"Do you want applause for your ferocity?" Cozbi's voice carried across the ruined crater, unhurried, amused. "You should be thanking me instead. Every soul screaming in this architecture screamed first because I taught the world what a dragon's death was actually worth." He glanced once toward Kurama, still folded around his own ruined chest, and something almost fond moved through the cruelty. "Though I confess, I will delight in meeting the mighty Dova'Kan properly. It's a shame he'll be utterly defeated for the trouble of coming. Your kind are nothing more than animals, livestock I put to better use than any of you managed on your own. What will you do, flex your superiority over a single stolen shred of essence?"

He looked away from Kurama entirely, attention drifting toward some point beyond the crater's edge that only he seemed able to read yet.

"It's unfortunate that Xhem involves himself in my business." The regret in his voice was genuine, and worse for being genuine. "Little does he know the seeds of his family's demise were planted long before tonight. He's far too occupied saving the world to notice he's saving what little of it will be left."

Filaments of raw essence began threading up through the soles of his feet, branching outward across the scorched crater floor in slow, deliberate patterns, and where they spread, black draconic scale began to knit itself into armor across his frame, plate rising over plate until the shape of him had stopped resembling anything mortal at all.

"It's time," he said, "that I spend what little sanity I have left keeping."

He laughed, and the laugh carried an edge underneath its delight that had not been there earlier in the night, the specific edge of a man who had just felt, for the first time in longer than he cared to measure, something worth calling afraid. Strong. Whatever was closing on this field was strong, clearly, undeniably strong, one of the old Kan bloodline itself, and some ruined, ancient part of Cozbi's architecture recognized the shape of that strength the way a smaller predator recognizes a larger one crossing into its territory.

*I have not been afraid of anything in three hundred years,* Cozbi thought, and the thought arrived almost delighted despite itself, delighted the way a starving man might be delighted by the smell of a meal he was not certain he would survive eating. *I find I have missed it.*

This would be legendary. He intended to make sure it was legendary on his terms rather than anyone else's.

He lifted one hand.

"Infinite Summoning Arts." The words left him with ceremony this time, none of his usual economy. "I call upon the arms of the six old gods. Become my pillars, the stations at which I am anchored. Let this vessel become the scepter through which they act."

His form writhed as he spoke it, and four spiritual arms tore outward from his waist in a single convulsive motion, each one sheathed in the same black armored scale climbing the rest of him, joints articulating with a wrongness no natural anatomy should have accommodated.

"Conjunction. Pneuma."

Massive orbs of Pneumosphera bloomed into being around him, each one a compressed sphere of animating breath and echo-signature drawn not from a single soul but from the whole gathered chorus still bound inside his architecture, interconnecting through threads of visible light until the six spheres and the four new arms and Cozbi's own borrowed frame all reported back to a single coordinating point. This was the working the highest schools of Animatria called, when performed cleanly, the First Breath. A scaffold beneath true Eidolon invocation, a soul-forged extension of the caster meant to assist rather than to consume.

What Cozbi had built was its hollow mirror.

Each soul feeding the lattice was not a Pneuma in any sense the discipline's founders would have recognized, not a compressed node of the caster's own spiritual function, but a conscripted echo forced to perform the role a true Pneuma filled willingly. The feedback ran not to the body standing in the crater but past it, threading back across planes to the true Cozbi seated among candles that never guttered, feeding a Pneumata Lattice built to sync a hundred stolen breath-signatures to a single borrowed pulse rather than one soul's own.

And Rescind, for all its severing precision, had never touched a single thread of it.

Dhaerin's working had withdrawn consent from claims of dominion, the specific metaphysical architecture by which one soul asserts ownership over another and expects the Continuum to recognize the assertion as law. A Pneuma was never that. A Pneuma was structurally treated as an extension of the caster's own being rather than an external claim requiring anyone's recognition, and Cozbi had built his lattice inside that same loophole, dressing conscription in the architecture of extension so that Rescind found nothing to withdraw consent from. There had never been a claim here for the Continuum to un-recognize. Only a body wearing the shape of one.

"Unravel."

The word left him quiet now, all ceremony spent, the single syllable carrying the full weight of a forbidden Mnemata discipline meant for dismantling memory rather than flesh, the peeling back of emotional resonance and cognitive patterning and Wellspring-memory binding until whatever truth lived beneath a soul's surface fractured under direct exposure. At its gentlest, the technique could liberate a mind from a trauma it could not otherwise escape. At its most violent, it was soul-flaying, an act that did not merely kill but erased, unmaking identity down to the studs before it bothered unmaking the body wrapped around it.

Cozbi fed the technique every trauma his stolen chorus carried, every death, every scream, every fragment of fear the false dragon had spent the night harvesting from the field around it, and pointed the compounded weight of all of it toward the presence he could feel gathering somewhere past the crater's edge, vast and cold and already refusing to be ignored.

"Disintegrate everything before me." His voice climbed, the manic edge finally surfacing through the composure he'd worn all night. "Liberate its physical form. Shred its spiritual form. Let it become nothing more than flayed meat cooling on a spit."

Reality answered the command the way it answered nothing else Cozbi had cast tonight, incisions opening directly into the fabric of the world itself, thin fracturing seams racing outward from his outstretched hand, each one carrying the compressed memory of every trauma and death and terror the false dragon had ever inflicted, aimed at whatever waited beyond the crater's ruined edge with the full unraveling weight of a hundred stolen lives behind it.

Above the crater, far beyond the ash and the fractured mountainside, the sky had already begun refusing to remain neutral.

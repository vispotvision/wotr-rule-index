Cozbi sat astride the Great Spider Impharous the way another man might sit a favorite chair, one leg draped loose over the joint of a foreleg thick as a ship's mast, watching the creature work its mandibles through the ribcage of a deer it had killed sometime before dawn. Blood had dried black along the white fur ringing its mouthparts. The carcass gave under each bite with a wet, splintering sound that carried across the clearing louder than it had any right to, and Cozbi listened to it the way another man might listen to rain against a window, present, unbothered, faintly pleased by the rhythm of it.

He rubbed his chin with two fingers, unhurried, and looked toward the treeline a full breath before either man stepped out of it.

"It seems Rengai has finally decided to make his move." He said it to the spider as much as to the clearing, running his palm through the coarse white fur along Impharous's flank, and the great creature's mandibles paused mid-bite, sensing something in its master's attention shift. "It'll be interesting, fighting the son of Dougou. I've heard a great deal about the Iron Bison's boy. I confess I'm curious whether the ash falls as far from the tree as everyone claims."

Dhaerin and Rengai had walked for months to reach this clearing, through three mountain passes and one stretch of coastline that had taken as long to cross as the mountains combined, and neither man's boots had fully dried from the last river crossing before they stood at the tree line looking at exactly what they'd come to find.

Cozbi leaned up off the spider's back, unhurried, black hair sliding down over one shoulder in a single dark sheet, and rubbed his chin again, studying the two of them with the specific patience of a man who had already decided how this conversation would end and was only curious about the route it would take getting there.

"So I take it you two are here to kill me."

Rengai did not answer immediately. His ember-red eye moved across Impharous's frame with the flat, cataloguing attention of a man reading terrain before a fight rather than a man making conversation, marking the soft joints behind each foreleg, the pale, unarmored underside where the fur thinned to nothing, the place along the abdomen where a killing blow would need to land if the fight came down to the mount rather than the rider. He rubbed his own chin, an unconscious mirror of the gesture Cozbi had just made, and when he finally spoke his voice carried none of the heat Dhaerin had expected to hear in it.

"I want to understand why you did what you did first." Rengai's jaw worked once around the words before he let them out clean. "What the purpose was. You took a child and turned him into something he can't ever walk back from, and you did it, from where I'm standing, because it entertained you."

Cozbi laughed, low and genuinely delighted, the sound rolling out of him with none of the performance most men used when they wanted a laugh to land as a threat.

"Do I need a reason." He said it flat, not quite a question, and let the silence after it stretch long enough to become its own kind of answer. "Your people never needed one to slaughter mine. The Mahou were livestock to the Zettari and the Moto both, ground into the mud between two armies too busy hating each other to notice they agreed on one thing completely. Nobody asked why then. Nobody demanded my family's suffering explain itself before it was allowed to happen." His grin widened, and something old and unamused sat underneath it. "But look at me, doing the same arithmetic your ancestors invented, and suddenly everyone wants a reason. That's the whole of it, isn't it. It's only wrong when it's done to you. That's not philosophy. That's just the difference between the weak and the strong, dressed up as something more complicated than it is."

*He believes every word of that,* Rengai thought, and the thought arrived cold rather than hot, colder than the anger he'd been carrying through three mountain passes to get here. *That's what makes him worse than a monster. A monster doesn't need to be right. He does.*

Dhaerin had said nothing yet, his attention moving across Impharous's frame in a different register than Rengai's, reading the creature the way he read everything, as a construct built from a formula rather than a body built from meat and instinct. The spider's Essence signature ran too clean along its joints, too evenly distributed to be a natural predator's musculature, threads of Cozbi's own architecture woven through muscle and chitin both, a mount grown rather than tamed.

"You keep saying strong," Dhaerin said, his voice level, almost conversational, the specific tone of a scholar correcting an error in someone else's proof rather than a man addressing his own executioner. "But strength that requires borrowing eleven other souls just to stay upright isn't strength. It's leverage wearing strength's clothing. I've read your architecture from here, Cozbi. That mount beneath you isn't yours. It's assembled. Every piece of what you're calling power tonight was taken from someone who didn't consent to the taking, which means the word you actually want isn't strong." His eyes, deep green with their faint liquid-ink sheen, held steady on Cozbi's face. "It's parasite. I only mention it because you seem like a man who prides himself on precision, and precision matters to me too."

Something in Cozbi's expression flattened for exactly one breath, the grin holding its shape while whatever sat behind it went very still.

"Clever," Cozbi said. "A Valorin's the last person I expected to lecture me about borrowed strength, given what your own bloodline does to the ink running through your veins." He let that land, watching to see if it would, and when Dhaerin's expression didn't change he shrugged, unbothered, and turned his attention back to Rengai as though the exchange had cost him nothing. "But he's not wrong that I take what I need. I've never once pretended otherwise. The difference between us, ash-son, is that I don't dress the taking up as destiny or duty or the will of some ancestor who conveniently isn't here to disagree with me. I take because taking works. Everything else is decoration men add so they can sleep afterward."

"You made a two-year-old bleed for it," Rengai said, and this time the heat did show, banked but visible, ash lifting faintly off his shoulders in the cooling evening air. "You bound a dragon's hatred into your own blood and called it necromancy's mastery, and somewhere in that whole ledger of yours you decided a child's veins were an acceptable line item."

"He'll never remember the needle." Cozbi's voice stayed light, almost gentle, which made it worse than shouting would have. "He'll grow up golden-eyed and beautiful and powerful beyond anything either of you will ever touch, and one day he'll thank me for the shape I gave him, the same way every bloodline eventually thanks the ancestor who did the ugly work required to make the descendants comfortable. You're not angry that I used him. You're angry that I was honest about using him, while your own families dressed the exact same arithmetic up in banners and called it glorious."

*They always arrive believing the conversation is the point,* Cozbi thought, watching Rengai's hand drift toward the flask at his hip with something almost like fondness. *As though words could unmake a ledger already closed. I will let him have his speech. Grief deserves a hearing before it deserves an answer, and I am, whatever else they will say of me tonight, a patient host.*

*He almost makes it sound reasonable,* Dhaerin thought, cataloguing the shape of the argument the way he catalogued everything, filing it for later even as some quieter part of him recoiled from how cleanly it filed. *That's the danger of him. Not the power. The grammar. He's built a sentence structure evil can live inside comfortably, and comfortable evil spreads further than the desperate kind ever does.*

Rengai's hand drifted toward the flask at his hip, and Impharous's mandibles stopped their slow work on the deer carcass entirely, eight eyes swiveling in unison toward the ash-red glow building faintly along Rengai's knuckles.

"I didn't come here for a debate about grammar," Rengai said. "I came here because you burned a nation of children I never met and you're about to tell me it was philosophy. I don't have the patience left in me to hear the rest of the lecture."

"No patience for the lecture, and yet you still corrected my grammar about your father's name before you said a single word about the dead." Cozbi tilted his head, something almost gentle in the observation. "I wasn't alive when the Mahou were ground into the mud, Rengai. Neither were you, when the Zettari and the Moto were doing the grinding. Strange, isn't it, how quickly a man decides an old war belongs to somebody else's guilt the moment his own family sat on the winning side of it. You didn't choose Dougou's wars. I didn't choose what was done to my people either. The difference is only that I decided the debt was still owed, and you decided the debt expired the day your family stopped needing to pay it."

Rengai's jaw tightened, and for a moment the retort sat visibly behind his teeth before he let it go unspoken, because there was a grain of truth buried inside the cruelty of it and he had never once been a man who could pretend not to see truth once it was pointed at him directly.

"That doesn't make what you did to a two-year-old anything other than what it was," he said instead, quieter now, the fire in it banked rather than doused.

"No," Cozbi agreed, easily, without a trace of defensiveness. "It doesn't. I never claimed it did. I only claimed it made sense, which is a different thing entirely from claiming it was clean."

Cozbi's grin held, easy, unbothered, and he swung one leg back over Impharous's flank with the unhurried grace of a man settling into a saddle he had ridden a thousand times before.

"Then let's stop lecturing," he said, "and find out whether the ash falls as far from the tree as everyone claims."

Impharous rose onto all eight legs at once, the deer carcass falling from beneath its foreclaws in a wet, final heap, and the ground beneath its bulk groaned under a weight built from more than muscle and chitin alone.

Rengai uncapped the flask at his hip in one motion, ash spilling free and hanging suspended around his fist rather than falling, and the ember in his red eye brightened until it threw a dull, coal-warm light across the churned grass beneath him.

Dhaerin's hand moved to the grimoire at his belt without ceremony, thumb already finding the worn edge of its first page, the fine gold script along his throat beginning to surface and glow faintly in the gathering dark.

Neither man spoke again. There was nothing left in the conversation worth finishing before the rest of it was settled with something other than words.

Cozbi hit the ground hard enough to plow a furrow through the churned earth before he stopped moving, the Fixatio-anchored seal Dhaerin's dying working had written into his vessel constricting the instant he reached for the harvested spirits still nominally bound to him. The reach itself was reflex, muscle memory older than the fight, and the seal answered it the way a snare answers a struggling limb, tightening precisely in proportion to the pull rather than resisting it outright. He felt three separate conscripted souls slip from his grasp entirely before the constriction eased back to its resting pressure, gone the instant he'd tried to claim them, the covenant the Continuum now carried refusing, flatly, to recognize his hand on them anymore.

Blood ran freely from his mouth, more of it than the fight itself should have cost him. He lay still a moment longer than pride wanted to allow, then turned his head and looked up past the ruined treeline to the moon, indifferent and unmarked by any of what had just happened beneath it.

"I allowed myself to become too confident." The words came out thick, blood coating his tongue with every syllable. "I won't make that mistake again."

He coughed, doubling slightly where he lay, more blood spattering the scorched grass beside him. His cloak had burned through in a dozen places, and what remained of his clothing beneath it had fared no better, singed black along every seam the Titan's own Dominion Strike had touched earlier in the night. He tried to push himself upright and made it only to one forearm before the effort cost him more than it was worth, his whole body still answering to a seal that treated every gathering of Essence, however small, as another attempted theft to be punished.

A construct arrived without footsteps, translocated directly into the clearing in a soundless fold of space, and it took Cozbi a full second to place the wobbling, uneven gait as Amalgamation designation forty-five.

"M-master." The stutter came out worse than usual, urgency fraying the creature's already unsteady voice further. "I-instability. I c-come to assist you. We m-must not wait."

Cozbi looked at the thing he'd built, and something almost like fondness moved behind the blood still running down his chin. "Collect the fallen Zettari. We're leaving."

"A-are you certain, Master." Forty-five hesitated, joints shifting in their approximation of concern. "Your current vessel shows n-no signs of controlled Essence. The seal reads s-strong. Perhaps we should n-not move you until-"

"It doesn't matter." Cozbi's voice stayed flat even through the blood, even through the effort it plainly cost him to keep it level. "I never intended to end this particular facade using strength that wasn't my own." He coughed again, longer this time, and did not try to hide how much it took out of him. "Let us return to our base of operations."

*He believes the seal humbled me,* Cozbi thought, watching forty-five wobble toward Rengai's unmoving shape at the edge of the clearing. *Perhaps it did. I will decide later whether humbled and instructed are the same lesson wearing different names.*

Forty-five gathered Rengai's slack weight across its uneven shoulders with more care than its trembling frame suggested it was capable of, and did not ask a second time whether its master intended to be moved.

*The instability is worse than he is willing to name in front of me,* the creature thought, feeling its own cobbled joints strain under a burden they had never been built to carry alongside a wounded god. *But he built me to obey rather than to argue, and tonight is not the night either of us discovers what happens if I choose differently.*

The space around them folded inward at Cozbi's last remaining effort, translocation drawing both master and captive out of the ruined clearing in a single soundless collapse, and where they had been standing only scorched grass, cooling ash, and the moon's indifferent light remained.

If he had to die here, he decided, then he would at least choose the manner of it.

The thought arrived quiet, without drama, exactly as his truest decisions always had, and underneath it came Xhem's face unbidden, the boy he had raised into a man capable of running an entire academy without needing his uncle's hand on the reins anymore. Would he be fine without him. A lifetime spent in the margins of other people's grimoires, a lifetime committed entirely to the education of a discipline most men only ever borrowed for convenience. Had he been mentor enough. Had he been person enough, underneath the scholar. Would anyone remember the shape of what he'd actually done, or only the footnote of how he'd finally stopped doing it. He understood, distantly, that these were likely the exact questions his brother had carried in the last clear moment before his own death, and the understanding did not comfort him so much as settle him, a door already walked through once making the walking of it himself feel slightly less unbearable now.

He felt nothing and everything in the same breath, a wave of feeling with no clean edge to it, grief and resolve and something almost like relief all arriving through the same channel at once.

Dhaerin stood.

His white hair had come loose from its tie somewhere in the last exchange and hung matted with his own blood down one side of his face, and he did not bother pushing it back. There was no one left in this clearing whose opinion of his appearance mattered more than what he was about to do.

He understood, with the specific clarity of a scholar finally reaching for the one working he had spent a lifetime hoping never to need, exactly what shape this rite would have to take. Not Rescind alone. Rescind unsupported was a blade with no handle, dangerous to caster and target both, forbidden in most circles precisely because it treaded so close to ontological collapse when performed without a frame strong enough to hold its own withdrawal steady. It needed a Sacrament around it, a Saishiki-grade covenant the Continuum itself would witness and remember, binding the working into something the spiritual hierarchy could recognize as lawful rather than as the raw paradox Rescind became when left naked. And a Sacrament of this magnitude, performed under this little Temperance and this much blood already spent, demanded a cost proportional to its ambition. It demanded Oblation. One-directional. No promise of return.

"Hoc sacramentum meum ultimum est."

He spoke the frame first, since every Saishiki rite required establishing the covenant before the cost or the command could be layered inside it. His voice did not shake. Whatever fear remained in him had already been spent on lesser things tonight. This is my final Sacrament, the words meant, and meant nothing less than that.

"Aufer me. Immola me. Fac me oblationem uni potestati, uni veritati."

Take me. Sacrifice me. Make me the offering to one power, one truth. The Parun glyphs surfaced along his forearms unbidden, Lo for devotion, Ma for the structure the covenant would need to hold its shape, Ra for the binding ascent that would carry the working past his own dying body and into something the Continuum could not simply forget once he was gone. Beneath them, layered into the same script, Ka surfaced too, destiny offered rather than seized, the glyph every Oblation required to mark the exchange as freely given rather than extracted.

"Oblatio."

His own Essence began to leave him in earnest now, not stolen as Cozbi stole, not conscripted, but given, poured out through the Ritus circle his blood had already traced into the churned earth beneath his knees without his noticing he'd been tracing it. This was the cost. Vitality, memory, years he would not get to spend, offered freely and without guarantee, because Oblation had never once promised the offering would be honored with anything in return. Some rites asked. This one only gave, and trusted the giving to matter regardless of whether anything answered it.

"Rescindo."

The final glyphs completed the working, Fi for the withdrawal at its center, Th for the liminal edge between what had been sanctioned and what was about to be unmade, Ur pressed hard against its own inverse, null origin invoked to strip the borrowed authority Cozbi had built his whole architecture upon back to the moment before it had ever been granted. This was not destruction. Rescind did not break what it touched the way Nullity broke things or Oathrend shattered them. It withdrew consent. It told the gathered, harvested, conscripted souls bound into Cozbi's borrowed dominion that the world no longer agreed to recognize the claim he'd made over them, and a claim the world stopped recognizing had never, in the strictest metaphysical sense, been real at all.

The Sacrament closed around both workings at once, sealing them into a single covenant event exactly as the rite demanded, Fixatio anchoring the seal against drift, Benediction lending the working the shape of blessing rather than raw violence, Conjunction binding caster and cost and target into one witnessed truth the Continuum would carry forward whether Dhaerin remained alive to see it carried or not.

Pressure answered from every direction at once, the specific pressure of something ancient and legal recognizing that a law had just been rewritten inside its own jurisdiction. The ground beneath Dhaerin's knees split outward in a perfect ring. The moonlight overhead seemed, for one impossible instant, to dim in acknowledgment rather than obstruction.

Across the clearing, Cozbi's borrowed architecture convulsed.

Every soul he had ever harvested, ever conscripted, ever forced into service beneath his claimed dominion, felt the world's consent withdraw from beneath the claim all at once, and the authority he had spent decades assembling did not shatter so much as simply stop being true, the way a debt stops being owed the instant the ledger recording it is declared to have never existed. He was not killed. Dhaerin had never intended that, could not have managed it even at full strength, and some clear, scholarly part of him even now insisted on the distinction. He had only made Cozbi, for the length of this working and however long after the seal held, exactly as strong as the truth of his own soul and nothing borrowed besides.

Dhaerin's own heart, asked to carry a working this size on Essence he had already spent past the last safe measure of it, faltered once, then again, then did not resume its rhythm cleanly.

*Tell Xhem I finished the one working I always told him I'd never attempt,* he thought, the thought arriving slower now, already half out of reach of the body it belonged to. *Tell him I did it anyway, and that anyway was always going to be the whole of who I was.*

He fell forward into the ash and the blood and the churned, ruined earth, and did not move again.

Niran woke to glyphs.

They hung suspended in the still air above the cot, rotating slowly through pale gold rings, each one reading some fragment of his condition back into itself, an instrument built to measure bodies in Aether rather than pulse. The room smelled of cut limestone and something faintly medicinal underneath it, resonance stones set into the walls at even intervals, their low hum the only sound besides his own breathing.

He pushed himself upright before his body had finished agreeing to the motion, and the dizziness hit him a half-second later, the whole room tilting sideways around a fixed point that refused to stay fixed. He caught himself on one arm, breathing through it, the Cymorath behind his eyes mapping the vertigo with the same flat, unasked-for precision it mapped everything else now.

Mira was sitting beside the cot.

Seven years had put height and a settled kind of strength into her that hadn't been there the last time he'd seen her, but she'd kept her hair cut the same length it had always been, just past her jaw, just the way he'd once told her he liked it best, and something in him recognized that single unchanged detail before it recognized anything else in the room.

She had his hand before he'd finished blinking the room into focus, both of hers wrapped around his, gripping hard enough that he felt the tremor running through her fingers.

The Septarch surfaced behind his eyes for one unbidden moment, cataloguing pulse and posture and the specific chemical signature of grief still fresh enough to register as recent rather than old.

*Not her,* Niran thought, and pushed the reading back down before it could finish. *Whatever I've become since the Veil, it doesn't get to read Mira like a battlefield.*

"Where am I?"

Mira started crying before he'd finished the sentence, the tears arriving fast and without permission, her composure breaking open all at once, held in careful reserve for exactly this possibility until the possibility had finally, actually, arrived.

"You're in a hidden room underneath the Vaeloris village." Her voice caught and she pushed through it anyway, the same direct, unguarded cadence he remembered, grief not softening the precision so much as running underneath it now. "I didn't think I'd get to see you again. I thought you were gone."

Niran looked at her properly then, past the tears themselves and into the shape of what had put them there, the specific exhausted grief of someone who had spent seven years carrying an absence and had only just now set it down.

"I told you I'd return." He reached his free hand toward her cheek, thumb finding the track of a tear and wiping it away with a care his still-unsteady arm barely managed to hold. "Why are you crying?" A pause, and something in his chest ached at the question even as he asked it, because he already understood the answer better than the question let on. "Our dream isn't finished. You still believe in me, don't you?"

"I never stopped." She said it fast, almost fierce, both hands tightening around his. "Not for one day in seven years. Not when the elders said you'd died in the Old World. Not when there was nothing left of you to hold onto except what I remembered." She pressed his palm briefly against her own cheek before letting it go. "I believed in you when believing in you was the only thing keeping me upright some mornings."

Niran let the words settle into him, the Cymorath quiet for once, no reading required to understand grief spoken this plainly.

"There's more," Mira said, and her voice changed register, the fierceness folding down into something steadier, more deliberate, the tone of someone choosing to deliver a wound cleanly rather than let it fester unspoken.

*He is going to try to stand the moment I finish this sentence,* she thought, watching the color already returning to his face despite everything his body had clearly been through. *I cannot stop that. I can only make sure he hears all of it before his legs decide the conversation is over.*

"You need to know before you're fully on your feet, because you're going to want to move the moment you hear it, and I need you to hear all of it first."

Niran's grip on her hand tightened, some old instinct reading the shape of what was coming before the words arrived to confirm it. "Tell me."

"Dhaerin Valorin is dead." She said it without softening it, the way she'd always said the things that mattered, direct because dressing it in gentler language would only have made the truth harder to trust once it finally landed. "He performed a rite against Cozbi. A Sacrament, layered with an Oblation and a Rescind, from what Xhem's been able to reconstruct. It stripped Cozbi of the spiritual authority he'd built from stolen souls. It cost Dhaerin everything he had to give."

Niran's jaw tightened. He said nothing yet, and Mira did not fill the silence with comfort she didn't have to offer.

"Rengai has been captured." Her voice held steady through this part too, though her grip on his hand betrayed the effort it cost her. "Cozbi took him when he retreated. We don't know where. We don't know what condition he's in. Xhem has people searching the Thread-field for any trace of him, but the seal Dhaerin's rite left on Cozbi is disrupting more than just his own architecture. It's disrupting everything nearby trying to track him."

Niran sat with that for a long moment, the weight of it settling into the same place his grief for Reimei had always lived, an old room in him that apparently still had space to hold more.

"Lyria," he said finally, quiet. "Asante."

"They know." Mira's voice softened at that, the first real gentleness she'd allowed herself since the news had started. "Xhem told them himself. Lyria's holding together better than anyone has a right to expect. Asante doesn't fully understand yet, and I think that's the only mercy in any of this."

Niran closed his eyes for a moment, and when he opened them again something in his posture had changed, the dizziness still present but no longer the only thing occupying his attention. He swung his legs over the edge of the cot, testing the floor beneath his bare feet before committing his full weight to it.

"Niran." Mira's hand found his shoulder, not pulling him back, just steadying. "You collapsed a Titan's borrowed authority field on your first hour back in a body. You are not walking out of this room today."

"I'm not asking permission to walk out today." He said it without heat, the same flat certainty she remembered from before, the Septarch's weight sitting just beneath the words without fully surfacing this time. "I'm telling you that whatever's coming next, I intend to be standing for it."

He planted both feet against the cold stone floor and pushed himself upright, swaying once before his balance caught and held, and did not sit back down.

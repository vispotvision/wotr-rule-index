Rengai stood outside the containment room with his hands closed into fists at his sides, close enough to the door that the wards along its frame registered his presence and far enough that he hadn't yet decided whether he trusted himself not to put a fist through it.

Niran was alive in there. Alive, and wrong in ways Rengai's ember-red eye read before the rest of him caught up to the reading, the Cinerion residue along his knuckles smoking faintly with a heat that had nothing to do with the corridor's temperature.

Cozbi. All of it, every last thread of it, led back to that name, and Rengai's jaw worked once, hard, around the shape of his own anger before he let it settle into something colder and more useful. He knew enough about the Kurotana to know what it demanded of the man who used it. A technique like that didn't borrow from the body. It borrowed from the mind wearing the body, and it always left something missing in the trade, some piece of the practitioner's own architecture spent and never quite refunded. What lay behind that door wasn't the boy who'd left for the Yukari Territory seven years ago. It was a vessel fate had forged fresh and pushed through the Veil, wearing that boy's face because the boy's face was the only shape left worth wearing.

Rengai could not stand here and watch the Inner World burn while Cozbi finished whatever this was. Watching had never been a skill he'd cultivated. He turned from the door before he'd fully decided to and went to find Xhem.

He found the old warden in the corridor beyond the outer wards, watching the last of his acolytes withdraw from the containment field at a measured, unhurried pace that did nothing to slow the burn still sitting under Rengai's sternum.

"So what do you make of all this." Rengai didn't phrase it as a question so much as set it down between them, a weight he needed someone else to help him carry. "The Kurotana's clearly manifested. Anyone with eyes can see that much. But there's something else sitting underneath him that isn't the technique. He's got senses now he never carried before, as though something handed him a set of eyes the rest of us don't get issued." He rubbed at the ash along his knuckles, unconsciously, a habit from every thought that was still sharpening itself into words. "He rushed the process. Anyone standing in this hallway can feel that much in the air alone. And if he rushed it because of what Cozbi's doing out there, that means Cozbi already knew what the rushing would cost him. Knew it before he ever lit the first fire."

Rengai looked past Xhem toward the sealed door, and something in his expression went very still, the specific stillness of a large animal deciding it had finished being patient.

"I'm not asking you for permission." He said it without heat, which made it land harder than if he'd shouted it. "I'm telling you where I'm going next. Cozbi doesn't get to burn half the Inner World and sit somewhere safe waiting for the ash to settle. I intend to make sure he doesn't get that luxury."

He turned from the warden and started down the corridor, ash lifting off his shoulders in a thin grey wake that dissolved before it reached the floor.

Niran was underwater, though the water was not water. It moved the way water moved and held the temperature water held, but every current inside it carried the specific pressure of a soul being taken apart one intention at a time and reassembled into something that answered to somebody else's name. He could hear them. Not words. The register beneath words, the raw shape of being used, stripped down past the point where a person could still call the screaming their own.

Ashura's voice reached him first, the way it always had, warm before it was anything else. *Save them,* it said, and the words moved through the water like heat moves through still air, rising rather than traveling.

But it was not her voice. He understood that even asleep, even drowning in whatever this was. Ashura had gone silent the moment he crossed back through the Veil, her voice replaced by something that lived inside his own architecture now, closer than a voice had any right to be, the Septarch that had always been the last piece waiting under every other piece he'd been given. It reached for a face he would recognize before it spoke again, and the face it chose was Reimei's.

She wrapped her arms around him in the dark water, and it did not feel borrowed. It felt like the thread extending exactly as far as it had ever extended, no further and no less. Her hand found his cheek, her palm warm against skin that had no business being warm this far under.

"You rushed the process getting here." Her voice held no accusation in it, only the specific ache of someone watching something they loved become larger than the shape they'd first loved it in. "You returned from death. And all you can think to worry about is saving people who will never know your name."

*I don't need them to know it,* he thought, or tried to, the thought arriving distorted by the water pressing in from every direction. *I only need them to still be alive when I'm done.*

"I know." Her thumb moved along his jaw, slow, the same unhurried care she'd always given him when the black spots first started forming and neither of them had a name for what was happening yet. "That's the whole of you in one sentence. It always was."

The water around them thickened, the screaming beneath it rising by a degree, and Niran felt his own hands close into fists at his sides without his permission, some part of him already fighting a fight the rest of him hadn't woken up enough to reach.

"Not yet," Reimei said, and her arms tightened around him, holding him still against the current trying to pull him toward the noise. "Your body hasn't finished remembering how to carry what you are. Rest first. The saving will still be there when you're strong enough to do it without tearing yourself apart to reach it."

Niran did not answer her, not with words, but his hands unclenched by degrees, one finger at a time, and beneath the water, in the dark, unconscious under the weight of the mage academy above him, he let the current hold him a while longer before it let him go.

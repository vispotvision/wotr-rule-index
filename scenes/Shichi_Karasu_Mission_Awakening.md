Kasei reported to Niran the way she reported everything, without preamble, because preamble wasted a thread that could be spent on the information itself. She found him sitting upright in the bed, one arm braced behind Mira's shoulders, holding her as a man holds something he had spent seven years afraid he would never get to hold again.

He had work to do. The fact that his legs still refused full weight changed nothing about that.

"Kasei." He said her name with full attention behind it, no economy of address the way there was economy in everything else about him. "I want you and the other six to hunt down and kill the Black Dragon. Coordinate together. But take this first."

He reached out his hand.

The Kurotana behind his eyes surfaced fully for the first time since his return, thread-patterns rotating past the range any of the Seven had seen from him before the Veil, and he reached into the architecture of six souls he knew the way he knew his own, unlocking what had been sitting dormant and capped beneath their trained expressions since the day each of them had sworn the oath. Kasei felt her own Zenshi widen without warning, the causal web she had spent ten years reading at sustained output suddenly deeper, further, the forty-meter radius of her signature field expanding past any boundary she had ever tested it against.

She grasped his hand before the feeling had finished settling into her, and something in her composure, the same composure the enclave had once called unshakable and frightening in the same breath, cracked open at the edges.

She looked at him with an unguarded attention she had not allowed herself since Asa's garden, and for one moment the oldest and steadiest of the Seven looked, plainly, like someone's little sister watching a brother she had feared losing come apart at the seams in front of her.

"We will not fail you. I promise." Her voice caught, and she pushed through it, precision holding even when the feeling underneath it didn't. "I promise. Your dream." She stopped, corrected herself, the correction mattering to her more than the sentence itself. "Our dream."

Sōma stood in the doorway with his arms crossed, and if the unlocking had done anything to him he did not show it beyond the faint tightening of the scar along his left palm. "The Black Dragon isn't a target. It's a region-sized problem wearing one body." His voice carried its usual flat economy, no more words spent than the statement required. "I'll need Kasei's field to find where its coordination threads actually originate before I cut anything. Otherwise we're removing legs from something that regrows them."

"You'll have it," Kasei said, not looking away from Niran. "Ninety seconds is what I can sustain. Use them well."

Rui had already started crying, not from fear, her fifteen-year-old face doing the thing it always did when feeling outpaced her ability to hold it quietly, the loose braids at her shoulders coming further undone with every second the emotion ran through her. "I've never felt the web this wide before." She said it half to herself, wonder threaded through the tears. "The reach isn't forty meters anymore. I can feel the bend I'd need past the horizon. That isn't fair to whatever's standing on the other end of it."

"Good," Jinmu said, quiet, the specific stillness of a man who spent more of himself in the archive than in the room he was physically standing in. "The dead Yukari who fought creatures at this scale before us will have left techniques in the Soul Plane worth reading. I'll have counters ready before Sōma finishes his first cut."

Serei's eyes carried their slow pulse, faster now than Niran had ever seen it run, the grid of thread-marks along her forearms catching the lamplight. "The Lock on this engagement isn't clean yet." She said it without alarm, only the flat report of someone incapable of pretending a reading was simpler than it was. "There are at least three branches where we don't return intact. I'll find the branch where we do. Give me the field, Kasei, and I'll hold the timing."

Kurama had said nothing yet, and when he finally spoke his voice carried the particular weight of a man who had absorbed more dead practitioners' final moments than anyone in the room could count. "Every Yukari who has ever faced something this size left a piece of themselves in what I carry." He rolled one broad shoulder, the thread-marks along it older and stranger than the others', layered rather than singular. "I'll draw on all of them if I have to. The Archive doesn't run out. It only gets heavier."

Mira had not let go of Niran's other hand through any of it, and now she looked at the six of them gathered in the doorway and back to the man beside her, still too weak to stand on his own and sending six people to kill a god-sized calamity anyway because there was no version of this where he did not.

"You're sending them without you," she said, quiet enough that it wasn't quite an accusation.

"I'm sending them with everything I have to give them." Niran's grip on her hand did not loosen. "That has to be enough today. It won't be enough forever. But it's enough today."

*Six souls I've spent seven years learning by heart,* Niran thought, watching the door where they'd gathered. *I would rather bleed the rest of this borrowed body dry than send them at half of what I could have given them.*

Kasei released his hand last, stepping back into the doorway beside the others, and looked at each of the Seven in turn the way she looked at everything, cataloguing what the Zenshi already knew and would not need to explain to any of them twice.

"We move within the hour," she said. "Sōma, map the coordination threads the moment we're in range. Rui, hold the bend until Serei calls the window. Jinmu, keep the archive open the entire engagement, not just at contact. Kurama, you draw last, not first. We use the living Seven before we spend the dead one."

No one argued with the order of it.

Kasei turned back once more toward the bed, and something almost soft crossed her face before she let the Zenshi's usual stillness reclaim it.

"Rest," she told him. "We'll bring you its head."

She left the room without waiting for him to answer, the other six falling into step behind her, and the door closed on six sets of footsteps already moving with the same borrowed certainty, six threads reading the same Lock-point and arriving, independently, at the same conclusion.

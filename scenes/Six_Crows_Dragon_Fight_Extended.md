The dragon screeched, and the sound arrived as a property of the world rather than a noise inside it.

Its form writhed along its full length, and everything the writhing touched began to die. The stand of half-burned trees behind Jinmu, still holding enough residual essence in their roots to have survived the first burning, lost that essence in the space of three heartbeats, bark going grey and papery, heartwood collapsing inward on itself as though a century of rot had been granted permission to happen all at once. Even the small fire Rui had built beside herself for warmth guttered and died, the flame simply losing the argument for its own continuation.

The roar came a half-second later, and space bent around the shape of it, the air visibly folding in concentric rings outward from the dragon's throat.

It lifted both foreclaws and drove them into the temporal disruption still pinning its tail, tearing at Serei's spike-ring with a violence that had nothing tactical in it, only the raw refusal of something enormous being told it could not move. Scale came away in sheets. Muscle tore where the spikes had threaded through it, and the dragon paid that cost without a flicker of hesitation, because pain had stopped being information to it the moment Cozbi finished assembling it out of things that could still feel.

Then it pushed off its rear feet.

Five hundred meters of ground stopped being ground.

The shockwave rolled outward as a single expanding ring of pure displacement, and the earth beneath it caved inward rather than outward, a concave collapse folding toward the point the dragon's mass had just vacated. Trees went down in rows. The scorched grass sheared away entirely, topsoil peeling back to bedrock in a spreading circle that reached the treeline in less time than a breath took.

Kasei was already kneeling when it reached her.

She pressed both palms flat to the earth and imprinted her essence downward in a hard oval, sixty meters on the long axis, anchoring the ground beneath Rui and Jinmu against a collapse that had no business leaving anything standing inside it. The oval held. The land beyond its boundary dropped four feet in a single grinding lurch, and the land inside it did not move at all.

*They cannot be interfered with,* Kasei thought, teeth set, her Zenshi already rerouting the sustained field around the disruption before the disruption finished arriving. *Fifteen minutes. Nothing touches them for fifteen minutes, including the ground itself.*

Kurama had not waited for the order.

He stood between the two seated Crows and the incoming wall of dying essence with both arms raised, and a barrier of layered light unfurled around them in a slow rotating helix, the accumulated harmonization patterns of a dozen dead Yukari drawn out of his Crystal and given shape. It did not block the shockwave. Blocking would have meant meeting force with force, and force at this scale would have flattened him. It parted the wave instead, splitting the front of it around the oval's edge and letting it close again behind them, and it did so without touching a single thread of the six-pillar working still assembling in the air above Jinmu's bowed head.

"Shit," Serei murmured.

Sōma was already airborne, and not by choice.

The shockwave caught him full across the chest and threw him three hundred meters back across the churned bowl the clearing had become, and he did not manage to stop himself so much as convert falling into a controlled skid, boots plowing twin furrows through bedrock, the Danshi corona in his eyes spiraling wide as his Crystal severed his own momentum's threads to the ground one at a time to bleed the velocity off before it broke his spine.

He came to rest on one knee, spat blood, and dragged the back of his palm across his mouth.

*Eleven minutes,* he thought, watching the dragon settle back onto its foreclaws two hundred meters out. *And the bastard has already worked out that the ones sitting down matter more than the one hitting him.*

His essence flared, black flame gathering along both forearms in coils tighter and hungrier than before, and he focused it down to a single specific purpose: separating the structural content of the dragon's scale from itself, thread by thread, at the level where scale had agreed to be scale rather than the loose keratin and calcified plate it had started as.

He dropped his hammer.

It did not hit the ground. The haft unfolded along a seam no craftsman had ever put there, and the whole weapon began to mend and reflow, metal running fluid without heat, the head splitting and thinning and drawing itself out until two blades hung in the air where a hammer had been, each one carrying fine crow-wing etchings along the fuller that caught the dawn light and refused to scatter it.

"Kyōdan."

Something otherworldly took him. The Veil itself seemed to push at his back, and Sōma went from a standing start to full flight in a single instant, rising above the dragon's guard as its claws swept the space he'd occupied a fraction of a second earlier. He passed over the strike with a bird's economy, no wasted motion, no flourish, and came down along the ridge of the creature's spine with both blades trailing.

He hit the back and slid.

Two hundred feet of descent down the dragon's dorsal ridge, blades dragging through scale that parted for them the way a hillside parts for a plow, black flame boiling out of every seam the edges opened. The dragon convulsed under him. Its whole body torqued in an attempt to throw him, muscle bunching along its flank hard enough that the movement alone cracked the plates Sōma hadn't yet cut, and he rode the convulsion down rather than fighting it, letting the beast's own violence carry him toward the tail.

The dragon stopped trying to throw him and rolled instead.

Forty meters of body turning over at once, aiming to crush him against its own bulk and the collapsing earth beneath it. Sōma read the roll a half-second before it committed and kicked off the spine hard, and the dragon's mass came down where he had been standing with a concussion that threw a fresh wave of debris across the bowl.

Serei was already in position.

She had been moving the entire time, back and forth, left and right, small precise adjustments that looked pointless from any angle except the one that mattered, each step placing her body incrementally closer to the position the future required rather than the position the present suggested. She brought her hands together, locked her fingers, and opened them.

"Kyōjin no Ma."

The blades appeared in her hands, exact mirrors of Sōma's, the same crow-etched fullers, the same weight, the same impossible reflowing metal. She had not been given the technique. She had read its Lock in the interval between Sōma's commitment and its manifestation, and in that interval, the technique's shape had been available to anyone standing close enough to the moment of its becoming.

She closed her eyes.

The dragon's claw came down directly onto the ground where she had been standing, and found a mirror instead, a shadow of her holding shape for exactly as long as the strike took to arrive before it dissolved into nothing.

Serei was already in the air, angled hard toward the ground, dropping directly along the line of the descending claw.

She unsheathed both blades and brought them together, and the two became one, weight and edge and mass all folding into a single denser shape, a katana longer than either blade had been and heavier than both combined.

"Falling Fang."

She came down.

The katana ran liquid in her hands as she swept it forward, and the imitated grammar of Ruin burst through the joints of the creature's claw, black fire finding every seam where bone met bone and telling each junction it no longer had permission to hold. The claw came apart at three separate articulations at once. Serei swerved off the strike's follow-through and let her momentum carry her beneath the creature's stomach, blade already reversing in her grip.

The dragon roared, and this time the roar carried something new underneath it.

It stopped trying to crush them.

The tail, still bleeding from Serei's ring of spikes, swept low and fast across the bowl at ankle height, not aimed at either fighter, aimed at the oval of stabilized ground where Kasei knelt with both palms pressed to the earth. Not tactics born of thought. Tactics born of something older, the instinct of a thing that had finally worked out which of the small shapes moving around it were the ones that mattered.

"Kasei." Serei's voice cut across the field, flat, urgent, the number arriving before the warning did. "Four seconds. It's going for the pillars."

"I can't move." Kasei did not lift her hands. "The moment I release this oval, they drop with the rest of the field."

"Then don't move." Sōma landed hard between the incoming tail and the oval's edge, both blades already reforming into a single haft, black flame running the full length of it. "Serei. Read me the Lock."

"Two seconds. Low, then a whip upward at the last third. Don't meet the low sweep. Meet the whip."

Sōma did not answer. He waited through the low sweep with the tail passing close enough beneath him to take skin off his shins, and when the whip came he was already rising into it, hammer coming down on the tail's upswing with the full weight of Ruin and the full weight of his own falling body behind it.

The tail broke.

Not cleanly. Something enormous inside it simply stopped agreeing to remain connected to the rest, and forty feet of limb went slack and dropped, plowing a final furrow through the collapsing earth before it came to rest.

The dragon screamed, and this time it was unmistakably a scream, a hundred conscripted voices finding the same note at once through a throat that had never been built to carry any of them.

Behind the treeline, inside the oval, Jinmu's eyes opened.

"I have it," he said quietly, to no one in particular, his voice carrying the strange doubled register of a man half-standing in the Soul Plane and half in the world. "The frequency. Every soul it's wearing. I can hear where the seams are."

Rui's hand tightened once against his shoulder, holding the shore steady, and the first of the six pillars began to rise out of the ruined ground.

Sōma led the six into the dragon's ruined territory before dawn, Kurama held back at Kasei's word despite everything in his bearing that argued he belonged at the front of this fight rather than the rear of it. Only one of them could purify the whole of what the beast had become, and Kasei had read the engagement's Lock-cascade clearly enough to know that spending him too early meant not having him at all when the purification actually mattered.

Kasei's instinct had settled on the Six Pillar formation the moment she'd mapped the dragon's scale against what the Seven could sustain unassisted, a branch of sealing craft the six of them had been quietly refining together for the better part of a year, built to hold rather than to kill outright. Rui and Jinmu were its two load-bearing pieces, and both of them settled into position well behind the coming violence, cross-legged in the ash beneath a stand of half-burned trees, Jinmu's eyes already closed.

He let his ears, his mind, his own body become a single branch of resonance extended out across the field, the archive behind his eyes quieting into pure reception, listening past the dragon's roar to the finer architecture underneath it, the specific frequency at which stolen souls scream when they've stopped being able to tell their own voice from the voice of the thing wearing them. It was a dangerous listening. A mind that opened this wide risked losing the edge that told it where its own thoughts ended and the resonance began.

Rui sat close enough to touch his shoulder, and did.

She was the tuning fork that kept him from drifting past the edge of himself, her Orihi's constant low hum of feeling giving his open resonance something anchored and human to return to whenever the archive's weight threatened to pull him further out than he could safely go. Her presence didn't stop the listening. It gave the listening a shore to come back to.

Kasei looked toward Sōma, the field of her Zenshō no Ma already unfurling invisibly across the forty-meter radius that would let all six of them read the engagement as one intelligence rather than six separate sets of eyes.

"You only need to hold him fifteen minutes." Her voice carried its usual flat patience, no reassurance offered because none was owed. "Hopefully you and Serei can manage that much."

"Fifteen minutes." Sōma didn't look back at her. "Generous of you to sound uncertain about it."

He walked forward alone, hammer resting against one shoulder, black flame licking low along the haft in slow, deliberate coils that did not consume the wood beneath them, Ruin's particular grammar of decay held on a leash tight enough to burn only what he chose to feed it. The dragon's vast head turned toward him across two hundred meters of scorched ground, one molten eye finding him and holding.

"Hey." Sōma's voice carried flat across the distance, economical even now, each word spent because it would do something rather than because the moment called for noise. "Shit-face. Wake up."

The insult was not cruelty for its own sake. It was bait, precise in its crudeness the same way every other word out of his mouth was precise, calculated to pull the dragon's full attention onto him and away from the six-pillar working still quietly assembling itself behind the treeline.

It worked.

The dragon's roar cracked the air as it surged forward, and Sōma jumped to meet it rather than away from it, black flame trailing off his shoulders in widening coils as he closed the last of the distance in a single committed arc. His hammer came down against the ridge of the creature's skull with the full weight of Ruin folded into the swing, the point of impact carrying not blunt force alone but the finer, quieter work of his Danshi threading through the strike, severing the connective architecture binding scale to bone at the exact point of contact rather than merely bruising it. Chitin and bone alike lost their cohesion where the hammer landed, the specific silent failure of a structure whose threads had simply stopped agreeing to hold each other together.

The dragon's tail answered before the skull had finished registering the blow, whipping around in a wide, serpentine arc aimed to swat Sōma out of the air the way a man might swat an insect that had bitten too deep to ignore.

Serei was already moving before the tail had finished its first quarter-turn.

Her Teishi read the Lock the instant it committed, the strike's outcome fixed in the interval before its physical arrival had caught up to what the causal web already knew was coming, and she occupied the space the outcome required rather than reacting to the motion itself. From where Kasei watched through the sustained field, it looked like Serei simply stopped being where the tail was going to be a half-second before the tail arrived to confirm it.

Her spikes rose out of the scorched ground in a tight ring around the tail's midsection, driven through scale and muscle both, essence bleeding off each point of contact in a deep, faint purple that shimmered the air around it the way heat shimmers rise off summer stone, distortion rather than color exactly, the visible signature of pressure concentrated past the threshold where ordinary matter stopped agreeing to stay still. The tail's arc lost its momentum mid-swing, pinned through six separate Lock-points at once, the whole limb's motion arrested not by force meeting force but by Serei having simply already been standing in the only future where the strike failed to land.

*He's slower to recover from Ruin's cost than he lets Kasei see,* Serei noted, reading the faint hitch in Sōma's second landing even as her own attention stayed split three directions across the field. *Nine minutes left before that cost catches up to him in front of the dragon rather than behind it.*

Sōma dropped back to the scorched earth in a controlled fall, hammer already rising for the next strike, black flame trailing thinner off his shoulders than it had a moment before, the specific cost of Ruin's grammar deducting itself from him one severed connection at a time even as it deducted itself from the dragon.

"Nine minutes," Serei called out, flat, the number arriving with the same certainty she gave everything. "Kasei. The pillars need to be ready before eight."

Behind the treeline, Jinmu's breathing had slowed to something barely visible, his open resonance already threading itself through the first faint outline of the six-pillar working, and Rui's hand tightened once against his shoulder, holding the shore steady while the tide of listening pulled harder against him than it had all night.

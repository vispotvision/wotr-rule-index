# The Word Under the Hammer

*Beneath the Grand Church at Altherion. The eleventh day.*

---

Ammonia first, because that was what a room became after a man had been in it upright for eleven days with nowhere to go.

Under that, wet iron, thick enough to taste at the back of the throat. Under that, the sweetish rot of tissue that had been open too long in a warm room and had begun the work of dying at a different rate from the rest of him. And under all of it, faintly, incongruously, beeswax, because the frame they had hung him on had been a choir stall two months ago and somebody had not stripped the polish before they cut it down.

Aurelian Prudentius could smell his own legs.

They had taken the flesh off both thighs in long strips beginning at the hip and working down, six on the left and five on the right, each one lifted with a hooked blade and peeled with the patience of a man skinning a hare he intends to eat. The strips had been laid on the flags below him in the order of removal because Sesk was tidy. What remained was fascia, pearl-white where it had not yet dried and grey-yellow where it had, and beneath the fascia the long red bellies of the muscles running down toward the knee, and in three places the pale glimmer of the sheath where they had gone deeper than the trade required and had stopped, because killing him was against instruction.

The nails were the newer work.

Ten of them, one to each fingertip, driven in under the nail through the pulp and seated into the bone of the distal phalanx. They were not iron. Iron would have rusted and rust was a smell he knew. These drank the lamplight off their heads and gave nothing back, and the blood that had come out around them on the first day had run red for perhaps a minute and had then gone black, and the black had kept coming, and it came still, and it fell from his fingers onto the stone in a rhythm he had stopped counting on the fourth day.

The first nail had been the loudest thing that ever happened to him.

The third had been worse and he had understood why by the fifth: the hand learns. By the third finger the whole limb was braced for it, so the blow arrived into tissue that was already screaming, and the body had nothing left to give the new injury except the old one amplified. By the seventh he had begun to hear the tap of the mallet before he felt anything at all, which was a mercy for about a quarter of a second and then was not.

The nails were not the beginning. The beginning had been the mouth, on the second day, when somebody upstairs still believed this would take an afternoon: a wedge between the back teeth and a pair of drawing pliers, and four molars out of the lower jaw before Sesk arrived and put a stop to it on the grounds that a man who cannot form words is no use to anybody. The sockets had abscessed by the fifth day. They had drained on their own on the eighth and the taste of that had been in the back of his throat since, and he had stopped noticing it around the time they started on the thighs, which was the only benefit the abscesses had conferred.

They gave him water twice a day and they gave him enough. That was the part that had told him most about who was giving the instructions. A man being tortured for information is kept thirsty because thirst breaks people cheaply. A man being kept is kept hydrated. Somebody upstairs wanted him alive on a timeline that extended past the end of Sesk's patience, and Aurelian had been turning that fact over for six days and had got nothing out of it except a growing certainty that the questions were not the point of the questions.

His arms were overhead and had been for eleven days apart from the hours they took him down to work on him properly.

That was the part nobody wrote down about a hanging frame. The shoulders went first, the capsule stretching past what a capsule is for, and then the nerve bundle under the collarbone began to complain and then stopped complaining, and both hands had gone numb somewhere around the sixth day, which had been an unlooked-for kindness until they drove the nails in and he discovered that the numbness had never reached that far. And under all of it the ribs could not fall. A man hanging by the wrists inhales easily and exhales against his own weight, and so every breath out for eleven days had been work, and the work had a floor under it, and below that floor was the point where a man simply stops having the strength to exhale and drowns in a dry room.

He was a long way from that. That was the difficulty.

"Lux, lux," Aurelian said. "Veniet, veniet."

Blood came out of his mouth with it, more than the words justified, and hit the flags and spread.

"Omnis dolor abluetur. Omnia patefient, appendentur." A breath, dragged up against his own hanging weight. "Iudicabuntur."

---

Corwin Sesk had been sitting on an upturned crate for two hours writing nothing.

He was fifty and unremarkable in every particular, which was the qualification. Sandy going grey, a bald patch he combed nothing over, a paunch, forearms like a butcher's because that is what the trade is. Brown wool, good quality, the sleeves rolled and pinned above the elbow so the cuffs stayed clean. A leather apron he took off when he ate. He had a ledger on his knee and a lead stylus behind his ear and he had made four entries in eleven days, and three of them were about supplies.

He was not cruel. That was the thing Aurelian had established by the second day and had found genuinely disturbing. Sesk had no appetite for any of it. He had put in the nails the way a cooper puts in a hoop, checked his work, wiped the mallet head, and sat down.

*Eleven days. I have had bricklayers open in half this. He is not resisting. That is what nobody upstairs will understand when I write it. A man resisting has a shape to him, he is pushing against something, you can feel where he is holding and you go there. This one is not holding anywhere. It is like putting a chisel into water.*

"Word," said Sesk.

Aurelian's eyes came back into the room. They did that now, went and came, and the going was getting longer.

"Master Sesk."

"You have said that same line four hundred and eleven times. I have not been counting because I was told to. I have been counting because there is nothing else to count." Sesk set the ledger on the crate. "What does it mean."

"It means the light will come."

"And?"

"And all pain will be washed. And everything will be laid open and weighed." Aurelian's mouth moved into the small closed shape it made. "And judged."

"That is a threat, then."

"It is a description." Blood ran off his chin. "You are in it too, Master Sesk. That is the part people miss. It is not a curse laid on you by me. It is a statement about the structure of the room we are both standing in, and it applies to me identically, and I have considerably more to be laid open than you do."

Sesk looked at him for a while.

"That is the first thing you have said in eleven days that was not in Vulgar Church," he said. "I will have to write that one down."

He wrote it down.

Then he got up, took the mallet off the crate, and broke Aurelian's left kneecap with it.

---

The knee was not a question. The knee was a man who had run out of procedure.

It went with a sound like a fired pot dropped on a flag, and the leg swung, and the flayed muscle over the joint tore across its own length because there was no skin left to hold it together, and everything below the break turned outward at an angle that legs do not have.

Aurelian's whole frame convulsed on the chain and his shoulders took it and something in the right one let go with a wet pop that he heard through the bone of his jaw.

And he suspended it.

That was the honest description of what he did and there was no other. He did not endure it and he did not master it and he did not offer it up. He caught the entire arriving mass of it at the interval, in the sum-cham, in the half-beat between the event and the body's report of the event, and he held it there, unpaid, and he set it on the stack with the other eleven days.

The stack was very tall now.

It had been tall before he came here. Thirty-one years of it, everything from twenty-seven onward, and the bottom of it was a thing he had never written down and could no longer clearly remember, and the whole edifice released from the bottom on a schedule nobody had ever explained to him.

The lamp on the wall bracket stood up straight and thinned to a blue thread.

Every surface within a yard of him went to the temperature of a hand held too long against skin. The film of blood on the flags under his feet stopped being wet at the edges. The old choir-stall wood at his back gave off a smell of hot beeswax, and somewhere behind Sesk a rat that had been living in the corner for six days went out under the door and did not come back.

Sesk stepped away and put a hand up in front of his face.

"That again," he said. "You do that every time and you do not scream. I have taken a knee off a Dawi hammerman and he screamed until he was sick. What is it you are doing."

"I am deferring it."

"To when."

"I do not know."

*And that is the answer and it is the truth and he will not believe it, because everybody assumes a man who can put a thing off has chosen when to pick it up.*

---

They took him down after the knee.

Two of them, unhurried, one at each armpit, and the chain ran out through the ring in the ceiling and his own weight came off his shoulders for the first time in nine hours and the relief of it was so total and so obscene that he made a sound he had not made in eleven days. They laid him on his side on the flags in his own blood while Sesk washed the mallet head in a bucket. Nobody spoke. One of them lifted the broken leg by the ankle and set it straight with a professional disinterest that Aurelian understood, half a moment later, was so that the knee would knit crooked rather than not at all, because a joint that heals wrong can be broken again in the same place and a joint that has come apart entirely cannot.

Then they put him back up.

The bells started again around the second hour.

There were no bells beneath the Grand Church at Altherion. He had established that on the third day when he still had the coherence to establish things. What he heard was on the other side, coming through where his attention had worn the membrane thin from leaning on it, and it was not a peal and it was not a tolling. It was the small flat sound of a handbell being rung once, at irregular intervals, the way a bell is rung in an orchard to tell the pickers to move down a row.

And under it, the pruning.

That was the sound he had no account for and had stopped trying to build one. Blades. Two of them, crossing, closing on green wood and cutting through it clean, and then a pause of the correct length for a hand to move to the next stem, and then again. Somebody was working a very large garden very carefully and had been doing it for eleven days without once hurrying and without once resting, and each time the shears closed Aurelian felt something in the room's ambient give way and reseat slightly differently, the way a floor does when someone walks across it two rooms away.

He had spent thirty-one years reading the Plane of Fate and had never in that time heard anyone maintaining it.

He would have given a great deal to know what was being cut back.

"Lux," he said. "Lux."

His head had gone down onto his own chest. He got it up.

"Veniet. Veniet."

The four Attestants were alive. That was the arrangement and he had accepted the arrangement with his eyes open and had walked here on his own feet, and the arrangement was that he stayed and they lived and the scriptorium remained a scriptorium. He held that fact the way a man holds a rail. Four people and a room full of books, against one fifty-eight-year-old body that had already been running on borrowed thermodynamics for three decades. It was arithmetic. It was good arithmetic. He had checked it twice on the road and once in the cell.

"Omnis dolor abluetur."

He could have taken the block apart on the first morning. He could take it apart now, one-legged, ten-nailed, with a dislocated shoulder, in about four seconds, and he knew the number because he had worked it out on the second day and had not permitted himself to work it out again since.

That was the whole discipline. Not the pain.

"Omnia patefient. Appendentur."

Sesk had gone back to his crate and picked up the ledger and was looking at the one line he had written.

*He is going to outlast me. That is the thing I am going to have to put in the report and I do not yet know how to word it so that it does not end my contract. Whatever they think they are extracting, it is not in there, and I have opened him up far enough to see most of what is.*

"Iudicabuntur," said Aurelian.

The blood came off his fingertips in ten black threads, ten separate small sounds on the stone, out of sequence with each other, none of them keeping time.

Out in the corridor a door was unbarred, and boots came four paces closer, and stopped, and stood there a while, and did not come any nearer.

Aurelian Prudentius hung on the chain in the heat coming off his own body and set his teeth against the inside of his cheek and put the knee onto the stack a second time, because the first suspension had begun to slip, and started the line again from the beginning.

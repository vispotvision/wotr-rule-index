# The Testament of the Sixty-Fifth

Nowhere under Altherion. Continuous.

---

[Rubric] From the Rubric, the received text, on Transposition. Kept at the Alabaster Court in the same press as the Sacrament and read from far less often.

[Rubric] Transposition is the overwriting of a position. No distance is travelled. Nothing crosses the ground between, and nothing arrives that was not already spoken for.

[Rubric] The bill of goods: a mirror or a still water, a threshold with a lintel, the mark of duality, the mark of crossing, the mark of the binding chain, and an anchor.

[Rubric] The anchor is a soul that holds the celebrant's name at weight and will hold it while he is not in his place. The rite does not care whether the anchor has been asked. The older register held that it should be. The Court has ruled for neither reading, and no Palatine has been willing to be the one who does.

[Rubric] Do not perform Transposition without an anchor. The soul does not remap. What comes back is entered in the day-book as the celebrant, and the entry carries a marginal note, and the note is never removed.

[Rubric] Recorded failure. The Fourth Prince of Rovann exchanged with a corrupted dreamform in the ninth month of the war. The body was recovered intact and continued to hold court for eleven years. The register does not say what was in it. The register says the household stopped bringing in food at the end of the second year and nobody in the household could afterward say when they had decided to.

[Rubric] Accord standing law, the only one it has ever written about a rock: Crevice Shale, Tier Nine, is forbidden to move, to hold, or to trade. No mechanism is given. Enforcement has hanged eleven men under it and has never once been asked to explain the sentence.

[Verinus] Such meaningless words.

He said it and heard it leave him and go out and keep going, and there was nothing for it to come back off. Nineteen years of a building that returned his voice to him a half-second late and doubled, and he had built a whole method on that half-second, and it was gone, and the sentence went out into a distance with no far side and was still going.

The cell had stopped being anywhere.

He had felt it happen and had been unable to attend to it. The Flame had taken the choir stall first, and then the flags, and then the strips of Aurelian on the flags, and then the walls of a room that had been a chapter store since the reign before last, and it took none of it violently. The stone went pale, and then went fine, and then went. Dust off a dry hand. The rock under the Cloister Ward that had held up a church for four hundred years turned out to have no argument against purity at all, and the Flame did not push it down. It found nothing in it that had a right to continue and it stopped continuing, and Verinus, in the middle of that, kept standing because there was nothing left to stand on and therefore nothing left to fall through.

Ash on his tongue. Beeswax in it, which was the stall, and cold lime, which was the mortar, and under both the sweet dead smell of the leg-strips going, and he shut his mouth on all three and could not stop tasting them.

Then the Song.

It came up under him where the draw-hum had been, low in the sternum, and it had no source and no direction. Voices. A great many, matched, singing with each other and never at each other, holding a chord that had one more voice in it than a chord can carry, and the extra voice went through his chest and out the other side of him and he had to put his hand flat against his breastbone to keep himself from following it.

The priests at Altherion had a name for this. They put it in the mouths of the dying and in the ninth-year sermons and on four walls that Verinus had walked past every winter of his working life. The Song of Grace, given to the Judger's most devoted, and every man who had ever claimed to hear it had been sanctified inside a generation.

He heard it. He declined the name.

[Verinus] A servant hears a song and calls it a reward. I have never in my life been the Judger's servant, and I have said so in front of two Curias and had it struck from the minute both times, and I will say it here where there is no clerk to strike it. A servant can resign. A servant can be dismissed and can be forgiven and can be sent home to his village to keep bees. I am not the man in the room with the Judger. I am the part of him that does the weighing, and a hand does not serve an arm.

The Song went up a degree.

And the fourteen were in it. That was the thing the walls at Altherion had left out, and the sermons, and the ninth-year readings, and Verinus heard it and understood at seventy-one what he had been getting written out of minutes since he was fifty-two. The chord had Urion in it, and Urion alone would have been a single note held forever, which is a tone and not a song. Selhar tuned it. Maelor held what had already been sung so the next part had somewhere to stand. Auren ended the phrases, because a phrase that will not end is a scream. Kaetra put in the false interval that made the true one land. Uurgath took the whole thing apart at the turn so that it could be something else on the far side of the bar.

Balance was the whole instrument sounding, and a man who had spent his life weighing had been given one string and told it was the choir.

They would have had him on a bench for that at fifty-two. They would have had him on a bench for it this morning.

His hands were wrong.

He looked down at them because the Song had gone into his forearms and the skin there was moving. The chalk went first. Second knuckle down, both hands, six years of coming straight from the slope and the basin and forgetting to wash, gone off the skin that had been holding it. Then the liver-marks under it. Then the long ropes of tendon standing off the backs of his hands settled down into flesh that had no need to show its working, and the joints of his fingers, which had been swollen since a winter in his fifties and had made him give up the small chalk and take the large, came down smooth and narrow and quick.

He turned them over. Somebody else's palms. No callus at the base of the second finger where forty-five years of holding a stylus had built one.

The teeth came in.

That was worse than the knee and he had not expected it to be. Four sockets in the lower jaw, blown out that morning by a rite that took Aurelian's fourteen days in the order Sesk had removed them, and the bone in them went hot and then went tight and then the crowns came up through the gum with a pressure that had a sound to it in the skull, four separate small grinding arrivals, and his eyes ran and he could do nothing about that at all.

The left knee reassembled. The kneecap that had gone on the flags of a cellar that morning, and the older ruin under it, the one that had complained on every cold wall in the Alabaster Court for ten years and had made him take the second step instead of the alabaster chair, both went. The joint straightened and stayed straightened and there was no ache in it, anywhere, in any position, and Verinus stood in an infinite nothing with a knee that had stopped talking to him and missed the ache the way a man misses a dog.

The robe hung heavy and dark from both hips where eleven strips of thigh had been paid across a room. The cloth stayed dark. Underneath it the flesh came back, and the fascia over it, and skin, and the pattern of hair on it, and none of it hurt.

His hair was black.

It came down over his eye, which was a thing that had happened to him last at twenty-six, and he pushed it back with a hand that had no chalk on it and no callus and put his cap on straight, and the cap was too loose, because a skull at seventy-one is a different object.

[Verinus] Twenty-five. Twenty-six. I know the hands. I have not seen these hands since the year I was made a Lector, and I remember what I thought with them, and I am not going to be permitted to think it again.

There was a word for this and he had heard it four hundred times from the pulpit and had refused it four hundred times in writing. Apotheosis. The Court would use it before the week was out. Somebody would put a window in.

He had exactly one hour of use for it and the hour had started.

[Verinus] "Such meaningless words," [narrator] Verinus said again, to the wound.

He said it flatly, in the voice he used to read a lesson from the low lectern on an ordinary Fifthday, and the Song carried it, and it went out into a place where a great deal was listening.

[Verinus] "You have spent your speech on renewal. On mercy, and the obscenity of a corpse being made to feed something. It is a good speech. I have heard four versions of it in this life from men holding considerably less than you are holding, and every one of them made the same error, and I am going to name it once and then I am going to stop being polite about the Titan."

The Song under him took a step down the register.

[Verinus] "I pray for no renewal. Understand that plainly, because it is the last plain thing I am likely to say. I do not want the Titan loosed. I do not want the Titan restored, or repaired, or fed, or given a shape on the far side of what you did to him. I do not ask for him to be freed and I do not ask for him to escape, and I have no interest in whether he was merciful. I have read the account. I have signed the appendix that says the account is incomplete."

His right hand had come up, palm out, without instruction from him.

[Verinus] "I want the wrath."

Golden fracture opened along the underside of his forearm and ran to the wrist and did not close.

[Verinus] "I can feel it. It is arriving in me at this moment, and it is not a doctrine and it is not the Court's Scale and there is nothing measured in it whatsoever. It is raw. He is furious, and the fury has no procedure in it, and it has come down the same line the Song came down and it is in my chest with my own. His hatred is my hatred. I have spent nineteen years insisting to a Curia that Urion is a function and does not feel, and I was wrong in the minute and I would like that entered as well."

The fracture reached his elbow.

[Verinus] "You will defeat me in power. That has been true since I was nineteen years old and it has been true of nearly every man I have ever stood in a room with. The Court kept a figure for me and the figure was the lowest ever set against a Palatine's name, and I read it aloud at my own investiture and asked that it be minuted, because I have never wanted a single soul in that building to be confused about what they had appointed."

The gold went into his shoulder.

[Verinus] "What I have instead is will. My soul is spoken for. My fate is spoken for. Neither is available to be bargained with, because neither of them is mine to bring to the table, and there is nothing you can offer a man who is already the property of the thing he loves."

The fracture crossed his back and both shoulder blades opened.

[Verinus] "So kill me. Erase me. Take the soul apart and file the pieces where they cannot find each other. It costs you nothing to do and I am not going to pretend it will be difficult." [narrator] The hair was in his eye again. He left it. [Verinus] "And out of the flame of Urion I will come back up. In pieces, and late, from under whatever you leave, and I will keep arriving, and you will have to do it again, and the second time it will be more expensive than the first."

The wings came out of him.

Sheets. Two of them, high, off the blades of his shoulders, gold and cracked through with a hundred jagged lines that had light standing in the lines, and they were the same fracture that had been on him in the cellar and there was more of it now and it had found a shape to be. No feather. No pinion. Every painter given a wall at Altherion had put this on it and had called it wings and had been wrong about the object and right about the outline, and Verinus, who was twenty-six years old and had a cap that no longer fitted, understood at last that the painters had been copying somebody who saw it.

[Verinus] "I am Verinus," [narrator] he said. [Verinus] "Sixty-fifth Palatine of the Holy Sea of Alabaster. I am going to be the testament to what we are, and I am going to be it here, and the record of it will be a rock in a field that nobody can lift."

He put his hand out.

There is a part of the Void that is adjoined to the thing that rules it, and there is a part that is only Void, unadministered, running on nothing, and the second is the larger. Verinus took the second in his hand.

He had no force to apply. He had never had any. What he had was thirty-one years of knowing what a rite is: an address, a bill, an order of operations, and a thing in the world that is already doing the work and only wants telling where.

He told it where.

The pressure went out of him in gold and it was not a blast and it did not travel. It arrived, everywhere it was going, at once, and the unadjoined Void felt it and had no procedure for being weighed. Reality bent downward. Spirals, wide as counties, turning down and inward with the patience of water finding a drain that has been shut a long time, and in the trough of each spiral something came into being that had been missing, and Hataraki was doing it, the Work at its barest, function performing the labour it was made for and asking nobody.

The missing pieces were made. The Void was completed.

And what is completed has weight, and weight falls.

They came down across the whole world. Black stones, none of them larger than a loaf, matte, taking the light and giving nothing back. One went into the Sound off Alabaster and the herring boat that saw it come down put about and did not report it. One landed in a Kharven woodpile at the thin end of the season and cracked the night-stone beside it and the household stood in the doorway and did not go near it. One came through the roof of a chapter hold in the Old World, and one into a salt street at Deepvein where the watch had entered a tremor and gone back to bed, and one into a physic garden that Verinus had walked in nine days ago, and thousands more, into fields, into water, into the middle of a road, into places where nobody was standing and would not be for years.

He did not see them land.

He knew that they had, the way he had known about the standpipes. He was pleased. He counted it as Balance, because a hole in the world had been filled and the ledger came out even, and he did not have the instrument to read what he had actually put on the ground, and there was no Measurewright within four worlds of him to say the word shale in his hearing.

Both wings were out to their full extent now and the Song had gone up into a register he could no longer hear, and could feel.

[Verinus] "Dilue caelum," [narrator] Verinus said.

Dilute the sky.

Gold came into the nothing overhead, thin at first, a shimmer laid over absence, and thickened.

[Verinus] "Luceant super hunc mundum stellae mundi primi."

Let the stars of the first world shine on this one again. They came out in the gold, in the wrong places, in the arrangements that had been over the ground before Malakar and had never once been over it since, and Verinus looked up at a sky nobody living had seen and set the last three terms of the rite in order in his head: the mirror, the threshold, the anchor.

He had no mirror. The gold overhead was a still surface with a light behind it and it would answer.

He had no lintel. The tear in the world had two edges and a top, and a thing with two edges and a top is a door, and the Rubric had never specified whose.

He had no anchor, and the Rubric is not gentle about that, and he had read the note in the margin about the Fourth Prince of Rovann in a cold room at fifty-two and had been unable to sleep after it.

He took Aurelian.

He did it in the half-second available and without asking, and he knew exactly what he was doing, because there was one soul left in four worlds holding his name at weight, and it was standing in a cellar under Altherion in the wrong skin with no teeth, believing that four people were alive. The bond had been made at dawn with a surety and a Sacrament and it would hold. It would hold and it would carry, and whatever came back up out of this would come back up that line, and Aurelian would be standing at the other end of it with no warning and no procedure and no idea that he had been made a fixed point.

[Verinus] I told the Bishop to spend the objection where the cost is measurable. This cost is measurable. I am measuring it. I am going to do it anyway, and I have run out of the distinction I said was the only one I had left.

[Verinus] "Transponar."

The threshold took him.

There was no crossing and no interval and nothing between the two places, because there is nothing between the two places, and the Rubric says so in its first line and Verinus had read the line aloud to a class of Lectors in the year he turned sixty. His position stopped being where it had been. It was written somewhere else, at the origin, at the exact point that all of it was coming out of, close enough to reach.

The gold sky came with him. The stars came with him, wrong and old and burning in their first arrangement over a place that had never had a sky.

Verinus arrived in the dark at the root of the wound with both wings out and his cap sliding, and he reached up with a young man's hand and pulled it down hard onto his head, and set his feet.

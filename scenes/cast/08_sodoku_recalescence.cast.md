# Recalescence

The narrows. Immediately.

---

The eye would not shut.

That was the first cruelty and it was a mechanical one. He had gone into Asami on that road and the whole trained apparatus had gone quiet with it, the Ledger and the sorting and the ranking, and what a man loses when he stops holding the tools is the ability to put them down on purpose. Reigan was open. It had been open since the hand came off his shoulder and it had no operator in it any more, and so it showed him everything at once and in no order and did not stop to ask.

Muken first, because Muken was first and because everything after it ran downhill from there.

No wound. That is the whole of it and it is why Sodoku has never been able to put the loss anywhere. The Tenrai do not fight, they arrange, and what the Line arranged was convergence-friendly channels and a killing built so that the people who lived through it would spend the following years arguing whether it had been murder or misfortune instead of asking who came out of it holding the ground. Nineteen years of a reign and his son cannot point at a moment. There is no moment. There is an outcome, and the outcome was in the room before anybody arrived, and the Houses sent condolences.

Then the corridor.

He had not been in it. That has never once helped, and it did not help now, because Reigan does not care where a man was standing and shows the room from wherever the room can be seen.

Tomuka against the wall with a blade laid flat on his carotid at fifteen degrees off the edge, twelve years old, in a nightshirt and one boot, because he had got one foot dressed before dressing stopped being the relevant activity. His mother's hair. His father's jaw, already too wide for the face and still coming.

Ezo nine, four feet to the left, not restrained. Nobody had bothered. He had his palms flat on the stone behind him and was pressing back into it, and his eyes were Muken's amber-gold in a face still round, and the tears were running in a continuous unbroken flow, no sobbing anywhere in it, a body performing the last function available to it. He was not looking at the blade. He was looking at his brother's face.

And the thing holding the blade was in no hurry at all.

It smelled of damp stone and lamp oil and something sweet under both, flowers left in a sealed room until they turned. It had a second blade at the left hip in a scabbard two inches too short, so the last of the steel stood exposed and had gone dull grey with it. It had shut every route in that wing before it opened its mouth, and it had put the shutting into the guards' heads instead of into the doors, so that men who had walked those corridors for years found their own feet refusing turnings they had used a thousand times.

It was waiting for his mother.

Two sons. One question.

That was the instruction and it had come out of a war room at the Amber Citadel in a level voice, and the design of it was the part Sodoku had never been able to stop reading. No blade went anywhere near her. A choice, made real, made to feel like the one mercy still on the table. Let her believe that naming one saves the other.

Then kill the one she named.

The one she chose to save, in front of her, so that the hand that did the cutting was never the hand anyone remembered. The mother did it. The decision did. The love did. The Tenrai only supplied the architecture, and they were paid for the architecture, and no bench in four worlds has ever found a way to enter that on a docket.

Ayame Yuno came down that corridor in the White Concord's pale silk with the silver thread still on her from the morning's ceremony.

She answered the question.

Sodoku has never once put which of them she named into a sentence, and Reigan showed him the moment anyway and he took it and did not make it into words even now. She got down onto the boards afterward and took what was left of one of her sons up against the pale silk, and the sound she made is on record in exactly one place, which is inside a four-year-old who was awake in a room off that corridor, and it has been living there since, waiting on a trigger.

Both of them were dead inside the year. That was the other thing the architecture had allowed for.

His mother went later, by her own hand, in the manner the household never described and which Sodoku has never asked anybody to describe.

The Saishiki went with her. Nobody left holds the liturgy. Every Ice-out since, the ground opens and the whole winter's dead go in together and their names are read in the order they died, and what reads them now is a man with a list doing his best.

And Mizuyi, who was alive.

That arrived with the rest and had no business in a list of the dead and came anyway, because the eye does not sort. His sister in a Zettari holding a very long way south, married into the heaviest material in the Inner World by a father who had called it architecture and had been correct, and loving the man she was given to with the whole load-bearing devotion of a woman who decided the giving would mean something. Alive. Out of reach. And her name in a Tenrai war room as a claim to be pushed, aimed at her own brothers' succession, without anybody having asked her.

Seiji Tenrai Moto was eight miles up that road with six hundred men and a claim and four sets of messengers on four corridors.

And then the Tenrai settlement.

Reigan showed him that too. It did not ask and there was nobody left inside him to decline. Years of a sealed archive, held in the High Council alongside the exile decree, and every page of it had been in his own hands originally, and the eye ran the whole of it in front of him in the correct order at full weight while his own family's dead were still arriving.

He watched himself do it.

And behind all of that, arriving last because it had happened last, the cistern.

He saw it as the Plane shows things, which is to say completely, and there is no arrangement of words that improves it.

Hild Ice went in the doorway that Osric had put himself in. The black came through both of them together. It opened her from the left shoulder down through the ribs and out at the hip in four lines that were not cuts and did not behave like cuts, and what came away came away in strips, and the strips went to pieces before they reached the floor. The circlet was not on her head. She had left it on a mounting block that morning and forgotten it and a man had picked it up and put it in his belt.

At the Attraction Layer her anchor was intact for the length of one and a half seconds afterward. It went on holding a man four hundred paces up a road while the body it had been seated in stopped being a body, and there was nothing in it that knew yet, and that was the whole of the one and a half seconds and Sodoku had all of them.

Then the anchor came out of him.

An Attraction Layer bond does not tear. It is seated, and when the far end stops existing the seating has nothing to hold against, and the whole structure comes out of the Crystal in one piece and leaves the socket open. Twelve years of Lattice built around that seating, the whole of the girl's life. All of it load-bearing, none of it designed to stand without the anchor in place, and the collapse ran through the Shell in the order the Lattice had been laid down.

---

Sodoku Moto went down onto both knees in the ice.

Blood came out of his mouth and off his chin and out of both ears, and there was blood coming through the mail at the left shoulder where nothing had touched him. That is what happens to a body that is asked to carry a bond severing at that magnitude without warning: the vessels go at the weakest points first and the weakest points are wherever the last four hours put them.

Everything went out of the middle of him.

Grief has heat in it and shape and a direction to lean, and this had none of the three. What he had was the removal of the entire object he had been leaning on for twelve years, and the leaning continued for a while afterward with nothing under it, and that is the sensation and it has no name in any tongue he had.

The voices came then.

Nothing acoustic about them. He knew the difference and it did not help. Everyone he had ever failed arrived along the open eye at the same time and each one brought their own account, itemised, accurate, in their own voices, and the accuracy was the torture. Tomuka's account. Ezo's. His mother's, which had no reproach in it and was worse for that. Osric's, four minutes old. And Hild's, which was not an account and was not words and was the sound a four-year-old had heard on a floor in Kharven-Seat five years ago, arriving now in the wrong voice.

And under the voices, the emptiness he had met twice before, coming up through the floor of him the way water comes up through a stone floor in the second week of a thaw.

He knew this one. He had been here at thirty-two and he had been here in the water, and both times it had gone through him and out and left him standing, and this time it did not go out.

It stayed.

It began to organise, which is a thing emptiness had never done in front of him before, and he had no framework for it and watched it happen from the inside with a kind of clinical interest that would frighten him later.

---

Yoko Mishiro got to him on the fourth stride.

She came out of the ice at the edge of the road at a flat sprint with her ears down and her tail out for balance, and she went to one knee beside him and put her hand around his forearm.

There was nothing to close on.

Her fingers went to the mail and through the place where the arm inside it should have been, and met resistance the way a hand meets deep water, and closed, and held nothing. The arm was there. She could see it. The Shell around it had gone somewhere and taken the surface of him with it.

Nothing.

[Yoko Mishiro] I told him once that my nose would catch anything long before it got close. That was the whole of what I had to offer and he took it and called me a tool, and I made my peace with the word on a step in the rain, and the nose is the one thing in me that has never been wrong.

It is wrong now. He is under my hand and there is no scent on him at all. Absent entirely, the absence of a room whose door you open a season after the person in it stopped living there.

He is still breathing. So it has all gone inward. Whatever he is, it is all in the middle of him now and there is nothing left at the edges for anybody to reach, and I have been reaching for this man since before he ever said my name out loud.

[narrator] She had come out of a fight to do it. There was a spearhead through the meat of her left thigh, snapped off at the socket, that she had not mentioned and would not mention, and she was kneeling on that leg, and there were old marks around both ankles under the boot tops that had stopped being wounds a long time ago and had never stopped being marks.

She kept her hand where it was.

That was all. She did not speak and did not pull and did not try again, and she knelt in the ice with her hand closed on the shape of an arm and stayed there.

And Sodoku felt it.

No skin was left in the transaction anywhere. It came in at the layer where an anchor lives, which is the one layer that had not gone anywhere, and it arrived as warmth and it was warmth, and it was the only thing arriving from outside him in the entire world.

[Sodoku Moto] She is still here.

Everything else is gone and she is still here and she has been still here since we were children and I have never once said anything about it to her.

---

[narrator] He had been supercooled for half his life.

That was the honest account of it and he arrived at it in the instant the warmth reached him, without words, along the route a man takes to a thing his body already knew.

A liquid held below its freezing point does not have to freeze. If nothing in it gives the crystal a place to start, it will sit there, colder than the point at which it should have gone solid, holding the whole latent heat of the change it has not made. It can do that for a very long time. It will do it indefinitely. And the entire stored quantity is still in there, undischarged, and the only thing standing between the liquid and the change is that no seed has arrived.

Fourteen years. Everything from the corridor onward, held below the point, undischarged, waiting on a seed that never came because he had spent fourteen years making certain that nothing got close enough to be one.

Three assessments had recorded the same unexplained inefficiency in his line and none of them had found the cause. The cause was that a supercooled thing is indistinguishable from a cold thing at the surface, and no instrument the Court owns can tell you what a soul is holding that it has not yet spent.

The cistern was the seed.

Recalescence.

It went through him at a speed no decision could have set. The front started at the Soul Crystal and propagated outward and it did not travel evenly, it went in branches, dendrites, running out along the paths that were already there, and the paths that were already there were the avenues of his arms. Left first, then right, then both together to the fingers. He felt the front go under the skin of the forearm and reach the wrist and split at the wrist and go into every finger separately, and where it passed, the thing behind it was no longer the thing that had been there.

And it got warmer.

That was the part he could not have predicted and did not have time to be astonished by. The whole latent quantity that half a life had been holding came back out at the moment of the change, all of it, faster than anything could carry it away, and Sodoku Moto knelt in the ice at the ninth hour of the worst day of his life and steamed.

The ice around both knees went. Four feet of it, to the mud, in under two seconds, and the steam came off him and stood, because there was no wind in that valley and had not been since the aurora.

Every man still upright inside sixty feet turned to look. Some of them had been fighting eleven seconds earlier. None of them started again.

---

There was a bill and he paid it while the front was still running.

Gisei does not ask twice and it does not accept an offer a man was already willing to make. The Plane reads sincerity and it cannot be deceived, because the practitioner's own grief at the loss is what does the conversion, and a thing surrendered lightly produces a flicker.

He had almost nothing left to offer that was worth anything, and the price list is short and he had read it that morning in a place with no ground in it. Memories. Bonds severed at the Attraction Layer. Capacities relinquished out of the architecture. Traits dissolved back into the years and the suffering that crystallised them.

The girl was gone and could not be spent. Yoko's hand was on his arm and was the one bond remaining at that magnitude, and he did not offer it and would not have, and the reason was no longer a virtue. It was that the offering had to be something he could actually hand over, and he could not have got that one out of his chest with both hands.

So he gave the kitchen.

The bowl against her hip and her forearm around it. The other hand stirring. The thing she had said about his whereabouts, without looking up, and his agreeing to it because it was sensible. The particular light in that room, which had come in low from the left because the window faced the water. All of it, offered whole, the last complete and undamaged thing he owned about Freya Moto, the one he had gone to in the dark for twelve years as a man goes to a fire.

The Plane took it at the rate the loss deserved, which was very high, because Oblation converts identity into intensity and the exchange runs on what the surrender costs rather than on what the surrendered thing weighs. A meal produces a flicker. A kitchen produces enough to stand a man up.

He felt the conversion happen and did not feel what left. That is how it is built. The output arrived in his hands and the room went out of him at the same instant, and afterward there was a shape where the kitchen had been and no contents in the shape, and he would go looking for it that night and find the shape and not understand what was supposed to be in it.

He would never understand. That is the price and it does not come back and there is no bench that hears the appeal.

---

The sky cracked.

Sodoku got his head up in time to see it and what he saw was not lightning and had no path in it. The black over the narrows, the black that had been over Kharven since the aurora came down, went to fractures from horizon to horizon, and through the fractures came the strands.

They came down.

The Plane itself, presenting, with no light in any of it, and where they crossed the boundary into the Material they resolved and split, because a thing with an axis in it does that to what passes through, and every strand arrived in two rays travelling at different speeds and separated into its colours down its whole length. Green in one and gold beside it and a violet under both, running, moving along the strand as it came.

The first of them went down into the Ashgate valley, four miles west, to a point Sodoku could locate exactly and had no way to reach.

He knew whose point it was. He had known since the whistle and had put it away twice, and he put it away a third time now, because there was a thing coming down out of the sky into his brother and it was not his to look at and he had never in his life been given anything he could do about that man.

The rest came to him, and there were more of them than he could have counted with the Ledger running.

They came into the shoulders and the crown and the back of both hands and they went in without resistance, because there was nothing at the surface of him left to resist with, and the crystallisation front reached the last joint of the last finger of his right hand at the moment the first strand landed.

Sodoku Moto put one hand flat in the melted mud, and got his boot under him, and stood up out of the steam with Yoko Mishiro's hand still closed on his arm.

# The Seven, and the Price of Each

The axe head had gone in past the beard of the blade and the ash had closed over it without a sound, and what came up out of the wound in the ground was the smell of a struck flint held under the nose, hot and mineral and gone almost before it registered. Darius felt it on the roof of his mouth rather than in his nose. The haft stood upright between the big man's heels, and the burial linen wound around it settled by a quarter turn under its own weight, and the noise that made was the only noise in the whole of that country.

Then Gimbzo got up.

He came up out of the cross-legged seat without using his hands, which at that mass should not have been possible and was, and the ash under him did not release him so much as let him go. The two grey uprights of the gate had read as a doorway while a seated shape occupied the space between them. Standing, he reduced them to a pair of reference stakes somebody had driven either side of him, and Darius revised, and revised again, and stopped revising at somewhere past eight feet because there was no further use in the number.

The crest of bone-white hair cleared the crossbeam of the branch shadow. The gut hung and the shoulders above it were a headland. Everything Darius had catalogued while the man was sitting had been an underestimate assembled by a Measurewright at the bottom of a scale, and the correction arrived all at once, in the body, ahead of the intellect.

Dust in the six feet around him stopped falling at half rate and stopped falling at all. It hung. Then it resumed, at the ordinary pace, everywhere except directly beneath his hands.

[Gimbzo] Sixty-one of them through that gate and this is the first one who counted me twice going up. [narrator] Gimbzo rolled one shoulder and the beard came after his jaw a beat late.

[Gimbzo] "I'll teach you," [narrator] Gimbzo said.

Darius stood in the ash with his ribs not hurting and did not answer immediately, which was a habit that had cost him two friendships and saved his life once.

[Darius] "Under what authority."

[Gimbzo] [laugh] "HOO." [narrator] The laugh went through the uprights and came back off the trunk. [Gimbzo] "There it is. Four hundred men and a fifth fracture and a coat with the threads picked off and the first thing out of him is a question about standing." [narrator] Gimbzo put a palm on the axe haft, casually, the way another man leans on a fence. [Gimbzo] "None. I hold no writ. I sit in a gate. If you want a charter you'll be a long time looking and you'll find that the thing you're standing on doesn't issue them."

[Darius] "Then why."

[Gimbzo] "Because you're in the wood and the wood keeps what it keeps, and a post that can read is worth more than a post that can't." [narrator] He said it flatly. [Gimbzo] "And because I've watched sixty-one of you come through and I've taught four, and the four were the ones who asked that question first instead of asking what it does. Don't thank me for it. It's a sorting, not a kindness."

Above them the chisel went tock.

[Gimbzo] "This training won't be easy," [narrator] Gimbzo said. [Gimbzo] "You'll go through the labors one by one. There's seven and there's no order that suits everybody, and the one that suits you is Balance, because your soul already understands judgement and Balance is the labor judgement was cut out of. It'll come to you like a language you spoke as a child and forgot. It's the only one that will."

[Darius] "Balance."

[Gimbzo] "Aye."

Darius heard his own pulse in his ears, which had not happened since he was thirty and which he had assumed the Plane had also taken away from him along with the ribs and the knuckle.

Urion is the Balancer of Scales. Sel-Ur sits in a warm windowless room and returns weight in a grammar, and eleven hundred years of doctrine and four grades and eleven procedural firewalls and every writ his order had ever carried out into a village and executed on a person rested on the proposition that the grammar was one word wide. Epsilon had catalogued the pattern across three centuries and reached the only conclusion available and sealed it. Darius had read it. Darius had held a door open in his own Crystal so it could not be taken off him.

And the man in the gate had just handed him the same finding as a curriculum, in the tone of a foreman assigning a work order, with the phrasing of somebody who had never in his existence considered that it might be news.

[Darius] "Name them," [narrator] Darius said.

[Gimbzo] "Ruin, which clears what has to end. Purity, which sets what has to stand. Spirit, which moves what has to cross. Sequence, which puts things in the order they go in. Knowledge, which says what a thing is. Balance, which keeps two things that hate each other from finishing the argument." [narrator] He tipped his chin up the trunk. [Gimbzo] "And the seventh, which holds the other six in one house so the house is one house. That one you don't learn. That one learns you, and I've seen it happen twice, and both times I dug what was left out of the root and Kizami cut the column."

[Darius] "Zentai-sei no Hataraki."

[Gimbzo] "Is that what it's called now." [narrator] Gimbzo did not appear either impressed or annoyed. [Gimbzo] "It's had names. Three of them ended in a word that meant labor and two ended in a word that meant task and one of the seven has a name that isn't in the same tongue as the other six at all, which tells you the age it got hung on it. I don't use them. I was here before the mouth that made them."

[Kizami] "Baransu," [narrator] said Kizami from up the trunk, without stopping. Snap. Twist. Curl. [Kizami] "The odd one is Balance. Loaned in. Everyone notices and no one says."

[Gimbzo] "Nobody asked you."

[Kizami] "Nobody asks me anything and I am the only one keeping a record."

[Kizami] Four hundred and six years since anyone said the names aloud, and this one gets the order wrong, and I correct him, and neither of them thanks me. [narrator] Kizami's chisel came down.

Darius put a hand out and steadied himself on nothing, and stopped himself, and let the hand fall.

[Darius] "You've said Ruin first."

[Gimbzo] "That's the order it goes in."

[Darius] "Sancta Lux teaches that Balance is the floor and that the rest are branches off it."

[Gimbzo] "Then Sancta Lux is a house that taught itself the roof was the foundation because the roof was the part it could see." [narrator] Gimbzo turned the axe a quarter rotation in the ash by the haft and let it settle again. [Gimbzo] "You've got it in your head that the Judger owns one of these. That he stands for Balance and the other six belong to somebody else and your lot have been trespassing without knowing it. Aye?"

[Darius] "That is the finding, yes."

[Gimbzo] "It's a falsified idea and I'd like to know which of you started it, because it's been repeated to me by every one of you that's come through here and not one of you could tell me the source." [narrator] He said it without heat. He said almost everything without heat, which made the flat delivery worse than shouting. [Gimbzo] "The Judger understands all of them. All seven. He understands Ruin the same as he understands Balance and he understands it first, because before creation comes destruction, and anybody who has ever cleared a field or torn out a rotten sill or buried a village knows the order of that and has known it since before there was a word for either half."

The wind that did not exist did not move anything.

[Gimbzo] "Ruin clears what must end," [narrator] Gimbzo said. [Gimbzo] "Purity sets what must remain. You cannot set a thing where a thing already stands. Every act of making that has ever been performed in any world by anybody was performed in a space that had to be emptied first, and the emptying is a labor, and it is a sacred one, and your order has spent eleven hundred years using the word for it as an accusation." [narrator] He leaned on the haft. [Gimbzo] "You burned four hundred men at a gate and then you spent thirty-five years building a machine to make sure nobody could ever do that again. That's your whole life. And your whole life is you deciding that one of the seven labors of the world is a crime."

Darius's jaw set. He was aware of it setting and could not stop it.

[Darius] "They were four hundred men."

[Gimbzo] "I know what they were. I'm not telling you it was righteous. I'm telling you it was a Work, and you've been calling it a sin for thirty-five years because that's easier than carrying it, and the machine you built is a machine for making sure nobody ever has to carry one again." [narrator] Gimbzo's small sunk eyes did not move off him. [Gimbzo] "You didn't build eleven firewalls to protect the world from another Eclipse Bastion. You built them so that the next time it needed doing there'd be a procedure to blame."

[Darius] "That is not."

[Gimbzo] "Say the rest of it."

[Darius] "That is not what it is."

[Gimbzo] "Then say what it is." [narrator] He waited. The waiting had the quality of ground. [Gimbzo: long beat] "You've had thirty-five years and three tribunals and you've never once answered the question you were asked. I've got nothing to do and I'm not going anywhere. Take your time."

Darius did not answer.

The tock came down out of the branches, and a curl of grey bark separated from column two hundred and eleven, and it fell slowly and it fell straight and it landed against the toe of his boot beside the first one.

[Gimbzo] "HOO," [narrator] Gimbzo said, and there was no laugh in it at all this time. [Gimbzo] "I'll tell you what I actually cannot credit. Not the burning. Men burn men. That's older than the wood and it'll outlast it." [narrator] He hefted the axe out of the ash with two fingers and let the head hang. [Gimbzo] "It's the title. The Will of the Judger. You people came up with that. Somebody sat in a room with a quill and decided that the martial half of a measuring house would be called the Will, and everybody in the room nodded, and eleven hundred years of you have been saying it out loud with a straight face ever since."

[Darius] "It is a formal office."

[Gimbzo] "It's an unbelievable piece of naming and I want you to hear why." [narrator] The axe head came up and pointed, and it was not a threat and it landed harder than a threat. [Gimbzo] "A will is the part of a thing that turns wanting into doing. That's all it is. That's all it has ever been. A man's will is the hinge between the reading and the act, and you were handed the office named for that hinge, and the first thing you did with it was write eleven rules to make sure the hinge could never move without four other men's hands on it."

[Darius] "That was the point."

[Gimbzo] "Aye, it was. And then you refused the investiture on top of that, six years running, so the hinge doesn't even have a man in it." [narrator] Gimbzo let the axe head drop back down. [Gimbzo] "You're the Will of the Judger and you have not willed one thing since you were thirty-five years old. If you are truly the Judger's will then you are pathetic. Not wicked. I've had wicked through this gate and wicked at least does something. Pathetic. A hinge that has bolted itself shut and calls it architecture."

Darius stood there in a grey coat with no grade markings on the collar, an old man with four fracture lines running through a Soul Crystal that would not survive a fifth, and he found that his hands had closed.

[Darius] "You do not know what I have done."

[Gimbzo] "I know what you've filed."

[Darius] "I held a door open in my own Crystal for the length of a decision that a Measurewright would not survive attempting, and I did it alone, without an institution, without a Ladder, without one procedural firewall between me and the consequence, and it is the reason my ribs do not hurt and the reason I am standing in your ash." [narrator] His voice had risen. It had not risen in a tribunal in twenty years. [Darius: loud] "That was a will. That was the only one I have permitted myself in thirty-five years and I paid for it in the only currency I have left, and you may call it pathetic when you have told me what you have willed since somebody put you in the ground on a salt flat."

The silence that followed had weight in it.

Kizami stopped cutting.

[Kizami] Oh. [narrator] The chisel hung a quarter inch off the bark. [Kizami] Oh, this one is going to be interesting or he is going to be in the root pile inside the hour.

Gimbzo looked at him for a long time.

[Gimbzo] "There he is," [narrator] he said.

Darius's hands were still closed.

[Gimbzo] "That's the first true thing you've said since you landed, and you had to be insulted into it, and I'd have got there quicker with an axe but the axe doesn't take." [narrator] Gimbzo set the haft back down in the ash. [Gimbzo] "Good. Hold on to it. You'll need it, because the terms are ugly and I'd rather give them to a man who's angry than a man who's grateful."

[Darius] "Give them."

[Gimbzo] "Seven labors. You learn them one at a time and you learn them here, because they aren't learned anywhere else and they never have been. Every hour you're in the wood the coordinate takes another knuckle. You came in at the ankle. Balance alone will put you at the knee." [narrator] He said the numbers the way a mason says numbers. [Gimbzo] "And every Work that seats in a man opens him. Nobody is punishing you and I am charging you nothing. That is what seating means. A thing that large does not go into a soul already full without first making the room."

Darius understood before the sentence finished.

[Darius] "Fractures."

[Gimbzo] "Aye."

[Darius] "I have four."

[Gimbzo] "You have four."

[Darius] "A fifth kills the Crystal."

[Gimbzo] "It does."

[Darius] "Then the arithmetic does not work." [narrator] Darius's voice had gone flat and precise, which was what it did when he was frightened, and three tribunals had mistaken it for composure. [Darius] "Seven labors. One fracture. There is no version of that where I complete the training."

[Gimbzo] "None," [narrator] Gimbzo agreed.

[Kizami] "Five is the root pile," [narrator] Kizami said helpfully. [Kizami] "I have seven in the root pile. There is no room in it. I have mentioned this."

[Darius] "Then what am I being offered."

[Gimbzo] "One." [narrator] Gimbzo's grey-iron eyes had not left him. [Gimbzo] "You're being offered one, Weighing Man. Balance, because it's the one that fits you, and because it's the one your order has been standing on for eleven hundred years without ever once knowing what it was standing on. You'll seat it, and it'll take the fifth, and your Crystal will not survive that and you and I both know it, and what you'll have between the seating and the ending is a stretch of time I cannot measure for you because nobody has ever done it in that order before."

[Darius] "How long."

[Gimbzo] "Don't ask me that. I'll tell you the truth and the truth is I don't know, and a number I don't know is worse to a man like you than no number at all."

The ash held. Somewhere three hundred feet up the branches stopped being branches and became directions, and the columns ran down the grey bark in a hand no living scholar could read, and every one of them was full.

[Darius] "And if I walk out," [narrator] Darius said. [Darius] "Today. Ankle-deep. Limp for a season and dream of a tree."

[Gimbzo] "Then you go back with four fractures and a coat and the Seventh Objection in your fist, and you tell three men in a sealed vault what you've seen, and they weigh it, and they file it, same as everything else, and in nine hundred years somebody else lands on my path." [narrator] Gimbzo shrugged one slab shoulder. [Gimbzo] "That's a real option and it's the sane one and I'll open the gate for it and I won't say another word to you. I've opened it for eleven. Nine of them took it. I don't hold it against a man."

[Darius] "And the other two."

[Gimbzo] "Are the columns you're standing under."

Darius looked down at his boots.

Two curls of grey bark lay against the toe of the left one, cut from column two hundred and eleven by a chisel-jawed clerk with an opinion about queue order, and beneath them the packed ash held the print of an axe head driven in to the beard of the blade and pulled back out. The print was clean. It had not filled.

He thought about a warm windowless room with two lamps in fixed rotational relation. He thought about the reverse of every Measurewright's writ, where a sentence attributed to him had been printed for thirty-five years. He thought about the Word of Judger sitting in Altherion with the clasp of the indigo mantle hanging loose against his chest, and about the vote neither of them had cast, and about how each of them had spent six years arranging his affairs on the belief that the other one would move first.

Darius stepped forward and set both boots into the print the axe had made, and put his weight down, and the ash came up over the welt and closed around the leather and did not let go.

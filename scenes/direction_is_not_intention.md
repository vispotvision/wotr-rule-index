# Direction Is Not Intention

*The quarry stair above Coldwater, and the lip, and the dawn after.*
*POV: Ruvaen Aloreth. RP reply. Dova'Kan, Gorgi and Xmere are not written; their beats stay open.*

---

## I. ELEVEN FLIGHTS

The stair went up in eleven flights and he took them at the pace of the man behind him, which was slower than his own and not by much.

Chalk under the boot is a poor tread. It wears to a dish in the middle and then it wears through, and Brannoch Skell had been cutting new noses into these flights for eleven years and had got to the seventh and stopped, so the top four were bowl-shaped and slick with dust. The rail was iron and cold and pitted and came away on the palm as orange grit. Below them the beck ran in the leat and the sound of it dropped off with each turn.

He said none of that out loud. There was a man on the stair behind him who had thrown a cane into the grass about forty minutes ago and there was no version of *mind the seventh* that did not arrive as something else.

*I am being respectful, and I want the entry to record that I know the difference between being respectful and caring.*

He did not care. That was the shape of it and there was no point dressing it.

A hundred and forty miles of frontier and four hundred spears and eighty-one people over a ridge with their pigs, and nine animals in a pass a hundred and forty miles north that had come into a war they had no part in and had been taken apart with purpose-built ironmongery. He could hold both of those and feel the exact weight of each, and the weights were not the same, and pretending otherwise in front of a dragon would have been a lie a dragon could smell.

They had crossed into his lands. Things that cross into a land in a war year get killed in it. That is not cruelty and it is not policy. It is the arithmetic of a border with an army on the other side of it, and he had been doing that arithmetic since he was thirty-one.

He answered the easy things first, going up, over his shoulder, in the order they had been given.

"The seam stays where it is. Nobody refines it. I will have a test quantity cut, and it will be small, and it will be cut with a hand pick and not with powder because I do not know what powder does to it and neither do you. Chalk alone, iron-stone alone, the glass alone, and then the mixture, and each one against a different margin so that a result means something." He took the fourth turn. "And I will write down which of you said which part, because if this works I am not having the Court attribute it to me."

"The Measurewright. You are right and it costs me something and I am going to say what.

"I had intended to send her where I wanted her, because where I want her is where the leak shows. Blind her to the sites and I lose that. She measures honestly either way, so the readings are the same readings. What I lose is knowing which of her sheets somebody at Velthaeir cared about, because if she does not know which ground matters then neither does whoever reads her post."

He went up two more treads.

"I will do it your way. Honest baselines are worth more to you than a name is worth to me this month, and I can get the name another road."

*And I will be four months slower getting it, and in four months he will have come over the Ollavaeth line twice.*

---

## II. ALTITUDE

"Altitude I cannot give you and you should know why before you ask twice.

"Everything I have is ground. The Cliff Watch sits at four hundred feet on chalk and that is the highest fixed instrument in the Principality and it is a man with a glass, not a plate. Balloons are a Vessel Coast novelty and they go up in still air, which is not the air a wyvern flies in. I have nothing that takes a reading at height and stays there long enough for the reading to be worth anything."

He stopped on the seventh landing, because the seventh is where the seam runs out of the chalk and there is a black band four feet wide in the white and a man can put his hand flat on it.

"There is exactly one altitude instrument on this continent. Velthaeir is a floating archive-city. It has carried a Measurewright corps at height for four hundred years and it has the only continuous atmospheric series anybody has ever taken.

"It is also the institution I told you an hour ago I believe has sold us."

He put his hand on the black band and took it away again with the dust on it.

"So there is your bind, and it is mine and not yours. The only people who can answer Gorgi's question are the people I cannot ask without telling them what the question is. I have had that in front of me since the fourth turn. No way round it yet."

---

## III. WHERE AN OUTSIDE NATION SITS

"Now the one you are not going to like, and I would rather you had it on a stair than at the top."

He kept climbing while he said it, and did not turn round, because it was easier to say to the chalk.

"If they are being used as tools, that was not at my request. I do not care whether they are. If they come over the Ollavaeth line in front of Korvaeth infantry, I will kill them, and I will not regret it, and I am telling you that now and not afterward so that nobody can say they were not told.

"I value my citizens. Every one of them, including the eighty-one over that ridge and the quarryman who fired four barrels at a thing he could not hurt. I will do whatever I judge necessary for them and I will not consult you about it.

"And you should understand where you are standing. You are an outside nation in somebody else's war. Not a neighbour and not an ally, and the moment you put your weight on either side of this you stop being a third party to everyone who is watching, and the whole of the New World is watching. There are eleven houses on my own side who will read a dragon on my common as Eresse buying foreign strength. There are more on hers who will read it as proof of everything she has been saying about me for a decade.

"I am not asking you to leave. I am telling you what the room looks like from the floor of it.

"I have no idea why the wyverns came here. I do not care why they came here. That is not indifference to them, it is an accurate statement of what a man has room for in the eleventh year of a suppression." The tenth turn. "You may run your investigation on my ground and I will feed it, and I meant every word of what I have given you and there will be more. Only do not get in my way.

"I will not rest until the elven nations bow to the true heir."

Behind and below, the water in the leat.

---

## IV. WHAT SHE WOULD HAVE SAID

*She would have hated every syllable of that.*

It came up on the eleventh flight, uninvited, the way it did.

His sister had run a court that convened over a misplaced witness and had spent the first two years of her reign trying to get an Accord article amended so that a chartered holding could not be starved in a siege. She had argued it four times and lost four times. She had thought that animals in a war were the clearest case in the whole of jurisprudence, because a thing that cannot be told what the war is about cannot be a party to it, and a thing that cannot be a party to it cannot be an enemy.

She had put it to the Sunroot Court in exactly those words. It is in the minute. He has read the minute perhaps nine times. He could recite the clause. He will not.

*Innocent in nature. That was her phrase and the Court laughed at it, and she went on using it for eleven years, and it is in a book in Velthaeir with her name at the foot of the page.*

*I would kill three hundred of them in a morning if they came up that ridge in front of his infantry, and I would sleep, and she would not have been able to look at me.*

He had thought that thought before and had never once let it change a decision.

That was the whole of what was left of her in him. Not the voice and not the face, both of which had gone somewhere in the fourth or fifth year and had not come back. A clause in a minute, a phrase the Court laughed at, and the flat daily knowledge that the man who held her seat would have disgusted her and was going to hold it anyway.

---

## V. THE TOP, AND WHAT HE CAN HAND OVER

The lip is a spoil shelf about ninety feet across with an iron rail on the seaward side and a wind that does not stop.

He got his boots off again and sat down in the dust with his soles flat on the chalk, which is not a posture any prince in the history of Eresse has adopted in front of a foreign power, and did not explain it.

"I take both conditions, and I will say them back so you know I have them and have not simply nodded at a dragon on a stair.

"If there is no second bearing, I will tell you there is no second bearing. I will not find you a pattern because you came eleven miles for one.

"And direction is not intention. That one I have had put to me before, by a Marshal, about a Korvaeth column, and I ignored him and lost two hundred men at the Hollow Gate. I am not going to make that mistake in front of somebody who warned me about it first."

Then the other thing, and it needed saying before it happened.

"There is a piece of this I can hand to you and I want you to know what it is before it is done to you, because everyone I have ever done it to has been angry with me afterward.

"I cannot show you what I see. That is not the shape of it. What I do is compress my own field, and what I can do instead is open it and hold the shape of it open, and anything inside that volume is standing in a reference I made. A man inside it reads the room against a known quantity instead of against nothing. He does not borrow my eyes. He gets a lit room, and he looks with his own eyes, and what he finds in it is his.

"What comes through is what I am good at, which is wakes and reaches. Not colour and not faces and not anything you could draw. You will get mass and bearing and how hard a thing is pulling and at what.

"And what comes through is also everything I am bad at, which you should have in writing. I have no warning of my own limits any more. If I overdraw while you are inside my reference, you will find that out at the same moment I do, which is to say slightly after it has happened.

"It costs me the next several hours of resolution and I have budgeted that.

"It costs you the grief. Three hundred of them, narrow and clean and all at once, in a skull that has not been trained on it. I have had grown officers put their hands over their faces. If you want it, say so and I will open, and if at any point you want it shut, put a hand on my shoulder and it shuts inside a breath."

He put his palms flat on the chalk on either side of him and looked out over eleven miles of dark green going blue.

"Start with the living. I had already decided that before you said it, and it is better for having been said out loud by somebody who is not from here."

---

## VI. DAWN

The night went the way those nights go, which is slowly and then not at all, and he had the second bearing separated out by the hour before first light, and it was there, and it was not pointed anywhere he had expected.

He did not say so immediately.

He was still holding the shape of it open when the light came up over the eastern forest and put a hard orange edge on four hundred feet of chalk, and in the first minute of that light, something a hundred and forty miles north and east of him began to move.

He felt it the way you feel a table shift under your elbow.

The single reach. The narrow one, the one running harder than three hundred grieving animals, the one with the cooked left side and the debt that could not be tallied. It had been sitting for nine days in one place, and at dawn it came off its anchor and started west, and it did not stop, and behind it there was the rest of the thing.

Six hundred is a number a man can hold as a shape. This was not that.

It arrived in his read as a change in the texture of a hundred and forty miles of country, the way a tide arrives in an estuary, a broad slow front of ordinary appetite moving at the pace of a walking man. He counted nothing. There was nothing to count. There was a bearing and a rate and a width, and the width was the part that made him take his hands off the chalk.

*West. Not south-west toward the Ollavaeth line. West, along the coast road.*

*The Ollavaeth line is a ridge and a wall and eleven years of him breaking his teeth on it, and he has come off it, and he has gone round.*

*The coast road ends at the causeway. The causeway ends at the sea gate. Vaelmarr has four hundred spears on the Landward Wall and a hundred and ten of them are boys, and the Landward Wall faces the wrong way, and Ithuran Melloran is on it with a bad knee and a chart that is four days old.*

*And I am four hundred feet up a chalk face at the far end of my own frontier with my boots off.*

He stood up.

He stood too fast. The lip went sideways and came back. That had never once happened to him in a hundred and forty years, and it told him something about the last nine hours that he did not have time to enter.

Below him the beck ran down the leat into eighty feet of stone course. The four at the top of the common lifted their heads off the water together, because whatever they were standing in had just changed.

Ruvaen Aloreth put his hand flat on the iron rail, took the bearing off the sun with his thumb at arm's length the way a Landward officer takes a bearing when he has no instrument, and held it there while he counted the rate a second time to be certain of it.

Then he turned round and went for his boots.

# Geturo — Ignite, Tide

*Aetherion Academy, the Combat Arena. Geturo Mahuo against Taesyn.*

---

Geturo let him finish.

The sand had kept the afternoon in it. It came up warm through the soles of his boots, and the arena smelled of raked grit and scorched stone, and under both of those faintly of hot metal where the burn-hole had been filled and tamped. Above him the dome ticked as it cooled. Along the broken seam the ward-glass held its flat fish-eye cloud, and his father's upturned hand held the seam.

"All right," Geturo said. "No favours."

He didn't look like a fighter. He knew that. Hiromi called things and they came, and the gallery leaned forward when Hiromi stepped onto sand. Nobody leaned forward for Geturo. He had never had the draw for it. What he had was evenings: a week of them, at the desk under the dormitory window, laying Essence into ink a line at a time until the paper held exactly as much as the glyph could use and not a drop more. His brother fought. Geturo arrived prepared, and then drafted whatever the preparation had missed.

His hand went inside his coat to the third pocket of the bandolier and came out with a single sheet of rag paper, folded twice. Lead in the crease gave it weight. To anyone in the gallery it would have looked like a note passed in class. Two glyphs sat on it in iron-gall ink, brown-black and dull, one on each face of the fold. [Ig]. [Let]. The ink looked like ink. That was the point. Someone who knew what to look for might have thought the drafting was Mu-jin's own, and Geturo had copied the hand from his father's margins for four years until his wrist forgot it had ever written any other way.

"Ignite."

He threw it underhand. The lead carried it flat and low across the sand, and it landed halfway between them, face down, and lay there.

Nothing happened. A voice in the gallery started to laugh and stopped.

[Ig] does not burn when it is told to. It waits for the outcome it was drafted around, and Geturo had drafted it around contact. The paper had to touch ground, and lie still, and stop being a thing in flight. When it had done all three, the glyph took. There was no flame. A line of orange ran through the ink and only the ink, tracing the glyph exactly, bright as a filament and no wider than the pen had been. The paper around it did not catch. The heat stayed pinned where he had pinned it, one fixed temperature held at one fixed surface, and it poured down into the sand.

The sand took it and gave it to the air.

He watched it happen the way Mu-jin had taught him to watch a flask, from the side and without blinking. A hand's height of air over the patch went thin. Hot air is lighter than cool air, and light moves through thin air faster than through thick, so a ray crossing the patch low bent upward, away from the heat and toward the cooler air above. His father had drawn it for him once on the back of an assay slip, in a single curved line. What it meant, on a field, was that everything seen across the patch was seen along a bent path.

Taesyn wavered where he stood.

It was the light coming off him that bent. The lower half of him stretched and lagged a beat behind the upper, and the sand at his feet rose into a sheet of bright sky that wasn't there, and the far wall of the arena behind him bent inward at the base as if the stone were being drawn toward the paper. Where the air over the patch churned, the images broke and stacked and pulled apart from themselves, two Taesyns, then one and a half, then one too tall. Sailors on the long hauls had a name for it when coastlines did that over cold water. Over hot sand, Accord surveyors logged it as an instrument error.

That was the first glyph. It was only light.

"Tide."

[Let] woke on the underside of the fold.

The second glyph did not heat anything. It kept time. A pulse went out from the paper, low and regular, and he felt it before he saw it, a small tug under his breastbone on the beat and a release after it, the way a ship's deck lifts and settles. This one answered to charge. The arena sand had been raked an hour ago, and raking grinds grain against grain until the fine fraction carries charge the way a wool sleeve does in dry weather. On the beat, the field lifted the finest of it. Off the beat, it dropped.

The ground moved.

It began at the paper and walked outward in bands, each one a finger high, a skin of lifted grit that rose and fell and rose again a little further on. It was slow. The Warden had allowed nothing fast in a ring with half a dome, so Geturo had built something that could not be fast if it tried. The bands came on the way water comes in across flats. Each pulse reached a hand's breadth past the last. Seen through the shimmer, they stood higher than they were and curved up at their leading edge like a wave about to break. Nobody in the gallery was laughing now.

The light was a trick and the tide was real, and the one thing Geturo had actually drafted it to do could not be seen at all.

[Let] draws on whatever in its reach runs hottest. The rhythm pulls, and what it pulls is Essence, spent too hard and too fast through a Shell. A slow hand inside that field kept everything it had. A hand that reached for its reserve would feel that reserve start to pour out through the soles of its feet, on the beat, and pour faster the harder it reached. His father had spent ten minutes telling them not to gamble on what the other person wouldn't use. Geturo had brought a field that made the other person pay for whatever they did.

It took from him too. On every beat the tug under his breastbone came again, because he was standing within the field's reach and he was the one feeding the ink. He kept his own draw low and even and counted, one, and, two, and, breathing on the release. He had never had much of a draw, so he had drafted a working that punished anyone who did.

Overhead, on the fourth beat, the seam in the dome flickered.

It was faint, a dimming along the fish-eye glass that came and went. Geturo marked it as the shimmer again, heat off the patch bending his view of the dome the way it bent everything else, and let it go.

The fifth band reached Taesyn.

It came up over the toes of his boots, a skin of lifted grit, and settled against the leather on the release, and lifted again on the beat. Behind it the paper lay face down in the middle of the field, its glyph a thin bright wire. The ink had begun to eat toward the crease.

### Phenomenon lines (pending ruling)

**[Ig], the shimmer.**
- Real phenomenon: the inferior mirage; over a turbulent hot layer, image stacking and stretching as in a Fata Morgana.
- Law: air's refractive index rises with density; a ray crossing a steep vertical temperature gradient bends toward the cooler, denser air.
- Boundary condition: a single surface temperature at the ink line only; gated on a chosen outcome (contact: land, lie still, stop being in flight).
- Fault: only low-angle sightlines across the patch bend; wind or cold water flattens the gradient; stepping onto the patch means stepping onto heat.
- Cost: paid in advance in the week of evenings laying Essence into the ink. The ink burning toward the crease is the clock.

**[Let], the tide.**
- Real phenomena: triboelectric charging of raked sand; electrostatic lofting of charged grains; driven in pulses, a periodic saltation front.
- Codex use (the real point): the rhythm siphons overcharged Essence. A slow, cheap hand keeps everything; a hand that reaches for its reserve bleeds on the beat.
- Boundary condition: the rhythm, a fixed period.
- Fault: takes from whoever runs hottest in reach, the caster included. It also points upward: the largest Essence spend in the building is Mu-jin holding the dome seam. The flicker on the fourth beat is unexplained; Geturo misreads it as shimmer. Whether it's the tide pulling at the seam is Isaac's call.
- Cost: Geturo's Shell, a tug under the breastbone every beat. Tempering-limited.
- The Warden's "nothing fast" shaped the working.

**Precedent:** Naruto's explosive tags, FMA's circles. Diverges: the tag doesn't explode; it changes the rules of the ground and waits.

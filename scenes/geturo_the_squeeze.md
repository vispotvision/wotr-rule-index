# Geturo: The Squeeze

This was a fight about hands, Geturo decided, and about what you had done with them the night before.

He was the more experienced of the two. He told himself so and let it stand, because it was at least half true. He had been at his father's bench since he was nine, and most of what he could do this afternoon he had already done weeks ago over a low flame, and stoppered, and sewn into his coat. Anyone who had to build answers live on this sand would pay the Tide its cut for every one. Geturo carried most of his in a canvas bandolier.

The Tide was running. Six bands in now, the lifted grit coming across the ring in low brown ridges toward the iron-gall paper staked at its centre, each ridge hissing over the sand and pulling at whatever burned brightest inside the people it crossed. It pulled at Geturo hardly at all. He was spending hardly anything. The heat off the paper put a shiver in the air between them, and Taesyn swam in and out of true through it.

He unbuttoned the coat to the third button and took vial one out of its loop.

Modulation came first. The combat alchemists in the Division used the word the way a smith says *temper*, as if everyone knew, and what it meant on a floor like this was a node: one working bound into one small thing, so the thing could be thrown and the working would go where it went. His father's nodes were worked glass the length of a finger. Geturo's were the width of his thumb, and he had blown two of them wrong before he got one that held.

He broke the wax with his thumbnail.

The air came in. It came with a thin rising whistle and then no sound at all, and every loose thing within an arm's reach leaned toward his hand: the grit at his boots skated a finger's width, the cuffs of his sleeves drew tight, the short hair at his temples lifted and pointed. The Fixation in the glass took the air around it and drew it down into a shell the size of a plum and held it there, fixed, the volatile made still in the old books' sense of the word. He could see the shell. Air packed that tight bent the light, so the vial sat inside a clear lens of its own weather. It was heavy in his palm. A whole cask of air had gone into a space he could close his fist around, and a cask of air weighs about what a good loaf does. It was hot as well, the way air always goes hot when you crush it, and he held it by the brass wire twisted round its neck.

That was one. He wanted four.

The copying was harder, and it was live. He slid three empty vials from loops two, four and six, uncapped them with his teeth one at a time, and spat each stopper into his other hand. Each held a supersaturated liquor, clear as gin and sitting just short of crystallising. A solution like that wants to set. Touch it with a seed and it copies the seed's lattice and sets in that shape, and nothing else. His father called this step Multiplicatio and called it, in the same breath, the least honest stage of the old Work, because the copy was only ever as true as the seed. Geturo set the node's glass against the first empty vial, lip to lip, and read it.

Reading was the part he was good at. It was holding a sum in his head while someone shouted numbers at him. The shape of the Fixation went across into the liquor, the liquor went cloudy and then glass-clear, and the air around the second vial whistled in and fixed.

The Tide bit him for that. It was a low tug under the sternum, a hand pulling him forward by the shirt, and it left the taste of tin on his back teeth.

He did the next two against the original as well. Never against a copy. A copy of a copy came out soft, the way a key cut from a cut key sticks in the lock, and he had learned that at eleven with a node that let go in his lap. Vial four held a different liquor, one that set in the seed's shape but left one face of the shell open. Vial six held nothing at all but a crust of iron-gall sediment scraped from the same batch as the paper, and when the Fixation took in that one it did not draw air. It drew grit. The sand at his feet streamed up into it in a brown thread and packed into a ball that ticked faintly as the grains ground against each other.

Two tugs under the sternum. Something began to ache behind his left eye.

Four nodes. He shook his fingers loose and looked at where Taesyn was not quite standing.

The throw was sidearm, low, off the back foot, and he rolled his wrist over at the release so the vial left his fingers spinning hard. Dexterity was one of the few things about him nobody had ever bothered to argue with. The node went out at hip height and began, a few paces out, to hook. A spinning shell drags the air round with it, piling it up on one side and thinning it on the other, and the ball goes toward the thin side. Every cricket bowler in the Concord knew that in his wrist without knowing a word of the reason. The node passed a hand's width wide of Taesyn's hip, curled in behind him, and dropped onto the iron-gall paper at the centre of the ring with the flat clink of a dropped spoon.

The second line in the stopper broke against the stake. The shell did not burst. It bled.

What happened next happened in three parts, and he felt all three. The fixed air let go slowly, a cask's worth breathing out across the paper. Then the next Tide pulse came through and took the air as well as everything else, because a pull toward a point pulls whatever is loosest, and nothing on that floor was looser than air coming free. In Geturo's Crystal the cost was already paid. The wax had broken on work bought at the bench a fortnight ago, and the Tide found almost nothing on him to take. And the Fixation, set down on the paper, stopped being a shell and became a bias, so the whole field now leaned toward holding what came into it.

A pull toward a point, held steady on air, is what a world does to keep its sky. Stack air toward the centre and the air nearest the centre carries the weight of all the rest.

The bands changed. They stopped being ridges of grit and became wind, a low brown wind running inward from every side of the ring at once, and it did not blow out again. The sand went hard and smooth under it. The mirage over the paper, which had shivered, now bent: air that dense bends light harder, and the ring behind Taesyn swung out of true and stayed swung. Sound changed too. The hiss of the grit came up thicker and closer, and when a porter shouted something at the far end the words arrived fat and wrong.

Geturo did the sum while the second node was still in his hand. By the third pulse, at Taesyn's distance from the paper, the squeeze would be that of a pearl diver ten fathoms down. Ears first. Then the sinuses. Then the ribs.

He threw the second node flat and fast along the same curve, the other side of the paper, to keep feeding it.

At the ring's edge the warden's gauge needle climbed. She saw it, said something short and filthy, and took a step off the boards.

Mu-jin raised two fingers of his right hand without looking round. His left stayed palm up at his side. The warden stopped with one boot on the sand.

Behind her the lanky porter had leaned on his rake to watch. He was long in the shin and the neck and hollow in the chest, twenty perhaps, with straw-coloured hair that stood up at the crown in a cowlick nobody had ever won against, a narrow freckled face and a front tooth chipped at the corner. His porter's smock was two sizes wide and belted with twine, the hem gone black with trench mud, and a column of chalk tallies ran down the ash handle of the rake under his hand.

"Two to one on the quiet one, Warden," he said. "If you're buying."

"Rake," said the warden.

He raked.

Up at the lower tunnel mouth the girl with the silver cord had come back, and she had brought a taller boy with her who carried no card at all and only watched. Geturo registered them the way he registered the exits.

He thought about his feet. The mirage could lie about his shoulders all day, but the ground would tell the truth about where his weight went down to anyone who thought to look at it. So Geturo stopped giving the ground anything to say. He took his next step on the crest of a passing band, where the inward wind was carrying sand over and filling whatever it crossed, and the one after that on the next crest. His prints smoothed shut behind him almost as fast as he made them. It meant moving on the Tide's time instead of his own. He did not mind. He was used to that.

The ache behind his eye had settled in and made itself comfortable. His fingertips had gone cold.

He took vial four in his right hand. The open face of its shell was a small hard gust against his palm, air fixed on every side but one and pushing out through that one, and he held it with the gap turned toward his own wrist. He threw it high. A lob, no faster than a stone from a boy's hand, well under the dome and nowhere near the east wall, so the warden could keep her boot where it was. At the top of the arc it turned over. The open face came round toward the sky and the air in the shell went out through it all at once, and the vial kicked down and sideways as if a boot had caught it, falling now toward the sand a pace behind Taesyn's heels.

Geturo was already reaching into his coat for the fifth vial, the one that was always warm, and his thumb was on the wax.

## Author notes

**Scene.** Geturo vs Taesyn. POV Geturo, close. Taesyn is not written; everything touching him is environment.

**Originated, pending ruling.**
- **Modulation / the node** (Isaac's term). One working bound into a thumb-width vial. Hermetic: Fixation in the alchemical sense. Physics: air drawn in and held as a dense shell (Boyle's law; compression heats; dense air refracts). Two stopper lines: the first forms the shell, the second releases it.
- **Multiplicatio** (Isaac's duplication, named). Seeded crystallisation: a supersaturated liquor copies the seed's lattice. Transmogrification rule: the copy takes the seed's form, its matter is the liquor's, so different liquors give different effects. Fault: a copy of a copy comes out soft. Cost: live, so the Tide bites (sternum tug, tin taste, ache behind the left eye, cold fingertips).
- **Clones.** Two true air nodes; vial four open-faced (a jet); vial six a grit node packed from the Tide's sand.
- **The Magnus throw.** Sidearm with spin; the spinning air shell curves the ball.
- **The Squeeze.** A bleeding node on the Tide's centre turns its inward pull onto the air; a steady pull toward a point is how a world holds an atmosphere. Roughly three atmospheres at Taesyn's distance by the third pulse. Slow, survivable. Denser air bends the mirage harder.
- Warden rules respected: low throws, lob under a thrown stone's speed, nothing east.

# The Petition

*Beneath Vaeloris. The council chamber, continuous.*

---

Corwin Sesk nodded through the whole of it and did not once realise he was being taught.

That was the part he would take back up the stair with him and would fail to put down. He had come into that chamber to deliver a report and take an instruction, and somewhere in the middle of it the shape of the thing had turned over and he had found himself being told what people were. This person, no. People. The mechanism of them, laid out flat and unhurried by something that had evidently been watching the species long enough to have stopped finding it interesting, and every single item of it fitted what Sesk had spent twenty-two years learning the expensive way, one prisoner at a time, in cellars, mostly by being wrong first.

He had known that fear takes certainty. He had never had the sentence for it.

*I have been doing this work since I was twenty-eight and I have never once had it explained to me. I have had it corrected. I have had it priced. Nobody has ever sat down and told me what I am actually doing.*

He caught himself somewhere around the word *ethical* and made himself stop, because a man who enjoys being taught by his employer stops noticing what he is being taught toward.

Then he thought about it a moment longer and understood that noticing had not helped, and that he was going to think about the lesson tonight in his room with the lamp on, and that he was going to enjoy it.

---

He also understood, with the part of his mind that had kept him alive through four contracts that had ended badly for other people, that he had been talking too long.

Aurelian was a husk. That was the honest word and he had been avoiding it for four days. Broken men are the easy ones. They have a shape, the shape is bent, and you go to the bend. A husk is what you get when the man has moved out of the body and left it hanging on the frame as a formality, and Sesk had spent eleven days taking apart a house that nobody had been living in since about the third morning.

He was holding onto something else. Something outside the cell and outside the arrangement and probably past the far edge of Sesk's competence.

The only question left in the whole business was whether they would work out what it was before he decided to leave, and Sesk did not have that answer and had said so, and had gone on speaking past the point where saying so was useful.

Kalaabad had heard enough.

---

He did not speak. He turned his head, and he looked at Sesk, and that was all of it.

Sesk's chest stopped.

There was nothing in the room. He wanted that recorded somewhere and had nowhere to record it. Wind, sound, cold, pressure against the skin: the body is built to report all four and reported none of them. What happened was that the floor became more true. It acquired a claim on him that floors do not ordinarily have, and his knees registered it before his mind did, and the whole column of his body from crown to heel became a quantity of weight standing on stone, and stopped being a man standing in a room. His diaphragm went down and would not come back up. He got about a third of a breath and held it and could not spend it, and the ledger under his arm developed a mass entirely disproportionate to a book, and a thin line of sweat went down inside his collar in the space of two seconds.

His hands stayed at his sides. He was proud of that afterward and it was the only thing he was proud of.

It lasted perhaps four seconds and then the floor was a floor again and Sesk got a whole breath in and the sound it made was audible and humiliating.

*Twenty-two years and I have never once been on this end of it. That is the whole of the trade, that is the only thing I sell, and he did it to me with his neck.*

---

Kalaabad stood upright and brought the halberd down off his shoulder and set the butt of it on the black stone in front of him with both hands folded over the head, and the sound it made was wrong for the floor.

He was very large and that was the least of it.

The proportions were the problem. Something in the length of the arms, a knuckle too much, and the way the plate across the shoulders sat as though it had been fitted correctly to a frame that had since changed its mind. Skin the colour of something that has been kept out of light for a long time and has not decided whether to go pale or grey. The helm sat a few degrees off true, and Sesk had assumed a stretched strap on the way in, and standing this close he could see the strap was fine and the skull underneath it was not the shape helms are made for.

Around the throat, above the gorget, a band of scar tissue ran the full circumference, smooth and raised and old.

That accounted for the voice. It came out of him strangled and half-formed, every word arriving as though it had been pushed past something that was still in the way, and the terrible thing about it was that it was clearly not painful to him. It was simply what he had now. Somebody had put a hand around that throat a very long time ago and closed it and had never entirely let go, and he had gone on speaking anyway for however many centuries it had been.

"My lord."

The word came out of him like a man being drowned in a room and continuing to talk.

"Let me expand your domain."

---

"I have listened to the contractor for a quarter of an hour and I do not fault him. He has done what he is for. But you have been holding a knife in a drawer while men with ledgers explain to you what a knife is."

The halberd head, above his folded hands, was a thing Sesk made himself look at once and then leave alone. It was dirty. That was what he took from it. Everything else in the chamber was maintained to a standard that made his own hands feel unwashed, and the halberd was dirty, and had been dirty for a long while, and the marks on it were old.

"Give me the Mahuo."

The malformed voice went flatter instead of louder.

"What is left of them. There is a line of them still walking and every one of them is a precedent with legs, and precedent is the only thing that people like these have ever had that outlasts a generation. They will not fight me the way a soldier fights. They will file. They will produce a finding and expect the finding to hold, because it has always held, because nobody has yet stood in front of one and simply continued. I want to be the thing that continues. I want it established, once, in front of witnesses who will run, that a Mahuo determination does not stop a body that has decided to keep moving.

"Then give me the Valorin. What remains of them and wherever they have put it. They have spent a great deal of effort on being difficult to find and I would like to demonstrate that being difficult to find is a strategy with a floor.

"And when you are ready, my lord, and only when you are ready, give me the Zettari, since you have taken an interest in them and an interest of yours is a thing I would prefer to have tested by me and not by circumstance."

He shifted his hands on the halberd head.

"My weapon has been quiet for a long time. I do not say that as poetry. I was put somewhere quiet and I came back out and the quiet came with me, and it sits behind my teeth all day, and the only hour it stops is the hour there is blood on the floor and somebody in the room has understood what is happening to them. That is what I have instead of appetite. You gave it back to me. You gave me the ground to stand on and the leave to be seen standing on it, and a thing that is given ought to be used, or the giving was an error and I will not have you thought to have made one."

---

Three houses.

Sesk had a clerk's ear and eleven days of practice at listening to a man say things in a specific order, and he heard the shape of it and could make nothing of the shape at all.

Mahuo. Valorin. Zettari.

There was a gap in it.

He could not have said where and he could not have said what belonged in it, and there was nothing in the sentence to point at, and he was as certain of it as he had ever been of anything in his working life. The list had been assembled. It had an order and the order was argued for. And somewhere in that assembly a name had been walked around, deliberately, by a man who had walked around nothing else in the entire speech, and had been walked around so smoothly that the only evidence of it was that the speech was slightly too complete.

*He is not asking for everything. A man in a fever asks for everything. He has asked for three and he has ranked them and he has given a reason for each and he has put the one he actually wants somewhere else.*

*And I am the only person in this chamber who has spent eleven days listening to a man ration what he says, and I am not going to mention it, because whatever is in that gap is worse than this room and I would like to go back up the stair.*

Sesk decided, standing there, that it was bloodlust and that he had been reading too much into a soldier.

He was wrong, and he would not learn it, and the being wrong would keep him alive for another eleven weeks.

---

"And I will say the last of it plainly, my lord, because the contractor has been permitted to be plain and I want the same courtesy.

"They are going to come north. Slowly, over years, because they are slow and they argue and they will spend two winters establishing whose jurisdiction it is before one of them puts a boot on a road. But they will come, and when they come they will come as a coalition, and a coalition is an object with a seam in it.

"I am asking to be sent early so that the seam is already there when they arrive. Break the Mahuo and every house that has ever relied on a determination holding starts privately costing what it would mean if determinations stopped. Take the Valorin and every house that has hidden itself starts wondering how it was found. Neither of those is a battle. Both of them are arithmetic performed on people who are not yet at war, and it is cheaper to do it now than to do it later against the same men standing in a line."

He shifted his grip once on the halberd head.

"And I will bring back whatever your Generals want measured. Wellsprings, wards, whichever of their systems bends and which of them snaps. I have no interest in the maps and I will carry them anyway. The measuring is yours. The killing is what I am asking for, and I am asking for it as a wage and not as a favour, and I would rather it were entered as a wage."

*Say yes to any of them. Any one. Give me a road going north and a reason to be on it, and by the second season nobody will be able to reconstruct which of the killing was your business and which of it was mine.*

---

The petition ended and nothing answered it.

Kalaabad did not appear to expect anything. He stood with the halberd butt on the black floor and his hands folded over the head, patient in the specific manner of a thing that has been made to wait for a very long time in a place where waiting was the entire content of the day, and beside him Sesk stood with his ledger under his arm and did the arithmetic he could not stop doing.

It was the trade. Twenty-two years of walking into a room and pricing the man in it. What would open him. Where the load sits. What he loves, what he owes, what he is protecting, what he would trade for it.

He ran it on Kalaabad.

There was nothing there.

No family that he could see, no faith, no appetite that could be starved, no future being saved for, no name that would shame him, and a body that had been altered past the point where damage was a language it still spoke. Sesk had once broken a man in four hours by threatening a dog. He looked at the halberdier and found no dog anywhere in him, and no shape where a dog had been, and understood that if he were ever handed this one as a contract he would take the fee and walk out of the city that night.

Then he understood something worse and considerably more immediate, which was that Kalaabad had almost certainly run the same arithmetic on him during the four seconds of the floor, and had finished it, and had not found it difficult.

The grey powder under the far edge of the table had grown again.

He thought about the four Attestants who were not where anybody believed they were, and about a man on a frame eleven days deep who had never once caught him in a lie, and about the fact that he had walked into this chamber intending to recommend restraint and had done it and had been heard.

That should have been a good morning's work.

He tried to hold onto it and found that it would not stay held, because standing four feet to his left was a thing that had asked, out loud, in front of witnesses, for the entire remainder of three houses, and had asked for it in the tone a foreman uses to request a second cart.

Somewhere far above them the forges took a surge and the whole chamber's air changed pressure for a moment and came back, and neither Kalaabad nor anyone at the table gave any sign of having noticed, and Sesk, who had noticed, filed it with the powder and the smell and the four seconds on the floor, in the growing back part of his mind where he kept the things he had decided not to want to know.

Corwin Sesk moved his ledger from his left arm to his right, which was a small thing to do with his body and the only thing available, and stood in the quiet beside the halberd and waited to be told what he was.

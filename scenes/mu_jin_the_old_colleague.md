# Mu-jin: The Old Colleague

Mu-jin stops at the head of the service stair with one hand on the cold iron of the rail.

He does not look at the fifth light again. He has seen it once, and he knows himself well enough to know that a second look would become a third, and a third would keep him on this gallery until the lamps were lit. He looks at the boy instead. Loose fighting robes, hands tucked into the sleeves: a Zettari heir who has just been handed a House's weight by a man with a cigarette.

"Zaire." Mu-jin takes his hand off the rail and turns it palm up between them, open, the scarred forearm and the chalk in the creases and the old reagent stains at the fingertips all offered at once, the way the river people offer a hand so the other man sees it before he takes it. "Ask the hands, not the man. Your people say that. I've always thought it the most honest proverb on the continent." He lowers the hand. "Mine will write what the Grand Mage asked for tonight and stop where the oath stops. You'll see where the line falls. If I ever step over it, tell your keepers I did. I'd want them to know."

He inclines his head to the boy, a fraction deeper than he inclined it to the Grand Mage, because the boy is younger and is carrying something older than both of them.

Then, to Xhem, with the corner of his mouth moving: "She settled on all four before the match was finished, Grand Mage. By supper she'll have found a fifth, and it'll be me again, for a different reason."

He goes down.

Madam Ha's finger is still pointed at Geturo when Mu-jin reaches the bedside and answers her.

"You'll have him when he chooses to come, Madam." He says it gently. He has argued with this woman before. He lost. "He built the thing alone and hid it from both of us for eleven months. I've learned my lesson about deciding which rooms he walks into." He glances at his son. "Ask him yourself. He'll give you an answer. It'll be very short."

Geturo looks at the old woman for a long moment. "After the tournament," he says. "When I can hold a pen."

Madam Ha lowers the finger. Something happens at the corner of her set old mouth that could be approval, and could just as easily be a woman memorising the name of a boy who has told her no. The Dawi orderly takes the cold pipe out of his teeth and puts it back in, which Mu-jin has come to understand is the only form of applause he ever gives.

Mu-jin turns to his wife.

Frithia is on her feet again. She has never been able to stay sitting while something in a room is still wrong, and she stands a full hand taller than her husband, long in the bone and angular, the brown hair coming down from its pins, the four blue lines of the ink on her chin standing out darker because the rest of her face has gone pale with holding in her temper. The ringed-seal hat she stitched herself before she ever saw an Accord road is pushed back off her forehead. She looks down at him like weather off the ice.

"I have some matters of my own to see to this evening," Mu-jin says. He says it carefully, because he already knows how it will land, and it lands exactly there. "I may not be back for supper. An old friend's coming into town on the evening train."

Her eyebrows come together.

"Malphas," Geturo says from the cot, to the ceiling. "The one who writes you all the letters. With the eastern franking and the green wax." He shifts on the bolsters and regrets it and does not show that he regrets it. "Your old colleague from the assay-house. You read the last one twice at breakfast and then put it in your coat instead of the drawer."

Mu-jin looks at his eldest for a moment. *He watches the doors*, he thinks. *He always has. I never once asked what else he saw from out there.*

"Exactly him," he says. "We shared a bench the winter I was twenty-two, and he's been correcting my arithmetic by post ever since. But the investigation comes first. I've a notebook to copy out and eight stubs to draw at their true size before the morning bell, and the Grand Mage will know if I've drawn them from memory instead of from the page." He touches his coat, where the notebook sits against his ribs. "I'll see Malphas for an hour. Then I'll work."

Frithia says something under her breath in her own tongue, the ice-country speech that comes to her before Accord common whenever she's frightened, three short words with a hard stop in the middle. Mu-jin has never been told what they mean, though he has heard them once before, spoken over his own sickbed, years ago.

"I've never met this man," she says in common. "Sixteen years of letters and I've never once seen his face." She looks down at Geturo's sling and back at her husband, and the worry comes out of her in the only shape she lets it take, which is an order. "You'll be alright. Won't you."

She has not phrased it as a question. Mu-jin answers it as one anyway.

He steps in and puts his hand on her waist, at the knot of the apron strings, and he has to reach up a little to do it. He leaves it there for as long as it takes to breathe once.

"I'm the strongest person you know," he says quietly, and a little of the Sum-gol court comes back into it, the way it always does when he means a thing too much to say it plainly. "You've nothing to worry about." The hand comes away. "All the same, I'll take Hiromi with me. Somebody has to keep him from wandering off to find trouble, and it's easier if the trouble can see me standing next to him."

Hiromi straightens as if stung.

"No-o-o-o." It comes out in four notes, rising and falling, loud enough that the sleeping girl in the far cot stirs and turns over. "Dinner with *Father's letters?* With an old man from an *assay-house?* That's a total drag, that's the biggest drag in the history of *drags*!"

Frithia turns her head.

She does not say anything. She does not need to. She simply looks down at her middle son from her full height, with her chin tattoos and her walrus scar and the long cold patience of a woman who once waited out an ice storm in a skin tent with a dead man, and the look goes straight through Hiromi like a draught under a door.

"I mean yes!" Hiromi's grin comes back so fast that it arrives crooked. "Yes. Sure. I'd *love* to go. Old men. Letters. Arithmetic." He laughs, high and nervous, and rubs the back of his neck, where his white hair has dried stiff with sweat. "Love it. Can't wait."

*The picnic*, he thinks, and the grin slips for one heartbeat, just at the edges. *I still owe her the gift. The one I have to make by hand. I haven't even picked the reagents.* He counts the hours between now and the next free afternoon, and it comes out to more than he likes. *Later. There's time later. There's always time later.*

Frithia looks at him a moment longer to be sure. Then she bends and fixes the sling knot at Geturo's neck, although it does not need fixing.

"Bring them both back," she says to the knot.

---

The Halt Hotel stands across the cinder yard from the Academy station, three storeys of soot-dark brick with a wrought-iron canopy over the door and a brass draw-gauge in the lobby that nobody has calibrated since it was hung. The evening train from the east is in. Its engine stands at the platform breathing, huge and patient, letting off steam in long white sighs that roll across the yard and fog the lower windows of the hotel. The whole building smells of coal smoke, beeswax polish and the mutton the kitchen has been roasting since noon.

The private room is at the back, off a corridor with a worn red runner. It has a coal fire, a table laid for two with a third place added in a hurry, and a window onto the dark yard with the steam going past it.

Malphas is standing when they come in.

He is taller than Mu-jin remembers, or else Mu-jin has settled into his bench years and got shorter. He is long in the face and narrow in the chest and wide across the shoulders, the skin of his face the colour of steeped tea left to cool, the veins blue-grey at his temples in the firelight. There is more grey at those temples than there was. The black above them is cut too short to move. His coat is dark, good cloth, buttoned to the throat, with no Guild seal and no insignia of any kind, and he wears the absence like a man who knows people will notice it.

For a moment he does not see Hiromi at all. He sees Mu-jin, and his whole face changes. The lecturer's reserve goes out of it all at once, the way a gauge-needle drops when a district main is cut, and what is left is a man of fifty-odd looking at somebody he last saw at twenty-two.

"Mu-jin." He comes around the table with both hands out and takes Mu-jin's right hand in both of them, and holds it, and does not let go. "Look at you. You got old. Good. I had money on your not living to do it."

"It's good to see you after so long, Malphas." Mu-jin's voice has gone warm and unguarded, the voice his students have never heard him use. "Too long. Sixteen years of your handwriting and I'd started to forget you had a face attached."

Then Malphas sees the boy behind him.

He lets go of Mu-jin's hand. He looks at Hiromi: at the white hair, the red eyes, the black drop earrings, the grey gone into the skin under the eyes and the cuts across the bridge of the nose. His face goes very still, and then something in it opens again, slowly, a different thing this time.

"And who's this you've brought me?"

"My son," Mu-jin says. "Hiromi. The middle one."

"Your *son*." Malphas says the word as if he were turning it over in his hand to look at the maker's mark. "You never wrote me you had a son. You wrote me about sublimation rates and the price of mercury at Sum-gol. Sixteen years of letters, Mu-jin, and not one line about a son." He looks back at Hiromi, and the lines at the corners of his eyes deepen. "So the boy who used to fall asleep at his bench with the furnace still lit went and settled down." He sounds, for a moment, very close to proud. "I'd not have laid money on that either."

Hiromi, who has been told by his mother's look to behave, sticks his hand out.

"Hiromi Mahuo." The volume is lower than usual, which for Hiromi means that the far end of the corridor can hear him rather than the lobby. "Greatest summoner who'll ever live. Pleased to meet you, sir. Father reads your letters twice."

"Does he." Malphas takes the hand. He holds it a beat longer than a handshake needs, his long fingers closing over the boy's, and his eyes drop briefly to Hiromi's wrist, where the skin is thin and the pulse is running fast from an emptied Crystal. Then he lets go. "Then I've been writing to the right man."

The second man in the room has stayed by the fire. He is younger than Malphas by twenty years and heavier by three stone, broad in the face and broad in the coat, and his coat is cheaper and not as clean. A burn scar runs from his left ear to the hinge of his jaw, the skin pulled tight and shining in the firelight. He has put out a cigar in the saucer on the mantel, and the room smells of it.

"Velder Crace," Malphas says, without turning. "My secretary. He carries my cases and reminds me to eat."

Velder nods to Mu-jin, and then to Hiromi. He does not offer a hand. "Professor," he says. His voice is careful. "Young sir."

Hiromi looks at the man's hands, because Hiromi always looks at hands first now, since the arena. There is no ink on them. There is no ink under the nails, or in the crease of the second finger where a secretary's pen rests all day. Across the backs of both hands, the skin is freckled with small, pale, round burn-scars, dozens of them, the old splatter pattern of a man who has spent years standing close to things that spit.

Hiromi says nothing. He files it with the tall Class S boy's back foot.

They sit. The mutton comes, and the potatoes in their fat, and a jug of dark beer that Malphas pours for Mu-jin with his own hand and never pours for himself. Malphas asks after Sum-gol, and after the price of mercury, and laughs at the right places, and for half an hour the room is a room where two old bench-mates talk about furnaces and dead masters and a Guild examiner at the assay-house who once set his own beard alight trying to demonstrate a flame test. Hiromi eats, because nobody is watching him not eat, and finds, to his annoyance, that he is enjoying himself.

When the waiter leaves the bill on a brass tray, Malphas draws it over to him without looking at the total. He reads down the column once. Then he takes a pencil from his coat, strikes one figure through, writes the right one small beside it, and slides the tray back to the edge of the table without a word, in the way a senior corrects an apprentice's arithmetic.

Mu-jin sees him do it and smiles, because it is exactly what Malphas has always done, and it is a comfort after sixteen years to see that some things keep.

Malphas folds his long fingers on the tablecloth, thumbs parallel, and leans back.

"Now," he says. "Tell me what the Academy has you doing, Mu-jin. You've a notebook in your coat that you've touched four times since you sat down, and fresh ink on the side of your hand. Four days ago you wrote me that you teach from dawn to lamplight and never touch a pen, only chalk." His dark eyes are kind, and very steady. "What are you working on?"

## Author notes

**Notes**
- **Replying to Xhem:** Mu-jin offers Zaire his open palm the Zettari way and quotes their proverb "Ask the hands, not the man" from the Zettari Standing Inventory. He accepts Zaire as the House's witness to where his oath stops, then answers Xhem's joke about Frithia and goes down the stair.
- **The ending is open:** Malphas asks what Mu-jin is working on, and the notebook about the archive he has been correcting is in Mu-jin's coat. Whether Mu-jin tells him, lies, or changes the subject is the player's.
- **The seed:** Malphas corrects the bill in exactly the phrase Mu-jin used in the last post for the second hand in the margins, "the way a senior corrects an apprentice's arithmetic." Mu-jin only smiles. The reader should notice it.
- **The session's lie:** Malphas calls Velder "my secretary." Hiromi sees that Velder's hands have no ink on them and are covered in old splash burns.
- **The beat's line "It is nice to meet you after so long Malphas whose this…":** it could be read as either man speaking, so it was split. Mu-jin says the greeting and Malphas asks "who's this you've brought me?" about Hiromi.
- **Why Malphas never heard of the children:** Frithia asked not to be written down (the Frithia ruling from today), so Mu-jin never mentioned his family in sixteen years of letters.
- **Frithia's body** here follows the Frithia ruling; The Reader's Oath was corrected to match before archiving.
- **Made up here, not yet ruled:** the Halt Hotel by the Academy station, that Mu-jin and Malphas shared an assay-house bench when he was 22, that the letters come with green wax and eastern franking, Frithia's three muttered words and the ice-storm story, and the flame-test beard story. Please check the bench at 22 against the Alftian Codex timeline, which puts Ashgate at 24.

# Nine Hours of Daylight

*Coldwater common, continued.*
*POV: Ruvaen Aloreth. RP reply. Dova'Kan, Gorgi and Xmere are not written; their beats stay open.*

## I. THE GROUND COMPLAINING

The dragon came down across the edge of the common and the common accepted it, which Ruvaen had not expected.

He had spent two seconds working out where the mass was going to go and had got it wrong in every particular. A thing that size settling on soft ground ought to sink and shear and take the topsoil out sideways in a wave, and it did not. It came down the way a hand comes down on a table when the man intends not to break the table. The earth complained along a hundred yards of its length and went on holding, and the shadow took the cottages and the mill and the top third of the chalk face, and a sound like a barn settling came up out of the whole of it and then stopped.

He did not move.

Forty spears at the rise did, and he heard them do it, and he did not turn round.

The eye came down level with him and it was larger than the window of the reeve's house.

*The Aether did not move.*

*A body of that mass entering a volume displaces what is in it. Everything does. A cart does. A gull does. She does, and she is the largest thing I had ever stood beside until about four seconds ago. This did not displace anything. It arrived and the medium was already shaped to have it there, and I watched it happen from eleven feet and I have no idea what I watched.*

*Write that down as well. Unentered.*

"Continue," the dragon said, and the drinking course behind the mill carried it and gave it back a half beat later off eighty feet of wet stone.

Ruvaen Aloreth inclined his head to the eye, at a range where inclining his head was a gesture roughly the size of a blink, and continued.

---

## II. THE SMELL

"You are right about the smell," he said, "and you left out the two worst of it, which I take as a courtesy and I am going to decline it.

"Grain is the one people notice. Burnt grain travels further than anything else on a battlefield and it travels for days and it is the smell everybody writes songs about. Underneath it there is the pit where the horses go, which is the smell that makes a man who has never been in a war understand what he has walked into, and there is one of those nine miles north of here with four hundred animals in it and it has been open since the Thin Weeks because we have run out of the lime to close it.

"And there is the other one. Two hundred and six people at Harrowbeck went into the ground four days ago in weather that has not broken, and Harrowbeck is a hundred and forty miles off, and it is in this wind. That is what is underneath the blood you can smell. Not fresh. Four days old and in the ground and still arriving here.

"I have been on the Landward Wall for eleven years and the thing I have never got used to is that the burial is louder than the battle."

He looked up at the eye.

"You said it makes you miss battle, and then you took the dying part out of it, and I am not going to correct a veteran about his own war. I will say the version I have. It does not make me miss it. I have simply stopped being able to smell anything else, and a man who can only smell one thing stops calling it a smell and starts calling it the air."

---

## III. THE HOOKS

"Now the hooks, because you have gone straight to the thing that matters and nobody on my side has.

"I can give you a date. Not a guess. A date, and it is worse than the one you are working with.

"Equipment does not appear in a soldier's hand. It goes pattern, proof, ruling, quantity, carriage. Somebody draws the pattern. A smith makes one and it is tried and it is wrong, because the first one is always wrong. It is drawn again. Then it goes before whoever rules on issue, and on my side that is a quartermaster called Velenn Ithalan who is the best I have ever had and who will not release a copper against an unsealed tally, and she takes four weeks on a ruling and considers that fast. Then the quantity is made, which for a forged hook with a socket and a haft is five weeks for a hundred if the smiths do nothing else. Then it is carried to a company in the field.

"On my side that is fourteen weeks and I would call it good work.

"Korvaeth's deep roads are better than my roads. Carriage under the ground in the dark with no weather and no ambush is the one thing they have on me and it is a large thing. So call it nine. Call it eleven if the pattern was tried twice, and it was tried twice, because a hook that takes a wyvern at the wing root without killing it is not a thing anybody gets right on a first attempt.

"The Fourth had them in the second week of the campaign."

He let the arithmetic stand on the common for a moment.

"That order was given a season before the first wyvern came over any pass. It was given before there were wyverns to see. It was given before anybody standing on any ridge on this continent had any reason to look up.

"Nobody watched for this," Ruvaen said. "Somebody was told."

*And the only people in the four quarters who track where wyverns are and where they are going are archivists.*

*Velthaeir has four entries on dragons. It has eleven hundred on migration, and a floating city to keep them in, and a Measurewright corps that will level a plate anywhere in the world for a fee, and it has never once refused a fee.*

*It is somebody in my own Archive. It is somebody who sat in that gallery and voted, and then sold a migration table across a border to a woman with no name, and I am going to find out which one and I am going to do it before the winter.*

That was where he went and he was going to stay there. There was nothing on this continent in a position to move him off it.

---

## IV. WHAT HE SOUNDED LIKE

"You don't have to worry," he said.

It came out of him differently and he heard it happen.

"I will have vengeance for every one of them they killed. All nine. And Harrowbeck, and the four hundred horses, and eleven years of the Landward Wall. I am going to kick the doors of that capital off their hinges and take back what is mine by right, and I am going to do it with my own hands, and there is not going to be a piece of that woman's doctrine left standing when I am finished with it."

The eye above him did not blink. The water ran along the western edge of the common.

Ruvaen Aloreth stood in the middle of a burnt village and listened to what he had just said in front of a thing older than his civilisation, and did not take one word of it back.

*There it is. That is not a High Elf speaking. There is no Eressean in that, not the diction, not the cadence, not the grammar. That is the Vessel Coast dockside and the wall at night and men with bad teeth shouting over a parapet, and it is what comes out of my mouth now when I stop managing it.*

*I have been told. Not by the Court, because the Court does not say things. By a byname.*

The Drow call him Kinslayer, which is an accounting term in their grammar and which he had always thought was fair enough as far as it went.

His own people call him the Crimson Blade.

They had started in the season after the third wave at Ollavaeth, when he had come back down the causeway at Vaelmarr in a state that Eressean has no word for, and a Vessel Coast factor standing on the dock had said it in Common, and it had gone round the fish market and up through the garrison and into the city inside eleven days. A Common byname. In Eresse. Where a name is an office and a byname is a thing the yard grants or refuses.

And the Sunroot Court had never struck it.

Nine hundred years of the most litigious naming law in the four quarters, an institution that has voided a name for a misplaced witness, and it had looked at a Common word for savagery stuck to the front prince of the realm and it had declined to convene.

*They did not strike it because it is accurate, and everybody in that room knows it is accurate, and a Reckoner does not strike an accurate entry.*

*My own people gave me that. Not hers.*

---

## V. THE SEAM

"The stone," he said, and his voice had come back down inside two breaths, which was a thing he had learned to do a long time before he learned why he needed to.

"You are right and I am going to do it now rather than think about it, because the moment I ride away from here somebody will work it out.

"Sergeant."

"My lord."

"The black seam at the fourth course is Crown stock from this hour. Not quarry tithe, not village stock, not Brannoch Skell's, and I will pay him what it is worth as blunted drills and not one copper more than that, and he will be told why. Nobody cuts it, nobody carts it, nobody sells it. That includes the Court, that includes Velthaeir, and that includes the Vessel factors before any of them hear the word. The spoil heap gets a fence and two men and the two men are told it is a boundary dispute."

"On what article, my lord."

"None. I am inventing one and Corvienne Sorreth will hate it and she will draft it properly by tomorrow evening because she hates it, and until she does, the instrument is that I said so." He turned his head. "And Sergeant. Whoever guards it does not know what it is. Tell them it is chalk."

*Which is the whole trouble and I can hear it as I am saying it.*

*The fastest way to make a thing valuable is to put a fence round it and post two men.*

*There is no version of this where I do not advertise. I have a choice between a seam anyone can cart away and a seam with a fence and a rumour, and I am taking the fence, and in about nine weeks somebody is going to ask the right question in the right tavern.*

"When you have a name for what the stone does," he said, "and if you are willing to give it to me, I will keep it as close as you keep it. If you are not willing, I will take the instruction without the reason and I will not go digging for the rest of it. I have had a boundary put into a wound on my frontier by a man I met an hour ago. I am not going to repay that by getting clever."

---

## VI. THE PLATE

"Same method, not impressions. Agreed, and I can give you the method.

"Neyra Corvenn of Velthaeir, eleven hundred miles from home, currently at Vaelmarr with a brass plate she has levelled on a dock, on a barge in a swell, and twice inside a dead pocket in the Reach where the needle went flat and stayed flat. She is the best instrument on this continent and she is attached to an Archive I have just told you I think has sold us.

"I am going to send for her anyway.

"Two reasons, and you should hear both. The first is that no other corps in the four quarters takes a comparable reading and impressions are worth nothing, which is your point and it is correct. The second is that if there is a hand inside Velthaeir, then a Measurewright with a plate out on my frontier taking baselines is the fastest way to find out whose hand it is, because whatever she sends home will be read by somebody, and what happens next will tell me which somebody.

"She will work out that I am doing that. She will do it anyway and she will do the readings honestly, because she wrote *unentered* on a plate that had been driven to the pin when she could have written a number and made her name on it.

"Her baselines are yours. Every sheet, as she takes them, in Common and in her own hand, and I will not have them copied through my Court."

---

## VII. THE HIGH PLACE

He reached back over his shoulder and drew the blade for the first time since he had come down the stair, and turned it, and laid the flat of it up along his shoulder with his hand at the middle of the grip.

It was not a threat and it was not a flourish. It was how a man carries three and a half feet of steel up four hundred feet of quarry stair without putting it through his own calf on the sixth turn, and every Landward company does it, and it was the single most common sight in the whole of his life.

He looked up the face.

The stair went from the leat hatch in eleven flights with a dogleg at the fifth where the seam ran out, black in the white, four hundred feet of it and visible from the river. At the top there was an iron rail, a spoil lip, a wind that never stopped, and nine hours of daylight left in it.

*Three hundred of them reaching, and I will have it separated out by the middle bell if the second reach is there at all.*

*I read grief for a living. I have read three hundred wyverns and eleven waves of infantry and a man a hundred and forty miles away I have never met, and I can tell you to the yard where a company lost its captain.*

*I put my own read on myself in the ninth year, sitting in the lower yard, and there was nothing there. No reach. Not a small one, not a buried one, nothing pointed anywhere at all, and I sat in that yard until it got dark working out whether the instrument was broken or the man was.*

*What I have instead is this, and it goes outward, and it is the only thing in me I can still find.*

He put his free hand flat on his chest, briefly, to the eye above him and to the man beside it, which was the form and which they had both earned twice over.

"Follow me this way," Ruvaen Aloreth said, and turned, and went up the first flight of the quarry stair with the edge on his shoulder and the chalk coming up white off his heels.

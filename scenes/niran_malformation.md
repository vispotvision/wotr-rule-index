# Malformation

*The Yukari Enclave. The meditation floor, before dawn.*

---

Cold pine, lamp oil gone cold in a lamp nobody had lit, and the flat mineral smell that comes off stone floors in the hour when they are at their coldest and have stopped giving anything back.

Niran sat on the boards with his shins folded under him and his hands loose in his lap, and the boards were old enough that they had stopped being smooth and had gone to that fine silvered roughness that pine takes after two hundred years of people kneeling on it. Somewhere below and to the east, a door was opened and closed by somebody who did not want to be heard doing it. The wind came around the eave and made the eave's particular sound, which was three notes and always the same three.

He closed his eyes.

It came up as it always came up. Sight had no part in it. The room stopped being a room with things in it and became a set of relations, every object in the Enclave holding onto every other object by a line, and the lines held tension, and the tension could be read. The pillar behind him leaned four degrees of load onto a beam that had been carrying it since before his grandfather. The Enclave's outer wards sat in a lattice he could walk with his attention the way a man walks a fence. Below him, thirty-one people asleep, each one a small dense knot where a great many lines converged and did not go anywhere else.

He let it widen.

That was the discipline and it was the only part of it he had ever been good at. Reaching gets you nothing. Widening. Letting the web take in the valley and then the ridge and then the country beyond the ridge, so that the lines got thinner and more numerous and the tension in any single one of them stopped meaning anything and the shape of the whole began to.

He was four breaths into it when he found the place where the web stopped.

---

There was a hole in it.

That was wrong on its face. The web did not have holes. Emptiness had lines. A dead man had lines, a burnt field had lines, the deep water had lines, and Niran had once spent an afternoon reading the tension across a two-hundred-year-old crater and had come away with a headache and a great deal of respect for whoever made it.

This had none. Slack would have been something. This was absent, and the absence had an edge.

And the edge had teeth.

He was aware of how that sounded and he had no better account of it. Where the lines came up to the boundary of the thing they did not fray or terminate; they were bitten off, and the bitten ends had a serration to them, and the serration was regular in the way that a saw is regular and a wound is not.

The dread arrived four seconds later and it arrived in his body first.

His scalp tightened at the crown. Both forearms went to gooseflesh in one pass, hair standing along the outside from wrist to elbow, and his stomach dropped in a long cold slide with no height anywhere in it. His mouth flooded. His hands, loose in his lap, closed on their own and he did not notice them do it, and a fine shiver started in the muscle over his right shoulder blade and would not stop, and he sat there on the floor of the safest building he had ever been in with the specific animal certainty that something enormous had turned its head.

*This is not fear of a thing. This is what a mouse feels standing in a field with no cover, and the mouse has not seen anything, and the mouse is correct.*

He should have withdrawn. He had been taught to withdraw. Instead his attention went on out through the bitten edge and kept going, because he had spent eleven years being told what he could not do and had learned only that the telling arrived after.

The Veil took him and he expanded.

Every direction at once. There was no distance in it and no duration, and the closest he had ever come to describing it afterward was that he stopped being a knot and became a quantity of surface, and the surface was very thin and got thinner, and somewhere in the thinning he became aware that he could not currently find his own hands.

---

He heard a woman singing.

It came from no direction and had no words that he could hold onto, and the melody ran in four phrases with the third one falling. Music has a kind of beauty and this had a different one, the kind a thing acquires when you know it is the last of its kind and nobody else in the room has noticed yet.

He listened for a stretch he could not measure and got nothing out of it except grief and the certainty that he had never heard it before.

Then it went out.

It went out the way a held note goes out under a closing door. Somebody stopped, or something between him and her closed, and Niran hung in the thinning surface of himself with the after-shape of four phrases and no idea whose they were.

"You shouldn't be here."

---

He came back into having edges all at once, and there was a floor under him again, and it was not the Enclave's floor.

The thing standing across from him was wearing his face.

Likeness is too weak a word for it. It had his height and his build and the particular set of his left shoulder sitting lower than the right from a break at fourteen, and it had the burn scar along the inside of the wrist, and it was standing the way Niran stood when he was pretending not to be tired. Every detail correct and the whole thing wrong, the way a word is wrong when it is spelled right and used in the incorrect sentence.

The eyes were the tell.

The light in them came from behind rather than sitting on the surface, and it was moving, and it had already taken the iris and was going out into the white in slow radial spurs, and by the time Kurotana finished its second sentence there was nothing recognisably an eye left in either socket.

"Have you finally decided to embrace your inner truth, Niran?"

The voice was his own voice and it had been taken apart and reassembled by somebody who had heard him speak but had never had to mean anything.

"You have been sitting on that floor since the third hour. Widening. Reading the tension. Being extremely careful." Kurotana came one step closer and its bare feet made no sound on a surface that was not there. "How is the carefulness going. Eleven years of it. Your father is dead, your house is a ruin with a nice name on it, the thing that did it is out there somewhere getting comfortable, and you have spent this morning measuring load on a beam."

"I know what you are."

"You know what you have been told I am, which is a different object and considerably less useful." The ruined eyes did not blink. "I have never once lied to you. Attend to that, because everybody else in your life has, and most of them did it kindly. I am the only thing you have ever spoken to that has told you exactly what it wants."

Niran said nothing.

"I want in. That is the whole of it and I have always said it in those words." Another step. "And in exchange you get the only thing you have ever actually wanted, which is not justice and is not peace and is not your father back. You want to be sufficient. Once. For one hour. You want to stand in front of the thing that took your house and not have to be careful, and I can give you that, and I am the only offer on the table, and the table has been getting emptier every year since you were fourteen."

The dread was still there, underneath. That was the part Niran held onto. Whatever this was, it was not the thing that had bitten the web, and it was frightened of the same thing he was, and it was hurrying.

"You are talking fast," Niran said.

Kurotana stopped.

---

"You want control."

The new voice came from behind him and to the left, and Niran's whole body turned before any part of him decided to.

Naiser stood there.

At about fifty, and not at the end, in the grey working coat with the left cuff worn through, with the reading-lenses pushed up into his hair where he left them and then spent a quarter of an hour looking for them. Broad through the chest and going soft at the middle. The heavy jaw. The two deep lines that bracketed his mouth and got deeper when he was about to be unfair to somebody who deserved it.

Niran's chest did something that took the whole of his attention for two seconds.

*He is here. He is actually here, he is in this, this is not a memory because a memory does not have the coat wrong, the cuff went through in the last winter and I never once saw him in it before*

He was wrong, and he would go on being wrong about it for a long time, and nothing in that place corrected him.

"You want control," Naiser said again, with his eyes on the other one. "You want to use something that was never meant to be yours, and you have found a mouth to tell you it is a gift." The lines at his mouth deepened. "It is not a gift and it is not yours and it is not even the interesting problem in this room."

He raised his voice and it went out flat across a place with no walls to carry it.

"REVEAL YOURSELF."

---

The dark did not open.

It stood up.

Wings first, because wings are the widest part, and they came out of the black on both sides at once and kept coming until the span of them was the whole of what there was to see, membrane stretched over long finger-bones and torn through in four places along the trailing edge, the tears old and rimmed and healed into thick pale welting that had never closed.

Then the body, and then the heads.

Five of them. Fused at the shoulder into one mass, human in every particular and human in none, hairless, the skulls pressed against each other so hard that the bone had given way where they met and grown together in ridges. Faces at four different angles. Two looking forward and one turned nearly to the back and one down and one at the sky, and every one of them had the deep lidless sockets of a face that had been finished by somebody working from a description. Mouths open. All five.

The skin was the worst of it and Niran would not be able to stop seeing it.

It was covered from crown to heel in raised branching cords, thick as a finger, standing up off the surface in a pattern that ran and split and rejoined, and it took him a long moment to understand that he was looking at a body that had been opened and had healed and had been opened again along the same lines, for so long, that the scarring had become the anatomy. There was no skin under it. There was only the pattern. Across the chest a cavity stood open the width of two hands, and inside the cavity, packed, were more of the same forms, small and folded and unfinished.

It was decaying while he watched. Slowly and without any apparent inconvenience to it. A strip of the left forearm came away and did not fall.

Every muscle in the thing stood proud under the cording, over-defined, drawn at the exact instant a body takes the whole load, and it was not moving at all.

Niran's ears filled. His vision went narrow at the edges. Something in his chest that had been beating gave up on its rhythm for four beats and then found it again badly, and he tasted iron, and he understood with total clarity that he was not in the presence of a monster.

*That is not one thing. That is many things that were made to be one thing.*

He had spent one read and he had spent it there and it went silent afterward, and he got nothing else from it for the rest of it.

"Hru."

The sound came out of all five mouths and out of none of them, arriving a fraction before the mouths moved and continuing a fraction after they stopped, so that the whole of it sat wrong in the ear.

"You think you understand malformation."

---

"Small one. Careful one. Sitting on a floor reading tension."

The heads did not turn. The one facing backward spoke the next part and Niran heard it as clearly as the others.

"Your kind has a word for me and the word is corruption, and the word is a comfort, and I have never contested it because a comfort keeps a thing quiet for another century. Corruption is a thing that happens to a whole. It presumes there was a whole." A second head took the sentence up without a seam. "There was no whole. There were nine, and there was an intention that they should be one, and the intention did not consult them, and what you are looking at is what an intention like that produces when it is carried all the way through and nobody stops it."

The cavity in the chest shifted.

"Your houses gather. All of them. Gather the lines, gather the names, gather the seven into the one, and every one of them tells itself that the gathering is a kindness because from inside the gathering there is no way to tell the difference between taking a thing in and eating it." Three mouths at once. "I am the difference. I am what it looks like from underneath. That is my entire function and I did not choose it and I have never once been thanked."

The wings folded halfway and stopped.

"So. You are here because you went looking. Looking is forbidden, and I will let it pass, because the going looking is the interesting part." A pause with no breath in it. "The one wearing your face wants to be let in. Your father, who is not here, wants you to refuse. Both of them are having an argument about a door.

"I am telling you there is no door. There is a hole where a door would have to be, and it has an edge, and you have already put yourself through it, and the thing about an edge like that is that it does not care which direction you crossed it in."

Niran got his knees under him.

The floor of the Enclave was under his hands. His palms were flat on two-hundred-year-old pine and his forearms were shaking and there was blood in his mouth from the inside of his cheek and the lamp was still unlit and the wind was still doing its three notes around the eave.

He put his fingers against the boards and pressed down until they hurt.

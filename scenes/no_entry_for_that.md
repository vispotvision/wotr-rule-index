# No Entry For That

*Part One: the quarry stair above Coldwater, first light.*
*Part Two: the Korvaeth camp above Harrowbeck, the same morning.*
*POV: Ruvaen Aloreth, then Aeldros Korvaeth. RP reply. Dova'Kan, Gorgi and Xmere are not written; their beats stay open.*

# PART ONE: THE STAIR

## I. WHAT HE SHOUTED UP

He stopped on the fourth tread of the second flight and turned round with one hand on the rail, and the wind took the chalk dust off his shoulders in a white rope.

"Taken. All of it, and I will say the three that matter so you know which ones landed.

"I will not take vengeance for your dead. You are right and I had not thought about it and I would have gone on not thinking about it, because a man who has been counting other people's losses for eleven years starts adding them to his own ledger without asking. Their dead belong to their broods. If the broods want it, it is theirs to take and I will not stand in front of it and I will not stand behind it either.

"Find the alteration before you find the traitor. That is the best thing anyone has said to me in a decade and I am going to say why, because you should know how badly I needed it.

"I had already decided. I had it as a fact by the fourth turn of that stair. Somebody in my own Archive, somebody who sat in the gallery and voted, and I had the shape of the man and I was four days from putting people on him." He shook his head once. "Rage loves a straight line. Mine has been running along one for eleven years and it has never once been wrong about the enemy and it is wrong about this and I could not see it until a stranger said it out loud.

"Blind sites. Original sheets kept. Compare what leaves her hand against what comes back from Velthaeir. It will take nine months and it will produce a fact instead of a hanging.

"And Vaelmarr is mine and the three are yours. Understood. I am glad of it. Now the part you did not ask for."

---

## II. THE PART HE DID NOT ASK FOR

"Your wyverns have a home in the New World for as long as they want one, and that is not a courtesy and it is not gratitude. I will keep them as safe as I am able and I will fail at it in places and I will tell you where I failed.

"All of them are welcome. Not the four at my water. All of them, including any that come over that ridge against me, which is a sentence I have thought about for exactly one night and am going to stand behind anyway.

"I am tired of it.

"There is a doctrine in my own house that says an elf hides from the world and keeps the world off the step, and that the hiding is what has kept us alive for nine hundred years. It has not kept us alive. It is the reason we have spent nine centuries killing each other over a naming law, because a people who will not look outward will find their enemies indoors, and there has never been a shortage of doors in Eresse.

"We are not short of enemies. We are short of unity, and short of peace, and the two are the same shortage wearing different coats.

"I want peace. That is the whole of it and it sounds like a thing a man says at a treaty table with a scribe behind him, so let me put a price on it so you can tell it is real.

"I do not want to be king. I have been holding a chair for eleven years that was handed to me by an assembly because everyone with a better claim was dead, and I have not enjoyed one hour of it. I do not want to be emperor and the title they have hung on me is a claim and not a fact and I told you that yesterday. I want to see my people alive. Every one of them, including the ones on her side of that ridge, including the levies that came at my wall in the fourth wave, including the Under-House infantry who are somebody's sons and are not in this because they chose it.

"I suppose that is a dream and nothing else."

*And I can hear the other sentence sitting underneath it, and so can he, and I am not going to be the man who pretends it is not there.*

"You heard me say yesterday that I will not rest until the elven nations bow to the true heir. Both of those are mine and they do not fit, and you are entitled to ask which one is the lie.

"Neither. The crown is the instrument and the peace is the thing, and the only way I have ever been able to imagine the killing stopping is one hand on the whole of it and that hand being mine.

"I know exactly what that sentence is. Every man who ever emptied a country said it first, in that order, meaning it. I have read the minutes. I am not going to insult you by claiming I am the exception." He put his hand back on the rail. "All I will tell you is that I know it, and that knowing it has not changed what I am going to do, and that the day it stops bothering me is the day somebody should put me down."

He went down two more treads.

"Go and get your three. If they were taken so that somebody could learn how to drive them, then you are owed every word of what you said on that stair and a good deal you did not say. I will not be anywhere near it when you collect."

Then he went the rest of the way at a run, badly, with his hand skipping off the rail at every turn, because the blinding had started an hour ago and the treads were going soft on him and he had four days of coast road and a horse at the bottom.

# PART TWO: THE CAMP ABOVE HARROWBECK

## III. WHAT HE SAID IN FRONT OF THE OFFICERS

The tent smelled of wet felt and lamp oil and cold meat, and Aeldros Korvaeth came out of it into a morning that was too bright and stood in it with his weight on the right leg.

The army was moving. Six hundred going west along the coast road in a column half a mile long, and the sound of it was boots and wheels and the particular flat clatter of scale on scale that a drow column makes when nobody is talking. The forge carts had gone at the second bell. The wounded had gone with them.

Sennuvar Vhaeloth was waiting at the tent line with the board under his arm.

"My lord Validate. The three we took in the passes."

"What about them."

"They are still on the pickets. The column is past them and the drag teams are asking whether they are coming or staying and I have to enter something."

*Enter it. Say it now, in front of eleven officers and a Vhaeloth with a board, and say it in the voice.*

"Kill them if they do not oblige," said Aeldros Korvaeth.

He said it into the middle distance, flat, at the volume he used for orders that were not to be discussed.

"They are animals. Treat them as cattle and view them as cattle, and an animal that will not work is an animal that costs you fodder. We are not High Elves and we are not wood elves and we do not compose songs about useless creatures. Enter it as I said it."

"My lord."

"The column does not wait on it. Go."

He walked out past Sennuvar Vhaeloth with the limp swinging the left through from the hip, and did not look at the faces of any of the eleven, and heard the pen start behind him before he had gone four paces.

*There. It is entered. It is entered in a Vhaeloth hand in the field book and it will be copied to Rovann inside nine days and she will read it in her own gallery and it will be exactly what she wanted said.*

*And every officer on this ground now knows what the order was, which means no officer on this ground has any business coming down to the pickets to check on it.*

---

## IV. THE PICKETS

They had put them in the old stock enclosure behind the burnt mill, because it had a wall on three sides and a floor of packed clay and because that is where you put things you do not want walking.

Three.

The large one lay along the north wall with the left wing out flat because the left wing could not fold any more. The other two were smaller and were against the east side, and one of those was a third the size of the other and had the short crest of something that had not moulted twice.

The stays were Rovann work and the sight of them was the thing Aeldros had been not looking at for nine days.

Iron pins, eleven inches, driven through the web at the wing root where the membrane runs thinnest and seated into blocks of quarried stone sunk in the clay. Two per wing on the large one. One each on the smaller. The heads of the pins were hooded in a housing of grey alloy and the housings held a stay-light, and the stay-light was on, and it made a white glow at the pin head that had not gone out in nine days and would not go out for nine more.

A stay does not hurt. That is the whole design and it is the part that had been keeping him awake. The light in the housing is a suppressant. It sits on the Shell and it holds it shut, and an animal that would ordinarily be able to spend something on the pain and on the wing and on getting up cannot spend anything at all, and so it lies there entirely conscious with eleven inches of iron in the meat of it and nothing in the world to put against that.

Four guards on the gate. All four on their feet, all four facing out, which told him what they thought of the enclosure.

"You can go. I will take care of this."

The corporal hesitated for about a second and a half.

"My lord, the order was."

"You heard the order. You are being told a second thing now, which is to go." He did not raise his voice. "Take your men to the column. Do not come back for the gate and do not come back for the stays and do not send anybody. If anyone asks, they were dealt with."

He waited until the last of them was over the rise.

Then Aeldros Korvaeth stood alone in a stock enclosure with three wyverns and discovered that he was frightened of them in a way that had nothing to do with eleven years of anything.

*I have stood in front of Eressean line infantry at forty feet. I have taken a ridge eleven times. I have never once in my life been afraid of a thing I could not argue with.*

*They cannot be talked to, they cannot be bought, and there is no account between us that either of us can read. If that one gets up, I do not have a doctrine for it.*

---

## V. THE HAND

The large one growled when he came inside the wall.

It came up out of the floor of the chest and went through the clay and up through the soles of his boots, and the stay-light at the near pin flared white and stepped it down and the sound cut off in the middle like a rope going. That was worse than the growl. He had made a living for eleven years out of things that could not be put out, and he stood in a stock pen and watched a thing be put out mid-breath by a housing the size of his fist.

He went forward anyway, and he went forward on the bad leg because the bad leg was the one that would not run.

Eleven feet. Nine. The eye tracked him the whole way and did not blink and the colour in the back of it was the colour of the inside of a storm.

He put his hand flat on the shoulder plate.

It was cold. That was the first thing and it was the thing that undid him, because he had expected the heat of a large animal and got a surface like a wall in winter, and a body that cold in a body that size is a body that has been spending nothing for nine days while the wound spends for it.

"Do not worry."

He said it in Rovann, which the thing did not speak, and he knew it did not speak it, and he said it anyway.

"I do not think I can kill you." His hand stayed where it was. "I am going to try to set you free."

---

## VI. WHAT HE COULD NOT DO

He got the first pin out with a crow bar and a mallet and it took him nineteen minutes and he was sick twice.

The stone block came up with the pin because nine days of a body pulling against it had cracked the clay around the seating, and the pin came through the web the way a nail comes out of green wood, which is to say not cleanly, and the large one made no sound at all because the stay was still live on the other three. He got the housing off the pin head and killed the light with the key and threw the housing over the wall.

Then the second. The third. The fourth took him longest because it had seated crooked and had to come out the way it had gone in.

And when they were all out, and the four housings were dark on the other side of the wall, and the Shell came back to a thing that had not been allowed to hold one for nine days, the large one tried to get up and could not.

That was the part he had not planned for and should have.

Nine days of no fodder, no water past what a bucket detail throws at a picket, four penetrating wounds at the wing root going the wrong colour at the margins, and a hamstring at the shoulder cut with a hook meant to take the joint without killing. It was not chained any more and it was still not going anywhere, and neither were the other two, and the smallest had not lifted its head off the clay the whole time he had been working.

So he tried to heal it.

He had never done it. Nobody had ever taught him and he had no business attempting it and he knew both of those things while he was doing it, and he put his hands on the shoulder and reached for the wound the way he had watched field surgeons reach, and gave.

Nothing went in.

He is a man who has spent his entire practice learning to hold a thing shut. Eleven years of taking the radiant share out of a fire and refusing to let it leave, which is one operation performed in one direction with the whole of his architecture built around performing it. Asked to run the other way, his Shell did what a door does when you pull the handle the wrong direction, which is nothing, politely, for as long as you like.

He tried four times and got four nothings, and on the fourth one his hands were shaking.

*I can black a village. I can hold the light out of a burn for two hours and carry it in my own hip until the fuel is gone. I have been told since I was sixty-one that I have the finest working in the Under-Houses.*

*And I cannot put one thing into one animal.*

---

## VII. WHAT HE COULD

He sat back on his heels in the clay and looked at his own left hand for a while, and at the dead skin over the back of it that did not sweat and did not tan and had not felt anything in eleven years.

There was one direction he could run.

He had a reservoir. It was not Essence and it was not healing and it was not anything a surgeon would recognise, and it was eleven years of radiant share taken out of other people's fires and stored in the only container he had ever had for it. It had been going into him a village at a time since Ollavaeth. It had cooked the tissue along that whole side and thickened it and taken the sweat out of it and given him the limp.

He had never once put any of it anywhere else.

The animal in front of him was cold.

Not injured-cold. Nine-days-cold, the kind that is a body shutting rooms it cannot afford to heat, and a cold body cannot clot and cannot fight infection and cannot do one useful thing with a wound even after the iron comes out of it.

He put both hands flat on the shoulder plate, and opened his left side, and let it go.

It went out of him as heat and nothing else. No light, because there was never any light in it. It came off his hip and his flank and his shoulder in a wave that made the air over the clay shimmer and stink, and it went into a body the size of a barn the way water goes into sand, and the plate under his palms came up from winter-wall to something with an animal in it.

He felt it leave, which he had not expected at all.

Eleven years of carrying a thing, and the first sensation of not carrying it was not relief. It was the lightness of a man who sets down a bag he had forgotten he was holding. Then finds out he had been leaning on it.

He was still giving when the great head came round and the muzzle hit his chest and knocked him flat on his back in the clay.

---

## VIII. THE ORDER IT CHOSE

He did not get up immediately, because getting up on that leg from flat requires a hand and a plan.

The head stayed over him. Serrated horn in three ranks against a bright sky and an eye the size of a shield boss eleven inches from his face, and a stink of ozone and hot slate and nine days of a body eating itself, and Aeldros Korvaeth lay on his back in a stock pen and waited to find out.

The muzzle moved. Away from him, and left, and stopped.

It was pointed at the east wall.

At the larger of the two smaller ones, which had the same scale pattern across the brow and had been lying with its head toward the north wall for nine days, which he had noticed on the second day and had explained to himself as coincidence.

Then the muzzle moved again, further along, and stopped a second time.

At the small one. At the one a third the size that had not lifted its head.

Then the eye came back to him.

It did not have a word and he did not have a word and the whole of the transaction took less than four seconds and there was no part of it he misunderstood.

*Not me.*

*Her. Then the young one.*

*And I am the only thing in this pen that is still refusing what it needs, and it has been nine days, and it is telling me the order to work in.*

He got his elbow under him and got up on the bad leg with his hand on a wall that was not there and did it anyway, and stood in the clay with his own heat going out of him in a shimmer and his left side emptying for the first time in eleven years.

He looked at the two on the east wall.

Then Aeldros Korvaeth picked up the crow bar and the mallet and walked across the pen to the female with the pins in her, and got down on the good knee, and set the claw of the bar under the first housing.

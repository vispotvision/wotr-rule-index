# On Foot

*The coast road, eleven miles east of the causeway. Immediately after the parley.*
*Part One POV: Ruvaen Aloreth. Part Two POV: Aeldros Korvaeth.*
*Dova'Kan, Xmere, Gorgi and Baraxes are not written; their beats stay open.*

---

# PART ONE: THE ROAD BEHIND THE LINE

## I. WHAT IT COST THEM TO WALK

Ruvaen Aloreth saw what they had done before either of them said a word, and it stopped him where he was.

Two men on the road. On foot. No shadow on the ground, nothing over the sea, nothing standing off the flank where four thousand men would have seen it and would have gone home and told their villages about it for forty years.

*He left them behind.*

*I told him on a stair what the weight of a dragon on my ground would read as, and he heard it, and he has walked eleven miles up a coast road carrying a hammer the size of a gate post so that no man in either of these armies can point at anything and call it foreign strength.*

*I did not ask for that. I would not have asked for it. It is the single most expensive courtesy anybody has ever done me and it has cost him the only advantage he had.*

He came down off Pegasus, which he had not intended to do again before contact, and did it on the far side so the horse stood between himself and the hammer, because the animal had four thousand men and a drow army in its head already and did not need a stranger the size of a door.

"Noted," he said. "All of it, and by me personally, and it will be entered in those words."

Then the rest of it, fast, because the drow line had started doing something on the right and he had about ninety seconds.

"Three things and then I have to be somewhere.

"One. My word, and you may hold me to it in any form you like. There is not a wyvern in Vaelmarr. Not in the city, not on the causeway, not in the sea-gate barracks, not in any holding under my charter. There never has been. You can go there yourself, today, with no escort and no permission from me, and if anybody at that gate asks you your business you may tell them the Prince of Eresse invited you to search his own city and they will stand aside. Take anyone with you. Take the wing you left behind, I do not care any more.

"Two. On the tools." He looked once at the man with the short blades, and gave him the whole of it and not a softened version, because a softened version would have been worse. "I think the pattern came out of her capital. Not from a smith on a ridge, not from a company quartermaster improvising. Out of the middle of it. And I will not know until I am standing in it, which is the thing I am on this road to do.

"I also told a dragon on a quarry stair that I was certain the knowledge came out of my own Archive, and I have not stopped being able to believe both of those, and I cannot make them fit. Somebody knew where wyverns were going a season before they went there. I have two candidates on two sides of a war and no way to reconcile them and I am not going to pretend to you that I have."

*And there is a third that neither of us has said aloud, which is that the knowledge did not come from this continent at all.*

*I will not put that in front of the man with the blades. Not because it is cruel. Because it is a guess, and he will take it and go, and I have watched what a certainty does to a man for eleven years and I have been the man.*

---

## II. THE THIRD THING

"Three. Him."

He tipped his chin at the one with the two short blades held low.

"You want to hit somebody and you have not decided who yet. I know the state. I have been in it since I was sixty-eight and I have never once been out of it, and I am not going to lecture you about it on a road.

"What I will tell you is that I have four thousand men behind me who feel exactly the same and most of them have less reason than you do, and the only useful thing I have learned in eleven years of standing in front of them is that the state is not the problem. The state is fuel. The problem is that it will take the nearest available shape and call that the answer, and the nearest available shape is almost never the right one.

"Your three were taken with equipment nobody on this road designed. The man who signed for it is over there and I am going to kill him today, and I still do not know whether he drew the pattern or was handed it, and neither do you, and if you find out first I want the name before you use it."

The drow line had moved.

Not the whole of it. Six teams off the centre coming forward through the front rank to the open ground, and each team was pushing something on a low carriage and each carriage had a frame on it about the height of a man.

He had them at four hundred yards, which was every inch of range he had left.

*Casters.*

*Windlass, cord drum, and a socket on the frame at about chest height. That is not artillery. Artillery has a bed and a trail and it is not built to be pushed by four men at a walk. That is a thing built to put a line out sixty or eighty yards and stick it in something and then hold on.*

*Six of them, and the hooks come out of the ammunition carts behind, and there is a haft crew standing ready behind each one with nothing in their hands yet.*

"Sergeant."

Hesh Orovain was there inside four seconds.

"Two thousand off the right, at the double, and put them wide toward the shingle and have them stop at three hundred and not one yard closer." He was already going back up onto the horse. "Not a charge. I want a wall of men between those carriages and this road and I want it standing still. Nobody looses. Nobody closes. If a caster team so much as levels a frame, they go through it and they do not stop for anything.

"And Sergeant. Get word down the line, all of it, front to back. Nothing with wings is on our side of this road. Nothing. If any man sees anything in the air today it is not ours and it is not theirs either and he is to shout it and stand still."

---

## III. THE THING HE SAID

Two thousand men came off the right and went wide across the shingle at the double, and the sound of them going was gravel and harness and four hundred men swearing at once.

He watched the casters come up and settle.

"You are likely to see," he said, without turning round, "whether the thing I think is the thing.

"And it does not surprise me. It has never surprised me. The drow have been doing this to wildlife since before there was an Accord to stop them. Nine hundred years of the deep roads and every one of those roads went through something's breeding ground, and they have a word in Rovann for an animal that has outlived its usefulness and no word at all for one that has not been weighed yet. It is not cruelty with them. It is filing."

*And that is a true sentence and a cheap one, and I have just said it out loud in front of two men who came to this road on foot out of a courtesy I did not earn, and I said it because it is easier to be right about a people than to be careful about a man.*

*She would have taken the skin off me for it.*

He did not take it back. There was no time and it would have been worse.

"Vaelmarr is mine and it is still mine and I am not asking you for anything," he said instead. "If you see a wyvern in chains on this field, that is yours and it is yours entirely. Nobody on my side of this road will be within forty yards of it. I gave that order before either of you came up the road, and Hesh Orovain has it in a Common hand on a tally board, and you may read the board."

Then he put the helm on and took the reins in both hands and went back up the line at a canter toward the front of four thousand men with the white hair coming out under the back of it, and the dust of two thousand more going wide across the shingle on his right hand.

---

# PART TWO: THE KORVAETH LINE

## IV. THE REPORT

"Sire."

Aeldros Korvaeth was eleven paces back from his own front rank with his hands behind his back and his weight on the right leg, and had been standing like that since he came up the road from the parley.

"Sire, the right. Ollovan has a glass on it. He says there is a wyvern with them."

*No.*

"Where."

"Behind their right, sire. Past the shingle. He says the shape is wrong for a horse and too big for a man and it has not moved in four minutes."

The caster teams were already coming forward. He had not given that order and had not needed to, because standing orders exist so that a commander does not have to think about the obvious, and he had signed the standing order himself in the spring.

*Six casters. Ninety-one hooks in the carts. Three haft crews to a frame and every one of those men drilled on it for four months by a Fourth-company sergeant who has never seen the animal he was drilling against.*

*I have the only army in the world that can do this and I built it and it took a season and I wrote my name at the bottom of the requisition.*

"Sire, if you give the word we fire the hooks."

"You do not fire anything."

It came out harder than he had meant it and he watched the man's face take it and put it away, and he brought it back down inside two breaths, because a commander who snaps at the first report gets a second report that has been improved on the way to him.

"Level nothing. Seat nothing. The frames stay down and the cord stays off the drum until I say a word out loud, in front of you, using your name."

"Sire."

"And hear the rest of it, because you are going to have to carry it to five other crews and I want the wording.

"If that thing is in this war, if it comes at this line with a rider on it or without one, you put it down and I will sign for that myself and nobody will hang. If it is not in this war, if it is standing behind their right because it has nowhere else on this continent to stand, then you will go out there on your own feet and cut it loose from whatever is on it and you will do that before you come back to me.

"Say it back."

The man said it back. He got the second clause wrong and was made to do it again.

---

## V. WHAT HE WENT TO LOOK AT

Then Aeldros Korvaeth walked out through his own front rank to see it with his own eyes, which is not a thing a commander does at eleven hundred yards from an enemy line, and did it anyway at that limp with six hundred men watching him go.

The wind was off the sea and the light was bad and he took the glass off Ollovan and put it up one-handed.

The shape behind the Eressean right was a man.

A large one, in no colours, standing entirely still with something across one shoulder, and beside him a second one, smaller, holding a blade in each hand low against the thighs.

Not a wyvern. Not anything with wings.

Nothing on that road had wings. He would have staked his name on it, and his name was the only thing he owned worth staking.

*Ollovan is twenty-six. Sixteen days above ground and his eyes are going the way they all go, and he saw a big shape at eleven hundred yards through sea haze and his head supplied the rest, because for four months every drill this army has run has been about wyverns.*

*And if I had been forty feet further back, or tired, or angry, or the man I was in the spring, I would have said the word.*

*Six casters. Ninety-one hooks. At two strangers standing on a road doing nothing at all.*

He gave the glass back and stood in the open with the wind coming across and the whole of the Eressean line four hundred yards off his front, and did not go back immediately.

*Four days ago I knelt in clay and pulled eleven inches of iron out of a wing root with a crow bar and I have not slept since.*

*And the order that nearly went out just now would have gone out in my voice, from my own standing instruction, off a pattern I signed for, at something that is not even there.*

*Nobody would have called that a crime. There is no article. There would not have been an entry.*

*That is the whole problem with the way we file things and I have been eleven years working it out and I got there four days too late to be any use to anybody.*

He turned and went back through his own front rank, and the men opened for him and closed behind him, and he took his place eleven paces back and put his hands behind his back at the small of it and stood.

"Runners," he said. "All six crews. Frames down, cord off the drum, and nothing moves until I use the crew chief's name out loud. Anybody who levels a frame before that is off the crew and walking home."

Then he looked up the road at four thousand spears and a white horse coming along the front of them at a canter, and set his feet square, and waited for the other man to start it.

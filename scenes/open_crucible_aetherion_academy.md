# Open Crucible — Aetherion Academy

*Aetherion Academy. The broken cold room under the south range. Continuous.*

Mu-jin's body was coming apart, and he knew it the way he knew everything, precisely.

His calves had gone grey-green from the knee down, and the smell of them rose round him sweet and thick. His thighs were knotted wood. The burn on his right thigh had finally arrived, all of it, and kept arriving. His lungs were filling. His blood was wine. He lay on his hip in the black with his legs folded under him, and the vault turned around him in a long slow spiral, the ribs of the ceiling wheeling past the grey hole and the storm and back again, and the lens was in another man's hand.

He got his left hand under him. He pushed himself up onto his knees, and the needles in his calves tore as he rose, and he did not feel it yet.

Then he reached across his body with his left hand and took hold of his dead right hand by the wrist, and lifted it, and pressed the two palms together in front of his chest. The dead fingers lay against the living ones, cold and slack. He held them there. Fingers to fingers, the heels of the hands together, the way a child is taught to hold its hands at a shrine.

He was swaying. Everything was spiralling.

*Is this the end for me?*

The thought came quite calmly, in Concord Common, in his teaching voice.

*Is this the way I go out? On my knees in a cellar, drunk on my own blood, with a lesson half explained?*

And somewhere very far away and very near, in a room with a table and two cups on it, Geuk-hon stood up from his chair.

Mu-jin heard the chair. That was all. The scrape of wooden legs on a wooden floor, in a room in Hon-guk he had sat in once and had not been back to, where a man who had given him no name had told him to put down the arm and count what was left. The chair went back. Someone stood. The second cup was still on the table, still full.

*Count what is left.*

He counted.

He had his reserve, five hundred million and more, though no amount of it could out-pour chemistry. He had a dead arm and a dying pair of legs. And he had the stone, choked under a skin of sulphur, still at the centre of him, still what he was.

Essence went out of him, inward first.

It did not go to the room. It went into his own body, a pocket of it, every channel he had turned toward the inside, and he let Lapis go with it. A body is a furnace full of catalysts, and it had never been anything else. The liver is a vat of them. Every cell has its own small enzymes, the body's own philosophers' stones, patient and slow, each one built to take one poison and hurry it downhill into something harmless. They had only ever needed time.

He gave them none. He made them hurry.

The alcohol in his blood went first. The liver's enzymes took it apart, alcohol to the sharp intermediate that makes a drinker's face flush, to vinegar, to water and air, in a few heartbeats instead of a long night. The heat of it came off him. Steam rose from his collar and from the backs of his hands, from his hair, in the cold of the vault, as though he had stepped out of a bath into winter. The spiral slowed. The vault steadied. The glyph chains he had not been able to read swam back into focus one at a time, doubled, then single, then sharp.

The acid in his thighs went next. It was carried off and turned back into sugar the way a resting runner's liver turns it, in a tide instead of a trickle, and the knotted wood of his legs loosened all at once and left them weak and shaking.

The sulphide in his lungs went last, and it went the hardest. His own cells knew how to burn small doses of it, the way they burn food, and he made them burn it in a flood. And the sulphur on the stone itself, the poison that had been sitting on Lapis since the first breath of miasma, met the heat and the hurry and began to burn off him.

His catalyst began to fume.

A faint blue flame ran over his skin. It was sulphur's own flame, the soft cornflower blue of brimstone burning, and it crept over the backs of his hands and along his jaw and through his overgrown hair. It gave almost no heat. It was the way a refinery burns a poisoned bed clean, with heat and air, until the active places come up bright again. The blue ran off him and guttered out. And under the sulphur reek, faint, then clear, came the smell of struck matches.

He could not make the dead flesh of his calves alive again. Nothing he was could run the world uphill. But he could stand on it.

"No," Mu-jin said. His voice cracked and steadied. "I refuse to die here."

He got one foot under him, and then the other, and stood, and stumbled. His legs took his weight like a pair of crutches cut too short. He swayed forward a step, and something came up out of his chest that was not phlegm, and blood ran out of the corner of his mouth and drooled down over his chin onto his collar. He did not wipe it. His palms were together.

Malphas had stopped looking through the lens.

He stood five feet away with the round glass lowered in his good hand and his crown of spurs wet in the grey light, watching the steam come off the man he had been taking apart. For the first time his face was doing something he had not decided it would do.

Mu-jin breathed in, and it hurt, late, the way everything hurt now.

"Domain Expansion."

The vault took a breath.

"Open Crucible."

---

A Domain seals. That is what every practitioner learns: draw a boundary, and inside it your terms are the terms, and the strength of the thing is the strength of its wall. Every counter to every great Domain ever catalogued begins by breaking that wall.

His had no wall.

It went out from his joined hands to two hundred feet in every direction, through the broken vault and the old charnel yard and up through the hole into the hail. It excluded nothing. Malphas came inside it, and the carter at his shoulder, and Ansel in his chains, and Velder against his pier. The dead girl lay on the floor. The Black Ground lay heavy at the far end, and the yellow gas in the niches. The storm overhead came in, and the bear inside the storm. And Kwon Mu-jin came in too, with every drop of his own blood on the black flags.

An open crucible accepts the whole charge. That is the first step of an assay. Any assayer will tell you that the sample you exclude is the sample you have already decided the answer for.

Malphas's eyes widened.

He had stood beside this man for years and drunk what leaked off him, and he knew Lapis, and he knew the stone and its poisons. He had not known this. Whatever Mu-jin had made on the Ashgate road had been made after the reading floor, far from any table Malphas had ever sat at, and nothing of it had seeped. Mu-jin watched him understand that, a quarter-second too late, and the understanding went across his face like a crack across glass.

Then the crucible began to work.

An assay is a separation and nothing else. You bring the whole charge up together, you supply one condition, and under that condition the base content reacts and the noble content does not. What reacts melts and is drawn away into the porous walls. What does not react stays, and gathers into a bead. The principle has not changed since men first heated silver in a cup of bone.

The condition Mu-jin supplied was ownership.

Everything in the Domain was assayed against one question: does this belong to the thing carrying it?

The borrowed content went first.

It came off Malphas in sheets.

The lightning went first. Raga's strike, banked in the man's gut as stored chemistry since it poured into the Star, had never been his. It met the condition, and reacted, and could no longer hold its surface against the man it sat in, and it slid. It came out of him in a pale sheet, a play of colour running along its trailing edge the way it runs across molten silver in the last moment of a cupel, and it went outward. Mu-jin's Pressure went with it, every scrap Malphas had eaten off the air. The girl's share of overflow went, and the carter's, and the boy's, the banked surplus of a hundred ferments that had never been his to keep. All of it slid off him and went to the walls.

The carter went down.

His thread had held through the Calcination. It did not hold through this, because the seed in his chest belonged to Malphas and the body around it belonged to the carter, and an assay knows the difference. The seed came out of him and slid away into the field. He sat down on the floor where he stood, the way a tired man sits on a step, and then he lay back, and his breath fogged once in the cold and did not fog again.

On the floor by the chains, Ansel stopped pulling.

The boy's thread was the thinnest of all, and it went the quickest. The formula in his chest let go of him and slid away, and his steaming hands went slack in the field, and his face turned toward the hole and the grey light. Whether there was anyone left behind his eyes to see it, Mu-jin could not read and would never know.

Against the pier, Velder Crace felt the crucible pass through him and come out the other side, having found nothing on him that was not his own. His left hand lay on his boot.

What was left on Malphas was smaller, and it was entirely his.

*There,* Mu-jin thought. *That is the reading. The first honest one anyone has taken off him since the retting ponds.* His own Ardency at his own Depth, on his own Dissolution. His own Mortalis current, which he had only ever borrowed and which the assay could not decide about, so that it flickered, half on him and half sliding. The crown of spurs stayed. Those were his. His own Crystal had grown them, and they stood out of his scalp wet and pale and real. Underneath all the borrowed weight there was a man of the tenth Stage with a D-Grade body and a cooked left hand, and it was not small and it was not nothing. It would still have killed nine of every ten men breathing.

But it was not what he had been ten seconds ago, and he had never once had to fight anybody with it.

---

The walls of a crucible have to be porous, and they have to be made of something that will not react with what they take.

Cupels are made of bone ash.

Mu-jin had learned that on the Ashgate road, at the moment the stripped content reached the boundary of his Domain and had to go somewhere, and the boundary of his Domain was not in the air. It was in him. It was in every long bone, and the Lattice through them.

It came into him now.

The lightning came first. Raga's own strike, given back by the assay, went into his femurs, and his thighbones filled with it, a pressure from the inside of the marrow outward. It was not pain. It was the unbearable fullness of a vessel taking on more than it was fired to hold. His Pressure came home into his upper arms, into the dead one as well as the living, and the dead arm jerked once at his side. The overflow of a hundred ferments came into his pelvis and his ribs and the vault of his skull, the rot Malphas had banked for years, and his bones took it because bone is what a cupel is made of. Bone ash is calcium phosphate. It is chosen because it does not react with what it absorbs and because it is porous enough to take it. A man's skeleton is the same material in a different arrangement.

But a man's skeleton is not fired for this.

His legs were dead from the knee down and could not take their share, so it went higher. His lungs were half full of gas and fluid. His Crystal had spent nearly four hundred million honest units across a morning, and it held the boundary open on four hours of migraine stacked behind his eyes.

*Reserve 481,600,000 EU.* Open Crucible had cost him forty-two and a half million to open, and the bones were paying the rest in a coin the ledger did not count.

He held it open anyway.

*Nine tenths of an assay is the temperature.* The old lesson came up in him in his own teaching voice, the way it always did when he was frightened. *Too low and the crust seals the bead in, and you learn nothing, and you destroy the sample. Too high and you lose part of what you were trying to weigh.*

*I have no thermometer. I have never had one. I have only ever had the one instrument, which is that I can tell what a thing is worth by looking at it. And I have perhaps four seconds of honest judgement left in me.*

The Domain held. The last of the base content went off Malphas, and the play of colour ran across the bead, and finished.

Malphas stood in the middle of it, stripped, swaying on his own two feet for the first time in years. He looked at his hands. He breathed out, and the breath was thin and yellow still, the one poison that had always been his own. Then he looked up at Mu-jin, at the steam and the blood and the blue sulphur flame still guttering in the hair. His eyes went to the shaking legs and stopped there, reading.

The crack across his face closed.

"Bring nothing borrowed," Malphas said slowly. He was quoting. It was the sentence written at the bottom of Mu-jin's own notes on the Ashgate road, which Malphas had never read and should not have known, and he said it in Mu-jin's Latin cadence, with Mu-jin's Sum-gol vowel. The crucible had no wall. Everything inside it came in, and everything Mu-jin knew was in the field with them, seeping across fifteen feet of open air at the speed of ink in water. "That is your counter, is it not? Bring nothing borrowed." He nodded to himself, like a reader marking a margin. "Then I will use only what is mine."

He opened his good hand. The lens dropped from it onto the black floor and lay there, unbroken, staring up at the hole.

Nihiloth was his. It always had been.

Somewhere at the far end of the vault, the last lamp that still burned on the wall began, very quietly, to go out.

# The Report

*Beneath Vaeloris. The council chamber.*

---

Nothing.

That was the first thing and Corwin Sesk had been turning it over for the length of the descent, which had taken a quarter of an hour and had gone down through architecture that nobody had cut. The corridors smelled of nothing at all. Stone, damp, lamp oil, the mineral cold that every deep place in the world gives off: none of it, anywhere, at any point on the stair. He had worked in cellars and gaols and one abandoned mine and a tannery, and every one of them had told him where he was before his eyes did, and this told him nothing, and after a quarter of an hour of it the inside of his own nose had begun to feel like a fault in his equipment.

His boots made sound. That much worked. Kaalabad's made more, out ahead of him and slightly to the left, the halberd across the left shoulder and the helmet sitting a few degrees off true where a strap had stretched and nobody had shortened it.

They came out onto the floor of the chamber and Sesk stopped where he was put.

The table was long and black and polished to a depth that made the eye keep going after the surface had finished. Around it stood the Generals, and Sesk kept his eyes off them, on the grounds that his mother had raised him with a certain amount of sense. He looked at the table instead. He looked at the table because the table had a problem.

At the far end, near the head, a patch of it had gone wrong.

Cracked was the word he reached for and cracked was wrong, and he was a professional and knew the difference. The polish had stopped being polish. The edges of the inlay had lost their line, not chipped, just less certain about where they were, and under the lip of the table there was a small conical heap of fine grey powder on the floor that had gathered the way flour gathers under a leaking sack.

Sesk had no account for it whatsoever and he made himself stop looking at it.

*I am going to think about that later, in a room, on my own. And I am going to do the thing I have done every night for eleven days, which is decide that I do not want to know.*

At the head of the table, the Regent stood.

Sesk bowed his head, held it the length of two breaths, and came up straight, because grovelling was a thing that read as weakness to every employer he had ever had and he had made a policy of it at twenty-six.

"My lord Kujo. The Word of Judger. Day eleven."

---

"I will give the report in the order I keep the ledger, and I would ask leave to give the whole of it before any part of it is judged, because the last part changes the first part."

He got his stylus out from behind his ear and did not look at it.

"The physical work is complete to the limit of what can be done without killing him. I will state it plainly and without decoration because I understand this room has no appetite for either.

"Four molars out of the lower jaw on the second day, which was before I arrived and which I stopped. Eleven strips of flesh off the thighs, six left and five right, taken down to fascia, three places opened past the fascia to the sheath and closed off. Ten nails, one to each fingertip, seated into the bone. Both shoulders dislocated by suspension, the right twice. Left kneecap broken with a mallet on the ninth day and set crooked on purpose so that it could be broken again in the same place. He has been on the frame eleven days apart from the hours we take him down. He is fed. He is watered twice a day and given enough, on the standing instruction.

"That is the whole inventory and there is not a great deal left on the list. What remains is eyes, tongue, hands and the genitals, and every one of those is terminal to his usefulness, and I will not do any of them without an instruction in a form I can produce afterward."

He turned the stylus once between two fingers.

"Now the part I came here to say.

"He is getting harder.

"I want that understood as a measurement. It is arithmetic and I am lodging it as arithmetic. In my trade a man softens on a curve. It is the most reliable thing about the work. Day one he is defiant, day three he is bargaining, day five he is telling you things you did not ask for to see which ones you want, and by day eight he has stopped being a person with a position and become a person with a preference for it stopping. I have had bricklayers open in half this. I have had a Dawi hammerman scream until he was sick, and that one had kept both his knees and eight of his fingernails.

"The Word is going the wrong way along the curve. He was further from me on day nine than he was on day four. On the eleventh day he corrected my Latin."

Somewhere behind him, out along the wall, one of the Generals moved. Sesk did not turn around.

"So I will say the thing that will cost me, because you will find out and it is better arriving in my own voice.

"I tested him."

---

"On the seventh night I left the chain slack. Slack, my lord, and I mean the word exactly: enough that a man with one working arm could have got the ring off the hook inside a minute, and I sat in the passage with the door on the latch and a lamp out and I waited the whole night to see what he would do."

He let that sit exactly as long as it needed to.

"He did nothing. He hung there with the slack in the chain and left it alone, and in the morning I came in and took it up again and he watched me do it and said one word about it, which was good morning, and my lord, that is the single most frightening thing that has happened to me in twenty-two years of this trade.

"Because the chain is not holding him. The chain is a courtesy he extends to me every morning when I take up the slack. He is staying."

Sesk's hand had gone slightly damp on the stylus and he changed hands, which was the only thing his body did in that chamber that he could not stop.

"And a man who is staying cannot be broken by anything I am able to do to his body, because his body is not what is holding him in the room. I have been hammering at a door for eleven days and the door has been open the entire time and the man is standing on the other side of it out of an arrangement.

"So I would ask, formally: do I pick up the measures."

He paused.

"And then, having asked it, I would like to argue against it, and I want both things in the record, because I have watched better contractors than me get killed by giving a lord only the half of the answer he wanted."

---

"The arrangement was four Attestants and a scriptorium."

The powder under the table edge had grown. He was certain of that and had no way to prove it and would not have said it aloud in that room for a year's fee.

"He came here on his own feet because he was given a note listing where four of his people were, in the present tense, and asked to come and discuss a procedural matter. He accepted the trade. He believes he is buying their lives and the books with his own hide, and he did the arithmetic on the road and he checked it twice, and he told me so on the fourth day in about that many words.

"My lord. Those four are gone."

He said it into the black table.

"They went out through the low cloister in the second week of the shutdown, along with a great many other churchgoers, and they went because nobody in our organisation had thought to close a door that has been unlocked since the reign before last, and I have the roster and I have the names and I checked it myself because I could not believe it either. There is nobody left to threaten. The scriptorium is a room with shelves in it. The leverage that put him on that frame ceased to exist somewhere around the day I was hired and neither he nor I knew it, and I am the one who found out, and I have not told him.

"He is hanging in a cell paying for something we cannot take away because we have already lost it."

He put the stylus back behind his ear.

"Which brings me to why I would counsel against escalation.

"At present he believes he is buying four lives. That belief is the only wall between this operation and a man who I now judge could take the block apart at his convenience. He has not tested it because he does not need to and because he thinks testing it costs four people. The moment he learns the truth, the arrangement lapses, and the reason he is staying lapses with it, and I am the man standing nearest to him when that happens.

"Escalate and I increase the chance he finds out. A tongue answers no questions once it is out of the head. Take a man's eyes and you have removed everything he was still protecting, and with it every reason he had to be careful. And every additional person we bring down there to do heavier work is another mouth that might say the wrong thing in a corridor within his hearing, and my lord, on the ninth day I established that he can hear the guard's boots stop four paces short of the door and I do not know what else he can hear.

"So my recommendation, for whatever a contractor's recommendation is worth in this room, is that the physical work stops. Stops entirely, and I am choosing that word over the softer ones. Keep him fed, keep him watered, keep the chain up, keep the arrangement alive in his head, and put the effort into making certain nobody ever tells him."

---

"One more item and then I am finished with the ledger.

"On the second day I told him the arrangement stood. I said the words. Four alive and the books untouched, and he asked me to say it plainly and I said it plainly, and he thanked me." Sesk's jaw worked once. "That was true when I said it. I have since established that it was already false by about nine days and I did not know, and I want it in the record that I did not know, because a man in my trade lives on his prisoners believing his statements of fact and I have exactly one asset in that cellar and it is that.

"He has never caught me in a lie. Eleven days, across the nails and the knee and whether I was coming back in the afternoon. I told him on the ninth day that I was going to break his kneecap and I told him which one and I told him why, and then I did it, and he thanked me for that too.

"If I am now instructed to tell him something false about those four, I will do it, and I will do it well, and I want you to understand that I am spending the only thing I have. After that every fact I give him is worth what any other man's is worth, and I stop being useful to this operation the day he stops believing me."

Nothing answered him.

The chamber had been built to carry a voice intact and it carried his and gave him back nothing at all, and Sesk stood in the middle of that with his hands at his sides and discovered the specific thing about silence that he had used on other men for twenty-two years and had never once been on the wrong end of.

Silence makes you keep talking.

He knew it. He had built a career on it. He knew exactly what was being done to him and he opened his mouth anyway, and the sentence that came out was the one he had decided in the passage on the way down that he was not going to say.

"There is a third option and I dislike it and I will give it to you because you will think of it in a week and it will go worse for me then than now.

"He does not know they escaped. So he does not need them to be alive. He needs to believe they are.

"Give me four people. Any four. Their names are irrelevant and so is their history. They have to be describable accurately from a distance and they have to be producible, once, at range, in poor light, and after that they are a name on a list I read out to him on a schedule. He will not be able to verify any of it. He is on a frame in a cellar. All he has is what I tell him, and I have eleven days of him believing what I tell him because I have never once lied to him about the physical work and he has noticed that."

He stopped.

He looked at the small heap of grey powder under the table edge and thought about a thing at some distance from all of this, which was that he had a daughter in Foraye whom he had not written to in five weeks, and that the letter would have to be worded carefully now.

*Twenty-two years and I have never proposed a thing I did not want to be given. That is what this place does. You come down here to make a report and you go back up having volunteered.*

"That is the whole of it, my lord," said Corwin Sesk. "I have nothing further and I would ask leave to withdraw and take an instruction in writing at your convenience."

He bowed his head, held it two breaths, came up straight, and stood there with the halberdier's shadow beside him on the black floor, waiting in a room where the air had begun to feel like something that was only there provisionally.

Nobody spoke. The heap of grey powder under the far edge of the table had grown by about the width of a thumbnail since he had come in, and Sesk stood in the enormous polite quiet with his ledger under his arm and his hands at his sides, and counted, out of twenty-two years of habit, the number of exits from the chamber.

There were four.

He did not receive an answer.

# Shiori's Findings — Aetherion Arena

Shiori does not flinch at the cigarette pointed in her direction. The word Zettaris lands. She waits until the room has finished deciding what to do with her name, and then she speaks.

"My father chose distance because he understood what proximity costs." Her voice carries none of Wystan's institutional flatness. It is warmer than that, the warmth of a woman who grew up in a house where an Immortal weeded a garden and cooked rice because the woman he loved found peace in those things. "He wanted to be a farmer. Not because he forgot what he was, but because he remembered and chose something quieter. My mother was happiest with her hands in soil and her patients in the next room, and he built his life around that happiness." She pauses, and the pause is not hesitation. It is the deliberate close of a subject that does not require further defence. "You would have to forgive him for not sending notices. He would not know where to address them."

She turns to Zaire. The motion is unhurried. She looks at the boy who called her cousin and something shifts behind her face, a recognition that has the quality of something expected and something new arriving in the same breath.

"Ma'Kovu's ward." She says it with the weight of a woman who grew up hearing that name from the other side of the family. "My father spoke of his brother, though not often, and never with the sort of detail that would have warned me someone would call me cousin in an academy arena before I had finished my first briefing." Something moves at the corner of her mouth. The shape of a smile, held back. "It is good to meet you, Zaire and Taesyn." She inclines her head to the second boy, brief and correct. "I would like to see Kushara properly. When the work allows."

The word work lands. The greeting is over, and the woman who was trained by Wystan Ashmore is the one who turns back to face the Grand Mage.

She steps forward. One measured pace that brings her beside Wystan rather than behind him. The positioning is deliberate, the stance of someone who has earned the right to stand next to her instructor and is choosing this moment to occupy that ground because what she is about to say requires it.

"Three practitioners recovered from the eastern district." Her voice changes register, tighter now, carrying the precision of someone trained to deliver findings the way a surgeon delivers a prognosis: the facts first, the interpretation second, the grief held at a professional distance that is not the same thing as absence. "Two from the northern quarter, one from a village outside the draw-grid entirely. The village had no Guild presence, no anchor-points, no containment. The practitioner there was eleven when she was taken."

A brass tap from the wardsmith's rod rings across the arena. Shiori waits for it to pass. The habit learned from a man who respects the instruments of people whose work is listening to the room.

"In the two district cases, the degradation followed a pattern I could reconstruct." She addresses Xhem directly, evidence presented the way an investigator presents to a commanding officer, weighed because she knows the man receiving it is qualified to weigh it. "Shell erosion beginning at the Third Layer and progressing inward. Consistent with forced Resonance at a frequency the Crystal was not structured to sustain. The mechanism is not corruption in the way the Night Register classification implies. It is overwriting. Someone introduced a secondary Essence Signature into the Crystal architecture and the original signature could not hold against it."

On the training floor below, the sparring pair have stopped. The younger practitioner sits on the edge of his platform with his forearms braced on his knees, breathing in the deep controlled rhythm of a body that has been pushed to the place where the lungs take over the counting. His partner stands three feet away, wraps loose around one hand. He watches the group above the arch with the particular alertness of a young person who can feel the temperature of a conversation without hearing the words.

"The village case was different." Shiori's voice drops, and the drop is for accuracy rather than effect. Her shoulders settle, the weight redistributing, the posture of someone about to say something that cost her sleep and has not stopped costing it. "There was no secondary Signature. There was absence. The Shell remained structurally intact but everything behind it, the Core, the Layers, the entire Essence architecture, had been drawn out and removed. As if someone had found a way to separate the Crystal from the practitioner while the practitioner was still breathing."

She pauses. Her left hand presses once against her thigh. A single compression and release. Then still. The same gesture Wystan made with his thumb and forefinger when he described what the word Rotted had earned. The apprentice and the teacher carrying their weight in the same places.

"My eyes see origin-points." She says it the way someone states a professional qualification rather than a bloodline inheritance. "Where a working began. Where the first alteration occurred." The how belongs to her father's blood, and her father's blood is not what Xhem asked about. "In both district cases, the origin-point traced to the same Wellspring. Mortalis."

The word sits in the arena air. Below them, the girl with the wooden sword across her knees lifts her head. She does not know what Mortalis means. But she heard the syllable the way a body hears a low frequency, something felt before it is processed, and her grip on the hilt has tightened without her deciding to tighten it.

"The Mortalis Wellspring governs the boundary between animate and inanimate Essence, the mechanism by which living tissue transitions to the inert and the Crystal releases what it once sustained." Her gaze holds Xhem's and does not yield. "In the healthy body it manifests as natural decay. What I saw carried none of that order. The Mortalis signature in those Crystals was dense enough that the Aetheric residue read as post-mortem." Two words. She lets them land the way Wystan lets things land, with air around them. "The practitioners were alive. Their Crystals were not."

A porter crosses the upper walkway behind them. Older than the one who passed earlier, grey-haired, carrying a stack of folded ward-cloth in both arms. He does not look at the group. He looks at the floor in front of his feet with the careful attention of a man who has worked in an academy long enough to know when a conversation above his clearance is happening three feet away. His footsteps are soft. The ward-cloth in his arms smells of brass polish and something sharper underneath, the astringent bite of a containment solvent that has not fully dried.

"The alchemical component is what concerns me most." Shiori does not follow the porter with her gaze. It remains on Xhem. "What was done to these practitioners is not standard Wellspring manipulation. Someone has combined Mortalis-aspect workings with a process that resembles reanimation architecture, and the process has been applied across multiple subjects with increasing precision. The subjects who survived are classified by our division as abomination-grade. The classification is generous." She lets that land. "What I saw in the village was a girl whose Crystal had been hollowed and whose body had been repurposed as a vessel for a working that was still running inside her. She was breathing. Her heart was beating. The working did not care about either of those things."

In the tiered seats, the student who had been watching the group above the arch stands. He does not leave. He moves two rows higher and angles his body so that his back is to the railing and his face is toward the arena floor. The motion is casual. The decision behind it is not. He has concluded that whatever is being discussed above him is something he would prefer not to have overheard, and the conclusion has the quality of something deliberately constructed rather than naturally arrived at.

Rovhen sees the student move. He sees the calculation in it, the careful rearrangement of a young man who has decided that not hearing is safer than hearing. The judgment in Rovhen's face is not unkind. It is the recognition of a survival instinct he has seen before, in institutions where knowing the wrong thing at the wrong time carries consequences that outlast the knowing.

*That one learned something here that was not in the curriculum.*

"The cores are accelerating." Shiori's voice pulls the room back. In common speech a core is a worked site where Aether pools and density breeds, where monsters spawn from the Saturation the way mould grows from damp. "Wherever this process has been tested, the local emergence rate has climbed by a factor we cannot explain through natural Saturation drift. Entire villages outside government protection are being pushed toward extermination. We have contracted three independent guilds for containment in the eastern district alone." A beat. "The contracts are not holding. The rate keeps climbing."

Her jaw tightens. The same motion Wystan made when he said the word Rotted. The apprentice's inheritance from the teacher, worn differently on a younger face.

"And none of this accounts for the Inquisition."

She says the name the way Wystan said The Mother. Without reverence or scorn. With the flat recognition of a second problem that has been sitting in the file behind the first, patient, waiting for someone to turn the page.

"A holy order. Organised and motivated, operating on the conviction that practitioners below a certain threshold are better eliminated than protected." Her voice does not harden. It thins, the sound of a woman who has seen what elimination looks like when performed by people who believe they are doing sacred work. "Their presence in the same districts where the Rotted have appeared is something I do not believe is coincidental. The overlap is too exact." She draws a breath. "Whether they are responding to the crisis or accelerating it is a question I do not yet have enough evidence to answer."

She stops. The way someone stops who has delivered everything she carried into the room and is waiting to see what the room does with the weight.

Her hands are at her sides. Her weight is even. She stands the way Wystan stands, the posture learned from years of watching a man who plants himself and does not move until the room has answered. But her chin sits a half-inch higher than his. The difference is the angle of a woman whose eyes see the origin of things and who has decided that looking away is not something she is prepared to do.

Below, a second wardsmith has appeared on the arena floor. Younger than the first, a woman in Guild-stamped leather with her own rod and her own slate, and she crosses to the nearest anchor-point with the quick stride of someone who has been summoned rather than scheduled. The first wardsmith meets her at the crystal and holds out the slate. The younger woman reads the number that was written, crossed out, and written again. She looks at the crystal set into the stone. She looks at the first wardsmith. Neither of them speaks. The younger woman pulls her own slate from her belt and begins her own count from the beginning.

# Spent, Not Dead

*The quarry lip above Coldwater. First light.*
*POV: Ruvaen Aloreth. RP reply. Dova'Kan, Gorgi and Xmere are not written; their beats stay open.*

---

## I. THE BOOTS

He got the left one on and stopped with the right in his hand, because the answer to the question was going to take longer than the boot and he was not going to give it over his shoulder.

The wind at the lip does not stop. It comes off eleven miles of green and hits four hundred feet of chalk and goes up the face, so that a man standing on the spoil shelf has it from underneath, pulling at the hem and pushing the dust out sideways in white ropes. The iron rail sang a little in it. Below, the four at the drinking course had their heads up and had not put them back down.

"Two things before I answer you, and the second one is the answer.

"I am not a monster."

He said it flat, the way you state a measurement, and did not decorate it.

"You have heard one side of what I said on that stair and I let you hear it, because I was angry and because I would rather be thought worse of than misunderstood in a way that gets somebody killed. I should have been specific. I will be specific now. Then I will tell you where your three are."

---

## II. SPECIFIC

"A wyvern that comes over my line because it has chosen a side is an enemy and I will treat it as one. A wyvern that comes over my line because a man with a hook and a haft put it there is not an enemy. It is a weapon, and you do not hang a weapon for what the hand did.

"I will still kill it. Understand that part and do not soften it in your own head on the way down the stair. If there are four hundred spears behind me and a thing the size of a barn coming at them, I will put it down and I will do it without a second of hesitation, because the boy holding the spear does not get to consult a court about whose idea the animal was.

"And afterward I will count. That is what you asked for and you were right to ask and you did not have to.

"Afterward I will establish whether it came or whether it was brought, and if it was brought then the account goes to whoever brought it, entered in full, with the animal recorded as a casualty of his order and not as a combatant. That is not a courtesy. That is the difference between a battle and a thing they hang men for, and my Reckoner will enter it correctly or I will get a Reckoner who will.

"Kill the driver before you congratulate yourself for killing the shield. I have had that in worse words from a Marshal with a bad knee and I did not need it twice."

*And she said it first, and she said it to a court that laughed, and I have arrived at her position by a road she would not have walked and for reasons she would not have accepted.*

"I will not helplessly murder a thing I know to be innocent. It would be the same act as killing children and I have declined to do that on four separate occasions in eleven years when doing it would have shortened this war.

"I am not asking you to take that on my face. It is on record. The record is at Velthaeir and you are welcome to the whole of it, including the fourth one, which I lost men over."

---

## III. SPENT, NOT DEAD

"Now the other thing, because you said last night that your continent is going poor, and you said it the way a man says a thing he has stopped arguing about. I am going to argue about it.

"My people have understood Wellsprings longer than anyone alive and have been insufferable about it for nine hundred years. I will grant you the insufferable. I will not grant you the conclusion that half the world has drawn out of us, which is that a spent ground is a finished ground.

"The Measurewrights' own register says it in four words and nobody reads past the paragraph. The ground is not dead. It is spent. Those are different conditions and only one of them is permanent.

"The humans have it as doctrine now. The Vessel factors price land on it. Half the New World has decided that once a country stops producing, it will never produce again, and they have decided it because it is convenient, and because a country that can never recover is a country you may as well finish emptying."

He put the right boot on.

"The Epoch of the Body did not build a world on the assumption that nothing would ever be broken. They knew. Everything they made assumed it would be torn down and would have to come back, because the only Essence conduit they had that could walk was a person, and a person heals.

"A body rejuvenates. A body gets better. It depends entirely on what is done to it and how long the doing goes on and whether anything is put back, and that is as true of four hundred miles of country as it is of a man.

"Spent is a condition, and somebody has to say so out loud before a people give up on a continent and start behaving like men in a house they have already sold.

"And I can show you one.

"There is a vein under the Sunroot terraces called the Low Draw and it went quiet in my grandmother's time. Not thinned. Quiet, the way a spring goes quiet, with the plates reading it as a dead pocket for sixty years and the Court writing off the terraces and the tenants moving off them.

"Eresse did one thing about it, and the one thing was nothing. No cutting on it. No working over it. No grazing inside the old boundary, no building, no roads. Four generations of a family called Thennow held that fence line and were paid to hold it and were laughed at for the whole of it, and the youngest of them is alive and has a bad hip and still walks it.

"It came back thirty-one years ago. Not to what it was. To about a third of what it was, in sixty-odd years of being left entirely alone, and that is the only figure in this conversation I will stand behind, and I stand behind it because I have walked the ground myself.

"That is the whole of the Eressean method, and it is why nobody buys it. There is no technique in it. There is a fence, and four generations of somebody's family, and a court that has to keep its hands off a thing for longer than any of the people who voted for it will be alive."

*And I have no idea how long. Nobody does. The Measurewrights have a hundred and forty years of series and the series is shorter than the thing it is measuring, and every number anyone gives you for recovery is a guess wearing a decimal.*

"One more and then I am finished and you will not enjoy it.

"There is a reading of Eressean restraint that we do not teach and the Measurewrights published anyway. It says the doctrine that a Wellspring must be respected and never exploited is not wisdom we inherited from the collapse. It says a people with no conduit and no pump cannot exploit a Wellspring at scale in the first place, and that we called our incapacity a virtue and have been charging for the virtue ever since.

"I think that reading is largely correct.

"I also think it does not matter, because a restraint you adopted because you were weak is still a restraint, and nine hundred years of it has left my ground able to come back. Your continent has four processes emptying it and the largest of them is that a mature dragon draws more ambient Aether than a town does, and there are hundreds of you, and Drakyssia learned that the expensive way."

He straightened up off the rail.

"I am not saying that to score. I am saying it because you asked for honest comparisons taken by the same method, and an honest comparison of your ground and mine is going to say something about your kind that you will not want in a Measurewright's hand. You should decide now whether you still want the readings."

---

## IV. THE HAFT

"Your question about the hooks. Gorgi has it and Gorgi is right and the tool says so.

"A hook meant to kill does not need a haft. You want a head and a socket and a shaft you can seat in the ground, and the weight goes into the animal and stays there. What was issued to the Fourth has a socket and a haft, which means a man holds it, and a man who holds a thing is steering it.

"You do not put a steering handle on an instrument of execution. You put one on an instrument of control.

"Six dead and three not dead is not a bad afternoon's shooting. It is a yield. Somebody wanted three and got three and the six were the cost of learning how."

---

## V. WHERE THEY ARE

"So. Your three.

"They are alive. I had them last night in the read and I have them now, and I will tell you exactly how much that is worth so that you do not build on sand.

"What I have is three reaches, narrow, low and flat, in the same place they were in yesterday, a hundred and forty miles north and east, at the Korvaeth camp above Harrowbeck. Low and flat is what a body does when it is running on nothing at all. It does not tell me a chain. It does not tell me a pen. It does not tell me what their condition is beyond the fact that they are spending very little and have been for nine days.

"Here is the part that matters and it is the reason I did not speak until I had counted it twice.

"At first light the whole of that army came off its anchor and started west. Every reach in it. Six hundred, and behind the six hundred a front I could not count with a width on it that made me take my hands off the chalk.

"The three did not go.

"They are still sitting where they were. An army that is marching does not leave three crippled animals behind out of sentiment, and it does not leave them behind because they are slow, because a wyvern that cannot fly can still be dragged and they have the drags. They left them because leaving them is the plan, or because something is holding them there that is not the army."

*Or because they are staked out, and a thing that is staked out is bait, and the man who commissioned a tool with a handle on it nine weeks before he had anything to use it on is a man who thinks that far ahead.*

"I will not press that reading harder than the evidence carries it. Three reaches, low, stationary, ninety miles behind a moving front. That is the whole of what I have, and I have given you the whole of it, and the rest of what I think is a guess with nothing under it."

---

## VI. THE FORK

"And here is where we are, and I am going to lay it out rather than steer you, because you are not mine to steer and you have been straight with me twice.

"The western bearing ends at the coast road. The coast road runs four days to the causeway. The causeway runs eleven arches to the sea gate, and behind the sea gate is Vaelmarr, which has four hundred spears on a Landward Wall that faces landward, and a hundred and ten of those are boys, and my Marshal is on it with a bad knee and a chart four days out of date.

"He has come off the Ollavaeth line. Eleven years of putting his head against that ridge and this morning he went round it. Everything I have built for a decade is a wall in the wrong direction.

"Your three are the other way. Ninety miles behind his army and a hundred and forty from here, and nothing is guarding them that I can read, and they will not be there in a week.

"I cannot do both. I have a horse at the bottom of that stair and four days of coast road between me and my own gate, and by the time I am on the causeway he will be on it too.

"You have a dragon who has offered to put you above the coast before that army makes its first mile."

He stopped and let the wind take the rest of it for a moment.

"I am not going to ask you for the lift. If I ask you for it, you are in my war by tomorrow morning and every house in the New World will have read it by the end of the month, and I told you on the stair what that costs you and I meant it and it has not stopped being true because I am the one it would help.

"Go and get your three. That is the thing that is yours, it is the thing that is disappearing fastest, and it is ninety miles behind a man who will not be looking backward for another four days."

*And it is four days I do not have, and I am giving them away in exchange for nothing, and she would not have been able to look at me for the last eleven years and might have managed it for this.*

"If you find them chained, take them. If you find out who ordered the hooks, I want the name. I will trade for it in anything I own. And if you find them staked out with nothing at all guarding them, get off the ground and do not land, because that is not neglect."

He put his hand flat on his chest to the eye beyond the lip and to the man beside him, and held it there, and took it away.

Then he went to the head of the stair.

He started down it two treads at a time, with the edge on his shoulder and the beck running white below him into eighty feet of stone course, and he did not look back up the face to see which way either of them had gone.

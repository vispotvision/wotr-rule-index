# The Axiom of the Kept

*The blackened plain under the cracked sky, the fourteenth day, at the height of the morning*

It would take more than this to tear him apart.

The Funeral of Heaven came across the plain at him like the end of the world drawn out into a spear, *VRRRRRRRRRROOOOOOOOM*, black at the tip and crimson in the coils and roaring at the edges, and the trench it plowed behind it was a quarter of a league long and smoking, and the air it passed through did not close up again after it but hung open, raw, crackling with forked red light. Faisal watched it come. He had perhaps the length of three heartbeats. In the first of them he understood what it was, layer by layer, the way he had always been able to read his brother's workings: as a sentence in a language he had spent eleven thousand years learning to answer.

The outer sheath was Cataclysm, a turbulence that would not settle, throwing off fresh instability into everything it touched. Under it, Abython, a pressure so crushing it bent the light round the spear the way a lens bends it. And at the core, smallest and worst, a thing his brother had never shown him in all the old wars, though he had always suspected it was there, down at the bottom of him where the jokes ran out: a knot of phases that refused to agree with one another, a little storm of *before* and *after* knotted so tight that cause and consequence had begun to forget which was which.

Higher Chaos.

Yes. So this was the true depth of his brother's power. Anyone could break things. This broke the *agreements* under things, the old quiet promises the world makes to itself every instant, that a push will be followed by a moving, that a wound will come after the blow and not before it, that a correction will correct. He had felt the first taste of it a moment ago when his brother laid a thread of it round one station of the Paru sentence and turned its timing, so that every repair the station made became the seed of the next fault, and the station had cracked, *KRRRAAACK*, and the eighty-six fists had arrived a breath apart instead of all at once, and that breath had been enough. Some of the fists had landed. He had seen it: the black blood, the torn shoulder, the cracks running through gray flesh, his brother laughing up out of a crater with the gold script still burning on his skin. And some had been thrown off their lines.

Faisal had not been angry about it. He had been, if he was honest, and he could only be honest, a little proud.

[slow, even, a judge's patience] "You found the seam," he said, across the ruined plain, as the spear came. "You always find the seam."

Then it struck him.

*KRRRRRRAAAAAAAAAAAAA-DOOOOOOOOOOOOM.*

---

The Vow-Light met it first. The straight gold falling out of the crack in heaven bent toward him as the spear came in, the way water bends toward a drain, and laid itself in front of his chest in a shield of coherent light, and the spear went into the shield, and the world went white.

*SHIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIN.*

He felt every layer of it.

Cataclysm hit the shield first and the shield began to boil. The Funeral had been built so that every correction he made would become the beginning of a new fault, and it worked exactly as it had been built to: where the light steadied the outer turbulence, the steadying shifted the load onto the light beside it, and the light beside it wavered, and when it was steadied in turn the wavering moved again, round and round the face of the shield in a racing ring of fault and repair, fault and repair, faster each time. Faisal had seen the same sickness in mortal things. A man trying to steer a skiff in a hard current, always correcting a little too late, swinging further each time until he goes over. A singer chasing a note she cannot hear, sharp, flat, sharp, wilder and wilder. Every system that corrects itself by looking at what has already gone wrong is always a little behind, and a thing that is always a little behind can be shaken until it shakes itself apart. His brother had built a weapon out of lateness.

Abython came through behind it like a mountain falling on a man. *GRRRRRRRRRNNNNNNNNNN.* The pressure went into his plate, into his ribs, into the long bones of his legs, and drove him back across the black ground, his boots plowing two furrows through the soil, *ZZZRRRRRRRK*, a full fifty paces, and the dull silver of the breastplate bent inward over his heart with a sound like a bell being crushed in a fist, *GRAAANK*. His knees wanted to fold. Everything about the weight wanted him on his knees.

And the core came in.

It went past the shield and the plate and the bone, through a crack the Cataclysm had opened for it, straight toward the hole in his chest where the Crystal had been and where everything he had been given that morning now burned. Faisal understood in the instant it touched him that his body was only in the way. His brother had seen what he had become, and aimed at that: at the white fire of all those oaths and hopes packed together in the place where the Crystal had been. The Causal Schism went into them like a knife into a hive, and did the one thing in all of creation that could hurt a Midreach Celestial most.

It made them disagree.

A mother in a salt gallery, hoping her son would come home from the war alive. A widow in the Crownlands, hoping the soldier who had burned her village would never come home at all. The same soldier. Two true hopes, sworn on two honest hearts, and the Schism took them and pressed them face to face and turned their phases until they cancelled, until keeping the one meant breaking the other. And there were thousands of them. Ten thousand. Every hope inside him that had ever pulled against another hope anywhere in the world, all at once, wrenched round until they faced each other like drawn blades.

Two oaths of equal weight that cannot both be kept.

The old wound of the Midreach. The seizure. Faisal felt it come over him the way frost comes over a pond: every hand in the air above the plain stopping where it was, every line of script on the fists freezing mid-stroke, the gold in his veins going still and heavy as poured lead, his breath stopping in his chest. *Kch.* The whole of him locking around the impossibility, the way every Celestial of his Choir had always locked, a bright mind trying to keep two promises that could not both be kept and grinding its gears to dust trying.

His brother had remembered. Of course his brother had remembered. He had spent the whole of the Great Spirit War growing contradictory oaths in the Midreach like a farmer grows thistles.

The plain was very quiet. The seizure held. Faisal stood in it with the Funeral of Heaven pressed against his chest and blood running down his chin and his whole long body going rigid as a statue, and for the length of one heartbeat, his brother's heart and his, *thrum, thrum*, the Grand Angel of Servitude could not move at all.

And in that heartbeat he did the thing he had always done. He knelt.

He went down onto one knee in the black soil, *DOMP*, under the full crushing weight of the spear, and bowed his head, exactly as he had knelt for seven hundred years under the cracked Bell. His body knew it better than anything. And kneeling, he found the thing he had always found there: that the weight of an impossible thing is not answered by choosing between its halves. It is answered by putting yourself underneath both.

The mother's son, and the widow's justice. Both hopes true. Both could not be kept, not by any two people, not by the world as it stood.

They could be kept by one, if that one paid for the difference.

[softly, to no one, on his knee] "Then I will be the difference."

He said it in Vow-Cant, which has no grammar for a lie, and so it bound, the instant it was finished. And every contradiction inside him that the Schism had turned blade against blade found, between the blades, something that had not been there a moment before: him. The gap they could not close, he closed. The cost they could not both carry, he carried. Ten thousand impossible pairs, and in every one of them the Grand Angel stepped between the two hopes and took onto himself whatever the keeping of both would cost, and the seizure, which is only the Crystal failing to resolve an impossible instruction, found that the instruction was not impossible any more. It had a price. And the price was being paid.

*Kchk.* The hands moved again.

The price went out of him like the color going out of a sunset. Down the length of his new wings the feathers began to burn. One here, then a hundred there, the many colors of them going white at the edges and then to light and then to nothing, *fsssh, fsssh, fssssh*, a slow soft shower of sparks drifting up off the wings into the morning, and Faisal knew what each one was as it went, because he had been given each one. He did not spend the hopes. He would not. Every feather that burned was a hope he gave back to the one who had hoped it, whole, unspent, safe, so that the mother in the salt gallery would wake that evening and find her hope for her son still in her and never know it had been a feather on an angel's wing at noon. What burned was what he had paid to keep them all. His own substance. The rest of his time.

Under the plate, along his forearms and his neck, long white cracks began to show in his dark skin, fine as the crazing on old porcelain, with light shining through them.

He lifted his head.

[through blood, but even] "You asked how many promises it takes to keep my heart beating," Faisal said. His brother could not have heard him through the roar of the spear; he said it anyway. "The answer is all of them, brother. And none of them will be spent on me. I am spending me."

---

Then he stood up inside the Funeral of Heaven, and began to take it apart.

He started with the weight, because the weight was the part he knew best.

*TALETH.* The Tally's Weight. The Host has a name for it and mortals do not, because mortals have only ever seen its effect: a Midreach Oath-Keeper standing under a load that should have crushed him and not moving. It is the oldest thing Faisal knew, older than the Thousand Hands, older than his Domain. It was what he had done at the edge of creation for seven hundred years, and what he had taught Muken Moto, kneeling beside him under the threads, and called the Work of Bearing Load. A load need not stop in you. It can pass through. The Titan Atla'zon holds the worlds on his back because he bowed first, and every Oath-Keeper is a little of him: the weight that comes in at the top goes down through the body, down the long bones, along the lines a builder draws when he works out where the stress in a cathedral runs, and out through the soles into the ground, and the ground, which is very large, takes it and does not notice.

He wrote it into himself as he rose. [Th], Foundation, that imposes geometry and reinforces the load paths. [Grv], Gravity, the span calibrated to its weight. [Ur], Balance, that equalises opposing forces. The script came up on the inside of his plate and along his bones in white fire, and the Abython's crushing pressure, which had been a mountain on his chest, found that there was suddenly somewhere for it to go. It went down. *DOOOOMMMMM.* The black ground under his feet sank a full fathom in a circle round him and the soil turned to stone with the pressure, a great slab of new rock shoving down into the plain, and the cracks ran out from it fifty yards in every direction, *krk-krk-krk-KRRRRK*, and Faisal stood upright on top of it with the spear still in his chest and the weight of a mountain running through him into the world, and was not moved.

Next, the storm.

*DAHLREN.* The Walked Boundary. He did not try to calm the Cataclysm. That was what the Funeral wanted: every correction a fresh fault, his lateness turned against him. He had watched the turbulence for three heartbeats now, and turbulence was a thing he understood. A river runs smooth until something gives it a difference to tear at, a rock, a bank, a faster current beside a slower one, and then the water shears and curls and breaks into eddies, and the eddies break into smaller eddies, all the way down. Turbulence lives on difference. It feeds on the gradient, the steep place, the edge where one thing is very unlike the thing beside it. Take away the steepness and it has nothing to eat.

So he walked a boundary round the spear instead. A ring of Paru script, [RenB], Boundary, that establishes borders, linked with [Ur] at every joint, Balance, that stabilises Essence gradients, closing round the outside of the Cataclysm sheath like the iron hoop round a barrel, and leveling the ground under the storm instead of pushing on it. Wherever the Cataclysm found a steep place between hot and cold, between fast and slow, between charged and spent, the boundary quietly filled it in, a little here, a little there, the gold flowing into the hollows and off the ridges like water finding its own level in a basin, and the storm, reaching for a difference to tear at, found everything around it the same. *Fsshhhhhhhhhh.* The roaring of the outer sheath sank to a hiss. The red lightning that had been forking off it in great branches came off in thin sparks, then in flickers, then not at all. Cataclysm was still there. It simply had nothing left to be a storm about.

And then the chains.

He had heard them from the moment they came up out of his brother's shadow, under all the roaring: the Maw Chains, wrapped round the Funeral's rings, and every link of them full of voices. He knew what they were. Souls. Mortal and once-mortal, bound under infernal contracts into the iron, screaming quietly in the links the way the wind screams in a rope. His brother had used them to hold the spear together and then poured what was left of their strain into the strike. Faisal had listened to oaths for eleven thousand years, and every one of those chained souls had sworn something once, signed something, said yes to something, and that yes was the iron that held them.

He knew the weight of a yes. He had weighed more of them than anything alive.

*SARNRUTH.* The Seal Entered. He turned his head toward the chains, and his eyes burned gold, and he read the contracts in the links the way a clerk reads a ledger: [Sel], Measure, that reveals the true weight of intent. And the true weight of intent in every one of those contracts was the same. Nothing. A yes said by a starving man to bread. A yes said by a mother to save a child. A yes screamed under the knife. A promise extracted by force is a promise without weight, and in every law the Host had ever kept, and in every mortal court worth the name, a thing sworn under duress does not bind. He wrote it across the chains in a single line of white fire, [Va], Law, that binds an act to its lawful consequence, and [Vo], Voice, the spoken tag that fixes an oath to its listener, and the line said, in the oldest script in the world:

*Entered. Void. Under duress.*

*KLANK.*

The first link went dull. Then the next. *KLANK. KLANK. KLAN-KLAN-KLANK.* All down their length the Maw Chains went slack and gray and quiet as old iron in a field, and out of every link as it died rose a small pale light, a soul with nothing holding it any more, drifting up past the spear and the crack in the sky like a spark going up a chimney, toward the cycle where every spirit goes. Hundreds of them. Thousands. Faisal watched them go with his chest full of the spear, and something in his still face eased.

[quietly, watching them rise] "Go home," he said to them. "Nobody is keeping you any more."

Without the chains feeding it, the strain went out of the Funeral of Heaven all at once, and the spear shuddered in his chest like a fish on a line.

Only the core was left.

---

The core of the spear sat against the white fire in his chest and went on doing the thing it was made for: scrambling the phases of everything around it, cause against consequence, before against after, so that nothing near it could agree with anything. Faisal looked down at it. A small black-crimson knot of storm the size of a closed fist, humming with discord, crawling against the hope inside him like a wasp against a window.

He could not correct it. He knew that. That had been the whole lesson of the morning. His brother's Higher Chaos fed on correction. Every repair made behind the fault was a little late, and the lateness was the weapon, and a thing that waits for a fault in order to mend it will always be a step behind a thing that makes faults faster than it can mend them.

So he would not mend it. He would do what he had come down to the Material to do, the thing the Host had named him for before it broke, the thing his brother had always called the most infuriating thing about him.

He would make it true.

*The Axiom of the Kept.*

This was the deepest of what he was, and he had never once used all of it. An axiom is the thing at the root of a law that does not need to be proved: the premise from which everything else is drawn, so that nothing downstream of it has to be held in place, because it already follows. A builder does not prop up every stone in a wall if the first course is true and level; every stone above it sits straight because the first one does. A correction is a hand chasing a fault. An axiom is the ground the fault would have to stand on, and it is not there.

Faisal's Order had always been, at the bottom, one premise, and his will and his logic were the shape of it, and he could set it into the world the way a mason sets a cornerstone, so that the world's own truth arranged itself to follow. What is kept, *is*. A thing that is promised and kept is real and remains real; it cannot be made to have been otherwise. The Bell had held that premise for eleven thousand years. He had held the Bell. With the Crystal broken and nothing between him and all he carried, he could put the premise down anywhere, at any scale, as the first stone of the local law, and everything built on that ground had to agree with it.

And the Causal Schism was all disagreement.

He looked at it, and he thought of the oldest thing he knew about the world, older than Choirs: that the world, given a choice of ways to go from here to there, does not take one road. It tries all of them. Every possible path a thing could take between two moments is laid down at once, a faint ghost of each, all of them together, every one with its own phase turning like the hand of a little clock. Where the paths agree, where their little clocks turn in step, they add up, and that is the way the thing actually goes. Where they disagree, where one clock points up and the next points down, they cancel, crest against trough, and vanish as if they had never been. A ball thrown across a yard tries every crooked road in the world to reach the other side, and every crooked road is cancelled by its neighbor, and only the one true arc survives. All the probabilities in creation, diverted and folded down into one single line, the line where everything agrees.

That was his axiom. That had always been his axiom. All paths are taken, and only the kept one stands.

And his brother had built the heart of the Funeral of Heaven out of phases that refused to agree.

[clear, carrying, every word a law] "Axiom."

He put his right hand flat over the black-crimson knot in his chest. The script came up across the back of his hand and down his fingers, white-hot, glyph after glyph, the oldest he knew and the ones he loved most: [Len], Line, that defines a clean sequence between two events and prevents causal tangling. [Ho], Order, that locks causal chains and restores chronology. [Ir], Continuum, that preserves causal traceability across time. [Ed], Edgetruth, that cuts away false threads to reveal the viable paths. [Ora], Truth, that rings with the truth-tone.

"OTHANTHA."

The Unbroken Line.

*TIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIIING.*

It went out of his palm as a sound first, a single clear note like a struck glass the size of a cathedral, perfectly pure, with no other note in it at all. And every path the Causal Schism had scrambled heard it.

The Schism was a storm of phases that would not agree. In the sum of all paths, phases that do not agree cancel. The more violently it scrambled the before and after in itself, the more of its own possible ways forward it set against each other, crest against trough, and the more of them wiped each other out; the whole terrible engine of discord his brother had packed into its heart had only ever been a machine for cancelling itself, held together by the Maw Chains and the rings and the sheer will behind it. Faisal's axiom did not fight it. It only insisted, at the root of everything around his hand, on the oldest law there is: that of all the ways a thing might go, only the one where everything agrees is real. And in all the Causal Schism, in all its crawling contradiction, there was exactly one line on which cause came before effect and every phase turned in step.

It ran from the spear's tip, straight back, the way it had come.

*Fwip.*

Every other path the core could have taken collapsed into its neighbors and went out. Ten thousand ways forward cancelled to nothing. The black-crimson knot in his chest went thin, and thinner, and then it was not a storm at all but a single fine dark thread, and the thread lay along one line, and the line pointed back across the plain at the hand that had thrown it.

The Funeral of Heaven came out of his chest the way a splinter comes out of a thumb, all at once.

*SHHHHUUUUK.*

And went back, simply going the only way it now could, the one true road, unwinding along its own trench across the black ground, its storm stripped off it and its chains dead and its weight sunk into the stone under his feet, a single dark thread of consequence returning to its cause the way an echo returns to the mouth that called it.

Faisal did not watch it arrive. That was his brother's business.

He was looking at his own hand.

The cracks had run out along his fingers, white and fine, and light was coming through them, and the tips of the two smallest fingers on his right hand had gone clear, like glass, like ice on a window, and he could see the morning through them. The Axiom had cost what it cost. Each time he set the first stone of the world under his feet, a little more of him went into the foundation and did not come back. He had known that. He looked at the clear fingertips for a moment, and turned them over in the light, and they were quite beautiful, and he let his hand fall.

---

He walked out of the crater the spear had made, up the slope of the new stone, slowly, and stood on the edge of it, and looked across the plain at his brother.

He was smiling. Truly, at last, as he had not smiled in seven hundred years: the small, plain, helpless smile of a man who has been hit very hard by someone who loves hitting him and has hit back, and has had, for one strange moment in the middle of the end of everything, a wonderful time.

[warm, almost laughing, still unhurried] "You called me a magnificent idiot," Faisal said. "You are right about that as well. You always were. You said the first truly selfish thing I ever did was to throw away forever so that I could fight you." The light came through the cracks in his face. "You are right about that too, brother. It is the most selfish thing I have ever done. And I would do it again."

He touched the dented plate over his heart.

"You said I finally learned to hit something. I did. It took eleven thousand years, and a boy on a frozen lake to teach me, and it was worth every one of them." His gold eyes went to the crack in the sky, to the souls still drifting up into it like sparks. "You said every promise in me was terribly convenient. You aimed at them. You found the oldest wound my Choir has, and you put your thumb in it, and I am proud of you for it. Nobody else in all the ages ever got so close." He looked back down. "But you have forgotten something too. You told me chaos is more than disorder. Here is the other half of that. Order is more than repair. Order is the ground the break would have to stand on, brother, long before any hand comes to mend it."

He lifted his hands, palms up, empty, the way a man shows another man he is carrying no weapon.

"You said you have all the time in the world. I do not. I have this morning." He breathed in, and the light came through every crack in him at once. "So I will spend it the way you wanted. All of it. Here."

---

He brought his hands together in front of his heart, fingertips to fingertips, the way he had stood on the plain at the very beginning. And then he turned them, slowly, the right hand over and the left hand under, until the palms lay one above the other with a hand's breadth of air between them, and in the space between them, a single line of gold Vow-Cant began to write itself.

The plain went still.

The wind stopped. The drifting souls stopped in the air. The red lightning in the low clouds froze in its branches like cracks in black glass. The whole Material World held its breath, the way a hall holds its breath in the moment before a verdict.

[every word set down like a stone] "This is the only thing in me that I grew myself," Faisal said, softly. "Everything else the Bell lent me. This, I made. Over eleven thousand years, one oath at a time, the way the mortals make anything worth having." The gold script between his palms grew, and turned, and began to bloom. "I have never opened all of it, brother. Not once. Not in all our wars. There was always something I was saving it for."

He looked across the plain.

"It is you. It was always going to be you."

He drew his hands apart.

"Domain Expansion."

*DOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOOONNNNNNNNNNNNN.*

"OTHSAL."

The Position Held.

It came out of him like a lotus opening at dawn, if the dawn were the size of the sky. Lines of gold Vow-Cant, fine as hair and bright as wire drawn hot from a die, burst out of the space between his hands and unfurled in long curving ribbons, hundreds, thousands, millions, every oath ever sworn on honor and kept, written out in the oldest script there is, and they went out across the black plain in great sweeping petals, *shhhhhaaaaaaaaaaaaaa*, curving up and over and down, petal behind petal, layer inside layer, every ribbon of them singing with the note of the thing that had been promised. They swept over the crater and the trench and the new stone, over the slack gray chains and the black-red soil, over the low clouds and the frozen lightning and the crack in the sky, and they did not stop at the edge of the plain. They went on. Out to the ridges. Over the ridges. The whole horizon writing itself in gold, every hill and cloud and stone of it laced over with lines of a law, until the plain where the two brothers stood was the heart of a vast golden flower of script whose outermost petals curled over the sky itself, and there was no direction anyone could look in that was not written on.

Inside it, the light was warm, and heavy, and perfectly clear, like the inside of a hall where nobody has ever told a lie.

This was the law of OTHSAL, and it was very simple, as all deep laws are. Within the Position Held, every spoken commitment becomes real, binding and more than binding, a thing with weight in the world, so that the air thickens a little each time a promise is made, and a promise kept stands up in the one who kept it like a second spine, and a promise broken parts with a sound like a rope snapping, *TWANG*, loud enough for anyone present to hear, and drops the one who broke it to his knees under the weight of what he let fall. Vow-Cant has no grammar for a lie. Nothing false can be written here at all.

And every word spoken on that plain since dawn was already written into it. The gold ribbons had found them in the air and taken them up, all of them, and they hung now in the bright script round the brothers like banners: every true thing, every boast, every promise. *I have all the time in the world. I'm going to tear what's left of you apart. I want to hear every single one of them pray.*

Faisal stood at the heart of his Domain with his white hair lifting and the light coming through his cracks and his ruined, many-colored, burning wings spread to their full width behind him, and looked at his brother through a sky made of kept promises.

He knew this Domain's counters as he knew his own hands; he had grown it. Silence passes through it untouched. And two oaths of equal weight that cannot both be kept, sworn inside it, will seize it and him with it, as they always had. There was a seam. There always is. His brother had found every seam he had ever made.

He was not afraid of that. He had been looking forward to it.

[gentle, and absolutely certain] "Every word you have said to me since you came up out of the ground was true, brother. I heard them all. You never once needed to lie to me, and I loved you for it." The gold lines round them brightened, and hummed. "In here, a true word is a chain you forged yourself. You promised to tear what is left of me apart. In here, you will have to keep that promise." He opened his hands, empty, at his sides. "Or kneel."

The petals of OTHSAL turned slowly in the sky above them, every line of them singing.

"Come, then," said Faisal. "Keep your word."

# The Brink

*The camp edge above the ridge line. The third evening after Karreth.*
*POV: Ruvaen Aloreth.*

---

## I. TWENTY THOUSAND

He sat the white horse at the edge of the camp with the reins slack and looked down at the largest administrative problem of his life.

Twenty thousand.

Not killed. Twenty thousand taken, over eleven days, off a ridge line that had been fortified for a decade and had been taken apart company by company, and every one of them was now alive and sitting in eight fenced enclosures on the reverse slope with a number written against his name.

*Nineteen thousand four hundred and eleven at the muster. We are twenty-one thousand now with the coast road men, and we are holding twenty thousand, and I have more prisoners than I have soldiers and the ratio went the wrong way on the ninth and has not come back.*

*Velenn has not slept in four days. She came to me on the second morning with a sheet and put it in my hand and did not say anything, which from her is shouting.*

The enclosures were the thing he had spent most of his own time on, which had caused more argument in his own council than any order he had given in eleven years.

Fed, at the same ration as his own men and off the same carts. Four wells dug on the reverse slope rather than three, because three was adequate and four meant the queues did not produce fights. Latrines downhill of the water and inspected by a man whose entire job was that. The wounded taken in the same order of severity as his own wounded, which had put a Vessel Coast surgeon in front of him at the eleventh hour on the second night with an objection, and which had ended with the surgeon going back to work.

Nobody struck. That was written in Common on eleven boards and read aloud at every watch change.

*And every single one of my generals has been at me about it since the ninth.*

---

## II. WHAT HE WOULD NOT ALLOW

Hallavaen Orroth had made the argument first and had made it honestly, which was worse than if he had made it badly.

Twenty thousand men off eleven years of Korvaeth colours. Among them a quartermaster who has seen the legionary carriages, and a runner who has carried to Rovann and back, and somebody who knows where she is and what she is bringing and how many of the things survived the seating. All of it on the reverse slope eating our bread. And a way to have it inside a night.

*He is right about every part of it except the last one and the last one is the only part that matters.*

*It would work. That is what nobody wants said out loud. It works. It produces bad intelligence at volume and good intelligence occasionally and the good pieces are worth having and any commander who tells you otherwise has never been offered it on a night when he needed it.*

He had not allowed it. He had not allowed it on the ninth and he had not allowed it on the eleventh when Orroth came back with the quartermaster's name, and he had said the same three sentences both times.

I am going to hold this continent or I am going to die on it, and either way what I do in these eight enclosures is what the whole of it was for.

You do not win a war against a woman by becoming the argument she has been making about you for eleven years.

And I told a dragon on a coast road that terror is the only crime on a common, and I have four hundred of her own men in my line who walked over without being asked, and that happened because of the enclosures and for no other reason in the world.

*And the cost of it is that I do not know where she is.*

*I do not know what a legionary is. I do not know whether there are eleven or sixty. I have a Measurewright taking baselines and a Reckoner reading returns and nineteen thousand men and I am blind about the one thing that is coming, and I am blind on purpose, and I have signed for that.*

---

## III. THE RIDER

She came up the line at a trot on the liver chestnut and did not announce herself, because she never has.

Kira Maellon. A hundred and thirty-nine, which is four years behind him and has been four years behind him since they were children in the same three streets of the lower Sunroot quarter, and there is nobody else alive who knew him before the wall.

Riding leathers, not harness, because she had come off picket and had not been back to her tent. Black hair in the short Landward crop that most Eressean women refuse. She had cut it herself with a knife at forty and never grown it out, and it had gone grey at the left temple only, in a streak two fingers wide that she had never once mentioned.

Broad across the shoulders. Thick through the forearms in the way of somebody who has spent a century on a horse and another century on a bow. Wide flat cheekbones, and a mouth that sits closed and slightly amused when nothing is funny, which had got her thrown out of two courts.

A break in the left forearm that had set with about three degrees of bow in it. Twenty-two years old, that one. He had been holding the horse.

Mud to the thigh. Four days of it.

She brought the chestnut up alongside Pegasus, close enough that the two animals' shoulders were almost touching, which is a thing you can only do with a horse that knows the other horse.

Neither of them said anything for a while.

Down on the reverse slope the eleventh watch was changing, and somebody was reading out a board in Common, and the sound came up thin and flat with the words in it worn smooth from repetition.

---

## IV. THE QUESTION

"Do you think it will be enough."

"I hope it wakes them up."

He said it looking east, at nothing.

"Twenty thousand men are going to go home eventually and every one of them is going to tell the same story in four hundred villages. Fed. Not struck. Wells dug. Wounded taken in order. That is what I am building on that slope. It is worth more than the ridge was, and I would trade the ridge back for it, and I have said that to Orroth's face and watched him decide not to believe me.

"She has spent thirty years telling four Under-Houses that the Sunroot Court is a machine for deciding who is properly a person. I intend to have twenty thousand witnesses who can say otherwise from their own mouths, in their own halls, for the next four hundred years.

"That is the war. The rest of it is transport."

Kira let that go past.

"Ilthára will come."

"She is already moving. Two days, three at the outside."

"Do you think you can beat her?"

"If I trust the read," he said, "I can beat anything that comes at this camp."

*Which is true and which is the answer to a different question, and I have known that woman for a hundred and thirty years and she is not going to let it stand.*

"That is not what I asked you."

She had not raised her voice and had not turned her head.

"I asked you whether you can beat *her*. Not what comes at the camp. Her. And you gave me a sentence about your instrument, which is what you do."

---

## V. THE HONEST ANSWER

He took a while over it.

*She is the only person in the four quarters who gets to do that.*

*Ithuran will say a thing once and let it go. Hesh enters his objection and obeys it. Corvienne writes what I said and what I did and lets the gap sit on the page for somebody in forty years. Velenn will fight me about lamps and win and never about this.*

*Kira asks twice and then she asks a third time, and she has been doing it since we were forty, and she is the reason I am not a worse man than I am.*

"I do not know," he said.

"Good. Now say the rest of it."

"Ilthára Korvaeth is not Aeldros.

"Aeldros was made. Everything he was, he built out of an accounting problem, and a thing that is built has seams and I found his in eleven minutes on a coast road.

"She was not made. She was trained from the day she could stand, by people who knew exactly what they had, and she was dubbed at nine.

"The Common word is valkyrie. The yard has a longer one and I am not going to use it out here. Nine years old. There have been four in eleven hundred years and two of them were posthumous.

"And underneath the training there is the other thing, which is the part nobody in my council will look at directly. Raw potential of a kind that does not come round twice in an age. If she had been handed a name at seventeen and put in a chair, she would have been the finest thing this continent produced in nine hundred years and none of this would have happened, and the yard looked at her three times and said no.

"She has never been beaten in single combat. Not once, not as a child, not in thirty years of a continent full of people who wanted to be the one who did it."

*And she was in that house when they took it.*

*I have not said that to anybody and I have been carrying it since the muster and I do not have it as a fact, I have it as a shape.*

*Nine women holding one name. A form our law allows perhaps four times a century, and the reason it exists at all is that a shield is supposed to be indivisible.*

*Nine.*

*Why nine. It is an ugly number for a shield and the yard hates ugly numbers and a Reckoner would have argued about it for a season.*

*Unless it was meant to be ten, and one of them was refused, and they convened anyway with the gap in it.*

*And thirty years later somebody went through all nine and did not stop at eight, and knew exactly why stopping at eight would not have been enough.*

He did not say any of that either. Not to Kira, not yet, and he could not have said why.

---

## VI. THE BRINK

"So no," he said. "I do not beat her on skill. I have never beaten anybody on skill and I am not going to start at a hundred and forty-three against the best blade in four quarters.

"That is not the plan and it has never been the plan.

"I do not need to be better than her. I need one moment. One place in an exchange where she is committed and cannot recover and does not know it yet, and I need to be reading when it arrives, and I need to spend everything I have on it in the half second it exists.

"Everybody has one. Every single person I have ever killed had one and most of them were better than me. Aeldros had four and I only needed the second.

"Hers will be smaller than anybody's I have ever taken and it will be there, because it is a body, and a body committed to a movement is a body that has made a promise about the next half second.

"That is the whole of it. Not a duel. An arithmetic problem with one correct instant in it."

Kira turned in the saddle and looked at him properly for the first time since she rode up.

"And what does it cost you to be reading at that resolution when it comes."

*There it is.*

*That is the third question and she has been walking toward it since the picket line, and the other two were the approach.*

"Everything I have left," he said.

"Which is what, now."

"Four hundred yards. It was six." He kept his eyes east. "It came back short after the quarry lip and it has not moved since and I do not expect it to. Vigilance is gone entirely and has been for years, so I will not feel my own ceiling coming, and if I spend the whole of it inside a single exchange I will not know I have overdrawn until it has already happened.

"So the honest answer to your question is that I get one attempt. If I read her opening and take it, it is finished. If I read it and I am wrong, I will not have anything left to read the second one with, and there will be a second one, and I will be standing in front of her holding a sword like a man who has never held one."

"And you have known that since the coast road."

"Since the second day of the coast road."

"And you have told nobody."

"I have told nobody."

Kira Maellon sat her horse beside his and did not say any of the eleven things she could have said, which is the other reason she is the only one.

"Then I will be at your left," she said.

"Kira."

"At your left, where I have been since the fourth wave, and I will not be asking. And when you have spent it and cannot see, I will be the one telling you where she is, and you will have to trust that, and you are bad at it and you will do it anyway."

The watch board finished down on the slope and the queue at the fourth well started moving again.

Ruvaen Aloreth reached across the gap between the two horses and put his hand flat on the chestnut's neck under the mane, where the animal was warm, because reaching across to Kira was not a thing he had ever managed in a hundred and thirty-nine years.

She let him do it and looked east and said nothing about it at all.

Below them a man in the fourth enclosure was singing something in Rovann, badly, two lines of it over and over, and about eleven others had started joining in on the second line only, which is what men do with a song nobody remembers the whole of.

*They have been doing that since the second night and Orroth wants it stopped and I will not stop it.*

*And Iesera has been attending to that enclosure for three days.*

*Not the ridge. Not the road west. Not the direction she is coming from. The fourth enclosure, on the reverse slope, where eleven hundred prisoners are eating my bread and singing two lines of a song, and I have walked the fence of it twice and found nothing at all.*

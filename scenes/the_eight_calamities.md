# The Eight Calamities

*The Crucible, the mist and the dark air, as Haruki's release unfolds*

---

Malphas did not let her Harmony touch him; he met it with obdurate refusal.

It came for him as a lattice of concordance, a vast harmonic web strung out across the arena from her closed eyes and pressed palms, every ally she had ever trusted threaded into it like beads on one taut string, the whole of it humming in a single shared note of unity against a common enemy. It was beautiful, almost dulcet. He could hear it through the Yukari nerves, a chord so clean and plangent that for an instant his new heart ached at it. Then his aura answered.

It came off him malevolent and fuliginous, a crimson-black tide rolling out of the seared ruin of his chest, and it did not push against her web. It cancelled it. Nihiloth was never a law imposed on a place; it was the hollow interference that unmakes whatever law a place already holds, and where his aura met her Harmony the clean chord began to fray. A synchronised thing holds together only while its parts can still hear each other, every firefly in a crepuscular summer field flashing in time because each one sees its neighbours flash. He poured noise between them, a malediction of static, and the coupling slackened. The equally divided branches, each carrying only its share of a single note, began to drift off pitch one from the next, and where they drifted they broke, and where they broke the concordance fractured into a cacophonous scatter of separate souls. Reality round him curled outward like vellum held over a candle, its edges calcined.

Her wormhole was still open in her palm. He reached for it with the same stygian hollow.

A hole folded through space stays open only while something with less than nothing in it props the throat apart; take that away and the bend closes on itself faster than a photon can cross it. His Nihiloth found the negative pressure she had poured into the throat, refractory and thin, and cancelled it the way a lamp cancels the dark. The wormhole buckled. It shattered into pieces, a starburst of folded space collapsing inward with the sound of a hyperborean berg calving, the last of the pressurised water inside it flashing to steam and then to nothing as the bend snapped shut. Whatever energy had held it open went the way all energy went near him now: into an incarnadine flare that broke her working down to its constituents and swallowed them.

He breathed in.

The Zettari lungs were punctured, the pleura torn, sanies in the wounds. He could feel the rents in them, two ragged tears where her mist had blitzed through the delicate membranes, and the breath came in wet and stertorous, rattling over the blood. He breathed in anyway, and drew the mist down into him with a kind of temerity.

Her photoelectric compound hung all through the air of the arena and all through his ruined chest, a fine aerosol of charged particles so small and so many that their combined surface was vast. He had read about such clouds as a student, in the incident ledgers of the flour mills and the coal galleries: a frowsty grain store where the dust hangs thick in the light from the high windows, quiet, inert, until one spark finds it, and then every mote burns at once because every mote is touching air, and the whole room becomes a single flame. A dust cloud is fuel waiting for a match.

Solfatara was the match. The deep sulphurous red of the volcanic Wellspring rose in him, the register of fume and vent and mephitic brimstone, and he let it out into the air on the breath. Every particle of her mist caught at once in a single fulgurant instant. The air of the arena around him combusted in a crimson deflagration, a great rolling sheet of effulgence that ran outward through her own compound faster than the eye could follow, consuming the oxygen as it went, the whole cloud she had laid round him turned in a heartbeat from a weapon into a furnace. The noise of it was a long concussive whump that shook the tiers. The heat came off it in a wall, candent and dry. At its centre, where the mist had been thickest, closest to the hands that had made it, the fire burned with a white, incandescent ferocity.

Her psychic weight was still on him, the compression folding the space round his body with a slow torsion, every cell of him resonating against its own perfect counterforce. He understood the trick of it at once, and admired it with a scholar's cupidity. She had read his field and laid its mirror image over him, a cancellation so exact, so adamantine, that his own strength pressed back against itself. But a mirror must see what it reflects. Every reading has a lag, the thinnest, most evanescent sliver of a moment between what a field is and what the reader knows it to be, and a field that changes faster than that sliver can never be matched.

So he changed. He let Ferment churn the frequency of his own essence the way a mash churns in the vat, never the same note two instants running, an inexorable seething instability, and her mirror, always a hair behind, began to cancel what he had been rather than what he was. The counterforce slipped out of phase. Out of phase, a cancellation does not cancel. It adds. For a moment the weight on him lifted and then turned, and he felt her own compression begin, juddering, to push the wrong way.

Her reactor flame came at him with its edge, the new element she had no name for, distilling and burning with an acrid edge, built to reject his cells, built to find the foreign in him and refuse it.

He met it with his chest open, in a gesture of almost sardonic welcome.

The ember-edge touched the seared, contused ruin of him and he felt it search. He felt it reach into the tissue for the small proteins by which flesh tells *self* from *other*, the marks a graft wears that a body's sentinels find and tear down; he felt it look for the Moto in him, and the Zettari, and the Yukari, three bloodlines sewn into one frame, and he felt it falter. There was almost nothing there to find. His culture had fermented those marks away in the caliginous dark of the cauldron-hall before the three were ever joined, dissolved them to a muck in which no sentinel could find anything to hate. Her reactor had been forged to reject the foreign. He had made himself a body with nothing foreign left to name. The embers seared and charred him to eschar and found little to refuse, and where they found nothing they only burned, and burning he understood.

Beneath the crimson silk his sable armour shifted. From the gauntlet on his right forearm, a long dark lance slid out along the bone with a hiss of oiled steel, its point damascened in gold along the fuller, the same gold as the crosses at his throat.

He drove it at her.

It went in low, under her guard, the whole weight of the Zettari body behind it from his rear heel through his hip to his shoulder, a straight, precipitate thrust at the centre of her abdomen, through the place where her expanded aura was thinnest now that so much of it had been spent on the release and the network and the reactor all at once. The point was not a blade's point; Dissolution rode it, the old putrescent art. It was built to touch her aura and tell the bonds that held it together that they could let go; to touch her skin and do the same; to go on telling every bond it met, through skin and fascia and muscle and the soft wall beneath, that it was free to come apart, until the flesh it reached simply broke down, softening like fruit left too long in the sun, unbinding molecule from molecule into the entropy it had always been leaning toward. That was what the lance was for. Whether it reached her, and what it did when it did, was hers to answer.

He was already reading what she would do, with a strategist's sangfroid.

If she reknit the Harmony and pulled her allies back into one note, he held the second hollow ready. A network shared is a road shared; whatever ran along the coupling to strengthen her ran both ways. He kept a Walking Dose curled at the tip of his tongue, a seed that could go body to body down a shared bond with no thread back to him, carrion-patient. If she opened another wormhole, he would cancel its throat again, and the second time he would let it close on whatever she had sent through, a sprung postern. If she brought the reactor back with a new selector, he would keep the seething of his frequency running. Whatever she tuned it to would always be the self he had been an instant before.

He held it all ready. He smiled at her through the smoke with a saturnine patience.

---

Sodoku saw his father look down at them both with something worse than rancour.

They had come down hard. Muken had smashed Sonzai into the broken floor with the chains, and now he reached down and took him by the collar and wrested him up one-handed as easily as lifting a sack of grain, and swung him, and Sodoku, coming in low from gedan with Kurosetsu rising in a crescent from the hip, saw his brother's body coming at him through the air a heartbeat before it struck. They went down together in a sprawl of sinew and steel and violet light.

"I will not be beaten by these lesser..." The dead king's voice came down on them like brumal weather. "...weaker versions of you."

He let them rise, both of them, with a terrible, deliberate hauteur, and as they came he closed the maai and moved.

The afterimage of him still stood where he had spoken when his hands closed on their throats, one on each, the fingers of the right hand and the fingers of the left, and the world round his hands crazed. Hibiware ran out from his palms through the skin of their necks into the floor of the world, fine argent cracks spreading out in a web from each grip, and he lifted them, both his sons, by the throat, one in each hand, high above his head.

"Where are my sons?" The amber light in his scars blazed a refulgent white. "My sons, who were destined to be like the great Moto of old?"

He brought them down.

The choke-slam drove them both into the floor of the Crucible back-first, side by side, with the whole of his weight and the whole of the Hataraki behind it, impact and verdict in one. The ashlar burst under them in a spray of spall. Sodoku felt his shoulder-blades meet bedrock and felt the bedrock give, a concussive shock that blew the breath out of him in a single bloody gout and sent the world white at the edges. Before it had finished going white his father's foot came down on his chest.

It came down on the sternum. Sodoku felt it press, heavy as a millstone, felt the old breaks in his ribs, every cicatrix, flare under it, felt the frost on Kurosetsu, still in his hand from tsuba to kissaki, crack and fall away in flakes.

"Where is the RAGE?" Muken roared it like a tocsin, and the tiers shook. "WHERE IS THE HATRED YOU SHOWED THE TENRAI?"

The silence after it was sepulchral.

Muken shook his head with lugubrious slowness, the Kamigan revolving.

"You disgrace me."

Beside Sodoku, his brother's hand came up and closed on their father's ankle. Blood ran from his brother's mouth, sanguine, into his hair. He gripped the foot that stood on his brother's chest the way a drowning man grips a rope.

"Asami..."

It came out of him in a rasp, a rale with blood in it.

"Wazawai. Midare-gami."

*Disaster. Gods of disorder.*

The Calamity rose.

It came up out of his brother's body in a swirling column, violet-black, shot with sick glaucous light, and where it went it found purchase in the world itself. Sodoku felt it take hold. It did not crack the world the way Hibiware cracked it, along its seams. It disordered it. The Calamity reached down into the laws the floor stood on, the rules that told a stone which way to fall and a breath which way to go, and it began to scramble them, the way a noise scrambles a signal until the signal means nothing at all. Every law has a sign, a direction, a this-way-and-not-that. The Calamity flipped the signs.

The ground flipped with them.

Sodoku felt it as a lurch in the pit of his stomach, the terrible vertiginous wrongness of a man who rolls over in his sleep and finds the bed is now the ceiling. Down became up. The weight that had pinned him to the broken floor was suddenly a weight hanging from it. Dust that had been settling on the stone fell away from it, upward, into the dark sky, and the dark sky, the torn, tenebrous sky over the Crucible, was suddenly *below* them, a vast black floor yawning beneath their feet with the split of Malphas's wormhole like a well in it. The ground was the sky, and the sky was the ground. The stands hung over their heads, an inverted cyclopean bowl.

"Come," his brother rasped. "Calamity Spirit. *Magatsuhi.*"

The chains began to glow.

They had lain slack on the floor, the Defiled Chains, Kegare-gusari, their links gone dull, a patina of soot from Muken's grip. Now they lit from within, violet and green, the livid colour of a bruise going bad, and they began to grow. They came up out of the ground, out of the floor that was now the ceiling, shooting outward from his brother's body in every direction, dozens of them, hundreds of links, the air filled with their clangour as they lashed and coiled and multiplied. The air was a knell.

"*Ōmagatsuhi.*"

The Great Calamity.

The chains locked. They came together round Muken from every side at once, round his arms, his waist, his chest, his ankles, link over link, swirling round him in a great spiralling cocoon, a garrotte of a thousand links, and they pulled. They pulled him toward the sky, the sky that was now the ground. Muken's foot came off Sodoku's chest and the dead king was dragged away from his sons, out into the dark air, falling upward, falling toward the black floor of the sky with the chains winding tighter round him at every turn and the Calamity howling up the links after him with a skirl like pipes.

His brother rose.

He came up off the broken stone with his whole bloodied body glowing, violet and green, the corruption in his Crystal blazing out through his skin in a lattice of light like veins of molten slag in a cooling ingot. He drew his fist back.

"*Yasomagatsu.*"

The Eight Calamities.

They came one after another, too fast for a single blow and too distinct for any two to be the same. Each was the same bloodied fist.

The first came as flood: a fist driven in behind a crushing hydraulic ram, the sea breaking a harbour wall.

The second came as fire, wreathed in an exothermic roar that ran up the chains.

The third was plague. It carried rot in it, the Calamity's own sickness, built to sicken flesh and Crystal from the inside, necrotic and slow.

The fourth was famine, a blow that took instead of giving, built to drain the reserve it touched until it was hollow as cachexia.

The fifth came as storm, the fist arriving inside a howling vortex.

The sixth came as landslide: a blow that sheared, built to find the planes of weakness in whatever it struck and let everything above those planes come down, the way a mountain lets go of its scree.

The seventh was drought, built to pull the water out of tissue and leave it sere.

The eighth came as the quake. His father's own calamity, Naifuru's own art, turned inside out: a strike that carried the world's note in it, every crack and seam made to ring, but rung in disorder, the Calamity's disorder, not the clean coherent tremor of the Hibiware but its scrambled mirror, built to shake apart the one man who had always been the steadiest thing in the world.

Eight calamities, one after another, up the chains, at the dead king falling upward into the sky.

Sodoku lay on the floor that was now a ceiling with his chest caved and his sword in his hand, and watched his brother's fist go up into the dark, and did not know, for once in his life, what his father would do.

**Opponent read (Haruki's last post).**

**What she is doing.**
- *Release, not Domain.* Harmony is divided equally among her allies, a shared field linking them against one enemy.
- *Her photoelectric mist* passes through Malphas's frame, saturating his Aether with hers, clouding his intent, collapsing tissue and puncturing his lungs.
- *A palm wormhole* vents a high-pressure jet of water that throws him back, then she seals it fast.
- *A psychic compression* tuned so each of his cells resonates against its own equal counterforce, his own force turned on him, the air thinning.
- *A new "element".* Psychic energy, photons and Harmony fused into a "reactor": a bladed flame that rejects his new body's cells instead of reacting with them.

**What the physics resembles.**
- The shared Harmony is coupled oscillators locked in phase (synchronisation, as in fireflies or grid generators), with the power divided like current across parallel branches.
- The mist is an ionising aerosol (the photoelectric effect strips electrons; fine particles inhaled deep into the lung).
- The wormhole is a hydraulic jet: momentum flux, a water-cannon impulse.
- The compression is active cancellation: matching his field's phase and inverting it, so it destroys itself by destructive interference.
- The reactor flame is an edged plasma, built to work like graft rejection, with the immune system's self/not-self recognition turned into a weapon.

**The weaknesses that physics implies.**
1. *Phase lock is fragile.* A synchronised network fails when the coupling drops below a threshold or when broadband noise detunes it. An equally divided field also means every branch carries only its share; cut the coupling and each ally stands alone.
2. *A fine combustible aerosol plus oxygen plus ignition is a dust explosion.* The mist is fuel she has spread around her own body.
3. *A wormhole throat must be propped open by negative-energy matter.* Anything that cancels that support collapses it, and every jet also kicks back on her (Newton's third law).
4. *Active cancellation must read and match his frequency in real time.* Every reading has latency; if he changes frequency faster than she can update, the cancellation slips out of phase and starts adding instead of cancelling.
5. *Rejection needs a marker to reject.* His body was built by fermenting away exactly those self/not-self markers (*The Relics*), so the reactor's selector has little to grip.
6. *Overcommitment.* Eyes closed, both hands occupied, holding a release, a network and a reactor at once means her attention and reserve are split across all of it.

**Openings.** The mist is around *her*. The network ties her to every ally, so whatever travels along it travels both ways. She expects his speed (she said so) but not a change of medium.

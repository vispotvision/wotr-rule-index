# The Empty Place

*Rovann. The upper hall and the Deep Gate, the eleventh day after the coast road.*
*POV: Ilthára Korvaeth.*

---

## I. THE GAUNTLETS

She did the gauntlets first because the gauntlets are the ones that take the longest and because nobody else was going to be permitted to do them.

Rovann harness goes on from the feet upward in every manual ever written and she had never once done it that way. Hands first. Everything else can be managed with a hand that is already armoured and nothing at all can be managed by a hand that is not, and she had learned that at thirty in a gallery with a broken arming point and had never forgotten it.

Black lacquer over eleven plates to the knuckle, mail voiders at the wrist, the cuff flaring out over the vambrace so a blade running up the arm goes off the lip instead of into the joint. Old work and good work and she had worn it for nineteen years.

No enamel anywhere on it.

*And there is the place where the crest goes, on the left breast, four inches across, and it has been bare since the armourer delivered it and he did not ask me why and I did not tell him.*

*Every drow who has ever looked at this harness has looked at that first. Not the plate. Not the lacquer. The absence.*

*I have let them. For thirty years I have let them look at nothing and draw the conclusion themselves, because a woman who explains an absence has conceded that it is one.*

Dessa Oth-Karreth stood at the door with the board she carried everywhere and had not written a word on it in four minutes.

"My Lady. Are you certain about the field."

"Say the rest of it."

"You are the Empress." The board did not come up. "You do not need to fight. There is no article that requires it and there is no house that expects it and there are sixty thousand men whose entire office is that you should not have to."

---

## II. THE ANSWER

Ilthára Korvaeth set the second gauntlet and flexed the fingers twice and felt the plates go down in sequence the way they were built to.

"If I stay here.

"On a marble chair, in a hall, nine hundred feet under stone, while men who have never met me go into a ridge line on my word and do not come back off it. Tell me what that looks like from the fourth gallery.

"It looks like every seat this continent has ever had.

"I have spent thirty years telling four Under-Houses that a thing is true whether or not a yard has written it down. That standing is what you do and not what somebody hands you. That is the whole doctrine, it is the only doctrine I have ever had, and the day I decline to stand in a line because there is a chair available to me instead is the day the doctrine is a convenience and everybody sees it at once.

"I will not be given a name. Fine. I stopped wanting one at thirty-four.

"I will not be given this either. Whatever I hold when this is finished, I will have held it in a place where somebody could have taken it off me."

She reached for the gorget.

"Move out of my way, Dessa."

"My Lady."

"You will regret standing there. Not because I will do anything to you. Because you will remember doing it and you will think about it for four hundred years and you will not enjoy one minute of that." She got the gorget seated and felt for the catch behind the neck by memory, which is a thing nobody can do for you and which she had been doing unassisted since the year the arming point broke. "Now help me with the back plate or fetch somebody who will."

Dessa Oth-Karreth put the board down and helped with the back plate.

---

## III. THE SITUATION

They went down the long corridor toward the Deep Gate and the Queen's Guard came in behind her off the side galleries in twos, which is how they do it, without an order and without a word.

Eleven of them. Six women and five men, black-lacquered like her and like her wearing nothing on the left breast, because a guard takes the crest of the house it serves and there is no crest to take.

*They chose that. I never asked for it and I would have forbidden it if I had been asked, and by the time I noticed there were eleven of them walking about Rovann with a bare plate and the thing had already become what it is.*

*If I fall on this road they do not become anything. There is no name to pass and no house to be absorbed into and no entry anywhere that will say what they were. They go back to being eleven people who used to stand somewhere.*

*And not one of them has ever raised it with me.*

Morrath was waiting at the Gate with four runners behind him, ninety-one years old and forty of those under arms, with the deep-road pallor that never comes off a man who has spent his life below the fourth gallery.

"The situation."

"Bad, Grace, and worse than the first report."

"Then give me the worse one."

"Aloreth has cut the ridge line. Not turned it. Cut it, on the ninth, at three places at once, and he came through the Warden's house and took it in about four hours." Morrath did not soften anything, which is why he still had the office. "The first ridge army is gone."

She stopped walking.

"Twenty thousand."

"Twenty thousand, Grace, under Karreth, and I have four survivors' accounts and they all say the same thing, and I did not believe it until the fourth one."

"Say it."

"They did not break." Morrath's jaw worked once. "That is the part. A broken army runs and you can count what comes off the field. This one was taken apart in order. Company by company, over eleven days, and every time a company lost its captain the weight came onto that company inside the minute. Before a runner could have carried it. Before the men either side of them knew."

*Every time a company lost its captain.*

*Inside the minute.*

*There is no glass that does that. There is no signal system in the four quarters that does that. There is no scout in the world fast enough to be that and there is no traitor who could be everywhere at once.*

*He can see it.*

*Somehow that man is standing on a hill and watching my army lose its officers, and he has been doing it for eleven years on a wall and we called it luck and we called it the Landward Companies being good and we built eleven waves of doctrine on top of the wrong explanation.*

She looked east down the gallery where the Gate opens and where the light came in dirty and orange off the surface, eleven miles of it, the smoke standing up over the ridge country in three columns that had not moved in two days.

"How close."

"Nine days at the pace he is making. Six if he leaves the baggage."

"He will not leave the baggage. He has nineteen thousand and he is feeding two thousand of Korvaeth's own that he took off the coast road and did not hang." She turned back to Morrath. "That is not mercy and nobody in this hall is to read it as mercy. It is nine days of food spent to make certain that every man on this continent hears what he did with prisoners."

"Grace, the houses are already saying it."

"Of course they are. That is the purchase." She started walking again. "How many of the two thousand have gone over to him."

Morrath did not answer immediately, which was the answer.

"Four hundred, Grace. That we know of. The accounts say they were not asked."

*Not asked.*

*He fed them for nine days without a word and four hundred of my people put themselves in his line without being invited, and he will not have had to spend one syllable on it, and he will not enter it as a recruitment because it was not one.*

*I have been fighting a man for eleven years and I have had one question about him the whole time, which is why the Landward Wall never runs out of men.*

"I am going out to him. Bring the Guard, the Second, and the legionaries."

The pause was about a second and a half long.

"All of them, Grace?"

"All eleven that survived the seating. Yes."

---

## IV. WHAT DESSA SAID AT THE GATE

"My Lady." Dessa Oth-Karreth had caught them up at the arch and had her hand half out and not quite on the vambrace. "The city needs you."

*She has said that four times in eleven days and she has never once meant the city.*

"The city needs to survive," Ilthára Korvaeth said. "For my children."

That was meant to be the end of it.

---

## V. THE SMALL ONE

He came out of the side gallery at a dead run and he was not supposed to be on that level at all.

Eight years old. Rovann grey going toward slate at the ears the way they do at that age, white hair cut badly because he will not sit still for it, and a house coat two sizes too large that had been her brother's and that nobody had yet had the heart to take off him.

The Guard opened for him without being told, which they should not have done, and which she would not be raising with anybody.

"Mother."

She stopped.

"Mother, where are you going. You never tell me anything."

*Eleven days.*

*He has been kept off this level for eleven days because I gave that order myself on the morning the field book came up the ridge, and I gave it because a child who is told his uncle is dead in the same week his mother goes to a front is a child who will remember the sequence for four hundred years.*

*And the order was correct and it was also eleven days of his life in which his mother was a door that did not open.*

She went down onto one knee, which in three-quarter harness is a considerable piece of work, and did it anyway.

The armour would not let her hold him. That is what the armour is for. So she put two fingers against his cheek, very lightly, because a gauntlet on a child's face has to be done with the pads of the plates and not the edges, and she had worked that out when he was four.

"Your mother has things she has to do."

"That is not an answer."

"No," she said. "It is not. You are right and I am going to do it anyway."

*Vess.*

*That is not a name. That is a milk-name, out of a household, given by a woman with no standing to give anything, and he is eight and he has begun to notice that other children stop being called what they were called and he has not asked me about it yet.*

*He will. Inside a year.*

*And I will have to tell him that a name passes from a validated holder, and that his mother has never had one, and that the only person in this family who did is nine days in a road on the Eresse coast with his head off.*

*There is nobody left. Not a cousin, not a bench, not an instrument anywhere in the four quarters. My son is going to go into the ground with the word his nurse used.*

She had not thought about it in that order before. It arrived complete, on one knee, in a corridor, with eleven of the Guard standing four feet away pretending to be furniture.

*Onawa offered.*

*Sixty-one years ago, in her own hall, over a supper, and she said it the way she said everything, which was as though it had already been settled and she was only mentioning it. That she would petition. That a validated Empress may petition the yard on behalf of another house's children and the yard convenes and it is done in an afternoon.*

*And I said no.*

*I said no because I was thirty-four and had been refused for the third and final time eleven months before, and because taking it from her would have been taking it, and because I had just built a doctrine out of not needing it and the mortar was still wet.*

*She asked twice. She never asked a third time about anything in her life.*

*And she is dead, and my brother is dead, and my son is called Vess.*

---

## VI. THE PROMISE

"I will come back," she said.

He looked at her the way children do when they have already worked out that a thing is not certain and have decided to accept the version they were given.

"You promise."

"I promise."

*Which is a lie and he will find that out, and I would rather he found it out from the event than from my face on the day I said it.*

"Go up. Find your sister and tell her I said you are both to eat in the small hall tonight, and that nobody is to make either of you sit properly, and that the order came from me and may be shown to the steward."

He considered that. He had his uncle's way of considering things, which was to look slightly past you while he did it.

"Can we have the bread with the seeds."

"You may have the bread with the seeds."

"Both of us."

"Both of you. Go on."

He went.

He stopped twice on the way to look back. She stayed down on the knee for both, because getting up would have ended it, and she was not going to have the last picture of her be a woman standing.

Then he was round the corner.

She got up. It took the Guard captain's forearm, and a great deal of nothing being said about it afterward by anybody, ever.

Then Ilthára Korvaeth walked out through the Deep Gate, into the orange light, with eleven of them behind her and the smoke standing up over the ridge country in three fat columns that had not moved in two days.

She put her hand flat over the empty place on her own breastplate for about the length of one breath.

Then she took it away. She did not look to see who had watched.

And Ilthára Korvaeth went down the ramp to find her horse.

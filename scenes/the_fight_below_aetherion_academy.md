# The Fight Below — Aetherion Academy

Niran listened to all of it. Every word Haruki said, he heard to the end, and he did not move while she said them. The blood from the sack had found the seam between two floorboards and was following it toward the desk, slow as lamp oil, and he watched it go while she spoke. When her blade went down through the skull he did not flinch. He listened instead. The bone gave up its last ring under the steel and then went quiet, completely, every trace of Aether in it broken small and scattered. Her work had been thorough. Whatever that man had carried in his Crystal, the floor held none of it now.

Asante answered her before Niran could. *Dead men carry information too.* They were his own words, very nearly to the syllable, taken out of his mouth by a cousin he had met for the first time an hour ago. Niran let them stand as they were and added nothing. A thing said once by the right person does not need a second voice.

So he thought about the one who had done this instead.

Whoever it was would not survive it whole. Nobody carries that many dead on his back and walks away from the weight; the road would take its price out of him a seam at a time, the way it had taken it out of Niran on the far side of the Veil. But the cost would not stop at the man. Every nation that kept a road for its dead stood downstream of what he was doing. That was all of them. A knotted road does not care whose ancestors are walking it.

And pulling people from their rest was a disgrace in every culture Niran had ever heard of, and he had listened to a good many. In the valley the dead take their deep name at the end, and the house lays them facing east, the way Senri died, sitting against a stone with his face toward a warmth he would not live to see. Nobody speaks the deep name again until the next Telling. The Kharven sit the Waiting in an unheated room and keep the names in their mouths through a whole winter, and then they let their dead go. No people he knew of did otherwise. The dead were not meant to be brought back to life, by any hand or any formula. Whoever was doing this was taking girls who had already walked that road once, in whatever pain had killed them, and dragging them back up it to walk it again.

*We're meant to remember them. To cherish the time they had here, what their hands were like, what they laughed at. Not to force them back through more agony than the dying already cost them.*

The thread-marks on his forearms had brightened under his sleeves. He made them settle.

Haruki's eyes were on him. Lighter than her voice had been, and worried, and holding his longer than he expected. The last time they had stood this close he had struck her hand away and told her he was not a phantom. He had told her afterward that he did not want her near his Crows. She had come anyway, with a dead man's head in a sack and an apology she had no need to make.

Her warning had been for someone else's child. It found his two all the same. Naori, who read the web forward. Akira, who read the ground through her bare feet. He thought of where each of them was sleeping tonight, and then he made himself stop, because that thought had no bottom and there was work in front of him.

He met Haruki's eyes and did not look away first.

"You came," he said.

It was short of forgiveness, and it went further than anything he had given her since the Veil. He left it there and turned to Xhem.

"Eight minutes, and someone writing beside me. Agreed." He kept his hands loosely clasped in front of him, weight forward. "The bed, the room, the rope, and the paths between them. Nothing past the preserved scene. If a thread runs out beyond it, I mark where it leaves and stop, and we lay your anchors before anyone follows it."

At the door Hollis Grebe cleared his throat, a small sound in a room that had grown very large. He had a pencil already out, and a pad held against his chest like a shield.

"I write Pitwick shorthand, Grand Mage. A hundred and forty to the minute, clean." He swallowed. "If it's wanted. I don't add anything. I only take it down."

Behind him Wat Pellow was already backing onto the landing with the Grand Mage's orders moving on his lips, saying them over under his breath so he would not drop one on the stairs. *Warm the room, not the body. Don't move her. Every word as she says it. Nobody asks about the bell.* He nearly went down the first step backward, caught the rail, and was gone.

Niran inclined his head to Grebe and looked back at the three of them.

"I'll help in any fashion I can, of course." He said it plainly, as he would have promised a cistern by the end of the week. "If I see any change in the residue, on her bed, on the rope, anywhere I've read it, I'll tell you. You'll hear it from me first, and you'll hear it as it is. I won't make it gentler, and I won't make it worse."

"However."

The word came out and then he stopped on it.

His head had turned a degree before he decided to turn it. The crimson in his eyes slowed, the thread-patterns in it drawing down to points, and the stillness that came over him was a different kind from the one he had carried into the room.

The ink in Xhem's inkwell had begun to shiver. Fine rings ran out from the centre to the glass and back, too small for anyone to see who was not looking at it, and Niran was not looking at it. He was listening past the tower. Past the long quad and the lecture ranges and the drill floor behind the east range, past the infirmary at the end of the south range, down into the stone the Academy stood on, to the far edge of what Cymorath could hear. The rock carried it to him the way a table carries a knock from the other end.

Two Crystals, running hard. One working sang high and hot, the hiss and ring of metal being pushed out of one state and into another faster than metal wants to go. The other did not sing at all. It hummed half a tone flat, the same wrong note he had heard from the carriage road that morning, the note that had sat under the cedar line at home for four nights.

Down on the quad a groundsman pushing a barrow set it on its legs and straightened, and looked south, and did not know why.

Across the grass the chapel bell gave a single low tremor. Nobody had pulled the rope. It was full daylight.

"I can feel a battle nearby." Niran's voice was very quiet. "South of us and down, under the stone, past the infirmary. Two of them, both serious. One is working metal hot enough to change what it is." He listened a moment longer, and the patterns in his eyes went still. "The other one rings flat, like your bell. Like my dead."

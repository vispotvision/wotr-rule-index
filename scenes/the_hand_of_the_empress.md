# The Hand of the Empress

*Part One: the stock enclosure above Harrowbeck, mid-morning.*
*Part Two: the Warden's house above Ollavaeth Ridge, the same day.*
*POV: Aeldros Korvaeth, then Ilthára Korvaeth. RP reply. Dova'Kan, Gorgi, Xmere and Baraxes are not written; their beats stay open.*

---

# PART ONE: THE PEN

## I. THE FEMALE

She fought him, which he had expected, and she fought him in the wrong direction, which he had not.

Her pins were seated shallower than the big one's because she had been smaller to pin and the drag team had been in a hurry, and shallow seating means the block lifts with the shaft and the whole thing comes up crooked. He got the claw of the bar under the first housing and she pulled away from the bar. Toward the wall. Away from him, which put the load on the pin and drove the stone up out of the clay at an angle, and the web tore another half inch before he got his shoulder against her flank and held her off it.

"Stop. Stop pulling."

He said it in Rovann, into a shoulder plate, with both boots braced in the clay and a hundred-odd pounds of drow trying to stop a thing the size of a barn from doing itself further damage by trying to get away from the man helping it.

It took him most of an hour. The second housing had corroded into the pin head and would not take the key, so he broke the housing with the mallet instead and killed the light by smashing it, which is not the procedure and which cost him a cut across the back of the hand from the alloy going.

The young one took four minutes and was worse.

It did not fight at all. It lay in the clay with the single pin through its right web and let him work and did not move and did not make a sound, and when the pin came out it did not try to stand, and Aeldros Korvaeth knelt over an animal that had stopped expecting anything from anybody and understood exactly what nine days had done to it.

He gave it heat until his hands stopped shaking, and then longer.

---

## II. WHAT IT COST TO GIVE

The left side of him was emptying and he could feel the floor of it now.

Eleven years of radiant share taken out of other people's fires and put into the only container he had. It had gone in a village at a time. It was going out in about three hours, into three cold bodies, through the flat of both palms, and somewhere around the middle of the female he had felt the reservoir stop being a reservoir and start being a bottom.

He knew precisely what that meant and had known before he started.

*The next fire I put up will be orange.*

*Six hundred men, in the open, at whatever holding is next on that road, and every officer who has campaigned with me since Ollavaeth will look up at an ordinary flame and understand that something has happened to the Blackmatch. Sennuvar Vhaeloth will work it out on the first night. He will not say it.*

*It will be at Rovann inside a fortnight. It will be in front of her inside three.*

He did not stop.

There was a thought underneath that one and he let it come, because he had been holding it off since the second day and it had earned its turn.

*She would have killed them.*

Not in anger. That is the thing nobody outside the family has ever understood about his sister and the reason every ballad about her is wrong. She does not do anything in anger. She would have looked at three crippled animals and the fodder cost and the drag teams and the four guards standing a rotation, and she would have priced it out correctly, and the answer would have come out the same as his own order had come out, and she would have signed it and gone to her correspondence.

And she would have been right, on the arithmetic, which is the whole difficulty. Nothing in the account books contradicts her. There is no line item for a thing that cannot be told what the war is about.

---

## III. THE HAND

He came back to the big one last, because the big one no longer needed him and he wanted to look at it once with nothing left to do.

It had got its front up off the clay at some point in the last hour. The bad wing was still out flat and would be for a season if it ever folded again at all, and the four holes in the web had stopped weeping and had gone the colour of meat instead of the colour they had been going.

He put his hand on the head.

Between the ridges above the eye, where the plate is thinnest and there is a seam you can feel with a thumb, and the skull under it is the size of a cart and warm now, and he had put the warmth there himself out of his own hip.

It made a sound.

Low, and long, and down in the floor of the chest where the growl had come from nine hours ago, and this one did not have a threat in it anywhere. It came up through the clay and through his boots and went through him and stopped.

He did not know what it meant and he was not going to pretend otherwise. There is no grammar between them. There is no account. Whatever passed through the ground into the bones of his feet was a thing he had no instrument to read and he was going to spend the rest of his life unable to check it against anything.

*It sounded like thanks.*

*I am a drow standing in a stock pen putting a meaning on a noise because I want the noise to have that meaning, and if Sennuvar Vhaeloth entered that in a field book I would strike it myself.*

He took his hand off the head and stepped back, and put both hands behind him, and clasped them at the small of his back, and stood.

It is the posture a man takes at a Bench. Feet square, weight even, which for him meant the left leg complaining inside four seconds. Hands behind the back so the Bench can see they hold nothing. He had stood like that at sixty-one years old in a coat that did not fit him while eleven witnesses were paid to watch a name be put on him that he had not asked for.

He stood like it now in an empty pen in front of three animals, alone, on ground that would be behind an army by nightfall.

*This is treason.*

*Not disobedience. Not exceeding an instruction. I am the hand of the Empress, the order was mine and it is in a Vhaeloth field book in my own words, and I have gone directly against it inside the hour with my own tools. There is no reading of that which is not the article.*

*She will have to answer it. That is what I have done to her, and it is worse than what I have done to myself.*

He did not care.

That was the plain finding and he made it standing there with his hands behind his back and his left side empty and the light too bright over the mill.

He had spent eleven years paying an account that could not be tallied, in the only currency the Continuum would take, and every payment had been made against her and for her and in her name. This was the first thing he had ever done that was not on the ledger at all. No entry for it. He had said that to a boy of ninety kneeling in the mud four days ago and had made him an entry with a knife, and had gone to sleep, and had woken up nine days into a pen he could not stop thinking about.

*I will argue it with her.*

*I will stand in her hall with my hands like this and I will argue it, and she will be entirely correct about the fodder and the guards and the drag teams, and I am going to lose the argument, and I am not going to change what I did.*

He picked up the bar and the mallet, because leaving quarried Rovann tools on a floor for an Eressean patrol to find is how a thing gets into a register, and he walked out of the pen and left the gate standing open behind him.

# PART TWO: THE WARDEN'S HOUSE

## IV. THE WOMAN WITH NO NAME

The shutters were open and she had been sitting in it for two hours.

The Warden's house at Ollavaeth stands on the south face of the ridge with a long window cut for the light, because the elves who built it wanted to see their own valley in the morning and had never in nine hundred years imagined who would be sitting in the chair. The glass had gone in the fourth wave. Nobody had replaced it. So the light came in raw off eleven miles of ridge and hit the east wall and came back off the plaster, and the whole room stood in it.

Ilthára Korvaeth sat in the middle of that with her hands flat on the arms of the chair and her eyes open.

It hurt. It had hurt every morning for eleven years and it would go on hurting, and the pain of it was a small flat ache behind the eyes that arrived at the second hour and stayed until the shutters were closed at dusk. Her people had built a civilisation nine hundred feet under stone specifically so that nobody would ever have to do this.

She was fifty-one and looked it, which in a drow means nothing at all and in her meant she had never once spent anything on not looking it. Grey going toward slate at the knuckles and the throat. White hair cut at the jaw and kept there with a bone pin, no braid and no working of it, because a braid is a house style and a house style is a claim. Heavy through the shoulders and the forearms from thirty years of doing her own carrying. A long face, a flat mouth, and a scar through the left eyebrow that she had got at nineteen falling off a gallery rail and that she had never explained to anybody, and around which eleven separate stories had grown up because she had never explained it.

No crest. That was the thing people looked at and could not stop looking at. A drow of her standing wears the house on the left breast in enamel and she wore plain black wool, and the empty place on it drew the eye in a room the way a missing tooth draws a tongue.

She had no name.

The yard had refused her three times, at seventeen and at thirty and at thirty-four, in form, with the count called and the witnesses paid, and had never once been obliged to explain itself because the architecture does not require an explanation. A refusal is a refusal. There is no appeal because there is nobody to appeal to, and a woman without a validated name may not convene an assembly, may not be party to an entry, may not hold, give, receive, or contest anything the Bench recognises.

So she had spent thirty years building a doctrine out of the fact that she did not need one, and eleven years making an entire continent stand in that doctrine, and she was going to die inside it.

---

## V. WHAT SHE SAID TO THE WINDOW

The column would be on the coast road by now. Six hundred, and the levies behind them, and her brother somewhere at the head of it swinging that leg through from the hip.

She looked west along the ridge at country she had taken eleven times and held none.

"Bring my empire home, brother."

She said it out loud to an empty room with the light on her face.

"I would hate to have to come down there and finish it myself."

*Which is not true and he knows it is not true and he has known since he was nine years old.*

*I would not hate it. I have wanted to be on that road for eleven years and the only reason I am in this chair is that I cannot be spared, and the reason I cannot be spared is that I am the only thing holding four Under-Houses in the same doctrine, and the moment I am forty miles from Rovann the Bench starts asking who is holding the floor.*

*He knows that too. He has always known it. He is the only person alive who has ever had all of my arithmetic and he has never once used a piece of it against me, and I have spent thirty years waiting to find out what that is worth.*

She sat in the light and did the sum she did every morning, which was the cost of him.

If Aeldros took the causeway, the thing was finished and the New World would enter the second half of its history with her doctrine written across it and a name she had never been given on nothing at all.

If Aeldros failed, she had a problem with no instrument in it.

*I cannot try him.*

*That is the whole of it and nobody outside this room has ever worked it out. A failure of that size goes to a Bench. A Bench convenes on the petition of a validated name. I do not have one, I have never had one, and the only validated name in my family is the man who would be standing in front of it.*

*So there is no trial. There is no article, there is no entry, there is no finding.*

*There is only me, doing it myself, with my own hands, in front of witnesses who are not paid and do not count. Exile or his life, and either one delivered by a woman with no standing to deliver anything, which every house on this continent will read correctly inside a week.*

*They gave him a name fourteen days after they refused me for the last time. They did it in front of me. I stood up and I clapped, and I have never regretted it, and I have never once stopped understanding exactly what they were doing.*

*If he fails me I will have to kill my brother without a form, and it will not be the killing that finishes me. It will be the informality.*

She put her hand flat on the arm of the chair and left it there.

Below the ridge, somewhere in the direction she was looking, three pins were lying in the clay of a stock pen with the light gone out of the housings, and a gate was standing open, and the field book on its way to her had the order in it exactly as she would have written it herself.

The light moved a hand's width along the east wall.

Ilthára Korvaeth stood up out of the chair and went to close the shutters, one leaf at a time, with the ache behind her eyes arriving on schedule at the second hour, and did not close the last one.

# The Inhale Register

*Coldwater, on the landward frontier. Year eleven of the Suppression.*
*POV: Ruvaen Aloreth. Explaining voices: the POV's own read, and Kethyrros.*

---

## I. THE GROUND AT COLDWATER

Coldwater smelled of wet ash and pig and blown powder, and the powder was the wrong smell for a village of eighty souls.

It lay across the common in a grey fan forty feet wide, sulphurous and fine, still holding the shape the wind had put it in yesterday because there had been no rain since. Quarry powder. Coldwater sat under a chalk face and the face paid the village's tithe in cut block, and three times a season Brannoch Skell drilled the seam and packed it and fired it and brought down eleven tons at a go, and he had four barrels in the powder house and he had used all four.

Every one of them had gone off. Ruvaen could see where. Four scorch stars on the common, each one with the black radiating out from a centre, and the centres were forty, fifty, sixty and seventy feet from the barn, which meant Brannoch Skell had walked them in.

*He fired the first at seventy and it did nothing, and he walked toward it, and fired again at sixty, and it did nothing, and he walked toward it again.*

That was the thing worth knowing about Coldwater and Ruvaen had it inside four seconds of coming up the cart track.

The horse would not go further. He did not make it. He came out of the saddle at the top of the rise and put the flat of his hand on the gelding's neck and pushed it off to the left, and the animal went twenty yards and stopped with its head down and its legs locked and stood there shaking, which was as far as its courage went and further than he had expected.

"My lord." Hesh Orovain had come off his own horse and was already the wrong distance behind him, which was to say the right one. "There is a standing obligation. Article nine, and the Accord's is worse. A Domain Threat inside a chartered holding is a company matter and it is entered as a company matter or the Court has my name in the margin of it."

"Enter it as a company matter."

"With respect, my lord, the entry has to be true."

"Then enter that I ordered the company held at the rise."

*He has never once given me an order he expected me to be comfortable carrying.*

Hesh Orovain was ninety-one and had been on eleven fields and had a memory for paperwork that had outlived four Reckoners. He held the company at the rise. Forty spears and Garrick Dole and eleven of the boys, standing in a chalk cut on a frontier track watching the first prince of Eresse walk down into a village alone.

## II. THE SIZE OF IT

She was lying across the common with her back to the chalk face and her wings folded down over what was behind her, and she was larger than the barn and the mill and the reeve's house taken together.

Storm-grey going to a black at the shoulder, with the scale plates on the flank each one the size of a cart door and lapped the way roof slate laps, and the lapping was tight at the ribs and loose along the belly line where she had been breathing hard for a day. No forelimbs. The wing arms came out of the shoulder and carried the whole front of her, the long finger-bones running back and down into a membrane with a nap on it like wet felt, and she was standing on the wing knuckles and the hind legs the way a bat stands, hunched forward, with the weight forward and the tail out behind her across forty feet of common and the barbs at the end of it resting in the mud.

The head came up.

Serrated horn sweeping back off the skull in three ranks, the outermost as long as a man, and the crest between them raised. The eye was the size of a shield boss and the colour of the inside of a thundercloud, and there was light moving in the back of it that was not reflected light.

And there was the smell, which was ozone and hot slate and under that something animal and sour and wrong, the smell of a body that had been running on the last of itself for longer than a body should.

Ruvaen Aloreth walked down the cart track into the common with the blade sheathed across his back and his hands open at his sides.

Fifty feet off he read her, and wished he had not.

The disturbance she put in the Aether was not a wake. She sat in the flow the way a pier sits in a river, and the Density piled against her windward side and went round and closed behind her in a standing structure that had not changed in the time he had been watching it, which meant she was not spending anything to make it. It was simply what she was. He had put his read against eleven waves of Under-House infantry and against every practitioner Ilthára had ever sent up the Ollavaeth line, and none of it had ever come back at him as a thing that was there whether or not it was doing anything.

*Two manors. Three, with the tail.*

*And I have twelve seconds of standing here before she decides, and no part of that decision is mine.*

Then he saw what was behind the wings.

---

## III. THE SMALL ONE

It was perhaps a sixth her size and it was lying on its side in the churned mud with its near wing out flat and the far one folded wrong, and it was not moulting and it was not young enough to be a hatchling.

The wound ran from the base of the neck back along the shoulder for about nine feet.

He had seen a great many wounds. He had made a great many. This was neither a cut nor a burn nor a tear, and the edges of it were the thing that stopped him, because the scale plate at the margin had not been broken off or melted or peeled. It had gone thin. It shaded from full thickness down to nothing across four inches of plate, evenly, the way sea glass goes, and where it ended the flesh underneath had gone the same way and was weeping something clear.

He opened his read on it and shut it again immediately, because the flow over the wound was doing what the flow four leagues off the southern water had done. Going in. Not around.

*Nothing I have a name for did that.*

*Nothing anyone I have ever met has a name for did that.*

Brannoch Skell was standing in the door of the powder house with both hands wrapped in linen and had been standing there since before Ruvaen came down the track.

*Four barrels and I walked them in and I could not put a mark on her, and I would have walked in a fifth, and I have a daughter behind me in that house.*

Ruvaen looked at the fan of grey powder on the common and the four scorch stars, and then at the great one's flank, which was unmarked, and then at the small one's shoulder, which was not.

"I see," he said.

He said it in Common, because it was what he had.

"They are injured. But I don't think any of our men could do anything like that." He let his hands stay open. "You came here to rest."

---

## IV. THE VOICE

She did not open her mouth.

The sound arrived in his chest and in the flat bones of his face and in the bottom of the stone trough eleven feet to his left, which cracked. It was not loud. Loud is a thing that happens to ears. This came in below where ears work, and the whole of the common felt it at once, and on the rise forty spearmen took one step backward in a ragged line without any of them deciding to.

The shape of it was structured. That was what mattered and that was what he could read, whether or not he could translate a word of it.

**"Rha ven-eth kel."**

Line and self, arriving, generational, to ground.

He caught the persistence marker and got it wrong. There are four in Valorin and he had two of them, which is what eleven years of Accord clerking and one bad grammar copied out of Velthaeir buys a man. He heard *-in*, the seasonal, because that was the one he knew and it was the one that fit what he had already decided.

*They have come to ground for a season.*

*I can hold a frontier village for a season.*

**"Ssa-tha drakh,"** she said. **"Hath ssa-tha."**

Not-yours, the breath. The wound, not-yours.

That one he had. She was telling him what he had already told her, which is what you do with a creature you have decided is not a threat and not yet an equal, and he understood the insult in it perfectly and took it without moving.

Then she said something with eleven syllables in it and a doubled *rr* at the centre, and he had none of it, and she saw that he had none of it, and the crest came down two inches.

**"Kethyrros,"** she said. **"Nyr-ven-eth."**

A name. He knew the shape of a Draconic name, three parts, the sound at hatching and the deed and the holding, and she had given him two and the deed part was three days old. Salt-crossed. She had named herself after an ocean because at this particular moment the ocean was the only fact about her that signified.

He put his right hand flat on his own chest and left it there, which is the Eressean form for a name received and not returned, and she did not know the form and read it anyway, because reading a body is not a thing that requires a grammar.

She spoke again and this time she spoke in the register that did not crack troughs.

---

## V. WHAT SHE CHOSE NOT TO DO

Valorin has two registers, and the difference is not tone or volume or politeness.

It is which way the air is moving. Consonants voiced on the exhale carry force, because the throat that shapes them is the throat that carries the breath, and in a creature built the way she was built the two are the same apparatus doing the same work at two different amplitudes. A wyvern speaking on the exhale is not making a threat. A wyvern speaking on the exhale is performing the first half of the act that ends a village, and stopping.

Everything she had said to him, she had said on the inhale.

He had worked that out in the fourth sentence, from the way the standing structure in the Aether pulled toward her when she spoke instead of pushing outward, and once he had worked it out he understood the whole of the last two minutes differently and did not let it reach his face.

*She has been drawing in this entire time. Every word has cost her air she does not have to spare, in a body that has been running on the last of itself for a day and a half, so as not to kill forty spearmen on a rise who are not in her way and never were.*

*Nobody at Velthaeir knows this. I am going to have to write it down and they are going to have to enter it, and I do not think they are going to believe me.*

**"Ith,"** she said. **"Ssa nem. Ssa nem ith."**

The small one. No name. Do not name the small one.

And that he understood immediately and entirely, in a way that had nothing to do with grammar and everything to do with eleven years of a Reckoner entering a withholding in a book.

Draconic young hold no name until the first moult, because a name is a thing paid for by surviving, and to give one early is to spend something on a creature that may not be there to carry it. Which is not sentiment. It is the same arithmetic the Sunroot Court runs, in a different animal, and Ruvaen Aloreth stood in the mud of a frontier common and heard a language that does not distinguish between a speaker and her line telling him not to name a child.

There is no first person singular in Valorin. *Rha* is the self and the blood and everything the blood has been, and the only thing that separates one from the other is how long the verb says it lasts.

She could not have said *my young are hurt* without also saying *my line is hurt*, and she had not tried, because the distinction does not exist for her to try.

He had spent eleven years not saying one word in one room.

## VI. THE WATER

"Reeve," he said, without turning round.

Wenna Tull came out from behind the mill wall with her arms crossed and her chin up and both of those costing her more than she wanted him to see. Fifty-odd, broad through the shoulders, grey coming in at the temple in a hard streak, a burn scar up the back of the left hand from a lifetime of pulling bread, mud to the knee and a torn hem she had not had time to think about.

*He walked down here on his own and we have all been standing in our houses watching a man walk down here on his own, and I am going to hear about that for the rest of my life.*

"My lord."

"Is the beck still running?"

"Behind the chalk. It never stops, it comes out of the face."

"Then the village moves to the far side of the ridge tonight and takes the pigs and leaves the water." He was still watching the great one's eye. "All of it. Do not dam it and do not foul it and do not put a man near it after dark."

"And the powder house?"

"Brannoch Skell will walk his remaining stock out to the quarry face at first light and he will do it slowly and in the open where she can see him doing it."

"He has no remaining stock."

"Then he will walk out with his hands empty, slowly, in the open, where she can see him doing it. It is the same errand." Ruvaen turned his head at last. "Reeve. Nobody goes near the smaller one. Not with bread, not with water, not with a blanket, and not out of kindness. If a child of this village puts a hand on that animal, she will not distinguish between the child and the village, and she will not be wrong to fail to."

Wenna Tull looked at the fan of grey powder across her common and at the four scorch stars walking in toward her barn.

"Aye," she said.

---

He went back up the cart track on foot and led the gelding rather than mounting it, because the animal had spent everything it had and he was not going to ask it for more on a slope.

At the rise Hesh Orovain had the company standing exactly where he had left them, and had the tally board out, and had already written the hour.

"Entry, my lord."

"A Domain Threat, wyvern, adult female or male, unestablished, lying to the chalk at Coldwater with one juvenile injured. Company held at the rise by my order. The holding is not evacuated, the water is left running, and the beast is not engaged." He took the board and read what the sergeant had written and handed it back. "Add that she spoke."

Hesh Orovain's pen stopped.

"Spoke, my lord."

"In Valorin. Eight sentences, and I had three of them." He looked back down the track at the grey fan on the common, and at the mass lying along the chalk face with its wings folded down over the thing behind it. "Add that she held to the inhale register the whole time and that I do not believe Velthaeir has an entry for that. Add the word *unentered*, and let the Archive argue with itself about it for fifty years."

"And the term, my lord. She said one thing you could not carry."

"Eleven syllables, doubled *rr* at the centre, on the inhale, and she said it twice." He was already thinking about a seasonal marker that he had heard correctly and understood wrongly, and would go on understanding wrongly for another four months. "Write it as I say it and mark it unverified."

He gave the sergeant the sounds. Hesh Orovain wrote them down in a Common hand with no idea what he was writing, and drew a box round it the way the Landward companies box a thing they intend to come back to, and put the board under his arm, and whistled the company up off the chalk.

## THE VALORIN GRAMMAR, IN SHORT

*Valorin* is the spoken mode of Draconic: the register a dragon uses toward anything that is not one. The ancestral mode, which they use among themselves, has no attested name in any Concord document and the Accord has been trying to get one for nine hundred years.

**Phonology.** Built on the attested forms already in the Bestiary: *Vyrrhos*, *Vyreth*, *Hyrathos*, *Thyrrin*, *Kallessyr*, *Thalyndros*. Heavy in /v/, /θ/, /r/, /s/ and /x/. Doubled *rr* is a full trill and carries lexical weight. Endings run *-os*, *-eth*, *-in*, *-yr*, *-ka*. No apostrophes; an apostrophe in a draconic-looking word marks Abyssal Tongue instead, which is how *Xal'tharix* is spotted at a glance.

**Two registers, by airflow.** Consonants voiced on the exhale carry force, because the apparatus that shapes them is the apparatus that carries the breath. Inhale-voiced speech is the safe register and costs the speaker air. Exhale-voiced speech is the opening half of a breath weapon. A wyvern holding the inhale through a long conversation is doing something expensive and deliberate, and almost nobody outside Draconis knows enough to notice.

**No tense. Persistence instead.** A creature that lives in ages does not inflect for when. It inflects for how long the thing will hold.

| Marker | Span | Sense |
|---|---|---|
| *-ka* | momentary | this breath, this strike, and gone |
| *-in* | seasonal | a turn of weather, a hunt, a wound that heals |
| *-eth* | generational | the length of a life, or a line's tenure of a place |
| *-os* | unending | a thing that does not stop, ever |

*Vyrrhos*, the wyrm, is literally the unending one. *Vyreth* is the generational one. The Bestiary's two oldest names are a persistence contrast and nobody at Velthaeir ever noticed.

**One pronoun for self and line.** *Rha* is I, we, my blood, my line, and everything my line has been. It is disambiguated only by the persistence marker on the verb, and it cannot be split. There is no Valorin sentence in which a speaker's injury is not also her line's injury.

**Naming.** Three parts: the sound at hatching, the deed, the holding. *Kallessyr the Stone Maw. Thyrrin Cloudrend. Thalyndros the Root-Wreathed Sky.* The deed part is replaceable and often recent. Young carry no name at all until the first moult, since a name is paid for by surviving and spending one early wastes it.

**Number** is marked on nouns only where territory is at stake. A thing that cannot be held is not counted.

**Working lexicon.**

| Valorin | Sense |
|---|---|
| *rha* | self, blood, line |
| *tha* | you, the one facing |
| *ven* | to come, to arrive |
| *thyr* | to hold, a holding |
| *sorr* | to give up, to let fall |
| *nem* | name, to name |
| *drakh* | breath; and so force, and so the exhale register |
| *hath* | wound, opening, a gap in plate |
| *kel* | ground, stone, the earth one lands on |
| *nyr* | salt, the sea, the crossing water |
| *ael* | sky, open air |
| *vyr* | wing, storm, moving air |
| *ith* | small, young, unnamed |
| *ssa* | not, no, refusal (prefixed to what it negates) |
| *lo* | question particle, always final |
| *khaa* | to eat, to take in |

**The scene's lines.**

- **Rha ven-eth kel.** Line-and-self come, generationally, to ground. *We have come to earth and we are not going back.*
- **Ssa-tha drakh. Hath ssa-tha.** Not-yours the breath. The wound, not-yours.
- **Kethyrros. Nyr-ven-eth.** Kethyrros. Salt-crossed, for good.
- **Ith. Ssa nem. Ssa nem ith.** The small one. No name. Do not name the small one.

# The Overpressure

*The quarry lip above Coldwater. The second day.*
*POV: Ruvaen Aloreth. RP reply. Dova'Kan, Gorgi and Xmere are not written here; their beats stay open.*

---

## I. THE CLIFF

Chalk dust in the back of the throat, fine enough to coat, and wet stone under both palms, and the beck coming out of the face forty feet below him with a sound like a held breath that never finished.

Ruvaen Aloreth had been sitting on the quarry lip since the eleventh bell of the previous night.

He had his boots off. That was the first thing anybody coming up the cutting would have noticed and the last thing anybody would have expected, a man in half-harness sat on four hundred feet of chalk with his heels in the dust and his bare soles flat against the stone, and there was a reason for it, and the reason was that chalk carries.

Below and to the south the common lay open with its four scorch stars walking in toward the barn. The village was gone over the ridge. The water ran. The great one lay along the face with her wings down over the thing behind her and had not moved in nineteen hours except to breathe, and she was breathing worse than she had been yesterday.

He had told himself he was staying to keep the villagers honest.

*Which is true, and is about a fifth of it.*

---

He read the grief first, because the grief was the loudest thing in forty miles.

There is no honest way to explain how a man comes to perceive an emotion at a distance, and he had stopped offering explanations in the sixth year when two separate Velthaeir jurists wrote him down as a mystic. The mechanism is not mysterious. It is only distasteful, which is a different problem and a worse one.

The Attraction Layer sits outermost on the Crystal and it is not a passive skin. It reaches. What it reaches for is whatever the creature wants, and it does that whether the creature is a man deciding to want something or an animal that has never decided anything in its life. Feeling is the thing that drives the reach. Under ordinary conditions the reach is small and untidy and everyone's is doing it at once, and the sum of forty people wanting forty things in a market is noise.

Under trauma it is not noise. Under trauma it goes coherent.

That is the part nobody wants written down. Pain, and terror, and the particular flat wanting that arrives after a loss, all of them narrow the reach and sharpen it and drive it harder, and a body that has been hurt past its limit is a body producing a cleaner signal than it will ever produce again. A Wellspring answers an appetite. An appetite that has been stripped of every competing appetite is the purest form of the instrument.

Which is why the clearing to the south was legible to him from four hundred feet of chalk and eleven miles, and why he had been sitting up here all night with his eyes shut, and why he had not gone to sleep.

Hundreds of them.

Not one grief. Hundreds of individual reaches, each one narrow, each one pulling in a slightly different direction, and where they overlapped they did not blur. They interfered. Bands of reinforcement and bands of cancellation running across eleven miles of New World forest like the pattern on water in a trough somebody has knocked.

He could pick individuals out of it. That was the thing he had never been able to make a jurist understand. Not species and not numbers. Individuals. The one that had lost two and was still counting, going back over the count, starting the count again. The one that had lost nothing and could not stop watching the sky. The one that was pulling so hard at a shape that was not there any more that the shape had its own edge in the Aether and would hold it for another day and a half before it went.

*A mother, or the wyvern equivalent of one, and whatever she lost was small.*

*The structure of the reach has a size to it. She is reaching for something the size of a dog.*

## II. IESERA

He did not enjoy it. He wanted that on the record, if there had been a record, and there was not, because he had never told anyone.

He did not enjoy it and it fed him anyway.

That was the arrangement and he had understood the arrangement since the eighth year. Grief at that density is the richest thing he has ever read and reading it well makes him better at reading everything, and every one of the eleven waves at Ollavaeth had been turned by a man standing on a wall who could tell from six hundred yards which company had lost its captain. He had built a war on other people's worst hours. He had got extremely good at it. The getting good at it had never once felt clean, and the not feeling clean had never once slowed him down, and he had stopped pretending those two facts were going to resolve.

It fed his ambition. He used exactly that word for it in his own skull, because the softer words were lies.

And somewhere under the whole of it, the way it had been since the eighth year, something that was not one of the hundreds was speaking.

He called it Iesera.

He had not chosen the name and could not account for it. It had arrived one evening in the lower yard in the middle of an ordinary read, a two-syllable thing that had come with the certainty of a name already known, and he had said it out loud to an empty yard and then had not said it out loud again for three years.

It did not say words. What it did was nearer to the way a long piece of music tells you where it is going four bars before it gets there. Every strand he pulled came back threaded to every other strand, and the threading was not his work, and the threading had a direction, and the direction had an appetite in it.

Mangled. That was the honest description. Not a voice. A snarl of lines all running the same way, darkness with obsession worked all through it, the whole of it wanting something with a patience that had nothing human in it anywhere.

*It is not evil. I have read evil. Evil is small and it is in a hurry.*

*It is not good either, and I have stopped waiting for it to turn out to be.*

He had considered, exactly once, in the ninth year, that Iesera might be what a dead Vigilance sounds like from the inside. A man whose own warning system has been hollowed out by eleven years of spending everything outward, hearing his own architecture fail and mistaking it for company.

He had considered it for about four minutes and had put it down, because it explained the coincidence and it did not explain the accuracy, and the accuracy was the thing.

Iesera had never once been wrong.

## III. THE LINE BACK

He followed the interference pattern upstream, which took most of the fourth hour.

The pull of a wing is not symmetric. Loss leaves a hole and the hole has a bearing, because the thing that made it came from somewhere and the surviving animals orient on that somewhere whether or not they know they are doing it. Three hundred wyverns pulling at three hundred absences produced a vector field, and a vector field has a source, and the source was east and north and about a hundred and forty miles.

He walked it in.

The nine off the high road. Six clean, three not. The hooks at the wing root, which were purpose-made and therefore ordered, and therefore ordered in advance, by someone who had known there would be wyverns before there were any. Then the residue of a burn that had no light in it, which he had never encountered and which sat in the Aether like a hole in a sung line, all the radiant fraction gone somewhere and none of it in the air where it belonged.

And behind the burn, one reach. A single one, running so hard and so narrow it made three hundred grieving wyverns look like weather.

A man.

Not a large one, by the shape of it. Something wrong with the left side, the way an old injury shows up as an asymmetry in how a body holds its own Essence. And the appetite in it was not a soldier's appetite and not a conqueror's. It was the specific shape of somebody trying to pay a debt with a currency the debt will not accept, and failing, and increasing the payment, and failing, for eleven years.

Ruvaen Aloreth had never met Aeldros Korvaeth and had been across a border from him for a decade, and he sat on a quarry lip in the dark and read him the way you read a page.

*He is driving them. That is what the hooks are for. He has worked out that they run south-west and he is putting them in front of himself and walking them into the Principality like a fire walked into standing corn.*

That was wrong and he had no way to find out it was wrong.

The wyverns were not being driven. They were running from something that had been in the New World the whole time, and Aeldros Korvaeth had spent nine days killing refugees under the impression he was clearing his own flank. Two men on two sides of a war had now made the same error about the same animals in opposite directions, and neither of them was ever going to be told.

## IV. THE OBLIGATION

He heard Hesh Orovain coming up the cutting at the sixth bell, which was three hours before he had asked for him.

*He has not slept. He has not eaten. He has been up there with his boots off like a hedge-witch and the Court is going to want a form and there is no form.*

"My lord."

"Sergeant."

"The holding is clear. Eighty-one souls, four carts, nine pigs and the reeve counted them herself twice. The water runs and nobody has been near it." Hesh Orovain stopped eight feet back from the lip, because he was ninety-one and had a proper respect for edges. "The company stands at the rise. Article nine is now four days unentered and the Accord's is worse and I have Corvienne Sorreth's clerk asking me in writing."

"Then tell him the entry is coming and that it will be long."

"That is not an answer he can file."

"No." Ruvaen did not open his eyes. "Sergeant. There are three hundred of them in a clearing eleven miles south-south-east, on the river, and they have been arriving since the middle of last night."

Hesh Orovain was quiet for a moment.

"Three hundred."

"Give or take eleven. And there is something with them that is not one of them."

"A worse one."

"An older one." He turned his head slightly, which was as close as he came to looking at anything. "There is a thing you learn at about the fourth year of doing this, and I have never been able to put it in a report in a way that survives a Reckoner. A wyvern sits in the Aether. It is heavy and it bends the current round itself and you know exactly where it is. What came into that clearing this morning does not sit in it. The current is already bent before it gets there. It arrived and eleven miles of forest rearranged itself to have always accommodated it."

"Dragon."

"That is the word, and I do not have a better one, and Velthaeir's register has four entries on the subject and three of them are poetry."

*He says it the way a man says weather.*

"And they came for her," Hesh Orovain said, working it. "The one at the face."

"They came for all of them. She is simply the one with a hurt child, which makes her the one they go to first." Ruvaen put his hands flat on the chalk on either side of him. "Which is the best news this village has had in a week. Their own have come. An animal that is frightened and alone and guarding a wounded young is the most dangerous thing in the four quarters, and an animal that is frightened and surrounded by its own kind is an animal that can be talked to. Whatever happens in that clearing today, it is being decided by people who actually know what she is."

"And we stand at the rise."

"You stand at the rise."

Hesh Orovain closed his eyes briefly.

"My lord."

"Say it."

"The Empress, gods rest her, went to the yard alone once, and the Court has spent eleven years explaining it, and I was on the causeway when they brought her up." The old man's voice did not change at all. "I am not going to make the argument. I know what you will say. I am entering that I made it."

Ruvaen Aloreth sat very still on the quarry lip with the beck running out of the face below him.

"Enter it," he said.

---

## V. THE PRESSURE

He came off the lip and put his boots back on, and laced them properly, because a man who goes down a chalk stair in a hurry is a man who arrives at the bottom in pieces.

Then he stood up and opened his eyes.

Grey, and they had been in the dark eleven hours and the morning went through them like a blade, and he did not squint and did not shade them and let the whole of it come in.

Suppression is not a shield and it is not a disguise. It is a compression, and holding it is work, and he had been holding it since he was sixty-one years old for so many consecutive hours that the holding had become the resting state and the dropping of it was the thing that took a decision.

He dropped it.

Not a third of it, the way he had at the drill ground with six men to catch. All of it.

The effect did not travel as a sound or a light or a wind and it did not announce itself. It went out across eleven miles of New World forest as an increase in the pressure of the medium itself, the way the pressure increases when you go down, and there is no direction to it and nothing to brace against and no part of a body that is not already inside it. Birds did not scatter. Birds stopped.

In the clearing to the south, three hundred exhausted wyverns turned their heads north in a single ragged movement and did not know why they had.

Eleven miles away, something considerably older than three hundred wyverns felt a gaze arrive that had weight in it, and would have been able to state the bearing to within a degree.

*Deep water. That is what they will call it if they have a word, and they will think it is a description and it is not. Go down far enough and the pressure stops being a thing pushing on you and becomes the only condition you have ever known, and when it lifts you die of it.*

*I have been at that depth since I was sixty-one. It is not a weapon. It is simply where I live, and I am about to introduce myself by making eleven miles of forest stand in it.*

He held it open for four full breaths, which was three longer than any purpose required, so that whatever was down there would have time to take a proper measurement and would know it had been given the time on purpose.

Then he went down the quarry stair alone, at a walk, with his hands empty and the blade across his back and the chalk dust coming up off his heels in small white ghosts at every step, and forty spears stood at the rise behind him and watched him go and not one of them moved.

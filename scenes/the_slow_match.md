# The Slow Match

*The coast road, eleven miles east of the causeway. Contact.*
*Part One POV: Aeldros Korvaeth. Part Two POV: Lunn Aeveth.*
*Dova'Kan, Xmere, Gorgi and Baraxes are not written; their beats stay open.*

---

# PART ONE: THE ROAD

## I. HE LET THEM BREAK

The first caster went over with a sound he had never heard before and understood immediately, which was two hundred pounds of wound cord finding out it was free.

Aeldros Korvaeth stood eleven paces behind his front rank with his hands at the small of his back and watched a man he did not know take a frame apart at the windlass housing, and did not move, and did not speak.

*He is not cutting wood. He is cutting where the load is.*

*He knows that carriage better than the crew does. He has gone straight to the drum and then to the socket mount and he has not put one stroke anywhere that does not matter, and somewhere across an ocean there is a house that builds the honest version of this and he is the man who runs it.*

The second went. The third.

Sennuvar Vhaeloth was at his shoulder with the board and did not have to ask, and asked anyway, because that is the office.

"My lord. The crews."

"Off them."

"My lord."

"Say it out loud so they hear you say it. Every crew is off the frames. Nobody defends a carriage. Nobody dies for a windlass." He had not taken his eyes off the man on the road. "If a crew chief argues, he is relieved on the spot and somebody else carries it."

*Ninety-one hooks in those carts and I signed for every one of them, and I have been carrying that signature for nine days like a stone in a boot, and a stranger has walked onto my road and is removing it for me at a rate of one frame every eleven seconds.*

*Let him.*

*There is no order I could give today that would make me feel better about those carts than standing here and letting him finish.*

He heard a haft break over a knee. He heard what the man said about it and did not have the language, and did not need it, because the tone carried the whole of it across four hundred feet of open road.

Then the man turned and looked at him, and started to come.

---

## II. THE PRESSURE, AND WHAT CAME WITH IT

He felt it before he saw the distance close.

Not a working. A body coming at him that was carrying more than a body ought to, and the air in front of it doing the thing air does in front of weather, and every man in his front rank shifting his weight a half inch backward at the same moment without one of them deciding to.

*He is a practitioner and he is not spending anything. That is worse. A man who is not spending is a man who has not started.*

Aeldros Korvaeth had been hit by three Eressean line practitioners in eleven years and had killed all three and had a bad leg to show for one of them. He put this one above all three inside two seconds and did not enjoy the arithmetic.

He took his hands out from behind his back.

The first blade came in low, holding the line at his torso, and the second came across horizontal at the throat with no feint in it anywhere and no interest at all in establishing anything first.

He did not parry it. He did not have the reach and he did not have the speed and he had known both of those facts before the man crossed the last twenty feet.

He dropped.

Straight down, on the bad leg, which buckled the way it always buckled and which he used, and the cut went through the space where his throat had been and took the top two inches off the collar of his surcoat and a strip of the skin under it that he did not feel for another eleven seconds.

He came up backward and got four feet and put one hand in his pocket.

---

## III. THE TELL-TAPER

It is a deep-road habit and every drow above the fourth gallery carries one.

A twist of paper about the length of a finger, packed with cut leaf and a thread of grey powder down the centre, and you light it and you watch it. Under stone the air goes bad without telling you. A tell-taper burns white in good air, orange in thin air, and goes out entirely in the kind of air that is about to kill everybody in the gallery, and it does all of that four minutes before a man's body notices anything at all.

Aeldros Korvaeth put it in his mouth and lit it off nothing, which took less than he had left and still took something.

It burned white.

*Good air. Sea wind off the left, four knots, and Density on this road running high enough that I could seed it in my sleep.*

*Which is the whole problem, because there is nothing in me to catch what comes back.*

He smoked it.

Four hundred yards of Eressean spears on one side and six hundred of his own on the other and a man with two short blades eleven feet in front of him, and Aeldros Korvaeth stood in the middle of a coast road and smoked a tell-taper down to the paper while the ash went sideways in the wind.

*I tried to do the right thing.*

*I pulled eleven inches of iron out of three of them four days ago with a crow bar and I emptied the whole of eleven years into their bodies and I will never be able to say one word of it to this man, and he has come here to open my throat for the carts, and he is not wrong about the carts.*

It came apart in his fingers and the last of it went down onto the road and lay there glowing.

"You are angry," he said, "at the wrong person."

The wind took some of it. He did not raise his voice.

"I will give you the part that matters and then you may finish. Those frames worked. Six of them, ninety-one hooks, and they did what the pattern said they would do, and that finding has already gone to Rovann in writing, because findings go before commanders do.

"She has it. She has had it for nine days.

"If you kill me on this road you take the one man in her army who has ever put a hand on a stay to take one off, and you leave her the drawings and the crews and a reason. This war will look like nothing. She will build sixty of those carriages in a season and she will not be doing it to win a coast road."

---

## IV. THE SEEDING

Then he stepped back and he did it.

*Density on this road is the highest I have stood in since we crossed the pass. I do not have to reach for it. I have to only stop refusing it.*

The air between them arced.

Not a sheet and not a wave. Points. Discrete loci in the open air, appearing all at once across about forty feet of road, each one a place where the local Density had been driven past what the air could carry and had begun to shed, and there were hundreds of them and they hung there like a thousand small glass marbles caught in a window.

A seeded locus does nothing on its own. It is a place where the air has agreed to burn and has not yet been asked. He had used them for eleven years to put a fire where he wanted one in a room with no fuel in it, and he had never in his life put out this many at once, and he did it in under two seconds because the ground was rich and because there was no reason left to be careful.

Then he asked them.

And every man on that road who had ever seen the Blackmatch work waited for the light, and there was none.

A burn sheds the greater part of what it makes as radiance. That is the light. It is also the single largest door by which heat leaves a fire, and a fire that cannot use it does not go out and does not go quiet. It goes dark, and everything that would have left through that door stays inside and leaves through the two that remain, which are the air and whatever the fire is touching.

The air took what the air could take. The rest went into the road.

Forty feet of packed coast road, which is grit and clay and eleven centuries of broken shell, came up past what any open flame reaches and went to slurry and then went past slurry, and the shell in it popped like fat, and a trench opened across the road in front of him at the speed a thing melts rather than the speed a thing breaks. Four feet wide. Two deep at the centre. The edges of it ran and sagged and then set glassy where the sea wind hit them.

The colour of the burn over it was the colour of wet coal, and the shimmer above it was the only part of it the eye could hold, and there were no shadows anywhere on that road.

---

## V. WHAT IT COST HIM IN NINETY SECONDS

The radiant share does not vanish.

It is refused a door and it does not stop existing, and it has to sit somewhere until the burn is finished, and for eleven years it had sat in him. That is what the reservoir was. A man with a reservoir takes a village home in his hip over a week and it cooks him slowly and gives him a limp.

He had emptied it four days ago into three cold bodies in a stock pen.

There was nothing to catch it now. Every part of what he refused to let leave that trench came straight back down the working and into the left side of his body at the rate the fire made it, with no buffer of any kind between the two.

The first thing was the smell, and it was his own.

The second was his left hand stopping. Not opening and not closing, simply stopping, with the fingers half curled, because the tissue that moved them had gone to something that no longer moved.

The third was that he could not hear out of that side.

*Ninety seconds. Perhaps a hundred.*

*I have run this working for eleven years at the cost of a leg and I am spending the rest of the leg and the arm and the ear in a minute and a half, and I knew that before I started, and there was nothing else on this road that was going to stop him.*

*This is the last one. Not the last one today. The last one.*

He set his feet on the near lip of the trench, which was still running, and put his weight onto the right.

Then he let the loci under his own boots go.

Expansion does what expansion does. Forty feet of air combusting in a volume that had been ordinary air a second earlier goes somewhere, and what it went into was the road and the underside of a man standing on it, and Aeldros Korvaeth came off the ground at an angle with his coat burning at the hem and no light on him at all.

He got his right hand on the grip over his shoulder on the way up and had the broadsword out before he was at the top of it, four and a half feet of Rovann steel with a false edge and a ricasso, which is not a duelling weapon and was never meant to be, and which he had carried since he was sixty-one because a man with a bad leg wants something that ends an exchange in one.

The trench burned under him and threw no light, and the whole of that road stood in the shimmer, and he came down.

---

# PART TWO: FOUR HUNDRED YARDS

## VI. WHAT THE FOURTH RANK SAW

Lunn Aeveth watched the shadows go and did not understand what he was looking at for a long time afterward.

That was the part he would tell people about for the rest of his life and the part nobody ever believed, because everybody expects a story about fire to be a story about light. What he had was four hundred yards of coast road at the eleventh hour with the sun where it was, and four thousand men throwing four thousand shadows east, and then a section of that road where there were no shadows in either direction at all.

The heat came across the shingle about four seconds later.

It came the way an oven door comes open, in one flat push against the face and the backs of the hands, and it smelled of cooked shell and hot clay and something under that which was the road itself going, and the boy next to him in the fourth rank turned his head away from it and was sick down his own shield.

Then the sound. A long low crackle with no crack in it anywhere, and under it a hiss where the sea wind was hitting something that had recently been ground.

*There is no fire. I am looking straight at it and there is no fire.*

*There is a piece of that road where the air is bending and there is a black nothing sitting in it, and a man went up out of that nothing about a second ago and he had a sword.*

He did not know what any of it meant.

He was nineteen years old and four months on the Landward Wall, and the entire content of his understanding was that something four hundred yards up the road had just stopped being road.

A gull went over the shingle behind the rank, low and fast and going inland, and then another, and then the whole of them off the wet rocks at once, and not one man in the fourth rank turned his head to watch them go.

The rank did not break. That was Hesh Orovain, who had put himself where the rank could see him before any of it started and had not once looked up the road since, and who said nothing at all the entire time and simply stood there in front of nineteen frightened boys with his helm under his arm and his back to the whole of it.

Lunn Aeveth held his spear the way he had been shown, lower hand four inches down the shaft, and was frightened with the right hands.

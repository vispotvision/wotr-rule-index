# The Terms

*The coast road, eleven miles east of the causeway. The fourth day.*
*Part One POV: Lunn Aeveth, spearman, Landward companies. Part Two POV: Ruvaen Aloreth.*

---

# PART ONE: THE LINE

## I. WHAT IT SOUNDED LIKE

Sound first, and there was too much of it, and none of it was the enemy.

Four thousand pairs of boots on packed road, which is not a rhythm, because a column that long never gets one and the front is always half a step ahead of the middle. Harness. Somebody's cart with a dry hub that had been screaming since the second hour and that nobody had been allowed to stop and grease. A sergeant forty feet up the line saying the same four words to the same man over and over. Behind all of it, the sea on the left, which never stopped and which Lunn Aeveth had stopped hearing on the first morning and started hearing again whenever the column halted.

Then the pressure came up through the road.

He felt it in the shins before he understood it. A low irregular knocking in the packed earth that arrived under the boots and had nothing to do with the boots, and it got into the shins and then the knees and then it was in the chest, and the man beside him said something and Lunn did not catch it because his own pulse had come up into his ears.

Then the smell, on the offshore wind, four things at once. Wet felt. Lamp oil. Old blood in wool that had not been washed in a fortnight. And under it a cold mineral thing he had no word for, which was six hundred drow and eleven hundred levy and the ground they had been sleeping on.

He saw them last, and by then he had known for a minute and a half.

*They are on the road. They are on our road, and it is flat here, and there is the sea on one side and there is nothing on the other.*

Nineteen years old. Four months on the Landward Wall and four days of marching and a spear he had been issued in the spring, and he was in the fourth rank because the fourth rank is where you put boys and because the fourth rank is where boys are least likely to break the line when the front one goes.

The Marshal had stripped the wall. Lunn knew that much because everybody knew it. Four hundred spears off the Landward face, and the levies out of the Vessel holdings, and the quarry gangs with picks, and a column of about four thousand had come out of Vaelmarr in a day and a half and had gone east on the coast road to meet a thing that was walking at it.

*If we lose here there is nothing behind us. Not a garrison. Not a wall with men on it. Eleven arches and a sea gate and my mother is nine miles the other side of it.*

Hesh Orovain came down the rank with his helm under his arm.

"Grips. Everybody. Show me."

Lunn showed him.

"Wrong." The sergeant moved his lower hand four inches down the shaft and did not look at his face while he did it. "You are holding it like a broom because you are frightened. That is fine. Hold it like this and be frightened."

---

## II. THE HORSE

The column halted at the eleventh hour without any order that Lunn heard, the way a column does, front first and the stop running back down it in a wave that arrives at the fourth rank about ten seconds late.

Then the Prince came up the left side of it at a canter and Lunn Aeveth saw him properly for the second time in his life.

The horse was white. Not grey going white and not the flat dead white of an old animal, but a true white with a pink muzzle and a black eye, sixteen hands and heavy through the shoulder, and it came up the verge past four thousand men without any of them being able to stop looking at it.

He had heard the name off the farrier on the second night. Pegasus. Out of the Sunroot stud, foaled the year before the Empress died, and she had bred the line herself and had bred that one for her brother and had not lived to see him backed.

The Prince came out of the saddle and stood at the animal's head for a moment with his hands on the near side of the neck.

He said something. Lunn was forty feet off and did not get it and would not have had any business having it.

The horse put its head into the front of his armour and left it there, at the join where the cuirass meets the fauld, and stood like that.

*That is the most frightening man in the four quarters and he is standing on a road with his forehead against a horse.*

Then he was up, and had the helm on, and the white hair came out under the back of it and went over the shoulders of the plate, and the whole of the fourth rank stopped breathing more or less together.

"We march."

It went down four thousand men without a horn.

"We do not stop until we have what we are owed."

And the horse went forward at a walk, and the column went with it, and Lunn Aeveth found that his legs had done it without him.

# PART TWO: THE PARLEY

## III. WHAT HE COULD NOT READ

Ruvaen Aloreth came out ahead of his line by forty yards and stopped, and the drow came out ahead of his by about the same, and the two armies stood on a coast road with nine hundred feet of packed earth between them and the sea running on the left.

The reading came back wrong on the second day and had stayed wrong.

He had known it would cost him hours and it had cost him hours, and then the hours had run out and the resolution had come back and it had come back short. Four hundred yards instead of six hundred. A notch, permanent as far as he could tell, taken out of the one instrument he had, and he had paid it for a single night on a chalk lip and had not yet decided whether the night had been worth it.

Four hundred yards was enough for this.

*Six hundred drow. Eleven hundred levy, which is more levy than he had at Harrowbeck, so he has been taking them up on the road. Two hundred behind that I cannot resolve.*

*And there is the man.*

He had read Aeldros Korvaeth at a hundred and forty miles in the dark and had built the whole of his understanding of this war on what he found. The single reach, narrow and hard, running at something it could never reach, the asymmetry of the cooked side, the appetite of a man paying a debt in the only currency the Continuum would take.

It was still there. It was still narrow and it was still hard.

Something under it had gone.

He could not name what. There had been a weight on that architecture for eleven years, a loaded thing, a reservoir sitting in the left of him that Ruvaen had never had a word for and had simply read as part of the man, and it was flat now. Emptied. Four days ago it had been full and now there was nothing in it.

*He has been hurt. Somewhere between the camp and this road he has been hurt badly enough to burn through the whole of it, and nobody has told me, and it does not show on him.*

*That is why he is offering terms.*

That was wrong, and he was going to act on it, and there was nobody on that road in any position to correct him.

---

## IV. THE OFFER

The drow came forward eleven paces and stopped and put his hands behind his back.

Not on a horse. He walked, at that limp, swinging the left through from the hip, out in front of six hundred men in the open at forty yards from an enemy line, and stood there with his hands clasped at the small of his back like a man waiting on a Bench.

"Aloreth."

"Korvaeth."

"You can still accept the terms."

Level. Not loud, and it carried the whole nine hundred feet, and Ruvaen understood at last how the man had been turning armies with a voice for a decade and found that knowing it did not help.

"Say them out. My people have heard eleven versions off the Vessel factors and every one of them was flattering to somebody."

"The Empire of Eresse is restored under one crown. The Grand Principality is dissolved into it, the Sunroot Court is seated, the Landward companies are stood down and paid off at Rovann's expense, and nobody hangs. Not one. Not your Marshal, not your Reckoner, not the men behind you."

"And me."

"You submit a petition to the yard at Velthaeir for the voiding of your own name."

There it was.

*Of course. Of course that is the shape of it.*

"In form," Ruvaen said. "Convened. Count called, witnesses paid."

"In form."

"Because she cannot do it herself." He let the horse stand. "She cannot petition. She has never been able to petition. A yard convenes on the application of a validated name and there is one in your family and it is not hers, and if I do not stand up in that gallery and unmake myself with my own mouth then there is no instrument in the four quarters that can do it to me."

"That is correct."

"You could have lied about that."

"I have never lied to you," said Aeldros Korvaeth, "and I have not spoken to you before today."

---

## V. THE TERMS

Ruvaen Aloreth sat a white horse on a coast road and looked at the man who had burnt Harrowbeck.

*Nine of the Hales. The pear stock. Four hundred horses in a pit nine miles north of Coldwater with no lime to close it. Eleven waves at Ollavaeth and every friend I have ever had.*

*And a woman on a barge under a covering with my hand on it, and eleven years of not saying her name in a room.*

"The terms," he said, "are that your sister wrongfully killed mine."

He said it into the wind off the sea with four thousand men behind him and did not raise his voice, and it went the nine hundred feet the way the drow's had.

"That is the only term on this road. There is no clause under it and there is nothing to be added to it and no scribe is required. She took a validated Empress off a chair she had held in form for two hundred years and put her under a covering, and every article of the Accord and every line of our own law says what that is, and she did it because a yard told her no three times and she decided the yard was the problem.

"You want a petition from me. You will not get a signature from me on anything, ever, in this life.

"If you were wise, Aeldros, you would turn round and go back to Rovann with your life, because that is the last thing on this road that is still available to you and it will not be available in an hour."

The drow did not move.

He stood in the road with his hands behind his back and his head slightly down and took the whole of it, the way he had taken faces at twelve feet on a burnt common, and said nothing for a period that went on long enough for the front rank behind him to shift.

Then he said, "Was she wise."

"What."

"Your sister. At the end." Still level. Still nothing in it anybody could take hold of. "She went to the yard alone. I have read the minute. She went alone, in form, to argue an article about starving a chartered holding, and she went knowing exactly who was in the gallery." He tilted his head about an inch. "Was that wise, or was it the other thing, and do you hold it against her."

*Do not answer that.*

*He is not baiting me. That is the worst of it. He has just asked me the only question in eleven years that I have never once been able to put down, in front of four thousand men, and he has asked it the way a man asks after a mutual acquaintance.*

"No," said Ruvaen Aloreth.

"No to which."

"No."

---

## VI. WHAT NEITHER OF THEM SAID

*There is a thing I could say to him now.*

*Four days ago in a stock pen behind a burnt mill I put my hands on an animal that was nine days cold and gave it every ounce of eleven years, and I did it against my own standing order, entered in a Vhaeloth field book in my own words, and I cannot say one syllable of it on this road.*

*Not because he would not believe me. Because six hundred men behind me would hear it, and it would be at Rovann in nine days, and she would have to answer it, and the answering would kill her doctrine and then it would kill her.*

*So I am going to stand here and let this man look at me and find nothing in me but Harrowbeck, which is also true, and which I also did.*

Aeldros Korvaeth unclasped his hands and let them hang.

"Then we are finished talking," he said, "and I am sorry about it, and you have no reason on this earth to believe that."

He turned and went back up the road to his line at that limp, eleven paces, forty yards, with his back to four thousand spears the entire way and not one man on either side moving.

Ruvaen Aloreth watched him go and read him the whole distance, and got the flat place where the reservoir had been, and got nothing else at all.

*Something has been taken out of him and I do not know what and I have decided it is a wound.*

*Iesera has been threading since the second day of this road and it is not threading at him. It is threading past him, at something behind his line, and I have had four days to work out what that means and I have not worked it out.*

He put his hand flat on the side of the white horse's neck, under the mane, where the animal was warm.

Then he took up the reins in both hands and turned Pegasus back toward his own line at a walk, and the fourth rank of the Landward companies watched him come and did not know yet what the front rank had already worked out, which was that he had not dismounted to embrace anybody, and that a prince who stays in the saddle at the end of a parley has ended it.

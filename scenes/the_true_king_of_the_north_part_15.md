“Because I would prefer our return to be controlled.”

“You are traveling with two creatures larger than houses.”

“Yes.”

“Controlled may no longer be available.”

Sonzai looked down at the enormous Droval carrying them.

Then toward Sodoku’s.

Then toward the column.

Then at the endless north.

“I dislike how frequently you are correct.”

Selvara’s arms tightened around him again, more carefully this time. “You will survive.”

That sentence changed something behind Sonzai’s eyes.

Not because of its content.

Because of the certainty.

She had not said probably. She had not said I think. She had spoken as though his survival possessed some significance to her beyond the debt of being rescued from the snow.

Sonzai let the silence stretch.

Then his purple eyes hardened toward the horizon.

“Selvara.”

“Yes?”

“If you are lying to me about what you are, tell me before I discover it.”

The body behind him went very still.

Sonzai did not turn around.

“I am much kinder when people confess.”

A pause.

“Are you?”

“No.”

Her silence lasted longer this time.

Sonzai rested his hand beside the Chains of War at his hip and allowed the darker Droval to continue north beside its mate. He did not press. Not yet.

The Blood Hound had the scent.

And the closer Selvara insisted upon remaining, the less effort the hunt would require.

Edward Lambert counted the days.

The counting was the habit, and the habit was the man, and the man was standing at the northern gate of Kharven-Seat with his dark wool coat buttoned against the polar wind and his right thumb pressing against his right index finger in the silent rhythm that anyone who had spent more than a week in the Arithmetic's presence would have recognized as: Lambert is calculating. The thumb moved. The index finger received the movement. The counting continued at the speed of thought, which in Lambert's case was the speed of arithmetic, which was: fast, continuous, producing results before the problems that generated them had finished arriving.

He had received the Hallenfeld courier's report nineteen minutes ago. The report contained seven items. Six of the items were logistical: road conditions, grain movements, ward-station repairs, the specific category of administrative data that Lambert processed the way other men processed breath, automatically, continuously, without the processing interfering with whatever else the mind was doing. The seventh item was not logistical.

The seventh item was: two Titan-class Droval had been sighted moving south along the polar shelf approach road, and the Droval were carrying riders.

Lambert looked at the gate. At the two soldiers flanking the gate. At the specific quality of attention the soldiers were producing, which was the quality of men who had been told something they did not believe and who were processing the not-believing through the only system the soldier's body provided, which was: stand straighter, grip the weapon harder, look at the distance with more intensity, and hope the intensity substitutes for the understanding that the intensity cannot provide.

"You will see two Titan Droval in approximately three days," Lambert said.

His voice carried the specific temperature that Lambert's voice always carried, which was: iron-grey, even, producing each word at the same volume and at the same pace, the voice of a man who had decided that vocal inflection was a form of persuasion and that persuasion was a form of dishonesty and that dishonesty was the thing he did not do. The soldiers heard the sentence. The soldiers processed the sentence. The soldiers' faces produced the expression that Lambert had expected the soldiers' faces to produce, which was: the expression of men who had just been told something that belonged in a bestiary rather than in a gate-watch briefing.

"Do not be alarmed." Lambert's thumb continued counting. "The riders are ours. The Droval are bonded. If you draw weapons, you will achieve nothing except annoying a creature that could eat this gate and the wall it is attached to and the courtyard behind the wall without interrupting its stride. Stand your posts. Present arms when the riders approach. Address the lead rider as 'my lord.' Do not look at the Droval directly. Do not run. Running activates the predatory response. If you must express your feelings about the situation, do so after the riders have passed and preferably in a latrine where the sounds will be contained."

He turned from the gate.

"I must go inform our princess that Winter is coming."

The sentence left his mouth and entered the cold air with the specific weight that Lambert's sentences carried when Lambert was being precise rather than metaphorical, and Lambert was always precise, and the precision was the thing, because the sentence was not a metaphor. Winter was coming. Winter was riding a Titan Droval down the polar shelf approach road with glacier-crystal armor and a blade named Black Verdict and the specific, ancient, patient, cold presence of a man whose blood was the cold and whose cold was the season and whose season was three days from arriving at the gate Lambert had just briefed two soldiers about.

Winter was coming, and Winter was Hild's father, and Hild did not know yet, and the not-knowing was the thing Lambert was walking toward the throne room to end.

Leonardo Ashmore fell into step beside him.

Leonardo moved the way Lambert had trained him to move, which was: behind and to the left, the mirror position of Lambert's own station behind Hild's throne, the specific spatial arrangement that told the court: this man is the Arithmetic's shadow, and the shadow observes what the Arithmetic observes, and the observation is doubled. Leonardo was young, twenty-four, and carrying the lean, careful build of a man who had grown up inside the specific social architecture that bastards occupied in the northern houses, which was: present but uncounted, useful but unacknowledged, the specific human category that the Stark naming convention called Ice and that Lambert called the most honest position in the kingdom, because a man who had never been counted had never been miscounted, and a man who had never been miscounted was a man whose assessment of the world was uncorrupted by the world's assessment of him.

"However. Specifically. I want you watching for information leaving the city. Someone inside Kharven-Seat is funneling intelligence to the Tenrai. The Tenrai knew about the Stannvaard embargo before we did. The Tenrai knew about Bram's troop strength before Bram reported it. The Tenrai knew about Tabitha's arrival within hours of Tabitha's arrival. Three data points, Leonardo. Three instances where the Tenrai acted on information that should not have left this ruin. Three is not coincidence. Three is a channel, and the channel is inside these walls, and the channel has a name, and the name is the thing you are going to find for me."

They turned the corner into the corridor that led to the throne room. The corridor was stone, cold, carrying the specific echo of footsteps that stone corridors in ruined palaces produced, each footfall returning from the walls a fraction of a second after the footfall landed, and the returning was the architecture's commentary, which was: this place was built for more traffic than it currently receives, and the more-than is the ghost, and the ghost is the kingdom.

"The Moto houses inside Kharven need examination. The Byakuya and Kurenai have committed themselves to the princess. I accept the commitment at its reported value, which is: genuine, conditional, and operating under the assumption that the commitment serves the committing house's survival better than the alternative. That is the honest form of loyalty, and the honest form is the form I trust, because the honest form tells me what it wants and the telling is the contract."

Lambert stopped walking. The stopping was deliberate. The stopping said: the next instruction is the instruction that matters most.

"The Shirogane and the Amagiri have not committed. The Shirogane's position is: wait. The Amagiri's position is: unclear. I want the unclear examined, Leonardo. I want the Amagiri presence inside Kharven mapped. I want to know who is here, how they arrived, when they arrived, who they speak to, what they carry, where they sleep, and the specific arithmetic of whether their presence inside these walls was their choice or someone else's."

*He knows. He does not know who, and he does not know how, and he does not know the shape of the thing he is looking at. But he knows the thing is there. The three data points are the thing's shadow, and the shadow tells him the shape exists even when the shape is not visible, and the not-visible is the thing Lambert will not rest until the not-visible becomes visible, because Lambert is the Arithmetic, and the Arithmetic does not tolerate unknown variables, because unknown variables are the thing that collapses ledgers, and collapsed ledgers are the thing that kills kingdoms.*

Leonardo nodded. The nod was the receipt, the specific acknowledgment that the instruction had been received and filed and that the filing would produce action and that the action would produce data and that the data would be reported.

"One more thing." Lambert's voice dropped. Not in volume. In temperature. The iron-grey cooling to the specific register that Lambert used when the thing he was about to say was the thing he had been carrying longest and setting down most reluctantly, because the setting-down made the thing real, and the real was the thing Lambert respected, and the respect required the setting-down to be done correctly.

"Valdís Ashmore."

The name sat in the corridor the way all names sat when Lambert spoke them, precisely, without emphasis, each syllable carrying the same weight, the specific vocal economy of a man who did not permit names to be spoken with inflection because inflection was the thing that told the listener how to feel about the name, and Lambert wanted Leonardo to feel nothing about the name except the curiosity that the name required.

"She has been inside Kharven-Seat since before we arrived. She was here when Osric brought Hild to the capital. She was here when the throne room was cleaned and the roof was repaired and the ward-stations were re-inscribed. She has been the kingdom's sole alchemist for the duration of our occupation, and the sole-ness is the first problem, because sole means unchecked, and unchecked means her work is verified by no one except herself, and no one except herself is the number that concerns me."

He paused. The pause was Lambert's longest pause. Three seconds. In Lambert's vocabulary, three seconds of silence was a paragraph.

"Her abilities rival practitioners twice her level. Her knowledge of Wellspring-substrate interaction and soul-lattice alchemy is, by Osric's assessment, comparable to documented accounts of Cozbi Mahuo's work. She is a minor Ashmore by documented lineage, from a branch that should not have produced a practitioner of her calibre, and the should-not is the second problem. The third problem is that nobody has asked the question that the first and second problems generate, which is: why is she here. Why is a practitioner of near-Cozbi calibre living quietly in a ruined capital, producing alchemical compounds for a loyalist coalition that she has no documented reason to support, and the quietly is the word, Leonardo. The quietly is the thing that keeps me awake."

Seven hundred miles south, in a room that smelled of lamp oil and ink and the specific metallic sweetness of alchemical substrate cooling in ceramic trays, a man who was not his father sat at a desk that was not his father's desk and performed the function that his father had designed and that his father had not lived to complete.

Seiji Tenrai Moto was twenty-nine years old.

The age was the arithmetic. Sixteen when his father died. Sixteen when the Kokan Line's two surviving sons walked into the settlement and performed the thing that the sealed archive contained, and the thing had consumed the settlement and the settlement had contained Seiji's father, and the containing was the death, and the death was the foundation, and the foundation was the thing Seiji had built the next thirteen years on.

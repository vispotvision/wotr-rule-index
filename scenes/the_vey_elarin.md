# The Vey-Elarin

*Part One: the coast road, before he rode.*
*Part Two: the muster below the Ollavaeth ridge line, eleven days later.*
*POV: Ruvaen Aloreth, then Corvienne Sorreth, Reckoner of Vaelmarr.*
*Dova'Kan, Gorgi, Xmere and Baraxes are not written; their beats stay open.*

---

# PART ONE: THE ROAD

## I. WHAT HE SAID GOING UP

He took the offside stirrup and went up onto the horse, which took two attempts, because eleven hours of a day like that puts something in a man's legs that does not come out.

*Write to Halle Thennow tonight. Before the muster orders, before the Court letters, before anything.*

*She is a hundred and ninety with a bad hip and she has been waiting eighty years and I have had eleven of those and never once thought about her until a foreigner asked the right question.*

He got the reins in both hands and sat there a moment looking down the coast road at the thing that had stopped being an army.

"I do not know whether I can kill my sister's best friend."

He said it out loud and did not look at anybody when he said it.

"That is the true state of it and nobody on this continent knows it except four people and two of them are dead. They were girls together. Not allies and not correspondents. Girls, in the same rooms, for sixty years, before either of them was anybody, and my sister stood up in a gallery at Rovann and clapped for a name they gave to the wrong sibling because she was there, and she was there because Ilthára Korvaeth asked her to come and hold her hand.

"I have met her eleven times. She has eaten in my house. She taught Aelveni to play a card game I have never been able to win at.

"And she knew our law."

*That is the part.*

*Nine women holding one name. There are perhaps forty people outside the Sunroot Court who know that form exists and what the survivorship rule does, and every one of them learned it in a house where somebody was proud enough to explain it, and my sister was proud of the Thaelun and would have explained it to anyone who asked, and Ilthára asked about everything.*

*Whoever went through all nine of them and did not stop at eight had it from somebody who had it from her.*

"I look at her and what I see is madness. I have always seen it. She was refused three times by a yard that never had to explain itself and she built a continent out of the refusal, and there is not one person alive who could have come out of that sane.

"She was used. By her houses, by her Bench, by four Under-Houses who found a woman with nothing to lose and pointed her.

"And none of that makes her innocent. Both of those are true at once and I have spent eleven years refusing to hold them at the same time because holding them at the same time is harder than hating her.

"So I will hold the hate. I need it and I am not going to pretend otherwise and it is the only fuel I have ever had.

"I will not let it decide the route."

He turned the white horse's head west.

*All roads go to Rovann. They always did. Every gate on this continent opens onto that one eventually and I have known it since the fourth year and have been pretending the wall at Ollavaeth was the argument.*

# PART TWO: THE MUSTER

## II. WHAT A RECKONER IS FOR

Corvienne Sorreth had been forty years in the office and had never in her life been asked to enter a speech.

She stood on the tail of a baggage cart on the churned ground below the ridge, at the fourth hour, with the board on her forearm and the ink freezing at the edge of the horn, and looked at what had assembled itself over eleven days.

*Four thousand out of Vaelmarr. Two thousand two hundred off the coast road who were Korvaeth's on the ninth and are nothing at all on the twentieth, and we are feeding them. Eleven hundred out of the ridge holdings under Hallavaen Orroth. Nine hundred Varrisak who came over the high road without being asked and have said almost nothing to anybody. And the Vessel levies.*

*Nineteen thousand four hundred and eleven at this morning's count, and I have entered every one of them, and not one of them is here under any instrument I can name.*

That was the thing keeping her awake.

There was no article. No convocation, no muster writ, no charter invoked and no fee paid to any witness, and eleven days ago the Sunroot Court had sent a rider with a letter that used the word *irregular* four times, and the Prince had read it in front of her and handed it back and said nothing at all.

*I have spent forty years entering what men do so that the doing of it can be weighed later. And I am standing on a cart in a field looking at nineteen thousand people who have come here for a man rather than for a form, and there is no column on my sheet for that.*

Hallavaen Orroth came past the cart with his helm under his arm, sixty-odd, heavy through the middle, with the burst veins of a man who has held a ridge holding through nine winters and drunk his way through most of them.

"Reckoner. Is he going to speak?"

"He says he is not one for speeches."

"They all say that." Orroth spat into the mud. "My father said it twice and both times we were out there for an hour."

---

## III. THE MEN WHO ARE NOT ELVES

Pirren Aeshal got up onto the cart beside her without asking, which is a Vessel Coast habit and which she had stopped objecting to on the fourth day.

Thirty-one. Human, which showed in the face and in the hands and in the particular way she never stopped moving her weight from one foot to the other. Sea-captain's coat with the braid cut off it, a broken nose set badly by somebody who had been in a hurry, and a voice that carried further than she intended it to.

"Reckoner. Question."

"Ask it."

"Two thousand and forty of mine out there. Vessel Coast, Coldwater, the quarry gangs, four crews off the boom, and every one of them is standing in a field in Eresse waiting for an elf to tell them what they came for." She scratched the side of her nose. "Are we in this, or are we baggage that can hold a spear?"

*That is a better question than any the Court has asked in eleven days.*

"I do not know," Corvienne Sorreth said.

"That is honest."

"It is the only thing I am obliged to be."

Further down the line the Varrisak stood apart, as they had stood apart since the fourteenth, nine hundred of them in green and brown with their bows unstrung and the strings kept dry inside their shirts. Their captain was called Dalveth and had given no other name and had corrected nobody about it. He had come over the high road with nine hundred wood elves and a single sentence of explanation, which was that Harrowbeck had been a Principality holding and that forty of the two hundred and six had been his.

He had not spoken to the Prince. The Prince had not sent for him.

*And there is nothing in any register in the world that tells me what nine hundred Varrisak are doing in a Sunroot muster, and I will have to enter it by the eleventh, and I have started four drafts and burned three.*

---

## IV. THE HAND

The white horse came down the front of the muster at a walk at the fifth hour and the noise went out of nineteen thousand people the way it goes out of a room.

He put his hand up.

That was all. No horn, no rider, no word passed down the line. One hand up from a man on a white horse at the head of the filings, and the whole of it stopped, all nineteen thousand, in something under four seconds, and Corvienne Sorreth stood on a cart with a pen in her hand and felt the hair come up on her arms.

*That is not a form either.*

*Nobody taught them that. There is no drill in it. Four thousand of those men have never served under him and nine hundred of them are not his and two thousand of them are not even elves, and he put a hand up and they stopped.*

*I will not be entering that. There is no honest way to write it that does not read as a ballad.*

He rode out perhaps thirty yards in front of them and turned the horse round.

---

## V. THE SPEECH

"I am not one for speeches."

It went out flat across the field with nothing behind it and she got it word for word because she had been ready since the fourth hour.

"I am not one for nobility either, and I do not care what a court decides at the end of anything, and most of you have worked that out about me already.

"I brought you here for my own reasons and the reasons are selfish and I am not going to dress them. There is a woman in Rovann and I intend to reach her. That is mine and it belongs to me and none of you owe me one step of it.

"But that is not the only thing I brought you here for.

"I brought you here to put your hand on your own freedom and hold it, because nobody is going to give it to you and nobody ever has, and every arrangement any of you have ever lived under was decided in a gallery by people who have never met you.

"So understand what I am and what I am not.

"I do not want to be your ruler. I do not want to be your king and I will not be your emperor and there will not be another one after her, and I have said that in front of a dragon and I will say it in front of you.

"I want to be your comrade. I want to stand in a line with you and be your friend and your family, and I would rather have that than any chair in the four quarters.

"That is the way of the elves. That is the way of the Vey-Elarin. We hold to each other and we walk the paths of renewal until we have finished the cycles our ancestors left unfinished behind us."

*And there it is.*

*Vey-Elarin. Kin-of-the-name. The old word, out of the mouth of a man who told a dragon on a road eleven days ago that the elven nations must be split and stay split, and I was standing eleven feet away when he said it and I entered it myself.*

*He is going to have to answer for that and it is not going to be today.*

He had stopped.

He was looking at the left of the line, where two thousand and forty people in fishing coats and quarry leather were standing in a field in Eresse, and Pirren Aeshal had her arms folded, and there was a silence that went on about three seconds too long for a man in the middle of a speech.

*He has heard it. He has heard what he just said and who he said it in front of.*

Ruvaen Aloreth turned the horse about eight degrees to the left.

"That was badly done and I am going to correct it where I said it."

His voice did not change.

"Vey-Elarin means kin-of-the-name and it is the oldest word we have for ourselves, and I have just used it as a rallying cry in front of two thousand and forty people who are not elves and never will be, and four hundred of whom lost somebody at Coldwater or Harrowbeck.

"Captain Aeshal, you asked my Reckoner this morning whether you were in this or whether you were baggage that can hold a spear. She told you she did not know. She was being honest and I am going to fix it.

"You are in it.

"Not as auxiliaries and not on sufferance and not as a line item my quartermaster can strike when the winter charter gets difficult. In it, on the sheet, with the same pay and the same claim on the same ground and the same right to stand in front of me and tell me I am wrong, which several of you have already exercised and which I recommend.

"And the same for the nine hundred who came over the high road without being asked.

"Captain Dalveth. Forty of yours were at Harrowbeck. Nobody sent for you and nobody had any right to expect you and you came anyway and you have stood apart for six days and said nothing to anybody, and I have not sent for you, and that was my fault and not yours and I am saying so in front of nineteen thousand people.

"You will have whatever you want out of this. Ask me afterward. Ask me in front of witnesses if you would rather, and bring your own.

"The word was wrong. What is under the word is not.

"We hold to each other. That is the whole of it and it has nothing to do with what any of us are, and my people forgot that nine hundred years ago and have been paying the bill ever since, and if I have to stand in a field and be corrected by a woman with a broken nose in order to remember it, then that is cheap."

---

## VI. WHAT SHE ENTERED

Then he turned the horse and went back up the front of the line at a walk, and the noise came back into nineteen thousand people the way it comes into a room, from the back first.

Pirren Aeshal was still standing with her arms folded.

"Well," she said. "That will do."

Corvienne Sorreth wrote for some while.

She entered the muster and the count and the four contingents and the absence of any instrument under which any of them stood. She entered the speech in full, because a Reckoner enters what is said, and she entered the correction immediately under it in the same hand, because a Reckoner enters what is said.

Then she took out the other book, which was hers and not the Court's, and turned to the eleventh entry, which she had made in the ninth year of the withheld season and beside which she had written a private note that nobody had ever read.

*Withholding struck this day at the muster below Ollavaeth. The holder spoke the name in full form on the coast road, on the twentieth, over the body of Aeldros Korvaeth, with no count called and no witnesses paid.*

*The name is Onawa.*

*Entered, at last, by C. Sorreth, Reckoner of Vaelmarr, who has waited eleven years to close this page and never once asked him for it.*

She looked at the private note beside the eleventh entry for a moment, and did not strike it, and closed the book.

Down the line a sergeant was already shouting at somebody about a spear grip.

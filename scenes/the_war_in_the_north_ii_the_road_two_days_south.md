# The War in the North II — The Road, Two Days South

Two days from Kharven, the northern cold had finally begun to feel less like weather and more like a border. Snow gathered in shallow drifts between the black pines, the wind sliding down from the distant mountains hard enough to make the horses turn their backs to it. Dawn Guard had chosen a low hollow for the camp, protected on three sides by stone and old timber. Their horses rested beneath wool blankets near the fire, steam rising from their flanks after another long day on the road. Asante's chestnut mare stood closest to him while he finished brushing the last ice from her coat with the wooden comb Zuberi had made for him days earlier, moving with the seriousness of a child who had decided the animal was now partly his responsibility.

By the time darkness settled completely, Asante had eaten and disappeared into his own small tent with considerably less argument than usual. The journey had exhausted him, though he would never have admitted it before being told. Zuberi checked the horses one final time, tightened the lines against the expected wind, then ducked into the larger tent he shared with Ara. A small covered lantern cast warm amber light across thick blankets and travel packs, making the frozen world beyond the canvas seem farther away than it actually was.

Ara had already removed the heavier layers of her traveling clothes and sat wrapped beneath one of the blankets when he came inside. Zuberi lowered himself beside her with a quiet exhale, broad shoulders finally losing some of the rigid posture he carried while traveling. For a moment he simply sat there. Then he took her hand, turned it gently in his own, and brought her palm against his cheek.

He closed his eyes. The gesture was unusually quiet for him.

Ara's thumb moved slowly along his skin. "You do that more now."

Zuberi opened his eyes but did not release her hand. "Do what?"

"Hold onto me like I might disappear."

His gaze remained on her.

"That obvious?"

"To me."

He gave the smallest breath through his nose, somewhere between acknowledgment and amusement. His fingers closed more securely around hers, keeping her palm against his face while the wind moved softly against the tent walls.

"I've watched too many people become memories."

Ara's expression softened. "Zuberi."

"I know." His voice remained low. "I'm not expecting you to vanish."

She studied him for another moment. "That wasn't what I was going to say."

That earned a faint smile.

"Then I've interrupted you. Terrible habit for a sovereign."

"A terrible habit for a husband too."

The word settled differently.

Zuberi looked at her.

Ara's mouth curved. "Eventually."

"Eventually?"

"Unless the Sovereign of nearly the entire Old World intends to remain engaged until we're both old."

His expression became almost offended. "I had assumed there would be administrative preparation."

Ara laughed softly. "Of course you did."

Zuberi finally lowered her hand, though he kept it enclosed between both of his. The smile faded into something quieter as he looked down at their joined fingers. There had been battles where he had held swords with less care than he held her now.

"I think about it."

"Our marriage?"

"Our life after it."

Ara leaned slightly closer. "There's supposed to be a difference?"

"There should be."

Zuberi looked toward the dim lantern. "I don't want our marriage to become another extension of Kushara. Another political structure with us sitting in the middle of it because people need somewhere to point when they say stability."

Ara was silent for a moment. Then she squeezed his hand.

"So what do you want?"

He answered more slowly than he usually did.

"A home."

That seemed almost too simple coming from him.

Ara waited.

Zuberi continued. "A place where I am not Dawn Guard every moment I'm awake. Where you don't have to calculate what a room expects from you before entering it. Where someone can knock on the door and be told to come back tomorrow because we are eating."

Ara smiled.

"I would pay to watch you tell a delegation that."

"They would survive."

"You aren't sure."

"They should."

She laughed again, quieter this time, and moved close enough that her shoulder rested against his arm. Zuberi adjusted the blanket around both of them almost automatically.

"And children?" Ara asked.

That made him pause. Not uncomfortably. Seriously.

"I've thought about that too."

Ara tilted her head toward him. "You look like you're preparing a military report."

"I'm considering the question."

"It was two words."

"It is not a small question."

"No."

Zuberi's gaze drifted toward the tent entrance, beyond which Asante slept several yards away.

"I would want them."

Ara watched his face.

"But?"

"But I don't want them born owing Kushara anything."

That answer came more firmly.

Zuberi's jaw tightened slightly. "Being Zettari already places weight on a child before they understand what weight is. Royal blood makes it worse. People would look at them and see succession, bloodline, continuation, whatever title they decide matters more than the person standing underneath it."

Ara's expression grew thoughtful.

"And you don't want that."

"No."

His thumb moved across the back of her hand.

"I would teach them Kushara. Teach them the family. Teach them what being Zettari means. But I would not raise them believing they were born because the kingdom required heirs."

Ara rested her head against his shoulder.

"They'll still inherit something from you."

Zuberi looked down at her.

"Stubbornness?"

"At minimum."

His mouth twitched.

Ara continued, "Responsibility too. Whether you like it or not. They'll watch you."

"That concerns me more."

"Why?"

"Because children learn what you do before they understand what you tell them."

The words drew his gaze toward the entrance again.

*Asante.*

Ara noticed.

"You're thinking about him."

"Yes."

Outside, the wind briefly strengthened, carrying a low sound from one of the horses before settling again.

Zuberi leaned back against their travel packs. "I don't know what his life becomes after Kharven."

Ara was quiet.

"He has Kushara," she said.

"He does."

"And you."

"Yes."

"And me."

Zuberi looked at her.

Ara lifted an eyebrow. "That part wasn't optional."

A small smile touched his face.

"I assumed not."

Ara shifted so she could face him better. "Then why are you worried?"

"Because having somewhere to live is not the same thing as having somewhere you belong."

His expression grew more serious.

His expression grew more serious.

"Asante lost his mother. His father. Vaeloris. Whatever childhood he was supposed to have ended before he understood what was happening. Everyone around him keeps seeing what he survived, what he might become, what Kujo did to him."

Zuberi shook his head faintly.

"I want someone to look at him and see that he is still a child."

Ara's voice softened. "You do."

"I'm trying."

"That counts."

Zuberi did not answer immediately.

Ara studied him. "Do you want him to live with us?"

He looked at her.

She smiled faintly. "You've been thinking it. Your face does something when you're trying to turn an emotional decision into statecraft."

"My face does nothing."

"Zuberi."

He surrendered with a quiet breath.

"Yes."

Ara waited.

"If he wants to."

That qualification mattered.

Zuberi continued, "I don't want to decide his life because everyone else who could decide it with him is gone. If he wants his own quarters in the palace, he gets them. If he wants to live somewhere quieter, we arrange it. If he wants to remain close to us…"

His fingers tightened around hers.

"Then he remains."

Ara rested her head against him again.

"He'll probably stay."

"You sound certain."

"He follows you around when he thinks you aren't noticing."

"I notice."

"Of course you do."

A pause.

"He also watches the way you treat the horses."

Zuberi frowned slightly. "What does that have to do with anything?"

"Everything."

He looked unconvinced.

Ara smiled against his shoulder. "You fed them before yourself yesterday."

"They were carrying us."

"You fixed his tack."

"It was broken."

"You made him a comb."

"There was wood."

Ara lifted her head just enough to look at him.

"Exactly."

Zuberi stared at her for several seconds before realizing there would be no winning that argument.

"Hm."

Ara laughed softly.

The lantern flickered between them.

After a while, Zuberi spoke again.

"Kushara will change."

Ara's humor faded as she listened.

"It has to."

He looked toward the canvas ceiling as though seeing the Old World beyond it. "I spent years consolidating it because fragmentation would have killed more people than conquest did. Houses needed one law. Roads needed protection. Grain needed to cross borders without three different lords deciding it belonged to them."

Ara nodded.

"But that cannot be the final form."

"What comes after?"

Zuberi considered it.

"Institutions that do not require me."

Ara turned toward him.

"That sounds suspiciously healthy."

He gave her a dry look.

"I'm capable of learning."

"Occasionally."

Zuberi continued, ignoring her. "If Kushara falls apart because one Zettari dies, then I built a throne, not a kingdom. I want the Houses able to govern without waiting for my permission. Courts that function without my judgment. Trade that survives my absence. An army loyal to Kushara rather than to one man wearing its crown."

Ara's expression became more serious.

"And when you eventually step away?"

He looked at her.

"Eventually?"

"You just criticized me for that word."

"I'm reclaiming it."

She smiled.

Zuberi looked toward their joined hands again.

"I don't know when."

"But you think about it."

"Yes."

His voice became quieter.

"I would like to know who I am when nobody needs Dawn Guard anymore."

Ara held his gaze.

"Zuberi."

He went still. She squeezed his hand.

"That's who."

The answer was so immediate that he had nothing prepared against it.

For several seconds the sovereign who had negotiated kingdoms, conquered territories, survived wars, and carried the expectations of an entire continent simply looked at her.

Then he brought her hand back to his face.

Ara smiled.

"There you go again."

"Yes."

He pressed a kiss into her palm.

"I am."

Outside, Asante slept beneath another canvas roof while the horses rested around the dying campfire. Somewhere two days north waited Kharven, starving, fractured, politically wounded, and carrying problems that would require Dawn Guard again.

But not tonight. Tonight he was Zuberi. Ara was beside him. Asante was safe.

And for a few hours, the future of Kushara was allowed to be something other than a kingdom that needed saving.

# What We Did Not Do

*The coast road, after the bodies were counted.*
*POV: Ruvaen Aloreth. RP reply. Dova'Kan, Gorgi, Xmere and Baraxes are not written; their beats stay open.*

---

## I. THE CORRECTION HE OWED

He came back down the road to say it, which cost him more than the walk.

"I lied to you at Coldwater and I have been carrying it since, and I am going to correct it before you cross any water, because you gave me a Name with nothing hung on it and I gave you a false sentence back.

"I told you I was the last of my bloodline alive.

"That is true in form. It is true the way a thing is true when a Reckoner writes it and nobody can strike it. It is also a lie, and I knew it was a lie while I was saying it, and I said it anyway because it is the version that has been said about me for eleven years and I have stopped correcting people.

"Here is the form.

"A granted name does not pass. The princedom was given to me by an assembly, in front of paid witnesses, because everyone with a better claim was in the ground, and a thing given is held and not inherited. When I die it goes back to the yard and the yard gives it to somebody else or does not. There is no line behind me. In the register there is one holder and after him a blank, and the register is what my people mean by a bloodline.

"So when a clerk writes that I am the last, he is not making a mistake.

"He is describing a filing system.

"I have two children."

---

## II. THE PROMISE

"A daughter and a son.

"Aelveni is six hundred and eleven. Corren is younger and is not in this and I am not going to say more about him on a road with boards on it.

"Six hundred years is nothing. I want you to understand that before you form a picture, because I have watched people from other peoples hear a number like that and assume a person. She has never held a wall. She has never buried anybody she chose. She has spent six centuries in a court that convenes over witness fees and she is very good at it, and the world outside that court is a thing she has read about in a hand she trusts.

"She is not stupid and she is not soft. She is simply unfinished, and there is no shortcut, and in our people it takes about nine hundred years and a catastrophe.

"I promised them both I would not come back to them in a box.

"I made that promise the morning I rode for the Landward Wall the first time and I have made it again every year since, on the same day, in the same room, and neither of them has ever believed me and both of them let me say it.

"I do not think I can keep it.

"I have said that out loud to nobody until this minute. Not to my Marshal, not to my Reckoner, not to either of them. I am saying it to a man I met the day before yesterday who is about to cross an ocean, which tells you something about me that I do not enjoy knowing.

"So here is what I ask, and it is the only thing I will ever ask of you.

"When it happens, if you are still on this continent, treat her the way you treated me.

"Not gently. I am not asking for gentle and she would hear it and take offence and she would be right. Treat her the way you treated me, which was to correct her when she is wrong, in front of whoever is standing there, and tell her plainly what you will not do and why, and give her a Name with nothing hung on it and let her work out what to do with it.

"Nobody has ever done that to her. Everybody in that court has been managing her since she was two hundred, because she is my daughter and I am what I am, and a man who manages a prince's child is a man who has decided to keep his seat.

"She knows they do it. That is the part that will not let me sleep. She has known since she was four hundred and she has never once been able to get a straight sentence out of anybody, and she has begun to talk like them, which is the way it takes."

---

## III. WHAT HE ACTUALLY IS

"And I am going to answer the other thing, because you have been straight with me twice and I am not going to be gracious at you.

"You keep speaking to me as though there is a better man in here who has been prevented from coming out by circumstance.

"There is not.

"I am sorry I am not what you expected from a High Elf. I am sorry for the version of us in your archives, the one with the long words and the restraint and the nine-hundred-year memory, and I am sorry that what crossed your ocean to meet you is a man who took a head off an unarmed commander this morning and has not once been sorry about it in the eleven hours since.

"I feel hate. That is the whole inventory. I have gone looking, in the ninth year, properly, with the only instrument I have, and there was nothing else in there and I have stopped expecting there to be.

"And when I look at anything at all, what I see first is where to put the rage.

"That is not despair and I am not asking you to sit with me about it. It is a working condition. A man can command four thousand people in that condition for eleven years and do it competently, and I have, and the Landward Wall held every winter it was asked to hold."

He put his hands behind his back and clasped them at the small of it.

*Why am I standing like this.*

*I have never stood like this in my life. It is not an Eressean posture and there is no form in it and I have seen it exactly once, this morning, on a drow in front of a burnt common with his officers watching him.*

He did not unclasp them.

---

## IV. WHAT HE DOES NOT KNOW

"Now the Low Draw, and you are going to be disappointed, and I would rather disappoint you here than in a letter.

"You asked for the ugly material and the yearly readings and what we did not do, and you asked it better than any Eressean has ever asked it, and I am going to give you the honest answer first.

"We do not know why it came back.

"That is the whole of it. We have a fence and sixty years and a vein that started running again, and we have no controlled comparison anywhere, because we never fenced a second one to see. There was one Low Draw and there is one result and any Measurewright who tells you otherwise is selling you a doctrine.

"The Court has been claiming the fence for thirty-one years. The fence may have had nothing to do with it.

"Now the specifics, in your order, and I will mark the ones where I am guessing.

"No cutting, no working, no roads, no building. Those held, and those are documented, because the Court enjoys documenting prohibitions.

"No grazing inside the old boundary. That held for the first forty years and then it did not, because tenants are tenants, and there were sheep on the north end from about the forty-first year and everybody knew and nobody entered it.

"Hunting continued the entire time. Nobody ever stopped it and nobody ever thought to.

"Burial continued. That ground has a small house-yard at the eastern end and it took bodies throughout, perhaps four a year, which is a thing I have never once seen anybody ask about and which I would want to ask about if I were you.

"Fires I do not know. There were two large ones in the period and I do not know whether anybody put them out or let them run.

"Groundwater I do not know and nobody measured it.

"Predators I do not know. The wolves came back at some point in the third decade and I could not tell you the year.

"Forest succession nobody recorded at all, because it was terraces and nobody expected trees, and there are trees on it now and no man alive can tell you when they started."

---

## V. THE BOOK

"So the Archive will give you a great deal and most of it will be useless to you.

"What you actually want is not in Velthaeir.

"Four generations of a family called Thennow held that fence and were paid to hold it. The Court paid them and the Court regarded the payment as charity and the Court wrote down almost nothing, because a clerk does not record what a fence-walker does all day.

"The Thennows recorded it themselves.

"Not for anybody. For the same reason a farmer keeps a book, because you cannot hold a thing in your head across a winter. Rainfall by the week. When the beck came up and when it went down. What was flowering on the north end and what was not. The first year the wolves were heard and the first year they were seen, which are two different years. The year the sheep came. The year the first thorn took on the terrace wall and the year they stopped pulling it out.

"Four generations. One hand at a time, in Common, in a hand that gets worse in the second generation and better again in the third.

"It is the only continuous record of that ground in the world and it has never been in any archive and no jurist has ever read one page of it.

"The youngest of them is called Halle Thennow and she is a hundred and ninety and has a bad hip and still walks the fence, and she has the book, and it is in a chest in her house.

"I will not send for it.

"She would give it to me. Of course she would. I am the Prince and she is a hundred and ninety and her family has taken Court money for four generations, and if I put my name on a request she would have it wrapped and on a cart inside a day and would thank the rider.

"That is precisely why I will not ask her that way.

"I will write to her, today, and tell her a foreign lord wants to read it and why, and that the answer may be no, and that no is an answer she is permitted to give me. Then you or whoever you send will go and sit in her kitchen and she will decide.

"She will say yes. She has been waiting eighty years for one person to ask her what she writes in that book.

"And you should go yourself, if you can, and not send a clerk. She will talk to a man who came. She will hand a clerk a summary."

---

## VI. WHAT HE WILL NOT ATTACH

"Nothing is attached to that. No condition, no return, no understanding, no future consideration, and I will put the absence of those in writing over my own name if you want it in your hand before you sail.

"Because a man died on this road nine hours ago trying to make you part of our story, and he spent everything he had to do it, and I am not going to finish the job for him by handing you a gift with a hook in it.

"Take the book. Take the readings. Close half your country for a hundred years if that is what it wants and let your Ten Wings shout about it.

"And when they shout, which they will, you may tell them from me that the Sunroot Court spent thirty-one years calling the Low Draw a triumph of Eressean stewardship and has never once fenced a second vein to find out whether it was. Every Court on every continent would rather have a story than a second trial. You are not going to be the first man to beat that, and you should plan for losing it rather than for winning it.

"If it works, I would be glad, and I will never see it, and neither will you if the numbers are right.

"That is the whole of what I have.

"One more thing and then I will let you go north.

"You asked what *alone* means, precisely enough to build policy on, and that is the right question and it is the question my people have never asked. So take the answer from a soldier and not from a jurist.

"A prohibition holds for about forty years. That is the number and I would put money on it anywhere in the four quarters. Forty years is the working life of the men who remember why the rule was made, and when the last of them is dead the rule becomes a rule, and a rule that nobody can explain gets tested by somebody with a reason.

"The grazing came back in the forty-first year of a sixty-year fence, which is not a coincidence, and in thirty-one years of claiming the Low Draw as a triumph nobody at Court has ever put that sentence on paper.

"So whatever you close, do not close it with an order. An order has a life of forty years and your number is a hundred. Close it with a family. Pay somebody to walk it and pay their children after them and make the walking the honour rather than the chore, and in eighty years you will have a woman with a bad hip who will not let a dragon onto that ground for any reason whatsoever, and she will be worth more to you than every article your Wings can draft."

Then he unclasped his hands, and looked at them, and did not understand why he had been standing like that.

Ruvaen Aloreth turned and went back up the road toward four thousand men who were beginning the second stage of an aftermath, which is the counting, and put his hand on the white horse's neck under the mane where the animal was warm.

He stood there a moment with his forehead against the join of the cuirass, on the far side, where the counting parties could not see him do it.

# Wystan's Briefing — Aetherion Arena

Wystan does not sit. He does not reach for the case. He stands with his weight even and his hands at his sides, and the posture says everything about how many rooms like this he has stood in and delivered news that nobody wanted.

"Your faculty losses caught the Night Register's attention three months ago." His voice carries the distance it needs to reach Xhem and no further. The arena's hum fills the space between sentences. "Three instructors in eighteen months. The first was listed as voluntary departure. The second as medical leave. The third did not receive a listing at all, which is what drew the file to my desk."

A brass tap rings from the wardsmith's rod. Wystan waits for it to pass. He does not speak over things that are measuring the room he is in.

"We have been tracking an individual operating under the mantle of someone called The Mother." He says the title the way an investigator says a title: without reverence, without scorn, with the flat weight of a name attached to a file that has cost him sleep. "What we know is narrow. What we suspect is wider. What we can prove sits somewhere between the two, and that is why I am standing in your arena rather than sending a letter."

He lets that settle. A student on the far platform finishes her form and the wooden sword taps the stone once as she resets. The sound crosses the arena like a single dropped coin.

"The pattern is consistent across three districts." Wystan shifts his weight. The habit of a man who stands for long hours in rooms where the news is bad. "Young practitioners. Low Stage. Students without patronage or strong family ties. Someone approaches them with instruction the academy does not offer and promises the sort of advancement that their circumstances have denied them. The instruction is real. The advancement is not."

He pauses. His jaw tightens once, a movement so brief it would be invisible to anyone who was not watching his face the way an investigator watches a face. In the tiered seats above, two students laugh at something unrelated, and the sound sits wrong against what Wystan is about to say.

"The Night Register has classified the result as Corruption. In practice, the classification does not capture what I have seen." His voice does not change. His hands do not move. But his left thumb presses once against the pad of his index finger, a single compression and release, and then is still. "Their Crystals are altered. The Shell degrades in a pattern we have not been able to reverse, and the degradation does not stop. It eats. The subjects we recovered were adolescents when they were taken. By the time we found them, the youngest could not be identified by her own family. The word my division uses is Rotted." He does not soften the word. "The word is earned."

The wardsmith has stopped tapping. She stands three anchor-points away with her brass rod held against her chest and her slate pressed flat under one arm, and she is not looking at Wystan. But she has stopped moving. People who work containment for a living develop a particular quality of stillness when someone describes Crystal degradation in their space, and the wardsmith has it now. Her fingers have gone white around the rod.

Wystan notices her. Files it. Continues.

"We do not have the source. We do not have a mechanism. We have a pattern and a geographic centre, and the centre is this academy." He meets Xhem's gaze and holds it. Not a challenge. A delivery. "I am not here to audit Aetherion, Grand Mage. I am here because whoever is doing this has been using your students as raw material, and the fact that three of your faculty vanished in the same window suggests that at least one of them saw what I saw and did not survive the seeing."

He lets the silence hold. The draw-hum swells underneath it, and the sound of forearms meeting on the near platform continues below them, bone and palm, the rhythm of young people who do not know what is being said fifteen feet above their heads.

Then Wystan turns. A controlled quarter-rotation toward Shiori. The movement is not casual. It is the introduction he has been holding the way a man holds a document: face-up, because the value is in the reading, not the concealment.

"My apprentice. Shiori Zettari." The surname first, by itself, with the professional brevity of a man providing a name for a record. Then the rest, in the tone of a man providing context. "Her mother is Lady Mizuki Moto."

He watches Xhem's face when the name lands. Not the words after the name. The face during them. Wystan has learned more from what a face does in the half-second after a bloodline lands than from most interviews conducted across a desk.

"Her father is the Immortal, Rashani Zettari."

The second name, delivered quieter. Two syllables heavier than anything else he has said.

"She was raised outside both houses. Her father's decision." A beat. "She is here because I asked her to be, and because she has a quality of attention that I have found to be professionally useful."

Shiori stands where she has been standing. Two steps behind. Hands at her sides. Her face gives the room precisely what the room has earned, which at this moment is nothing at all. But her left hand has uncurled from the fist it made at the entrance arch, and the fingers are open, and the openness is not relaxation.

She inclines her head to the Grand Mage. Brief. Correct. The motion of a woman who was taught how to greet people by a man who greets no one carelessly.

*He sees me looking. Let him.*

On the floor below, the sparring pair have shifted drills. The younger practitioner's forearms are reddened to the elbow and his breathing has thickened, the particular rhythm of a body that is spending faster than the body beside it, and neither of them has stopped. The girl with the wooden sword sits cross-legged at the edge of her platform with the blade across her knees, watching the pair. The students who were arguing about footwork have gone quiet. One of them has turned in his seat. He is looking at the group above the arch with the expression of a young man who heard the word Arbitration and is deciding whether it concerns him.

A porter in Academy grey crosses the upper walkway behind Rovhen. Clean practice wraps on one arm, a bruise salve kit in the other. He glances at Rovhen once, at the folded arms and the face that is watching the arena without watching anyone in particular, and the porter adjusts his path to give the man three extra feet of clearance. A small practical kindness offered to someone who looks like he would appreciate not being walked into.

Rovhen has not moved. His weight has not shifted. But his eyes have tracked from the arena floor to the wardsmith, from the wardsmith to the porter, from the porter to the corridor behind him. He is counting exits and entrances. Not because he expects to need one. Because counting is what Rovhen does, the way Wystan listens and Shiori looks, and none of them stop.

The draw-hum fills the space. Beneath it, brass tapping resumed, the wardsmith's hands moving again though the quality of her attention has changed. The wardsmith marks a number on her slate, crosses it out, and writes it again.

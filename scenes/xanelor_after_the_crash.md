# Xanelor — After the Crash

Xanelor did not laugh.

He watched the two boys pull themselves from the wreckage. The one with violet eyes, flat on his back, staring at the ceiling. The taller one, emerging from beneath a broken drawer with a piece of wood on his shoulder.

*They are not hurt.*

That was the first thought. The second was that neither of them had tried to bail off the broom. They had ridden it through the doors and into the desk and into the floor, together, and the taller one's first word had been *ouch* and his second action had been to look at his cousin. Not at the instructor. Not at the room. At the person beside him.

Xanelor understood that. He filed it the way he filed the exits and the grain lines.

Mu-jin was already moving. He looked at the taller boy and rubbed the bridge of his nose with the heel of his chalk hand, leaving another pale streak on skin that was accumulating them like a tally.

"What did I tell you about flying the broom on school grounds?" The words came out flat, the kind of flat that meant the lecture was loaded and he was choosing, with visible effort, not to fire it.

He flicked one finger toward the destroyed desk.

The wood moved. Splinters lifted from the floor, turned in the air, found their seams, and pressed back together with a series of clicks that sounded like knuckles popping in sequence. The drawer rebuilt itself around the papers that had scattered. The two halves of the desk surface rose, aligned, and sealed with a grain-match so clean the break was invisible. The chalk returned to its ledge. Four seconds. Mu-jin did not look at it while it happened.

"Get to your seats, and no more ruckus."

The door to the adjacent preparation room opened and Frithia walked out, and the room shifted.

She was taller than Mu-jin by a finger's width, though she stood in a way that made the difference feel larger. Brown hair, darker than Geturo's, pulled back from her face in a knot that looked practical rather than considered. Her features were angular, precise, the bone structure that Lily had inherited and sharpened. She wore the Academy instructor's coat buttoned to the collar, spotless, with a weapon instructor's sash tied at her left hip. Her eyes moved across the room the way a surveyor's eyes move across ground.

"If you talk to them like that, Mu-jin, these boys will run all over you." She did not raise her voice.

She looked at the two boys. The look was direct, sustained, and calibrated to a specific duration.

"If I hear any more mess out of you two, I'll make you run fifty laps around the entire school. You got it?"

*Haha.*

The thought arrived and departed. He did not smile. Somewhere in the quiet architecture of Xanelor Rafminar, something registered that this room contained at least one person who knew how to hold a line.

Frithia's gaze swept to Hiromi.

"Hiromi. Get your FEET off the TABLE."

Hiromi's boots hit the floor so fast the motion blurred. His spine went straight. His hands found his lap.

"Urgh... that's what you focus on..." Hiromi muttered.

"You'll be doing one thousand push-ups later. Don't talk back."

Geturo sat very still. Lily's eyes tracked Frithia with the attention of someone taking notes on a methodology she intended to adopt.

Frithia turned back to Mu-jin. "I'll be back, honey. The other Class X instructors are setting up the lesson plans. Hopefully you can handle being the homeroom teacher."

Mu-jin gave a small laugh. "I'll try my best."

Frithia left. The room exhaled.

"Yes, that's right. Welcome to Class X, everyone." Mu-jin's voice settled into the teaching register. "As some of you may know, my name is Kwon Mu-jin. I'll be your homeroom teacher this year, and your instructor on basic magic and rudimentary summoning. For the most part I'll be responsible for your safety and your teaching at the Academy, so if you need anything, feel free to simply ask."

The cold-pressure girl slipped through the threshold, caught the doorframe, fixed her hair. She chose the back. Near Xanelor. Her books arranged like a fortification. One old enough that the binding had gone soft and the pages had the colour of tea-stained linen.

He did not speak to her. He let her have the back of the room the way you let someone have a clearing: by being present in it without filling it.

# Xanelor — Rikudoku and the Address

The room had settled into something approaching order. Conversations had found their grooves, the cousins were seated, and the cold-pressure girl had retreated behind her book. Hiromi's feet were on the floor. Mu-jin was at the board.

The door opened one more time.

The boy who came through it filled the doorframe in a way most thirteen-year-olds could not manage without standing on something.

Rikudoku was tall. Not tall-for-his-age. Tall the way a post is tall, structurally, from the ground up, as though his skeleton had received its blueprint from a different office than the one the Moto bloodline typically used. Six foot three at minimum, possibly four, with the dense, long-limbed frame of a body that had committed to its dimensions early and was still growing into the consequences. Broad through the shoulder. Heavy through the chest.

His skin was deep brown, Moto-dark, the warmth of a complexion defined by the bloodline's founding stock and unchanged across however many generations separated him from the source. His hair was white, the flat, particular white of the Moto line, falling to his shoulders unbrushed. The tips were black. Not dyed. Not transitional. Black, as though the pigment had started at one end and reversed halfway through, the white giving way to ink at the final inch like snow meeting tree-line on a mountain's lower slope.

His eyes were half-closed. They had been half-closed in the corridor. They remained half-closed as he crossed the room. The half-closing was not style or disinterest. It was sleep. Genuine, unmasked drowsiness that sat on his body like a second gravity and bent every movement toward the horizontal. His jaw was loose. His stride was long but unhurried, each step landing with the heavy placement of someone navigating by feel because sight had been downgraded to advisory.

He found a desk. He sat. He put his head down. His white-and-black hair spread across the surface, and his breathing slowed, and Rikudoku departed Class X within four seconds of arriving in it.

Mu-jin looked at him. Opened his mouth. Closed it. Chose another battle.

The cold-pressure girl had shifted. Xanelor felt her attention cross the room before he saw it move. She used his name. Mentioned the Sage Arts. Drew a line between dream and goal and placed the distinction at his feet.

He picked it up.

"A goal." His voice through the mask, low and dense. "I made a promise to someone. He is gone. The promise is not."

He held her gaze through the leaf-shaped eye-holes.

"He taught me to be still. I came here to learn what the stillness is for."

Then he looked down. Grain lines under his fingers. The green cross over his heart. The answer was finished.

Three rows ahead, the taller cousin had turned sideways and found Hiromi, and the word *cousin* crossed the room carrying history. Hiromi's head came up. The grin rearranged into something less performed.

"Taesyn! Of COURSE you're here. I knew they couldn't keep you out."

Geturo closed his eyes. Lily's pen paused. Her gaze moved between the cousins and Hiromi, filing.

Mu-jin set the chalk down and clasped his hands behind his back. His voice shifted into the teaching register, steadier, warmer.

"First things first. We are going to learn about magical phenomena." He let the word land. Not theory. Not history. The applied thing. "The Academy has arranged a connection with the Night's Watch. They'll be here later in the week to teach you about investigation and inquiry into active magical events. Field work. Real cases."

His eyes moved across the room. Hiromi leaning back, feet on the floor. Geturo's notebook open. Lily writing. The cousins settled. Naori upright, Akira against his arm. Rikudoku face-down. The cold-pressure girl behind her book. Xanelor at the back, masked, still.

"Before that, we cover foundations. Tempering. The different classifications of magic. How phenomena are categorized and documented." He raised a hand, cutting off the exhale building across the room. "I know. The boring stuff. Bear with me."

He paused. Three of his children in this room. A wife who could fold all of them into laps. A classroom of students the Academy had deemed too unusual for ordinary progression.

"But first, introductions. Who wants to start us off? Any takers?"

Hiromi's hand went up fast enough to create a draught.

Geturo's hand went over his face.

Xanelor sat at the back and waited to see who spoke first, and filed the waiting.

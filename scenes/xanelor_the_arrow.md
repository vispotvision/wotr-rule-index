# Xanelor — The Arrow

Up at the rail, Mu-jin understood the boy's gauntlets the moment the masked arm sagged.

The lecture halls called it gravity magic, or the manipulation of the invisible force, when they called it anything. Pour essence into a small enough space, refine it hard enough, and the air inside that space grew thick and the ground's pull on everything in it grew stronger, and anything caught there got heavier by the breath. His son's fists left a well of it on whatever they touched. Each landed strike deepened the well. For most people that was the end of the conversation.

Most people forgot the other half. The invisible force was only force, and force did not care whose it was. Enough skill and enough finesse, and a man could take the weight somebody else had hung on him and spend it.

The bow came down into the well.

It had fallen the whole height of the dome end over end, and the last yard of that fall happened faster than the rest. It dropped into the thick air around Xanelor's feet and struck the sand flat, and the blackwood groaned under the sheer weight of the air. The limbs flexed under nothing at all. It had been built on an island for island weather and was never designed for this sort of atmospheric pressure.

Xanelor did not look at it.

Hiromi came on, and Xanelor let him. That was the strange part, the part that pulled Naori forward on the bench and made Dabney's hands go still on his knee. The masked boy stopped slipping. He stopped turning. He planted both feet in the churned sand and put his forearms up and let the gauntlets hit them.

The first fist rang off his right forearm. The arm dropped a hand's width and he brought it back up. The second took the left and the whole shoulder went down with it. The third, the fourth. Each one landed where he had placed his arm to be landed on, and each one hung more weight from the bones, until his forearms were heavy enough to slouch his upper body forward and down, bowing him over at the waist.

Mu-jin had told himself the boy would bait the gauntlets and step around them. He was not stepping around anything.

Hiromi could see it too, and it made him grin wider. Blood ran down over his teeth.

"Fight back! you can't win by blocking all of my strikes..."

Xanelor slid backward.

He did it without lifting a foot. He poured essence down through his feet and out through the soles of his boots, the spiritual force going out of him in two hard jets, and the ground shoved him away from it, and he skidded back across the ring on two long fans of sand thrown up from under his heels. The boot leather smoked. It was the tide again, but narrow this time, sent where he meant it to go, and it cost him the way a first try always costs: badly, and all at once. His knees nearly went when he stopped, ten paces out, with the bow lying at his feet.

He shoved the essence upward next, into his forearms, and pushed back against the weight.

It did not lift. It held. The two forces met inside his arms with nowhere else to go, and his bones took the difference. He felt the long bones of both forearms creak, the radius and the ulna flexing like green wood, and then the left one began to fracture with a small dull sound he heard through his own flesh, a hairline running up the shaft. The right followed. A thing that pushes on the world gets pushed on back, every time, and his body was where that bill came due.

*One. I only need one.*

He crouched, and got his fingers under the bow, and lifted it out of the sand.

It came up heavy. He set an arrow to the string, turned the bow crossways in front of him, and hooked three fingers of his right hand on the string at the nock, and then he did not pull. He let go of the effort instead. He let the right forearm be exactly as heavy as Hiromi had made it, and angled his body so that all that dead weight hung from the string, and the weight drew the bow for him. The pressure Hiromi had hung on him had become his leverage.

The string came back.

The arena shook as it came. Grit danced on the flagstones at the foot of the gallery. The limbs of the bow bent past anything their maker had imagined for them, around and around toward each other, and the laminations along the belly began to crackle, and a split opened in the upper limb near the tip and ran. The string bit into his three fingers down to the bone. He steered the draw with his wrists and his body and let the weight do the rest, and at the rail Mu-jin did the sum in his head and did not like the answer. Twenty times what a grown man's arms could draw. More.

Essence began to leak out of the arrowhead, a thin bright fume off the iron. The arrow had not been made for this either. The shaft was splitting along its grain behind the head. The fletching curled. The whole of it was coming apart in the draw, and it only needed to hold together for one instant more.

Mu-jin's hand was already inside his coat.

He had read it from the moment the string started back, and had known how it ended before the bow reached half draw. So his fingers had closed on the vial while the string was still moving, and his arm had gone back, and he threw while the arrow was still on the string, underhand and flat, into the one line in the whole ring that arrow could take.

Xanelor released.

It was impossible to track with the human eye. There was the sound of the string, a flat terrible crack, and then there was a line of fire between the bow and Hiromi. The air in front of the arrowhead could not get out of its way fast enough and piled up ahead of the point in a standing wall of crushed air, hot as a forge mouth, and the arrow drove into that wall and ablated as it flew, the shaft shedding burning fragments, the head glowing white. It went down the ring as a literal ball of fire. It broke the sound barrier inside the first pace, and the sonic booms trailed it, one and then a second on top of it, a double concussion that hit the gallery in the chest, and under its path the raked sand tore open into a trench as long as the ring.

Hiromi had nowhere to go. The gauntlets that had made each fist so heavy made his arms slow to come up, and his feet were still planted where he had thrown his last punch. He saw the fire and fell back from it, and his back was already hitting the sand when the arrow met the vial.

The glass burst in midair a body's length short of him.

The vial transmogrified it. What had been iron and ash wood and fire became, between one instant and the next, a heavy bright liquid, silver shading to orange, that went on travelling forward only a hand's width before it lost the shape of anything that could fly. It splashed outward in a wide flat sheet and fell.

The thing had changed its form and kept every scrap of what it had been doing. That was the first law of Mu-jin's whole art, and the oldest: you could turn a thing into another thing, but you could not make the sum of it go anywhere. All that speed was still in the liquid, as heat. It hit the arena floor between Hiromi's boots and a spread hand from his hip and did not stop at the sand. It went through the sand. It went through the packed clay under the sand and the stone under the clay, hissing and spitting, a sheet of bright metal sinking into the floor on a column of white smoke that stank of burnt flint and hot iron.

Naori had watched the glass leave Mu-jin's hand and still could not have said what was in it.

Xanelor was on one knee. The bow had split through the upper limb and hung from his left hand in two pieces joined by the string. Both his forearms were bent very slightly wrong. Three of his fingers were bleeding onto the sand.

Mu-jin swung one leg over the rail, then the other, and let himself down into the ring the slow way, hanging by his hands from the oiled wood before he dropped.

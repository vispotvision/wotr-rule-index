# Xanelor — The Challenge

The library door was still swinging on its hinge where Verona had gone through it, a slow arc out and a slower one back, and from the far side of the room came laughter that had nothing to do with anybody's future. The bracket kept its pale glow on the slate. Under a desk two rows over, a summon yawned hugely and settled its chin on its paws. Someone near the window had started a list in the margin of their notes and was already crossing names out.

Xanelor closed his eyes.

He did it the way the old turtle had taught him on the flat rocks at Omoro with the tide coming in, breath first, then the shoulders, then the attention let out past his own skin until the room stopped being faces and became weights resting on the floor. Most of them told him the same story, heavy and warm and poured out without anyone minding where it went. The next generation of the families that owned the maps. He had expected that and it settled nothing.

Then one weight near the back of the room, and his throat tightened before he had decided anything about it.

Dabney. Where the others spilled, that boy was drawn down to a single point and held there, dense and fine as a filed edge, and the only honest comparison Xanelor's body offered was the flat of a blade laid cold against the soft of his neck. The free, loose running of Hiromi at the front, of Taesyn across the aisle, had nothing in common with it. That one had been made to sit that way by somebody, over years.

His left hand had found the limb of the bow. He did not open his eyes.

At the back, Dabney sat exactly as he had sat since the introductions, hands folded on the desk, gaze on the bracket, and did not look up.

Naori noticed Xanelor at once.

He could not see the reach inward. He saw what it left on the outside, the masked head tipped a degree toward the back wall, the thumb gone flat against the bow. The crimson in his eyes held steady and gave nothing. He shifted his head toward the boy in the far corner and studied him a moment longer than was polite.

"I may have competition after all."

His sister had his hand in both of hers and was hauling on it hard enough to shift the chair. He let her pull. He did not get up. When he looked at Neraveth at last, his face was level, and he said it to her the way their father said things he did not intend to repeat.

"We shouldn't involve ourselves with the weak."

On his other side Akira's feet had found their rhythm again, swinging against the desk leg, her pale jade eyes aimed at nothing in particular and pleased about it.

Hiromi's chair went back.

He stood up all at once, grin arriving before the rest of him, and his hands came out of his pockets, which on Hiromi was a declaration. He swung one arm across the full width of the classroom and pointed.

"HEY XANE!"

The room turned. Xanelor's eyes opened behind the shell. Whatever he had been listening to went out of him like water off a flat stone.

"Huh..."

Hiromi was already walking, weaving between the desks, the two long white strands swinging past his jaw, his red eyes bright and delighted as if somebody had just handed him a present. He stopped at the end of the back row.

"I challenge you to fight. It will be nice to fight someone with a wildly different fighting style."

Xanelor looked at him for a long moment.

The respectful answer was no. Drogo would have given it, and would have given it kindly. But thirty people were looking at the back corner now, and the arithmetic from before had not changed: the masked one with no Crystal, who backed away from the first challenge of the week before a single match was drawn, went down in every head in that room as the soft bracket, and stayed there.

*A shell that never closes is only a bowl.*

"If you are sure. I don't mind fighting you. However, I do advise against it."

Hiromi bent from the waist and looked straight into the mask, close enough to see his own white hair in the lacquer, trying to find the eyes behind the dark holes and finding only dark.

"Advise against it huh? That just makes me want to fight you all the more..."

Three rows down, Geturo had closed his notebook. Taller than his brother by three fingers and broader through the shoulder, carrying the weight their father had carried and Hiromi had somehow skipped, chestnut hair pushed back off Frithia's mouth. He shook his head slowly.

"You can't be seriously picking a fight already Hiromi."

Hiromi turned his head and gave him a smile full of teeth and nothing resembling an answer.

At the board, Mu-jin had watched the whole thing build with the cold cup in one hand.

He knew how this went. Say no, and the pair of them would settle it tonight behind the dormitories with no wards up and nobody watching, and he would learn about it from the infirmary. Say yes, and it happened in a warded ring with a surrender line and him standing close enough to call it. He looked at his son's back, and then for rather longer at the lacquered shell in the far corner. He set the cup down.

*Whelp. No choice now.*

"Fine, class, we can go to the Combat Arena... but let's be careful. Still much for you to learn here at the academy."

He picked his coat up off the bench, shook the chalk dust from the sleeve, and walked to the door without looking back to see who followed.

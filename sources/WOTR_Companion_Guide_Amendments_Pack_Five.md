## A. THE SPLIT, NAMED

The governing statement, and everything below follows from it.

**WOTR runs an Eastern skeleton under Western flesh.**

The skeleton stays exactly as it is. Sixteen Temperance Stages, sixty Wellsprings, Bands and Grades and Coherence and eta, the Master Codex glyph grammar, Family and Physics Domain assignment, the seventeen-section character sheet, Trigger through Counterplay, EU costs to the unit. That systematic density is a light-novel and xianxia inheritance and it is the correct engine for this project. It does not get diluted, softened, or made impressionistic. Section 3.3's division of hard and soft labour stands untouched.

The flesh changes. Prose register, humour, characterisation method, narration authority and tonal architecture now derive from the Western tradition: Martin's POV discipline, Tolkien's cultural register and elegiac capacity, Abercrombie's dry brutality.

**The operative consequence:** the system is author-facing and document-facing. It lives in the Codex, the character sheets, the in-world treatises, and the technical-reveal beats where a mechanism earns explicit naming. It does not live in prose texture, narrator commentary, or character affect. A reader who never opens a codex should experience WOTR as a Western epic that happens to be unusually rigorous about what magic costs.

## B. THE MANHWA ENERGY DIRECTIVE, REPEALED

Struck in full from the project instructions and from Section 4's sanctioned-exception clause. The following are no longer craft law and should be treated as active tells if they appear in a draft.

- **Personality vomiting.** Characters being a trait in every line.
- **Reaction shots as characterisation.** The cutaway to someone's face registering a development.
- **Loud opinions as a default register.** Characters who announce their stance on everything present.
- **Bizarre or exaggerated minor NPCs** as a standing invitation.
- **Absurdist method coexisting with genuine stakes** as a tonal permission.
- **Humour as the sanctioned exception to tone consistency.** Section 4's carve-out is deleted; humour is now governed by Section D below and is no longer an exception to anything.
- **Escalation as a prose-level pacing register.** It survives as plot architecture only (Scene Writing Process Guide Sec. 2 retains its structural function and loses its tonal one).

### Three survivors, and why

**The Foil Function (Directive Sec. 3.8) survives intact.** It was never Eastern. Merry against Pippin, Sam against Frodo, Tyrion against Bronn, Jaime against Brienne, Glokta against Severard. Deliberate moral and rhythmic contrast between paired characters is Western-native and remains actively tracked.

**Yoko against Sodoku survives.** The precision-versus-instinct dynamic is a foil, not a comedy routine. What changes is the delivery: the humour in it now arrives through what Yoko declines to say, never through a deadpan beat landing on a punchline.

**Humour survives, reclassified.** See Section D.

### What this changes on the page

Characterisation moves from **declaration** to **inference**. A character is no longer established by being vividly themselves in every line. They are established by what they choose under pressure, what they refuse, what they notice first in a room, and what other people say about them when they are not present. The reader assembles the person. Martin builds Stannis almost entirely out of other people's accounts before granting him a line.

## C. THE THREE WESTERN INSTRUMENTS TO INSTALL

These replace what Section B removed. All three are proposed craft law pending Isaac's ruling.

### C.1 Dramatic irony through POV lock

**The primary tension engine, replacing reaction-shot escalation.**

Martin's method: the reader holds information the POV character does not, and the pleasure is watching a competent person walk correctly toward a wrong conclusion. Ned Stark's entire arc is a man reasoning flawlessly from a corrupted premise.

Operative rules:

1. **Information is tracked per POV, not per reader.** Before a scene, name what this POV knows, what they wrongly believe, and what the reader knows that they do not.
2. **Where a scene could be written from two POVs, write it from whoever knows least about what is coming.** This is a default, breakable with reason.
3. **The POV's wrong conclusion must be well-reasoned.** Irony built on a character being stupid is not irony. Ned is not a fool; he is applying Northern law in a Southern court.
4. **The narration never winks.** No line acknowledging that the reader knows better. This is where Section E does its work.

WOTR-specific application: the Codex is a gift here. A reader who has absorbed the Family system knows that Judicium and Crymorath are the same Family before Sodoku does. Every scene where he treats his shift toward cold as a betrayal of what he was is dramatic irony the system itself generates for free. **Systematisation and dramatic irony are the same tool used twice.** That is the argument for keeping the Eastern skeleton in a Western book.

### C.2 The elegiac mode

**The single largest instrument WOTR currently lacks, and the setting is purpose-built for it.**

Tolkien's dominant emotional register is grief for what cannot be recovered, and wonder is downstream of it: the long defeat, the diminishment of the Elves, Lothlorien fading whether Sauron wins or loses. Every victory in that book is also a loss and the prose says so without raising its voice.

The Withering is exactly this and has never been written as such. It currently functions as a setting condition and has never been given elegiac weight.

Operative rules:

1. **Elegy attaches to a named concrete thing that is gone.** A road nobody maintains. A dye colour nobody can make since the salt corridor closed. A word that survives only in a formula whose meaning is lost. A craft with three living practitioners. Never to abstractions; grief for "the old world" is furniture.
2. **Flat delivery.** Section 1.2's saga restraint governs. State the loss and move on. The reader does the mourning.
3. **The loss is often irreversible and often nobody's fault.** This is what distinguishes elegy from grimdark's systemic cruelty, and WOTR needs both. Some things are taken. Some things simply end.
4. **Budget: one sustained elegiac passage per scene at most,** and it may sit anywhere except the closing beat, which per Scene Standards still ends on physical action.

Where it earns its place immediately: Kharven's return arc (a house coming back to a seat that has moved on without it), the Long Reading, the folk-world documents, any Dawi material, the Withering as lived experience.

### C.3 Register by culture, extended into narration

The Racial Voice and Dialect Guide already governs dialogue. Tolkien's actual innovation went further: the *narration* shifts register when the story enters a people's sphere. The Shire chapters are colloquial and domestic. Rohan's prose takes on alliteration and archaic inversion. Gondor's is latinate and formal.

Free indirect discourse (Directive Sec. 1.1) already licenses this. The extension raises it from a per-character instruction to a per-culture one.

Proposed narration registers, to be built out per culture as POVs arrive:

- **Dawi POV.** Stressed monosyllables, short clauses, concrete nouns, tools named by their proper names. Abstractions distrusted at sentence level as well as in dialogue.
- **Eresse POV.** Subordination, latinate diction, periodic sentences that withhold the verb. Time referenced in long units.
- **Concord human POV.** The house baseline. Everything else is measured against it.
- **Moto POV.** To be built. Flagged as the highest-priority gap since it carries the current arc.

Cross-reference: this makes the psychic-distance-band item on the Open Docket load-bearing instead of tidy-up, because band and register have to be assigned together.

## D. HUMOUR, RECALIBRATED

No longer an exception to tone. Now a property of specific characters, in the Abercrombie register.

**Permitted:** dry understatement, gallows wit from people whose profession earns it, class-inflected contempt, a character being funny without knowing it, and the joke that is also a threat.

**Barred:** the beat structure of setup, deadpan and reaction. Humour no longer gets its own rhythm. It arrives inside dialogue and narration already in progress and does not pause the scene to land.

**The test:** could this line be said at a funeral, by someone who means it, without breaking the room? If the humour requires a comedic frame to work, cut it.

**Who carries it, current cast:** Lambert (institutional dryness, arrives in the debrief). Pietro (contempt as wit). Yoko (accidental, through literalism). Sodoku does not make jokes and should not start.

## E. WHAT TOLKIEN CANNOT HAVE

The synthesis has one genuine incompatibility and it needs resolving instead of blending.

**Tolkien's narrator adjudicates. Martin's never does.** Tolkien tells the reader that Denethor's despair is a failure and that Sam's loyalty is the greater thing. That authorial moral voice cannot coexist with grimdark's requirement that antagonists get a sympathetic case built as seriously as the villainous one.

**Ruling proposed: Martin wins on narration authority. Tolkien's elevated and elegiac register is available, his moral voice is not.**

Concretely:

- Third-person scene narration is POV-locked and never adjudicates. No line tells the reader what to think of a character's choice.
- The **elevated register**, meaning invocation, myth-cadence and the high style, is available to in-world documents, songs, oral-tradition passages, the mythic strata, prophetic and Celestial Host material, and the opening or closing frame of a mythic-scale event. Not to ordinary scene narration.
- The **elegiac register** is available to scene narration, because elegy in the saga mode reports a loss rather than judging it. Section C.2's flat-delivery rule is what keeps it on the correct side of this line.
- **Free indirect discourse remains the carve-out** and Pack Four's gloss test still governs. A moral verdict in the POV's own idiom, which could be wrong, is characterisation. The same verdict in the narrator's neutral register is Tolkien's voice and gets cut.

## F. WHAT THE LIGHT NOVEL KEEPS

Isaac named light novel alongside Tolkien and Martin. The compatible half is structural, not tonal.

**Kept:**

- **The chapter as a hard unit** with one clear objective and a hook at close. Serialised discipline, directly relevant to The Withering Road.
- **Legibility as reader pleasure.** The satisfaction of a system the reader can reason inside. This is the whole argument for the Codex and it survives the register change intact.
- **Fast entry.** Chapters open inside the situation.

**Not kept:**

- Status screens, panels, HUD-style rendering. Already banned; restated because the light-novel framing invites them back.
- Narrator explanation of the system to the reader. Section 3.1's Mechanism Standard delivers mechanism through physical description at trigger points, never as instruction.
- The isekai commentary register. Wry narrator asides about the world's rules.
- Character-sheet vocabulary surfacing in prose. Stage, Grade, eta and Band are author-facing.

## H. PENDING RULINGS

1. **Does Martin win on narration authority (Sec. E)?** The proposed answer is yes, with the elevated register quarantined to in-world documents and mythic strata. This is the load-bearing call in the pack.
2. **Does the elegiac mode get installed as standing craft law (Sec. C.2)?** It changes the emotional shape of the Withering permanently.
3. **Per-culture narration registers (Sec. C.3):** build out now, or defer until a POV in each culture actually exists? Moto register is needed either way for the current arc.
4. **Retroactive application.** The eight Kharven prose scenes were written under the manhwa directive. Forward-only, or does the arc get a register pass?

# AMENDMENT THIRTEEN
## Target: `WOTR_Scene_Writing_Process_Guide.md` — replaces the standing rule at the end of Section 6

### The Gloss

The guide already says: *cut any follow-up sentence that explains a line's own implication.* That rule has been in the document since the first edition and has not been catching anything, because the failure does not present as a sentence that says "and this meant." It presents as texture. It presents as the narrator being observant.

**Name it. A gloss is any sentence whose only function is to state the significance of the sentence before it.**

### The three disguises

**I. The etymological gloss.** The narrator explains why a character chose the word they chose.

> Lambert said, "Girl."
>
> *He had called her that since she was six winters old, and it was the only permission in the room he had never asked for.*

The italicised sentence is a gloss. The word *Girl*, in a throne room, from a man in that position, is already the entire transaction. Explaining the word's history retracts the word. The reader had it, and then the narrator took it back and handed them a receipt.

**II. The register gloss.** The narrator explains what a tone signified. *She said it flatly, which from her was as close to a refusal as anyone would get.* The flatness carried it. The clause after the comma is the author standing behind the reader making sure.

**III. The stakes gloss.** The narrator restates, in abstract vocabulary, an event the reader has just watched in concrete vocabulary. Usually arrives at paragraph-end. Usually the most quotable sentence in the paragraph, which is exactly why it survives review.

### The deletion test

Delete the candidate sentence. Read the passage without it.

- **The passage means the same thing.** It was a gloss. It stays deleted.
- **The passage genuinely loses the meaning.** The failure is in the line above, not the line below. Rebuild the original so it carries its own freight. Never restore the gloss.

There is no third outcome. If the answer feels like "it's clearer with it," that is the gloss doing what a gloss does, and clarity purchased that way costs the reader the pleasure of arriving.

### Where significance is permitted to live

Three places, and narration is not one of them.

1. **A physical tell adjacent to the line.** Hild's hand on the arm of the chair. Whether it opens or closes.
2. **The other character's reaction.** Somebody in the room hears the word and does something about it with their body.
3. **A callback**, governed by the rule below.

### The Third Recurrence rule

A loaded word gets **one physical response on first use and no comment at all.** Meaning arrives on the **third** appearance, and it arrives through change: someone else uses the word, or the person who always uses it withholds it, or it lands in a register that makes the earlier uses retroactively legible.

**Withholding is the strongest of the three.** The scene where Lambert does not say *Girl* is the scene where the reader learns what it meant, and it requires no narration whatsoever, because the absence does the entire job.

### The carve-out, which matters more than the rule

**Free indirect discourse is not gloss, and this amendment must not be used to strip interiority.**

The test is **whose vocabulary**:

- The sentence is in the **POV character's idiom**, could be **wrong**, and reveals the character doing the interpreting. **Keep it.** That is characterisation. Sodoku mis-reading a room is worth three paragraphs.
- The sentence is in the **narrator's neutral analytic register**, is **authoritative**, and reveals nothing about anybody's interior. **Cut it.** That is the author explaining their own scene.

Worked pair, same beat:

> *He would keep calling her that until one of them was dead, and she would keep letting him, and neither of them had ever decided this.* — **Keep.** Sodoku's judgement, in Sodoku's cadence, and he might be wrong about the second clause.
>
> *The name was a claim of intimacy that the room's formal register made into a challenge.* — **Cut.** Nobody in the world thinks in that sentence.

### Budget

**Zero glosses per scene.** Not rationed. There is no acceptable first instance.

### Worked replacement

A gloss removed leaves a hole, and the hole wants a **reaction**, never a smaller gloss.

> "Girl," he said.
>
> She kept her hands on the map. Six winters, and she still had to read the corners of his mouth before she answered him. The corners gave her nothing. They gave the Kharven envoy nothing either, and the boy who trimmed the lamps, and every man who had ever tried to price him.

### Proposed, pending ruling

**Gloss rights as a character-card field**, sitting beside voice. Lambert yes, since reading a room is his function. Yoko diagnostic only. Cozbi unlimited, because his interiority is the horror. Sodoku never. Emira never, since her whole pathos is that she notices and does not interpret. Say the word and I will add the line to the seventeen-section template and backfill Volume I.

# AMENDMENT FOURTEEN
## Target: `WOTR_AI_Writing_Tells_to_Avoid.md` Section 2, and `WOTR_Manual_Verification_Guide.md` Section 13

### Local Burstiness

Sentence-length CV is a document-level metric and prose is experienced at paragraph level. A scene can post a clean CV of 85% while every emotional beat in it suffocates, because the fragments cluster in the fight and the long chains cluster in the feeling. The script passes it. The reader puts it down.

**Amended: variance is enforced per paragraph. The global CV stays as a floor and stops being sufficient.**

The house register is legalistic, cumulative and clause-heavy. That register is correct for procedure, ritual, ledger work, negotiation and aftermath. It is wrong at impact, and it is arriving there by default because it is the ambient setting.

### The four local rules

**1. The run rule.** No three consecutive sentences within forty percent of each other's word count. Lengths of 22, 19 and 24 constitute a run, and a run is a flat stretch of road however good the individual sentences are.

**2. The chain ceiling.** No more than **two** consecutive sentences over 25 words. The third breaks, or the paragraph does.

**3. The clause cap at the beat.** Within the three sentences bracketing a value turn, no sentence carries more than **one** subordinate or coordinate clause. A reversal delivered inside a chained sentence has been delivered at the same volume as the furniture.

**4. The landing rule.** The emotional hit takes the **shortest sentence in its paragraph** and sits **last**. Anything trailing it is the paragraph clearing its throat after the point.

### The approach ladder

Decelerate into the hit. Long, then medium, then the landing, so the reader's eye is slowing as it arrives and the short sentence reads as a stop instead of one more item. Expand again afterwards: consequence is cumulative in fact and belongs in cumulative syntax, which is the register's proper home.

**Failing** — hit buried mid-chain, paragraph runs past it:

> He had heard the word used about other men in other halls and it had always sounded like an office rather than a person, and the word *father* was a coal in his chest as he listened to Pietro say it, which was a thing he had not expected to feel in front of this many witnesses.

**Corrected** — ladder, landing, stop:

> He had heard the word used about other men, in other halls, and it had always sounded like an office rather than a person. Pietro said it as a person. The word *father* was a coal.

Three sentences: 27, 5, 7. Variance inside the paragraph is enormous, the beat lands on the short one, nothing follows it to soften it.

### Hard ceilings

| Metric | Ceiling |
|---|---|
| Consecutive sentences over 25 words | **2** |
| Words in the payoff sentence | **10** |
| Subordinate clauses in the payoff sentence | **0** |
| Sentences under 8 words, share of scene | **18% floor** |
| Paragraphs closing on three consecutive sentences over 18 words | **0** |

### Conjunction audit

Count coordinators per sentence: *and, as, while, which, because.* Two or more inside a sentence carrying a beat is a fail. Outside a beat it is style and left alone. The tell is never the long sentence by itself; it is the long sentence arriving at the exact moment the reader needed a short one.

**Burstiness is not randomness.** Scattered fragments and scattered chains read as nervous. The unevenness is shaped to the beat, which means knowing where the hit is before the paragraph starts. If the hit turns out to be mid-chain, rebuild the paragraph around it instead of trimming it.

# AMENDMENT FIFTEEN
## Target: `WOTR_Master_Style_Directive.md` — new subsection under the Metaphor and Simile Calibration

### Reification, and its budget

An abstract noun given a physical verb and handled by the characters as an object in the room. *The silence sat between them. He handed her the governance of it. The arithmetic moved across his face. Permission changed hands.*

**The tell is the verb, not the noun.** *The silence went on* is ordinary description. *He handed the silence back to her* is spending budget. Any of *hand, pass, set, carry, place, take, give, hold, put, return, offer, lift, drop, weigh, lay, arrive, land, sit, settle, drain* attached to an abstraction counts as one instance.

**This is not a bad move and it will not be banned.** It is a genuine Hermetic-register instrument, it belongs to the native tongue, and any single instance reads well. That is the problem exactly. The calibration section waves it through because it evaluates instances, and the failure is **density**. Ten good instances in a scene produce prose in which nothing is solid, every noun floats, and the reader is asked to carry weight that has no material anywhere.

### The Licensed Class

The reason the move keeps passing review is that **a closed class of WOTR abstractions genuinely are objects**, and writing them as objects is description rather than device.

A debt is a transferable instrument that can be declared never to have existed. A claim is carried. A verdict is issued and can be rescinded. A name, a standing, a bearing, a holding, a writ, a seal, a title, a warrant, an attribution, a tally: the Guild Accord, *The Bearing and the Holding*, *Value, Coin and Trade* and the Bench of Attribution all treat these as things that move between hands and can be seized, forged, voided or lost.

**Licensed instruments are unbudgeted and free.** The failure is generalising the move outward to abstractions the world does not file, where the reader is watching a stylistic tic imitate a metaphysical fact.

> **The test: could a Guild clerk file it?** If the thing could appear as a line on a ledger, in a writ, or in a Bench finding, reify it freely. If it could not, it costs budget.

### The rules

**1. Two per scene, different abstractions.** The third is a fail regardless of quality. The same abstraction may be reified **once** — silence is handled once or the scene has a tic.

**2. One per paragraph.** Two unlicensed reifications in a single paragraph is the bogged-down feeling on its own, independent of the scene total.

**3. Concrete first.** Before reifying, check whether an object already in the room can carry the same freight. **The object always wins.** Silence does not need to sit between two people if there is a cup between them going cold and neither of them reaching for it.

**4. Never at the beat.** Reification is atmosphere and a poor delivery vehicle for a turn. The value flips on a body or an object, never on a personified noun.

**5. Bodies are exempt.** *His hands shook. Her jaw set. The tail went still.* Concrete nouns doing concrete things, not on this budget.

**6. One per page in procedural or ledger scenes.** The licensed class is already doing this work there, and unlicensed instances go invisible against it.

Worked, same beat, four down to one:

> **Before:** The silence in the hall took a seat. Sodoku felt the weight of the succession settle onto his shoulders, and the arithmetic of it worked across Hild's face while the room's permission drained away.
>
> **After:** Nobody filled the hall. Hild was counting, and her mouth moved slightly on the third name. Somewhere behind Sodoku a boot scraped and stopped, corrected by someone who had not been asked to correct it.

Count is one, the reader has three physical facts instead of four abstractions, and the succession is heavier for never being named.

### Watch-list nouns

Recurring offenders in the current drafts, listed so the check is greppable rather than felt: *silence, weight, arithmetic, governance, permission, distance, authority, refusal, grief, history, patience, the question, the cost, the moment, the space between them.*

# ADDENDUM
## Target: verification pipeline — `wotr_beat_check.py`, checks 15 to 17

`wotr_verify.sh` v2 hunts prohibitions. All three amendments above are **quotas**, which is why every one of them passed a clean run. The checker ships with this pack and is tested against the existing scene archive rather than written and hoped over.

```
bash wotr_verify.sh <draft.md> --scene
python3 wotr_beat_check.py <draft.md>
```

| Check | Enforces | Behaviour |
|---|---|---|
| **15. Gloss watch** | Amendment Thirteen | Flags etymological, register and "which meant" constructions. Warns rather than fails, because the whose-vocabulary test cannot be automated and the FID carve-out is the whole ruling. Every flag gets read. |
| **16. Descent** | Amendment Fourteen | Fails any chain of 3+ sentences over 25 words. Fails 4+ flat runs. Fails a short-sentence share under 10% and warns under 18%. Reports every paragraph closing long-long-long. Arithmetic, so its failures are real. |
| **17. Reification** | Amendment Fifteen | Fails at 3+ unlicensed instances or any paragraph carrying two. Licensed instruments are detected, reported separately, and never counted against budget. |

Check 17 throws occasional false positives on genuinely physical uses (*the weight carried*, of an actual load). Read the flagged line and clear it. **Both scripts run before presenting, failures are fixed, both re-run. No single-pass delivery.**

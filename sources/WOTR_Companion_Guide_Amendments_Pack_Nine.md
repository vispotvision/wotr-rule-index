## PART ONE. THE FOUR CRAFTS, RATIFIED

The four-media structure — **Magicraft, Spellcraft, Runecraft, Draftcraft** — is confirmed canon as the naming scheme for how a working gets performed. Chantcraft stays pending.

**Still open, not decided by this pack, and not to be treated as resolved:** Chantcraft accept/reject/fold, the Law V gate (Stage VII proposed), Law III's rewrite, whether Runecraft outranks Spellcraft everywhere or by culture, the golden-age question, and the scope of the Latinate/vernacular doublet. All flagged in the source document's Part Four docket and still Isaac's to rule.

## PART TWO. CRAFT NAMES ARE NOW SKIN, NOT ONLY SKELETON

The rule barring system vocabulary from prose is **narrowed, not repealed.** It continues to bind absolutely against Wellspring names, Family, Physics Domain, Stage/Grade, EU and every other metaphysical unit, and Codex glyph designations. **It no longer binds against the craft register.**

A POV may be shown cutting a ward, speaking a working, pouring a Draft, singing one (pending Chantcraft's ruling). This isn't a mechanism-explanation carve-out — the Four Crafts document's own register table already makes naming the craft a matter of class and voice, not causality. The Accord Latin (*Ars Operis*, *Ars Vocis*, *Ars Scripti*, *Ars Vasis*) stays document-only. **The common tongue — the work, the speaking, the cutting, the pouring — is now prose-legal,** said the way the character in question would actually say it.

Which word a mouth reaches for is characterisation: an Engraver says *the cutting*. A Kharven soldier wanting help says he needs *a man who can do the writing*. Nobody says "Runecraft" aloud except an Accord examiner filling in a form.

**Untouched by this pack:** the mechanism gloss ban (Check 20), the metaphysical-units ban (Check 21), the chemistry ban, the rule that Effect stays physical. A line may say a man cut a ward into the doorframe. It may not say why the cutting suppressed the Essence.

## PART THREE. THE POWER-DISPLAY VISUAL REGISTER

**Scope, and it is narrow on purpose.** This applies only to Stage-ascension beats, a Pressure drop, and a technique's first display or finisher — the same short list the Ability Guide and Pack Seven already flag for the physical standard (what the body did, what the room did, what the target felt, what it cost). Everywhere else — wounds, environment, objects, ordinary combat, material culture — the Visual Aesthetic Guide's Berserk/Vinland grounded North Star stands untouched.

New register for that narrow list, drawn from the manhwa power-panel reference Isaac supplied:

- **Eyes as the tell.** At a Stage threshold or a dropped suppression, the eyes go first — light behind them rather than in them, overtaking iris and sclera both. Rendered as a fact the room reacts to; the Apparatus Rule still applies, one read and then silence.
- **Aura as fracture, not glow.** Where Essence discharge needs a visual, it reads as broken geometry around the body — jagged, shard-edged light, not a soft radiant bloom. "It glowed" stays banned; this is its replacement specifically for a Stage-display beat, not a general substitute for the phenomenon bank.
- **The body under load.** A Pressure spike or a peak-effort feat is rendered as visible physical distortion — vein, tendon, muscle read exaggerated under strain, ink-dense the way a splash panel goes dense at the one panel that matters. WOTR is licensed exactly one splash-panel beat per scene, at the display, never twice.
- **Silhouette-first entrance.** A practitioner arriving at a Pressure the room hasn't classified yet is staged backlit, shape before detail — the existing silhouette tool in Section 3, now explicitly licensed for a power-arrival rather than rationed to dread generally.

This adds one mode to the palette. It does not replace it. Depth staging, practicals, and the Berserk-density/Vinland-restraint split all still govern everything outside the narrow list above.

**Cost, unchanged.** The ignorance quota, the misreading budget, and the one-read Apparatus Rule still bind a Stage-display beat exactly as hard as any other scene.

# WOTR COMPANION GUIDE AMENDMENTS · PACK SEVEN

## The Soft Magic Amendment

*Paste-in diffs. Precedence: an amendment overrides the base guide where they conflict, and a later pack beats an earlier one. This pack is the latest and therefore governs.*

*Trigger for this pack: the Ability & Technique Design Guide went to a fifth edition and moved off the Sanderson frame. Four other guides still run on it, and two of them mandate on the page exactly what the fifth edition files in the workbook. This closes that gap and adds the instruments the new frame needs.*

---

## PART ONE: WHAT THE FIFTH EDITION ESTABLISHED

Stated once, because five other documents now defer to it.

**Hard world, soft magic.** The world obeys itself absolutely and nobody explains why. A reader needs the cost, the limit, and the rough weight. Never the mechanism.

**The three hard rails, which never bend:** the economy (finite, spent, visible), the ladder (sixteen Stages, felt before seen), the lineage (everything came from somewhere traceable).

**The three soft zones, never explained:** mechanism, catalogue, origin.

**Character first, system second.** Refusal, wound and conviction produce the phenomenon. The Codex files it afterward. The Codex is not a generator.

**Two ledgers.** The page gets Effect, Cost, Limit, Counter, and one line of what nobody knows. The workbook keeps every other field and is never published.

---

## PART TWO: MASTER STYLE DIRECTIVE

### Amendment 7.1, Section 3.1, the Scientific Mechanism Standard

**Repealed as a prose standard. Retained, renamed, as a workbook standard.**

The chain (Trigger, Function, Mechanism, Numerical Effect, Target Response, Consequence, Limitation, Weakness, Cost, Counterplay) is a design instrument and it is a good one. It stops abilities being vague and it forces the counter to exist. Every field survives.

**What is repealed is the instruction to put it on the page at trigger points.** A first display, a major injury and a Domain activation no longer trigger a mechanism disclosure. They trigger a *physical* disclosure.

**Renamed: the Design Chain.** Filled before writing, consulted while writing, published never. The name change is doing real work, because "Scientific Mechanism Standard" reads as an instruction about prose and has been followed as one for six editions.

**What replaces it at trigger points.** At a first display, a major injury, a Domain activation or a finisher, the page gets:

- What the body did.
- What the air, the ground and the objects in the room did.
- What the target felt, in the target's own physical terms.
- What it cost, visibly.

That is the standard. The mechanism stays in the workbook and shows up in prose only where a character is actively performing scholarly diagnosis, filtered through their own shorthand, and probably somewhat wrong.

### Amendment 7.2, Section 3.3, Hard and Soft

**Replaced entirely.** The old formulation split by *scale*: Wellsprings and techniques hard, Titans and Epoch-scale soft. That division was correct for a Sanderson system and it puts every technique in the world on the wrong side of the line.

**The new division splits by kind, not scale.**

**Hard, and it never bends:** cost, limit, relative weight, cause and consequence, the sixteen Stages, the eight Families, the sixty Wellsprings, the Codex grammar, and who is stronger than whom.

**Soft, and never explained:** how any of it works, how much of it exists, and where it came from.

**The surviving line, unchanged and still load-bearing:** the Titans, the Archons, the Crevice, the Withering and anything Epoch-scale generate problems, dread and awe. **They never resolve a plot.**

**The line that replaces the rest:** a character may be rescued by something the reader cannot explain, provided the reader can reason about what it cost.

### Amendment 7.3, Section 3.4, the Fast Prose Rule

**Mostly survives.** Exact numbers still belong at a character-sheet breakdown, a major injury, an armour or structural failure, and a high-stakes finisher.

**Struck from the trigger list:** mechanism reveal, counterplay explanation, training explanation. Those are workbook events and no longer occasions for numbers on the page.

**The corollary stands and is strengthened.** Numbers attach to physical reality only. A metre, a temperature, a mass, a duration. Nothing metaphysical takes a unit, ever.

### Amendment 7.4, Section 5, the Classification Skeleton

**Already correct and reinforced.** The taxonomy is author-facing and surfaces in-world only through a character who is actively being a grammarian of magic.

**Added:** the same restriction now covers the Design Chain by name. A character explaining the Trigger, Mechanism and Counterplay of their own technique is character-sheet vocabulary in dialogue and is cut on sight.

### Amendment 7.5, Section 11, the open item, now closed

The question was whether existing named techniques get retroactive Trigger-to-Counterplay writeups.

**Answer: they get retroactive Design Chains in the workbook, and no page changes at all.** The standard they were being held to no longer applies to prose, so there is nothing to retrofit into the archive. What the archive does need is the cost audit in Part Five.

## PART THREE: COMBAT CRAFT GUIDE

### Amendment 7.6, the trigger-point line

**Struck:** a technique's first display, a major injury or a finisher hits the Scientific Mechanism Standard.

**Replaced:** a technique's first display, a major injury or a finisher hits the **physical** standard. Real units on the body, the armour and the ground. The metaphysical half of the old chain moves to the workbook.

The three-layer hit model survives intact. Physics, Essence and Hermetic correspondence, two visible per meaningful hit, all three at a first display or a finisher. **Clarified:** the Essence and Hermetic layers are rendered as *phenomena*, never as explanations. What the air did, not why it did it.

### Amendment 7.7, Pressure enters the Combat Guide

**New instrument, and it needs a home in the duel guide because it decides fights before they start.**

Presence precedes technique. A stronger practitioner is felt in a room, physically and involuntarily, before anything is cast.

- **One Stage up.** The room's attention bends. People finish sentences shorter.
- **Two.** Sweat, dry mouth, the nausea of standing too close to something large.
- **Three or more.** Involuntary submission. Legs give, weapons leave hands, and the practitioner producing it frequently cannot stop.
- **Band gap.** Animals leave. Local weather changes. Flames behave wrongly.

**Writing rules.** Felt in the body, never described from outside as an aura. The POV misreads it at least once, as fear or the room or the drink. Suppression is a skill, it is tiring, and deliberately dropping it is a threat that requires no dialogue.

**Why this is in the combat guide:** it is how a duel is written between mismatched Stages without a fight happening, and it is how relative power reaches a reader without a number.

### Amendment 7.8, the read

**New, and it follows from the fifth edition's Section 8.**

A duel between competent practitioners is an information problem before it is a physical one. The winner is usually whoever identifies what the other is doing first.

**Taught techniques** are documented, and an opponent who studied the same material arrives with the counter already prepared. Write the recognition: the moment a fighter sees the guard and knows the school.

**Self-derived techniques** exist in one person, have no documented counter, and must be read live, under pressure, in the two or three exchanges before they kill you. Write the reading: what the fighter notices, what they conclude, and, per the misreading budget, at least one conclusion that is wrong.

## PART FOUR: MASS COMBAT AND THE OTHER GUIDES

### Amendment 7.9, Mass Combat Craft Guide

The suspension line survives with the rename. **The Design Chain is suspended in the Close Register.** It requires an analytical observer, and it remains available in a pre-engagement scene, a commander's briefing, or the aftermath.

**Added:** Pressure is the exception. A practitioner's pressure is legible in the press when nothing else is, because it arrives through the body rather than the eye. A line that breaks before contact broke because of something it felt.

### Amendment 7.10, Item and Equipment Writing Guide

**Largely unaffected, and this is worth stating.** Objects are the Tolkien half of the system and the guide was already written that way. Wellspring, Family, Physics Domain and glyph sequence are workbook fields; the self-review already bars system terms from the skin.

**Two edits.**

The tertiary-source note cites Shardblades as a mechanical lesson in hard-magic objects. **Replaced:** what Miura does with the Dragonslayer, an object that became a geological feature through accumulated use, is the lesson. An object's rules matter less than its history.

The inscription **Function** field says physical terms first, system terms second. **Amended:** physical terms only. The system term goes in the workbook line.

### Amendment 7.11, Scene Writing Process Guide

**New pre-write question, added to the canon check.**

Before writing any scene containing a working: **what does the POV understand about what just happened, and what do they merely observe?** The gap is written and left open.

**New self-review check.** No line in the scene explains why a working worked.

### Amendment 7.12, Pack Five and Pack Six, reconciled

Pack Five's bar on narrator explanation of the system to the reader **now agrees with the base guides instead of contradicting them.** It cited Section 3.1 as the permitted delivery route; that route is closed and the bar is absolute.

Pack Six's chemistry ban and Apparatus Rule are **unchanged and strengthened.** Pack Six restricted the Mechanism Standard to technique trigger points. Pack Seven removes the trigger points as well.

## PART FIVE: THE ARCHIVE

Three passes this pack creates, in priority order.

**One. The cost audit.** Working estimate is that sixty percent of existing technique entries fail the test that overuse-strains-him is not a cost. Every failure needs a real category from the wheel: something the character still carries when nobody is fighting. This is the largest and most valuable job on the docket.

**Two. The character-first re-derivation.** For each major character, state what they refuse, what they survived, and what they believe that costs them, then check the phenomenon actually comes out of it. Where it does not, the ability was generated from a Physics Domain and needs rebuilding from the person.

**Three. The naming pass.** Every existing technique name against the one-beat test. Flag failures, do not rename anything without Isaac's ruling, since some of these are established in prose.

---

## PART SIX: VERIFICATION

Two new checks for the Manual Verification Guide and the script.

**Check 20, the mechanism gloss.** Any sentence in prose that explains why a working produced its effect. Detection is imperfect and manual review is required. The physical description of what happened is not a hit. The causal account of why is.

**Check 21, metaphysical units.** Any number attached to a non-physical quantity. Metres, degrees, kilograms, seconds and distances pass. Anything measuring Essence, Coherence, resonance or Wellspring output in prose fails.

---

## OPEN RULINGS

**The release and true-name mechanic** from the fifth edition's Section 6 is pitched and unruled. Taking it means a naming pass per culture and rewritten first displays across the cast.

**The vow clause** remains pitched, unruled, and now more load-bearing, since narrowing is one of the six escalation routes that replace bigger numbers.

**Pressure suppression profiles** need assigning per named character. Who can hide, who cannot, and who stopped bothering. Sodoku and Cozbi are the two that matter most and they should differ.

**The missing archetype.** The practitioner whose weapon is knowing a thousand techniques by name, prepared instead of powerful. The Research and Archives Division is where he lives and WOTR does not have him yet.

**Whether Packs One through Seven get folded into the base guides at last.** Seven unfolded packs is past the point where precedence is trackable by reading, and Pack Seven touches four guides at once.

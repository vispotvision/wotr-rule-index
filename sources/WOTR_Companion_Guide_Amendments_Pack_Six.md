## PART I: OPACITY

### 1. THE LADDER. BUDGET ZERO.

The most frequent structural tell in current WOTR prose, and the one Pack Four's gloss test failed to catch because it climbs across clauses instead of sitting in its own sentence.

**Definition.** A chain of coordinate clauses in which each link converts the previous noun into a more abstract one, ending on a thematic statement.

Documented instances, all from one page:

> *the filing was information and the information was a map he did not want to read*

> *the testing was discipline and the discipline was survival and the survival was habit and the habit was the Yukari thing*

> *the weight was information and the information was grief and the grief was not new*

Every one of these is the narrator digesting an observation on the reader's behalf. The rhythm makes it feel earned. What it actually is: Pack Four's stakes gloss, extended across four rungs and given a cadence so it reads as style.

**The rule.** Two or more clauses of the form *X was Y and Y was Z* in the same sentence is an automatic cut, never a rewrite. Cut back to the first concrete noun and stop there.

> *He filed the smell.*

That is the whole permitted content of the first example. Whether it is a map he does not want to read is the reader's business.

**The related form, also barred.** Restating a concrete observation as an abstraction in the following sentence, even without the *and* chain. *Twenty-three heartbeats where sixty-one should have been.* Stop. Do not add what kind of receiving that is.

**Added to the verification script as check 18.**

### 2. THE APPARATUS RULE

The second structural failure, and the one that produced Isaac's complaint most directly.

**Diagnosis.** Any character with a perceptive faculty has been functioning as a narrator-prosthesis. The Cymorath files chemical signatures, maps ward degradation, counts survivors by heartbeat, separates resonance into component frequencies. The faculty makes total explanation *diegetically legitimate*, which is precisely why it corrupts the prose. The scene stops being a man smelling burned people and becomes a chemistry report with a man attached.

The same disease is available to the Shingan, the Ketsumyōgan, Yoko's nose, and every scholar POV in the setting. It will spread unless it is named.

**The rule, three parts.**

**One reading per scene.** A perceptive faculty may deliver a single explicit read. After that it is silent and the character perceives with a body: eyes, nose, skin, memory, guesswork.

**The read delivers a fact, never an interpretation.** *Twenty-three.* Not *twenty-three, and the Cymorath filed the count with the flat unasked-for fidelity it filed everything.* The faculty is an instrument, and instruments produce numbers, not meaning.

**The faculty is never the grammatical subject.** Barred: *his Cymorath mapped the thinning.* Permitted: *the fourth layer had thinned.* The character perceives. The apparatus does not act on its own behalf.

**Corollary, the chemistry ban.** Real-world scientific vocabulary explaining a sensory impression is out unless the POV is a practitioner of that specific craft actively diagnosing. *Calcium oxide releasing its held breath* is the narrator being a chemist in a scene about a burned village. The Mechanism Standard (Directive Sec. 3.1) governs technique trigger points and nothing else. It was never a licence for ambient exposition, and Pack Six restates that boundary because the drift is documented.

### 3. THE IGNORANCE QUOTA

**Minimum one per scene, mandatory.** The POV notices something and cannot interpret it. The narration does not rescue him. Resolution comes in a later scene, and preferably a later chapter.

This is the engine Pack Five's dramatic irony section requires, and it currently does not exist anywhere in the corpus. In the enclave scene every single perception is fully understood at the moment of perception. That is why the world reads as transmitted rather than inhabited.

Good opacity is **specific and physical**. A wrong smell that never gets named. A door standing open that should not be. A gesture from a stranger that clearly means something locally and is not explained. Bad opacity is vague foreboding.

### 4. THE MISREADING BUDGET

**At least one POV inference per scene is wrong, and is not corrected on the page.**

Competent characters reaching wrong conclusions from good reasoning is the whole Ned Stark instrument. A character who is never wrong is a camera. The misreading must be *reasonable*, must be consistent with what that POV knows, and must be left standing so the reader can find it later.

### 5. WHAT SURVIVES THIS PACK UNCHANGED

The material density stays. Seven of thirty-two structures burned. Hanuri re-spinning thread from an older garment. Bedding planes in limestone. The rust described in full remains house law (Directive Sec. 1.2) and Pack Six removes nothing from it.

**The distinction: describe the world at full density, interpret it at zero.** Objects, weather, injury, wear, labour, all rendered exhaustively. Meaning, significance, thematic weight, all withheld.

## PART II: FURNITURE

### 6. THE DIAGNOSIS

WOTR's recurring textural obsession is its own magic system. When a scene needs density it reaches for the Codex. The result is a world with enormous information and almost no idiom: no signature foods, no greeting, no obscenities, no proverbs, no colloquial reckoning of time, no customs around the dead in prose, no object every household owns.

Identity in fiction is not built from mechanics. Tolkien's fingerprint is roads, food, songs, trees and leaving. Martin's is what is on the table and who sits where. Neither is the magic.

### 7. THE GOVERNING RULE

**Texture comes from the Standing Inventory. Mechanism comes from the Codex. A scene that reaches for the Codex when it needs texture has failed.**

This sentence is the pack's centre and should be carved into the Master Style Directive at Section 1.

### 8. THE STANDING INVENTORY

A per-culture register of non-magical material idiom, built and maintained as canon. Nine required categories:

1. **Food.** Named dishes, staples, what the poor substitute, what the rich display, the order in which people eat.
2. **The greeting.** Words and the gesture that goes with them, plus what a refused greeting means.
3. **Oaths and obscenity.** What people swear *by*, what the worst insult is, what constitutes blasphemy.
4. **Colloquial time.** What a farmhand says instead of a date, as distinct from the formal calendar.
5. **The dead.** What is done with a body, on what timescale, and what the survivors are obliged to do.
6. **The household object.** The one thing every home has, its lifecycle, and what its damage means.
7. **Exchange.** What is given at a birth, how debts are discharged, what counts as theft.
8. **Proverbs.** Six to eight per culture, fixed wording, never paraphrased.
9. **The body.** Posture, hands, what people do with their eyes, the culturally specific tell.

Each entry must be **causally downstream of the culture's material conditions**, per the Craft Research Foundation and the grounded-worldbuilding research substrate. Inventory items invented for flavour without a material cause are rejected.

### 9. THE RECURRENCE RULE

**Identity is repetition. Novelty is its enemy.**

The current failure mode is that every scene invents fresh texture and abandons it. Four references to four different foods across four scenes builds nothing. Four references to the same food builds a world.

Operative:

- **Every scene draws its texture from the Inventory before inventing anything.**
- **Anything newly invented in prose is entered into the Inventory the same session** or it does not exist.
- **The Recurrence Ledger:** each culture designates three to five items as its high-frequency signature, and those recur across the volume until they are load-bearing enough that damaging one is a plot event.
- Invention remains permitted where the Inventory has a genuine gap. It is never permitted as a substitute for reaching for what already exists.

### 10. THE INVENTORY AND THE ELEGIAC MODE

Pack Five's elegiac register (Sec. C.2) requires a named concrete thing that is gone. **The Inventory is where those things live.** A dye colour no longer made, a dish nobody can assemble since the corridor closed, a proverb whose second half has been lost. Elegy without an Inventory is grief for abstractions, which Pack Five already bars.

The two packs are one instrument.

## 11. SCRIPT AMENDMENTS

**Check 18, the Ladder.** Flag any sentence containing two or more instances of `was [a-z]+ and the [a-z]+ was`. Also flag the variants using `is`, `became`, and `meant`.

**Check 19, apparatus subject.** Flag any sentence where a named faculty is the grammatical subject of a perception verb. High false-positive rate by design; review by reading.

Both appended to `wotr_verify.sh` alongside Pack Four's checks 15 through 17.

2. **Retroactive application.** The Cozbi cycle and the enclave scene are the worst offenders in the corpus. Forward-only, or does a Ladder pass get run across the archive? A Ladder pass is mechanically cheap since check 18 finds them.
3. **Inventory build order.** Kharven and the Moto are drafted. Proposed sequence after: Stannvaard, the Dawi, Ketsuen, Nalūn, the Yukari enclave.
4. **Whether the Inventory is a Notion wiki section or a standing markdown guide.** Proposed: Notion, one page per culture under a new Standing Inventory section page, since it will be queried mid-scene.

Until item 1 is ruled on, Natalie writes to the proposed answer and flags any passage where the ceiling visibly bites.

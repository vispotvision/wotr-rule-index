# AMENDMENT SEVEN

### The Fourth Grammar

Pack One established three combat vocabularies: Blade, Verdict, Percussion. Cozbi Mahuo was explicitly deferred rather than forced into one of them.

Running him properly against the framework shows why. **He does not fit, and canon states the reason in his own vulnerability line.**

Cozbi's own body, absent constructs or conscripted force, sits below his soul-architecture ceiling. A direct physical contest against a comparable presence goes badly, and this is documented as proven rather than theorised. The true Cozbi sits three planes distant from whatever vessel is operating. His Domain is not a place he maintains, it is a condition his existence imposes.

He is therefore not a fighter who has a poor grammar. **He is a fighter whose entire proposition is that he is not the thing you are hitting.**

### IV. Expenditure Grammar

Blade grammar hunts openings. Verdict grammar reads and concludes. Percussion grammar hunts fatigue.

**Expenditure grammar hunts cost.** The practitioner does not seek to defeat the opponent. They seek to make the exchange asymmetric, so that everything the opponent does is expensive and everything they themselves do is spent from somewhere else. Victory is arithmetic and arrives late.

*Assign to:* Cozbi, Legion summoners, construct-users, anyone operating through a conscripted lattice or a projected vessel, and any fighter whose combat proposition is that the target of the blow is not the source of the working.

| Move | Description | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| **The Offering** | Something expendable is put where the opponent must deal with it | **Converts the opponent's capability into expenditure.** The construct is not expected to win. It is expected to cost. |
| **The Standing** | The practitioner does nothing at all for a period the opponent finds intolerable | **Converts time into advantage.** Only available to a fighter who does not experience time as a resource, which is exactly Cozbi's documented strength and why almost nobody else can use this. |
| **The Ledger** | The accumulated cost is called in at once | **Converts the whole preceding engagement into a single effect.** The Pneuma Unravel Strike is this move at full commitment and is un-repeatable per encounter for that reason. |
| **The Absence** | The vessel is destroyed and the practitioner is unaffected | **Converts a victory into nothing.** The opponent has won and gained no ground, and discovering this is the psychological centre of fighting anyone in this grammar. |

**Expenditure answers to plate:** irrelevant. Plate is a cost problem rather than a defence problem, and an expenditure fighter is content to let armour do its job for as long as the wearer can afford to keep wearing it.

**Expenditure tell:** the fighter is not looking at the opponent. In prose, have them looking at something adjacent, or crouching to examine something on the ground, which is Cozbi's documented posture and reads as either serene or obscene depending on what the reader already thinks of him.

**Counterplay, per canon:** reach through the projection to the claims it carries, which has been done once and required a Rescind-type working. Or force a direct physical contest, which has also been done once and worked, because his own frame is genuinely below his ceiling. Both are documented. Neither is easy and both are available.

### Cozbi Mahuo, assigned

| Field | Assignment |
|---|---|
| **Grammar** | Expenditure |
| **Signature** | The Standing into the Ledger. He will do nothing for as long as it takes and this is not a bluff. |
| **Answer to plate** | Indifference. He is not trying to get through it. |
| **Tell** | Crouches to examine, never postures to threaten. Stress default is speed, so a Cozbi who has begun talking faster than his usual cadence has been genuinely alarmed and the reader should notice before the other characters do. |
| **Status** | Canonical. Transcribed from the character card and the combat material, not invented. |

**Craft note for writing him:** an Expenditure fighter is boring to write badly and terrifying to write correctly. The temptation is to give him a spectacular exchange. Refuse it. The scene should feel like the opponent is winning, repeatedly, at increasing cost, until the arithmetic arrives. Everything before the Ledger is the opponent succeeding.

### 12. Naval and Riverine

**The sensory hierarchy inverts.** Section 1's order (sound, pressure, smell, sight) was built for a man in a press. At sea it runs sight, motion, sound, smell, and the reason is that a ship gives you a horizon and takes away your footing.

- **Sight extends and becomes reliable.** A sailor can see the whole engagement, which is the exact opposite of the infantryman's condition, and it changes the POV problem completely. The Field Register is available continuously and must therefore be rationed by a different instrument: not what the character can see, but what they can do about it. Write the helplessness of watching rather than the confusion of not seeing.
- **Motion is the second channel and has no land equivalent.** The deck under a body that cannot plant. Every physical action costs balance. Fighting on a moving surface is closer to fighting drunk than to fighting on a slope and should be written that way.
- **Sound carries differently.** Orders travel further and arrive clearer over water, which means naval command is genuinely better than land command and naval engagements are consequently more decisive and less confused.
- **The rout has nowhere to go.** Section 6's central rule does not apply. A broken crew cannot run, so naval casualties concentrate in the fighting and in the sinking rather than in a pursuit. This is the single largest structural difference and it makes sea actions bloodier per engagement and rarer.
- **Fire and water are the two deaths.** Not the blade. Write accordingly.

### 13. Siege

**The problem is that investment is months of tedium punctuated by terror, and no register handles tedium.**

- **Use the Line Register as the default**, not the Close. A siege is experienced as a unit condition rather than as personal action: the state of the camp, the state of the food, the state of the flux, whether the digging advanced today. Weeks pass in the Line Register at a paragraph each and this is correct.
- **The Close Register is reserved for the three things that break the tedium:** a sortie, a mine breaking through, and the storm. Each should arrive without warning and end faster than the reader wants.
- **Tedium is written by specificity, not by length.** One precisely observed repeated detail carries a month. The same gap in the wall, seen from the same place, on a different day, with something small changed.
- **The disease is the antagonist.** Per *Walls and What Beats Them*, the besieging camp gets the same sickness as the besieged at the same time and frequently loses more. The enemy in a siege scene is the ditch, and the garrison is scenery.
- **The parley is the scene.** Everything a siege is about happens in the negotiation, because both commanders know what a storm means. Write the parley as the climax and the storm, if it comes, as the failure of the actual drama.

### 14. Cavalry

**A rider's information environment is entirely different and better, and this changes what the POV is for.**

- **The rider sees, and the rider moves.** They have both things the infantryman lacks, and the consequence is that a cavalry POV is the only ordinary soldier who can carry the Field Register honestly. Use them for orientation when you need it and be aware you are paying in intimacy.
- **The horse is a character with opinions.** It is frightened, it is tiring, it can smell blood, it will not go where you point it if what you are pointing it at is a hedge of spears. Every good cavalry scene in the literature is partly about negotiating with an animal.
- **A charge is three seconds of decision and forty of consequence.** The decision is whether the enemy line will hold, made at distance, by both sides simultaneously. If it holds, the charge fails before contact. If it breaks, there is no contact at all, only pursuit.
- **The pursuit is the cavalry's real function and it is not combat.** Per section 6, it is butchery of the unresisting from behind at a canter for as long as the horses last. Writing a cavalry POV means writing the person who does that work, and the honest version does not let them enjoy it and does not let them refuse it either.

### 15. Practitioner POV in a Mass Action

The guide's own open gaps flagged this and my instinct was that the POV should be given rarely. The ruling is narrower than that and more useful.

**A high-Stage practitioner inside a battle experiences none of the constraints this guide is built on.** They can see. They are not in the press. They know what is happening. Handing the reader that POV during an engagement dissolves the fog, the pressure and the helplessness that every other section exists to construct.

**Ruling: the practitioner POV is available before and after an engagement and is closed during it.**

- **Before** is where it belongs. The costing-out arithmetic, the decision about what to spend and on whom, the knowledge that the levy in front of them exists to be expensive. That is the most damning interiority available in the setting and it is wasted if it appears while they are fighting.
- **After** is the second place. What a practitioner looks at on a field they made. Whether they can name anyone.
- **During**, use the soldier. If the practitioner must appear, they appear as weather: something the POV soldier experiences and does not understand, arriving from outside the four-foot wedge.

**The single exception:** a practitioner who has been costed out. The moment their magazine empties they inherit every constraint in section 1 at once, and a POV that begins with a person who can see the whole battle and ends with a person who can see twelve feet is the strongest use of the perspective available. Spend it once.

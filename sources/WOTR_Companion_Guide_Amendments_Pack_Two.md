# AMENDMENT FIVE
## Target: `WOTR_Scene_Writing_Process_Guide.md` — new section, follows the battle carve-out

### Interior Action at Scale

The standing requirement is that every set piece shifts character dynamics rather than only plot state. Pack One deferred this to the aftermath and supplied no method. This is the method.

**The problem.** A duel does interior work continuously, because two people are reacting to each other and the reader can hold both. A battle cannot. The POV is overloaded, the cast is dispersed, and nobody has the attention to spare for a relationship. Writing a battle that tries to advance six relationships produces a battle in which nothing happens twice.

**The rule: a battle advances exactly one relationship, and it advances it by a single unrepeatable act.**

### The Single Act

Choose one act, performed by one character, witnessed by one other, that could not have been performed anywhere except inside that engagement.

It must satisfy four conditions:

1. **It is physical.** Not a decision, not a realisation, not a look held a beat too long. A thing done with the body under load.
2. **It is unrepeatable.** The circumstance that permitted it does not recur, so the act cannot be walked back, re-explained or matched later.
3. **It is witnessed but not discussed.** The witness sees it and neither party speaks of it during the engagement, because there is no time and no breath.
4. **It is ambiguous at the moment of occurrence.** The witness does not know what it meant. Neither does the reader. Neither, frequently, does the actor.

Everything else in the battle is exterior. The Single Act is the only interior freight the engagement carries, and it is carried without commentary.

### Where it resolves

**In the aftermath, and not by discussion of the act itself.**

The relationship has changed and both parties behave differently. Neither raises it. The scene three days later is about a bucket, or a burial detail, or who takes which watch, and the change is legible in how they handle the bucket. If a character says what the act meant, the amendment has been violated and the scene has been spent.

### Worked shape

- **Inside the engagement:** A holds the line two seconds past the point where holding it was survivable, because B is still getting up. B sees it. Neither says anything. Nothing further happens between them for the rest of the sequence.
- **Aftermath, day three:** B hands A a full canteen without being asked and walks off. A drinks it. That is the entire resolution and it is sufficient.
- **What is banned:** any version in which either of them explains, thanks, refuses thanks, or names the debt. Characters never narrate the meaning of what they did.

### The count

**One Single Act per engagement.** Two dilutes both. Three is a soap opera in armour.

A campaign arc may run several engagements and therefore several Acts, and they should not all involve the same pairing, because the reader will begin to expect the shape and expectation is what kills it.

### Diagnostic

If you can remove the battle and preserve the relationship change, the Act was not unrepeatable and the battle was decoration. Rebuild the Act around something the engagement made possible and nothing else could.

### Standing Rulings from the War Cycle

These were established in *The Practitioner Problem* and belong at the desk rather than in a lore document. They govern the design of any technique intended to appear in an engagement involving formations.

### 1. The currency rule

**A practitioner converts Essence into effect. An army converts time into ground.** These do not exchange.

*Design consequence:* no technique holds ground. A technique may take, deny, break or unmake ground and it may not occupy it. Any technique whose stated function is holding a position for a duration has been designed wrongly and should be rebuilt as a denial effect with a Sustain.

### 2. The magazine rule

Essence is finite per practitioner per day, replenishment is slower than expenditure under load, and the practitioner always knows what they have left.

*Design consequence:* every technique's Cost field must be legible to the character using it. A practitioner never discovers mid-technique that they are empty. They knew three techniques ago and were spending anyway, and that knowledge is a characterisation instrument rather than a mechanical one.

### 3. The replenishment rule

**A practitioner is refilled by a Wellspring, or not at all.** Essence cannot be shipped: it leaks in transit, cannot be assayed without an instrument that does not travel, and dies with its owner.

*Design consequence:* no consumable restores a practitioner's magazine. Any technique, item or draft proposed to do so is refused at design. This is the hardest rule in the set and it is what makes the Cost field mean anything.

### 4. Domains break, they do not kill

A seated Domain does not kill a formation. It corrodes cohesion, the line breaks, and the killing happens in the rout.

*Design consequence:* assess any Domain-class technique by **Re-form** rather than by casualties. A Domain that kills sixty and does not break the line has failed. Write and design accordingly.

### 5. Voidic is the counter

Aether Class VI devours where others emit. A Voidic practitioner is a poor attacker and the finest suppression instrument in the world, and those are the same fact.

*Design consequence:* Voidic techniques are priced as artillery. Slow to bring, slow to move, cannot defend themselves, require a guard detachment, and affect friendly output as readily as hostile. A Voidic technique with a friendly-side exemption has been designed wrongly. Every attempt to build one has failed and that failure is canon.

### 6. The speed ruling

High-Grade couriers give functional real-time command across a theatre. Speed is Essence, couriers are interdicted first, and formations trained on instantaneous command collapse harder when they lose it than formations that never had it.

*Design consequence:* movement techniques are priced as expenditure, not as mobility. A character who arrives fast arrives depleted. There is no free travel at Grade and a scene that has one has spent a resource silently.

### 7. Frontage, Sustain, Re-form

Restated from Pack One because it belongs in this guide as well as in the Style Directive. Any technique deployed against a formation carries all three fields in addition to the standard seven. A technique that cannot be given all three does not belong on a battlefield.

**Counterplay to any scaled technique is always one of three things:** exceed its Frontage, outlast its Sustain, or shorten its Re-form.

## II. THE INNER WORLD BASELINE

Five strata, mixed on one map. A reader should hear several inside a single scene and never wonder which world they are in.

**Korean.** Mahuo institutional logic. Lineage name first, two-syllable given name carrying a generational syllable shared across a cohort, plus the hollow-seat that subdivides the lineage. *Kwon Mu-jin. Cozbi Mahuo. Ara Min Mahuo.*

**Japonic.** Archaic Bloodline institutional logic. Full life-stage slots, name-taboo on the living, compound topographic surnames, renaming at promotion or vow or change of allegiance. *Sodoku Moto. Ayame Yuno. Niran. Jinmu Yukari.*

**Chinese.** Lineage-hall institutional logic. One-syllable surname, a generation character drawn from a poem fixed centuries in advance, a courtesy name taken at capping that becomes the name equals adult use, and an optional studio name for scholars. *Illustrative only: Shen Ruiyan, courtesy Zhaoheng.*

**Far-Northern.** Carried-name logic. No inherited surname at all. A name is a person, held by the dead and given on to the living, and kinship terms follow the name instead of the blood. *Illustrative only: Amaqjuaq, called Ice on the roll.*

**Northern.** English and Norse. A small given-name pool producing constant disambiguation by trade, a frozen surname if the family is chartered and a live patronymic if it is not, and an earned byname available to anyone. *Bram Greymane. Edward Lambert. Hild Ice. Tabitha Hallenfeld. Josse Hillborn. Rannvei Foss.*

---

## III. THE RULE THAT DECIDES WHICH

**Stratum follows institution, never geography and never blood.**

The Japonic and Korean registers belong to the **archaic bloodlines** — houses whose naming survived intact from before the Concord because their naming *is* their continuity. Moto, Yukari, Zettai, Yuno, Mahuo. A name in that register is a claim of unbroken descent, and the claim is the point.

The Chinese register belongs to the houses whose continuity is a **record** rather than a bloodline or a charter: the scholar-physician lineages, the Measurewright families, the alchemical houses, and the great trading lineages of the Inner World. Their answer to the question is *we wrote it down ourselves, in a book, and the book is the house.*

The Far-Northern register belongs to the tundra peoples, the sealers, the timber towns, the Keld who withdrew north, and most of the Kharven commons. Their answer to *who wrote your name down* is the one nobody else can give: **the dead did, and nobody wrote it anywhere.**

The Northern register belongs to everyone whose name went through the Accord's filing system: **Banner Houses, chartered families, guild lines, the muster, the commons.** Greymane, Lambert, Stark, Hallenfeld, Ashmore, Thornwall, Keld, Hillborn, Foss, Ice.

So these are not five peoples. They are five answers to the question *who wrote your name down*, and at least four of them are in the room at Kharven-Seat every day. A Moto king takes counsel from a Lambert. A Greymane holds the ridge for a bloodline that predates his charter by eleven hundred years and has been in the north four generations. That friction is free and it is already on the page.

**This retcons nothing.** Every existing name in the Inner World already obeys it.

---

## IV. CONTACT

**Mixed parentage takes one register, and the choice is a statement.** Hild Ice is the model: a Moto father, a Stark mother, and a maternal Northern name carried openly. Nobody has to explain what that means to anyone.

**Service can transfer a register.** A retainer raised inside a bloodline house may be given a house-register name, and the giving is an event with a cost attached.

**Renaming at change of allegiance is Japonic-only.** It does not travel into the Northern stratum, where a man who changes sides keeps his name and simply becomes a man with that name who changed sides. This is why the Greymane split has no naming event in it and the Moto exile does.

---

## V. OUTLIERS

Permitted, deliberately, and rationed.

A name is an outlier when it fits no stratum: a foreign fostering, a mother's whim, a name bought off a dying man, a bloodline child given a Northern name as an insult or a shield. **Bram** is the standing example and needs no justification on the page.

**Budget: roughly one outlier in eight named characters, and each one has a reason that exists in the workbook whether or not it ever reaches prose.** Above that ratio the registers stop reading as systems and start reading as an author picking names he liked.

## VIII. ONE THING DECOUPLED FROM THIS

**The Kharven Standing Inventory is material culture, not naming register.** Airag, borts, aaruul, the deel, the night-stone, the blubber lamp, the woodpile count, the death-house and the Waiting, sky burial, and the eight proverbs. All of it is downstream of permafrost and scarcity rather than of anybody's language, and it is load-bearing in four scenes written this session.

Your own standing principle is that naming register is not ethnicity. That principle cuts both ways: striking the Mongolian *names* does not require striking the cold-survival *culture*. **Recommendation: keep it, and the Far-Northern register settles it.** The Kharven Inventory always had two inheritances layered on each other: Mongolian grassland and Inuit cold-survival. The grassland half goes with the Mongolian names. The cold-survival half stays and now has a naming stratum standing under it, which is what it was missing.

Consequent trims to the Inventory: **airag** and the insult attached to refusing it, **borts**, **aaruul**, the **deel**, and **Tengri** are steppe items and should go or be renamed. Everything else survives untouched: the woodpile and *how's your stack*, the night-stone, *wet wood*, the Thin Weeks, the death-house and the Waiting, sky burial, seal fat and blubber lamps, the permafrost root cache, meat-sharing with the hunter's household eating last, labour-debt, breath-plume as the emotional read, and all eight proverbs.

## X. THE CHINESE STRATUM, IN FULL

*Added by amendment. Takes its place in sections II and III above.*

### Slots

**Surname.** One syllable, effectively always. This alone separates it on sight from the Korean stratum, where the lineage name arrives with a hollow-seat behind it.

**Generation character.** The load-bearing element. A lineage hall fixes a poem of twenty or thirty characters generations in advance, and every child of a given generation takes the next character in sequence as one half of the given name. The consequence is the gift: **hearing one syllable tells you a stranger's generation, and therefore whether he outranks his own uncle.** Two men who have never met can establish seniority in the time it takes to exchange names, and they do it without either of them saying anything about it.

**Given character.** The individual half. Chosen by the family, and the only part of the name that is actually about the person.

**Courtesy name.** Taken at capping, and this is what adults use. The birth given name becomes the property of parents, teachers and the very senior; anyone else using it is either a superior or being deliberately offensive. A stranger who has your courtesy name has been introduced. A stranger who has your given name has read your file.

**Studio name.** Optional, self-chosen, and a statement. Scholars, physicians and alchemists take one. It is the only name in any Inner World register that a person awards themselves, which is exactly why the other houses find the practice slightly ridiculous and slightly enviable.

**Posthumous name.** Conferred by the hall, recorded in the book, and occasionally an insult.

### Telling it apart from the Korean stratum on the page

Both are lineage-first with a generational element, so the distinction has to be legible without a footnote.

- **Hyphen means Mahuo.** *Kwon Mu-jin.* No hyphen means the lineage halls.
- **A hollow-seat means Mahuo.** Two Mahuo who share a surname and not a seat are not kin. The halls do not use seats; they use the book.
- **A courtesy name means the halls.** No Mahuo character has one, and none should acquire one.
- **A two-syllable surname means neither.** That is Japonic.

### What it buys

The Inner World now has a naming tradition whose continuity is **documentary**, sitting beside one whose continuity is **liturgical** (Japonic name-taboo), one whose continuity is **genealogical** (Mahuo lineage), and one whose continuity is **administrative** (the Accord's filing). Four institutions with four theories of what makes a name real, all of them operating in the same market town.

That is a supply of arguments that never runs out, and it costs nothing to write, because every one of them is already true of the houses as they stand.

### Phonotactics

Open syllables, permitted nasal codas, no clusters. Avoid the wuxia register the base guide already warns off: no four-syllable given names, no sect-title constructions, no honorific stacking. The register should read as a bureaucracy with a very long memory, which is what it is.

### Contact

A hall child fostered into a Banner House keeps the surname and drops the generation character, and the hall records the omission. That is a small, quiet, permanent thing to do to somebody, and it is available whenever you want it.

## XI. THE FAR-NORTHERN STRATUM, IN FULL

*Added by amendment. Takes its place in sections II and III above. Structure only: the mechanism below is drawn from a living tradition and every name, term and rite in WOTR built on it is original. No real personal names, sacred terms or ceremonial vocabulary are imported, exactly as the Elven register handles its own anchor.*

### The mechanism

**The carried name.** A name is not a label attached to a person. It is a person, and it survives them. When someone dies the name is held, unspoken, until it is given on to a child, and the child carries it. Not in memory and not in tribute. The name is the same name and the people who loved it treat the child accordingly.

**The kin-turn.** Kinship terms follow the name instead of the blood. A woman whose mother's name is given to her newborn daughter calls that infant *mother*, and means it, and the whole settlement uses the term with her. A man can be uncle to a child three times his age. Nobody finds this confusing and everybody outside the north does.

**Names are not gendered.** A boy may carry a woman's name and the kin-turn goes with it unchanged.

**A person may carry several.** Three or four names from three or four dead is ordinary, and each one brings its own kin-turn, which means a single child can hold four different relationships to the same household.

**Name-avoidance.** The name of the recently dead is not spoken until it has been given on. This interlocks with the Standing Inventory at no cost: **the Waiting is the silence.** The body waits in the death-house because the ground is frozen, and the name waits with it, and it is unsayable for exactly as long as the family sits. A Kharven household that has not finished the Waiting is a household with a hole in its vocabulary, and everyone in the room is steering around it.

**No inherited surname.** There never was one.

### Where the surnames came from, and what that is worth

The Accord's registers cannot file a person who has no fixed name. So the filing system assigned them: a frozen surname taken off the nearest legible thing, a trade, a landmark, a factor's guess at a sound, handed down in a ledger to people who had never used one and mostly still do not among themselves.

**Ice is one of those.** So is Foss. A Far-Northern character therefore has a roll-name that the Accord, the muster and the tax survey use, and a carried name that the household uses, and the gap between the two is the whole political history of the north in two words.

A Concord clerk reading a name aloud off a page and getting the wrong person entirely is a scene that is now available and costs nothing to set up.

### Phonotactics, for coining

Three vowels, **a i u**, with phonemic length. Uvulars are the signature: **q** where another register would take k. Velar and uvular fricatives, geminate consonants across a syllable boundary, no onset clusters. Heavily suffixing, so a name can carry three or four morphemes and still be one word, and the morphemes should mean something rather than sounding good. Stress light and late.

Bynames are descriptive, freely coined, and stick. They attach to the person and not to the carried name, which is the one thing in the system that is genuinely and only about the individual.

### Contact

**A carried name does not transfer out.** A Far-Northern child fostered south keeps it and simply stops hearing it used, and the kin-turns lapse, and that lapse is one of the quieter griefs available in the setting.

**Giving a carried name to an outsider is possible and enormous.** It makes the outsider a specific dead person, with all the kin-turns that follow, and the household is bound by every one of them. It is not an honour so much as an adoption performed on the entire family without their individual consent.

### What it buys

Five registers, five theories of what makes a name real: liturgical, genealogical, documentary, administrative, and now **inherited from the dead**. The Far-Northern one is the only theory in the Inner World that treats a name as a living thing with its own continuity, and it is held by the poorest and most numerous people in Kharven, and the Accord cannot file it.

That is a grievance with no villain in it, which is the best kind this setting has.

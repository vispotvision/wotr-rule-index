# WOTR Racial Voice and Dialect Guide — Amendment
### Closes docket item 20. Three registers that were flagged as gaps and have never been built.

*Follows the guide's own method: differentiation through syntax, clause structure, idiom source and formality rather than eye-dialect. Maximum two to three consistent phonetic markers per culture. One or two signature turns of phrase. A stated stress-tell. Dialect never equals unintelligence.*

---

# I. FLESHSHAPER GOBLIN

### The problem this register solves

Goblinoid registers in the existing guide cover the Expanse broadly. The Fleshshapers are a craft caste inside it and they do not sound like their neighbours, because their entire vocabulary was built by people who work with living tissue for a living and who had to develop precise language for it before anyone else in the four quarters bothered.

**The governing principle: they are surgeons, and they talk like surgeons who have never been told to be squeamish about it.**

### Syntax and clause structure

Short declarative clauses in working order. They describe procedure the way a competent tradesman describes procedure: sequence first, reason second or not at all. Compound sentences are rare and always join a step to its consequence.

They front the object. *The arm, I open here.* This is the single most identifiable structural feature and it comes from a working language where the thing being worked on is named before the working.

### Idiom source

**Anatomy, applied to everything.** Not as metaphor and not as a shock device. It is simply the vocabulary they have, extended outward the way a sailor extends rigging or a smith extends heat.

- A political situation has a *seam*, and seams are where you go in.
- A liar is *badly sutured*.
- Grief is *swelling*, and swelling goes down or it does not.
- To understand something is to have *opened* it.
- Somebody trustworthy *holds under load*.

The register's power comes from its total absence of squeamishness paired with total absence of cruelty. A Fleshshaper describing what they are about to do to a person is not threatening them. They are being courteous, and the reader's discomfort is entirely the reader's.

### Phonetic markers (two, consistently)

1. **Dropped final -g on gerunds** — *openin, holdin, closin.* Universal in the caste and does not vary with education.
2. **"Th" hardens to "d" in unstressed positions only** — *dis, dat, dere* when the word is doing grammatical work; *this* and *that* survive when the word carries stress. The inconsistency is the marker and it is consistent.

Nothing else. Fleshshaper speech is otherwise clear and precise, because imprecision in their work kills people.

### Signature turns

- **"That closes."** Said of anything settled: a wound, an argument, a debt, a life. Their all-purpose full stop.
- **"Who holds it?"** Their first question about any structure, organisation, alliance or plan. They do not ask who leads. They ask what is bearing the weight.

### Stress-tell

Under stress they become **more procedural, not less.** The sentences shorten to pure sequence and the reasons drop out entirely. A Fleshshaper reciting steps in a flat order is a Fleshshaper who is frightened, and their own people read this instantly and outsiders never do.

# II. WINTER ELADRIN

### The problem this register solves

The guide flagged a non-verbal convention and did not build one. The difficulty is real: a culture whose primary communicative channel is not speech cannot be written with a dialect, and writing them as silent produces either mystique or furniture.

**The governing principle: they speak. The convention is not silence. It is that speech is the least of what they are doing, and the prose has to carry the rest.**

### The convention

Winter Eladrin communication runs on three channels simultaneously. Prose must render at least two in any exchange of consequence.

1. **Speech.** Sparse, grammatically complete, and almost never carrying the actual content. What is said is the frame.
2. **Distance.** Where they stand relative to you, and how that changes during the conversation. Approach is agreement. Half a step back is a serious objection. Turning the body without moving the feet is refusal. This is a full grammar and it is deliberate, and Eladrin read it in each other with total fluency.
3. **Stillness.** How much of them is moving. Complete stillness is not calm and is frequently the loudest thing in the room.

### The prose rule

**Never write an Eladrin line of dialogue without a distance or stillness beat attached.** A bare Eladrin line is an incomplete sentence in their language, and the reader should be able to follow an entire Eladrin conversation with the dialogue removed.

*Bad:* "I disagree," she said.
*Correct:* "I understand your position." She had not moved, and the half-step she did not take was the answer.

### Syntax

Formal, complete, and slightly archaic through the retention of full forms: no contractions, ever, and this is the one absolute in the register. Present tense preferred for permanent conditions. Questions phrased as statements with an expectant pause rather than with rising inflection.

They do not interrupt and they do not overlap. An Eladrin waits, visibly, for a full stop, and the waiting is itself commentary.

### Idiom source

**Weather, season and the behaviour of cold.** Never as decoration and always as the actual analytical vocabulary.

- A relationship is *thawing* or it is *set*.
- A grudge *keeps*, in the preserving sense, and this is a compliment to the grudge.
- A person who acts hastily has *melted*.
- Something obvious is *tracked*, as prints are tracked in snow.

### Phonetic markers

**None.** This is deliberate and is the register's structural point. Winter Eladrin speech in the common tongue is unaccented and grammatically perfect, which reads to outsiders as coldness and is in fact enormous effort. They are being courteous in a language that is not theirs and nobody notices.

### Signature turns

- **"It keeps."** Said of anything that does not need resolving now. The most misread phrase in the four quarters.
- **"You have moved."** Said to someone who has changed position in an argument. Frequently said to people who have not physically moved at all, and the ambiguity is intentional on the speaker's part.

### Stress-tell

Under stress the **stillness increases and the distance grammar stops.** An Eladrin who has stopped adjusting their position has withdrawn from the conversation entirely and is now only present in body, and this is the single most alarming thing they can do to another Eladrin. Outsiders read it as composure.

# III. THE CELESTIAL HOST

### The problem this register solves

The Host is the order's originary referent and has never had a voice. The failure mode is obvious and universal: they end up sounding like scripture, which is a voice nobody can sustain across a scene and which makes every Host character identical.

**The governing principle: they are not speaking scripture. They are reporting, accurately, and their difficulty is that accuracy in their register does not translate into a mortal one.**

### Syntax

**Declarative, present tense, and unhedged.** The Host does not say *I think*, *perhaps*, *it seems* or *in my view*, because those constructions describe a relationship between a speaker and a claim, and the Host does not have that relationship. They state.

Sentences are short and the structure is flat: subject, verb, object. Subordinate clauses are rare. When one appears it is because two things are genuinely conditional on each other and the Host is being careful.

They answer the question asked. Precisely the question asked. Not the question intended, not the question underneath it, and the gap between those is where every conversation with the Host goes wrong.

### The core device

**They cannot be imprecise, and they know their precision is useless.**

A Host asked how long something will take gives an answer that is correct and unusable. Asked whether an action is right, they answer a narrower question than the one asked and will say so. The register's whole tension is a speaker who is never wrong and is frequently no help, and who is aware of this and cannot do anything about it.

This is what stops them sounding like scripture. Scripture is confident. The Host is accurate, which is a different and lonelier thing.

### Idiom source

**Measure, position, and law.** Distance, weight, order, precedence, standing. Never light, never fire, never wings. The obvious celestial vocabulary is banned in this register precisely because it is obvious.

- A wrong action is *out of position*.
- A person's worth is their *standing*, in the legal sense, not the moral one.
- Something impossible *has no place*.
- To forgive is to *release a claim*.

### Phonetic markers

**None, and no contractions.** Same structural reason as the Eladrin and a different effect: the Eladrin are making an effort, and the Host has simply never learned that speech is supposed to be comfortable.

### Signature turns

- **"That is not the question."** Their most common line and the source of most of the friction they generate. Never said dismissively. It is a genuine correction offered as a courtesy.
- **"I do not have that."** Said when they lack information, and said in exactly the tone they use for everything else, which mortals find unnerving because they expect an admission of ignorance to be inflected as one.

### Stress-tell

Under stress the Host becomes **more precise and slower.** Sentences shorten further, qualifications appear for the first time, and they begin specifying the boundaries of their own claims. A Host saying *within the terms I can observe* is a Host who is badly frightened, and this is the only hedging that ever appears in the register.

### What breaks the register

Any Host line that could appear on a temple wall has failed. If it scans as a maxim, cut it and replace it with a report.

# NOTES

- All three are originations and none contradicts existing lore, because no lore existed. If any of these cultures has established speech in a document I did not reach, these are overridden by it and I would rather be told than have it discovered in prose.
- The Host register is the one I would test first, because "accurate but useless" is easy to state and hard to sustain, and a scene will reveal in two exchanges whether it holds.
- The Eladrin distance grammar is the most demanding on the prose and the most rewarding. It requires a beat attached to every line, which will feel heavy for the first page and then stop being noticeable.

# A Reader's Codex

This codex began as an apology. At the autumn cloth fair in Tessbury, Tibalt Wenlow, a saffron factor (a merchant's agent, buying and selling on another's account) up from the warm southern marches, rose at the Mercers' election feast with six hundred guests under a pavilion hung with ribbon, lifted his cup to the new Warden's aunt, read the silver token at her throat as best he could, and toasted her as the finest Apprentice he had ever had the honor to meet. Dame Ysolt Varley was a Master and an elder of the Vermilion Bell, a school whose fight songs carry two streets from any bracket in the cloth towns, and she had been sitting through speeches for some hours. The coat of aura she wore went from a banked ember orange to the white-gold at the heart of a dye-furnace before he had finished the word. Every ribbon on the pavilion caught. Forty bolts of Wenlow's own saffron silk, hung overhead as his gift, went up in one sheet of yellow fire that they saw from the next valley, and the pipers, who had been paid for the whole evening, played on. The new Warden took his oath at the horse trough with his hair singed. Nobody died, which Dame Ysolt called a courtesy. Wenlow lost his eyebrows, two teeth and the price of the pavilion, and the next morning he went to Sabeline Oade, a scrivener with a stall under the cloth hall stair, and paid her to put on one sheet everything a stranger ought to have known before opening his mouth. She gave him forty terms, one for each bolt, so that he would remember the sum.

Copies of that sheet have been sold at fair gates ever since, and every scrivener who copied it added something: the world on a page, the ladder, the houses. What you are holding is its great-grandchild, checked against the registers by an under-archivist of Research and Archives, who would like it known that the entry for Apprentice has been correct from the start.

*The short version. Everything here is what the stories assume you already know. Nothing here is a rulebook, and nothing here is exhaustive. If a term or a house does not appear, you do not need it to read. A few lines on the crown of Kharven are marked Later hand: they run past the present year, because the stories do.*

## The World in One Page

![[Assets/Diagrams/four-quarters.svg]]

Reality is spoken of as three planes, though only two of them are realities. The Plane of Fate stands above the two lower realities: the control of Attraction Force and Obsession Force and the workings of reality itself, where probabilities can be manipulated to work outside what reality already knows. A careful scholar will add that it is no reality at all and that the name is a courtesy, since it is everything that flows, every possibility, idea and concept, anything that can happen in the two below it, and the law and construction of the world. Beneath it the Soul Plane is where law becomes a felt truth, and the Physical Plane, which half the books call the Material Plane, is where truth becomes event, body, ruin, and mountain range. Wherever one plane's law borders another's, a threshold has to exist, and that threshold is called the Veil. Every act of magic in this world crosses all three planes at once, and every named power in it, human, bloodline, or god, answers to one of the three.

Geography follows the same rule of layers. The world is one great body of land with isles off its shores, cut into Four Quarters, and each one solved a different problem. The North, the Inner World, is an old glaciated shield with rich ground and hardly anyone working it, and it runs in three parts of its own: a North under the crown of Kharven and the Banner Houses, a South of old dynasties and the Holy Sea of Alabaster, and a Center wedged between them around the Scourge of Hell, a vast wasteland filled with monsters long ago. The West, the Old World, is worn-out country with the deepest workings and the most thoroughly worked ground anywhere, and it survives entirely on arrays, keepers and hired hands it can no longer do without. The East, the New World, is a live volcanic arc, young, wet, and still building itself, rich on the surface and barely worked. The South, the Outer World, is split between a dragon empire on old spent ground with almost no magic left in it and materials nobody else can get, and a young Vampire empire under veiled northern skies. The quarter with the most raw power has the least industry, and the quarter with the most industry has the least power, and nobody has ever found a way around that trade.

Two systems of legitimacy sit on top of this geography and neither one can get rid of the other. The Crown holds land, law, and the right to hang, and its authority is inherited. The Guild Accord, a coalition of many kinds of guild across many nations, holds rank, credit, enforceable contracts and a portable legal identity that works in every kingdom, and for a fighter its authority is measured, on an instrument, in under a minute. A king's blood does not show up on a coil reading (the coil being the Accord's assay instrument, which you stand inside while a clerk writes down what it says), and a coil reading does not entitle anyone to a throne. Most of the politics a reader will meet is the friction between those two claims.

Time in this world comes in two counts that are never to be mixed. The Ages are historical: counted, dated by physical evidence, running from Year minus fourteen hundred to the present. The Epochs are cosmological: given in figures running to the hundreds of thousands, and the Guild that checked those figures found that not one of them came from an actual count. A bloodline that traces itself to an Epoch is making a claim about where it came from, and the arithmetic is a separate argument that the claim never has to win. The present moment, where every story in this codex is set, is Year 715 In Concordance, deep in the Withering Era, roughly seven hundred years after the sealing of the Concord Codex and twenty-five years into the Dark Ages, and the defining fact of the age is that the West's ambient magic has been quietly running out for seventy years and nobody can say why.

## The Dark Ages

Call it the Dark Ages because that is the name everyone in it actually uses, and no two of them agree why. The street dates it from the Black Noon of 690 IC, when the moon crossed the sun over the Ironlink brackets, and it will run to the turn of the century, 800 IC. A visitor would never guess the name from a market day. Sect banners and painted shields hang off every bridge, the temples are gold-leafed and older than the count with a pie stall against every wall, and more people can do something impossible than at any point in the record. Practitioners are common, about one person in ten, and everyone can read the ranks: outer disciple, inner disciple, core disciple and elder in the sects, the Guild's own titles in the halls. Schools run like a town's great love and worst liability, with their colors, songs and grudges. Fame moves on foot: heralds cry the news at the crossroads, minstrels carry it further and wrong, lords nail bounties to doors on hand-copied bills, and bookies take odds on all of it. Fighters shout the names of their arts across the field, and the room laughs at a big name until the name works.

Magic in this world is also something you hire, and for every lamp and warded door there is a person who wants paying and may on any given morning decline. Cities are kept by living hands on regular rounds. Lamp-wardens feed the street lamps from dusk to dawn, and each lamp burns the color of whoever keeps it. Ward-cutters cut and re-cut the wards on lintels and strongrooms, and leave the last stroke uncut until the balance is settled. Hedge-workers take the frost off an orchard and talk a fever down in the country beyond any company's bounds. Core keepers hold a worked Wellspring site and sell hours at the pool, and they can close it. Guild craftsmen shout the names of their contraptions and brag about their guild marks, and the contraptions misfire. Small powers are everywhere and petty, a trade done by hand: the noodle seller's knife that never dulls costs money, and the noodle seller can tell you to come back tomorrow. Parish stones rise at street level for public use, warm and waist-high, cut by a ward-cutter and kept charged by a warden. Assay posts sit wherever one fee-band meets another, holding a brass fork in a glass case that rings truer as the ground thickens, locked because the lock is the only thing standing between an honest reading and an argument about one.

A kept street has a hum under it, not loud, not quite a sound, and a person who has lived their whole life on one stops hearing it after two weeks and hears its absence within seconds if it stops. Work comes in grades. Sealed household work is cheap, capped, and usually thinner than the fee claims. Trade work runs stronger and gets searched. Bonded work is uncapped and issued against a person's name, which makes it a legal fact about someone before it is a service. Unsealed work is illegal everywhere, universal everywhere, and called a botch. None of this is run by the Guild Accord itself. It is run by chartered companies, one to a trade in each city or quarter, monopolies that set their own fees under a license from the Accord, which is exactly the arrangement it sounds like. All of it can stop, too: wardens walk off a round, ward-cutters down chisels, a whole street stops paying, and everybody in a city lives with that the way a sailor lives with weather.

What is built and carried stays medieval, whatever a smith and a guild hall can make, and that ceiling has a history. For most of history, making anything at scale was held back by four physical obstacles that had nothing to do with magic: water flooding a shaft, foul air at depth, the honest limit of a rope hauling weight, and a furnace that could not get hot enough to melt iron. Essence-driven arrays, tended by hired crews, solved the first two and the last one, in that order, over about five centuries, and each solution created the next problem in turn. The obstacle that held longest was moving weight over distance, and it has given way only as far as the kept road: waystones cut in tuned pairs, one filled by a road-warden at the station and its twin under a carrier's cart-bed taking the heat across a hand's breadth of air, burning nothing. There is no steam and no coal-fired engine anywhere in this world. Past the last warden's round the towns and the wilds burn oil, and news goes by road, ship, relay rider and beacon line, in shuttered colors from hilltop to hilltop, at a Guild price. Older than all of it are the old works, left from the Great Spirit War and the days of the Physical Epochs, when Bara, Ashura and Aegor walked in human form: dead and dangerous battlefields, relics people kill for, and old beings who remember.

Under the bright streets runs a plain class war. Stages can be bought, the great houses hoard the top Drafts, and the wealthy are stronger because they are wealthy. The poor rise by dungeon luck, guild patronage and blessings, and every ballad in this codex's century has a commoner in it who came up out of the ground holding a monster's core.

The crossbow is old, cheap, and everywhere, and it is the entire reason armor never went away. A bolt costs about three coppers; a working is pulled against a reserve that took years to build, so the economics of a fight between a soldier and a practitioner never favored the practitioner. It is the cheap thing that can kill a high-Stage fighter in a room, read by Speed Grade like any projectile, and there are no guns in any era. Armies field crossbowmen by the thousand. The Accord does not, because a bolt carries no signature a coil can read and a working does, and nobody has found a way to make a bolt legally accountable the way a spell is. Proofed harness, armor loosed at and stamped with the bow and range it survived, is the compromise the whole world lives inside: cheap, universal violence on one side, expensive, traceable violence on the other, and neither one has managed to make the other obsolete.

## How Magic Works

![[Assets/Diagrams/soul-crystal.svg]]

Every practitioner casts through a Soul Crystal, never through a wand or a word alone, and the Soul Crystal is the soul itself, in so many words. The Crystal has three layers, one per plane. The Essence Core, on the Soul Plane, holds identity, memory, and Traits. The Aether Shell, on the Material Plane, converts that inner truth into outward force. The Attraction Layer, on the Plane of Fate, is a soul's own share of fate and governs bonds, oaths, harmonizations, and Domains. Every soul has all three, and a Path is the one a Crystal has learned to lean on: the Body Path on the Shell, the literal understanding and manipulation of physical reality, which closes and hits low down and high up reaches as far as there is matter to read; the Spirit Path on the Core, everything to do with spiritual reality, souls, spirits, the dead and what a thing means; the Fate Path on the Attraction Layer, the hardest road there is, which fights with Range, Aura Pressure, presence and bonds, and on rare days at a ruinous price moves what a blow or a war depends on, with true Fate Arts at its deadliest end. A Path stands as Mastered, Walked (three parts in ten of a person's practice or more) or Studied (knowledge and small uses, open to anyone, opening no gates). One Path masters fastest, two take about two and a half times as long and leave holes, and three take about six times as long, past a mortal span, and are looked down on. No Path is better than another: each has plain limits and a way of beating each of the others, which is why some people combine them. Aether itself is an infinite ocean that belongs to nobody, thick in some places and thin in others. A practitioner pulls it in from the environment, refines it in the Crystal into Essence, the water a person has circulated and stamped with their own identity, the soul's own substance, so that spending it is spending yourself, and drives that Essence out through the Shell, and there is only ever as much of it as a soul has earned or paid for. So where you stand matters. Rich ground fills you faster and thin ground starves you; scholars call the difference Aetheric Density, and the street says fat and lean.

A Wellspring represents an alchemical and a spiritual transformation and concept. It can be seen as a source of power to pull from, and it is more the governing idea that transforms how a soul uses Aether: a lens on magic already potent. It shapes Essence differently on each Path, starting small and reaching double only with years of mastery on its home Path, and half again at most away from it. A soul gets one at a Core in a dungeon, from a school, from a Draft or blessing, or by birth. None of them is a spirit. The scholars' account is physics, and every figure in it stands: the old books call the Wellsprings sixty types of Aether, each carrying a fixed law, running whether or not anyone is working it, the way a pressure gradient runs whether or not something is in its path. Harmonizing, they say, phase-locks the Aether Shell so that Essence passing outward is shaped by that Wellspring's law before it reaches the world, which is why the same stat harmonized to two different Wellsprings behaves in two entirely different ways at the same magnitude. There are sixty Wellsprings in total, sorted by kind, and each one is a way some Titan's law shows up in the world, so each entry names its Titan along with its hue, the ground it favors and the Wellsprings that fight it. Most people awaken three to five Wellsprings across a lifetime. Serious adepts harmonize with a dozen or more. Nobody a reader is likely to meet has all sixty. A Wellspring also has sites in the world where its Aether pools, and common speech calls such a site a Core (no kin to the Essence Core inside the Crystal). Most lie in dungeons, and some are said to lie in vast territories. A worked Core is a dungeon someone cleared and now holds: it recharges and has keepers, and its keepers can close it. The soldier's way to beat an enemy fed by one is to take the fight off rich ground, onto salted, bled or thin ground: salted ground has been worked by hedge-workers so that Aether will not pool on it, and bled ground has been pulled empty.

A Wellspring's danger arrives from one of four directions, and its kind, which names its home plane and locks nobody out of it, tells you which. Thirty-three of the Sixty are Wild, physical phenomena and alchemical transformations: a Wild encounter acts on physical material, reshaping stone or metal that was already there, and a practitioner who takes such a Wellspring up shapes their inner Essence around the thing itself. Fifteen are purely spiritual: a Spirit encounter works on the soul, usually with little or no effect on the physical world, so the landscape looks ordinary and the person is the thing being authored. Six are Wellsprings of Fate: a Fate encounter looks and reads like nothing at all, but luck goes strange, oaths bite and strangers keep meeting. And six are Medium, at home on all three: a Medium encounter works at the Veil itself, in every direction at once, which is why a river or a beam of light can leave a person changed by the mere act of working with it. None of this is invented by the practitioner. A glyph, the boundary a practitioner sets, creates nothing: the law was already running, and the glyph only moves where its edge falls.

A working itself has a fixed grammar underneath every named art in the world: a Path (which layer of the soul it leans on), a Lens (the Wellspring that shapes it), a Form (what the hands are doing, often worn in the discipline's own suffix, such as -mancy for perception or -kinesis for direct control), an Ability (the internalized platform, invisible on its own), and a Technique (the specific, repeatable thing a person actually does, with a stated cost). And every working has to be written into something. The spoken or gestured working is fast, cheap to learn, and held in open contempt by the cutters' guilds precisely because it requires no years of training. The cut working, a rune, is permanent, outliving the person who made it and misfiring for as long as nobody finds the error, and its makers have the highest opinion of themselves in the world. The poured working, a Draft, is a technique stabilized into a bottle that anyone can carry, sell, tax, or drink, and it is how the overwhelming majority of ordinary people touch magic at all. The sung working, a chant, is sustained rather than instant or permanent, and it cannot be practiced by one person alone. All of these are skills and trades anyone can level, and the old rankings among them survive as guild feuds, with bottles.

Your own soul keeps the score. A window is the soul reporting its numbers to its owner, in the owner's own tongue, and no one else can see it; scholars argue about who built it. It shows Grades, percentages, bars and levels, often and in the middle of a fight. A normal look gives the sheet: race and lineage, the Eight Primaries with Grades, a Sub-Stat when it is used or when it moves, Traits with their levels, skills with their Grades, and any blessings. Skills rise with use. Traits have their own levels, which rise with use and grow with overall Level and skill, and a person's life spends most of their stat points by what they do, leaving a few to place at each Stage. Techniques show no level and take a bigger name when they jump. At thresholds the soul is offered a named class fitting how the person has lived, which gives abilities, shapes growth and evolves by how it is lived, each time the leading ladder rises. Three ladders stand beside the name, one to each layer of the soul: Aether Class, Muridic through Radiant and on, for how cleanly power leaves the Shell and works on the physical world; Essence Class, Listener through Ferryman and on, for the same on spiritual reality; and Thread Class, Touched through Weaver and on, for how much of Fate a soul can pull down and hold, where most souls stay at nothing for life.

Fate is the rarest of it and the hardest. To work the Plane of Fate is to manipulate probabilities and control the fate of something, and all spirits and all physical things have fates. People fight and wage wars with it, and the Moto are the model of how deadly a lineage with it is, but it is the law and construction of the world, the Archons and the great principles they embody hold sovereignty there as on the planes below, and a real pull is rare, ruinous and often fails, so that most Fate Path fighters live by their reach, their presence and their bonds. Real Fate Arts run mostly in bloodlines and are considered incredibly overpowered. Most people only touch Fate through a blessing, a power out of a person's natural reach, even in Level, granted by a higher spirit, an Archon, an Epoch or a person of higher Temperance, and a great giver spends almost nothing. A blessing is its own line on the sheet, and it can lie, because the giver wrote the line. A curse is often a blessing with terms you did not read.

Three arts go everywhere with a fighter, and each culture has its own name and its own argument about them. A Guild roll says reinforcement for the aura coat (the constant visible coat of a practitioner's own aura, bought with reserve), Pressure for the weight a strong soul lays on a room, and sensing for the trained read of another person's Essence. Bracket crowds say the shine, the weight and the nose. A Kharven soldier says the hide and the low sky, the Moto say *matoi*, the wearing, and *atsu*, the press, and hold that a coat anyone can see is badly worn, and a Marrowkin will tell you which shift you are on and what load you are carrying. Ordinary fighters use street words like these for all of it, and scholars use the real terms, and each side thinks the other is missing the point. The law under the words is short. The lens tempered on longest sets a coat's base color, the other lenses add texture and sound, and a class or a big Trait adds a shape. Pressure is felt in the room first, and then the window reports it. A stronger Gnosis Perception reads a weaker Resilience Ward; anything else is an estimate said aloud, and people can hide or fake it.

## The Ladder

![[Assets/Diagrams/stage-ladder.svg]]

Advancement in this world is called Temperance, and Temperance is subtraction, with nothing accumulated along the way. A practitioner does not train their way up sixteen Stages. Tempering melts the Crystal down and rebuilds each layer, refines the Essence and makes it stronger, and the body gains Traits, and the Stage that results is built out of whatever was actually in the Crystal at the moment it broke and reformed. Something has to set that off: a survived crisis, a seclusion, a tribulation, or a purchase. Nine souls in ten will live their whole lives inside the first four Stages alone, and the tenth is disproportionately somebody's heir.

The sixteen Stages, in order, are Murmuring, Welling, Ascension, and Flourishing, where almost everyone stays; Splintering, Glory, and Refraction; Transcendence, Invocation, and Realization; Dissonance and Emanation; Principality and Zenith; and finally Revelation and Apex, which sit beyond the fourteen the Codex actually numbers.

Two other counts ride alongside the Stages, and a reader will meet both. Level is the count of how many times a Crystal has meaningfully densified, from 1 to a ceiling of 500 that only a soul at Revelation or Apex ever passes, and past which nobody numbers it, in five Bands of a hundred: Mortal Foundation, Awakened, Sovereign, Mythic, and Absolute. It moves by danger overcome, so killing something stronger moves it more than a hundred weak kills, and crews argue over who got the kill. The first four Bands each end at a gate, a Stage the level counter will not pass without, so a practitioner can pile up Level and still be stopped dead for want of a Catalyst. The Tiers of Standing are the Accord's nine titles, the name that travels on a silver token, the same in every guild and grading trust in that trade: a merchant may deal up to his, a craftsman make up to hers, an adventurer take commissions up to the same Threat Tier. A Grandmaster merchant may not be able to throw a punch, and a forged token is a great crime. The fighting guilds keep the Stage table as their yardstick: Initiate, Apprentice, Journeyman, and Adept for the first four Stages, one apiece; Expert for Splintering through Refraction; Master for Transcendence through Realization; Grandmaster for Dissonance and Emanation; Archmaster for Principality and Zenith; and Paragon for Revelation and Apex, folded into a single rank because a muster roll cannot administer a blank.

Every Threshold between Stages needs two things at once: a measurable floor of accumulated pressure, and a Catalyst, the push that opens it. Each Stage has a Catalyst Condition, the quality of lived experience the Crystal asks for when nobody is paying, and Drafts and blessings can supply it instead, which is what the top of the apothecary trade is for. A bought Stage opens the door and no more: a Release must still be found and mastered by fighting, and sects teach what money cannot buy, Releases, Domains, techniques, skills and the rites for their Wellspring. The conditions themselves are worth knowing, because they are what Pressure actually feels like from the inside. Murmuring is a hum with no direction, weather happening in a body that has not been told there is weather yet. Welling is that hum given a direction with no ability to meter it, so the cup goes over and the linen scorches, and it is caused by nothing gentler than surviving first contact with a Wellspring by accident. Ascension is the first thing a person ever builds entirely out of their own understanding, and nothing else in the Stage matters next to that. Flourishing asks for a genuine bond with another soul, so nearly every stable adult practitioner in the world got there because somebody mattered to them enough to hold weight, and the ones who got there on a bottle are often easy to spot at dinner.

Splintering is the first real crisis: the architecture that has held since Murmuring reaches its limit and has to break to grow, and it breaks along whatever the soul was already carrying badly, whatever happened to cause the break. Glory is the first Stage whose Catalyst is a decision rather than an accident, a choice to accept real cost for something the practitioner values more than their own structural integrity. Refraction is the Stage at which the Crystal becomes an object a person can look at, which means the fracture record Splintering wrote becomes visible and impossible to unsee. From Transcendence onward the world itself starts changing around a practitioner: an unhealed wound becomes load-bearing structure and produces a true Domain; at Invocation the practitioner stops working alone and starts asking Wellsprings, Titans, or Archonic law to co-author the result; at Realization the world itself begins to accommodate what the practitioner has become, and that accommodation never reverses. Dissonance is chosen rather than survived, a deliberate walk toward something that could end the practitioner, met with enough coherence to choose how it is met; Emanation is what radiates outward afterward, when a practitioner's mood becomes an environmental condition for the district around them. Above Principality, advancement is no longer earned or bought in any ordinary sense; Zenith in particular is a recognition in place of a breakthrough, conferred rather than reached. Revelation and Apex lie past all of it, in territory where the Codex itself declines to number them at all.

Every Threshold also leaves a residue called the seat. A clean seat means the melt cooled around agreeing evidence and the practitioner's output reads as one consistent signature. A rough seat means it cooled around contradictions, and rough-seated practitioners are unpredictable rather than weak, which is exactly why some of the most feared people in the world cannot be read in advance. A coil reads a bought Stage the same as a fought one. The seat is another matter: a coil reads it in under a minute, and what it is actually reading, honestly and without prejudice, is what somebody's family could afford them.

## The Soul Crystal and What Things Cost

![[Assets/Diagrams/stacking.svg]]

Nothing in this system is free, and the system does not pretend otherwise. Essence is spent, never summoned from nowhere. Domains bleed their holder. Traits fatigue with use. Pushing past a sustainable limit, called Overchannel, leaves a permanent scar rather than a temporary one. Any practitioner who tells you a technique costs nothing is either lying or has not yet been sent the bill.

The market sells the climb itself. An Essence Draft, a working stabilized into a drinkable substance, can raise a practitioner to the quantitative floor a Threshold requires, the best of them supply the Catalyst as well, and licensed use of Drafts is the backbone of the apothecary guilds' entire trade, with the top of the stock locked in great houses' vaults. The most potent Drafts, rendered from monster cores, carry the clearest cost: Identity Contamination, in which the Essence taken carries the identity it was taken from, and enough of it leaves a practitioner unable to say cleanly which convictions are actually their own. A Crystal cut from a dead practitioner makes the strongest Draft of all, the worst crime in every realm and the most profitable, and it is why the [[Materials, Alchemy & Trade/Alchemy/The Standing Index|Standing Index]]'s Class IV ledgers (its top class of stock, no relation to Aether Class IV) have never balanced.

Costs are not only personal. They are civic. Under the round-fees described above, a household or a workshop is billed for its lamps, wards and warm stones the way it would be billed for water, and the first question in any scene involving a working is who is paying for it. At the very top of the scale, the handful of rites keyed directly to a Titan rather than to a Wellspring bill in currencies no market sells: one takes small memories from everyone standing nearby, caster included; another ages every innocent caught inside its effect at a forced rate; only a single one of the fourteen costs the caster nothing at all, and every order that studies it treats that single clean entry as the most dangerous line in the whole register, on the theory that a cost which lands nowhere has simply not been found yet.

## Monsters, Cores and Dungeons

Monsters move and live across the whole Inner World, and beasts range more widely still. The Scourge of Hell is the one area known for extremely dangerous ones. A monster gains Levels by killing, evolves at its own thresholds, and sits on the same Level and Grade scale as a person, so a strong enough Gnosis can read it and a weak one gets question marks. The Accord grades them T1 to T9 like its commissions, and a famous one earns a name, an epithet and a bounty. Its parts make gear. Its core is in a way its Soul Crystal, worth more the stronger the monster: an alchemist renders it into Essence Drafts used for tempering, and swallowed raw it gives everything at once, and most who try die or come out wrong.

A dungeon grows down in floors around a Wellspring Core, with something guarding the heart. Kill that and clear the place, or leave it and it grows. It is alive and learning, it moves its galleries and breeds what kills crews best, and scholars argue whether it thinks. See [[The Bestiary/Dungeons|Dungeons]].

## Gear

Items carry two ratings: Grade for power, on the fighters' letters, and Rarity for scarcity (Common, Uncommon, Rare, Very Rare, Legendary, Mythic, Unique). The old words (Marked, Proofed, Tempered, Instrument, Hallowed, Storied, Found, Numinous, Proscribed) survive as the Accord's stamps. A smith's title caps the Grade she may make and a merchant's caps the Rarity he may sell. Bonded gear grows with its owner, other gear does not, and a good forger beats a weak appraiser. A Master Work does what its Grade and material should not be able to, made once at the end of a lifetime, always Unique or Mythic, and named.

## The Houses and Powers a Reader Meets

![[Assets/Diagrams/three-grounds.svg]]

### The Moto

The Moto are the archaic bloodline behind more of this world's crowns than any other family, founded in myth by Bara Moto, the First Epoch Bairos given human form, and Mizuki Moto, who turned that authority into something a family could actually inherit. Every Moto carries the Shingan, a sacred ocular inheritance split into four branches: Meigan for revelation, Reigan for the soul and curse-work, Tengan for fate and probability, and Kamigan, the crown eye, which unifies all three into sovereign judgment. The house also carries Hataraki, the deeper governing power authored by Bara before time itself, which is why a Moto technique tends to feel administrative rather than simply violent: a Moto strike can reveal, rank, condemn, or complete a situation rather than merely hit it harder. Fate is the hardest road there is and nobody grips it bare, so each Work of Hataraki is a distinct, inherited way of taking hold of one function, and that inheritance is what makes the house so deadly on a battlefield. People who hold Hataraki can also take back or remove a blessing, which is a large part of why they are feared. Of the house's seven great lines, six are landless, scattered across temperate ground after the ancestral seat in the Inner World's western shield was ruined by the first blow on the white shelf, about 310 IC, the breaking the Moto registers call the split. Only the Kōkan Line, the Kamigan-eyed line of crown authority, holds an actual seat, and that seat is Kharven.

### Kharven, the Ashen Crown

Kharven, the Ashen Crown, is a feudal monarchy on frozen ground in the far north of the Inner World, the one true kingdom in the North, and its city, Kharven-Seat, is older than the king most people credit with it. Its constitution is the cold itself: a climate that kills the unprepared and rewards nothing but endurance, preparation, and showing up when called. Six Banner Houses hold their lands around it and are not kingdoms, however they carry themselves at table. *Later hand:* Muken Moto, a Kōkan practitioner, took its crown and did not build it, and Kharven later stands as the capital of the Northern Empire of Whitemere, which the Kharven have never once called anything but the Stack. Kharven's queen consort, Ayame Yuno, came from the Kagura Branch of House Yuno on Yasoshima, a confederation of family groups governed by open assembly with no king and no standing army. Her ritual architecture, the Saishiki, once gave the northern clans' oaths real spiritual weight; her death left only the ceremony, and Kharven's oaths have carried no more than any oath's small Fate ever since.

### The Guild Accord

The Guild Accord is what makes the Moto's kind of authority answerable to something other than itself. It is a coalition of many kinds of guild across many nations, adventurers' guilds, merchant guilds, craft guilds, alchemists, healers, couriers and more, bound by the Concord Codex, a treaty that asks for a fragment of a member's own Soul Crystal in exchange for a portable rank recognized in every member kingdom, and the signing is still going on, one hall at a time. Halls sign on for shared rank, enforceable contracts and passage, and crowns tolerate it and fear it. Its heartland is the Sealed Charter of the Concord, in the Center of the Inner World, with its seat at Sealhall. Five open Divisions serve it, Strategic, Enforcement, Arbitration, Research and Archives, and Logistics and Supply, plus a sixth, the Black Concord, that the Codex does not publicly admit exists. Its culture is summarized in one line carved into every guild hall gate in every realm it reaches: Balance before Dominion. The Accord is not a crown. Past the Sealed Charter it holds no territory in the old quarters and cannot hang a kingdom's subject; the only necks it answers for are its own sworn members', whom its Tribunal Marshals execute under the Codex. In the New World, chartered from nothing, its halls are the only law there is. Everywhere else its real power is that its courts are faster and more predictable than anyone else's, which is usually enough.

### Eresse

Eresse, before it fractured into the smaller houses a reader meets today, was the High Empire of the Vey-Elarin, the first people the goddess Florwyn sang into being and the first to be given Wellsprings at all, on the doctrine that the land is a relative rather than a resource. Five Royal Houses answered to a throne that ruled by ratification rather than decree, and the whole civilization's most treasured racial trait, Domain Stability, was paid for in a war against a corruption that moved through the Wellsprings themselves rather than arriving as an army. The doctrinal splits born in that war are still open wounds in every Eressean successor state a story is likely to visit.

### The Marrowkin

The Marrowkin, called the Dawi in older rolls and by older people, live inside a dead god and sell pieces of it. Their holds are galleries cut through the bones of something that died in the Great Spirit War, they forge with what they cut out of it, and they keep a tally of what was taken because someday the body will want it back; what exactly died stays an argument among scholars. They are eight branches descended from one Titan, Varūn the Deepwright, who taught that the universe requires endurance rather than strength. Three deep lineages, the High Line, the Cask Line, and the Deepkin, held the ancestral halls; five more, the Kuntur Reach, the Tallow Clans, the Windward Herds, the Tower-Kin, and the Passwardens, were scattered by later collapses and had to reinvent what endurance meant on ground that shared nothing with a mountain's interior. The Ember Holds of Marrowhold, the realm in the Ring whose rim mountains are the ribs, supplies Kharven's metalwork and rations its grain shipments with calculated precision.

### The Keth-Gorrum

The Keth-Gorrum is the open war-moot of the Crimson Isles of Vorruk-Khal, a volcanic archipelago of forty-three islands settled by goblinoid peoples driven entirely out of the Old World generations ago. It has no king, no warlord, and no hereditary seat of any kind, only one hundred and twelve delegates from Hobgoblin, Bugbear, and Tinker Goblin communities, sitting under a charter carved into a cliff face, with seven hundred years of moot records behind it. It never signed the Concord Codex and trades with the Accord without belonging to it.

### The New World

The New World is the East, a live volcanic arc at the far end of the continent, still building itself, rich in surface gold, silver, and a light, corrosion-proof metal called Cloudmetal that only the eastern arc can produce, and almost entirely free of the Old World's dependence on fixed arrays, because its Wellspring sites actually move as the ground beneath them shifts. It runs on its own calendar, separate from the Inner World's, and its politics have not yet crossed the eastern rise to touch the rest of this codex's world. What has come this far is a name: Ilthára Korvaeth, called by her enemies the Mad Queen, whose war in that far country is, at bottom, a fight over whether a name can be held as property. It is a story still being written, at an end of the continent this codex otherwise leaves alone.

## The Archons, the Titans, and the Three Epochs

This is how the world explains itself to itself, and it is worth reading as belief rather than as fact, because the world's own record-keepers say so. Before there were three planes there was Omnara, totality before it needed compartments. An event no surviving record explains, called the Division, split that totality into three currents, and from those three currents came the three planes and three sovereign intelligences called the Epochs: Bairos, who authored causality and sequence and gave the Plane of Fate its shape; Aegor, who gave form weight and mass and built the Physical Plane out of it; and Ashura, who gave awareness the capacity to feel, and is the reason the Soul Plane can hold memory at all. Aegor's plane and Ashura's are the two lower realities, and Bairos stands over what lies above them, which a careful scholar declines to call a reality at all. From the tension between the three, fourteen Archons condensed into what the world calls the Pantheon of Abstract Dominion: a Primordial Dyad of Order and Chaos, and six paired principles beneath it, Form against Time, Memory against Horizon, Will against Desire, Insight against Dream, Motion against Stillness, and Harmony paired with Renewal. Most if not all of them are Sovereign across all three planes, though some are deemed greater than others, and Uurgath and Urion are unparalleled in Fate, because what they are cannot be conceived. Archonic Magic sits between the geologic authority of the Titans and the everyday currents of the Wellsprings, and every spell, Domain, and godlike ability in this world is required to trace its permission back to one of those three strata.

The Fourteen Titans are a different kind of thing entirely, and the distinction is treated as important rather than pedantic. An Archon is a principle enthroned, with a court, a doctrine, and people who pray to it. A Titan is a principle bearing weight, with no theology worth the name and an enormous amount of engineering built around it instead: nobody prays to the Titan of gravity, they build to it. The fourteen are seated across four orders, the Physical (Atla'zon and Gaia), the Spiritual Seat (Omnis, Florwyn, Dazkar, Nysera, Sylorin, and Ilgrimm), the Strata (Valmir, Kaldrion, and Chronaeus), and the Abyssal Depths (Prometheus, Zar'thuun, and Vohrin), and a small number of rites keyed directly to them exist at the absolute outer edge of what a mortal Crystal can survive holding.

But the world's own institutions are honest, in their own dry way, about how much of this is verified. The Guild of Measurewrights, the body that corrected the historical calendar, drew a hard line between the two kinds of number in this section. The Ages, from Year minus fourteen hundred to the present, are counted and attested by physical evidence, and every date attached to the Guild Accord, its Codex, and its Divisions falls inside that count and is trustworthy as written. The Epochs, and everything about Omnara, the Division, the Titans, and the Archons that comes with a duration attached, are cosmological rather than historical: no calendar existed to record them, no observer stood outside them to mark their passage, and the Guild's own finding is that the old figures are accurate as statements of scale and meaningless as statements of years. This is not treated as a demotion. A bloodline that claims descent from an Epoch is making a claim about origin, and that claim survives the correction untouched. It is simply, and permanently, a different conversation than the one the history books are having.

## A Short Glossary

*Sabeline Oade wrote forty. Her successors could not stop.*

**Aether**: the infinite ocean of raw magical potential. It belongs to no one and is never used up.

**Essence**: Aether a practitioner has pulled in, refined in the Soul Crystal, circulated, and stamped with their own identity. It is the soul's own substance, so spending it is spending yourself. Finite, and only ever as much as has been earned or bought.

**Practitioner**: a person whose Soul Crystal is awake and working, about one in ten. Everyone else touches magic through what they buy.

**Continuum**: the lawful fabric of reality, which measures a soul and permits as much as its architecture will bear. It grants no power.

**Soul Crystal**: the soul, literally, which every practitioner casts through, with three layers, one per plane.

**Essence Core**: the Soul Crystal's soul-plane layer. Holds identity, memory, and Traits.

**Aether Shell**: the Soul Crystal's material-plane layer. Converts inner truth into outward force.

**Attraction Layer**: the Soul Crystal's fate-plane layer, a soul's own share of fate. Governs bonds, harmonizations, and Domains.

**Reserve**: the store of Essence a practitioner carries, shown on their window as a percentage and a bar. Every working spends it.

**Reinforcement**: Essence driven through the body to push it past its passive base, showing as a coat of the practitioner's own aura. Lost asleep, drugged or surprised.

**Pressure**: the weight a stronger soul lays on a room. One Stage up bends attention, two brings sweat and nausea, three drops people where they stand.

**Wellspring**: one of sixty fixed alchemical and spiritual transformations and concepts. It can be seen as a source of power, and it is more the governing idea that transforms how a soul uses Aether: a lens that shapes Essence differently on each Path and, after years of mastery, can double its effect on its home Path, half again at most away. Its kind (Wild, Spirit, Fate or Medium) names that home. Scholars explain each as a type of Aether with a fixed physical law. Each is one manifestation of a Titan's law.

**Kind**: the home plane a Wellspring names: Wild (thirty-three), Spirit (fifteen), Fate (six) or Medium (six, at home on all three). It says where a Wellspring is strongest and locks nobody out.

**Harmonization**: taking up a Wellspring as a lens; in the scholars' account, phase-locking the Aether Shell to its law.

**Trait**: a law the soul itself carries permanently, held in the Essence Core rather than borrowed from a Wellspring. Each has a level that rises with use.

**Temperance**: the sixteen-Stage ladder of advancement. Subtraction and reorganization, with nothing accumulated. Tempering melts the Crystal down and rebuilds each layer.

**Stage**: one of the sixteen structural reorganizations a Crystal undergoes under pressure. Can be bought, and a bought Stage comes with no Release.

**Level and Band**: Level counts how far a Crystal has densified, moving by danger overcome, from 1 to a ceiling of 500 that only Revelation and Apex pass, uncounted; the five Bands are its hundreds: Mortal Foundation, Awakened, Sovereign, Mythic, Absolute.

**Path**: the layer of the Crystal a soul has learned to lean on, and so the direction its advancement actually took: Body, Spirit or Fate. Nobody chooses one; it is read afterward, and it gates how far a stat may rise. It stands as Mastered, Walked or Studied.

**Coil**: the assay house's measuring instrument, a great coil of brass a practitioner stands inside. It reads a bought Stage the same as a fought one, and the seat differently.

**Tier Grade**: a practitioner's raw output magnitude, lettered from Hollow through F, E, D, C, B, A, S, SS, SSS, X, and EX, to EX+.

**Class**: a name the soul offers at thresholds, fitting how the person has lived, giving abilities and evolving each time the leading ladder rises. **Aether Class**: the Shell's ladder, Muridic through Absolute, for how strong a person's influence on the physical world is and how cleanly the Shell delivers output. **Essence Class**: the Core's ladder, Listener through Absolute, for influence on spiritual reality. **Thread Class**: the Attraction Layer's ladder, Touched through Absolute, for how much of Fate a soul can pull down and hold.

**Catalyst**: the push that opens a Threshold. The Catalyst Condition is the lived experience a Crystal asks for; a Draft or a blessing can supply it instead.

**Threshold**: the moment a Stage transition actually occurs.

**Release**: a practitioner's Trait let off its safety limit on purpose, in a shape their Crystal has learned to hold, on a clock. First from Stage IV, Second from VII, Third from IX, each found and mastered by fighting.

**Domain**: a standing region of the world operating on a practitioner's own terms while they are present in it. Anyone at Stage VIII can have one, their own concept made real, once the Second Release that opens it has been found. See [[The Magic System/The Soul and the Climb/Domains|Domains]].

**Vow**: small Fate, a condition a person binds on themselves, spoken aloud, anyone's to make. It lands at once if broken and needs no witness. See [[The Magic System/Practice and Institution/Vows and Oaths|Vows and Oaths]].

**The Seat**: the residue a Threshold leaves behind. Clean if it cooled around agreement, rough if it cooled around contradiction.

**Draft**: a working poured into a substance and bottled, graded by the Stage it serves. Advancement Drafts, Essence Drafts and fight Drafts are the common kinds.

**Essence Draft**: a working stabilized into a drinkable substance, so someone whose Crystal has never woken can still be affected by it. The tempering kind is mostly rendered from monster cores.

**Identity Contamination**: the risk of a creature-sourced Draft: the identity it was rendered from bleeding into the drinker's own.

**The Guild Accord**: the coalition of many kinds of guild across many nations, bound by the Concord Codex. Its heartland is the Sealed Charter of the Concord.

**Concord Codex**: the treaty binding every Accord member; asks for a fragment of the member's own Soul Crystal in exchange.

**Concord Lattice**: the living record that carries every Accord member's deeds, permanently.

**High Concordant**: the Accord's supreme head, whose soul is woven into the Heart Vault.

**Tiered Path**: the Accord's nine Tiers of Standing, the same titles in every guild, grading trust in that trade: Initiate, Apprentice, Journeyman, Adept, Expert, Master, Grandmaster, Archmaster, Paragon. Fighting guilds read them off the coil and the Stage table.

**The Black Concord**: the Accord's sixth, unacknowledged Division.

**Commission**: a sanctioned Accord job, classified by Threat Tier, T1 to T9, and by purpose. Monsters are graded on the same scale.

**Threat Tier**: how dangerous a job or a monster is, T1 to T9, and the shorthand for its price. A monster's stone and parts are read from its own Grade and Level on the coil, and most beasts line up with their T.

**Core**: in common speech, a Wellspring site where one type of Aether pools, mostly at the heart of a dungeon. A worked Core is a dungeon someone cleared and now holds: it recharges, and its keepers sell hours at it and can close it. The survey keeps "Wellspring Core" for its top density band, and the Essence Core is the Crystal's own layer.

**Parish stone**: a public street-level working point, cut by a ward-cutter and kept charged by a warden.

**Company**: the chartered monopoly, one to a trade in each city or quarter, licensed by the Accord, that holds the Core rights and sets the fees.

**Botch**: illegal, unsealed work done with a stolen glyph, robbing a lawful company of its fee.

**Parun**: the fixed grammar every working's "sentence" is composed in, whether it is spoken, cut, poured or sung.

**Glyph**: a boundary condition set on an already-running Wellspring law. Creates nothing new.

**Omnara**: the totality that existed before the Division, before there were three planes at all.

**The Division**: the unexplained event that split Omnara into the three planes and the Three Epochs.

**The Three Epochs**: Bairos, Aegor, and Ashura, the sovereign intelligence of the Plane of Fate, the Physical Plane, and the Soul Plane.

**The Fourteen Archons**: the crowned principles administering meaning between Titanic Law and Wellspring Law.

**The Fourteen Titans**: load-bearing cosmic laws with no theology, maintained by engineering rather than worship.

**The Veil**: the threshold running between all three planes wherever their laws border each other.

**The Withering**: the unexplained, decades-long decline in the West's ambient Essence, ongoing since Year 645 In Concordance.

**Window**: the soul reporting its numbers to its owner, in the owner's own tongue, unseen by anyone else. Grades, percentages, bars and levels.

**The Sheet**: what a normal look at the window shows: race and lineage, Eight Primaries with Grades, Traits with levels, skills with Grades, and any blessings.

**Skill**: a thing a person does and gets better at by doing it, shown as a Grade. Drafts, runes, tags and chants are skills and trades. Anyone can level a skill hands alone can do, pouring included; the cutting, tags and chants want a woken Crystal.

**Fate**: the control of Attraction Force and Obsession Force and the workings of reality itself, manipulating probabilities to control the fate of something. It stands above the two lower realities and is no reality itself: every possibility, idea and concept, and the law and construction of the world. It is the hardest thing in the system. Fate Arts are rare, mostly by bloodline, and considered incredibly overpowered.

**Blessing**: a power out of a person's natural reach, even in Level, granted by someone spiritually stronger. Listed on the sheet, and able to lie, because the giver wrote the line.

**Curse**: often a blessing with terms you did not read.

**Monster core**: in a way a monster's Soul Crystal, worth more the stronger the monster. Rendered into Drafts, or swallowed raw by people who mostly die of it. See [[Materials, Alchemy & Trade/Alchemy/Monster Cores|Monster Cores]].

**Dungeon**: what grows down in floors around a Core, with something guarding the heart. Alive and learning.

**Grade and Rarity**: an item's two ratings, one for power and one for scarcity.

**Master Work**: a piece that does what its Grade and material should not be able to, made once at the end of a lifetime, always Unique or Mythic, and named. See [[Materials, Alchemy & Trade/Alchemy/Master Works|Master Works]].

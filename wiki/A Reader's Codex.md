# A Reader's Codex

This codex began as an apology. At the autumn cloth fair in Tessbury, Tibalt Wenlow, a saffron factor (a merchant's agent, buying and selling on another's account) up from the warm southern marches, rose at the Mercers' election feast with six hundred guests under a pavilion hung with ribbon, lifted his cup to the new Warden's aunt, read the silver token at her throat as best he could, and toasted her as the finest Apprentice he had ever had the honor to meet. Dame Ysolt Varley was a Master and an elder of the Vermilion Bell, a school whose fight songs carry two streets from any bracket in the cloth towns, and she had been sitting through speeches for some hours. The coat of aura she wore went from a banked ember orange to the white-gold at the heart of a dye-furnace before he had finished the word. Every ribbon on the pavilion caught. Forty bolts of Wenlow's own saffron silk, hung overhead as his gift, went up in one sheet of yellow fire that they saw from the next valley, and the pipers, who had been paid for the whole evening, played on. The new Warden took his oath at the horse trough with his hair singed. Nobody died, which Dame Ysolt called a courtesy. Wenlow lost his eyebrows, two teeth and the price of the pavilion, and the next morning he went to Sabeline Oade, a scrivener with a stall under the cloth hall stair, and paid her to put on one sheet everything a stranger ought to have known before opening his mouth. She gave him forty terms, one for each bolt, so that he would remember the sum.

Copies of that sheet have been sold at fair gates ever since, and every scrivener who copied it added something: the world on a page, the ladder, the houses. What you are holding is its great-grandchild, checked against the registers by an under-archivist of Research and Archives, who would like it known that the entry for Apprentice has been correct from the start.

*The short version. Everything here is what the stories assume you already know. Nothing here is a rulebook, and nothing here is exhaustive. If a term or a house does not appear, you do not need it to read. A few lines on the crown of Kharven are marked Later hand: they run past the present year, because the stories do.*

## The World in One Page

![[Assets/Diagrams/four-quarters.svg]]

Reality has three planes, and they are nested rather than stacked. The Plane of Fate lies deepest, the lattice everything else rests on. The Soul Plane lies between, where law becomes a felt truth. The Physical Plane sits on the surface, where truth becomes event, body, ruin, and mountain range. Wherever one plane's law borders another's, a threshold has to exist, and that threshold is called the Veil. Every act of magic in this world crosses all three planes at once, and every named power in it, human, bloodline, or god, answers to one of the three.

Geography follows the same rule of layers. The world is one great body of land with isles off its shores, cut into Four Quarters, and each one solved a different problem. The North, the Inner World, is an old glaciated shield with rich ground and hardly anyone working it. The West, the Old World, is worn-out country with the deepest workings and the most thoroughly worked ground anywhere, and it survives entirely on arrays, keepers and hired hands it can no longer do without. The East, the New World, is a live volcanic arc, young, wet, and still building itself, rich on the surface and barely worked. The South, the Outer World, is old dry ground with almost no magic left in it and materials nobody else can get. The quarter with the most raw power has the least industry, and the quarter with the most industry has the least power, and nobody has ever found a way around that trade.

Two systems of legitimacy sit on top of this geography and neither one can get rid of the other. The Crown holds land, law, and the right to hang, and its authority is inherited. The Guild Accord holds rank, credit, and a portable legal identity that works in every kingdom, and its authority is measured, on an instrument, in under a minute. A king's blood does not show up on a coil reading (the coil being the Accord's assay instrument, which you stand inside while a clerk writes down what it says), and a coil reading does not entitle anyone to a throne. Most of the politics a reader will meet is the friction between those two claims.

Time in this world comes in two counts that are never to be mixed. The Ages are historical: counted, dated by physical evidence, running from Year minus fourteen hundred to the present. The Epochs are cosmological: given in figures running to the hundreds of thousands, and the Guild that checked those figures found that not one of them came from an actual count. A bloodline that traces itself to an Epoch is making a claim about where it came from, and the arithmetic is a separate argument that the claim never has to win. The present moment, where every story in this codex is set, is Year 715 In Concordance, deep in the Withering Era, roughly seven hundred years after the sealing of the Concord Codex and twenty-five years into the Dark Ages, and the defining fact of the age is that the West's ambient magic has been quietly running out for seventy years and nobody can say why.

## The Dark Ages

Call it the Dark Ages because that is the name everyone in it actually uses, and no two of them agree why. The street dates it from the Black Noon of 690 IC, when the moon crossed the sun over the Ironlink brackets, and it will run to the turn of the century, 800 IC. A visitor would never guess the name from a market day. Sect banners and painted shields hang off every bridge, the temples are gold-leafed and older than the count with a pie stall against every wall, and more people can do something impossible than at any point in the record. Practitioners are common, about one person in ten, and everyone can read the ranks: outer disciple, inner disciple, core disciple and elder in the sects, the Guild's own titles in the halls. Schools run like a town's great love and worst liability, with their colors, songs and grudges. Fame moves on foot: heralds cry the news at the crossroads, minstrels carry it further and wrong, lords nail bounties to doors on hand-copied bills, and bookies take odds on all of it. Fighters shout the names of their arts across the field, and the room laughs at a big name until the name works.

Magic in this world is also something you hire, and for every lamp and warded door there is a person who wants paying and may on any given morning decline. Cities are kept by living hands on regular rounds. Lamp-wardens feed the street lamps from dusk to dawn, and each lamp burns the color of whoever keeps it. Ward-cutters cut and re-cut the wards on lintels and strongrooms, and leave the last stroke uncut until the balance is settled. Hedge-workers take the frost off an orchard and talk a fever down in the country beyond any company's bounds. Core keepers hold a worked Wellspring site and sell hours at the pool, and they can close it. Guild craftsmen shout the names of their contraptions and brag about their guild marks, and the contraptions misfire. Small powers are everywhere and petty, a trade done by hand: the noodle seller's knife that never dulls costs money, and the noodle seller can tell you to come back tomorrow. Parish stones rise at street level for public use, warm and waist-high, cut by a ward-cutter and kept charged by a warden. Assay posts sit wherever one fee-band meets another, holding a brass fork in a glass case that rings truer as the ground thickens, locked because the lock is the only thing standing between an honest reading and an argument about one.

A kept street has a hum under it, not loud, not quite a sound, and a person who has lived their whole life on one stops hearing it after two weeks and hears its absence within seconds if it stops. Work comes in grades. Sealed household work is cheap, capped, and usually thinner than the fee claims. Trade work runs stronger and gets searched. Bonded work is uncapped and issued against a person's name, which makes it a legal fact about someone before it is a service. Unsealed work is illegal everywhere, universal everywhere, and called a botch. None of this is run by the Guild Accord itself. It is run by chartered companies, one to a trade in each city or quarter, monopolies that set their own fees under a license from the Accord, which is exactly the arrangement it sounds like. All of it can stop, too: wardens walk off a round, ward-cutters down chisels, a whole street stops paying, and everybody in a city lives with that the way a sailor lives with weather.

What is built and carried stays medieval, whatever a smith and a guild hall can make, and that ceiling has a history. For most of history, making anything at scale was held back by four physical obstacles that had nothing to do with magic: water flooding a shaft, foul air at depth, the honest limit of a rope hauling weight, and a furnace that could not get hot enough to melt iron. Essence-driven arrays, tended by hired crews, solved the first two and the last one, in that order, over about five centuries, and each solution created the next problem in turn. The obstacle that held longest was moving weight over distance, and it has given way only as far as the kept road: waystones cut in tuned pairs, one filled by a road-warden at the station and its twin under a carrier's cart-bed taking the heat across a hand's breadth of air, burning nothing. There is no steam and no coal-fired engine anywhere in this world. Past the last warden's round the towns and the wilds burn oil, and news goes by road, ship, relay rider and beacon line, in shuttered colors from hilltop to hilltop, at a Guild price. Older than all of it are the old works, left from the Great Spirit War and the days of the Physical Epochs, when Bara, Ashura and Aegor walked in human form: dead and dangerous battlefields, relics people kill for, and old beings who remember.

The crossbow is old, cheap, and everywhere, and it is the entire reason armor never went away. A bolt costs about three coppers; a working is pulled against a reserve that took years to build, so the economics of a fight between a soldier and a practitioner never favored the practitioner. It is the cheap thing that can kill a high-Stage fighter in a room, read by Speed Grade like any projectile, and there are no guns in any era. Armies field crossbowmen by the thousand. The Accord does not, because a bolt carries no signature a coil can read and a working does, and nobody has found a way to make a bolt legally accountable the way a spell is. Proofed harness, armor loosed at and stamped with the bow and range it survived, is the compromise the whole world lives inside: cheap, universal violence on one side, expensive, traceable violence on the other, and neither one has managed to make the other obsolete.

## How Magic Works

![[Assets/Diagrams/soul-crystal.svg]]

Every practitioner casts through a Soul Crystal, never through a wand or a word alone. The Crystal has three layers, one per plane. The Essence Core, on the Soul Plane, holds identity, memory, and Traits. The Aether Shell, on the Material Plane, converts that inner truth into outward force. The Attraction Layer, on the Plane of Fate, governs bonds, harmonizations, and Domains. Aether itself is an infinite ocean that belongs to nobody, thick in some places and thin in others. A practitioner pulls it in from the environment, refines it in the Crystal into Essence, the water a person has circulated and stamped with their own identity, and drives that Essence out through the Shell, and there is only ever as much of it as a soul has actually earned. So where you stand matters. Rich ground fills you faster and thin ground starves you; scholars call the difference Aetheric Density, and the street says fat and lean.

Aether comes in different types, and they affect the Soul Crystal differently. Those types are the Wellsprings: sources of power that also represent alchemical principles and spiritual transformations. None of them is a spirit or a gift. Each one carries a fixed law, running whether or not anyone is working it, the way a pressure gradient runs whether or not something is in its path. Harmonizing with one adds nothing to a practitioner. It phase-locks the Aether Shell so that Essence passing outward is shaped by that Wellspring's law before it reaches the world, which is why the same stat harmonized to two different Wellsprings behaves in two entirely different ways at the same magnitude. There are sixty Wellsprings in total, sorted into eight Families by the real physical domain each one answers to: Caloria governs heat, Fulguria electricity and light, Vectoria force and motion, Spatium distance and position, Materia matter itself, Vitalia biology, Fluxia fluids, and Limina entropy, void, and mind. Most people awaken three to five Wellsprings across a lifetime. Serious adepts harmonize with a dozen or more. Nobody a reader is likely to meet has all sixty. A Wellspring also has sites in the world where its Aether pools, and common speech calls a worked site a Core (no kin to the Essence Core inside the Crystal). A worked Core recharges and has keepers, and its keepers can close it. The soldier's way to beat an enemy fed by one is to take the fight off rich ground, onto salted, bled or thin ground: salted ground has been worked by hedge-workers so that Aether will not pool on it, and bled ground has been pulled empty.

A Wellspring's danger arrives from one of three directions. A Wild encounter acts on physical material already present, reshaping stone or metal that was already there. A Spirit encounter generates its effect from inside the practitioner's own Crystal and projects it outward, so the landscape looks ordinary and the practitioner's own body is the thing being authored. A Medium encounter works at the Veil itself, in both directions at once, which is why a river or a beam of light can leave a person changed by the mere act of working with it. None of this is invented by the practitioner. A glyph, the boundary a practitioner sets, creates nothing: the law was already running, and the glyph only moves where its edge falls.

A working itself has a fixed grammar underneath every named art in the world: a Family (what kind of phenomenon), a Method (how the practitioner relates to it, encoded in the discipline's own suffix, such as -mancy for perception or -kinesis for direct control), an Ability (the internalized platform, invisible on its own), and a Technique (the specific, repeatable thing a person actually does, with a stated cost). And every working has to be written into something. There are four media, called the Crafts, and one name the Accord lays over all of them. Spellcraft is the spoken or gestured working, fast, cheap to learn, and held in open contempt by every guild precisely because it requires no years of training. Runecraft is the cut, permanent working, the highest-status craft in the world, outliving the person who made it and misfiring for as long as nobody finds the error. Draftcraft is the poured working, a technique stabilized into a bottle that anyone can carry, sell, tax, or drink, and it is how the overwhelming majority of ordinary people touch magic at all. Chantcraft is the sung working, sustained rather than instant or permanent, the only craft that cannot be practiced by one person alone, and the one the Accord has never made an examination category. Magicraft is the umbrella term the Accord uses on its examination forms; no working practitioner ever calls their own craft by that name.

Three arts go everywhere with a fighter, and each culture has its own name and its own argument about them. A Guild roll says reinforcement for the aura coat (the constant visible coat of a practitioner's own aura, bought with reserve), Pressure for the weight a strong soul lays on a room, and sensing for the trained read of another person's Essence. Bracket crowds say the shine, the weight and the nose. A Kharven soldier says the hide and the low sky, the Moto say *matoi*, the wearing, and *atsu*, the press, and hold that a coat anyone can see is badly worn, and a Dawi will tell you which shift you are on and what load you are carrying. Ordinary fighters use street words like these for all of it, and scholars use the real terms, and each side thinks the other is missing the point.

## The Ladder

![[Assets/Diagrams/stage-ladder.svg]]

Advancement in this world is called Temperance, and Temperance is subtraction, with nothing accumulated along the way. A practitioner does not train their way up sixteen Stages. They survive something that structurally reorganizes the Crystal, and the Stage that results is built out of whatever was actually in the Crystal at the moment it broke and reformed. Nine souls in ten will live their whole lives inside the first four Stages alone.

The sixteen Stages, in order, are Murmuring, Welling, Ascension, and Flourishing, where almost everyone stays; Splintering, Glory, and Refraction; Transcendence, Invocation, and Realization; Dissonance and Emanation; Principality and Zenith; and finally Revelation and Apex, which sit beyond the fourteen the Codex actually numbers.

Two other counts ride alongside the Stages, and a reader will meet both. Level is the count of how many times a Crystal has meaningfully densified, from 1 to a ceiling of 500 that only a soul at Revelation or Apex ever passes, and past which nobody numbers it, in five Bands of a hundred: Mortal Foundation, Awakened, Sovereign, Mythic, and Absolute. The first four Bands each end at a gate, a Stage the level counter will not pass without, so a practitioner can pile up Level and still be stopped dead for want of a Catalyst. The Tiers of Standing are the Accord's public ladder, the name that travels on a practitioner's silver token: Initiate, Apprentice, Journeyman, and Adept for the first four Stages, one apiece; Expert for Splintering through Refraction; Master for Transcendence through Realization; Grandmaster for Dissonance and Emanation; Archmaster for Principality and Zenith; and Paragon for Revelation and Apex, which the Accord folds into a single rank because a muster roll cannot administer a blank.

Every Threshold between Stages needs two things at once: a measurable floor of accumulated pressure, and a Catalyst Condition, a specific quality of lived experience the Crystal will not accept a substitute for. This is what Pressure actually feels like from the inside. Murmuring is a hum with no direction, weather happening in a body that has not been told there is weather yet. Welling is that hum given a direction with no ability to meter it, so the cup goes over and the linen scorches, and it is caused by nothing gentler than surviving first contact with a Wellspring by accident. Ascension is the first thing a person ever builds entirely out of their own understanding, and nothing else in the Stage matters next to that. Flourishing cannot be reached alone at all; its Catalyst is a genuine bond with another soul, so every stable adult practitioner in the world got there because somebody mattered to them enough to hold weight.

Splintering is the first real crisis: the architecture that has held since Murmuring reaches its limit and has to break to grow, and it breaks along whatever the soul was already carrying badly, whatever happened to cause the break. Glory is the first Stage whose Catalyst is a decision rather than an accident, a choice to accept real cost for something the practitioner values more than their own structural integrity. Refraction is the Stage at which the Crystal becomes an object a person can look at, which means the fracture record Splintering wrote becomes visible and impossible to unsee. From Transcendence onward the world itself starts changing around a practitioner: an unhealed wound becomes load-bearing structure and produces a true Domain; at Invocation the practitioner stops working alone and starts asking Wellsprings, Titans, or Archonic law to co-author the result; at Realization the world itself begins to accommodate what the practitioner has become, and that accommodation never reverses. Dissonance is chosen rather than survived, a deliberate walk toward something that could end the practitioner, met with enough coherence to choose how it is met; Emanation is what radiates outward afterward, when a practitioner's mood becomes an environmental condition for the district around them. Above Principality, advancement is no longer earned in any ordinary sense; Zenith in particular is a recognition in place of a breakthrough, conferred rather than reached. Revelation and Apex lie past all of it, in territory where the Codex itself declines to number them at all.

Every Threshold also leaves a residue called the seat. A clean seat means the melt cooled around agreeing evidence and the practitioner's output reads as one consistent signature. A rough seat means it cooled around contradictions, and rough-seated practitioners are unpredictable rather than weak, which is exactly why some of the most feared people in the world cannot be read in advance. A coil reads a seat in under a minute, and what it is actually reading, honestly and without prejudice, is what somebody's family could afford them.

## The Soul Crystal and What Things Cost

![[Assets/Diagrams/stacking.svg]]

Nothing in this system is free, and the system does not pretend otherwise. Essence is spent, never summoned from nowhere. Domains bleed their holder. Traits fatigue with use. Pushing past a sustainable limit, called Overchannel, leaves a permanent scar rather than a temporary one. Any practitioner who tells you a technique costs nothing is either lying or has not yet been sent the bill.

The market has one honest product and one dishonest use for it. An Essence Draft, a working stabilized into a drinkable substance, can genuinely raise a practitioner to the quantitative floor a Threshold requires, and licensed, legitimate use of Drafts is the backbone of the Accord's entire apothecary trade. What no Draft has ever done, and what no compound ever rendered will do, is supply the Catalyst Condition itself. A practitioner pushed past their sustainable grade without having actually met the Catalyst does not skip the work. Their Crystal begins fracturing at an accelerating rate until either the gained stat collapses or the Threshold is forced through outright crisis, and whoever comes out the other side is built from whatever was in the Crystal in that moment of forced reorganization, fear included. The most potent Drafts, made from magical creatures, carry the clearest version of this cost: Identity Contamination, in which the Essence taken carries the identity it was taken from, and enough of it leaves a practitioner unable to say cleanly which convictions are actually their own.

Costs are not only personal. They are civic. Under the round-fees described above, a household or a workshop is billed for its lamps, wards and warm stones the way it would be billed for water, and the first question in any scene involving a working is who is paying for it. At the very top of the scale, the handful of rites keyed directly to a Titan rather than to a Wellspring bill in currencies no market sells: one takes small memories from everyone standing nearby, caster included; another ages every innocent caught inside its effect at a forced rate; only a single one of the fourteen costs the caster nothing at all, and every order that studies it treats that single clean entry as the most dangerous line in the whole register, on the theory that a cost which lands nowhere has simply not been found yet.

## The Houses and Powers a Reader Meets

![[Assets/Diagrams/three-grounds.svg]]

### The Moto

The Moto are the archaic bloodline behind more of this world's crowns than any other family, founded in myth by Bara Moto, the First Epoch given human form, and Mizuki Moto, who turned that authority into something a family could actually inherit. Every Moto carries the Shingan, a sacred ocular inheritance split into four branches: Meigan for revelation, Reigan for the soul and curse-work, Tengan for fate and probability, and Kamigan, the crown eye, which unifies all three into sovereign judgment. The house also carries Hataraki, the deeper governing power authored by Bara before time itself, which is why a Moto technique tends to feel administrative rather than simply violent: a Moto strike can reveal, rank, condemn, or complete a situation rather than merely hit it harder. Of the house's seven great lines, six are landless, scattered across temperate ground after the ancestral seat in the Inner World's western shield was ruined by the first blow on the white shelf, about 310 IC, the breaking the Moto registers call the split. Only the Kōkan Line, the Kamigan-eyed line of crown authority, holds an actual seat, and that seat is Kharven.

### Kharven, the Ashen Crown

Kharven, the Ashen Crown, is a feudal monarchy on frozen ground in the far north of the Inner World, and its city, Kharven-Seat, is older than the king most people credit with it. Its constitution is the cold itself: a climate that kills the unprepared and rewards nothing but endurance, preparation, and showing up when called. *Later hand:* Muken Moto, a Kōkan practitioner, took its crown and did not build it. He conquered the region in fourteen months and spent sixteen years turning a country of feuding clans into a functioning state. Six Banner Houses hold personal oaths to whoever wears the crown rather than to the crown as an institution, which is why the kingdom fractured completely on the night the Tenrai, a rival Great Line of his own house, came for him in a coup, and the oaths, unrenewed, simply expired. Kharven's queen consort, Ayame Yuno, came from the Kagura Branch of House Yuno on Yasoshima, a confederation of family groups governed by open assembly with no king and no standing army. Her ritual architecture, the Saishiki, once gave the northern clans' oaths real spiritual weight; her death left only the ceremony, and Kharven's oaths have been words alone ever since.

### The Guild Accord

The Guild Accord is the institution that makes the Moto's kind of authority answerable to something other than itself. It is the communion of guilds, academies, and orders bound by the Concord Codex, a treaty that asks for a fragment of a member's own Soul Crystal in exchange for a portable rank recognized in every member kingdom, and the communion is still being signed, one circle at a time. Five open Divisions run it, Strategic, Enforcement, Arbitration, Research and Archives, and Logistics and Supply, plus a sixth, the Black Concord, that the Codex does not publicly admit exists. Its culture is summarized in one line carved into every guild hall gate in every realm it reaches: Balance before Dominion. The Accord is not a sovereign. It holds no territory in the old quarters and cannot hang a kingdom's subject; the only necks it answers for are its own sworn members', whom its Tribunal Marshals execute under the Codex. In the New World, chartered from nothing, its halls are the only law there is. Everywhere else its real power is that its courts are faster and more predictable than anyone else's, which is usually enough.

### Eresse

Eresse, before it fractured into the smaller houses a reader meets today, was the High Empire of the Vey-Elarin, the first people the goddess Florwyn sang into being and the first to be given Wellsprings at all, on the doctrine that the land is a relative rather than a resource. Five Royal Houses answered to a throne that ruled by ratification rather than decree, and the whole civilization's most treasured racial trait, Domain Stability, was paid for in a war against a corruption that moved through the Wellsprings themselves rather than arriving as an army. The doctrinal splits born in that war are still open wounds in every Eressean successor state a story is likely to visit.

### The Dawi

The Dawi are eight branches descended from one Titan, Varūn the Deepwright, who taught that the universe requires endurance rather than strength. Three deep lineages, the High Dwarves, the Barrel Dwarves, and the Deepkin, held the ancestral halls; five more, the Kuntur Reach, the Tallow Clans, the Windward Herds, the Tower-Kin, and the Passwardens, were scattered by later collapses and had to reinvent what endurance meant on ground that shared nothing with a mountain's interior. Stannvaard, the Guild Seat that supplies Kharven's metalwork and rations its grain shipments with calculated precision, belongs to this world.

### The Keth-Gorrum

The Keth-Gorrum is the open parliament of the Hobgoblin Expanse, a volcanic archipelago of forty-three islands settled by goblinoid peoples driven entirely out of the Old World generations ago. It has no king, no warlord, and no hereditary seat of any kind, only one hundred and twelve delegates from Hobgoblin, Bugbear, and Tinker Goblin communities, sitting under a charter carved into a cliff face, with seven hundred years of parliamentary records behind it. It never signed the Concord Codex and trades with the Accord without belonging to it.

### The New World

The New World is the East, a live volcanic arc at the far end of the continent, still building itself, rich in surface gold, silver, and a light, corrosion-proof metal called Cloudmetal that only the eastern arc can produce, and almost entirely free of the Old World's dependence on fixed arrays, because its Wellspring sites actually move as the ground beneath them shifts. It runs on its own calendar, separate from the Inner World's, and its politics have not yet crossed the eastern rise to touch the rest of this codex's world. What has come this far is a name: Ilthára Korvaeth, called by her enemies the Mad Queen, whose war in that far country is, at bottom, a fight over whether a name can be held as property. It is a story still being written, at an end of the continent this codex otherwise leaves alone.

## The Archons, the Titans, and the Three Epochs

This is how the world explains itself to itself, and it is worth reading as belief rather than as fact, because the world's own record-keepers say so. Before there were three planes there was Omnara, totality before it needed compartments. An event no surviving record explains, called the Division, split that totality into three currents, and from those three currents came the three planes and three sovereign intelligences called the Epochs: Bairos, who authored causality and sequence and gave the Plane of Fate its shape; Aegor, who gave form weight and mass and built the Physical Plane out of it; and Ashura, who gave awareness the capacity to feel, and is the reason the Soul Plane can hold memory at all. From the tension between the three, fourteen Archons condensed into what the world calls the Pantheon of Abstract Dominion: a Primordial Dyad of Order and Chaos, and six paired principles beneath it, Form against Time, Memory against Horizon, Will against Desire, Insight against Dream, Motion against Stillness, and Harmony paired with Renewal. Archonic Magic sits between the geologic authority of the Titans and the everyday currents of the Wellsprings, and every spell, Domain, and godlike ability in this world is required to trace its permission back to one of those three strata.

The Fourteen Titans are a different kind of thing entirely, and the distinction is treated as important rather than pedantic. An Archon is a principle enthroned, with a court, a doctrine, and people who pray to it. A Titan is a principle bearing weight, with no theology worth the name and an enormous amount of engineering built around it instead: nobody prays to the Titan of gravity, they build to it. The fourteen are seated across four orders, the Physical (Atla'zon and Gaia), the Spiritual Seat (Omnis, Florwyn, Dazkar, Nysera, Sylorin, and Ilgrimm), the Strata (Valmir, Kaldrion, and Chronaeus), and the Abyssal Depths (Prometheus, Zar'thuun, and Vohrin), and a small number of rites keyed directly to them exist at the absolute outer edge of what a mortal Crystal can survive holding.

But the world's own institutions are honest, in their own dry way, about how much of this is verified. The Guild of Measurewrights, the body that corrected the historical calendar, drew a hard line between the two kinds of number in this section. The Ages, from Year minus fourteen hundred to the present, are counted and attested by physical evidence, and every date attached to the Guild Accord, its Codex, and its Divisions falls inside that count and is trustworthy as written. The Epochs, and everything about Omnara, the Division, the Titans, and the Archons that comes with a duration attached, are cosmological rather than historical: no calendar existed to record them, no observer stood outside them to mark their passage, and the Guild's own finding is that the old figures are accurate as statements of scale and meaningless as statements of years. This is not treated as a demotion. A bloodline that claims descent from an Epoch is making a claim about origin, and that claim survives the correction untouched. It is simply, and permanently, a different conversation than the one the history books are having.

## A Short Glossary of Forty Terms

**Aether**: the infinite ocean of raw magical potential. It belongs to no one and is never used up.

**Essence**: Aether a practitioner has pulled in, refined in the Soul Crystal, circulated, and stamped with their own identity. Finite, and only ever as much as has been earned.

**Soul Crystal**: the organ every practitioner casts through, with three layers, one per plane.

**Essence Core**: the Soul Crystal's soul-plane layer. Holds identity, memory, and Traits.

**Aether Shell**: the Soul Crystal's material-plane layer. Converts inner truth into outward force.

**Attraction Layer**: the Soul Crystal's fate-plane layer. Governs bonds, harmonizations, and Domains.

**Wellspring**: one of the sixty fixed types of Aether, each affecting the Soul Crystal differently and carrying a fixed physical law, a source of power that also represents an alchemical principle and a spiritual transformation. Sorted into eight Families.

**Family**: one of the eight domains a Wellspring belongs to: Caloria, Fulguria, Vectoria, Spatium, Materia, Vitalia, Fluxia, Limina.

**Harmonization**: phase-locking the Aether Shell to a Wellspring's law. Retunes the conduit; adds nothing new.

**Trait**: a law the soul itself carries permanently, held in the Essence Core rather than borrowed from a Wellspring.

**Temperance**: the sixteen-Stage ladder of advancement. Subtraction and reorganization, with nothing accumulated.

**Stage**: one of the sixteen structural reorganizations a Crystal undergoes under pressure.

**Level and Band**: Level counts how far a Crystal has densified, from 1 to a ceiling of 500 that only Revelation and Apex pass, uncounted; the five Bands are its hundreds: Mortal Foundation, Awakened, Sovereign, Mythic, Absolute.

**Tier Grade**: a practitioner's raw output magnitude, lettered from Hollow through F, E, D, C, B, A, S, SS, SSS, X, and EX, to EX+.

**Aether Class**: how cleanly a practitioner's Shell delivers their output, in seven Classes.

**Catalyst Condition**: the specific lived experience a Crystal requires before it will authorize a Threshold. Cannot be bought.

**Threshold**: the moment a Stage transition actually occurs.

**Domain**: a standing region of the world operating on a practitioner's own terms while they are present in it.

**The Seat**: the residue a Threshold leaves behind. Clean if it cooled around agreement, rough if it cooled around contradiction.

**Essence Draft**: a working stabilized into a drinkable substance, so someone whose Crystal has never woken can still be affected by it.

**Identity Contamination**: the risk of a creature-sourced Draft: the identity it was rendered from bleeding into the drinker's own.

**The Guild Accord**: the supreme coalition of guilds and orders bound by the Concord Codex.

**Concord Codex**: the treaty binding every Accord member; asks for a fragment of the member's own Soul Crystal in exchange.

**Concord Lattice**: the living record that carries every Accord member's deeds, permanently.

**High Concordant**: the Accord's supreme head, whose soul is woven into the Heart Vault.

**Tiered Path**: the Accord's nine Tiers of Standing, read off a practitioner's coil reading: Initiate, Apprentice, Journeyman, Adept, Expert, Master, Grandmaster, Archmaster, Paragon.

**The Black Concord**: the Accord's sixth, unacknowledged Division.

**Commission**: a sanctioned Accord mission, classified by Threat Tier and by purpose.

**Core**: in common speech, a worked Wellspring site where one type of Aether pools. It recharges, and its keepers sell hours at it and can close it. The survey keeps "Wellspring Core" for its top density band, and the Essence Core is the Crystal's own layer.

**Parish stone**: a public street-level working point, cut by a ward-cutter and kept charged by a warden.

**Company**: the chartered monopoly, one to a trade in each city or quarter, licensed by the Accord, that holds the Core rights and sets the fees.

**Botch**: illegal, unsealed work done with a stolen glyph, robbing a lawful company of its fee.

**Spellcraft, Runecraft, Draftcraft, Chantcraft; Magicraft**: the four Crafts, the media a working can be written into (the spoken working, the cut working, the poured working, and the sung working), and the Accord's umbrella term over them.

**Parun**: the fixed grammar every working's "sentence" is composed in, regardless of which Craft carries it.

**Glyph**: a boundary condition set on an already-running Wellspring law. Creates nothing new.

**Omnara**: the totality that existed before the Division, before there were three planes at all.

**The Division**: the unexplained event that split Omnara into the three planes and the Three Epochs.

**The Three Epochs**: Bairos, Aegor, and Ashura, the sovereign intelligence of the Plane of Fate, the Physical Plane, and the Soul Plane.

**The Fourteen Archons**: the crowned principles administering meaning between Titanic Law and Wellspring Law.

**The Fourteen Titans**: load-bearing cosmic laws with no theology, maintained by engineering rather than worship.

**The Veil**: the threshold running between all three planes wherever their laws border each other.

**The Withering**: the unexplained, decades-long decline in the West's ambient Essence, ongoing since Year 645 In Concordance.

## X. Techniques

**HARMONY (Signature Technique)**

Through her palm or an embrace, Emira gives her own Essence to support the living body and steady the person's existing power. Her working gives Calamity structure and direction without suppressing or purifying it; the same contact carries the vitality with which she restores a crushed arm and drives exhaustion back. The pulse thickens beneath her hand with a low double beat. The cost comes out of her own strength and reserve: she can finish a healing on legs that will not hold her steadily. The regulation is temporary, although it has lasted longer than she expected and kept rage from going over into Obsession. It does not remove the power she is helping somebody bear. Before contact, distance prevents the working; during it, breaking her contact stops further support; afterward, renewed strain can exhaust the temporary regulation, and healed flesh has no immunity to another injury.

**Bonded weapon** · none

## XIII. Physical Description

As a child she had a round face and dark eyes, and a small body that froze the way prey animals freeze. Nine years of training have done what nine years do to a body that trains: the hand that catches her brother's wrist is not a child's hand any more, and the grip is iron. She has strong arms and hugs too hard. The cold does not bother her the way it does other women; her cheeks go pink and she does not shiver.

When she heals, the pulse of vitality coming off her palm is thick enough that cold in another man's arm gives ground half an inch.

Her hair is white as washed wool, tied back clear of her hands, with loose strands gathering at her temples. The childhood roundness remains at her cheeks, beneath dark eyes that settle first on somebody else's discomfort. Her shoulders and forearms are strong from training and lifting people. A plain wool coat allows room across the back; beneath it, her linen sleeves are rolled above the wrists and worn soft at the folds. Small healed nicks mark her fingers.

## XIV. Psychology

**Voice block**

**Speaker type** · A young bedside nurse, affectionate with family and firm when somebody needs handling.

**Notices first** · The body in front of her: who is hurt, who is breathing wrong, who has not eaten, which bones are carrying too much. She reads a room by who in it needs a hand.

**Sentence length** · Medium and warm, running on through "and" and "besides" until it trails off. At a sickbed it snaps short: instructions, one after another, with no room for argument.

**Contractions** · sometimes: they come easily when she teases or pleads, and drop away when she argues a point of belief.

**Never says** · That anyone owes her, or that she owes anyone. Help is never a payment in her mouth, and she will not call it one even when it is offered back.

**Stumbles** · Often: she trails off mid-thought, softens a hard point with "no offense, of course", and stacks one question on another before the first is answered.

**Gloss rights** · Never.

**Under stress** · The speech shortens to a name called out and a hand already on the person; her fear rides out as questions, fast and stacked (what if you get hurt, who would pick you up).

**In grief** · She is nine again on the exile road: short, small questions asked of the nearest grown woman, "Are they talking about him?", "What did he do?"

**In joy** · She is not a guarded voice and does not try to be one. She laughs, teases, hugs too hard and cries without apology, and the words come faster than she can shape them.

## Lore

### The Life Behind the Card

#### Origin

She is Muken Moto's own daughter, the sister of Sodoku, Tomuka and Ezo and of other brothers, born to Ayame Yuno. At three she squirmed in her mother's lap while Ayame brushed her hair in the east wing. On the corridor night she was four, and awake nearby, and heard the sound her mother made. Her father's hand, in her memory, meant warmer things than the hands that came after.

#### The Making

She went into exile with her brother. In the fifth year, at nine, she slept behind locked inn doors, and on the road east Yoko Mishiro carried her and kept her close. She asked Yoko whether her brother had done what the town said he had done, and Yoko told her the truth. Sodoku has told her since that she was not raised by their parents.

She was not taught to battle, and she kept up her training anyway, the body and the magic both. At the north gate of Kharven she caught Sodoku by the wrist and asked to come with him, and offered her hands to the rebuilding. *"It's not because I'm doing this as a debt, I'm doing this because our father would've done the same."* He told her she was right about the debt and wrong about which of them was carrying it. At his table she argued that the Church's faithful be let keep their faith and be treated as harmony, not as something alien.

#### The Cost

At Kharven, with Kujo in the field, she found one of her brothers sunk in despair and pulled him out of it with a hug that nearly suffocated him. What came out of her there was harmony. It did not suppress his Calamity or purify it. It gave it structure and direction, restored his crushed arm and drove his exhaustion back. Kujo watched and named it: *"Harmony without suppression. Regulation without sterilization."* He told her she was not a fighter, and that this was good, and to keep developing it.

She told him it would not be permanent. It held longer than she said, and later kept his rage from going over into Obsession. When he asked her to heal Selvara she went, and put more into it than she had, and left on legs that would not hold steady: *"Don't get yourself killed, otherwise Sodoku is going to kill me."*

#### Where They Stand

After Hild died in the cistern doorway, Sodoku worked eighteen hours a day and slept five. In the third month of it she told Yoko she could no longer find the place where her brother ended and the work began. She had gone looking for it eleven times and had not found it, and she was not going to look again, because looking had begun to feel like an accusation.

Later, fetching her brother from a room where the empire was being named, she stayed, and offered the Kharven word for the mere under the ridge: *white-mere*, a thing gone quiet and not finished. She said it once and did not argue for it. Lambert wrote it down as Whitemere. Brida Ashwell has said twice, in private, that Emira is better at the work than Brida was at thirty.

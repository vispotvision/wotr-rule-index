# The Moto Narration Register

*The house register · how a scene narrated from a Moto point of view sounds*
> The Moto court is narrated in a more antique register than the plain default. **The old register comes from sentence shape, not from old words:** the diction stays plain, and the age is in the order things arrive, the counting, and what goes unsaid. Everything below governs any scene narrated from a Moto point of view. Seen from outside, through another man's eyes, a Moto is read by that man's register and not this one.

---

## Vocabulary

**Words it reaches for.** Count, number, order, weight, read, entered, arrangement, fault, cost, whole, correct, the smaller party. Plain military and household nouns: harness, ruts, flank, gatehouse, cistern, stack, broth. Exact figures (eleven, forty feet, four hundred paces, eleven seconds, eleven degrees). The Kharven Inventory's words, because the house sits on Kharven ground: the stack, the Thin Weeks, the death-house, wet wood, the Sky.
**Words it keeps out.** Named feelings beyond one word, and usually not that one. Abstract Latinate nouns for the self (anguish, despair, resolve). Any single Archon asked for anything. Modern words of any kind. Contractions, in narration and in speech.
**Its signature phrase.** "The whole of it." Seiji: "That is the whole of it and it has been the whole of it for eleven years." Sodoku: "That is the whole of the fault and it is mine." The narration: "That was the whole of what Sodoku had sent." A Moto closes a judgement by bounding it. The phrase is the prose form of "the offence is inaccuracy": a thing correctly read has edges.

## Sentence shape

- Opens on fragments of sense, without a verb, then one short declarative.
- Runs long clauses joined by "and," one after another, with the reason trailing behind in a "which" clause.
- Drops into general present tense inside past narration for a law of the world ("Nothing that size runs and nothing that size needs to.").
- Ends a run on a sentence of four to eight words.
- Asks no questions, in narration or in dialogue.

## What it notices

Temperature before anything else. Then weight, order and count: who moved first, how many went down together, how long a thing took. Hands and where they go. Silence, and who fills it. What a thing costs and who pays. Which party to an arrangement is the smaller.

## What it never says

It never names its own pleasure, triumph or grief outright; it records their absence. It never petitions. It never exclaims. It never explains a man's feeling for him when his hands have already shown it. It never uses a simile from outside a working life: comparisons come as "the way a man does" some ordinary thing.

---

## The rules

**1. Senses first, sight last, and the cold before all of them.** Arrivals open on a run of verbless fragments: temperature, then smell, then sound. Sight arrives only after the body has taken the place in.
> "Cold coming up off the road through the soles. Grease and old leather where the gorget sat under his jaw."
**2. The body moves before the reason arrives.** The action is written first and complete; the reason, if it comes at all, comes afterward, in a separate clause or a separate paragraph.
> "and there was a rider forty feet away and coming, and the sword was already out of Sodoku's scabbard and low along his right leg with the edge turned out."
**3. Parataxis: clauses laid side by side with "and," and none of them ranked.** The chain carries escalation without the words "because" or "therefore," so the reader draws the consequence.
> "*He will do it. He will do exactly it, and he will do it while she argues with him, and she will argue with him, and he will not move.*"
**4. Everything is counted.** Numbers are exact and given flat. Time is counted, not felt; the count is a discipline the POV keeps even while blind.
> "The read cost him eleven seconds. He knew the number because he counted while he was blind, which is a habit and had been a habit since he was fifteen, and at nine he heard the horse."
**5. Silence is an act, and the Moto lets others fill it.** A Moto waits, and the waiting is written as its own sentence. What another man says into the silence, and in what order, is read as the message.
> "Sodoku waited." And after it: "A man arriving with news will give it in his own order if you leave the silence alone, and the order he gives it in is half the message."
**6. Judgement is bounded: "the whole of it."** A verdict is stated once, closed, and owned. When the fault is the POV's own, it is named in his own voice with no self-pity and no appeal.
> "*A man who thinks in walls does not think of the door being opened from inside. That is the whole of the fault and it is mine and it has cost us nothing today, which is the only reason I am able to look at it.*"
**7. Feeling is recorded as absence.** The register names what is not there instead of what is. Satisfaction appears only as the sentence that says there was none.
> "That was the second thing Sodoku understood and it arrived a half-beat behind the first, and there was no pleasure anywhere in it."
**8. Speech is full-form, courteous, and gives nothing.** No contractions. Short sentences. Politeness carries the threat. From outside, a Moto answers pleasantly and at length and tells you nothing.
> Seiji: "They will believe two of them, which is better. A man who believes everything reconsiders. A man who believes half of it commits."
> And from Wren's side: "I have watched him give an instruction that ended a village, and the instruction was four words and one of them was please."
**9. Proverbs arrive uninvited, in someone else's voice.** Received wisdom does not get quoted on purpose. It comes up out of the POV in the voice of the person who first said it, with the place and the dead named.
> "His grandmother had said it about men who could not be relied on and about the fires they made. She had said it in a doorway in a house that no longer stood, with her hands in a bowl, and she had been dead forty years, and it came up out of him now with no invitation and in her voice."
**10. The world is weighed as arrangements and parties.** Bonds, debts and orders are thought of in the language of terms: who agreed, who is the smaller party, what was said aloud and what was not. People are filed by what they did.
> "and Sodoku had never once let himself forget which of them was the smaller party to the arrangement."
> "and Sodoku watched him do it and put the boy's name into the part of his head where he kept the ones who came back."

## A Sample in the Register

*The Bow at the Seat's table, before bread.*
> Cold in the floor of the hall, through the boot and into the heel. Seal fat from the lamp. A rye heel on the board, and nobody at the table would touch it yet.
> 
> Sodoku stood at the end of the table and made the Bow.
> 
> Fourteen inclinations, in the order they came up, and the table waited on them because the bread waited on them. Urion first. Then Uurgath, whom the house did not like and bowed to all the same, since a thing that devours is still owed its place in the count. He kept the depth of each one level. His father had kept it level, and Muken after him, and there was no merit in it, which was the point of it. A bow deepened for one of the fourteen is a petition with its mouth shut.
> 
> At the ninth his right hand went to the beads and he let it stay there.
> 
> The jade was cold. The iron was colder. His father's thumb had worn a flat on the third bead from the knot, and his own thumb found it without being sent.
> 
> *Eleven hundred years across the water, and this is what crossed. A cold room and a man bending fourteen times before bread.*
> 
> *It is enough. It has to be.*
> 
> He finished at Elyndra and straightened.
> 
> The bread went round.

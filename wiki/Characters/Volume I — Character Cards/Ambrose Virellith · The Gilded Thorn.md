## VIII. Traits

**BEAUTY MEANT TO BREAK (Primary Trait)**

Their presence lowers the emotional defences of those nearby. **Enemies falter, allies open their hearts. It is both a blessing and a curse.** *Harmonics Empathy at ceiling, and it does not switch off.* Catharsis carries the pressure outward through the Attraction Layer, loosening the effort with which nearby people hold feeling back. The ordinary field reaches three metres; it makes a feeling harder to hide without choosing what the person does about it. A rose-gold light gathers at Ambrose’s throat as the field catches. Its cost is the constant strain of holding other people’s exposed feelings against their own fractured Core, and the same opening reaches friends. Beyond its reach, it cannot lower a person’s defences; a prepared ward across the boundary also contests the outward current.

**WINGS OF NO RETURN (Secondary Trait)**

False wings embedded with razor-thin Aether threads. **Once they extend, their motion cannot be stopped until the wielder drops dead.** Before they open, the threads draw straight between the folded wings and the skin at their roots tightens. The cost is the loss of any safe ending once the motion begins. Their sweep remains tied to Ambrose’s body and the reach of the extended threads; it does not gain unlimited reach from the binding. Someone beyond that sweep is outside the cutting path, and a worked barrier strong enough to bear the threads keeps their edge from the body behind it while the wings continue moving.

**LACEBIND CURSE (Secondary Trait)**

**Anyone who harms Ambrose is marked by a subconscious romantic fixation that turns to guilt-ridden madness.** *Mirithane returning the act undistorted — the harmer is not cursed with love. They are shown exactly what they did and cannot introduce distortion into the memory.* At the marking, the harmer sees their own movement reflected in Ambrose’s eyes, even from an angle that should show something else. Keeping the act clear makes Ambrose carry its memory too, adding to the strain on their fractured Core. The mark holds the act that caused it; it supplies no account of events Ambrose never experienced. A sustained S-Grade Resilience Ward can screen the mark’s forced replay while it is held, although the mark and the person’s ordinary memory remain. The ward costs its bearer effort and reserve, and the replay returns when that protection fails.

**SILENT ARIA (Passive Trait)**

They do not speak, **but their body emits a harmonic frequency that alters battlefield morale. Despair and awe bloom in equal measure.** The tone comes from the body without words or a moving mouth, high and clear with a second note that will not quite settle beside it. Mirithane carries feeling on that repeated sound. Its cost is low, constant drain through the Shell and the unwanted exposure of Ambrose’s own distress; they cannot reserve its effect for one side of a battle. A closed barrier that stops the sound stops this Trait reaching the listener, while the separate close presence of Beauty Meant to Break still acts within its own reach.

## IX. Domain

**THE UNGUARDED ROOM (Domain)**

The Second Release opens a true Domain centred on Ambrose, six metres from their body in every direction. Catharsis carries Beauty Meant to Break through the enclosed air while Mirithane keeps the feeling being exposed faithful to the person who carries it. Those inside find their own grief, longing or fear harder to keep hidden; the field cannot give them a new memory, compel an answer or choose an action for them. Silver-grey and rose-gold light marks its edge, and the Aria holds throughout it. It forms with the Second Release’s visible flare, survives small interruptions and holds for two hours from a full reserve when maintained alone. That release pays its whole maintenance cost. Ambrose may end it deliberately; it ends at the release floor, loses ground to stronger Dominion, and collapses if their sustaining control is broken. Its boundary can be crossed normally. It travels with them and claims no permanent territory.

## X. Techniques

**WINGS OF NO RETURN (Signature Technique)**

**Effect** · The wings open and the threads go out, and from that instant the sequence runs to completion. **There is no pause, no reconsideration, and no version where Ambrose changes their mind partway.** Elegant, silent, finished. **Cost** · **It cannot be stopped until the wielder drops dead** — and *the wielder* in the original binding language meant Ambrose, not the target. *Nobody has established which reading the working itself uses.* **Limit** · Once extended, committed. **Counter** · Do not be in the arc, and do not harm them — *the Lacebind is a worse outcome than the wings.* **What nobody knows.** Whose memories the chrysalis was layered from. The wing roots lift before the threads spread, giving away the opening while the cutting movement itself stays silent. Before extension, someone already leaving the arc can clear it; during the working, distance beyond the threads’ reach denies contact without stopping their motion; after contact, separation behind a worked barrier able to bear the threads prevents another pass, but undoes nothing already cut. No safe timed ending is part of this working, and neither release releases the wing binding.

### The Life Behind the Card

*Written 2026-09-24 by the character-lore pass from this card and the record of The sainted, the fallen and the vessel. What those sources do not state is new here.*

#### Origin

The Violet Court made Ambrose Virellith by its forbidden Aethercraft, out of a chrysalis of layered memories. The House of Virellith, a violet-blooded noble line of the Inner World, held them bound. They carry its name.

Their body was real from the start, and their pain was more real still. Their sense of who they were came in fragments. Nobody knows whose memories went into the chrysalis. The Marionettist Order read what Ambrose was built on and found it whole, with no origin anywhere in it. On that reading the Order produced them, and afterwards it controlled them through Aetheric implants.

Ambrose was neither male nor female. The Court raised them for one office. They were to enchant enemy generals and then kill them, silently, with elegance. They were dressed in silk and shaped into a living work of war art, and a royal house, long fallen now, brought them out as its ceremonial war figure.

#### The Making

The office worked on the generals it was built for. Ambrose's presence lowered the defences of everyone near them, and it could not be switched off. Enemies faltered. Allies opened their hearts. Generals within reach of Ambrose had lowered their guard before the blade came.

They never spoke. Their body gave off a harmonic tone in place of a voice, and across a battlefield it moved the ranks to despair and awe in equal measure. Their wings were false ones, laced with razor-thin threads of Aether. Once those wings opened, their motion could not be stopped until the wielder dropped dead.

Over time the essence bindings weakened. Then the Violet Palace burned. Ambrose fled during the burning, walking barefoot through the ash in a ceremonial dress meant only to be worn in death.

They walked out of the Violet Palace in a dress meant for a corpse. They kept wearing it.

#### The Cost

Since the fire Ambrose has travelled the Inner World, aiding people in pain and never speaking of their own. The presence the Court built to open a general's guard opens the guard of anyone they sit beside. It was made to point outward at people who had not consented to it. It still does. Everyone Ambrose sits with is opened without being asked, and Ambrose keeps sitting with them.

Whoever harms them is marked. The harmer is shown the act exactly as it was done and cannot stop looking at it. What follows is a fixation that turns, in the end, to guilt-ridden madness.

Azure Serathiel was once ordered to execute them. He spared them. No record names who gave the order, and the two have not met again. Every new moon Ambrose leaves a violet rose on his path.

Ambrose went back to the burned Violet Palace once, and Emilia Veirynthiel was already in the ruin, pipe lit. One mirror had come through the fire, and the two of them stood before it together. Neither looked. In the smoke of the pipe Emilia gave Ambrose a name, The Beautiful Scar That Forgot Its Name, and Ambrose kept it. They left the mirror hanging where it was. Ambrose walked out of the palace a second time in the dress meant for the dead.

One line is kept in their name: "I do not mourn who I was. I mourn that they made me forget who I could've been." Some circles of the Midveil Deathweavers revere Ambrose as a silent martyr. Elsewhere they are mistaken for a spirit.

#### Where They Stand

Ambrose Virellith wanders the Inner World as an Echo-Borne, still in the dress.

Remnants of the Marionettist Order hunt them. The Red Alchemist seeks to restore them to their weaponized state, since he believes them a lost relic of perfect warfare. Ambrose evades him with sorrowful precision. The Order believes a relic can be restored, and Ambrose does not know whether that is true.

In the halls of a ruined shrine among the ruins of Elenthyr, Ambrose once met Evangelien Rhymaris. She was walking in her wedding garments with blood on her face from the tears, and the hymn that follows her was in the halls. Each bowed to the other. Neither spoke. They passed, and each went on the way the other had come. All who look on Evangelien see someone they mourned, and no record says whom Ambrose saw.

Other people keep readings of Ambrose that Ambrose has never seen. The Guild Accord's alchemical division kept a file on the relic of the Violet Court, and Azelra Kaine read it before she volunteered for the experiments that grafted her soul. A house whose general Ambrose had killed once sent the Obsidian Veil an offer for Ambrose's death. Rhaevan Serathiel turned it down. He taught his student from the file. No record says Ambrose ever learned of the file or the offer.

Nobody knows whose memories the chrysalis was layered from. In the original binding language of the wings, the wielder who must drop dead meant Ambrose. Nobody has established which reading the working itself uses.

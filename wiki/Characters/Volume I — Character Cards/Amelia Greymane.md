## VIII. Traits

**THE GREY WALL (Primary Trait)**
She took up the shield when nobody wanted her to, and her soul did that before it did anything else: stand in front. While there is someone behind her shield, her Anchoring and Fortitude read a Grade high, and Petralon carries what hits her down through her heels into the ground. It costs nothing she counts. Its limit is the one she said out loud in the tower: she does not know what she is with nobody behind her, and with nobody to guard the Trait goes quiet. Its counter is to take away whoever is behind her; its tell is that she steps in front before she has thought about it.

**ELEVEN NAMES (Secondary Trait)**
Formed on the north face of the Thornwall, the second night. Torvald, Ebbe, Hamund, the two Ashcroft lads, and six more: she says the eleven every night before she sleeps, because she is frightened that if she misses one night she will forget one and he will be gone like he never stood there. While she keeps the count, her line does not break; her Command holds the men beside her where they stand, and fear does not get into the file. Its limit is that it is grief that will not move: she cried for a week when her mother died and then stopped, and did not cry again for six years. Its counter is nothing in a fight; it is a count, not a guard, and it costs her at night.

**NO HOOKS (Passive Trait)**
Muken opened the Debt Recognition Field on her across the low table, the way he would on a stranger in a yard, and it came back with nothing to mark: no appetite, no hostility, nothing with a hook in it, only a solid, quiet, load-bearing weight like a stone in a river. She wants what she says she wants, and says it. Fields and reads that look for hidden want find nothing on her, because there is nothing hidden. It costs nothing. Its limit is that she cannot lie or bluff, and has stopped trying. Its counter is simply to ask her.

## X. Techniques

**THE HOOK AND THE HEW (Signature Technique)** · mark: Drilled
The bearded war-axe does two things, and she does them as one. The beard goes over the rim of a shield, or behind a knee, or round a lance-haft, and she pulls with her whole weight from the heels, and the man comes forward off his feet with his guard open; and the edge comes down on what the pull has opened, collarbone or neck or arm, before he has his feet back. It costs about three per cent of her reserve to set her Anchoring through the pull and the hew, near 2,000 EU, and the hooked spike at the top finishes what the edge did not. Its limit is that the hook needs a rim, a limb or a haft to catch, and a man who lets go of his shield when he feels the beard takes the pull out of it. Its counter is to let go, or carry a shield with no rim to catch.

**THE GREY FILE (School Art, Greymane)** · mark: Drilled
Greymane shields locked rim to rim, the file taking each blow down through every shield into the ground at once, so that a line of Shieldwardens stands like the ridge it is named for. She held the Thornwall's north face with it. It costs the file, not her. Its limit is its ends. Its counter is to break the file's end and roll it up.

**STONEFAST · Setting the Stone (First Release, Greymane)** · mark: Drilled
Called aloud, the house release: she sinks her heels, and her Fixatio and Petralon lock every joint from the shield-boss down to the heel into one column, so that a blow's weight goes down through her into the ground and the ground does not give. The rust-and-gold aura comes up off her shoulders like heat off a forge, and dust and grit settle in a ring round her boots. It costs about four per cent of her reserve to set, near 2,700 EU, and holds as long as she does not move her feet. Its limit is the feet: set, she cannot step. Its counter is to pull her, lift her, or fight her on ice.

## XIII. Physical Description

She is tall, near enough the king's own height, and broad through the shoulders the way a woman gets from carrying a shield for ten years. Her hair is a bright warm gold, cut short and ragged at the jaw so it cannot get in her eyes in a fight, with one thin Shieldwarden plait drawn back from the temple over the ear; the wind gets into it on the road and she shoves it back with a gloved hand. Her face is long and pale, the Greymane face, all straight lines, with a nose that was broken once and set a little to the right, and a thin white scar running from her left cheekbone back toward the ear. Her eyes are heavy-lidded and red as rowan berries after the first frost, and nothing moves in them; nothing moves in her face at all, until something does, and then it is like watching ice crack on a pond. Her left hand is ruined: the knuckles ridged with white scar and lumped where the bones broke and knitted, the two smallest fingers set crooked, bent toward each other like old men leaning together on a bench, a puckered burn the size of a coin on its back, and the palm callused shiny and yellow from the axe-haft. She wears a black high-necked undershirt under a coat of mail, a fitted oxblood leather cuirass over it crossed with buckled straps and hung with knives, a broad belt with round steel bosses and a pouch, a heavy fur mantle across both shoulders with a round steel boss at each, and a long split coat of grey-brown wool to the shins; leather bracers bound with steel, black fingerless gloves, tall boots with steel knee-cops and fur at the tops. A longsword rides at her hip at court. On the ridge and in the line she carries the war-axe across her back, its haft running from her right hip past her left ear, a bearded blade as long as a man's forearm and a hooked spike at the top, the steel dark with oil and the edge a clean bright line. She smells of axe oil and horse and road dust.

## Lore

### Origin

She was born to the Greymanes of the Ridgewall, Wystan's daughter and Godfrey's younger sister, in a house that raises its children to see themselves as stones in a wall. Her mother died when she was fourteen, and she cried for a week, and then stopped. Her father wanted her married into one of the river houses, somewhere safe, and took her to court three times; every time the young lord bowed and took her hand to kiss it, and felt it, and she saw in his face that he had picked up a dead bird. So she stopped going. Her father said a Greymane daughter did not carry a shield on the ridge, and she said then she would carry it somewhere else, and she did, and taught herself the axe.

### The Making

When the Keld came over the north face of the Thornwall on the second night, she had thirty-one Shieldwardens on that section, and held it till the Byakuya archers came up, and walked off it with twenty. She says the eleven names every night before she sleeps. The Shieldwardens started calling her the Grey Wall after that, and men looked at her and saw it and thought she was hard, and she let them, because it was easier. Her father rode south to the Kurenai blade, and she did not cry for him either.

### Where She Stands

Six months after the war, the Shieldwardens are standing about in the Seat's corridors instead of on the ridge, and her brother is sleeping three hours a night because of the king. Godfrey brought her up the tower after three other matches had failed, the Ashbury daughter, the Thornhaven cousin and the river-town widow, and left her at the door. She told the king she would tell her brother he had been kind about it. He told her he did not want to be loved for the title, and she told him she had known him about as long as it takes to boil an egg, and drank his cold tea. He told her what he saw when he looked at people, and she showed him her hand. He took it, carefully, and it did not crack. He said his sister's name aloud for the first time in six months and wept, and she sat down beside him on the window seat and put her cracked shoulder against his, and cried for the first time in six years without making a sound. She would not let him pay for the pauldron. She told him to come down to the yard and spar with her instead, because nobody courts a crown by hitting it with an axe, and Godfrey, Hand to the King, fell into the room when she opened the door. *Not a word.*

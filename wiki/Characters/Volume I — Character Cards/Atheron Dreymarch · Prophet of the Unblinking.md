## VIII. Traits

**UNBLINKING MANTLE (Primary Trait)**

**Unblinking Mantle** · **The embroidered "eyes" on his cloak are not symbolic — they are bound ocular spirits.** *They perceive guilt, lies and hidden truths, projecting them into a dreamer's mind with suffocating clarity.* Anamnesis reads the traces carried by a person and their oath; Judicium separates the sworn terms from the act, and Oneirion carries the finding into the dream. The cloak widens the Attraction Layer’s access to those traces. Its resting watch costs no separate expenditure; a deliberate reading and projection costs 1% of full reserve and leaves the voices of the witnesses in his hearing until the next reading clears them. The embroidered eyes turn together and make a dry sound like cloth pulled over stone. Guilt is read against the oath actually witnessed. A Ward matching or exceeding the read’s Grade prevents a conclusive reading, and a destroyed trace cannot supply missing evidence. Reading too hard can erase the trace being examined.

**OATH-CHAINS (Secondary Trait)**

**Oath-Chains** · **The chains draped on his form are physical representations of spiritual contracts sworn in his presence.** *Breaking such an oath summons Atheron in dreams, where he may imprison the oathbreaker's will in a geas or strip it entirely.* A freely sworn promise fixes the speaker’s identity and terms in his Attraction Layer; the chain carries that record into the dream-ground, where a breach calls him back. Establishing a new chain costs 5% of full reserve. An established chain needs no upkeep; entering a sitting and imposing a fresh judgment spend through The Oath-Bound Dream. The link belonging to the broken promise lifts from his chest and rings before he sleeps. An oath never sworn freely in his presence gives the chain nothing to hold. Waking him ends the active judgment without cancelling the original promise or erasing an already established geas.

**BLINDFOLD OF THE THIRD MOON (Passive Trait)**

**Blindfold of the Third Moon** · **Prevents his gaze from slipping fully into the dream-plane.** The cloth anchors a narrowing of the Aether Shell’s incoming dream-read, while Nyxial holds that narrow opening steady. The standing restraint costs him waking sight and keeps Tempering occupied; it has no separate activation charge. A fine line of lavender light follows the cloth’s lower edge when too many dreams press against it. Moving the blindfold breaks that restraint and floods the opening with the dreams it excluded. It grants no protection against a waking hand reaching the cloth, and a sudden change after sustained pressure can make the restraint overshoot before it settles.

## IX. Domain

**THE OATH-BOUND DREAM (Realm)**

The moonlit field occupies the dream-ground reached by his sworn chains. Its apparent horizon has no end; its authority reaches the sleepers tied to those chains, with Dominion Radius 630 governing the reach. The Moonlit Tribunal opens and powers his active presence there. Each chain preserves the person and promise it witnessed; judgments narrow the dreamer’s choices to those allowed by the sworn terms. Oneirion supplies the possible arrangements, Anamnesis reads the witnessed record, Judicium tests the breach, and Nyxial holds the field steady. A full reserve supports three minutes of an uncontested sitting for one dreamer before the release cutoff, with each additional dreamer multiplying the upkeep. Waking Atheron ends the sitting. A competing Domain can contest its ground, and loss of the dreamer’s oath-trace prevents a new judgment from finding its subject. The ground and established chains persist after the active field closes.

## X. Techniques

**THE OATH-BOUND DREAM (Signature Technique)**

**Effect** · An endless moonlit field where **chains descend from the sky, each link tied to a dreamer's fate.** *Those judged guilty are bound in nightmare until their chain falls, signalling their death.* **Cost** · **The blindfold, permanently.** *Tempering 478 is a man holding a faculty shut for his own survival and everyone else's.* **Limit** · **They must have sworn.** **Counter** · **Swear nothing. Or wake him.** **What nobody knows.** Whether the chains that fall were judged, or merely reached the end of their length. The sworn chain fixes which person and which promise the dream may judge. Within that boundary, the working uses the dreamer’s possible choices to form the confinement or geas; it cannot add a term the dreamer never accepted. The active sitting pays The Moonlit Tribunal’s upkeep, and a fresh sentence costs a further 5% of full reserve. Moonlight shows beneath Atheron’s shut eyelids, and the dreamer hears a long chain descending before the first link touches them. Before contact, the absence of a freely sworn oath bars entry. During contact, waking Atheron ends the sitting. After contact, waking him ends any continuing active imprisonment; a geas already fixed in the dream-ground remains subject to its original terms, and waking him does not undo that settled sentence. A fall in his reserve ends the active sitting before it can pay another sentence. Too many traces overload Judicium’s reading; forcing Nyxial to hold against a stronger field stores a correction that can discharge when the opposition lifts.

### The Life Behind the Card

*Written 2026-09-24 by the character-lore pass from this card and the record of The courts of waking and sleeping law. What those sources do not state is new here.*

#### Origin

Atheron Dreymarch was born in the Concord, in the keeper's rooms of a debtors' gaol. His father kept its night watch. The men in the cells were held for promises they had failed to keep, and they slept badly. From the time the boy could stay awake he sat the watch on a stool by the brazier. His father walked the row with a staff and prodded awake, through the bars, any sleeper who cried out.

Atheron learned the sleepers sooner than his father did. If he watched a sleeping man long enough, he saw the dream, and behind it every road the man's next day could take. One night he said that the debtor at the end of the row was dreaming of the gate. His father sent him to bed. In the morning the cell was empty. By evening the man who had stood surety for the debtor was sleeping in it.

#### The Making

He entered the Arbitration Division as a clerk of the Neutral Chambers, and stood witness when parties swore to keep what they had signed. On the morning after the first oath he witnessed, a chain lay across his shoulders. It did not come off. Another came with every oath sworn in his presence.

The first of those oaths to break summoned him while he slept. He stood in the breaker's dream with the chain in his hand and laid a geas on the man's will. The man kept his terms after that.

His sight widened over those years. One night in the Chambers his gaze slipped fully into the dream-plane. He saw every dream within his reach at once, and every future open to each sleeper, layered one over another. A clerk found him at dawn sitting upright with his eyes open and shook him until he answered. He bound a strip of cloth over his eyes that day. The Blindfold of the Third Moon took its place.

In time the chains stopped needing him. His oath-chains came to persist without him tending them, and to summon him when broken. They held in a place made of sleep, which had agreed to different terms.

#### The Cost

The blindfold was the price, and he paid it for good. The last waking face he saw was the clerk's, bent over him in the grey light. From that day he held the faculty shut, for his own survival and for everyone near him.

He never struck anyone awake. He carried nothing to strike with. Every sentence he passed arrived in sleep, through a chain sworn freely, on someone who had agreed to the terms. He sentenced no one who had not sworn. A man who had sworn nothing, standing in front of him by daylight, met a blindfolded judge who could not fight. Anyone who woke him ended the judgment he sat in.

Then came Altherion. Two carrying houses divided a route through the Outer World's drifting realms on lanes Orivane Caelthys charted between them. The master of one house signed the settlement and swore besides, in Atheron's presence, to keep it. A season later he sent a ship down the other house's lane. The chain summoned Atheron into his sleep, and Atheron laid a geas on his will. The house never used that lane again.

The other house sued in the Altherion Central Concord Tribunal for the carriage it had lost. Lord Maerion Lysandre argued for it: a ruling given in a dream paid no carrier, and dream-law was too abstract for waking politics. Riven Eltamyr Vonnel argued that the geas was a sentence and that no man could be sentenced twice for one breach. Atheron came blindfolded. He said the chain still held. Leontes Praevan read the written settlement, ruled on it and nothing else, and gave the house its carriage. The master went home liable awake and bound asleep. On the tribunal steps Orivane told Atheron that chains can become snares to the self.

#### Where They Stand

Atheron keeps the Neutral Chambers for the Arbitration Division, and Dr. Vaelion Rhest works at their Eastern Node for Research and Archives. He also holds the ancient title of Moon-Oath Binder within the Forgotten Court of Noxthallow, a half-mythic tribunal governing disputes in the dream-realms.

The chains of oaths sworn in his presence hang on him. The master's chain from Altherion is among them, and it has not fallen. Those he judges guilty lie bound in nightmare until their chains fall, and a chain falls when its dreamer dies. Whether the fallen chains were judged, or merely reached the end of their length, nobody knows.

Riven governs the waking law beside him. Elder Seravain, who poisons the mind awake, is still his rival, and Lucien Draeve will not deal with him directly. Atheron keeps away from Sandalphon Aestraen, whose fractured memory-song might twist and snarl the dream-chains. Sandalphon is barred from officiating in the Chambers. The blindfold stays on.

## Description

Short and dense through the torso, built close to the ground in the specific way women are built when their mothers and grandmothers all worked standing up. Round face, wind-chapped, permanently flushed across the cheeks and nose. Hair a colour that cannot decide between brown and red, settling on russet like autumn bracken, pinned in a twist at the skull's base with two bone pins that are not decorative and that she has used for field suturing on one occasion she mentions at meals with cheerful specificity.
Her eyes are hazel, green-brown, and they move across bodies performing assessment before performing greeting. She wears a leather satchel whose buckles have been re-set so many times the leather around the hardware has gone soft and shapeless. She brings willowbark when she has diagnosed something the patient has not noticed yet.
**Her function:** The warmth. In a court defined by Lambert's arithmetic and Osric's strategy and Lorn's honour, Brida is the person who sees the eleven-year-old underneath the Sword Princess. She is the one who reads the jaw-tension on the left side and knows it means the child is holding something too heavy. She is the one who tells Hild to eat and means it and follows up and checks.
**Her voice:** Firm and warm simultaneously. The voice of a thousand delivered instructions, each one as though it were the first, because the recipient needs to hear it as the first. *"The treatment is not negotiable."*

### Lore · The Life Behind the Card

#### Origin

Brida Ashwell was Concord-born, in a frontier settlement, to a family whose surname had been frozen on a charter while it was still the name of a place. The women of that family worked standing up. Her mother and grandmother carried weight from hip to shoulder, and the work built Brida as it had built them. She came out short and deep through the chest, close to the ground, good for twelve hours on her feet.
The nearest physician was days off. The settlement made do with women who had learned what the frontier asked of them, and Brida learned from them in the order it asked. Births came first. Then frostbite, the ruptures of lifting too much for too long, the fevers of the thaw. She knew willowbark before she knew her letters. By the time she could keep a ledger she read bodies across a room before she greeted the person inside them. She quit arguing with what the cold did to her skin at about the age she quit caring for anything that did not bear on a patient's survival.

#### The Making

By the winter Fern Stark died, she was physician to Fern's holdfast in the Ironwood March. The child lay on the floor of the altar room and would not eat or speak, until Lorn Stark put a wooden sword in her hand and told her to stand up. Once Hild was standing, Brida made her eat. She was still repeating the rule years later: training hungry sharpens the first hour and dulls the next four.
Every morning she was in the holdfast kitchen, because the patients came to the food, and she counted the whole holdfast her patients, consenting or not. She brought willowbark for what the patient had not yet noticed. Three times she told Hild that the left side of her jaw tightened first, where anyone who knew how to look could read it. Hild heard her three times. She changed nothing.
She said the worst thing in the kindest voice. At the altar she told the girl the harvest was missed and the granaries held eight weeks, ten on a cut ration. When a runner brought word that Wren Greymane had gone to the Tenrai, she kept a hand on Hild's shoulder through the report and read the pulse under it. Comfort waited for the altar room afterward, where the girl's sentences turned into questions. Hild said her name. Brida said, "I know," and was already moving to the supply cache.
At Kharven-Seat, Hild sent her south with supplies and the Greymane words to Bram's wounded, and the ridge learned her name for the men who stop asking for water: the third bowl. In the Thin Weeks she slept on the pallet in the altar-room with the girl. On the night Renard Greymane killed a Tenrai practitioner in a courier's clothes in the passage outside, she opened the door the width of a hand.
"She's asleep," Brida said. Very quiet. "Don't."
By then she was personal physician to Hild Ice and medical authority of the Kokan Loyalist Coalition, and the only person who told Hild to eat and meant it, and followed up, and checked.

#### The Cost

She had made keeping Hild alive her life's work.
On the day of the Ashgate road, the order given in the yard put Hild below the old cistern with Osric and shut the gatehouse behind them. Brida was at the broken sally gate. Lambert fought there with forty of the household and about ninety Ashwell tenants off the hill farms, men who had never stood in a line. She took a wound across the abdomen in the ninth hour. In that hour Hild Ice died in the cistern doorway Osric had put himself in. The girl was twelve.
Brida lay eleven days in the altar-room with the night-stone ticking as it cooled beside her. She herself called that wound the third bowl. She went into it and came back out, which almost nobody does.
On the morning of the first frost she climbed to the upper burying ground on a stick. Four hundred yards of path took her forty minutes. Lambert stood there before three stones, Hild Ice, Lorn Stark and Osric of Hallenfeld, and she stood beside him a while before she said the true thing.
"It must be hard to be the oldest," she said. "And be the only one alive."
He spoke to his brother's small stone. She put her hand on his arm and left it there, and did not tell him it was not his fault. The eleven days had burned out of her all patience for the sentences people say at gravesides.
Then Robin Ice rode in with word that the Goblin Senate was hanged. Lambert ordered the hostages killed. Robin said aye and turned his horse. Brida watched the boy go down the path. She said Lambert's name, and he said no before she had said anything else, and told her he had heard all of it in the quarter hour she had stood there.
"Your brother would have," she said.
He answered that his brother was a hole in an ordination roll, and that he would still have given the order.
In the spring she stood at the back of the lower hall with her stick while Lambert read the names. The stick meant for a year stayed. Her colour never came back.

#### Where They Stand

She runs rooms sitting down now, from a stool with her leg out.
Twice, in private, she has said that Emira is better at the work than she herself was at thirty. She has not said it in the room. A room has one voice. The Seat sends her to the ones who will not talk, because she says the true thing. She has sutured with the bone pins in her hair once, and tells it at meals.
No record says whether she heard what the dying man said before she opened the door, or whether Renard has told her since.

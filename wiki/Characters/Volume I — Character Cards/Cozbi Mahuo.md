## The Art, Read

He stands turned away and looks back over his left shoulder, as though someone said his name and he is deciding whether it was worth turning for. The hair is black, thick and cut ragged at the brow and nape, and where the light finds it it goes green, a mallard's head, wet pine. The skin is pale with a gray-green cast. The one eye we are given is gold-yellow, heavy-lidded and sidelong. The ear runs long to a point, with gold at the lobe.

He wears a long greatcoat of olive wool, heavily pleated down the back, a gold-fringed epaulette on the right shoulder, and over the shoulders a great mantle of white fur, long spiked guard-hairs with lavender and rose in their shadows. A gold boss of concentric rings sits at the nape with a finial hanging under it; three linked open lozenges cross the upper back in gold, and a column of gold lozenge plaques runs down the left side over a navy-black underlayer. The coat fits exactly and shows no wear anywhere. He is a man who is never caught out in his clothes. Behind him a white blade of light comes down on the diagonal, fringed pink and green.

**The colors.** The Mahuo colors are vermilion and white. He kept the white and refuses the vermilion, which is the color other houses' armies carried into the field over Mahuo banners. The olive is the coat of the Sum-gol Post. He will wear vermilion again on the day Sum-gol is his by his own hand. *(Proposal, pending Isaac.)*

## Who He Is

**What he survived.** He watched his house protected to death. The Moto put Mahuo banners over Moto columns; the Yukari and the Zettari each went to war on the excuse of saving the Mahuo; between the three of them the house was destroyed, and each of the three still tells it differently. He came out of it knowing one thing in the bones: to be carried as a banner is one way to die, and to be protected is another.

**What he believes.** Since the Soul Plane broke, death has been an estate without a steward. Spirits drift, hauntings spread, the tides of life and death run crooked, and every great house in the south argues over who owns the door while nobody carries the dead through it. That work belongs to the Mahuo. The house words were always *Call, and It Answers*, and his reading is that the Mahuo were never the ones made to answer.

**The want, out loud.** That the world recognize the Mahuo as the Order of the Spiritual Reality, the Keepers of Death: a function nobody can do without, held by the Mahuo the way the Corwen hold the springs and the Lamberts the count. A house that is needed cannot be carried as a banner, and a house nobody can afford to lose is very hard to kill.

**The desire, underneath.** He wants his own dead back. The people of Sum-gol killed in the war never arrived anywhere he can find them. The Order is a son's grief raised to a doctrine, and he knows it, and he would kill to keep anyone else from knowing it.

**The lie.** *"Every soul I keep was drift: unclaimed, unowned, already lost."* The Accord says otherwise, and the Accord is right about some of them. **Tell:** speaking of a soul he took from the living, his tense slips from past to present, because to him that person is still standing in the room.

## Why He Declines Naiser

Naiser Yukari offered to restore the Mahuo royal line at Sum-gol and step the Yukari back. Cozbi refuses, for four reasons, in the order he would give them.

1. **The Yukari tell the story of killing the Mahuo themselves.** By their own account the Moto stood back and the Yukari did the slaughtering. He takes a house at its word.
2. **A crown from Yukari hands makes the Mahuo tenants.** Naiser's terms at the Cedar Enclave put every touch of the spirit world under Yukari counsel. A Mahuo king seated by the Yukari is a doorman at a Yukari door.
3. **The Seikai were "our making."** Naiser said so himself. Cozbi will not take counsel on the dead from the house that built the people who broke the Soul Plane.
4. **To accept is to learn nothing.** A house that takes its seat back as a gift can lose it the same way.

> "Your own house tells the story of how it killed mine, Lord Yukari, and tells it well. Keep the crown. I'll take Sum-gol with my own hands. And when your father's seam starts leaking the dead into your cedars at night, send a crow to my post, and I'll come. That is what my house is for."

## The Order of the Spiritual Reality

*Proposed structure, pending Isaac.*

- **The Post is its face.** The Sum-gol Post carries every letter between the north and the Yukari valleys, and nobody has asked who pays the couriers. Cozbi does, and he reads what passes. The living get a messenger service; the dead get Keepers.
- **Passage is its trade.** Exorcism, the laying of hauntings, clearing Well-creatures where the dead have soured a site, and escorting a soul home: every one priced, recorded and done in olive and white, until the south sends for the Mahuo the way it sends for a physician.
- **The registered law is its proof.** At Principality the Continuum itself entered his law. He means to make the Continuum's word the south's.
- **Its own name** in Sum-gol's tongue: *Honryeong*, "the soul-order."

## Voice

He is patient as something that decided time is not a resource it needs to conserve, and he is never quiet about what he wants. He hosts lavishly, laughs loud and at the right moment, explains the dead at length and with real pride, and gives orders as invitations. Banter comes easily; mercy rarely.

**Fingerprint.** His dead in the present tense ("my mother is fond of this hall"). Once he has decided a living person will die, he speaks of them in the past tense. The table can learn to hear it.

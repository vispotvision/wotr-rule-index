## Traits

**THE UNTOUCHED WINE (Primary Trait)**
He does not move until he has weighed it all, and then he does not stop. He looked at Hayato's map under the one worked lamp in his hall for as long as it took his steward to bring wine and carry it away again untouched, and then he swore, and called out his horse, and has looked faintly amazed at himself ever since. While he weighs, nothing moves him, bribe or threat; once he has committed, his horse goes in at full tilt and does not turn. It costs nothing. Its limit is time: a man who needs his answer before the wine is poured gets no answer at all. Its counter is to make him decide fast; its tell is the cup going cold.

**THE JUNCTION STONE (Secondary Trait)**
Ashmore oaths are sworn hand-on-stone at the crossroads below Ashbury, and the stone holds them: his Oath sets like the stone he swore on, and a promise he made with his hand flat on it does not move. He swore there to Hayato in the king's name, and swore again with his hand on the black stone of the dais, as he had on the junction stone. Its limit is the stone's: it holds one promise at a time cleanly, and two set on the same stone grind. Its counter is to set two oaths against each other; its tell is the flat hand.

## Signature

**THE ASHMORE LINE (House Art, Ashmore)** · mark: Mastered
Five or six hundred big southern horses with lances and long swords in a long ragged line, coming at the gallop out of the Kessen road; it hit the Keld south flank at the Skeldmere at full tilt and buckled it. It costs the house its horses and the lord nothing he would call a working; his Command holds the line together and his Pressure goes ahead of it. Its limit is ground: it needs a field the horses can take at speed, and on ice or in broken country it is a line of men on frightened animals. Its counter is pikes, set, as Thornwall knows; its tell is the russet coming over the rise.

## Physical Description

He is tall and spare, with black hair worn to the jaw, parted loose and falling across his brow, a long pale face with a straight nose and a mouth set in a level line, and dark eyes under heavy brows that look at a map for a long time before they look at the man holding it. He wears a black cloak with a high standing collar, fastened at the throat with a heavy silver clasp worked with the Ashmore boar, over a coat of dark brown edged in pale braid, belted with a round-buckled belt, and a braided cord hanging from the clasp. Under it, in the field, the russet of his house, and the boar on his breast.

## Lore

Ashmore holds the grain, every sack of it between the Greenhollow and the treeline, and before the war Ulf Stark said they had worked out that a man who owns the grain in a famine does not need to fight anybody, and would sell to whoever stood at the gate with silver when the snow came, and call it neutrality. For twenty years the Tenrai held Ashmore's roads, so Ashmore took Tenrai silver because Ashmore had no choice, and Kagenori counted him bought. Then Hayato Amagiri rode into Ashbury with twenty riders and a map drawn in black and indigo, more indigo than black, and told him his grain could come up the indigo at no price at all, where no Tenrai factor would ever find the way. Edric looked at the map while the wine went cold, and swore at the junction stone below Ashbury with his hand on the stone, and called out his horse, and the Ashmore line came down onto the Keld's south flank at the Skeldmere with Hayato at the front of it. At the council of the road he laughed once at Ilse Hallenfeld and asked what Ashmore's part would be, and Muken gave him the river: hold it, let them come down onto the ice, and don't let them leave. When the Seat fell the Tenrai lords ran down the river stair onto the Marrow, and most of them knelt to him, Hayato's uncle among them. In the Hall he swore for the Ashmore marches, faintly amazed at himself all the way through, and in the spring the Ashmore horse went home to Ashbury.

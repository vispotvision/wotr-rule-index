## Traits

**VOW-LIGHT (Inherited Trait)**
What every Celestial's Aether carries, and in him at a Grand Angel's weight: a heavy, warm-pressure radiance under which a spoken lie becomes physically hard to say, a laboured breath, a tightening throat, a headache that will not lift until the speaker takes it back. In the Midreach it reaches further than in any other Choir and compels truth across the whole room rather than discouraging it in one man; people near him find themselves wanting to tell him things. It costs nothing. Its limit is that it makes a lie cost and does not detect it; a man willing to pay can still lie to him, once. Its counter is to say nothing at all.

**I DO NOT LET THINGS FALL (Primary Trait)**
"It is the only thing I was ever made to do." His soul does it before it does anything else: whatever is falling within his reach, a man off a cliff, a boy out of his own body on the Skeldmere, a city wall, a world, a hand is already under it. He hears every oath sworn on honour, even at the edge, even under the Bell's weight; he heard Muken's at the woodpile and Yukazuri's last one in the Shirogane house, and every word of both was true. While he is catching, his Anchoring and Fortitude read two Grades high and nothing he holds can be torn out of his hands. Its limit is that he can catch, and hold, and set down; he does not strike first, and a thing that is not falling he can only stand in front of. Its counter is to make him choose which of two falling things to catch.

**THE BOUND FLAME (Secondary Trait)**
When Malakar cracked the Lawbell, a cracked Bell could no longer hold the weight it was built for, and that weight was every oath sworn under it for eleven thousand years. Faisal went to the edge of creation, to the place where the threads run, took the weight onto his own shoulders so that it would not come down on what is beneath it, and has knelt there ever since. While he kneels, the oaths of the world have somewhere to rest; Malakar has gone to the edge more than once to break him, and he is still kneeling. It costs him everything he does not spend on it, which is why he comes to Muken only when he is needed and when he can, and why what kneels at the edge does not go far from it. Its limit is the kneeling itself: standing, he is only borrowing time from the weight. Its counter is to make him stand, and keep him standing; in the white space by the Foot he stood beside Muken, and Muken's rage cost him a piece of his wing.

## Domain

**OTHSAL · the Position Held (Covenant Domain)**
The only Domain the Midreach Choir has ever grown, and the thing that made him a person rather than an instrument. When it opens, the oaths he carries come out of him as script: lines of gold Vow-Cant, fine as hair and bright as wire drawn hot from a die, every oath ever sworn on honour and kept, unfurling from one point in long curving ribbons like a lotus opening, until the whole space around him is written in them. Inside it, every spoken commitment becomes visibly, physically binding: the air thickens a little each time an oath is sworn, and breaking one parts with a snap like a cord, which everyone present hears whether they speak the language or not. A man who swears inside OTHSAL and keeps it stands a Grade taller in Oath and Anchoring for as long as he keeps it; a man who breaks it is knocked to his knees by the weight of what he dropped. Vow-Cant has no grammar for lying, so nothing false can be written there at all. It costs him about one per cent of his reserve to open, near 3,100,000 EU, and a slow bleed while it stays open. Its limit is that it binds what is sworn and nothing else; a man who promises nothing walks through it untouched. Its counter is silence, and its deeper counter is the one every Midreach Celestial fears: two oaths of equal weight that cannot both be kept, sworn inside it, seize the Domain and him with it.

## Signature

**FAIETH · the Weight Kneeling · the Thousand Hands (Signature Technique)** · mark: Mastered
A Host-name, the kneeling and the weight; mortals who have seen it call it the Thousand Hands. Every oath he carries is a hand that was given, and he gives them back: the air around him fills with hands of solid gold Vow-Light, colossal, a man's height from wrist to fingertip, open-palmed and held out, hundreds of them at once, rushing out from him in a tide like a wave breaking sideways. They catch what falls and set it down. They take a blow on the palm and hold it there. Turned on an enemy, they come in from every side, open, and close, pressing the weight of every kept oath down onto one body from all directions at once until it cannot move, cannot breathe, and is held; the ground under the target cracks in a ring and the target goes to its knees, the way he has knelt for seven hundred years. Each hand carries what it catches into the ground the way his own knees do. It costs about two tenths of one per cent of his reserve per breath to keep them out, near 620,000 EU, and he has never used them to kill. Its limit is that the hands only hold, catch and press; there is no fist among them that strikes first. Its counter is the Choir's old wound: present him two oaths of equal weight that cannot both be kept, and every hand stops where it is.

## What He Has Taught

He gave Muken back *Fure*, the Kōkan's old word for shake, taken from the line in the cellars at Halcrest: every made thing has a note, and you hold it rather than strike it, and the thing tells you where it will open. *This is listening.* In the white space he named Hametsu no Gō, the Work of Ruins, the one Muken's blood leans toward, and taught it on rope: end the rotten ones, only the rotten ones. Under the threads he taught six of the seven Works, and Muken knelt beside him under the weight and learned the Work of Bearing Load, and Faisal's broken wing began to mend. He has withheld the seventh, Totality, whose Obsession face is the hunger to gather everything into one hand, which is what Gimbzo reached for. He has told Muken that there is a third waking under Hibiware, and will not speak of it yet.

## Physical Description

In the place where the threads run he is a shape of light kneeling, the bright places where eyes would be, the great wings standing from his shoulders, broken and mending, and he is so tall that standing he must look down a long way to meet a man's face. When he takes a face to speak to one, it is this: a tall, long-boned man with dark brown skin and a long, still, fine-cut face, the mouth closed, the eyes a burning gold under white lashes and heavy lids, kept half lowered as if out of courtesy. His hair is white, very long and very fine, loose to the small of his back and lifting a little all the time as if a wind came up from below him. A gold cuff worked like laurel leaves climbs the rim of his left ear, and a long gold earring hangs from it in a falling chain of leaves. He wears close plate of pale silver, scratched and dulled with use and never polished, with a high standing collar edged in gold and a line of gold running down from the throat, and nothing on it anywhere that shines but the gold.

## Lore

He was Sung at Midreach in answer to an oath that had no witness, and for about eleven thousand years he kept the Tally, oaths witnessed, kept and broken, and somewhere in those years he became a person: something the Lawbell had not intended, a Celestial capable of wanting things for reasons that were his own, with a Domain grown rather than borrowed. Lucius, the First Grand Paladin, drew on his Numen to raise the seven eidolons that named Sancta Lux, which is why that order runs its largest programme on every claim to have heard him. When Malakar cracked the Lawbell he came through whole, alone of all the Host, and went to the edge of creation and knelt under the weight of every oath ever sworn under it, and has not stood up. On the Skeldmere, when Muken fell out of his own body under the Iron Fist's blows, Faisal caught him, because he does not let things fall, and because he had heard the oath at the woodpile and every word of it was true. He gave back *Fure*. After the Seat road, in the white space by the Foot, he stood up for the first time in seven hundred years, put his hands behind his back, and told a boy training with such anger that he had not yet understood the reason his sister died; he had heard her last oath, and told him she could not make him keep an oath he never swore. Muken's rage cost him part of a wing. Under the threads, kneeling beside him, Muken learned to bear load, and the wing began to mend. *I will be listening.*

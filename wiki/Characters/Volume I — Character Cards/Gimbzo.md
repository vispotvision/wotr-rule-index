## VIII. Traits

**AS HARD AS HE COULD (Primary Trait)**
Before he ever tried anything, his soul committed all of itself to it. Every blow he threw carried the whole of the force he could raise at that instant, Coagulatio drawing it to one point and Fractura running it out along the target's faults, and nothing he struck took a partial blow. It raised his effective Penetration a full Grade against anything that stood and took it. The cost was that he could not throw anything less: no feint, no test, no blow held back, and a reserve that emptied twice as fast as a man of his Stage should have spent it. Its limit was the target's willingness to stand; a thing that gave way, rolled, or let the blow pass through it into the ground took far less than he sent, and the force he had committed went somewhere, often back into him. Its counter is to yield and let him spend it on nothing; its tell is that there is no tell, because he never once disguised a blow.

**FOREST FIRE (Passive Trait)**
His Shell put out his intent ahead of him the way a fire puts out heat ahead of the flame. Anyone with a reading sense saw him coming: Shigure Tenrai, who stood behind him twenty years, could read him three moves out, and said so. It cost him nothing and gained him a great deal in Pressure, since men felt him before they saw him and many broke before he reached them. Its limit, and its counter, are the same: anyone who could read him and keep their nerve could be somewhere else when the blow arrived. His son reads as a page with no ink on it. Gimbzo read like a forest fire.

**THE GOLD AT THE EDGES (Inherited Trait)**
The Kōkan blood, the Shingan with the gold in it, the first seam of the Kamigan. In forty years Shigure never once read the red line in his eyes, in a fight or out of one, even the night he went down into the cellars after it; at the end, at Shimane, his eyes went gold to the edges. It held his Shingan open at a cost of about 20,000 EU a second, near ten minutes from full, and at the end it was open all the time. It resolved fault and load, and ranked them; it could not show him intent, and it could not show him that what he was ranking was his own house. Its counter is to give it nothing whole enough to read.

**THE BLACK (Secondary Trait, a vow)**
After what the Recension made of the red line he swore, before his captains, that he would not wear its colour until he had made it worth wearing again, and he wore black lacquer, black lacing and black everything for twenty years. The vow bound his Attraction to a red he would not touch and fed the Overchannel that let him fight past his ceiling: a Grade of Overchannel inside the two-Grade stack, paid in a Crystal that never once in twenty years let the red line's own Wellspring-heat into him cleanly. Its terms are its counter: put red on him, the Kōkan red, and the vow breaks and takes the Overchannel with it. It was never broken. He died in black.

## IX. Domain

**NOBI · the Field Fire (Domain)**
A field set alight runs ahead of the wind, and nothing standing in it is spared because it is green. Inside NOBI his doctrine outranked the ground: every blow struck within it, his own and everyone else's, landed whole, no yielding, no rolling with it, no passing a load down into the earth; the field would not let a thing give way, and so everything struck in it either held or broke. It ran some four hundred yards out from him, took about six seconds to form, and held from a full reserve for 4 minutes 32 seconds at 60,000 EU a second; his Second Release opened it and powered it. For forty years it was the most terrible ground in the north to fight on. It ended when his reserve or his attention broke, and it could be broken by a stronger Domain, or from inside by anyone who could carry a blow through themselves anyway; the field's law forbade giving way, and a man who did not give way but passed the whole of the weight through his bones into the ground was obeying it to the letter. His son learned that kneeling under the Lawbell. Gimbzo never did that.

## XIII. Physical Description

He was old by the end, and the war was all over his face. His hair was the Kōkan white, worn long and never bound, falling past his shoulders and lifting in any wind like a banner nobody had told to come down; strands of it hung across his eyes and he did not push them back. His skin was weathered to a grey-brown like old saddle leather, cut deep across the brow with lines that ran the width of his forehead and gathered in heavy folds between the eyes, the cheeks sunk under high, hard bones, the nose broad and flattened at the bridge, and a short white beard and moustache grown rough over a mouth set in one long downturned line. His eyes sat deep in dark, bruised hollows under a heavy brow, half-lidded, as if he were always looking at something a long way off; at the centre of each burned a pinpoint of red, the only colour on him, brighter when he was roused, and at the very end, at Shimane, the gold came up round it to the edges. His son has his face: the set of that mouth, the brow, the way he went quiet before he moved, as if he had already moved and was only waiting for his body to catch up, and the way a room changed when he walked into it. Men who rode with him said they knew what he felt like at ten paces. He wore black, a high-collared coat of black under a pale campaign mantle, black lacquer and black lacing in the field, black everything for twenty years, and never once the Kōkan red enamel. He ruled from a saddle and a tent and never sat the red chair that was his, and left it under forty years of dust so that every lord at the ring would look at it and know he could walk in any day and take it.

## Lore

### Origin

He was born to the Kōkan, the red line, the old crown line of the Moto, three hundred years after the Tenrai had taken the Seat and while the red chair was still sat on sufferance. He was a man of some restraint, once, before his mother died. After her death there was no more reasoning with him; the Tenrai were in his ear every night with grief and ambition, and called the road to ruin destiny, and he drank both.

### The Making

He rode at the war's head for forty years, the one forty-year war against the Zettari, and in its long middle the north still thought he meant to set things right: Ulf Stark rode two summers beside him, Yusif Stark was the best of his captains, and Northern uncles went south behind him and most never came back. He hated the Zettari house more than anyone alive, and sent riders to the Yukari twice in the last four years asking them to join his grudge; Naiser Yukari refused him. He would not hear Kagenori Tenrai when Kagenori wanted the Starks and Greymanes wiped out; he wanted them for his front line. He ruled from a saddle and a tent and never sat the red chair. He wore black because of what the Recension had made of the red, and swore he would not wear its colour until he had made it worth wearing again.

### The Cost

About twenty years ago his own line told him no, in the hall, to his face, and he had them killed: the ones who spoke first, then their children, then their cousins, then anyone who had married into them. He burned the Akagane granaries at Hiroe with the children still inside. Sixteen years ago he went down into the cellars under Halcrest with a lamp after the last of his own blood, gold coming up through the grey in the eyes of the ones he cornered there, right to the end, and his sworn guard Kasane Byakuya walked up out of those cellars on her own feet with his son inside her coat and never said his name again. The Kurenai closed the book on the rest, and Sumie Kurenai, then twenty-three, signed for a closed book over the child. Yusif Stark quit him the next morning. Faisal says the cellars were his reach for the Work of Totality, the hunger to make the red line one thing, only one, himself. He found Kasane once more, three years later, in the salt, and his daughter was the result. Whether he ever learned his son had lived, nobody now alive can say; Shigure believed he thought he had burned the last of it.

### Where He Stands

He fell at Shimane in the war's last month, in the mud, to a Zettari prince of seventeen, with his eyes gone gold to the edges; how it was done is the Zettari's own to tell. The criers came down the galleries at Halcrest with it, and Kasane turned her face to the wall and died that morning. The other lines opened the register only long enough to write that too. The Moto began to eat themselves the day he died; they had been held together by one man's fist for a very long time, and somebody opened it. Within a year his son sat the red chair he never had, in red lacquer he never wore.

## VIII. Traits

**THE RIDGE DOES NOT MOVE (Primary Trait)**
His house's words, become the law of his soul before he could say them. When his feet are set on solid ground, a load that comes into him does not move him; Petralon routes it down through his bones into the ground, the way an arch carries a cathedral roof into its footings, all of it in compression. He goes on standing. The ground under him takes the blow instead: cracks in a ring, or settles, or sinks an inch, and a fine dust rises round his boots. It is constant and nearly free, and it is the reason a Greymane line of shields does not break. The cost is that he cannot move while it holds; a set foot is a planted foot, and lifting it drops the law. Its limit is the footing: loose snow, ice, mud, open water, a ship's deck, a moving cart, a high stair, anything that is not one with the ground beneath, gives the load nowhere to go but him. And Petralon fails in tension. It is stone. Push it and it holds; pull it, hook the shield and haul, lift him by the belt, drag him off his line, and it does not give gracefully. It parts. The counter is written in the Wellspring's own failure: never push the Shieldwarden. Pull him, or fight him on the ice.

**THE SWORN WARD (Secondary Trait)**
His father swore him on the ridge, the night before he rode south to the Kurenai blade, to carry the secret of Muken's blood until it could be carried by someone with a better right. Godfrey carried it every morning at the boy's right hand and said *sire* and said nothing, and it was the hardest thing he had ever done, and his Crystal kept the shape of it. Through the Spirit Axis that formed between him and his king, the one he is sworn to guard is tied into his own load path like a bay tied to a buttress: within a few paces, a blow aimed at the ward can be taken through Godfrey's set body instead, its weight running down him into the ground, so that the ward feels only a shove. He can hold one ward, and the ward is Muken. It costs him the full hit, landing on a body that may not be braced for it, and every catch costs about 1.5% of his reserve on top. Its limit is the reach of a long stride and the need to have his feet set. Its counter is two blows at once, one to the king and one to the warden, or a strike to Godfrey first, while he is still turning to the king; the tell is that his eyes go to the ward an instant before any blow lands.

**THE FLAT HAND (Passive Trait)**
A tall building in a gale carries a great mass hung near its top, tuned to sway against the building and soak up its swaying. Godfrey is that, for whoever sits beside him. Basilithe locks his own rhythm so steady that nothing drives it, and his Stability spreads it a little way: within arm's reach, a frightened or furious ally's pulse and breath start to fall in with his, the urge to stand up or lash out comes on slower, and a young king can sit through a room full of enemies without moving because a long grey hand has come down flat on the pine beside his own. It never touches, and it never compels; it only steadies, and anyone who decides to stand up stands up. It costs nothing. Its limit is arm's reach and the ally's own willingness. Its counter is distance: separate them, and the king's temper is his own again. It cannot steady Godfrey himself, who has nobody's hand beside his.

## X. Techniques

**LOAD PATH (Signature Technique, Greymane Shield Art)** · mark: Mastered
The house's first lesson and his best. He squares the Greymane shield into the blow, sinks his knees, and sets every joint from the shield-boss down through the forearm, the shoulder, the spine, the hips and the knees to the heel into one straight column, so the blow's momentum goes down the column into the earth, and his body is only the road it travels. A catch costs about 1.5% of his reserve, some 2,600 EU, and the work Petralon does to lock the column delivers around a gigajoule into the ground: B-Grade, a building's worth, spent in cracking the stone round his feet instead of breaking him. Its limit is the footing, as for his Primary Trait, and a column has one line: a blow that comes from the side of it, or two that come at once, shears it. Its tell is the squared shield and the knees going down before the hit. Its counter, the one every Greymane is taught to fear, is the hook: catch the shield's rim with an axe-beard or a hand, and pull, and the column that holds any push parts in tension. Before, stay off his line and attack the flank; during, hook and pull; after, he must reset his feet, and that reset is a beat.

**THE RIDGE GIVES BACK (Signature Technique)** · mark: Drilled
Stone squeezed hard enough holds the squeeze like a spring holds it, and gives it back when it is let go. Petralon keeps the last catch's load locked in the ground under his feet for a breath or two, and he can let it out all at once: the ground heaves up under the attacker in a cracked ring, and the shield comes forward with the earth's own rebound behind it in a bash that can throw a man in plate across a yard. It costs about 6% of his reserve, some 10,400 EU, and delivers up to 4.7 GJ: B-Grade, a ton of powder's worth. It can give back only what the ground took. With no catch before it, there is nothing to return. The tell is the ring of cracks running out from his boots a heartbeat before he moves. The counter is to give him nothing to give back: do not strike his shield, strike round it, or wait out the two breaths until the ground lets go of the load on its own.

**THE GREY FILE (House Art, Greymane)** · mark: Mastered · page: House Greymane
The Greymane foot lock their shields rim to rim in a long grey line, and Petralon in each of them ties into the next, so a blow on any shield runs along the whole file and down through every set of boots in it. The file is as strong as its weakest pair of feet and as long as its ends, and it is broken from the end, where the load has only one man to go through.

**Vows** · The oath his father swore him to on the ridge, kept. Spoken before a living witness, a vow lifts a technique's output to the top of its own Grade; he has never needed one in a fight and has never broken one

**STONEFAST (First Release)** · folk name: Setting the Stone
Called low, never shouted, the way his father called it. The grey wolf-fur of his mantle stiffens and greys and sets into layered stone-scale over his shoulders like slate on a roof, a skin of ridge-stone climbs from his boots to his knees, his amber-hinted aura flares to the full of what Flourishing allows, and the ground under him groans as Petralon takes him into it. THE RIDGE DOES NOT MOVE strikes at the top of its Grade. It holds 2 minutes 30 seconds from a full reserve, about 1,160 EU a second, and ends at a tenth. Silent, it comes in weaker: the stone stays at his boots and never reaches the shoulders. Its counter is the same as its Trait's, and worse: a man set in stone to the knee cannot step off a tensile pull at all.

## XIII. Physical Description

He is tall and long-limbed, built long rather than broad, with the wide even shoulders of a man who has carried a shield since he could lift one. His hair is pale gold, the colour of rye stubble in a low sun, worn shaggy and overlong, falling past his brows and across his face, one lock always hanging down between his eyes that he never pushes back. His face is long and pale, with high cheekbones, a straight narrow nose and a thin mouth that seldom moves; the north calls it grey, for the colour it goes in the cold and for what grief has done to it. His eyes are grey-blue, heavy-lidded, quiet, and since his father knelt on the black floor there have been dark circles under them that will not go. His hands are long and grey and very still. He dresses in the house's colours as he can get them: a close-fitting coat of deep navy, high-collared to the jaw, closed down the front with gold piping and a row of brass studs, a double chain of gold slung across the chest between two of them; over it a heavy mantle of black wool edged and banded in gold, worked with the ring-and-ridge circles of the Greymanes, and on the mantle's shoulders a great collar of grey wolf-fur, thick and silver-tipped, sewn for him by the women of the long houses the night before the march. The Greymane wolf sits on his breast. He carries the Greymane shield on his back when he is not holding it, its ridge device scratched and dented.

## Lore

### Origin

Godfrey Greymane was born on the Ridgewall, the fortress built into the ridgeline between Kharven and the hobgoblin Mandate of Undaar-Keth, to the house the north calls the shield. The Greymane Shieldwarden is addressed by title, never by name, because the function matters more than the man, and Godfrey was raised to it: a component of the wall before he was a person, a shield-arm before he was a son. His father was Wystan Greymane, Shieldwarden in his time. The house words are *The Ridge Does Not Move*, and they are not a boast. They are a description.

For forty years the Greymanes and the Starks held the passes against the Zettari for the Moto, because northerners were cheap, and buried their sons in it.

### The Making

When the Moto lady came north and the old Warlords knew their time was spent, Wystan swore his son on the ridge, by the woodpile, the night before he rode south with Ulf Stark: to carry the secret of the boy Muken's blood until it could be carried by someone with a better right. Then Wystan knelt beside Ulf on the black floor of the Hall of Verdicts and the Kurenai blade came down. That autumn Godfrey buried his mother on the ridge.

He went to the Foot and stood at the boy's right hand. He told him to sit and stay sat and let them come. He gave him the Keld in numbers. He said *sire* every morning, and every morning he was sorry, and he said nothing. When it came out, at the table before Kiyome and the Crown Prince, he did not ask the king's pardon. He told him he would swear it again and keep it again, and that he had been sorry every day.

He stood at Muken's left at the woodpile when the boy told six hundred people he was Gimbzo's son and let them walk away if they would. He walked at the head of the Greymane foot on the road to the Seat in a new grey cloak the women of the long houses had sewn for him in the night. And the night before the Hall, the king fell asleep sitting up against the wall with his head on Godfrey's shoulder, and Godfrey did not move until morning.

### Where He Stands

This morning, in the Hall of Verdicts, he stands among the Banner Houses for the Greymanes of the Ridgewall, very straight, in his new grey cloak, beside Lorn Stark. When the king asks the houses for their word he gives it quietly. *The ridge does not move. We'll hold it. For you, and for whoever comes after. My father would have, too.*

### The Long Watch

The record keeps the rest of him. He married, and buried his wife on the ridge, and rode to Kharven for the assembly the next morning. He raised two grandsons, Bram and Wren, and planted six acres of rye and turnips behind the estate's western wall so that two boys would learn their hands were for something besides weapons. When Muken's kingdom fell, the Shieldwarden held the western ridgeline for three years with no crown left to reward him, his grandsons beside him on the wall-walk, and when the stores ran out he held it another six months on discipline. He gave Bram the Greymane shield. *A true knight defends his people.* The Tenrai killed him. The ridge did not move.

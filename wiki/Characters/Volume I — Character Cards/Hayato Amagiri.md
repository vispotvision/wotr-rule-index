## VIII. Traits

**THE ROAD UNDER THE ROAD (Primary Trait)**
His soul reads ground for passage before it reads anything else: where a column can go, where a mule can carry two sacks in snow and where it must go single file, which herders' shoulder the smugglers never had a reason to find, which deer track comes down onto the north hill of a house that did not know it had a back door. Everything he has walked stays: Retention keeps it whole and Forecast turns it into time, two days, a day and a half, and he is always right. It costs him nothing and he cannot stop doing it; his finger draws roads on tables, in wet rings, in the air. Its limit is that it reads only ground he has ridden or a map he trusts, and it reads ground, not men; he can bring twenty riders to any door in the north and has no idea what will be waiting behind it. Its counter is country he has never walked, and a road closed since he last rode it; its tell is the finger.

**RAIN MIST (Inherited Trait)**
The Amagiri name means rain-mist, and their blood earns it. On a road at night, in wet or cold air, the Amagiri thin the light round themselves and the people riding with them: a party with him goes gray at the edges and hard to fix in the eye from a wall or a hold, and a sentry looking straight at the track sees weather. It is how twenty riders crossed out of the western shield unseen and came to the Yukari's north gate in a day and a half when the crows had expected three. It costs about two per cent of his reserve an hour on the move, less than he recovers. Its limit is that it hides from eyes and only from eyes, and only on the move, in the dark or the wet; in a hall, in daylight, standing still, it does nothing. Its counter is daylight, dogs that smell what they cannot see, and a lantern on a pole; its tell, to anyone who knows to look, is a patch of road where the mist is thicker than the weather explains.

**NOW I KNOW WHICH WAY (Secondary Trait)**
It formed in Kiyome Byakuya's white house the night he admitted he had spent a year mapping the roads out and had only been getting ready to run. Once he has chosen his road he does not turn off it: fear still reaches him, and his hands still shake, but his feet do not stop, and he rides at the front of six hundred horse with his sword out though he holds it badly. It costs nothing to choose and everything to keep. Its limit is the choosing; until he has picked his road he is the most hesitant man in any room, glancing at the door. Its counter is to put two roads in front of him at once; its tell is his restless fingers going still.

**SHINGAN · the heart-eye (Bloodline Faculty, Moto)** · mark: Held
The common Moto sight: where a thing is carrying its weight. In Hayato it turned to the ground early and stayed there, and he reads a slope, a bank, a frozen ford or the mended seam in a wall for where it will bear a horse and where it will give, before he reads the faces in front of it. ## XIII. Physical Description

He is a slight, sharp man, lean the way men get who live in the saddle, and pale, very pale, the pallor of someone who rides at night and sleeps by day. His hair is silver-white, cut to a soft fall over the brow and swept to one side, with the back grown long into a single tail he ties with a short gold cord and wears forward over his right shoulder. His face is narrow and fine-boned, with a long nose, level dark brows and gray eyes that hold a horseman's squint at the corners even indoors, and a mouth that rests a little crooked, as if he has just thought of something dry and decided not to say it. A long gold earring hangs from his right ear, a dangle of small gold pieces that moves when he turns his head; he paints his nails black, and wears a black fingerless glove on his right hand with thin gold bangles at both wrists. His fingers never stop: folding the corner of his cape, turning his tea cup a quarter so the glaze crack faces away from him, drawing roads on the table. At the Hall he wears a white haori with the Amagiri mon worked on its breast and sleeves, over under-robes of indigo so dark it reads black, their sleeves banded in gold, a gray striped hakama, gold-tasselled cords at the waist, and a folded fan tucked into his collar; and he carries a gilt fox mask, the mask the Amagiri used to leave at crossroads shrines for whatever walks the roads at night, which he turns in his hand and never puts on. On the road all of that goes under an indigo rain-cape, dark at the shoulders where the snowmelt soaks through, with a sprig of blue-black juniper caught in the toggle at his throat; it has been there since the Yukari enclave, and he has not taken it out.

## Lore

### Origin

He was born to the Amagiri, the indigo line, whose old office in the Moto house was route-reading: the roads, the passes, the way out. His grandfather kept a survey of every northern village, souls on the register and chimneys with smoke, and Hayato learned it by heart. Naiser Yukari met him once as a boy, before Hiroe, at one of the great gatherings of houses: a thin quick child with ink on his fingers, drawing maps of the hall's corridors on the backs of the menus. He grew into his face. He did not grow out of his fingers.

### The Making

He sat the indigo chair at the Verdict of Six, the youngest of six lords, glancing at the door. He told Torvald Stark he had once counted him among the few men in the capital with any honor, and then voted yes on his head, and the seven lines forgave him, and he did not. Kagenori Tenrai gave the Amagiri the roads to search for two children of the Kōkan line. Hayato had already spent a year mapping roads out of the western shield that a man could ride by night without being seen from the hold, and had told himself it was the Amagiri's office; he went by night to Kiyome Byakuya's white house with Tetsugen Akagane and admitted he had only been getting ready to run. Then he said, now I know which way.

He took the three of them to the Yukari in a day and a half by a herders' shoulder off the Hiekawa cut, and told the Zettari Sovereign that everything the Sovereign said was also true. He gave up his roads, which were all his house had that the Tenrai did not, to carry Kushara's grain under Yukari eyes. At the Foot he gave Muken the chimney count of Harrowmere, a hundred and four in his grandfather's survey and twenty-six now, and warned him what Karogane meant to shout between the lines, and read three of Karogane's letters on his own roads and saw the silver seal set on the left, in the place of precedence: Kagenori did not want the red chair, he wanted to stand behind the silver one. At the woodpile he swore of the Indigo Mist Line, plainly, the way he gave distances, and said what it was worth, since it was not much. Two nights before the Skeldmere he rode for Ashbury with his twenty and a map drawn in black and indigo, more indigo than black, and Edric Ashmore swore at the junction stone below Ashbury with his hand on the stone; and when the Ashmore horse came down onto the Keld's flank Hayato was at the front of it, riding badly, all elbows, sword out, yelling *two days and a road, sire.*

### Where He Stands

His uncle ran onto the Marrow ice with the other Tenrai lords when the Seat fell, and knelt to Edric Ashmore, and is held. Hayato found Shiro Tenrai in the keep's record-room and brought him forward. This morning he stands at the indigo chair for the indigo line, in his uncle's place, and swears to the red one, quickly, the words tumbling: two days and a road, always, whatever you need. In the spring the first grain goes north down his indigo roads on mules, with a Yukari witness at each end counting the sacks.

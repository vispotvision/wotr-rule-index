### The Veil

The Amagiri name means rain-mist, and the oldest thing the line does is make what is on a road look like weather. Hayato turned it inward the year he rode the passes, when his Crystal began to change the air round him the way a Band IV Crystal does, and he understood that a twenty-year-old lord of the indigo line who made the frost go strange in a hall would not live to be twenty-one under Gimbzo. RAIN MIST lies over his Crystal the way it lies over a column at night: every reading of him comes back gray, small and hard to fix, an Adept at Flourishing with a head full of roads, which is also, in every way that matters to him, what he is. It costs him a little of his reserve an hour, less than he recovers. Its limit is the mist's own: in a hall, in daylight, it thins, and the Veil holds there only because nobody looks hard at a quiet young man who apologises, and in great heat it does not hold at all. When he calls KIRISAME the mist comes off him and goes out over the ground, and he cannot gather it back in front of anyone who saw.

## VIII. Traits

**THE ROAD UNDER THE ROAD (Primary Trait)**
His soul reads ground for passage before it reads anything else: where a column can go, where a mule can carry two sacks in snow and where it must go single file, which herders' shoulder the smugglers never had a reason to find, which deer track comes down onto the north hill of a house that did not know it had a back door. Everything he has walked stays: Phreatis keeps it the way a riverbed keeps the river, Retention keeps it whole, and Forecast turns it into time, two days, a day and a half, and he is always right. At Dissonance it is more than a map. On a road he has walked, Transference treats the road as his: he can be anywhere along it faster than he could ride it, and so can whatever he carries. It costs him nothing to read and he cannot stop doing it; his finger draws roads on tables, in wet rings, in the air. Its limit is that it reads only ground he has ridden or walked, never a map, however much he trusts it; and it reads ground, not men; he can bring twenty riders to any door in the north and has no idea what will be waiting behind it. Its counter is country he has never walked, and a road changed since he last walked it; its tell is the finger.

**RAIN MIST (Inherited Trait)**
The Amagiri name means rain-mist, and their blood earns it. On a road at night, in wet or cold air, the Amagiri thin the light round themselves and the people riding with them: a party with him goes gray at the edges and hard to fix in the eye from a wall or a hold, and a sentry looking straight at the track sees weather. In him it does it to his own Crystal as well, and has since he was twenty. It costs about two per cent of his reserve an hour on the move, less than he recovers. Its limit is that it hides from eyes and readings and only from them, and best on the move, in the dark or the wet; in a hall, in daylight, standing still, it thins to almost nothing. It is mist, and mist is water hung in cold air: heat burns it off the ground the way the sun burns fog off a valley. Its counter is daylight, dogs that smell what they cannot see, a lantern on a pole, and heat; its tell, to anyone who knows to look, is a patch of road where the mist is thicker than the weather explains.

**NOW I KNOW WHICH WAY (Secondary Trait)**
It formed in Kiyome Byakuya's white house the night he admitted he had spent a year mapping the roads out and had only been getting ready to run, and told the two of them what he was, which was the same thing. Once he has chosen his road he does not turn off it: fear still reaches him, and his hands still shake, but his feet do not stop, and he rides at the front of six hundred horse with his sword out though he holds it badly. It costs nothing to choose and everything to keep. Its limit is the choosing; until he has picked his road he is the most hesitant man in any room, glancing at the door, and a Grandmaster who glances at the door is a Grandmaster who has not drawn. Its counter is to put two roads in front of him at once; its tell is his restless fingers going still.

**Trait risks carried** · Rigidity: once committed, he will ride a road after it has stopped going anywhere · Shear Break condition: carrying something down his roads that he swore they would not carry · Dissonance: Retention at 725, past the fracture line; KITSUNE-MEN spends it, and every road it spends is gone

## IX. Domain

**TSUJI · the Crossroads (Domain)**
The old Amagiri left masks at crossroads shrines for whatever walks the roads at night, because a crossroads is where a traveler can be sent the wrong way. The Second Release opens one round him out to about four hundred meters, the widest Domain any of the seven lines has recorded: Transference and Phreatis at Domain-law over ground he has walked, so that every way through the field is a road, and every road is his. He decides where each one goes. A man running at the king along twenty yards of open yard arrives behind himself, or at the far edge of the field, or back where he started; arrows fly their whole flight and come down at the archers' feet; a column marching into it marches out the way it came and believes it went forward. It holds three minutes from a full reserve, about 1,080,000 EU a second. The cost is that the field is only as wide as his feet have made it: inside the four hundred meters, ground he has not walked is ground he does not own, and in a strange place the field is a patchwork with holes in it until he has paced it, which takes time and is visible. Its limit is motion and Sovereignty: a man who stands still is taking no road and is not moved, a practitioner of his Stage with stronger Sovereignty walks his own road through the field at a cost, and what crosses without traveling, heat and light and sound, crosses the field untouched. Its counter is to stand still, to fight him on ground he has never walked, or to burn the mist off it.

## X. Techniques

**AMAGIRI-ASHI · the Rain-Mist Step (Bloodline Art, Amagiri)** · mark: Mastered
On a road he has walked, at night or in wet air, he goes to mist. The edges first, the haze round him thickening and the man inside it going gray, and then all of him, body and coat and sword and whatever he holds, gone into a fine cold fog that moves along the road the way fog moves along a river valley at dusk, low and quick and following every bend, and comes back together at the far end as a slight young man in an indigo rain-cape, apologising. It costs about a fifth of a per cent of his reserve a league, near 389,000 EU, which is why he can cross the north in a night and does not, since a quartermaster who arrives before his message raises questions. Its limit is the mist's: it needs a road he knows, it needs air that will hold water, and it will not hold in heat, in a hard dry wind, or in strong sunlight, which burn it off the ground and leave him standing where the mist ran out. Its counter is heat, wind, daylight and strange ground; its tell is a little more gray at the edges of him than the weather explains.

**MICHI-KIRI · the Road-Cut (Signature Technique)** · mark: Mastered
The Amagiri draw, which his grandfather taught him in a barn with the doors shut. He draws the plain sword and cuts once, across the air, in one long flat line, and Transference closes the road along that line: whatever is traveling across it, a blade, an arrow, a man running, a charge of horse, a river's flow, a working's current, stops traveling. It does not strike anything. It arrives at the line and goes no further, and pools there, the way a river pools at a weir, until he lets the road open again. It costs about five per cent of his reserve to draw, near 9,721,000 EU, which, set loose on anything, would deliver 8.75 TJ, S-Grade, a couple of kilotons; it holds a minute from the draw. At the Skeldmere he cut it across the Keld's left wing at three hundred yards and four hundred riders stopped against nothing in the snow, and the survivors swear it was a squall. Its limit is that it closes only what travels: heat, light and sound do not travel along a road in the way he means and cross the cut untouched, a man standing still on the line is not moved off it, and the line is as long as one cut of a slight man's arm and the reach of his Radius, never a wall round a city. Its counter is to go round its ends, or to bring something that crosses without traveling; its tell is the sword out, which everyone at the Skeldmere saw and nobody understood.

**SHINGAN · the heart-eye (Bloodline Faculty, Moto)** · mark: Mastered
The common Moto sight: where a thing is carrying its weight. In Hayato it turned to the ground early and stayed there, and he reads a slope, a bank, a frozen ford or the mended seam in a wall for where it will bear a horse and where it will give, before he reads the faces in front of it. It costs him about 19,000 EU a second to hold open, nearly three hours from full, paid as a squint that never quite leaves the corners of his eyes. It resolves load in ground and stone; it reads men poorly, and the Torvald he read at the Verdict of Six he read with the other five. Its counter is soft ground with nothing in it carrying weight: bog, deep snow, open water.

**KIRISAME · Drizzle (First Release)** · called "*Furu*," fall
The mist comes off him and goes out over the ground in every direction, low and fine and cold, and the gold flecks of his Crystal move in it like moths round a lamp; the haze that thinned to nothing in a hall becomes indigo weather for a hundred meters, and inside it RAIN MIST works in daylight and standing still, on his allies as well as himself, so that an enemy looking for any of them sees rain. Celerity, Evasion and Perception strike a Grade high for as long as it holds. It holds four minutes from a full reserve, about 810,000 EU a second. Called silent, the drizzle stays on his own coat and the gain is half. The cost is the Veil, gone for good in front of anyone who saw; and it is weather, and great heat lifts it. Its counter is heat and wind, and a nose.

**TSUJI · the Crossroads (Second Release)**
The rain on a cape becomes rain on a whole forest, and the drizzle settles into roads; see Domain above. Held past its limit it is a Crystal Fracture Event along the Verdict of Six, and he would lose the way home.

**KITSUNE-MEN · the Fox Mask (Third Release)**
He puts on the gilt fox mask, the one the Amagiri left at crossroads shrines for whatever walks the roads at night, and for as long as he wears it he is that. Inside TSUJI he is no longer bound to travel the roads he owns: he steps from any point of the field to any other without crossing between, as many times as he can breathe, and whatever he touches he can send down any road he has ever walked, to its far end, which may be three hundred miles off in a pass in the Grey Teeth with the snow coming down. It holds forty-five seconds from a full reserve, about 4,320,000 EU a second. The cost is the roads: every use of it spends one road out of him for ever, the way a riverbed is lost when the river is turned, and the road he spends is the one he used; he will never again know the way along it, and he is a man made of roads. He has worn the mask twice. He does not say which roads he lost. Its limit is the field and the touch: he can only send what he touches, and touching a thing means being within its reach. Its counter is to make him touch something he cannot afford to touch, or to burn the field off the ground.

## XIII. Physical Description

He is a slight, sharp man, lean the way men get who live in the saddle, and pale, very pale, the pallor of someone who rides at night and sleeps by day. His hair is silver-white, cut to a soft fall over the brow and swept to one side, with the back grown long into a single tail he ties with a short gold cord and wears forward over his right shoulder. His face is narrow and fine-boned, with a long nose, level dark brows and gray eyes that hold a horseman's squint at the corners even indoors, and a mouth that rests a little crooked, as if he has just thought of something dry and decided not to say it. A long gold earring hangs from his right ear, a dangle of small gold pieces that moves when he turns his head; he paints his nails black, and wears a black fingerless glove on his right hand with thin gold bangles at both wrists. His fingers never stop: folding the corner of his cape, turning his tea cup a quarter so the glaze crack faces away from him, drawing roads on the table. At the Hall he wears a white haori with the Amagiri mon worked on its breast and sleeves, over under-robes of indigo so dark it reads black, their sleeves banded in gold, a gray striped hakama, gold-tasselled cords at the waist, and a folded fan tucked into his collar; and he carries a gilt fox mask, the mask the Amagiri used to leave at crossroads shrines for whatever walks the roads at night, which he turns in his hand and almost never puts on. On the road all of that goes under an indigo rain-cape, dark at the shoulders where the snowmelt soaks through, with a sprig of blue-black juniper caught in the toggle at his throat; it has been there since the Yukari enclave, and he has not taken it out.

## Lore

### Origin

He was born to the Amagiri, the indigo line, whose old office in the Moto house was route-reading: the roads, the passes, the way out. His grandfather kept a survey of every northern village, souls on the register and chimneys with smoke, and Hayato learned it by heart, and his grandfather taught him the draw in a barn with the doors shut and told him the Amagiri had kept it eleven hundred years by never once using it where anyone could count. Naiser Yukari met him once as a boy, before Hiroe, at one of the great gatherings of houses: a thin quick child with ink on his fingers, drawing maps of the hall's corridors on the backs of the menus. He grew into his face. He did not grow out of his fingers. At nineteen he rode the passes alone for a year, at night, mapping, and went through every high place in the north where a current pools, and came down out of the Grey Teeth in the spring with his Crystal changing the air round him and understood that he had better learn to make it look like weather.

### The Making

He sat the indigo chair at the Verdict of Six, the youngest of six lords, glancing at the door, and by then the strongest of them. He told Torvald Stark he had once counted him among the few men in the capital with any honor, and then voted yes on his head, because a man who could have stopped the blade and did not would have had to explain why, and the explanation would have been the end of the Amagiri; the seven lines forgave him, and he did not. Kagenori Tenrai gave the Amagiri the roads to search for two children of the Kōkan line. Hayato had already spent a year mapping roads out of the western shield that a man could ride by night without being seen from the hold, and had told himself it was the Amagiri's office; he went by night to Kiyome Byakuya's white house with Tetsugen Akagane and admitted he had only been getting ready to run. Then he told them what he was, and Tetsugen laughed and poured him another cup, and Kiyome said nothing for a long time and then said, *then we are three*. Then he said, now I know which way.

He took the three of them to the Yukari in a day and a half by a herders' shoulder off the Hiekawa cut, and told the Zettari Sovereign that everything the Sovereign said was also true. He gave up his roads, which were all his house had that the Tenrai did not, to carry Kushara's grain under Yukari eyes. At the Foot he gave Muken the chimney count of Harrowmere, a hundred and four in his grandfather's survey and twenty-six now, and warned him what Karogane meant to shout between the lines, and read three of Karogane's letters on his own roads and saw the silver seal set on the left, in the place of precedence: Kagenori did not want the red chair, he wanted to stand behind the silver one. At the woodpile he swore of the Indigo Mist Line, plainly, the way he gave distances, and said what it was worth, since it was not much, and did not count himself in it. Two nights before the Skeldmere he rode for Ashbury with his twenty and a map drawn in black and indigo, more indigo than black, and Edric Ashmore swore at the junction stone below Ashbury with his hand on the stone; and when the Ashmore horse came down onto the Keld's flank Hayato was at the front of it, riding badly, all elbows, and when the Keld's left wing turned to meet it he drew his sword, once, and cut the air, and four hundred riders stopped against nothing three hundred yards off, and he put the sword away and went on yelling *two days and a road, sire.*

### Where He Stands

His uncle ran onto the Marrow ice with the other Tenrai lords when the Seat fell, and knelt to Edric Ashmore, and is held. Hayato found Shiro Tenrai in the keep's record-room and brought him forward. This morning he stands at the indigo chair for the indigo line, in his uncle's place, and swears to the red one, quickly, the words tumbling: two days and a road, always, whatever you need. In the spring the first grain goes north down his indigo roads on mules, with a Yukari witness at each end counting the sacks. He is the strongest man in the Hall of Verdicts, and he is turning his cup a quarter so the crack faces away from him.

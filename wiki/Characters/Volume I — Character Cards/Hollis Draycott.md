## Traits

**READS THE BELT (Primary Trait)**
It is how a man of the factor houses reads a room, the way a sailor looks at the sky: before a face, the belt, and the bronze seal-token on it, and the note it hums. He hears a token's contract the way other men hear a tune, sound or failing, old or new, and he reads a person by what they are bound to. It costs nothing. Its limit is a person with no token; a tokenless sergeant and his surgeon told him nothing at all, and he had to fall back on the plain eye. Its counter is to wear no token, or one that says nothing; its tell is the quick open glance at your belt.

**THE OLDEST CONTRACT (Secondary Trait)**
Thirty-one years of a sound contract have bound his Attraction to the Vessant house's oldest paper, and while he is keeping it he is calm, courteous and very hard to shift. When the contract is threatened, his thumb goes to his token and runs once round the rim, and when he lies to keep it his breath gives him away. It costs nothing to keep. Its limit is that it is loyal to the paper, not the people on the road. Its counter is to ask him what the contract has cost rather than what it pays; its tell is the thumb.

## Physical Description

He is younger than the job makes him sound, with a long, even face, a straight nose and a level mouth, and brown hair worn thick and untidy, pushed back and falling forward again across his brow. His eyes are a clear green and steady, and they go to your belt first. Ink stains the second finger of his right hand, the scribe's mark, though he has none of the scribe's stoop. He wears a long dark traveling coat with a high collar, cut close and lined with something soft at the throat, the snow still on its shoulders when he comes in from the street, and oiled boots; on his belt, where a fighting man would wear a knife, a bronze Vessant seal-token the size of a large coin on a short chain, humming faintly for as long as the contract holds.

## Lore

He found Muken of the Lanternless above a tea-house at Halcrest on the captain's word, looked at the two of them, found no token on either, and offered the escort of a single convoy, eleven wagons of Vessant iron stock and lamp oil, from the Halcrest gate to the river post in the eastern timber and back: forty bars a man, stamped Vessant, eighty for the sergeant, half at the gate. The last escort, he said, had light losses, three men; and Yukazuri, watching his breathing from the cot, said that he wasn't telling the truth, and he was made to say four, of twenty-six. Muken said *double it*, and Draycott said double, full at the gate, and that the house would want it sealed, and then explained why a seal could not bind a man with no house name in the book. He gave Muken the route himself, drawn on a slip of paper in his own hand, through every narrow place in the timber, while the house told the drivers the shorter river road with its Vessant way-station was closed. It was not closed. The road was Ulf Stark's test, and Draycott was the last man Ulf sent.

## Traits

**THE RED BEFORE THE WHITE (Inherited Trait)**
The Byakuya oath, said over her at nine with her grandmother's hand on her head: purity before power, peace before the blade, and the red line before the white, until the end of the red line. Her Attraction Layer was bound to the Kōkan king and his children, and near him, keeping it, nothing frightened her and nothing got past her. Its limit was the oath's own: it gave her dominion over the protection of the king and the king's children, and it had nothing to say when the king himself was the one holding the knife. Its counter is to make the king the knife; its tell is her lips moving over the words, which they did in the cellars, and did not stop.

**UNMEI NO SAMURAI (Primary Trait)**
The guard of the red chair, every one of them a Byakuya or raised by the Byakuya, red lacquer for the red line and white lacing for the white line that kept it. She stood at the Kōkan king's shoulder in every hall he ever walked into, half a step ahead and to his left, and her soul did that before it did anything else: it put her body between him and whatever was coming. Her Reflex and Anchoring held a Grade higher while he stood behind her. It cost her nothing, and in the end everything. Its limit is that it guards from outside; it cannot stand between a man and what is in his own ear. Its counter is to come at the king from where she cannot stand; its tell is where she stands.

**DOWN THE ROAD (Secondary Trait)**
She could always see further down a road than the rest of them. Forecast ran deep in her, and it formed into a law the night she understood where Gimbzo's road was going and saw the end of it. She sent four lines to her sister by runner, telling her to stay home, and walked into the salt with the boy before anyone knew she was going, and Yusif Stark was already waiting. Its limit is that it shows her the end of a road and gives her no way to change it, only to step off. Its counter is a road whose end she cannot see; its tell is that by the time anyone else understands, she has already packed.

## Physical Description

She was fair, and her hair was the Byakuya gold, the same as her sister's, long and loose to the waist with the length of it gathered over one shoulder in a low tail bound with a gold band; her eyes were a clear blue, steady, and seemed to be looking at something a long way down the road. She had a calm face and a small smile that came easily and went deep, and she tied her hair back on the training ground the same way her son ties his. At court she wore the long robes of her house, pale blue silk worked with dark blue peonies and edged in grey and white, with gold embroidery at the shoulders and a dark sash at the waist; at the king's side, the red harness of the Samurai of Fate, lamellar lacquered red as old blood and laced in white silk, which she wore six years and left in the white house in the Shiraki hills the night she went into the salt. In the Cuttings she wore whatever a salt-cutter wears, and no name.

## Lore

She was born to the Byakuya in the white house, twelve years before her sister, and their grandmother taught her the oath over the lamp and the knot and the order for dressing the armour. At fourteen, in the Yukari valley, she heard the Telling in front of Senri's stone, and asked for the name, and took it, and answered to it till she died. When the time came for a Byakuya to stand at the Kōkan king's side in the old way, as his sworn guard, it was Kasane they sent, and for six years she wore the red harness at Gimbzo's shoulder in every hall he walked into, and loved him, and married him before her grandmother's lamp because he asked her to and because she thought she could hold him. She tried to make him understand, for years. Yusif Stark was a big brother to her from the time she was a girl at the king's side.

On the night Gimbzo went down into the cellars under Halcrest with a lamp after the last of his own blood, she walked up out of them on her own feet with his son inside her coat, and never said his name again. The north says he took her; it was not that. Sumie Kurenai signed for a closed book over the child, and Shigure Tenrai saw and said nothing. Kasane sent four lines to her sister and went down three galleries into the Orrel Cuttings under no name the clerks would take, and cut salt, and dropped her own humming token down a dead shaft because a thing that hums can be heard. Gimbzo found her once more, when the boy was three, and the girl was his. She raised them on salt and stories of the Samurai of Fate told on the pallet, and showed them the Kōkan mark, and told them it was their name and not to show it to the clerks, and never told them whose it was. She said the salt was patient, and it would keep them. On the morning the criers came down the galleries with the news that the king had fallen at Shimane, she turned her face to the wall and did not turn it back, and died of salt-throat with her son and daughter sitting with her, and they put her in the salt. Her son was two seasons short of buying her number out of the drainage book.

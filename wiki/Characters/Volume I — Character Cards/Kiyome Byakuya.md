## VIII. Traits

**THE RED BEFORE THE WHITE (Inherited Trait)**
The Byakuya oath, spoken over every child of the house at nine with a grandmother's hand on her head, is in the blood as much as in the words: her Attraction Layer is bound to the Kōkan heir and his seat. Near him, keeping it, her Oath and Ward stand a Grade higher, fear reaches her late, and her hands do not leave the work. For sixteen years it bound her to nothing, a red line she believed had ended in the cellars, and she kept it anyway. It costs nothing to keep and everything to break. Its limit is its own reading, which she gave aloud at the Yukari's table: it binds her to the boy, and does not bind the boy to her; she can serve him and cannot command him, and when the king himself is holding the knife, as Gimbzo was, the oath has nothing to say. Its counter is to set the heir against his own good and make her choose; its tell is her lips moving over the words.

**GLASS (Passive Trait)**
There is something so still and so clean in her that wards go over it like water over glass and find nowhere to catch, and a reader's threads lie flat off her when she speaks the truth, which is always. It costs nothing. Its limit is that it hides nothing she chooses to show and protects nothing she is not; plain eyes see her perfectly, and she cannot lie through it, because she does not lie. Its counter is a plain question asked in plain light; its tell is that every reading of her comes back level.

**THE WHITE KNOT (Primary Trait)**
A Byakuya ties it when she goes into a house where she means to keep the peace, whatever comes: three quick turns of the white cord round the guard of the sword and the mouth of the scabbard, so that the blade cannot leave its sheath without the cord being cut. Fixatio holds the knot to the oath sworn over it, and while it is tied the peace she brought into the room holds a little better than it should: tempers come down, hands leave hilts, a parley lasts. It costs her about two per cent of her reserve to tie and nothing to keep. Its limit is that it holds her own peace and her own sword; it cannot make another man keep his. Its counter is to cut the cord, and the cutting costs the cutter, because the Dawi who made her sword felt her ancestor's knot in the steel for three hundred and twelve years and walked four hundred miles when it came undone. She untied it at the Foot, slowly, as if the movement had been waiting in her body for sixteen years, and gave the cord to her sister's son; it is Tsugigane's sageo now. It was always his.

**BROKEN AND MENDED (Secondary Trait)**
It formed at the Verdict of Six, when her voice shook in the Hall for the first time. When something strikes her the fine silver rings in her eyes turn once, very slowly, and her composure breaks, visibly, and mends in a breath; the breaking is the Trait as much as the mending, and the mending is real. Stability holds her through it. It costs her nothing she shows. Its limit is that it mends one break at a time; a second blow inside the breath finds her still open, and in the long room at the Foot, telling Muken who his mother was, it broke a second time and she did not mend it. Its counter is to strike twice before she mends; its tell is the rings.

## XIII. Physical Description

She is tall and very straight, as tall as Naevra Yukari, nearly, and pale, so that standing face to face with the Yukari they might be the same white figure and its reflection in dark water. Her hair is a warm gold, long, fine and loose, with heavy locks falling across her brow and into her eyes, and the length of it gathered low over one shoulder in a loose tail bound with a narrow band of gold. Her face is fine and long-boned, with a straight nose, a small composed mouth that lifts at one corner more often than people expect, and eyes of clear sea-glass green with fine silver rings in them that lie motionless as the face of a deep well and turn once, slowly, when something strikes her. The Moto sheen lies faint as frost along her brow and at her temples. She wears white: in the Hall, silk so near to white it seems under the lamps to give off a faint light of its own, crossed at the collar over an under-robe of soft blue and violet, with the veil of her line, fine glass beads, pinned back from her face so that they hang at her temples and click when she moves her head, and a halo-ring of pale metal at her crown; on the road, layered linen the white of fresh washing, grey at the hem from the dust and the slush. Her katana rides at her left hip in a white scabbard, without its knot. Her hands are long and pale, and since the Foot they have been red to the wrist more often than not.

## Lore

### Origin

She was born to the Byakuya in the white house, twelve years after her sister Kasane, and their grandmother taught them both the oath over the lamp at the hour of the evening bell, and the knot, and the order for dressing the armour of the red chair's guard. At nine she swore the oath on the prayer-room floor with her grandmother's hand on her head; that summer the houses swapped their children for a season, and she and Naevra Yukari lay on that floor and swore to be friends for ever. When the time came for a Byakuya to stand at the Kōkan king's side in the old way, it was Kasane they sent. Kiyome was the only witness, before their grandmother's lamp, when her sister married Gimbzo, because he asked her to, and because she loved him, and because she thought she could hold him.

### The Making

Then Hiroe came between the houses, and everything after it. On the night of the cellars a runner brought her four lines in her sister's hand: stay behind with our house; you are needed at home; Yusif Stark will keep me and the boy safe. She stayed. She looked for years and never found her, and when the council closed the book on the Kōkan she let herself believe Kasane had gone into it with them. She held her hall, sat the white chair, and kept the oath with nothing at the end of it. At the Verdict of Six she argued for delay and would not sign Torvald Stark's death, and stood through it, and alone knelt afterwards to close his eye. Eleven days later she rode east to the Yukari with Tetsugen Akagane and Hayato Amagiri and twenty riders and no banners, tied the white knot at the enclave gate, and told the Zettari Sovereign that the three lines were tired. There she learned what Stark crows and Shigure's testimony had been telling her: her sister had lived, and had a son.

### The Cost

At Kharven's Foot she saw a blank white banner and a boy with her sister's jaw, and her composure broke and mended. In the long room she drew the Kōkan mark in the dust and told him who his mother was, and said *is*, and did not take it back. When he swore no Moto would march on Zettari land, she untied the white knot she had worn into a house of peace and knelt to him and said the oath. She gave him the white house in the Shiraki hills. She sent four words by crow to Thornwall, *the red line lives*, and brought twelve hundred pikes onto the Skeldmere on a white horse. She worked her niece's surgeons' line after the shore, and sat the Waiting, and carried a stone to the cairn as if it were a child; she never once spoke to Yukazuri while she lived. She gave the cord to Borin for the boy's sword, and built the red harness overnight from stripped Tenrai iron, and dressed him in it in the old order, and thought he looked like every story her grandmother ever told, and like a boy in his dead mother's clothes.

### Where She Stands

This morning she stands beside the white chair in the Hall of Verdicts and does not sit, and speaks the oath with Hisae Thornwall, and kneels with her sword across her knees, and hands him the cloth after. She chose the women and men for the silver and gold chairs with him: someone with every reason to hate him and nothing to gain by it. He has her, who is his mother's sister, and who loves him, and whom he does not know.

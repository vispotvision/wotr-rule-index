### The Veil

The Byakuya guard has never let the seven lines see what stands at the king's shoulder. A guard whose strength is known is a guard whose strength is planned for. Under Gimbzo it became something more than custom: he put his own blood down the cellars for being strong enough to tell him no, and a Byakuya at Dissonance in the white chair would have gone down after them. So she let GLASS do what it does. Every reading of her comes back level, and what comes back level is whatever she chooses to show, which since she was fourteen has been a Stage VI lady with sense and no reach. It costs nothing; GLASS costs nothing. Its limit is the body: plain eyes see a woman whose hands do not shake when they should, and whose draw, if anyone ever saw it, would end the pretense at once. When she calls YUKIAKARI the Veil is gone and cannot be put back in front of anyone who saw it.

## VIII. Traits

**THE RED BEFORE THE WHITE (Inherited Trait)**
The Byakuya oath, spoken over every child of the house at nine with a grandmother's hand on her head, is in the blood as much as in the words: her Attraction Layer is bound to the Kōkan heir and his seat. Near him, keeping it, her Oath and Ward stand a Grade higher, fear reaches her late, and her hands do not leave the work. For sixteen years it bound her to nothing, a red line she believed had ended in the cellars, and she kept it anyway, and the pressure of keeping it with nothing at the end is what drove her up through the Stages alone in the white house: an oath with no object is a load with no ground under it, and her Crystal grew to carry it. It costs nothing to keep and everything to break. Its limit is its own reading, which she gave aloud at the Yukari's table: it binds her to the boy, and does not bind the boy to her; she can serve him and cannot command him, and when the king himself is holding the knife, as Gimbzo was, the oath has nothing to say and her blade will not leave its scabbard against him. Its counter is to set the heir against his own good and make her choose; its tell is her lips moving over the words.

**GLASS (Passive Trait)**
There is something so still and so clean in her that wards go over it like water over glass and find nowhere to catch, and a reader's threads lie flat off her when she speaks the truth, which is always. At Dissonance it also does the thing the Byakuya have used it for since the house began: it returns any reading level, and level is whatever she holds up to it, so that every assessor in the north has entered her at Stage VI for sixteen years. It costs nothing. Its limit is that it hides nothing she chooses to show and protects nothing she is not; plain eyes see her perfectly, and she cannot lie through it, because she does not lie. She has never once said she was Stage VI. She has only let people read it. Its counter is a plain question asked in plain light; its tell is that every reading of her comes back level.

**THE WHITE KNOT (Primary Trait)**
A Byakuya ties it when she goes into a house where she means to keep the peace, whatever comes: three quick turns of the white cord round the guard of the sword and the mouth of the scabbard, so that the blade cannot leave its sheath without the cord being cut. Fixatio holds the knot to the oath sworn over it, and while it is tied the peace she brought into the room holds a little better than it should: tempers come down, hands leave hilts, a parley lasts. It costs her about two per cent of her reserve to tie and nothing to keep. Its limit is that it holds her own peace and her own sword; it cannot make another man keep his. Its counter is to cut the cord, and the cutting costs the cutter, because the Dawi who made her sword felt her ancestor's knot in the steel for three hundred and twelve years and walked four hundred miles when it came undone. She untied it at the Foot, slowly, as if the movement had been waiting in her body for sixteen years, and gave the cord to her sister's son; it is Tsugigane's sageo now. It was always his. Her blade has been free to leave its scabbard since that day, and nobody but Tetsugen and Hayato understood what that meant. At Dissonance the knot survives in her as a working: MUSUBI.

**BROKEN AND MENDED (Secondary Trait)**
It formed at the Verdict of Six, when her voice shook in the Hall for the first time. When something strikes her the fine silver rings in her eyes turn once, very slowly, and her composure breaks, visibly, and mends in a breath; the breaking is the Trait as much as the mending, and the mending is real. Stability holds her through it, and at Dissonance it holds her Crystal too: the fracture of the four lines opens a hair every time she breaks and closes every time she mends, which is why she can stand at Stage XI without the Crystal running away from her. It costs her nothing she shows. Its limit is that it mends one break at a time; a second blow inside the breath finds her still open, and in the long room at the Foot, telling Muken who his mother was, it broke a second time and she did not mend it. Its counter is to strike twice before she mends; its tell is the rings.

## X. Techniques

**HAKKŌ · White Light (Signature Technique)** · mark: Mastered
The Byakuya draw. Her left thumb cuts the koiguchi, and Fulguria lays a channel of ionized air from the scabbard mouth to the place the cut will end, the way the faint stepped leader of a lightning stroke feels its way down to the ground before the bolt; for that breath a hair-fine violet line hangs in the air, and anyone with the eye can see exactly where she is going to cut. Then the blade comes out along it at the speed of the return stroke, and the cut is over before the sound of the draw arrives, and the thunder comes after, because the air along the line has been heated faster than it can get out of the way. It costs about four per cent of her reserve, near 5,790,000 EU, which delivers 5.33 TJ, S-Grade, about a kiloton and a quarter along a line a few inches wide. Its limit is the channel: the leader must have a path, and wet ground, rain, a grounded conductor or a body of water near the line pulls the channel off true before the blade can follow it, and a cut that loses its channel arrives at the speed of a very fast woman, which is still very fast. Before, the violet line is a full breath of tell; during, a grounded piece of iron thrust across the line takes the stroke; after, the thunder is loud enough to stun anyone near it who did not see it coming, her allies included.

**KAGAMI-WARI · the Mirror Split (Bloodline Art, Byakuya)** · mark: Mastered
The Byakuya Meigan sees hidden seams and the clean structural breach, and in her Luminalis turns that on workings as well as on steel and bone. She looks at a technique instead of at its owner, finds the seam where its boundary condition meets its law, and cuts there, and the working comes apart along its own join, like a mirror that falls in two clean halves. It costs about two per cent of her reserve, near 2,895,000 EU, and a heartbeat of looking that she cannot spend on anything else. Its limit is that a working must have a seam: a pure physical blow has none, a working that is all one law with no boundary has none, and a Trait, which is the soul's own law and not a boundary at all, gives her nothing to cut; she can split GŌKA at the seam where the fire is placed, and she can do nothing to ENSHŌ. Its counter is a working with no seam, or a fighter who never lets her stand still long enough to look.

**MUSUBI · the Knot (Application of Fixatio)** · mark: Drilled
What the white cord did to her own sword, done to anything she can see: three quick turns of white light round a thing, a raised blade, a fist in the air, a door, a fire's front, a working in the moment it fires, and Fixatio holds it where it was, in the state it was in, for as long as she keeps paying. It costs about three per cent of her reserve a turn of the breath, near 4,340,000 EU, and she can hold one thing at a time. Its limit is Sovereignty and sight: what she binds must be a thing she can see and name, and a practitioner whose Sovereignty outranks hers pulls out of the knot at a cost to both of them; she can hold a man's arm and not his will. Its counter is a will stronger than her Sovereignty, or a thing she cannot see; its tell is the three turns of light.

**SHINGAN · the heart-eye (Bloodline Faculty, Moto)** · mark: Mastered
The common Moto sight, in her held level and quiet, fine silver rings lying as still as the face of a deep well, so that the moment they turn is the moment everyone in the room learns something has struck her. With Luminalis it reads beneath a face to the mechanism under it as well as where a thing carries its weight. It costs about 4,000 EU a second held wide, about ten hours from full. It cannot read a man who is truly still inside, and it does not tell her what to do about what she sees.

## XIII. Physical Description

She is tall and very straight, as tall as Naevra Yukari, nearly, and pale, so that standing face to face with the Yukari they might be the same white figure and its reflection in dark water. Her hair is a warm gold, long, fine and loose, with heavy locks falling across her brow and into her eyes, and the length of it gathered low over one shoulder in a loose tail bound with a narrow band of gold. Her face is fine and long-boned, with a straight nose, a small composed mouth that lifts at one corner more often than people expect, and eyes of clear sea-glass green with fine silver rings in them that lie motionless as the face of a deep well and turn once, slowly, when something strikes her. The Moto sheen lies faint as frost along her brow and at her temples. She wears white: in the Hall, silk so near to white it seems under the lamps to give off a faint light of its own, crossed at the collar over an under-robe of soft blue and violet, with the veil of her line, fine glass beads, pinned back from her face so that they hang at her temples and click when she moves her head, and a halo-ring of pale metal at her crown; on the road, layered linen the white of fresh washing, gray at the hem from the dust and the slush. Her katana rides at her left hip in a white scabbard, without its knot. Her hands are long and pale, and since the Foot they have been red to the wrist more often than not. They do not shake when they should, which is the one thing about her a plain eye might have caught in sixteen years, and nobody did.

## Lore

### Origin

She was born to the Byakuya in the white house, twelve years after her sister Kasane, and their grandmother taught them both the oath over the lamp at the hour of the evening bell, and the knot, and the order for dressing the armor of the red chair's guard. At nine she swore the oath on the prayer-room floor with her grandmother's hand on her head; that summer the houses swapped their children for a season, and she and Naevra Yukari lay on that floor and swore to be friends for ever. When the time came for a Byakuya to stand at the Kōkan king's side in the old way, it was Kasane they sent. Kiyome was the only witness, before their grandmother's lamp, when her sister married Gimbzo, because he asked her to, and because she loved him, and because she thought she could hold him. Behind the closed door of the training ground the two sisters drew against each other a thousand mornings, and the younger one never once won, and never stopped trying.

### The Making

Then Hiroe came between the houses, and everything after it. On the night of the cellars a runner brought her four lines in her sister's hand: stay behind with our house; you are needed at home; Yusif Stark will keep me and the boy safe. She stayed. She looked for years and never found her, and when the council closed the book on the Kōkan she let herself believe Kasane had gone into it with them. She held her hall, sat the white chair, and kept the oath with nothing at the end of it, and in the white house, alone, behind the closed door, she kept drawing. An oath with no object is a load with nothing under it, and her Crystal grew to carry it, Threshold on Threshold, in a house nobody visited, while the seven lines read her at Glory and thought her too shallow to matter. That was the Byakuya way and it was also the only way to live under Gimbzo. At the Verdict of Six she argued for delay and would not sign Torvald Stark's death, and stood through it, and alone knelt afterwards to close his eye; she could have ended the six chairs in the time it took the blade to fall, and the oath had nothing to say, because the heir was dead and the king was the knife. Eleven days later she rode east to the Yukari with Tetsugen Akagane and Hayato Amagiri and twenty riders and no banners, tied the white knot at the enclave gate, and told the Zettari Sovereign that the three lines were tired. There she learned what Stark crows and Shigure's testimony had been telling her: her sister had lived, and had a son. That night, in the white house, the three of them told each other what they were. None of them was surprised.

### The Cost

At Kharven's Foot she saw a blank white banner and a boy with her sister's jaw, and her composure broke and mended. In the long room she drew the Kōkan mark in the dust and told him who his mother was, and said *is*, and did not take it back. When he swore no Moto would march on Zettari land, she untied the white knot she had worn into a house of peace and knelt to him and said the oath. She gave him the white house in the Shiraki hills. She sent four words by crow to Thornwall, *the red line lives*, and brought twelve hundred pikes onto the Skeldmere on a white horse. She worked her niece's surgeons' line after the shore, and sat the Waiting, and carried a stone to the cairn as if it were a child; she never once spoke to Yukazuri while she lived, and she has not forgiven herself for being the strongest woman in the north and on the wrong road that day. She gave the cord to Borin for the boy's sword, and built the red harness overnight from stripped Tenrai iron, and dressed him in it in the old order, and thought he looked like every story her grandmother ever told, and like a boy in his dead mother's clothes.

### Where She Stands

This morning she stands beside the white chair in the Hall of Verdicts and does not sit, and speaks the oath with Hisae Thornwall, and kneels with her sword across her knees, and hands him the cloth after. She chose the women and men for the silver and gold chairs with him: someone with every reason to hate him and nothing to gain by it. He has her, who is his mother's sister, and who loves him, and whom he does not know, and who is, by a long way, the strongest thing standing between him and the door, and he does not know that either.

Tall for a northern woman, nearly Bram's height, built with the angular, long-limbed frame the Stormfold produces in women whose families have lived on the ridge for generations. Hair the colour of dark straw, tied in a knot with a strip of linen that was white once. Face long and angular, cheekbones high and sharp, jaw set with the determination northern women wear the way northern men wear brigandine.
Eyes pale green, lichen on granite, performing assessment before her hands arrive. She reads Bram's silence accurately: not shock (cold skin, rapid breathing, dilated pupils) but choice. The man has chosen not to speak because the man is holding something inside the silence that will break if the silence is broken.
*He is not in shock. His body is present and functional. The silence is the man's choice. The man is holding something inside the silence, and the holding is the wound I cannot treat, because the wound is not medical. The wound is a brother's smile.*

### Lore · The Life Behind the Card

#### Origin

Lilia was Concord-born, in a ridge settlement of the Stormfold, to a family that had lived on the ridge for generations. The wind never dropped, the ground ran steep, and every mouthful was earned by work and spent on work. From the age of nine she hauled wet wool and dead weight. Her mother broke a leg once. Josse Hillborn, the farrier, set it and would not take the fee.
She came into the Greymane house young and took the work nobody fought her for: the linen, the sick-room, the burials. Lye and cold cracked her knuckles open. Her nails were cut to the quick, and the creases of her fingers kept a grey-brown stain no scrubbing moved. The Greymanes held the southern approach, and the ridge sent its men back cut. She told enough of them they would die to know how that sentence is said, and how its opposite is.

#### The Making

In time she was the estate's nurse and field medic. She kept her infirmary in the great hall's eastern end, where the roof still held and a fire could be kept against the wind.
Bram Greymane was carried in on two spear-shafts and a cloak, from the field where his brother Wren had stood under the Tenrai banner. He had two long cuts down his left side and would not speak. Lilia came through the column reading bodies as she walked.
"Lie still," she told him.
She pressed the longer cut first, because it bled more. "Shallow," she said aloud, for anyone in earshot. A hobgoblin blade with a short edge had chopped him on the turn, and the brigandine had taken the force. His skin was warm. His breathing was slow, his pupils sound. She judged the quiet to be his choice, and what he kept inside it a brother's smile.
She said his name and made him look at her. Then she told him: "You are going to live. The wounds are shallow. I am going to clean and close both cuts, and the cleaning will hurt more than the hobgoblins did, and the hurting is the treatment, and the treatment is not negotiable."
She sent the lower garrison's field medic for hot water, clean linen and the sewing kit. "I do not do stitching that fails," she said. He went at her word, though she held no commission. She closed both cuts one suture after the next and stitched the brigandine shut along the left side. His right hand stayed closed on the shield's rim.

#### The Cost

She could close what the hobgoblins had opened. The wound Wren opened lay beyond her needle, and she worked on what she could reach.
The shoulder came next. She ran a rope through a ring bolt in the ceiling of his chamber and set him twenty-one pulls, and she would know if he did nineteen. She had seen the hate he was running on. She made a decision about it and never told him what it was. She did not argue. She put up the ring bolt.
After the cellar he stopped eating, and she saw it and said nothing. She could have set a bowl before him. He would have eaten to make her go. She held back, because a man she managed could not stand in front of his men, and she would rather have him thin.
Letters from the capital came up with the salt cart, and she put them into his hand. He asked for the rest. She gave him the yard. Half wanted Josse hanged that afternoon, half wanted him let go over the ridge, and all had eaten at his fire. She said Renard's guards on the cellar door were correct, and that nobody had thanked him. "I am the wrong person to ask. I have set that man's arm and I have buried his wife." She said one thing more. "I have watched three garrisons go bad and every one of them went bad slowly and every one of them had a yard."
She had shortened the shield's elbow strap two fingers' width. She restitched it with waxed thread and never mentioned it. When Bram took the shield up, it hung true off the shoulder she had worked. He looked at the stitching and did not look at her. He went past her toward the cellar stair, where the man whose arm she had set was waiting.

#### Where They Stand

She keeps the infirmary at the east end of the great hall. The supplies Hild Ice sent south with Brida Ashwell went to Bram's wounded there. When the Crown's column halted in the Stormfold, its surgeon, Nella Forgeman, worked beside her over the wounded of the southern approach. Lilia kept the running of the room. Nella took the cases she was given.
The archive leaves her in Bram's chamber, the letter face down on his table. She has never told him the decision she made about his hate. He knows what she thinks from the ring bolt, and neither of them has spoken of the strap. The wound that Wren opened does not close.

## VIII. Traits

**STARK RIME (Inherited Trait)**
The cold in the Stark blood. Heat leaves everything near him the way it leaves a house with the door open in winter: the air within a few paces of him drops, timber silvers with frost, snow stiffens and squeaks, breath that should steam round him does not, and the heat goes somewhere, down through his body and out through his heels into the ground, which is why the Starks walk the way they walk. It runs harder under feeling. When his jaw tightens on the left side, the frost spreads. It costs him nothing he notices; he has never been warm in his life and does not miss it. Its limit is that it moves heat, and cannot make it: on frozen ground or in deep winter it has little to take, and against a great heat it is a bucket against a fire. Its counter is heat, and calm: a hot room, a furnace, a fire-worker; or keeping him steady, since a Stark who is not angry is only a cold man.

**A LINE IN THE SNOW (Primary Trait)**
House Stark defines where things end and begin, and his soul did that before he ever drew a sword. He draws the point of his blade through the ground, snow or earth or a stone floor, and the line becomes a border his Attraction Layer holds: the Stark rime gathers along it into a low white wall of cold a hand high and as long as he drew it, and anyone who crosses it toward what he guards crosses into the full cold, their fingers stiffening, their breath catching, their footing gone to rime. It costs about 2% of his reserve to draw and a little to hold. He must stand on his side of it; the line is his, and if he steps over it himself it is only a scratch in the snow. Its limit is its length: as long as one stroke of the arm and a step, a few yards, never a wall round a city. Its counter is to go round the ends, or to come at him from the side the line does not face; its tell is the point of the sword dragging through the ground.

**BY THE SKY THAT COVERS US (Secondary Trait)**
His father took him onto the wall-walk in the dark and told him all of it in twelve sentences, and made him swear by the sky that covers us to carry it until it could be carried by someone with a better right, and then rode south to the Kurenai blade. Lorn carried it. When he swears by the sky, with his right fist pressed once, hard, to his breastbone, the oath sets in him like frost in a crack: while he is keeping it, fear does not reach him, pain arrives late, and the arm that holds what he swore to hold does not open. It costs him nothing to swear; it costs everything to keep. Its limit is the oath's own words; it holds what he said and nothing else, and he chooses his words like a man choosing footing on ice. Its counter is the oldest one in the north: set two of his oaths against each other, so that keeping one breaks the other, and watch him freeze between them.

## X. Techniques

**THE BREAKING COLD (Signature Technique)** · mark: Drilled
Steel has a temperature below which it stops bending and starts breaking, the way a horseshoe left out in the hard frost will snap where in summer it would only have bent. On the bind, blade to blade, he pours the Stark rime into the other man's steel through his own, and in a breath the enemy's sword or axe-head or mail drops below that point, frost-flowers blooming across it, and the next hard blow it takes shatters it like glass. It costs about 5% of his reserve, near 11,000 EU, delivering some five gigajoules of cold, B-Grade. It works only on iron and ordinary steel; bronze and copper and the few fine steels that never go brittle in the cold take no harm from it at all. Before, carry bronze or copper; during, the frost-flowers on the blade are the tell, and the answer is to disengage and let the steel warm before you strike with it again; after, a blade that survived its chill is as good as it was.

**THE STARK WALK (House Art, Stark)** · mark: Mastered · page: House Stark
Heavy, even, unhurried, the weight going straight down through the heels into the ground and the hands still at the sides. It is how the Starks cross their own halls and how they cross a battlefield, and it is a fighting stance: a man walking it cannot be shouldered off his line or tripped, and he can draw from it without changing his feet. It is beaten by making him hurry, because a Stark who runs has stopped walking it.

**FROSTFALL (First Release)** · folk name: the White Sky
Called low: *the sky covers us.* His breath stops steaming, his beard and the fur of his collar go white with rime, a skin of clear ice runs over his father's mail, and the snow in a ring round him turns to silver and creaks like a lake taking weight. STARK RIME strikes at the top of its Grade, and A LINE IN THE SNOW runs twice as long. It holds 3 minutes from a full reserve, about 1,220 EU a second. Silent, the rime stays on his hands and blade and goes no further. Afterward he shakes, and the cold he has been holding comes into him all at once, and he needs a fire for the first time in his life. Its counter is heat, as for the Trait, and its own end: when it falls, the ground round him is ice.

## XIII. Physical Description

He is a big man, broad and long-armed, and he looks older than twenty-two; the forty-year war and one winter have put grey into his hair already, long streaks of it through the black at the temples and over the brow, and he wears it long and swept back from his face, loose to the collar. His face is long and weathered, high-browed, the nose straight and once broken, and he wears a full dark beard kept short and square, with grey coming into it at the chin. His eyes are Stark-blue, pale and cold, and raw at the rims from too many nights without sleep. His jaw tightens on the left side before he speaks, and works there when he is holding something in. He wears his father's mail, too big for him across the shoulders, under a heavy coat of brown leather and a great mantle of black bear-fur that swallows his shoulders; a long scarf of pale blue-grey wool wound high round his throat against the wind, the Stark wolf on his breast, and on either side of the mantle a long northern braid of leather cord hung with a pendant of rough clear crystal in a silver cage, the kind the Hollenmark women plait for a son riding to war. His father's axe rides across his back. He stands with his weight even on both feet and his hands still at his sides, exactly as his father taught him, and when he moves he moves like a Stark: heavy, even, unhurried.

## Lore

### Origin

Lorn Stark was born at Hollenmark, in the eastern highlands near the Ketsuen border, in the house that defines where things end and begin. His father was Ulf Stark, Warlord of the Starks. His brothers were Rob and Mark. The Starks were the first house to take the Kōkan alliance and the only one to take it undefeated, and for forty years they held the passes against the Zettari with the Greymanes, and buried their sons in it, and Lorn was raised on the drill-yard and the wall-walk to be one of the ones who held.

### The Making

The night before his father rode south, Ulf took him up onto the wall-walk in the dark and told him all of it, plainly, in twelve sentences, and made him swear by the sky that covers us to carry it until it could be carried by someone with a better right. Then Ulf and Wystan Greymane rode down the Kessen road to the Hall of Verdicts and the Kurenai blade, and Lorn put on his father's mail, which was too big for him, and his father's axe, and went to stand by the wall in the long room at the Foot.

He said *sire* to the boy every morning and carried the secret. When it came out, he said his piece from the wall and went back to the floor three feet in front of him. *Tell him Yusif never once in sixteen years called him Gimbzo's boy. Tell him Yusif called him Kasane's.* At the woodpile he pressed his fist to his chest. On the frozen road, when the king lost his mind and ran for his sister with no sword and no horse, Lorn caught him from behind and held on while the king drove an elbow into his ribs and tried to throw him, and set his feet the way his father had taught him, and did not let go. *I know she's on that road. But look at you.* He swore it to him by the sky and his father's death and his honour, and they went back.

When it was over and they sat on the floor of the death-house with nothing to say, it was Lorn who fed the fire, and Lorn who said, after a long time, that the first bowl goes to the one who can't fill it himself.

### Where He Stands

This morning he comes down the shallow steps of the black floor in his father's mail and stands at the foot of the dais and puts his fist to his chest and holds it there. *By the sky that covers us. House Stark. To the red chair, and to the man in it. My father would have. And Rob.* Four hundred Stark riders wait in the square below. Behind him in the Hall stand the seven chairs, and in one of them sits the woman in black and ash who carried the blade to his father's neck a month ago. She does not look at him. He does not look at her.

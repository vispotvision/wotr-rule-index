## Traits

**STARK RIME (Inherited Trait)**
The cold in the Stark blood, thin in him yet: frost on the fur of his collar when he is angry or afraid, and the heat going down through his heels into the ground. Its counter is heat, and calm.

**KEPT HOLD (Primary Trait)**
On the Vessant road the cub bit through his shield and took his forearm to the bone, raked his belly and his face and his good arm, and he kept hold of the short sword the whole time. His soul learned that first: whatever has him, the hand does not open. While he grips a weapon, nothing short of breaking the hand takes it from him, and his Fortitude reads a Grade high for as long as he holds on. It costs nothing. Its limit is that it holds the sword, not the man; he can be knocked down holding it. Its counter is to take the hand, not the blade.

**THE MUTTER (Secondary Trait)**
He talks when he is frightened, a steady stream of it under his breath, and the talking keeps his feet under him. While he mutters, fear does not reach his hands. It costs nothing. Its limit is the talking: the moment it stops, everything he has been holding off arrives at once, which is why his brother used to say "Aye" to him across a fight, to give him something to answer. Its counter is to make him stop.

## Signature

**THE PIN (Signature Technique)** · mark: Drilled · Source: Lived
Down on his knees inside the reach of whatever is on him, the blade driven up behind the foreleg or under the arm to the hilt, and then he holds it there, sobbing if he has to, while it dies. It costs about five per cent of his reserve to set the rime through the arm and hold, near 670 EU. Its limit is that he has to be under the thing to do it, and being under the thing is how he got his arm torn open. Its counter is not to let him get underneath you.

## Physical Description

He is tall and lean, built quicker than his brothers and not yet as broad. His hair is a coppery gold going to rust, long and loose to the shoulders and pushed back off his brow; he wears a short red beard now, trimmed close along the jaw, and under it the face is still the freckled one the company knew, long and straight-nosed, with pale blue Stark eyes. The cub's claws left a thin pale scar down his left cheek from the brow, and his left forearm under the glove is a long knotted seam where it was torn to the bone. He wears a heavy gray-brown wolf-fur mantle over a dark gray-blue cloak, pinned at the shoulders with two round brass Stark badges joined by a gilt chain across a worked steel breastplate; under it a long tunic of deep oxblood red, embroidered at the cuffs in pale gold, black gloves, and a broad belt of dark leather hung with rings and a long dagger. He carries a long northern sword low in his right hand, the way a man carries a thing he has not finished getting used to.

## Lore

He was the youngest of Ulf Stark's sons, and he went south out of Hollenmark two winters back with his brother Rob for reasons neither of them ever told anyone, and joined the Lanternless in Halcrest. On the Vessant road the cub came at him over his shield and he went down screaming, high and cracked, like a boy, with its jaws in his forearm, and kept hold of the short sword, and drove it in behind the foreleg up to the hilt and held it there while Rob finished it. *That was the small one, Sarge.* He rode into the river post with his arm splinted to the shoulder and a fever just broken, and a dog that was not his followed him about for three days. He knelt at the woodpile beside Rob and hailed the king. At the Skeldmere he was on the shore when the gray men came out of the lifting for the surgeons' line, and he rode to Muken with a black-glass arrow in his arm and his face the color of the snow, and said they had taken her, and that he had tried. He sat the Waiting with his arm strapped across his chest and laid a stone on her cairn. At the Seat he reached Rob in the Hall square a breath after Lorn, and that night he sat beside his brother's body in the Tenrai keep under a Stark cloak, and in the spring he rode home to Hollenmark beside the sledge.

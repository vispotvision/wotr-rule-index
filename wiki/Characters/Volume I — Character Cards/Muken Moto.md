## VIII. Traits

**EYE OF THE SPLINTERED WORK (Primary Trait)**
Before he ever knew it had a name he read a salt face for the place it would give before he set the pick, and his soul still does that before it tries anything else. Fractura runs through his Perception: within about fifty paces he sees where a made thing, a body or a line of men is carrying its weight, and where that weight has gathered in a crack, as fine gold lines laid over the world like crazing in old glaze. A locked arm, a knee that takes all the load, a wall built new over an old frost-seam, a clan wedge with a feud running down the middle of it: he sees them all, without trying, and his worst flaw is always the one he sees first. It costs him nothing he notices at rest and a slow heat behind the left eye under strain. Its limit is that it only reads load and fault; a thing truly whole, freshly forged or simply soft gives him nothing, and it shows him where a man will break, never what the man means. Its counter is to offer nothing flawed, or to move faster than a fifty-pace read can follow; its tell is the heat and the widening gold seam in his left iris. He has learned, since the war, that it does not switch off at the supper table.

**THE UNWRITTEN NAME (Inherited Trait)**
The Kōkan wrote their name in a mark no register in the inner world can read, and Muken carries that blood without the mark ever having been entered against him. Every Nalūn seal-token, every Moto fate-read and every working that finds a person by the house written over them looks at him and finds a page with no ink on it: Ulf Stark's token went cold and silent in his palm, and Shigure Tenrai's fate-rings flickered and slid off him on the stair. It costs nothing and he cannot turn it off. Its limit is that it only hides him from things that read by name and lineage; plain eyes, a blade and a good guess see him as well as anyone. Its counter is exactly that: fight him with your eyes open and your fate-sight shut. It cuts him too, since no token will ever hum for him and no house seal will bind under his name until the Kōkan mark is set on it by hand.

**DEBT RECOGNITION (Secondary Trait)**
It formed in the three days on the Seat road after the shore, when every man he looked at was either bringing her back or keeping her from him. Judicium marks, within about sixty paces, the people who mean him or his harm and the people who want something from him, the way a nose marks smoke: a pressure in the Eye, ranked worst first. It costs a little of his reserve while it runs hot and almost nothing when he is calm. It is strongest against intent that is clear and loud, and it goes quiet against a man who truly wants nothing; it marked Amelia Greymane as a solid, quiet weight, and that is all it could tell him. Its counter is to want nothing and to stand very still; its tell is his head turning toward a threat a moment before the threat moves. Its risk is that it marks appetite as readily as malice, and a king who reads everyone at his table as a debt stops sitting at tables.

**STONES ON A CAIRN (Passive Trait)**
Since the cairn on the ridge his voice comes lower and slower when he means it, each word set on the last like stones laid one on another, and the Attraction in it carries: cups tremble, lamps on their chains swing all together, and people who were talking stop. Kiyome Byakuya heard it in the death-house and thought it the first true Moto voice she had heard in her life. It costs nothing. Its limit is that it rides his voice and his conviction; it carries in a hall and dies in a battle's noise, and it does nothing when he does not mean what he says. Its counter is noise and distance; its tell is the lamps.

## X. Techniques

**SHINGAN · the heart-eye (Bloodline Faculty, Moto)** · mark: Drilled
The common Moto sight, woken on the Foot's stair: the eye reads where everything is carrying its weight. In Muken it came with something the other lines do not have, a hairline seam of gold running out from the amber fleck low in his left iris, and Shigure named it on the spot: any Moto brat can see where a wall wants to fall, and the gold in it is Kōkan. The seam is the first of the Kamigan, the crown eye that takes what all the other eyes see and ranks it; it has not opened, and Faisal ties its opening to the Work of Totality he has withheld. Holding the Shingan open costs about 150 EU a second, near five per cent of his reserve a minute, paid as heat behind the eye; each time he forces it past its depth the seam widens by a thread and does not close again. It resolves load and fault in matter, bodies and formations; it cannot resolve intent, a lie, or anything with no structure. Its counter is the same as the Eye's: give it nothing flawed, or nothing it can see.

**HAMETSU NO GŌ · the Work of Ruins (Bloodline Art, Hataraki)** · mark: Drilled
The Work his blood leans to, taught by Faisal in the Plane of Fate: the power to make a thing end. Every made thing has a note, and a crack has a note of its own; a dead crack, rot already through, rings sour and wavering against the steady note of the sound stone around it. He finds the dead note with the Eye, takes it in the Kharven breath, and holds it, and the weight that has gathered in the crack carries the crack forward for him until the dead thing comes apart along its own fault and nothing else does. A rope's worth of rot costs him about two per cent of his reserve, some 2,300 EU, and a few hundred EU a second while he holds it; the lying ward-stone over the Seat's gatehouse, held from half a mile on the Marrow ice, cost him a fifth. Its limit is his oath and its own law: held to the dead crack only, it leaves every sound stone untouched, and he has sworn by his honour to crack nothing that is not already dead; driven by rage, it ends what was rotten and then what was near the rot, and it does not stop. Its counter is to break the note, with noise, damping, cold or a hand flat on the struck thing, or to bring him nothing already dead; its tell is the breath, in through the nose until the ribs ache and out through the mouth until nothing is left.

**JUNKETSU NO GŌ · the Work of Purity (Bloodline Art, Hataraki)** · mark: Held
Purity establishes a thing in its proper shape and holds it there. He learned it by learning to look at the edges of a wound and never the wound, the way his sister did: he lays his palm flat over the hurt, listens for the living edges, and holds their note until they close. He knit his own broken ribs, the cut across his side, his forearm and his palm with it. A fresh wound costs about five per cent of his reserve and a quiet minute; old harm the body has already set crooked, the wrist from the stamping floor, the shin from the Veyrath ditch, his knuckles, it will not touch, because their proper shape is now the crooked one. It works only on what is living and willing; Faisal forbade him to do it to a man who has not asked. Its Obsession face purges whatever does not fit the shape, which is the Shirogane way. Its counter is to hurt him faster than he can listen; its tell is the flat palm and the silence.

**SEIREI NO GŌ · the Work of Spirit (Bloodline Art, Hataraki)** · mark: Held
The Work of what lies between life and death. He can feel a soul's thread where it runs, and hold it, or open his hand and let it pass. He held his sister's on the road out without knowing he was doing it, and let her go in the Plane of Fate, and wept for the first time since he was nine. It costs little in Essence and a great deal otherwise. It cannot call a thread back that has gone, nor keep one that will not stay. Its counter is distance and time; its tell is his closed hand.

**JIKAN NO SHIGOTO · the Work of Temporality (Bloodline Art, Hataraki)** · mark: Held
The Work of cause and consequence. Holding one thread, he can follow it back along what made it, a knot at a time, as he followed his sister's death back to the Shirogane's thousand-year wound of standing second. It costs about one per cent of his reserve a knot and leaves him heavy-headed. It shows causes; it changes none, and a long thread takes long hours. Its counter is a thread too tangled to follow before the moment passes.

**CHISHIKI NO SHIGOTO · the Work of Knowledge (Bloodline Art, Hataraki)** · mark: Held
Knowledge reveals what a thing is. He hears what a man is humming under his face: fear, grief, appetite. It costs about one per cent of his reserve a reading. It reveals and does not act, and it will tell him Kagenori Tenrai is grieving without telling him what to do about it. Its counter is a man who has gone quiet inside; its tell is his head tipping as if listening.

**BARANSU NO SHIGOTO · the Work of Balance (Bloodline Art, Hataraki)** · mark: Held
He learned it kneeling beside Faisal under the Lawbell's weight: a load need not stop in him, it can pass through and go down into the ground. Petralon gives it footing. It costs about three per cent of his reserve to open and a slow bleed while he holds it. It needs ground under him; airborne, braced on ice or on a floor that will not take the load, it has nowhere to send the weight. Its counter is to take the ground away.

**SHIGOTO NO KABE · the Work of Bearing Load (Application of Balance)** · mark: Held
When he stands in front of something that means to come down on everyone behind him, he plants both feet and becomes the wall: Balance takes the blow through his bones and into the earth, and he does not move. It costs about four per cent of his reserve to set, near 4,600 EU, and 200 EU a second to hold, some eight and a half minutes from full; and it cracks his bones a little every time. It holds against a load the ground can take; against a load that comes from every side at once, or from below, it has no path. Its counter is to make him move, or to hit him from where the ground is not.

**ZENTAI-SEI NO HATARAKI · the Work of Totality** · withheld
The crown of the other Works and the Kōkan's own, tied to the Kamigan. Faisal has not taught it and will not until Muken can stand before seven lines, a dozen Banner Houses and a Zettari prince and want every one of them to stay exactly what they are and still be one thing together. Its Obsession face is the hunger to gather everything into one hand; his father reached for it, and that was what the cellars were. Muken said he would wait.

**Releases**

**HIBI · the Hairline (First Release)** · folk name: the Crack
Called "*Fure*," shake, the word the Kōkan used before it was taken from the line in the cellars at Halcrest and Faisal gave it back. On the call the blade begins to ring at the natural note of one thing before him, a single blade, a bone, a plate of armour, an iron hand, and holds it there; Fractura and the Eye find that thing's worst flaw and the held note piles the strain at the crack's tip until it runs on its own. One target, one crack. It costs about eight per cent of his reserve to wake, near 9,200 EU, and 800 EU a second to hold, about a minute and fifty-eight seconds from full to Starvation; the price shows as heat behind the eyes and a tremor in the sword hand that grows with each new note he tunes to. Called silent, the blade only hums and the crack runs slow. It needs a flaw; something whole gives it nothing. A blade held on a note too long finds its own: the scrap-iron sword broke near its guard on the shore. Its counter is to kill the note with cold, padding or a hand flat on the struck steel, to change what he must read before he can hold one pitch, or to bring nothing flawed; its tell is the Kharven breath before it.

**HIBIWARE · the Crazing (Second Release)** · woken, not called
The single crack becomes a web: the blade rings at the note of everything around him at once, and the rings go out felt in the long bones and the back teeth, finding every hairline flaw in whatever they pass. It woke once, out of him, in the rage after his sister, and crazed the snow of the Foot's yard and the ends of the woodpile; Faisal named it and forbade it until he can do to every rope in a city what he did to one rope and leave every sound one standing. He is Stage V, and the Second Release belongs to Refraction; called now it would draw on reserve he does not have and fracture the Crystal along its grief. He re-closed the crazed snow by holding its note, which is the only thing about it he has mastered. A third waking lies under it; Faisal will not speak of it.

## XIII. Physical Description

He is the size of a grown man and then some, long in the arm and heavy through the shoulders and the back of the neck, with the forearms of someone who swung a pick before he swung a blade. His hair went white at the temples at thirteen and is white all through now, worn long and mostly uncombed past his shoulders, tied back with a strip of leather when he works and loose when he does not. His skin is a deep brown, roughened at the knuckles and cracked white in the creases the way every hand raised in the Cuttings goes; in the six months since the war it has gone ashy under the brown, and his hands shake when he has not slept. His eyes are slate grey, set deep under a heavy brow, with a fleck of amber low in the left one and, running out from it, a seam of gold fine as a thread that never quite fades; ring-lines show in both irises when the Eye is open. The left front tooth is chipped to a corner, and when he smiles, which is rarely, it makes him look about twelve. A pale seam of scar runs through his left eyebrow, and a long raised line from the hinge of his jaw to the corner of his left eye where a goblin-thing's broken hand opened his cheek on the Vessant road; there is a scar across the back of his right hand, a callus across the base of his right fingers from Yusif's ash stick, a long pale seam from his left armpit across the ribs where Shigure's sword went through the mail, and scars on the right forearm and the palm. His right wrist was broken on the stamping floor at eleven and set crooked, and stayed crooked; every knuckle on both hands has cracked and healed and cracked again. At court he wears a kimono of black silk worked in gold dragons whose coils pour from both shoulders down the sleeves, the unified banner of the seven lines in gold thread down the back, and he wears it open to the waist with the sash loose and crooked because there is no one left in the tower to tell him to close it. In the field he wears the Samurai of Fate harness, red lacquer so deep it is nearly black, laced in white, with a crestless helmet of bare black iron. Tsugigane rides through his sash at the left hip, edge up.

## Lore

### Origin

He was carried up out of the cellars under Halcrest inside his mother's coat, on the night his father went down them with a lamp after the last of his own line, and raised three galleries down in the Orrel Cuttings under no name the clerks would take. His mother was Kasane Byakuya, who had worn the red harness at the Kōkan king's side and loved him the whole way down; she cut salt and told her son stories of the Samurai of Fate on the pallet, and showed him a mark like a knot and a little like a closed eye, and told him it was their name and not to show it to the clerks. His hand could never draw it. His sister's could. A big quiet man with a grey beard came down four galleries once a season with salt-meat and wool and taught him the stick from the age of eight, and never gave his name, and said that you protect the weak and defend the man next door and keep the north quiet so the children can sleep in it, and that everything else is just weather. That was Yusif Stark, who knew exactly whose son he was teaching and called him Kasane's.

### The Making

At thirteen a steward asked him what his oldest was, the merchant's question, and he answered with his fists and broke the man's nose, and Yusif stopped coming. At fourteen he sold his sword. At sixteen the Lanternless made him sergeant, the youngest they ever had, and a month after the war ended and a month after his mother died of salt-throat on the morning the Shimane news came down the galleries, he took a convoy up the Vessant road for double pay and killed a thing he named Big Bertha with scrap iron and a candle's worth of essence. The road was Ulf Stark's test. Ulf gave him a dead Stark token and his word and a lieutenancy, and at Kharven's Foot he read the frost-seam in the new Tenrai wall, switched the ladders, and took the keep, and on its stair Shigure Tenrai knew his face and the Shingan woke gold in his eye. Ulf and Wystan Greymane swore him the powers of their houses and rode south to the Kurenai blade. The Foot knelt to him by the woodpile without being told. Kiyome Byakuya drew the mark in the dust of the long room and told him who he was, and the Zettari prince told him who had killed his father, and he chose not to hate him. He told his sister first, kneeling in the surgeons' tent, and she drew the mark in her own blood and held it up to the yard. He named himself Muken Moto, First of his name, son of Kasane Moto and son of Gimbzo, and swore that no Moto would march on Zettari land while he breathed.

On the Skeldmere he broke the Iron Fist of the Keld with a note Faisal gave back to him, and spared him. On the shore behind him the grey men took his sister.

### The Cost

She did not speak for three days in the carriage. In the Shirogane house she told them her brother would kill every last one of them, and Karogane cut once. Godfrey brought her home off the ice in his cloak. They buried her under the last cairn on the ridge above the Foot, and Muken drew the mark in the frost on his stone and for the first time in his life it came out right. Borin Ironheart folded her needles into the broken sword. In the Plane of Fate Faisal taught him six Works and withheld the seventh, and Muken let her go. He took Kharven-Seat in three days, broke only what was already dead, sat the red chair his father never sat, and killed Shizuha Shirogane and Karogane himself at the first bell, once each, cleanly, because he had passed the verdict and would not hand it to someone else. Then he went to bed in the old Kōkan house and listened for her note, and it was not there.

### Where He Stands

Six months on, the taverns under the Seat still sing about him and get the numbers wrong. He sits in the window of the east tower in a dragon kimono he does not close, Godfrey is his Hand and Lorn heads his Guard, every lord in the Hall looks at him as if he knows what he is doing, and he does not know how to tax a river town or how to sleep. He has split nine oak bedframes dreaming of her. This afternoon Godfrey brought his sister up the stairs, Amelia of the Shieldwardens, red-haired and as tall as he is, with a ruined left hand and a cracked pauldron she will not let him pay to mend; he told her he was afraid of what he would do to a wife, and she put her ruined hand in his, and it did not crack. They are going down to the yard to spar.

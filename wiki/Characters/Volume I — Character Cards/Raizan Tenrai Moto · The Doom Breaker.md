## VIII. Traits

**THE BLINK · Mabataki (Primary Trait)**
The eye is blind while it moves. Every time a man's gaze jumps from one point to the next, a dozen times a breath, his brain stops taking in what he sees for that tenth of a breath and paints over the gap with what was there before, and every time he blinks the world goes dark for longer, and he never notices either. Raizan moves only in those gaps. The Tengan times every pair of eyes in a room, their jumps and their blinks, and his body crosses the floor in the spaces where nobody is looking, so that he is seen standing by the door and then seen standing by the bed and never seen walking between. It is constant and nearly costless, about half a percent of his reserve a minute while he is crossing ground. Its limit is the number of eyes: one man's gaps are many, three men's gaps overlap less, and in a crowded hall the moments when every eye in the room is blind at once are rare, short and far apart. It does nothing against a sense that does not blink: a beastkin's nose, a hound's ears, a blind fighter's hearing. Its counter is the crowd or the dark: surround the target with watchers who look in different directions, or put the guard out of the light and let him listen. The tell, for anyone who knows it, is a whole room blinking at once.

**DOOM-BREAKING · Unmei-wari (Secondary Trait)**
The Tenrai read a man's doom: the knot of all the ways his next hours could go, drawn tight round the likeliest. Raizan reads it and breaks it. He watches a target for a full minute without blinking himself, and in the knot he finds the one strand where the man dies, and the single moment when every other strand thins at once, when the guard's eyes are on the door, the mail has ridden up, the dog has gone out, the luck has run. Like the still point between two waves that cancel, that moment is real and very narrow, and he strikes into it, and the knot collapses onto that strand. It costs about 8% of his reserve, some 723,000 EU, and something he cannot get back: every doom he breaks, the Tengan shows him his own last morning a little closer, as if a year of his own futures went into the gap with the other man's. He has broken a great many. He can see the morning now, quite clearly. It needs the minute of watching, close enough to read, and he will not work without it. It cannot find the death-strand in a man whose futures are truly open, a man in the middle of a real choice, nor read a doom that a stronger fate-sight is already holding; a Kōkan Kamigan ranks above a Tenrai Tengan, and under that eye his reading blurs. Its counter is to be unpredictable, to stand beside someone whose eye is stronger, or to never give him the minute.

**TENGAN · Eye of Fate (Inherited Trait)**
The Tenrai line's sight: probability pressure, convergence, the near-future paths of bodies and blades as lines of light that lean the way things are likely to go. In him it is fifty years sharper than in any Tenrai alive. It costs him nothing to keep open, and it is always open, so he has not slept a full night since he was twenty. Its limit is likelihood: it shows him what will probably happen, and anything genuinely improbable surprises him like anyone else. Its counter is a ward that returns or blurs a reading, Oblivara's or a Tenebra veil of one's own, or the plain unlikely act.

**HUSH (Passive Trait)**
Tenebra keeps him beneath the noise floor of a room: his footfalls absorbed, his scent thin, his Essence quieter than the lamps. Dogs do not bark at him; horses do not shy. It costs nothing and is always on. Its limit is Tenebra's own failure: it is shaped against the angles he expects, and from the one he did not plan for he is as bright as anyone, brighter, a figure suddenly sharp in the corner of a mirror or the eye of a second watcher behind the first. That is its counter.

## IX. Domain

**HITSUZEN · Inevitability (Domain)**
Inside his Domain the likeliest thing happens. Opened by the Second Release out to forty yards, it presses every future in the field toward the most probable one: no lucky dodge, no blade that turns on a buckle by chance, no miracle. Whatever the odds favor, the odds deliver. It holds 2 minutes from a full reserve, about 75,000 EU a second. Its cost is that it is blind to which side the odds favor; it serves him only while he is the likelier man, and a stronger fighter inside it is made surer of winning, not less. It is beaten by being the likelier side outright, by stepping out of the forty yards, or by a real free choice made in the middle of it, an act no reading could have leaned toward, which the field cannot press because it has no weight to press.

## X. Techniques

**ONE NEEDLE · Ippon-bari (Signature Technique)** · mark: Mastered · page: ONE NEEDLE
A single thrust with the Last Thread, a needle-blade the length of a hand, up through the soft gap between the base of the skull and the first bone of the neck into the brainstem, where breathing and the heart are kept. Death is instant and almost bloodless. The force behind it is small and the point is so fine that the pressure at its tip goes through mail rings and leather and bone as if they were cloth; almost all of its cost, about 0.4% of his reserve, some 36,000 EU, is spent keeping the point perfectly true. It needs the nape bare or soft. A stiff high collar of boiled leather, a mail gorget at the back of the neck, or a back to a wall denies it; the north's lords have worn high collars for fifty years because of him. The tell is his breath: it stops for three heartbeats before the thrust. Before, guard the nape; during, a turn of the head a finger's breadth sends the needle off the gap and into bone, where it skids; after, he is an old man with an empty hand standing behind you, and he has never once been there long.

## XIII. Physical Description

He is an old man, and looks it, and that is the first thing anyone is meant to see. The crown of his head is bald and brown and shines in lamplight; what hair is left at the sides and back is white as fresh snow, long and loose and blown back from his temples as if he had been standing in a wind. His beard is full and white and soft, cut square at the chest, and his mustache covers his mouth. His face is long and deeply lined, every line of it cut fine and deep, the brow creased in long furrows and the skin round the eyes folded into a hundred small creases, and an old scar runs pale and thin from the left brow down across the cheekbone. His eyes are pale gray-blue under heavy lids, quiet and tired-looking, and when he reads, fine spokes of light appear in the irises like the spokes of a wheel. He is lean and a little stooped, with narrow shoulders and long thin hands, the knuckles swollen, the fingers still perfectly steady. He wears the Tenrai in its plainest form: a high-collared coat of deep navy-black, close at the throat, the collar banded in dull gold; a thin gold edge down the front; at the throat, holding the collar closed, a clasp of gold Kharven knotwork, the one northern thing on him, from a marriage fifty years buried. His left sleeve hangs a little heavier than his right.

## Lore

### Origin

Raizan Tenrai Moto was born into the Tenrai, the gold line, the line of war-command and elegant timing and battlefield inevitability, whose ambition for the red chair the house calls its founding wound. The Tengan opened in him at nine, earlier and wider than in any child the line had recorded. At fourteen the Tenrai elders watched him read a sparring hall and tell each fighter which of them would fall next, and in what order, and why. They took him out of the war-school that week.

### The Making

The first doom he broke was a man the Tengan said could not die that year. Raizan watched him for a full minute from the rafters of a counting-house, found the one strand in the knot where he did, and the one breath when every other strand thinned, and put a needle into the back of his neck. The man fell over his ledger. Raizan's own futures, the next morning, were shorter by a year. He noticed. He did it again.

For fifty years he was the Tenrai's last word in any argument the chairs could not settle in the Hall. The lords of the north learned to wear high collars and sleep with a guard at the door and another inside it, and it did not help. He married, once, a northern woman who gave him the gold knotwork clasp he still wears. She died of a fever, not of anything he could read. He did not break any doom that year.

### The Corridor

Eleven years ago, Muken Moto and his sons Tomuka and Ezo died in a single night in the corridors, in a killing the Tenrai commissioned. The record does not say whose hands were in the corridor. The north says one of them was his. The Tenrai have never said otherwise, and neither has he.

### Where He Stands

He is seventy-seven. He lives in a plain room in a Tenrai house with a go board and a window, and he still goes out when the line asks, and he still comes back. He can see his own last morning now, quite clearly, closer every time. He has not told anyone the date. Every commander in the north still sleeps with a collar on.

## VIII. Traits

**ENSHŌ · Inflammation (Primary Trait)**
He was the hot child of a cold line, and his soul did this before it ever tried. A blow that lands on him does not rebound or tear the way it would in other flesh: his Shell drops the collision's restitution toward nothing, so most of the struck energy stops as heat in the tissue under the impact, the way a dead-blow hammer swallows its own rebound. The spot flushes, swells, burns and hurts, the four old signs of inflammation all at once, and the heat cauterises the wound as it opens, so a cut that would bleed a man white closes in a hiss. The heat stays resident in him rather than radiating, and Exuroth's tempered lattice and his Tolerance let him carry it. The cost is thermal debt: every joule he swallows has to leave, and if he cannot spend it or vent it, it leaves through the nearest organ, so a long fight he does not finish cooks him from inside, and he drinks like a horse after every one. It acts only on energy that arrives as force or heat; cold, poison, starvation of air, soul-work and Limina negation pass straight through it. Its counter is the cold: water, ice and saturated cold ground pull the banked heat out of the struck tissue faster than the Trait can hold it, and a fighter who never trades blows and bleeds him by attrition with things that carry no energy gives the Trait nothing to eat.

**ONNEN · Grudge (Secondary Trait)**
Forged at the block. What ENSHŌ swallows, ONNEN keeps, on Vantabriel's hysteresis: the heat does not decay, and his Reigan tags each portion with the soul that dealt it, so the bank is a ledger of who owes what. He can hold up to a quarter of his maximum reserve above that maximum, 38,388,750 EU, and he can spend a portion only against its author, where Tarturon couples it to the moral weight of the debt and it burns with the force of the original blow returned. It is always on and it never forgets; every insult that lands as force is entered. The cost is fever: a full bank runs him at a heat that would kill a man and makes him sleepless, and past a quarter the excess has nowhere to go but into the structure holding it, which is a Crystal Fracture Event. Its counter is twofold and findable by anyone who reads Vantabriel's page. Give him nothing to bank: strike from range with energy he cannot thermalise, or never strike him at all. Or overfeed him on purpose, several fighters loading him with more than he can spend, until the bank saturates and the overflow cracks his own lattice. A Catharsis or Lethegraal practitioner who can lay hands on him can also shorten the relaxation time and drain the bank, which he will kill to prevent.

**TSUMI-YOMI · Sin-Reading (Inherited Trait)**
The Kurenai Reigan reads curse anatomy and soul structure, and in the line's headsmen it reads the record every killing leaves in the Attraction Layer, scar on scar, the way fired clay keeps the dose it has absorbed. In him that reading hardened into a Trait: he cannot look at a chest without counting what it holds, and the count is the moral weight his Tarturon fire burns by. A target with a long record takes GŌKA at full; a target who has never killed takes it at its base and no more. The cost is that he cannot stop reading, and Ignivale's low barrier means a record heavy enough fires his heat before he chooses, which is how he wastes himself on the first butcher in a room while the real threat walks behind him. It reads only what has been done, never what is about to be done, and it reads the dead as nothing. Its counter is a soul with no killing in it, or any ward that returns or quiets a reading, Oblivara's retroreflection or a Tenebra veil, which leaves him counting nothing and burning at base.

**SHŌNETSU · Scorching Heat (Passive Trait)**
The Hell of Scorching Heat is where he lives, carried with him. It is his waste and his bank's leakage together: at rest he sheds as much heat as a smithy forge in full blast, so snow will not lie within ten yards of him, frost melts off the ground he crosses, iron in a room with him grows too hot to hold bare, and the untrained sweat, then sicken, then go down with heat-stroke inside the hour. Fighting at full output he sheds twenty-nine parts in a hundred of everything he spends, terawatts in the burst, and the yard turns to an oven, the air shimmering, wood scorching black, stone spalling. It costs him concealment and company: he cannot hide from anyone who can feel heat, he cannot be near anything he wants to keep, and he cannot be silent the way the Kurenai walk. Distance is its counter, and cold, wet ground that drinks the heat; practitioners with Tolerance or Abython's equalisation stand in it unharmed, and a fight on a frozen lake or in a winter river runs on terms he hates.

**HAKANASHI · The Unburied (Compound Trait, ENSHŌ and ONNEN)**
At the block he went into Asami and looked inward instead of out, the Shallow-Seen Unburied the Moto scholars refused to name, and then, at Gisei, he burned the bond to his wife for the fuel to break the irons. The two Traits fused into one law in that moment, the sentence he held as she died: what is done to me is never finished. Under that law ENSHŌ and ONNEN no longer switch off, Obsession Force has the whole of him, and the Crystal's Overgrowth has made his body the shape of the law, which is why the Church files a Moto beside the First Eon's demons. Its cost is the Rigidity Effect at its absolute: he must answer every blow that lands, from anyone, before he does anything else, and he cannot forgive anything because forgiveness would discharge the whole bank at once into his own structure. That rigidity is its counter. A fighter who lands something small on him from the side pulls his answer off whoever he was killing, and a crew can drag him by the nose across a field this way while his clock runs. The law can also shear: true Asami looks outward, and anything that could turn his eyes outward and hold them there long enough would leave the Unburied nothing to grip.

## XIII. Physical Description

He is tall and lean and hard, built like a drawn wire rather than a wall, all sinew and heat, and he moves as if he weighed less than he does. His hair is black, long and heavy, slicked straight back off his brow and falling past his shoulders, the sides shaved close above the ears and worked into tight braids that run back along his skull to the nape. His face is long and narrow and hollow under the cheekbones, pale as a man who has been a long time underground, with a hard straight brow, a thin set mouth and a jaw like a blade's spine. Scars cut across all of it: a long one down through the left eyebrow and the cheek beneath, short pale nicks across the bridge of the nose and the right cheekbone, a seam at the corner of the mouth, every one of them faintly warm to look at, as if heat still lived in it. His left eye is dark and carries the Reigan's ring-lines, and when he reads it fills with red capillary light. A small iron cuff pierces the rim of his left ear. Under his clothes, from the left shoulder across the pectoral and down the arm, runs the Covenant Scar, a spiral law-mark the Kurenai carved into every headsman at nineteen to hold the vow of no wrath, once crimson and now black-violet, inverted, glowing through the cloth when he spends. On each wrist under the sleeves is a cuff of the Hall Irons, black Kurenai iron a hand deep, fused to the bone-ends by heat, the verdict script still legible on the faces, the chains snapped to stubs, three links trailing on the left. He wears a long coat of dark worn leather cut close, its edges worked in gold thread in the old Kurenai knotwork, laced at the throat over a black shirt, with a heavy collar and mantle of rough brown fur across his shoulders and a cord of iron beads and small bones close round his neck. A broad belt of red-brown leather sits low on his hips with two black-steel crow-head fittings and one red-enamel eye between them, and thirteen small iron bells hang on cords from it, one for each Kurenai sent to bring him back. His boots are heavy and black and smoke on wet ground.

## Lore

### Origin

Shōki was born into the Kurenai, the line in black and ash that keeps the crimson hidden, the line that walks the condemned down the black floor of the Hall of Verdicts and closes the register on them. The Kurenai do their work without anger. That is the whole of their discipline: the verdict kills, the headsman is only its hand, and a hand that strikes in wrath has broken the law it serves. They carve the vow into every headsman at nineteen.

He was born hot. Wounds on him closed in a hiss and his fevers frightened the line's physicians, and the Kurenai spent his childhood laying their cold discipline over him like a damp cloth over a forge. It held. He became the best stroke in the Hall, the master-headsman Jinsuke Kurenai Moto's first hand, and for twenty-two years he closed the book on whoever the seven chairs sent down to him and felt nothing he did not choose to feel.

He married Tsuyu of the Akagane, the copper line, a quiet woman who did essence surgery with her hands and laughed at his fevers.

### The Making

A lord died at a feast with his heart stopped, and the Hall needed a hand that could have done it. The Akagane's Heart-Warrant can stop a heart. The seven chairs voted Tsuyu's death in one morning.

His Reigan read her chest in the Hall before the vote was finished, and there was no killing in it. He said so. The chairs voted anyway, and then they handed him the blade.

He refused it, the first headsman in the line's memory to refuse a verdict, and the Hall put him in the condemned's irons beside her for the refusal, and Jinsuke took the stroke himself.

What happened next the Moto scholars describe and do not name. He went into Asami at the bottom of the shallow steps, under the shaft of gray light from the round opening in the roof, and he looked inward, at everything the Work had made him hold down, and he discharged it. Then, as her head came away, he found Gisei, and he offered the one thing that would tear the biggest hole in him, his bond to her, while it was still alive and screaming. The Plane does not cheat the exchange. The irons broke at the chains. He killed Jinsuke on the black floor with the cuffs, and walked up the steps through a Hall whose Pressure had put every chair-holder on the stone, and nobody stood up to stop him.

The next morning his Reigan read his own chest, and the eye burned out in its socket.

### The Cost

The Kurenai struck his name from the register and wrote in its place a single word, the Sin, and the Holy Sea's tribunal filed him as the Corruption of Conviction in its third grade, perverted calcination, the fire that burns away doubt instead of vanity. Both entries are accurate. He does not say he may be wrong. He reads a chest and counts it, and the count is all the verdict he needs.

He cannot let anything go. Every blow that has ever landed on him is still in him as heat. He cannot sleep a full night or stand near anything he would want to keep, and he does not want to keep anything; he gave the wanting to the Plane on the black floor.

The Kurenai sent thirteen of their own after him across the first century. He wears their bells.

### Where He Stands

He walks the wild ground of the north, where snow does not settle behind him, collecting debts from anyone whose chest is heavy enough to fire him. The drovers call the ground he has crossed Old Furnace's road and drive their herds around it. He has been seen on the edges of the Scourge of Hell.

He is three hundred years old and the seven chairs still sit. Several of the lines that voted that morning have heirs in the Hall today. He has been counting them.

### Ties

- **Tsuyu Akagane Moto** · wife, executed in the Hall on a false verdict. He burned the bond to her at Gisei and remembers her perfectly.
- **Jinsuke Kurenai Moto** · his master, who took her head. He killed him on the black floor with the irons.
- **The Kurenai** · his line, which struck him and keeps the Sin in its register; thirteen came for him and none went home.
- **The Hall of Verdicts and the seven chairs** · the debt. Unpaid.

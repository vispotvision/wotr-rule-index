## VIII. Traits

**THE COPPER HEART (Inherited Trait)**
The Akagane Blood and Heart Arts keep a heart, and Tetsugen has kept his own since the year he took the copper chair. Anima Spirare holds his heart to the beat it had then, and Verdantia holds the body round it to the hour the beat was taken: the hair black, the face smooth, the eyes clear, the hands quick. He looks twenty-five, and has for most of his life; the seven lines have watched him sit the copper chair forty years without aging, and do not ask. It costs him about one per cent of his reserve an hour, every hour, asleep or awake, for as long as he lives, and it keeps the body, not what has been done to it: the scars on his hands stayed, and so did every year of what he did, which he carries like an old man and is tired like one. Its limit is the reserve. Starved, drugged or knocked senseless, the hold slips, and the years he has been keeping off come into him at once, gray into the hair and lines into the face and a sixty-one-year-old heart laboring in a young man's chest, until he can take hold again; the longer it lapses the more of them stay. Its counter is to starve him, or to catch him asleep; its tell is the copper pulse in his aura keeping time with his heart, which no young man's aura does.

**IN HIS PLACE (Primary Trait)**
His soul reads a field by asking what it would do if it were the other man, and it is very rarely wrong. Forecast and Analysis turn forty years of provisioning, levies and ledgers into the long read: the Keld were bought, with no tithe, seed grain and no Moto boot on their snow, because that is what he would have offered in Karogane's place; Karogane would not need to win, only to walk out between the lines and shout. It costs nothing. Its limit is himself: he reads every enemy as a man like him, and a man who would not do what Tetsugen would do, for better or worse, is invisible to it. Old Harald Keld moved four hundred riders through a whiteout so close to his column that he heard their horses blowing, in the second year of the war, and he never found out how. Its counter is an enemy who is not him; its tell is the phrase.

## X. Techniques

**HEART-WARRANT · the Akagane warrant (School Art, the Blood and Heart Arts)** · mark: Mastered
The Akagane can keep a heart, and they can stop one. With his open hand flat on a man's chest, Anima Spirare finds the heart's current and holds it still between one beat and the next, and holds it there. It costs about three per cent of his reserve to take hold, near 21,500 EU, and 500 EU a second to keep it, nearly twenty-four minutes from full; a man whose heart is held for six seconds falls, and for three minutes does not get up. The same hand, gentler, keeps a failing heart beating, and that was its first use and its oldest. It needs his palm flat on the chest, skin or thin cloth, and it is fought by the target's own Hemostasis and heart-ward: a strong practitioner whose blood is his own can push the hand off his heart, at a cost. Its counter is never to let him touch you; its tell is the open hand, laid flat, the way he lays it on his own chest when he swears.

**SHINGAN · the heart-eye (Bloodline Faculty, Moto)** · mark: Drilled
The common Moto sight: where a thing carries its weight. In him it is the long weighing look, the look of a man looking at a horse he has been told is unbreakable. It costs about 400 EU a second held wide. It reads load, and men by their load; it does not read a man with nothing to weigh.

## XIII. Physical Description

He looks twenty-five, and the face is the one he took the copper chair with forty years ago, kept. His hair is black, thick and curling, worn loose and untidy to the jaw with locks falling across his brow; his eyes are a warm gold, the copper line's color, narrowed when he smiles, which he does more readily than anyone who has heard his voice expects; there is a small dark mark beneath his left eye. His skin is pale and smooth, and the only marks the Arts did not keep off him are on his hands, which are scarred across the backs and the knuckles from forty years of whatever an Akagane lord does with his hands, and which he looks down at and turns palm up when a thing weighs on him, as if there were something written there. He is slighter than his voice: a flat iron voice, a low rumble like a cart going over a bridge, grunts where other men would speak, and the sound of it out of that young face stops rooms. He wears the copper of his house as a short shoulder-cape of copper-ochre, broad across the shoulders, over white robes with wide sleeves, a cream under-robe crossed at the breast with a band of white flower-and-bead work, and a gray sash with a pale cord hanging from it; on the road, a gray travel cloak with copper showing at the cuffs. He carries no sword. At his belt is a knife for meat with a horn handle, and an old soldier's hand goes to it on its own when a room goes wrong.

## Lore

### Origin

He was born to the Akagane, the copper line, and learned the Blood and Heart Arts at his grandfather's hands; his grandfather threw open an Akagane granary in a famine year, and it was the kind of thing his grandson did not do for forty years. He took the copper chair young, while Gimbzo's father was still alive, and the year he took it he held his own heart still for the first time and kept it there, and has looked the same since. He never liked the Tenrai, not when Gimbzo's father was alive and not after. In the second year of the war, young enough to think he could, he fought Old Harald Keld on the ice under the Grey Teeth, and lost every cart he had and a third of his men in a whiteout, and never found out how.

### The Making

He voted for every one of Gimbzo's burnings. He argued for the Sennen campaign in council, and his granaries fed the columns that went down that road, and he signed the provisioning orders himself, in his own hand, with his own seal, knowing what those columns would do; Naiser Yukari saw him once roaring at the head of one with a Zettari border town burning behind him. Gimbzo burned the Akagane granaries at Hiroe with the children still inside, and Tetsugen sat in the copper chair and went home and slept. Three winters ago the Tenrai withheld seed grain from his tenancies along the Kurogane valley because his eldest spoke against a levy in council, and he buried forty-one of his own tenants that spring. At the Verdict of Six he told Kagenori he did not like him and wanted it said where the script could hear it, and voted yes on Torvald Stark's head anyway, and did not look at Ren, the soldier of his who had refused to burn the granaries at Tōmi and whom Kagenori gave him to dispose of.

### The Cost

He did not hang Ren. He put him on a horse in the dark with a purse and the name of a man at the Marrow crossing, and told no one until he told Kiyome Byakuya in her grandmother's prayer room, and then said: I'll come. Sky help me. I'll come. At the Yukari's table he confessed Sennen to the Zettari Sovereign and handed over the books, and said he would like to be the kind of bad man who tells the truth about it, if that was still on offer. At the Foot he gave the boy the long weighing look and said *good*, and when Kiyome told him who the boy was, he said: that is not his father. He would not kneel; the Akagane never swore to the red. At the woodpile he stood with his hand flat on his chest before three hundred Starks whose fathers had eaten from his granaries, and said he signed for every sack, and that he had given the boy the books, every name, and his was at the top. He carried two stones to the girl's cairn and worked the bellows for her brother's sword, and sat up all night on a bucket while his smiths laced the red harness.

### Where He Stands

This morning he stands beside the copper chair in the Hall of Verdicts, in his copper coat, and does not kneel, and swears for the copper line standing: he will not obey the boy, he will follow him, and the boy will find out how different that is the first time he is wrong. The boy said he was counting on it.

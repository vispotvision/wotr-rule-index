### The Veil

The year he took the copper chair he held his own heart still for the first time, and kept it there, and the same hold that keeps his body at twenty-five keeps the outward face of his Crystal at the hour he took it: Verdantia holds what it holds, and a Crystal is a body too. He kept it there because Gimbzo burned whatever in the north was strong enough to say no to him, the Akagane granaries at Hiroe among it, and a Fox who looks harmless lives to sit in council. It costs him nothing beyond what THE COPPER HEART already costs. Its limit is the hold itself: starved, drugged or knocked senseless, the face slips with the years, and anyone looking sees all of it at once. When he calls TOSHI-GAERI he lets go of the face on purpose.

## VIII. Traits

**THE COPPER HEART (Inherited Trait)**
The Akagane Blood and Heart Arts keep a heart, and Tetsugen has kept his own since the year he took the copper chair. Anima Spirare holds his heart to the beat it had then, and Verdantia holds the body round it to the hour the beat was taken: the hair black, the face smooth, the eyes clear, the hands quick, and, since a Crystal is a body too, the outward face of his Crystal at the density it had that year. He looks twenty-five, and reads at Refraction, and has for most of his life; the seven lines have watched him sit the copper chair forty years without aging, and do not ask. It costs him about one per cent of his reserve an hour, every hour, asleep or awake, for as long as he lives, and it keeps the body, not what has been done to it: the scars on his hands stayed, and so did every year of what he did, which he carries like an old man and is tired like one. Its limit is the reserve. Starved, drugged or knocked senseless, the hold slips, and the years he has been keeping off come into him at once, gray into the hair and lines into the face and a sixty-one-year-old heart laboring in a young man's chest, until he can take hold again; the longer it lapses the more of them stay, and with them the Veil goes. Its counter is to starve him, or to catch him asleep; its tell is the copper pulse in his aura keeping time with his heart, which no young man's aura does.

**IN HIS PLACE (Primary Trait)**
His soul reads a field by asking what it would do if it were the other man, and it is very rarely wrong. Forecast and Analysis turn forty years of provisioning, levies and ledgers into the long read: the Keld were bought, with no tithe, seed grain and no Moto boot on their snow, because that is what he would have offered in Karogane's place; Karogane would not need to win, only to walk out between the lines and shout. It costs nothing. Its limit is himself: he reads every enemy as a man like him, and a man who would not do what Tetsugen would do, for better or worse, is invisible to it. Old Harald Keld moved four hundred riders through a whiteout so close to his column that he heard their horses blowing, in the second year of the war, and he never found out how. The Sin of Wrath is not buying anything, and Tetsugen cannot stand in a furnace. Its counter is an enemy who is not him; its tell is the phrase.

**THE SENNEN BOOKS (Secondary Trait)**
Formed at Dissonance along the Sennen road. He signed for every sack that fed the columns, in his own hand, with his own seal, knowing what they would do, and Anima Spirare entered every heart that ate from his granaries the way the books entered every sack: he can feel, faintly, at any distance, the heartbeat of anyone who was ever fed from an Akagane store, and whether it is steady, and when it stops. Three hundred Starks at the woodpile; the Kurogane tenants, forty-one of them stopped; a Zettari border town that ate Akagane grain the winter before the columns came. It costs nothing he can put down. Its limit is that it is a ledger, not a reach: it tells him where those hearts are and how they are beating and lets him do nothing to them at range, and it keeps him awake. Its counter is a man who never ate his bread; its tell is his scarred hands, turned palm up, as if there were writing on them.

## IX. Domain

**KODŌ · the Heartbeat (Domain)**
Released, the slow copper pulse in his aura becomes a drum, and the Second Release opens the field on it, out to about a hundred and twenty meters: Anima Spirare at Domain-law, so that every heart inside the field beats to his. He sets the tempo. His allies' hearts he steadies, holds and keeps, so that a man run through stays on his feet and a man bleeding out goes on bleeding slowly enough to be carried; his enemies' he slows, a beat at a time, until the blood stops reaching the brain fast enough and the knees go, which is how a field of men falls down without a mark on them. The cost is the drum itself: the field beats to his own heart, and anything that strikes his heart, or startles it, or stops it, collapses the field at once; and every heart he holds slowed is a weight on his own, which he feels as an old man feels stairs. Its limit is Sovereignty and nature: a practitioner of his Stage with stronger Sovereignty or Hemostasis contests it at the edge and in his own chest, the dead and the made have no heart to hold, and a body running a high fever, or standing in great heat, races its own heart harder than he can slow it. Its counter is to contest it, to strike the drum, or to bring something with no heart.

## X. Techniques

**AKA-TE · the Red Hand (Signature Technique)** · mark: Mastered
There is a window in every heartbeat, a few hundredths of a second as the heart's muscle resets itself for the next stroke, when a blow to the chest over the heart that would only have bruised it at any other moment throws its rhythm into a quivering fibrillation that does not pump, and the man drops where he stands; the real thing happens to boys struck in the chest by a ball, and kills some of them. The Akagane have known about the window for a thousand years. Tetsugen steps into the clinch, goes quiet, feels the other man's heart through his own with Anima Spirare, and puts his open palm on the breastbone in the window, every time, with the current behind it to hold the quiver once it starts. It costs about one per cent of his reserve, near 1,249,000 EU, which delivers about a terajoule, A-Grade, though the joules are almost beside the point; it is the timing that kills. Its limit is the rhythm: a heart he cannot catch, too fast, too irregular, or held still by its owner's own art, gives him no window, and a sternum armored in worked plate takes the blow and passes no shock to the heart. Before, he must be close and listening; during, the strike is a palm, and a palm can be caught; after, the man's own Hemostasis and his friends' hands can shock the heart back into time if they are quick and know how. Its counter is a heart he cannot read; its tell is the quiet before.

**HEART-WARRANT · the Akagane warrant (School Art, the Blood and Heart Arts)** · mark: Mastered
The Akagane can keep a heart, and they can stop one. With his open hand flat on a man's chest, Anima Spirare finds the heart's current and holds it still between one beat and the next, and holds it there; inside KODŌ he needs no hand. It costs about three per cent of his reserve to take hold, near 3,747,000 EU, and 25,000 EU a second to keep it, more than an hour from full; a man whose heart is held for six seconds falls, and for three minutes does not get up. The same hand, gentler, keeps a failing heart beating, and that was its first use and its oldest. It is fought by the target's own Hemostasis and heart-ward: a strong practitioner whose blood is his own can push the hand off his heart, at a cost. Its counter is never to let him touch you; its tell is the open hand, laid flat, the way he lays it on his own chest when he swears.

**KETSURO · the Blood Road (Application of Anima Spirare)** · mark: Drilled
What the Copper Heart does with his own blood, done to a body he has his thumb on: he moves the blood. He can shut a bleed from the inside, push the blood away from a wound until it can be stitched, or keep a crushed limb's flesh alive for the hour it takes to reach a surgeon; or he can drive it, raising the pressure in every small vessel of a man's eyes and brain and lungs at once until they give. It costs about one per cent of his reserve to open, near 1,249,000 EU, and about 30,000 EU a second to hold. Its limit is contact and Hemostasis: his thumb must be on skin over a vein, and a man whose Hemostasis outranks his reach holds his own blood where it is. Its counter is to keep his hands off you; its tell is his thumb on a vein, at the wrist or the throat.

**SHINGAN · the heart-eye (Bloodline Faculty, Moto)** · mark: Drilled

## XIII. Physical Description

He looks twenty-five, and the face is the one he took the copper chair with forty years ago, kept. His hair is black, thick and curling, worn loose and untidy to the jaw with locks falling across his brow; his eyes are a warm gold, the copper line's color, narrowed when he smiles, which he does more readily than anyone who has heard his voice expects; there is a small dark mark beneath his left eye. His skin is pale and smooth, and the only marks the Arts did not keep off him are on his hands, which are scarred across the backs and the knuckles from forty years of whatever an Akagane lord does with his hands, and which he looks down at and turns palm up when a thing weighs on him, as if there were something written there. He is slighter than his voice: a flat iron voice, a low rumble like a cart going over a bridge, grunts where other men would speak, and the sound of it out of that young face stops rooms. He wears the copper of his house as a short shoulder-cape of copper-ochre, broad across the shoulders, over white robes with wide sleeves, a cream under-robe crossed at the breast with a band of white flower-and-bead work, and a gray sash with a pale cord hanging from it; on the road, a gray travel cloak with copper showing at the cuffs. He carries no sword. At his belt is a knife for meat with a horn handle, and an old soldier's hand goes to it on its own when a room goes wrong. Released, he is a man of sixty-one with iron-gray hair and the same gold eyes, and the voice finally fits the face.

## Lore

### Origin

He was born to the Akagane, the copper line, and learned the Blood and Heart Arts at his grandfather's hands; his grandfather threw open an Akagane granary in a famine year, and it was the kind of thing his grandson did not do for forty years. He took the copper chair young, while Gimbzo's father was still alive, and the year he took it he held his own heart still for the first time and kept it there, and has looked the same since, and read the same since. He never liked the Tenrai, not when Gimbzo's father was alive and not after. In the second year of the war, young enough to think he could, he fought Old Harald Keld on the ice under the Grey Teeth, and lost every cart he had and a third of his men in a whiteout, and never found out how. When he took the chair he read the copper book through, as every lord of the line does, and found on Tsuyu Akagane Moto's page the Hall's own record of the morning she died, and a needle at the nape that no Akagane poisoner ever used, and told nobody, because a copper lord who reopens the Hall's verdicts does not keep his chair.

### The Making

He voted for every one of Gimbzo's burnings. He argued for the Sennen campaign in council, and his granaries fed the columns that went down that road, and he signed the provisioning orders himself, in his own hand, with his own seal, knowing what those columns would do; Naiser Yukari saw him once roaring at the head of one with a Zettari border town burning behind him. He was strong enough by then to have stopped it, and he wore a Refraction lord's face in council and said nothing, because Gimbzo burned what could say no to him. Gimbzo burned the Akagane granaries at Hiroe with the children still inside, and Tetsugen sat in the copper chair and went home and slept. Three winters ago the Tenrai withheld seed grain from his tenancies along the Kurogane valley because his eldest spoke against a levy in council, and he buried forty-one of his own tenants that spring, and stood at the edge of the pit, and his Crystal went into Dissonance under him, and he went on wearing the face. At the Verdict of Six he told Kagenori he did not like him and wanted it said where the script could hear it, and voted yes on Torvald Stark's head anyway, and did not look at Ren, the soldier of his who had refused to burn the granaries at Tōmi and whom Kagenori gave him to dispose of.

### The Cost

He did not hang Ren. He put him on a horse in the dark with a purse and the name of a man at the Marrow crossing, and told no one until he told Kiyome Byakuya in her grandmother's prayer room, and then said: I'll come. Sky help me. I'll come. That night he told her what he was, and she told him what she was, and the young man from the indigo chair, sitting by the lamp turning his cup, told them both what he was, and Tetsugen laughed for the first time in years. At the Yukari's table he confessed Sennen to the Zettari Sovereign and handed over the books, and said he would like to be the kind of bad man who tells the truth about it, if that was still on offer. At the Foot he gave the boy the long weighing look and said *good*, and when Kiyome told him who the boy was, he said: that is not his father. He would not kneel; the Akagane never swore to the red. At the woodpile he stood with his hand flat on his chest before three hundred Starks whose fathers had eaten from his granaries, and said he signed for every sack, and that he had given the boy the books, every name, and his was at the top. He carried two stones to the girl's cairn and worked the bellows for her brother's sword, and sat up all night on a bucket while his smiths laced the red harness.

### Where He Stands

This morning he stands beside the copper chair in the Hall of Verdicts, in his copper coat, and does not kneel, and swears for the copper line standing: he will not obey the boy, he will follow him, and the boy will find out how different that is the first time he is wrong. The boy said he was counting on it. He is still wearing the face. He has begun to think that the day he stops wearing it will be the day it is worth something.

### Ties

- **Muken Moto** · his king, followed and not obeyed.
- **Kiyome Byakuya, Hayato Amagiri** · who rode east with him, and know what he is.
- **His eldest** · who spoke against the levy.
- **Ren** · his soldier, alive somewhere past the Marrow crossing, who will be killed if he is ever seen again, so Tetsugen said.
- **Tsuyu Akagane Moto** · a name in the copper book, three hundred years dead, whose verdict he knows was false.
- **Kagenori Tenrai** · whom he never liked, and voted with.
- **Gimbzo** · whose burnings he voted for, and who burned his granaries at Hiroe.
- **Old Harald Keld** · who beat him in a whiteout forty years ago.
- **Borin Ironheart** · whose bellows he worked.
- **Torvald Stark** · whose head he voted for.
- **Naiser Yukari** · who calls him the Fox.

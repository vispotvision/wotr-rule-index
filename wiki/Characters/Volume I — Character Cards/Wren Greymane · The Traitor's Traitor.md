## VIII. Traits

**BEAR (Primary Trait)**

His Crystal spreads reinforcement through the joined parts of a braced limb, sharing a blow between forearm, shoulder and chest instead of leaving its whole load on the first joint. The altered Lattice property is the distribution of compressive load through the Aether Shell. It works at B while he braces and costs 50.3 EU per second under load, giving fifteen minutes from a full reserve to the Starvation floor if he spends nothing elsewhere. A dark band crosses the forearm and shoulder, and the sound is wood taking weight. The force still moves him; the Trait does not fix him to the ground, mend a broken joint or protect an exposed surface from a cut. A hooked arm, a twisting shield or a pull that opens the shoulder separates the supports and defeats the sharing. Prolonged pressure leaves the supporting muscles shaking. The standing bias becomes unreliable below the Starvation floor.

## X. Techniques

**BULWARK (Signature Technique)**

The shield's whole face takes an identical share of an impact instead of the boss taking it. Petralon carries the load across the face, with Foundation, [Th], fixing the shield's continuous face as the boundary; Wren keeps contact with the enarmes and calls the working. Blue rises at the boss and runs to the rim, and a hit makes a low sustained note. Each invocation costs 5,030 EU and holds for one interception or six seconds, whichever comes first. A full reserve could pay nine invocations before the Starvation floor, but the shield ordinarily survives only three heavy deliveries: the blue becomes grey, that capacity does not return during a fight, and a stronger blow can use more of it at once. The pressure still reaches his arm and moves his whole body. A broken rim or opened split interrupts the spread, and an attack around the edge bypasses it. Before contact, a feint can draw and waste the hold; during contact, a hook can turn the shield and open its side; after contact, the grey face and loosened bindings expose what cannot take another delivery. The release cannot return the grey to blue.

**COMPACT (Carried Charge)**

An iron charge the size of a duck's egg opens into a hard sphere of aetheric material twenty feet across, arriving all at once. The expanding face pushes what it meets away from the charge. Its working is stored in the item; one use consumes one charge, with no separate reserve payment by Wren. There are four on his belt and no replacement supply from the dead smith. A pulled brass ring and broken wax seal precede the white sphere, which sounds like a mountain clearing its throat. Its force follows the expanding face, so a working that governs direction can bend it away, as Kaalabad does; a practitioner of Wren's Stage can instead stop the ring being pulled, disarm him before release, or move clear of the opening sphere. It does not distinguish a friend from an enemy, and firing it in the hand puts that hand in its path. One of the charges he used before Ashgate did nothing. The cause remains unknown.

**Releases**

**WALL WALK (First Release)** · folk name: Letting the horse run

His forearms and shoulders take on overlapping ridges of dark grey aura, like the stones at the crown of an arch, and the wooden groan closes into one deep iron note. Petralon carries BEAR's joined supports across his braced upper body; the full spoken call brings its defensive expression from 200 to 275, the top of B. The total release drain, including BEAR, is 251.5 EU per second. From a full reserve, with no other expenditure or recovery, it holds three minutes and ends at 5,030 EU. Bulwark is paid on top and its damaged shield stays damaged. The form reaches only his body and what he holds, opens no Domain, and gives his sword no added cutting force. Released silently, the ridges and sound still appear and the same drain runs, but the defensive expression stays at 200. A pull that spreads his arms, a twist through the shield or a blow around his braced front breaks the support; the spoken call also gives an opponent a beat to close or withdraw. Afterwards his forearms shake and his shoulder carries the accumulated ache. Holding it below the last tenth is an overchannel with fracture risk.

## XIII. Physical Description

A man of the ridge. His palm is ridged across the heel from twenty years of a shield strap, and the shield is always the same one: a heater, four feet, oak core and hide, with an iron rim gone silver with wear and a boss cracked and rebound twice. He wears a grey coat. At the split he stood at the front of the Tenrai line with his arms folded, his weight on the back foot and his head tilted.

He has killed thirty men with his sword. In the left saddlebag, under the oilcloth, he carries a book.

His hair is brown like wet chestnut bark, cut short at the neck and rough above the ears. His face is broad through the jaw, with dark eyes set beneath a heavy brow and a mouth that rests level. He is thick through the chest, strong in the thighs, and broader across the left shoulder from carrying the shield. The grey coat fits closely over the shoulders and pulls across his upper back when he raises that arm; the cuffs are polished thin, and the left cuff is worn where it meets the strap. The ridged heel of the palm is his clearest mark.

## Lore

### The Life Behind the Card

> *The noise of the column changed key. That was the sound Wren would keep.*

#### Origin

Greymane children are raised as parts of a wall. On the wall walk their father told Wren and Bram that the ridge did not move. Wren asked whether it ever had, and got no answer, and took that as the answer. The kingdom fell while they were boys, and their grandfather Godfrey held the western ridgeline for three years after it with both grandsons beside him. In the second winter Wren's lungs filled with fluid. Bram sat nine days by the cot and changed the compress every two hours, and Wren lived. Bram lied to the Tenrai assessors about where his brother was. Behind the western wall Bram planted the rye and Wren planted the turnips.

For nine years the brothers held the southern approach together, with eight hundred men and a rock shelf.

#### The Making

On the field of the Greymane split Wren stood at the front of the Tenrai line, two hundred paces off, under the banner of the line that had killed his grandfather. Bram roared that he was the one who had kept him alive. Wren smiled, raised one hand, and the Tenrai line went back over the rise. He did not turn round.

For sixteen months after that he was called traitor in every village between the ridge and the Stormfold, his brother among the voices. He chose six hundred men for their faith and spent it: they were spat at in their own villages, eleven of their wives stopped writing, and three were killed by their own kin. He entered those three in the book. In a borrowed room he forged a letter in another man's hand under a seal with no device, and told Yūgiri Yukari: *"If I have to become the villain to eventually be seen as a hero, there is no greater honour."* He had left a rat behind for Bram to find.

In summer he walked the Ashgate narrows under a false name with a hired cart of salt fish, counted two hundred and ten feet from the ditch to the drop, and drew it on the back of a psalter. Kill the middle third of a column there and the head cannot turn and the tail cannot get past.

#### The Cost

On the Ashgate road, within sight of the Kharven gates, Kaalabad drew Zarakal out of a seam in the air. Wren made the sign and six hundred men turned round in four seconds. Kaalabad looked at him for the first time in sixteen months: *"You are the traitor's traitor."* *"Aye,"* said Wren.

He held the road with the shield and the four Compacts on his belt, iron charges the size of a duck's egg whose smith died in the Stormfold without telling anyone how. His sword went about three inches into Kaalabad's throat band, and the scar there is his. Then his Bulwark failed. Zarakal's fluke pinned him through shield, arm and shoulder to the ditch bank. With the seconds he had left he pulled a Compact with his teeth and set it off in his own hand. It rolled some sixty of the men lying in the road forty feet clear of the fight, and killed none of them. He looked up at Kaalabad and did not close his eyes.

He is dead from that road on.

#### Where They Stand

His name went down the road and the battle stopped. Kwon Mu-jin closed Wren's eyes without ever learning who he was. A Reckoner entered him: *ditch bank, entered by field observation, body not recovered.*

In the eighth month a bundle came to Bram from Lambert with sixteen depositions from the men who had marched in that column. Bram wrote a dedication into the Stormfold book before his own name: *"This campaign is dedicated to Wren Greymane, who was called traitor in every village between here and the Stormfold for sixteen months, by me among others."* Any man who says the word in Bram's hearing answers to him. Wren is not on Bram's roll, and Bram has never said why. The book still lies unopened in the saddlebag of a horse Wren slapped away south.

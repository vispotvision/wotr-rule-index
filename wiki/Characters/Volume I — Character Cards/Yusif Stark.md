## Traits

**LET YOUR FEET DO IT (Primary Trait)**
"You're thinking. Stop thinking. Let your feet do it." His soul learned to fight below thought, the body moving before the mind decides, and that is what he put into a salt-cutter's boy with an ash stick from the age of eight; the boy's body still moves before he decides, every time. While it runs, his Reflex reads a Grade high and his cuts come without a tell. It costs nothing. Its limit is that it answers what the body has met before; a fight that needs a new idea needs the mind back. Its counter is a fight you have to think your way through.

**STARK RIME (Inherited Trait)**
The cold in the Stark blood: heat leaves everything near him, timber silvers, snow stiffens, and the heat goes down through his heels into the ground. In him it ran quiet, a rime on the hands and the blade and no further, the way a man keeps a dog that does not bark. Its counter is heat, and calm.

**THE THING NOBODY WROTE DOWN (Secondary Trait)**
Formed the morning after the cellars, when he quit Gimbzo and never rode with him again. A contract is not the paper; it is the thing both sides know that nobody wrote down, and he kept one for sixteen years: the boy's blood, his mother's road, the name. While he keeps it nothing gets it out of him, not fear and not pain. Its limit is the keeping; it is a silence, and a silence cannot defend itself except by staying silent. Its counter is to ask him in front of the one he is keeping it from; he will walk away rather than lie to the boy.

## Signature

**THE LONG CLEAN LINE (Signature Technique)** · mark: Mastered
One cut, edge and weight and the whole body behind it along one line, with nothing wasted and nothing shown before it. The boy used it, sixteen and a king, to execute two people in the Hall square, once each, cleanly, and did not use Hibi. It costs about two per cent of his reserve, near 5,900 EU, and is B-Grade on the edge. Its limit is that it is one cut, and a man who sees the line can step off it. Its counter is not to be where the line ends.

## Physical Description

He was a big quiet man, and older-looking than his years: his hair went gray early and silver after, worn short and swept back off a lined, weathered face, with pale gray eyes narrowed against snow-light and a mouth that rarely moved. He dressed for the road, in a long dark coat buckled close down the front with straps of worn leather, heavy leather bracers and fingerless gauntlets, a broad belt, and a dark scarf wound high round his throat and over his shoulders like a short cloak; he carried a plain northern longsword and an ash stick. When he came down four galleries into the Cuttings he brought salt-meat and wool and stood in the low doorway stooping, and the children did not know his name.

## Lore

He was Ulf Stark's young uncle, younger than his nephew, and in the long middle of the war he rode with Gimbzo when the north still thought Gimbzo meant to set things right, and he was the best of them. He had been a big brother to Kasane Byakuya since she was a girl at the king's side. He looked at where Gimbzo's road was going and saw the end of it, and he was there the night Gimbzo went down into the cellars at Halcrest with a lamp after his own blood, and the next morning he quit him and never rode with him again. Kasane walked out of those cellars on her own feet with the boy under her coat, and Yusif kept them: once a season he came down four galleries into the Orrel Cuttings with salt-meat and wool, never gave his name, taught the boy the stick from eight and the girl the needle at five, sitting her on a sack of salt, and told her more men would live because of a needle than would ever die because of a sword. He knelt when he talked to them. When the boy was thirteen and broke a steward's nose over the question *what's your oldest*, Yusif stopped coming. He was killed about three years ago keeping the secret, and never once in sixteen years called the boy Gimbzo's. He called him Kasane's.

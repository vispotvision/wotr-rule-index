# The Fourteen Titans

> *"An Archon is a crowned principle. A Titan is a load-bearing law. Neither wants anything, and prayer presumes wanting."*
>
> *Catechism of Equal Breath, opening clauses, as cited by the Lectors of Sancta Lux*

The weir at Sallow Reach gave way in the spring of Year 588 and took Gib Harrowgill's mill with it, three hundred sacks of barley, two mules and the wheel he had cut himself, and Harrowgill, a big red man with a big red grievance, walked forty miles to the Bench at Carrowmark and laid suit against Atla'zon the World-Binder for the weight of the water. He had a case, as he explained to everyone on the road. Water falls because a Titan holds which way is down, the Titan had gone on holding it, and his mill was in pieces. The clerks could find no rule against hearing him, so the Bench heard him. It ran nine days in the hot end of May with the court doors open, half the county came in holiday ribbons with cold chicken, and the Stone Choir (the masons' order that listens to rock before anyone is allowed to cut it) sent six chanters in slate blue to sit on the defendant's bench, uninvited, and hum at the floor. A Scale lector argued for the plaintiff for the sheer joy of it. The sergeants broke up two fistfights among the junior lectors and one among their mothers.

On the ninth day the Bench ruled against Harrowgill in the one sentence the Accord keeps for such occasions, which you will find below, adding that the defendant had no seat, no court and no herald on whom a writ could be served, and could no more be sued for falling water than the floor could be sued for holding up the judge. Harrowgill threw his hat at the bench and walked home. He found the six chanters already in the river up to their chests, listening to what was left of his weir, because nobody had ever asked them anything so interesting. What they built him has stood for a hundred and twenty-seven years. He never paid them and they never asked, and his grandchildren still set out a lamp for the Titan on the anniversary of the judgment, out of spite.

## What a Titan Is

The Accord's working definition is one sentence long and it has survived every attempt to improve it. An Archon is a principle enthroned. A Titan is a principle bearing weight.

The distinction is administrative before it is theological. An Archon can be consulted, argued with in the abstract, and cited in a filing. It has a seat, a court, a lineage of heralds and a body of doctrine written by people who were not present. A Titan has none of that and needs none of it, because a Titan is not doing anything so voluntary as governing. Ask who is in charge of gravity and you have asked the wrong question, because gravity is the posture Atla'zon is holding. Remove him and the question of who governs falling does not pass to a successor. There is simply nothing left for the word *down* to mean.

This is why the Titans have no theology worth the name and an enormous quantity of engineering. The Stone Choir listens to rock harmonics before cutting. The Undercrypt Keepers whisper equations at buried buttresses. The Ledger Monks refuse to let a ruler erase a predecessor's failure from a Dynasty Hall. Every one of those orders is doing maintenance, and would be insulted to hear it called prayer.

The catechism at the head of this page is a catechism, and catechisms like to be tidy. "Neither wants anything" is true of a Titan and true of a Hollow Throne, which is the law with nobody home. An occupied Throne can notice you, as anyone who has pulled the wrong thread will tell you if they still have the use of their mouth.

**Where a Titan comes from.** Titans descend from Archons. An Archon is a principle enthroned, and a Titan is the same principle bearing weight, come down out of the throne room and into the load. The Epochs were the forging parties, because a law can descend all it likes and still needs somebody to give it a back. Dazkar's law comes down from Urion, and it was Bairos and Aegor who forged a body to hold it.

**What a Titan is to the Wellsprings.** Titans are the Wellsprings themselves, and [[The Magic System/The Foundations/The Sixty Wellsprings/The Sixty Wellsprings|the Sixty]] are their manifestations. Each of the Sixty is one way a Titan's law shows up, carried by [[Cosmology & Metaphysics/The Making of the World/Omnara and the Division|Aether]] (mana), much as a burn, a lamp and a hot wind are all ways one fire shows up. A soul that tempers on a Wellspring has based itself on some part of a Titan's law, which is a great deal less than an introduction. The Titans bear all three planes, so the Sixty make up all three, and a Wellspring's kind names its home: Wild to Aegor, Spirit to Ashura, Fate to Bara, Medium to the Veil. Every entry in the register names its Titan. The Sixty stay sixty, however many ways the old books count them.

**A Titan walks no Path.** A [[The Magic System/The Soul and the Climb/The Three Paths Routing, Recognition and the Gate|Path]] is a soul leaning on one layer of itself, and a Titan is no such thing. It has no lean, no ladder and no Stage, and nothing a window could report. Practitioners who call a Titan "a Body power" are reading the Order column below, which tells you where the weight sits and nothing about the Titan.

> Fourteen Titans are seated against fourteen Archons, and the symmetry is close enough that most mortal registers assume a one-to-one correspondence. **It does not hold.** Dazkar grounds Urion cleanly and Chronaeus grounds Valen cleanly, and after that the pairs go ragged. Kaldrion answers to nothing in the Archonic roster at all. The Measurewrights hold, without enthusiasm, that this is what an honest count looks like and that the neat version is the one that would need explaining.

Above the Titans stand the **Epochs**. Aegor, Bairos and Ashura appear throughout the entries as the forging and binding parties, and they are not Titans and are never to be filed as such. Where a Titan's Bonds field names them, the field is recording provenance rather than alliance. In the days of the Physical Epochs, Bara (whom the Archonic registers record as Bairos), Ashura and Aegor walked in human form. What those days left lying about, and what the Great Spirit War left after them, are the dead and dangerous battlefields, the relics people kill for and the old beings who remember, and every entry below keeps running into them.

A warning on figures, from the Guild's own [[Cosmology & Metaphysics/Ages and Reckoning/Errata to the Received Registers|Errata]]. A heart that beats once in a thousand years, a life measured in eons: these are Epoch figures, accurate as statements about magnitude and meaningless as statements about years, and none of them goes on the [[Cosmology & Metaphysics/Ages and Reckoning/The Concordance of Ages|Concordance]]'s count. Where an entry uses a received name for an Age (the Pyrrhean Epidemic, the Fractured Era), I have said what the Errata makes of it.

## The Seating

The orders describe what layer of the world the Titan is holding rather than any rank between them, and each one's law reaches past its order: the Bonds that tie a Titan to Bairos are how it reaches Fate. There is no Fate order among them, because nothing bears weight on what is no reality. Nothing in the register establishes a hierarchy and the Guild has repeatedly declined to invent one.

A word on the **Ground** field in each entry. It names what the old registers call that Titan's Wellspring, and the old registers are right: a Ground is the Titan's law at full scale, a law and a place at once. The [[The Magic System/The Foundations/The Sixty Wellsprings/The Sixty Wellsprings|sixty Wellsprings]] proper are that law in portions a soul can use. Each is a kind of Aether, acting on a Soul Crystal in its own way and standing for a principle and a transformation, and each has sites where its Aether pools (a Core, in common speech). So the Measurewrights no longer argue over whether Grounds should be counted among the Sixty. You can temper on Ignivale, and you can no more temper on Omnis's Breath than you can drink a sea. The Codex line closing each entry seats the working on one of the Sixty by name, which is the part a practitioner can use. Vohrin is the register's one exception: its line names the Titan's own power, worked at Wellspring level, with the northern frost arts drawing on it directly and outside the Sixty, and the Sixty stay sixty.

The old registers are also loose with the word **Essence**, and I have kept their wording here because they are the only witnesses we have. Essence proper is a soul's own substance, Aether refined in the Crystal and stamped with identity. Where one of these entries has Essence pooling in the ground, storming across the Veil or overcharging a region, read it as Aether, or as what the dead and the very large have left behind them, and a Measurewright will think better of you.

## World Spirits

The word gets used as a Stage and as a kind of being, and in the Guild's register it is one thing. A **World Spirit** is a soul whose Domain claim has grown into a place. It began as a person, or a beast, or a dragon. At [[The Magic System/The Soul and the Climb/The Sixteen Stages|Principality, Stage XIII]], the Continuum registers the law it embodies and the local biome starts carrying its doctrine, which the trade calls the first World Spirit threshold. At Zenith, Stage XIV, a vacant Throne recognizes it, and that is full World Spirit, a place with an opinion. Because a Domain is a claim made on the Attraction Layer, every World Spirit stands under Fate, whichever road it walked to get there.

That makes them the near opposite of a Titan. A Titan is a law that was given a load and never had a self to lose. A World Spirit is a self that has become a law and is slowly losing the rest of it. The old ones sleep. Slept long enough, they set like a cooling mountain and become **Titanstone**, found and never quarried, which is why a cracked block can take a city down with it and why the [[Cosmology & Metaphysics/Ages and Reckoning/The Four Ceilings|Four Ceilings]] argue about it in such careful language. The name is a stonecutters' mistake and every Titan-order monk will correct you on it at length.

## The Titan-Keyed Arts

Fourteen workings sit in the register under the category **Titan-Keyed**. Mortal practitioners call them Titanic Arts, and the Accord tolerates the phrase in the street and strikes it from anything carrying a seal, on the grounds that the art is not the Titan's. The Titan does not perform it and is not consulted about it. The practitioner is entering a process the Titan is already running and imposing a boundary on it, which is the whole of what a glyph does anywhere in the Codex and is only remarkable here because of the scale of the process being entered.

Three things separate a Titan-Keyed working from every other entry in the register.
1. **The gate is absolute.** Twelve of the fourteen require Stage XIV, Zenith. Two require Stage XV, Revelation, which sits *above* Zenith and is reached only through genuine exchange with an Archon or a Titan at a level where the practitioner participates rather than merely survives. Those two are **The Closing Eye** and **The Stillwater Rite**. Nobody in the Accord's living register holds both. The gate binds these fourteen workings and no others: the ordinary frost arts worked on Vohrin's power keep the lower floors their own pages state.
1. **Every one keys to a condition that must already exist.** A strain already visible. A silence already present. A fault line already open. A soul already dying. Not one of the fourteen originates its own condition, and this is the structural reason none of them can be used as an opening move.
1. **The cost lands somewhere specific and does not negotiate.** Eleven of the fourteen bill the caster's own body, memory or self-image. Two bill bystanders. One bills nobody at all, which is the entry the Stone Choir has spent nine hundred years finding sinister.
> Each entry carries the register's own fields. **Class** is an Accord convenience and appears nowhere in the Master Codex.
>
> **Method, Grade, Range and Medium are not held for any Titan-Keyed working, and the omission is structural rather than clerical.** A Method names how a practitioner relates to a phenomenon: reading it, controlling it, inscribing it, distilling it. **A Titan-Keyed working has no such relation.** The practitioner is not working a phenomenon at all. They are imposing a boundary on a process that was already running at world scale and would go on running whether or not anybody arrived.
>
> *Grade measures magnitude against a stat. Range measures reach from a caster. Medium names what the sentence was written into.* **None of the three has a value when the process is the ground you are standing on.**

## The Ledger of Costs

Read down this column and the register's actual argument becomes visible. Stop thinking of a Titan-Keyed working as a large spell, and think of a bill presented in a currency the caster did not choose.

| Titan | The Art | What the casting takes |
|---|---|---|
| ATLA'ZON | *The Bent Knee's Burden* | The caster's own joints and breath take on the Titan's characteristic strain. |
| GAIA | *Flesh Remembers Stone* | Mineral deposits form under the caster's own skin over a career. |
| OMNIS | *The Skyfire Pulse* | The caster's respiratory system takes the first pass of the purification. |
| FLORWYN | *The Green Chord* | Personality bleed-through into the successor, standard rather than exceptional. |
| DAZKAR | *The Mirrored Scale* | The caster is bound by the same mirror for the working's duration. |
| NYSERA | *The Somnum Flow* | The channel is bidirectional the entire time it stays open. |
| SYLORIN | *The Ferryman's Current* | The soul arrives with the full emotional charge of its death still attached. |
| ILGRIMM | *The Hollow That Watches* | The caster is not exempted by the silence they enforced. |
| VALMIR | *The Canyon's Patience* | Unresolved resentment steers the collapse worse than intended. |
| PROMETHEUS | *The Pyric Refinement* | Valued memories attached to the false part burn away with it. |
| ZAR'THUUN | *The Closing Eye* | Small memories erased from **everyone nearby**, caster included. |
| VOHRIN | *The Stillwater Rite* | Held too long, the target forgets their own purpose for living. |
| KALDRION | *The Deep Grid's Verdict* | **None to the caster.** The cost falls entirely on the subject tested. |
| CHRONAEUS | *The Era-Forge's Toll* | **Innocents inside the compressed era** age and suffer at the forced rate. |

Three entries break the pattern and each breaks it differently. *The Closing Eye* bills everyone standing nearby and the caster never learns what was taken. *The Era-Forge's Toll* bills a population that was never asked and will never know it was cast. *The Deep Grid's Verdict* bills nobody, which is the only clean entry in the register and is treated by every order that studies it as the most dangerous line on the page.

## The Fourteen Compounds

*Twelve of the fourteen arts require Zenith and two require Revelation, which places all fourteen beyond every practitioner alive. The world reaches them anyway, at a fraction, through a bottle.*

**Every Titan in this register has a compound keyed to it in the Standing Index, at T5 through T7**, and the correspondence is exact rather than approximate: Atla'zon to Strain-Bound Marrow, Gaia to Corpus Marrow Salts, Dazkar to Purganeth Mirror-Salt, Sylorin to Ethereal Current Oil, Ilgrimm to Hollow-Watch Incense, Kaldrion to Deep Grid Mortar, Omnis to the Skyfire Pulse Vial, Florwyn to the Verdance Reclamation Draught, Nysera to the Somnum Flow Ampoule, Valmir to the Crevice-Glass Shard, Prometheus to Pyric Refinement Ash, Chronaeus to Fossil Ring Dust, Zar'thuun to the Null-Essence Draught, Vohrin to the Stillwater Draught.
> **This is the actual reason those compounds cost what they cost**, and no price list anywhere says so. A Titan-Keyed art is a boundary imposed on a running process by a soul dense enough to survive standing in it. **A compound imposes the same boundary through a substrate instead**, at a smaller scale, for a shorter term, and available to a practitioner four Stages short of the gate.
>
> *The cost does not vanish with the scale.* Each compound bills in the same currency the art bills in, which is why the Stillwater Draught's long use is called a mercy killing by another name, and why the Verdance Reclamation carries the donor's grief across intact. **The bottle is smaller. The bill is the same bill.**
>
> The Bench has never described the Standing Index as a schedule of Titan-Keyed workings and would refuse the description if it were offered. *Every alchemist above Tier Five has noticed.*

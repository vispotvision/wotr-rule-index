# The Mahuo Family

Two notaries of Haeseong once went to law over this spelling and kept it up for a whole winter, at a copper a day in fees, and when the bench ruled for both of them they split the costs and a very bad supper.

The line is written two ways, and both are correct. **Mahou** is the line as an institution, the lineage the Zettari–Mahou Accord recognizes and binds; **Mahuo** is the surname a person of that line bears, which is why the Accord can name Ara Min Mahuo, of the Mahou line, in a single breath.

Up in Breath Hollow, in the dry summer of 688, a woman named Yeon-ju Mahuo was still drying her radishes the way her mother had. She was eighty-one and owned one good cooking pot. Every morning for seventy years she had stood on her step, breathed out, and called a storm, a bound weather-spirit of the middle tiers called Danbi (단비), the word for rain that comes exactly when wanted. It came down the valley about the size of a barn, shook a cupful over the kale, took the bowl of rice she left on the step, and went home. In her girlhood the calling had put her to bed for a day. By then it cost her about what a sneeze costs. That summer a Moto column came up the terrace road under a vermilion banner, four hundred men protecting the Mahuo from somebody, and its quartermaster explained that he would be requisitioning the rice. She said he would have to speak to the weather. Then she breathed out on her step, as she did every morning, and Danbi arrived with seventy years of breakfasts behind it and an old friend's opinion of the man. Nobody in the valley got wet. The quartermaster was found in the next province, and the banner was returned to Sum-gol by a heron-fisher, who asked for a fee.

She died two winters later. Danbi came to the funeral and sat over the hollow for nine days without raining, which frightened people badly, until her granddaughter, seven years old, went out on the step with a bowl and breathed out the way she had seen it done. It came as if it had never been away. The child did not know that she had inherited a grandmother's good standing with a storm, and with it a grandmother's bowl of rice, every morning, for life, and that if she ever missed one she would be answering for seventy years of them. They still leave a bowl on every step in Breath Hollow, sixty roofs now, and if you ask why, they tell you it is manners.

That is the whole house in one old woman, and the empire was only ever the large form of what Yeon-ju did with her radishes. They call things from across the Veil, the skin between the lower realities, and the things come, and it gets easier every time. What they hold that everyone needs was once a throne and is now a road: every letter between the north and the Yukari valleys goes through the olive coats of the Sum-gol Post, and since the Soul Plane broke and the South's dead began to drift, the same coats will lay a haunting for a fee. Their name was carried at the front of other people's armies until there was little left but the carrying. What they want, in the plain words of the table at the Cedar Enclave, is their own name back off other people's banners.

## Origin of the House

The valley has always said that breath goes out of a person and stays there, spread thin, and it counts breath before it counts coin. The oldest entry in the teaching house is a ledger of oxen, kept by a woman called Wonsum, who noticed that a beast she had counted through a bad night would come to her voice in the morning when it came to nobody else's, that this was true of her daughter's voice as well, and that it held good for several things in the hills that were not oxen. Everything after that is scholarship. Her descendants wrote it down, sealed their first great compact (a bargain with a power that owns its own soul) with the White Stag they call the Baengnok, and about the year 300 a Daeju Mahuo came down the terraces with the Stag at his shoulder and a large number of guests, and the South found that it had an emperor.

You know how that ended: the Calling of 602, the Stag walking out by the East Gate, three more generations as kings, and then the long war, which three houses fought under the excuse of protecting them. Today the line stands in pieces, and the pieces disagree. The head of the house is **Cozbi Mahuo**, twenty-five, Master of the Post and the man the [[In-World Documents & the Narrative Archive/The Zettari–Mahou Accord|Zettari–Mahou Accord]] condemns by name. The **Kwon cadet branch** gave the Accord its drafter, Kwon Mu-jin, called the Teacher, and lost generations ago the knack of hosting souls; it calls by [[The Disciplines/Named Arts/The Open Crucible — Kwon Mu-jin's Book of Summons|request form]] instead. His sister **Ara Min Mahuo**, the Healer, is the name most of the world puts on the lawful line. The **Aperture branch** at Yeol-gol keeps the household pattern of [[The Bestiary/The Called and the Divine/Summoned and Bound/Obrenkael · The Mule|Obrenkael, the Mule]], under a standing objection.

## The House on the Crystal

A Soul Crystal is the soul itself, in three layers: the **Aether Shell** on the outside, where Aether (mana) enters and force goes out; the **Essence Core** at the center, where it is refined into Essence (spiritual energy) and stamped with who you are; and the **Attraction Layer**, a soul's own share of Fate. The Mahuo run them unevenly.

| Layer | Plane | The Mahuo form |
|---|---|---|
| **Aether Shell** | Physical | Thin and undistinguished. Most Mahuo read a low rung here all their lives and leave the heavy lifting to their guests |
| **Essence Core** | Soul | Their strength. Early to hear, slow to fray, and used from childhood on oxen, grandmothers and the dead |
| **Attraction Layer** | Fate | Clean, which is all. It is where a compact is addressed, and what a Mahuo writes there spends their own share and pulls nothing down |

**Path.** A Path is the layer a soul has learned to lean on, recognized from how it lives, and no blood ever gave one. Mahuo children tend toward the Spirit Path with a long second road into Fate's shallow end, since a summons reads the Shell for the crossing, the Core for what the thing is, and the Layer for who it recognizes.

**The grip.** A bloodline's grip opens one ladder early and lifts no cap. The Mahuo's is **Essence Class**. A child of the line can read Listener on the assay coil years before Glory, Stage VI, when anyone else's ladders first read. The Stage ceiling stands over them as over everybody. They have no eye and no grip on Thread: Fate's ladder stays Ø for nearly all of them, and a Mahuo who has held to her compacts for decades may read I.

| Essence rung | Typical Mahuo |
|---|---|
| I · Listener | A teenager in Breath Hollow, who knows who is on the household roll |
| II · Answerer | A Post rider who can lay a small haunting; the first real compact |
| III · Confessor | Physicians of the line, reading a soul whole by its breathing |
| IV · Shepherd | Elders who hold the dead still long enough to ask them things |
| V · Ferryman | The head of the house, at an age that has made a great many scholars write to each other |

## The Answering — 응답 · Eungdap

**What it is.** Every summoner knows that a thing called once is easier to call twice. In ordinary hands that is a small saving, and it belongs to the one who earned it. In the Mahuo it runs as blood does: each answer makes the next come quicker and cheaper, on a curve no other summoner's will follow, and it is filed under the family. A child can come into a compact her grandmother paid down.

**How it works, in plain words.** A Mahuo calls on the out-breath, and the calling is an address, a name sent out with an offer. Nothing is dragged anywhere. A summons by recognition can be refused, which is why the art can be learned in a yard at all. Where others leave the reply at that, the house current, the Wellspring Anima Spirare, teaches the soul a different habit. Blood is the scholars' picture of it. Each grain of the red part of the blood takes up its first breath of air grudgingly, and every breath it has already taken makes the next go on easier, so it loads faster the more it carries. The Mahuo Core does that with recognition. The first answer is expensive. The second has a place to land. By the fiftieth the thing has almost no distance left to cross. And since Essence is stamped with identity, and a family's stamps run close, a called thing that has known the mother will know the daughter when she breathes out, as a dog knows a voice in the next room.

**The forms.**

| Form | Korean | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| **First Call** | 첫 부름 · Cheot Bureum | The full price, the long breath and the real chance of no. A seal in the house color opens on the thing's brow if it agrees |
| **Calling Back** | 되부름 · Doebureum | A compact called again. The price falls with each answering, and what the thing is owed starts to be kept by habit |
| **The House Roll** | 가문 명부 · Gamun Myeongbu | Inheritance. A compact entered on the family's list passes to a recognized descendant, with all its savings and all its arrears |
| **The Morning Bowl** | 아침 그릇 · Achim Geureut | The old compacts, answered so often they come almost before the breath is out, for nearly nothing. A lifetime's work, or several lifetimes' |

**What it costs.** The words run both ways, and everyone in the valley knows it. A called thing is paid in what it wants, and the great ones want things that cost. Inherit the compact and you inherit the bill. Mahuo children are read their debts along with their sums, and the Seven Debts of Sum-gol, cousins whose grandfather gave them all the generation-syllable *Chae*, are the valley's oldest joke because they are also its truest. Anything on the roll has standing. It can come to the door without being called, and ask.

**Its limit.** The Answering saves on the call and adds nothing to the thing that answers. A sealed compact runs on the creature's own Essence, so it is as strong as the creature and no stronger, and a house with a small roll is a house with a small roll. It needs a name that still means something, too. Recognition is a two-sided thing, and a compact written to run while the realm answers ends when it stops. Nobody living has the great roll of the empire. Scavengers in painted hoods come up out of the Answering Vaults under the Palace square with reports of a host standing in rows, waiting for a voice they would know.

**Who beats it.** Reach the caller: a bound will tests its leash, and a fighter who strikes at the holder's resolve instead of the beast has found the real weak point. Exhaust the creature, since its reserve is what is paying. Get a better contract onto it, which the rolls call theft and the yards call Tuesday. And do not mistake the Answering for what the Accord condemns. An answer is given. A soul taken from someone who was never asked, folded into a thread and spent, is conscription, the crime with the house's name on it, and the Accord's whole written code exists to tell the two apart in a form a soldier can read.

## Physical Appearance and Dress

There is no Mahuo face to speak of, three centuries of imperial marriages having seen to that, though black hair that goes green in lamplight runs in the main line and a long ear turns up in the eastern households. They are slight, narrow in the chest, and famous for their breathing, slow and very even, so that nervous people find themselves falling into it. Elders of the old school can be told by the small lines at the corners of the mouth, from a lifetime of breathing out through it. Voices run warm and carrying, and a Mahuo at table is always the host, whoever is paying.

Vermilion and white are the house colors. Sum-gol wears vermilion still, a sash or a rag or a dyed hat, because the cinnabar is local and cheap. **The Post wears olive.** A rider has an olive wool coat and a short curved hwando saber across the shoulders, a bag of salt at the belt and brass leg-tubes for the crows. An elder of Breath Hollow goes in undyed hemp with a soul-breath physician's red cord at the wrist. At fifty paces the thing to look for is a person walking quite alone with too much room around them. A poor cousin has one good cup, gray-green, from the old Sum-gol kilns, and will turn its small vermilion glaze-mark to face you before she pours.

## Lineage Traits

### [Trait] The Long Breath — Hearing Before Glory

The grip, as it sits in the body. A Mahuo child's Core takes to souls before it takes to anything else.

- Essence Class opens early and climbs steadily with use, up to what the Stage allows.
- Hears a soul in another's breathing: fear, lies, the first days of a wasting sickness. Sum-gol sells this as diagnosis and lineage-reading.
- Sensing, called *nunchi*, comes easily and rudely, and every grandmother in the line has it.

*A nursemaid in the Hollow teaches a child to count the ox's breathing before she teaches it the copper.*

### [Trait] The Blood's Own Answer — Anima Spirare in the Line

- The body answers a small hurt with all it has: bleeding stops fast and sickness is met early.
- A trained Mahuo can lend this to another by touch, which is most of what the Healer's school is, and it sits very ill with the Accord's enemies that the line's lawful art is mostly nursing.
- Under strain it fails as the cascade fails, running everywhere at once.

*They are the only house whose healers and whose monsters use the same breath.*

### [Trait] Blood Knows Blood — The Reading of the Roll

- A Mahuo can see, a little, what another Mahuo has called: seals, threads, the shape of a compact.
- A descendant knows when something on the family roll has moved, though not always what.
- It works in both directions, and no Mahuo has ever kept a summons from a sufficiently stubborn relative.

*Hiding a guest from your mother has never once worked in Sum-gol.*

## Doctrine and Arts

**The Host.** A Mahuo at war does not fight; a Mahuo gives a party at which you are killed. The doctrine is to arrive with enough room, lay the field like a table, and bring the guest who suits the company. Riders stay on the horse, draw a ring, and let the salt argue. Sum-gol salt, poured in a closed ring and named aloud, gives a called thing a door it must ask at, and that holds off the lesser drift for about as long as a rider needs.

## Culture

They speak Sum-gol's Korean-registered tongue, with given names of a generation-syllable and a personal one, the family name before or after as it falls, and syllables drawn from breath, ledger and care. A release is an untying, the same word as the old rites that untie a grief. Their ranks run outer disciple to elder, as any teaching house.

- **The First Count.** At three, a child sits up one night with the oldest ox in the village and counts its breathing to dawn. If it comes to the child's voice in the morning, the teaching house opens a page.
- **The Reading of Arrears.** On the eve of the first compact, an elder reads the candidate everything the family owes: what, to whom, since when. It has been known to take three days. Some walk out, and the house lets them.
- **The Turned Cup.** Anything called, from a beast to a thing with no face, is offered a cup before it is asked for anything, the mark turned toward it. Those who skip this, the valley says, are the ones who get eaten.
- **The Untying.** Mahuo burial law alone in the four quarters does not require the family to keep the body. The breath has already gone to the valley. Family members sit with the dying and take over the compacts one by one, aloud, until none is left in the room with nowhere to go.

**Who they marry, fight and owe.** They married the South for three centuries and are related to everyone who is not speaking to them. The Accord proposes a marriage with the Zettari Sovereign that is entirely the Healer's to refuse. Lord Naiser Yukari has offered the throne back, and it has been declined with very good manners. House Ryu holds the old revenue and sends a letter. Sum-gol's commoners have rent cheap enough to live in a duke's kitchen, the oldest law in the South, and a sealed bag no one may rob. Couriers are paid when the Post can pay, and they stay anyway, for reasons a good many people would like to know.

## Limitations and Soul-Costs

### [Curse] The Roll Reads Both Ways — Call, and It Answers

Everything a Mahuo has on the roll has standing. A compact missed comes to collect, by its own terms, and what comes may not be small. A newborn of the line can owe a power that was old when the empire was young.

### [Curse] Two Lenses, One Gate — The Contrary in the Blood

A body refusing the gate and a soul walking toward it make a poor marriage. Mahuo who push Anima Spirare fail by disseminated activation, the cascade running everywhere until they bleed from the gums, nose and eyes. Those who push Mortalis fail by Equilibration, cooling a little toward the boundary with every working. Elders of the line are often both, and they keep their rooms warm.

### [Curse] The Thin House — Nobody to Inherit

Sixty roofs, a school with more benches than pupils. A compact with no recognized heir does not end. It just stops being answered from this side, and everything owed goes on being owed. Somewhere in the iron hills there is a Stag. The house has a young head and very few children, and a great many things out there remember the name.

## How They Stand Against the Three Paths

**Body.** The old nightmare. A hitter who gets through the guests finds a slight person in a good coat, and a spear does not care what you mean. Every Mahuo rider therefore knows exactly how far away a charging man is, and how long the out-breath takes.

**Spirit.** Their own ground, and the most dangerous of their enemies. A Spirit fighter can read the compact, find the clause the charter forgot, or wound the Core that holds the leash. The trained ear finds the seams in anything built from borrowed souls.

**Fate.** Slow, and it hurts. They have no answer of their own to a true pull, and a Fate user can move what an answer depends on: the name that arrives late, the seal that fails to open. They beat it by reaching the hand on the thread, since a called thing is often standing nearer than the caller's enemy expects.

## Final Definition

The Mahuo are the family that made the world answer, and they went on making it answer until it noticed that the question could be turned around. What remains is a road, sixty roofs, some very old debts, and every morning a bowl of rice on a step in Breath Hollow, because you do not leave the weather waiting. Northerners ask what a summoning line does with its arts when it has no hall left to call them into. The answer in the valley is the one it has always been.

*Call, and it answers. Then it asks what you owe.*

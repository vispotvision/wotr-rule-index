*The Line of Bara · The Readers · The House That Bills*

> Founded by **Bara Moto** and **Mizuki Moto.** Bara stands as the mortal manifestation of the First Epoch in literal human form; Mizuki gives that primordial authority **continuity, inheritance, and dynastic form.**

Bram Mabry, whom the free companies called the Ox of Thrum, had burned a tithe barn with nine people in it, and on the feast of the first pressing he was sitting in the middle of Thrum Market in a coat of boiled red leather, eating a whole roast pig at a stall that had not been paid, with forty men around him and an axe across his knees the size of a door. Ikuko Akagane Moto came through the cider stalls alone, small, in copper fittings, with a warrant. He laughed so hard he got up to enjoy it, and the axe came round in a flat arc that cleared the pig and three stools. She was inside it by then, arriving at his chest on a step that nobody watching could afterward describe, as though her being there were simply the next correct thing, and her coat flared the raw orange-red of a copper pan on a hard fire as she laid two fingers on his breastbone and said, in the voice of a woman reading out a bill, **"HEART-WARRANT."**

She hit him about as hard as you would test a peach. His own account, given later, is that his heart stopped being his and recognized what it owed. The axe went down into the pig fat. Bram Mabry knelt in it and recited the nine names from the barn in order, with their ages and what they had been wearing, weeping like a child, in front of the whole market, while forty hard men looked at one another and began to walk quickly in several directions. Ikuko Akagane Moto then bled from both ears, and for three days could not have told you her own mother's name. The Akagane consider that a fair rate.

The rest of the world has never got over this. Other lineages hit you. Here is the family that serves you with papers from the inside of your own chest.

## Who They Are

**Bloodline nature** · An archaic divine lineage marked by inherited authority, spiritual weight, and unnatural bodily refinement.

**Core inheritances** · **The Shingan** as sacred ocular lineage and **Hataraki** as the family's deeper governing power.

**General reputation** · *Beautiful, unnerving, politically dangerous, and impossible to mistake for an ordinary noble house.*

Every other old family in the world tells you what it can do to you. The Moto tell you what you are for. They hold that the world began with assignments, that everything in it has been given a job, and that their house has the sight to read the job and the inherited hands to enforce it. They find this obvious. Most of the world finds it the most frightening thing anyone has ever said at dinner, and pays them anyway.

They pay because of what the house holds that everyone needs: **a determination nobody can argue with.** For the whole of the Long Reading, kings climbed a white stair to be told how their wars would end, and went home and signed the treaty. A contract sealed at that stair held anywhere. The stair is gone and the appetite has not moved. A judgment from the Hall of Verdicts is the only thing Kharven exports that nobody else can make, and foreigners still pay for one and then complain about it. A house that will read a bridge, a bride or a business partner is a house with a long line at its door, whatever it did the century before.

What they want is short and it has never changed: to keep the Loom (their word for the workings of Fate, the Plane of Attraction and Obsession) theirs to see, and to be correct. Their whole vocabulary of praise is one word, *correct*, and their whole vocabulary of blame is another, *inaccurate*, and I have seen a Moto lord take the second harder than a spear. What they fear is the same thing they do for a living. They have watched what happens to a family that is never wrong, because it was them, and they keep the record of it in their own training register and teach it without comment.

I have read this house for a long time, and I would rather you heard the rest from someone who is afraid of them.

## Origin of the House

**Bara authored the Works before chronology, and Mizuki gave that authorship a family.** Bara in human form walked in the days of the Physical Epochs. He took a wife who understood what he did not, which is that a law nobody inherits ends with its author. The house's whole story is the long working-out of that marriage: his side supplies the function, hers supplies the children, and every Moto ever born has been asked to be worthy of the first and to survive being the second. His private record, the [[The Disciplines/Schools and Bloodline Arts/Hataraki — The Works of the Plane of Fate/Hataraki no Sho — The Martial Application of the Works|Hataraki no Sho]], survives in his own hand and his own manners.

**Bara and Mizuki → the Long Reading at the white shelf → the Refusal and the crossing → the seven lines under one crown**

**The Long Reading.** On a shelf of white stone at the land's western edge the family held no conquest and needed none. It read: what a thing was, what it owed, what it would become. It read populations from birth and placed the determination into the body with the Covenant Scar, a law-mark in living flesh. No chain was ever needed, because a person who left their assigned function simply stopped being recognized by anyone. The family would have been offended to hear this called slavery, and the offense would have been sincere. The whole dreadful account is [[Factions, Bloodlines & Institutions/Houses and Bloodlines/The Archaic Bloodlines/The Moto Bloodline/The Long Reading — Moto Ancient History|The Long Reading]], and I recommend you eat first.

**The Refusal and the Stay.** The Tengan lines read a catastrophe approaching and could not read its hour. The Kōkan proposed to hold the ground by braiding every Work under one judgment. The other six called that a gathering dressed as a rescue, and went north with their registers and their retainers. The Kōkan stayed with everyone who would not leave, and the frame held long enough for the crossing, and then it failed with them standing on it. Every Moto alive descends from someone who crossed water on time purchased by a working their own line had declared unlawful. *Both readings were correct. That is the whole of the problem.*

**The north.** The lines came ashore with their registers and no standing, and superimposed themselves on clans who had never heard of them, because a people that migrates brings farmers and a house that migrates brings law. Kharven-Seat stood on its rock already. The Kōkan sat its red chair for a long time, until the Tenrai took the rock from them three hundred years ago in a manner the law has found no way to mention. Then came the forty-year war against the Zettari, the last king ruling from a saddle, and his cellars at Halcrest.

> **Two accounts this office has not reconciled.** One has the Kōkan broken on the coast below the white shelf, a line of unregistered families read out of the record by an honest clerk and sold into Nalūn. The other has them sitting the red chair at Kharven-Seat under a thousand years of worn stone. Both are in the registers, sometimes in the same hand. The house counts eleven hundred years since the crossing, and the Guild's reckoning is a good deal shorter; the house, as usual, has not troubled to argue. A compiler who tells you he has settled it is selling something.

### The Western Shield

*The Moto originate in the western reach of the Inner World.*

The Old World has no claim on it. The origin is the **western part of the northern shield**, past the ridgeline that now marks Kharven's border with Undaar-Keth, on ground that no crown currently claims and no border commission patrols. The fuller account is [[Factions, Bloodlines & Institutions/Houses and Bloodlines/The Archaic Bloodlines/The Moto Bloodline/The Moto Origin — The Western Reach|The Moto Origin]].

**The seat** · Standing, ruined, and depopulated. Broken structures, a well, a gatepost, frost on it most of the year. **A woman of the house, Somnalis-primary and counted on nobody's roll, has lived alone in a cabin there for the last stretch of her life** and no longer touches a wall without reading what happened against it, because the density of residual memory on that ground exceeds anything else in her record.

**The displacement** · The first blow on the white shelf, about 310 IC, which the Moto registers call the split, reshaped the western ground and made the seat untenable. The six lines scattered toward temperate ground, and the Kōkan stayed and were broken (see The Long Reading). Generations later Muken went north and east into the boreal forest, on reasoning that was strategic before it was sentimental, and took the crown of a Kharven-Seat that already stood, over clans who had never heard of the place his blood came from. The great split proper comes after him, about 740 IC, and he survives it.

**What this means for the house** · Every Moto seat in the current era is superimposed on somebody else's ground. Kharven is Concord-stock northern clans who predate the Moto arrival by centuries. The bloodline arrives, governs, enforces, and does not assimilate, because a people that migrates brings farmers and a house that migrates brings law.
> **The ruin** · The seat is described as *ruined*, and the residual saturation read there is consistent with mass death rather than slow abandonment. **What ruined it, and when, nobody will say.** The first blow is the obvious answer and is probably incomplete, because glaciation does not leave that kind of imprint and a massacre does.
>
> **A false seat** · Older gazetteers give the house the Malō of Sātūlagi. They are wrong. The Moto hold one seat and it is Kharven. The six non-Kōkan lines are landless.

## The House on the Crystal

### What the Blood Gives

Like every [[Factions, Bloodlines & Institutions/Houses and Bloodlines/The Archaic Bloodlines/The Archaic Bloodlines|archaic line]], the Moto get four things and never a Path. Theirs have an Epoch behind them, so they get an instrument besides.

| Slot | The Moto |
|---|---|
| **The eye** | The Shingan, in one of four branches, set out below |
| **The instrument** | [[The Disciplines/Schools and Bloodline Arts/Hataraki — The Works of the Plane of Fate/Hataraki — The Works of the Plane of Fate|Hataraki]], with one Work truly theirs and the rest known by rote |
| **The grip** | [[The Magic System/The Soul and the Climb/Classes and the Three Ladders|Thread Class]] may open in childhood, before Glory. Blood opens it early and the cap never lifts |
| **The stat lean** | Gnosis Perception and Analysis; Dominion Fate and Sense |
| **Lens affinities** | The Wellsprings of Fate and the Medium kind first, and each Work has its own list of what a Moto of that Work tends to temper on |

The Range, the Aura Pressure and the presence of the Fate Path are not among them. A Moto gets those by walking that road like anyone else, and most of the fighting house lead on the Body or the Spirit road, read with the Eye, and hit. The ones who do walk it are hidden under plain banners for good reason.

### The three layers

A soul is a Crystal with three layers, one to each plane. Here is what the blood has done to each, which is less than the songs say.

| Layer | Plane | In a Moto |
|---|---|---|
| **Aether Shell** (the outer layer, where mana comes in and force goes out) | The Physical World | Nothing at all. A Moto's Shell is an ordinary Shell of its Stage, clean or loud according to how its owner has lived. The sheen on the skin is a birthmark. This is the fact every Body fighter who has killed a Moto was relying on |
| **Essence Core** (the center of identity, where Essence, spiritual energy, is authored) | The Soul Plane | Sharply edged and heavily carried. The Work presses the soul into a useful shape from childhood, so a grown Moto has a trained self laid over a first self, and the two have rarely met. [[The Disciplines/Schools and Bloodline Arts/Asami and Gisei — The Two States of the Moto|Asami]] is the meeting |
| **Attraction Layer** (a soul's own share of fate, and its reach above it) | The Plane of Fate | This is where the blood lives. Generations of somebody else's grip are laid in here, ready for a child's hand to close on, and it is why the ladder that stays shut for nearly everyone alive may open in a Moto nursery |

### Path, as a tendency

A Path is the layer a Crystal has learned to lean on, recognized from how a soul has lived, and the blood has no vote. What the blood does shape is what a Moto child does with a free afternoon: stares at the wall, tells you which part of it is lying, and then wants to go and hit that part. So the lean of the untrained soul runs toward the Body or the Spirit road with the eye running ahead of either, and by the time the Crystal has an opinion, the fighting house sorts about like this on the Hall's rolls.

| Road | About how many | What it makes |
|---|---|---|
| **Body** | Half | A reader with a blade. Sees the seam, puts the steel in it, needs no thread at all. The house's great killers have nearly all stood here |
| **Spirit** | A third | A reader of souls, roads and rooms, who survives by being right. The war-schools, the surveyors, the soul-surgeons |
| **Fate** | What is left | The ones you will not meet. Range, Aura Pressure, presence and bonds are theirs only because they walked for them, at the same price as anyone. The few who lead on this road are the people under the plain banners, and they do not appear in the songs |

A great many of the Body and Spirit people also walk Fate as a second road, because practice on that road is counted in vows kept and bonds held, and a Moto is sworn to something from the age of nine. It costs them what it costs anyone: about two and a half times the years, and three-quarters of the force bonus on each road for life. A soul can also merely study it, which opens no gates.

### Thread Class in the house

**Thread Class** is the ladder that measures how much of Fate a soul can pull down and hold. It has no Stage climb, it moves by grip plus decades, and its numeral gives η (eta, the share of a spend that arrives as intended) as two rungs below the same numeral elsewhere. Most souls alive stay at Ø. A Moto may read I at an age when other children are reading their sums, which is the whole of what the blood does to the ladder. Blood opens it early and the cap never lifts.

| Rung | Typical in the house |
|---|---|
| **Ø · Unthreaded** | Plenty of cousins, and no shame in it. A white-haired child whose hand never closes on anything is still a Moto, and may still have the eye |
| **I · Touched** | The ordinary grown Moto who holds a Work, including lords who rule provinces. They feel the strap go. On a rare day they can lean on it for most of a reserve and a nosebleed |
| **II · Drawn** | A life given to it, an oath kept since childhood, or a war-school elder's fifty years. One lean in a fight, if the fight lasts |
| **III · Spinner** | Decades at the head of a war. One prerequisite of one blow, at a ruinous price |
| **IV · Knotwright** | Centuries, or something worse than centuries. Almost unattested among the living |
| **V · Weaver** | The old woman at Akagawa, with eleven years behind one morning. Each side's first job in a war is to find these people |
| **VI · Severer** | Lateral, an inverted way of holding. Cuts where the others pull. Attested in old Kurenai and Kōkan registers, in a hand the house does not let foreigners read |
| **Ω · Absolute** | Where the Archons stand. No Moto is entered here. The founder is not entered anywhere |

Aether Class and Essence Class, the Shell's and the Core's ladders, follow the person's road as they do for anyone, so a Body lord may read Aether IV and Essence Ø beside his Thread III, and a Spirit elder Essence V and Aether II. The clerk writes the Work on a line of its own, and it counts as no Trait, technique, blessing or Class: `Fate Art: Ruin · grip · Thread I`.

The names a soul offers at each rung run to offices on Moto ground. The 関守 **Sekimori**, Barrier-Warden, of the plateau's gate-keeps is the common one, her aura standing in the shape of a door-frame, and nobody laughs; it may go on to **Kimonban**, Demon-Gate Watch, and it is spoken quietly.

## The Eye and the Instrument

**The eye finds the job, and the hand fires the worker.** That is the whole of it, and every Moto child can say it before they can say anything else.

> **The Shingan determines what layer of reality the bearer can perceive most clearly. Hataraki determines what lawful function the bearer can impose once that layer is perceived.**

The founder puts it with less patience. The Eye without the Work is an archivist too frightened to write in the margins. The Work without the Eye is tyranny practiced by the blind.

### The Shingan

The *shingan* is the heart-eye, and it manifests in both eyes at once with one dominant branch. Every Moto who has it starts in the same place: **they see where a thing is carrying its weight.** A wall, a knee, a marriage, a company of foot, a lie held in the shoulders. Where a smith reads a bar, a Moto reads the room, and what comes back is stress lines, as fine as crazing on old glaze, gathering at the place a thing will give. Any Moto brat can see where a wall wants to fall. That is the common sight, it opens by crisis more often than by schedule, and most of a Moto's fighting is done with nothing more.

Then the line's branch opens on top of it, and tells the bearer what else the weight is.

| Branch | Line | Governs | What it will not show | The tell |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Meigan** · Eye of Revelation | Byakuya, Shirogane | Hidden structure, concealment, false surfaces, and the breaking of outer lies | A thing that is exactly what it says it is, or that has no seam at all | Rings flat and bright, the eyes going to a joint or a strap before a face |
| **Reigan** · Eye of the Soul | Akagane, Kurenai | Living essence, curse anatomy, soul structure, and bloodline law, and it can find a soul as far as four hundred miles off | What is about to be done. It reads the record in a chest and never the plan. It reads the dead as nothing | Capillary light in the iris, the gaze on your breastbone |
| **Tengan** · Eye of Fate | Tenrai, Amagiri | Probability pressure, convergence, causality bias, and near-future vectors | The hour, and anything truly improbable. It shows pressure and vector, and every disaster in the house's history began in that gap | Fine spokes in the iris, like a wheel |
| **Kamigan** · Crown Eye | Kōkan | **Unifies revelation, soul dominion, and fate imposition into sovereign judgment.** It ranks everything the other eyes see, worst first, and it outranks them: a Tengan's read blurs under it | A fault that is not clear. Its judgment has to have something clean to fall on | A seam of gold in the iris; at full expression a mandala of interlocking judgment-lines |

**How it shows.** Many Moto carry sigilic marks in the iris from birth: ring-lines, shard-patterns, mirrored flecks, capillary lights, spokes. A grown adept may show three rings at rest and a flare at the outer ring when attention shifts, and clerks go by the count, because the house's own names for the stages of the eye are not given to this office. The rings turn when something moves the person, so a court learns to watch them, and the white chair has been known to break and mend a whole verdict in one slow turn of an iris.

**How it reads, for the register.** It has no lens and needs none. Its Form is Read. A read of matter goes out through the Shell, a read of a soul or a meaning through the Core, and either way it spends the soul's own share and pulls nothing down, so a Thread Ø cousin with a good eye loses nothing by it. Against a person it is Gnosis Perception against Resilience Ward, Grade against Grade: a stronger Perception reads a weaker Ward, and anything else is an estimate said aloud. The eye may stay open at a steady cost of about a twentieth of the reserve a minute for a young reader and a hair of it for an old one, paid as heat behind the eye.

**What it costs.** Held open past its depth it opens further and does not close: the seam widens a thread, the ring stays lit. After a hard verdict comes **eye bleed, a sternum ache and twenty to thirty seconds of flat nothing**, and the retainers know to bring a lord his beads before his water, and they know that the flat half-minute is the moment to tell him anything he would otherwise kill you for. Worst of all, it does not switch off at the supper table. Ask a Moto's wife.

**Who beats it.** A whole thing has no seam, so freshly forged, truly soft or simply sound gives it nothing to read. Bog, deep snow and open water have no load in them. The eye shows where a man will break and never what he means, which is why one of the great Moto kings could not see that what he was ranking was his own house. A ward that returns or quiets a reading (Oblivara's mirror, a Tenebra veil) leaves it counting nothing. A man who has gone truly quiet inside, or wants nothing, gives it little. The plain unlikely act surprises a Tengan like anyone. And the Kōkan's own mark comes back from a fate-read like a page with no ink on it, which the other six lines find extremely funny until it happens to them.

**Against the other two.** The [[The Disciplines/Schools and Bloodline Arts/The Ketsumyōgan — The Binding Fate Eye|Ketsumyōgan]] sees the threads between things where the Shingan sees the things, and it reads where the Shingan judges. The Zettari's Everlasting Eye reads how a thing stands, and its far end asks where that thing began. The Shingan asks what a thing owes. The Moto will tell you this makes theirs the most useful question, and the Yukari will ask who gave them the right.

### The Fate Ledger

*Unmei-chō.* **A battle-reading discipline that internally ranks threat, cost, weakness, and probable turn as though the fight were a living legal inventory.** It is no gift of the blood: it is drilled, every morning, from about the age of seven, and it is the single most dangerous thing the house teaches, because it works for anyone with the eye and it makes the eye useful in a hurry. A cadet is walked through a fair and asked to enter the stalls in the order they will close. Hanae Tenrai Moto, aged nine, once entered an Accord factor's third wagon, his second son and his solvency for him at a feast at Harlsey, unasked, in that order, and then, as she had been taught, presented an invoice.

Written out for a tutor, an exercise looks like this:

```
[Ledger: the yard at Harlsey, an hour before Ice-out]
  1. Left gatepost. Frost-seam. Gone by the thaw.
  2. Third company sergeant. Unpaid two quarters. Will not hold.
  3. Gray mule, near axle. A week.
  To act on 1: a rope and a boy.
  To pull on 1: do not. See above, a rope and a boy.
```

That last line is the whole of the education. A grown Moto runs this without moving their lips, on every room, every day, and it never stops. They rank their dinner guests. They rank their children, and the children know it. In a fight it is why they seem to be two exchanges ahead and bored, and why, when it is wrong, they are wrong with complete confidence and a very neat hand.

### Hataraki: Seven Grips

*Hataraki*, literally translated as Work, is the governing function of the Plane of Fate. [[The Disciplines/Schools and Bloodline Arts/Hataraki — The Works of the Plane of Fate/Hataraki — The Works of the Plane of Fate|The Works have their own page]] and the founder's record has the arts. This is what they are in a Moto's hand.

Because Bara authored the Works **before chronology itself**, the Moto **inherit an unusual intimacy with Hataraki** that no later student of the discipline can study his way into.
> Their techniques therefore tend to feel **administrative rather than merely explosive.**
>
> **A Moto strike may reveal, rank, condemn, stabilize, sever, or complete rather than simply hit harder.**

**Attraction Force** is the law of recognition: it allows names, vows, lineages, domains, wounds, and outcomes to be acknowledged and remain lawful inside reality. **Many Moto arts work by making a thing recognize what it truly is, what it owes, what it hides, or what role it now must perform.**

**Obsession Force** is the corrupted inversion. When a Moto cannot release judgment, lineage, memory, or revelation, **function curdles into fixation, captivity, recursion, and spiritual suffocation.**

**Why Fate is the hardest thing there is.** Physical Reality and Spiritual Reality are places a person can stand and push. The thing the Guild calls the Plane of Fate as a courtesy sits above them both: everything that flows, every possibility and concept, what has to be so before anything can happen. There is nothing up there to brace a foot against, and nothing in a person to brace with, because your notions of cause, of likely and of what a blow is all came down from there. You cannot reason your way to a grip on the thing your reason is made of. Measurewrights have described threads for whole careers and never lifted one, and the founder, asked by a clever child whether a gifted descendant might dispense with the Works and take hold directly, wrote down the whole of his reply: *No. So I cut handles.*

**That is the inheritance: somewhere to put your hand.** Each Work is one distinct, inherited way of taking hold of one function, and it takes hold of nothing else.

| Work | The grip closes on | It will not close on | Held best by | What it curdles into |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Hametsu no Gō** · Ruin | What is already failing, at the seam where it fails | Anything sound | Kurenai, Shirogane, the Kōkan's breakers | Annihilation: ending as a general principle |
| **Junketsu no Gō** · Purity | What ought to stand, and the stain on it | A thing with no rightful form to go back to | Byakuya | Fanatic sterilization. Of the seven this one smiles the most |
| **Seirei no Gō** · Spirit | The crossing between life and death, and whoever stands in it | The living who have nothing owed there | Akagane, Kurenai | Soul-hoarding |
| **Jikan no Shigoto** · Temporality | What was done earlier and is due now | A debt of sequence never run up | Tenrai, Amagiri | Recursion: one wound, forever |
| **Chishiki no Shigoto** · Knowledge | What a thing is, under its name | A thing exactly what it says it is, which is why the honest sleep well | Byakuya, Shirogane, Akagane | Devouring certainty |
| **Baransu no Shigoto** · Balance | An excess already running, turned on the one running it | Anyone not overdoing anything | Tenrai, Amagiri | Suffocating stasis |
| **Zentai-sei no Hataraki** · Totality | All of the above, in one hand | Very little, which is why the founder would not trust the hand | Kōkan | **Tyrannical subsumation** |

**One Work truly held.** The grips do not trade. A hand trained to Ruin is no use on a Purity job, any more than tongs are a ladle. A Moto lives and dies with one Work truly theirs, the rest known by rote, and that is the honest reason the house needs seven lines and a great many aunts. *By rote* means real and small: a rote Knowledge hears what a man is humming under his face, a rote Temporality follows a thread back one knot at a time, a rote Purity can knit a fresh wound on a willing body. None of those has closed on anything above the person's own share. Plenty of the house's lords hold no Work at all and have never missed it. A war-school can drill one narrow line-art into a Tenrai's hand until the blood closes on just that much, and such a person will tell you, correctly, that they do not hold a Work.

**Three depths of a Work.** Most of what the world calls Hataraki is the shallow two, and a Moto who spends the third where the second would do has not been listening.

| Depth | What happens | How it is read | What it costs |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The read** | The Eye and the Work together name what the thing is doing | Essence or Aether by what is read. Pulls nothing | A small share. Heat behind the eye |
| **The form** | A house or line art rides a blade, a step or a palm onto something that already offers the grip a handle: rot in a rope, a stain on a blade, a debt in a chest. It rides the fighter's own Path and lens, and the blow reads its own ladder | The fighter's lead ladder. No thread is held | The art's own share, from a hundredth of the reserve to a fifth, by how far off the thing is and how much of it there is |
| **The pull** | A thread of Fate is brought down and what the result depends on is moved: this cut was always going to land | Thread Class, on top of everything else being paid. Dominion Fate against the target's Persistence and Continuity | See below. It has never been cheap |

**A Work lowers failure and never the price.** Everyone asks, and the [[The Magic System/The Soul and the Climb/Classes and the Three Ladders|price table]] answers: a pull costs a share of the reserve set by the rung against the distance of the result from likely, and a Thread II Moto leaning on a strap that is already going pays about half of what she walked in with, exactly as a stranger would. What she gets for her ancestors is that it usually lands where the stranger's often fails, and that she may try again next year, where a stranger's rare personal grip is won once and never comes back. Off her own Work she has no such help. What is out of reach for her rung stays out of reach, and what lies past the table's right-hand column is a Stage XII soul emptied to the floor in front of its own soldiers. It leaves the body bleeding, from the nose and eyes as a rule. It has a visible charge: a stillness in the eyes, the breath held on a count that is not the count of the exchange, the air gone slightly too tidy. And it moves only what already has a way of happening, which nine thousand people at Tobishima watched young Moto Naoki learn on a very good gate.

**What the house does most of the time is read, then hit.** The Shingan finds the seam, the sword goes in at the seam, and no thread has been touched. The founder is unusually clear about this. *For the rest of the fight you will read the seam and hit the seam with steel like a person with some self-respect.* Hataraki is no larger than other magic and no better, as any fool with a hammer will teach you if you stand close enough, but it is prior.

### Firing the Worker: the Two-Hand Office

A blessing, to a Moto, is a job someone has assigned to the odds, and a job can be read, and a worker can be fired. Nothing else in the world does this as a matter of routine, whoever gave the blessing, and it is why people run when a Moto starts reading their thumb.

**It takes two Works or two Moto**, because one grip does one job. The house calls the pair the *futari-yaku*, the two-hand office, and a remover who arrives alone is either bluffing or has brought someone you have not seen. In the spring of 692 Little Tolland spent its treasury on a letter north, and old Tokiwa Moto came down the Kessen road with two attendants and a bill, stood a blessed man on a bridge, read the line aloud, and said one quiet sentence to it. The brass print went out like a lamp, and he lived out the week in the best mood anyone had ever seen him.

| Motion | Whose hand | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| **The reading** | Knowledge | The line is read for what it is, giver and terms, and for a moment the holder's own window shows the whole of it in plain words, usually for the first time |
| **The naming** | Either | The job and its giver are said aloud, because Hataraki administers purpose and has to say what the purpose was |
| **The discharge** | Ruin or Purity | Ruin breaks a line that has become a false continuity. Purity makes a thing stand as it ought until a line sitting in the soul as a stain has nowhere to stay. A true gift, well held for decades, offers neither handle and takes days, with the holder awake |

To *take back* returns a line to its author, who feels it arrive, and that is a message. To *remove* ends it where it stands. Everything standing on the line comes down at once, and the giver knows who took the pen. The rest is in [[The Magic System/Practice and Institution/Blessings and Curses|Blessings and Curses]]. The Moto bill for it in obligations, and nobody has yet established what Little Tolland still owes.

### Beneath the Work: Asami and Gisei

Every Moto carries two truths, the person the bloodline built and the person who existed before any of that. [[The Disciplines/Schools and Bloodline Arts/Asami and Gisei — The Two States of the Moto|The two states]] are what happens when they meet.

**Asami, the Shallow-Seen,** is the first self surfacing so completely that the trained one stops holding its shape above it. The Ledger and the sorting go quiet with nothing suppressed. The practitioner reports no power at all, only quiet. Every art becomes native, Obsession Force finds nothing to attach to, and the aura becomes a kind of law, unaimed, that hurts whatever in the room is lying about itself. It lightens no pull: the hand stops shaking and the weight stays exactly what it was. Most Moto of sufficient depth reach it, and the correct outcome, the one the house was designed to produce, is to integrate it and live as one person. *The Work is not that you saw it. The Work is that you returned.*

Its inversion is **the Shallow-Seen Unburied**, the same stillness with the attention turned inward, discharging instead of inhabiting. Nobody can tell the two apart for the first several seconds. The Sin of Wrath, the burning man in irons who frightens children from the Foot to the Grey Teeth, is what it looks like after three hundred years.

**Gisei, the Offering,** spends what Asami found. A memory, a bond, a capacity or a Trait goes into the fire for good, on the Oblation mechanism, and the Plane skips the Temperance and checks the payment, so a Stage VIII can perform a Form four Stages over her head. Moto Sayo did it on the chain at the Akanoe water gate with the memory of her son's first word. It only pays if it tears a hole. Most Moto who find Asami never touch it, and the house's literature keeps one claim on the subject that it does not teach: that the Plane is not something a Moto reaches into at all, that it is what a Moto is made of, and that the whole architecture of the bloodline exists to keep that fact from ever becoming load-bearing.

### Who beats all of this

Somebody who arrives. A Work is slow to set against a straight exchange, and a Body veteran who reaches a Moto before the result lands is hitting an ordinary body at its Stage. Somebody sound: good straps, fresh horses, inspected bridges, paid companies and no bad habits give the house very little to read and nothing to pull on. Somebody with their eyes open and their fate-sight shut. Another Fate user, leaning the other way. A soul well enough bound to refuse the result. Any Spirit fighter who can see the pull on the Core before it lands and go for the hand on the thread. And the Moto themselves, who have buried more of their own children than anyone else has managed to.

## How the House Wages War

The levies who marched on Akagawa, twenty thousand spears against a Moto host of three thousand, lost to a rotten bridge pile, a thrown shoe, a turn of the wind and a rumor that was very nearly true, and by noon they were running where somebody under a plain banner had arranged for them to run. No other lineage on record fights like this, and the reason is a matter of inheritance rather than talent. Fate is the hardest thing in the system. Nobody grips it bare, and each Work of [[The Disciplines/Schools and Bloodline Arts/Hataraki — The Works of the Plane of Fate/Hataraki — The Works of the Plane of Fate|Hataraki]] is a distinct, inherited way of taking hold of one function, so a Moto child starts with generations of somebody else's grip. Even so, a field moved is a life's masterwork, and it may be done once in a war.

**They read first.** The Tengan, the Eye of Fate, reads probability pressure and convergence, and the founder's whole instruction on it in the [[The Disciplines/Schools and Bloodline Arts/Hataraki — The Works of the Plane of Fate/Hataraki no Sho — The Martial Application of the Works|Hataraki no Sho]] is that the adept asks which outcome has begun demanding itself of the field. Every officer of the house is drilled from childhood in the Fate Ledger, which is why their children can rank a field the way others do sums: threat, cost, weakness and probable turn, entered as an inventory. The Meigan does the same for masonry and harness, and notes where a thing has been pretending not to break.

**Then they arrange.** Most of a Moto campaign is ordinary war done by people who see more of it, and needs no thread at all. *Mikettei no Ame*, the Rain of the Unchosen, belongs to formations, command corridors, cavalry timing and the engineering of panic, and it has been done with nothing but banners, disciplined footwork and the knowledge that frightened men flee into the route that feels least chosen by them. The Tenrai are the war-command line and the Banner of the Next Turn and the Warroom Edict are theirs. The Amagiri work the roads, and Delay Harvest and Pursuer's Ruin are why a column chasing one arrives late and short of bread.

**Sometimes they pull.** Hōkai Senkoku pronounces collapse on what is already hiding its exhaustion, and in fortification warfare Kaimetsu no Hasuji waits for masonry to confess where the stress has settled. Innen Kusari makes one earlier truth arrive on time. Futatsu no Tenbin tilts a field just enough that its excess begins punishing itself, and for the rare battlefield where several laws are already colliding the Kōkan hold Teiho no Oza, which ranks them. The founder sums up the method as arranging the enemy into obedience, so that one need not always strike, and when he does strike the finishing form comes last, as the legal consequence of everything the target has already failed to survive. At Akagawa that part was done on the riverbank with ordinary spears.

**It is paid for and it can be stopped.** The art moves only what already has a way of happening, so a sound gate stands whoever pronounces collapse on it, and a careful enemy with inspected bridges, shod horses and paid companies gives the house very little to read. The one who does the moving pays in their own body, sometimes all of it. Each Work carries its corruption, and a commander who begins to enjoy ending things is the house's oldest way of losing. So every serious enemy's first job on a field with Moto on it is to find whoever is doing the reading and reach them, ahead of even the house's champions, which is one reason that person so often stands under a plain banner.

### A host in order

An army, the founder observes, is a thousand straps wearing at once, and this is why his house has so seldom needed to be the larger host. So a Moto campaign has a shape, and it has not changed since his record.

1. **A season of reading.** Nobody spends a drop. The Tengan officers rank the enemy's march, the Meigan engineers walk the bridges under some pretext, a Reigan surgeon is sent across under a flag to treat somebody important and comes home having read everyone in the tent. Most of what you will call victory is marching to the right place on the right day, and arithmetic.
2. **Find their Fate users first.** Before their champions, before their general, before breakfast. A single old woman in the baggage train can undo a season's work with one thing going wrong at the ford, and she is thinking the same about you.
3. **Give each Work its office.** Ruin for the bridge. Purity for your own line and your own water, which you will neglect. Spirit for the dead of the last battle, who are otherwise available to anyone. Temporality for the hour. Knowledge for their captains. Balance for the point at which they have overreached, which they will.
4. **Spend the pull once,** late, on something already going. At Akagawa the old woman under the plain banner had given eleven years to being able to do it once, and it took two days to arrange. Four of her people carried her home, and she did not walk again or know her own daughters.
5. **Then the riverbank.** Ordinary spears, which is the part every song leaves out.

| Line | Its place in a host |
|---|---|
| **Tenrai** | The command tent, the banners, the timing, and whoever is arguing with the king |
| **Amagiri** | Roads, passes, forage, the mule count, and getting everyone out afterward |
| **Akagane** | Surgeons and provisioners. They keep hearts going and they sign for every sack |
| **Shirogane** | Siege. Anything with a wall, and the people who were counting on it |
| **Byakuya** | The king's guard, half a step ahead of him and to his left |
| **Kurenai** | The executioner's office, carried in the baggage in black and ash, for the deserter and the broken oath |
| **Kōkan** | The judgment. The one hand allowed to rank what the others find |

The Fate users themselves wear plain hemp under no banner, with a very good bodyguard who has been told nothing.

**Why the long war took forty years.** Because the last Kōkan king had forgotten all of this, and the Zettari had never given him anything to read. A Zettari host is the soundest thing on two legs, every piece of it load-bearing and well kept, and a king on the Body road who struck everything he met as hard as he could was the opposite of the founder's method. What he got instead was the hatred of the north, and forty harvests' worth of fathers sent home in carts. A war against the Moto is won or lost in the quartermaster's tent as often as on the field, and nobody who has fought them ever again buys cheap harness. They have rather less to say about the wars they have lost.

## The Moto and the Archons

The Archons are crowned ideas, and the Moto are the only family alive who know what the crown is sitting on. A Work is a meaning at its barest, and an [[Cosmology & Metaphysics/Faith and the Powers Above/The Fourteen Archons/The Fourteen Archons|Archon]] is that meaning risen into sovereignty, with someone home. So the house stands in an awkward spot: its founder wrote what the Fourteen are crowned on, and its descendants are mortal people with bad knees.

They stand toward the Archons exactly as they stand toward a very senior officer of an employer they share, and that attitude has got more of them killed than anything else on this page.

**They bow to all fourteen, and they ask for nothing.** The house's faith is [[Factions, Bloodlines & Institutions/Orders, Faiths and Cults/Sōhai — The Whole Bow|Sōhai, the Whole Bow]]: fourteen inclinations at the hinge of the day, under two minutes in a cold room, and fourteen labors done with the hands. **Singling**, the elevation of one Archon, is the principal heresy. **Petition** is contempt: there is no Moto "help me." The only lawful invocation in a Moto mouth is *By the Fourteen*. A Moto who hears a Kharven say "Irath take it" hears heresy and will mention it.

**They read by delegated sight.** The Archons read the Parun lattice (the root script of law) from fourteen stations, and each station gives a hand. The Moto read the same object from above, from the Loom, on a leave the Kōkan once administered, so the Shingan and a cut glyph are looking at one thing from two very different chairs. That is why the Counted Speech has no Warrant word for what the house holds. The house takes this as proof of rank. The Archons have not said.

**They are not alone up there.** Most if not all of the Fourteen are Sovereign across all three planes, and on the Plane of Fate they are the most powerful things that still act, because they are the laws as well as the powers. Urion and Uurgath have no peers even among the rest, because what they are cannot be conceived, and Bara stands somewhere above them all, and has not written lately. A thread pulled down from that height may turn out to be somebody's. Some of those thrones have someone home, and a Hollow Throne runs on and never looks up. The house keeps a short list of which is which, and that list has been wrong twice.

Yorimasa Kurenai Moto, a Thread III headsman of no particular ambition, pulled on what he took for an ordinary debt at the Saltash cloth fair, and the thread turned out to have been Urion's. Nothing struck him, and he reached home. From then on every scale he stood near came out true to the grain, whoever had thumbed it and whatever the merchant had paid. Grocers crossed the street. Three towns barred him from their markets by name. In the last year of his life his household weighed his food in another room and carried it to him, and he remained, in the words of the Kurenai register, *correct, and very thin*.

| When a Moto pulls on | They are putting a hand on | What the house does about that |
|---|---|---|
| Ruin | What Uurgath's Disruption and Auren's endings are crowned on | Pulls only on what has earned it, and does not pray about it |
| Purity | What Thalen's Form and Elyndra's Life are crowned on | Stays out of a shrine's own business |
| Spirit | What Auren and Maelor are crowned on | Lets the dead go. *Release before you cling* is an order, and also a precaution |
| Temporality | What Valen's Time is crowned on | Never pulls twice on one hour |
| Knowledge | What Iesara's Insight and Maelor's Memory are crowned on | Reads aloud only what it means to answer for |
| Balance | What Urion's Balance is crowned on. See Yorimasa | Tilts the field and takes its hand away at once |
| Totality | All of it, at once, in one hand | The Kōkan's own watch on themselves, since Totality is the Work most easily mistaken for a crown |

**The charge against Alabaster.** The house's quarrel with Sancta Lux is doctrinal and it is about this: that the Scale consults Urion alone, and has kept better records of its devotion than any shrine. The order answers that a reference is chosen for its stability and not for its supremacy. One Moto heir put the house's side at a supper table: the Moto did not build the Scale, they are one of the things it was calibrated on. The Holy Sea has not replied, and it stands on the house's old white stone, reading the scar of the house's failed working, as a tenant who has never held the deed.

*Later hand:* the Kōkan arts having gone into the ground at Halcrest with everyone who held them, the last of the red line had his Works from a Grand Angel in the Plane of Fate, six of the seven, with Totality withheld for a reason he has accepted. The house has not decided how to enter this. It is the first time anyone but a parent has taught a Moto what they are for, and the Angel, to the house's considerable embarrassment, was right.

## Physical Appearance and Dress

### Shared blood markers

**Hair** · White appears throughout all seven lines and is **never exclusive to a single branch**: moon-white, pearl, frost, bone, ash, silver-white, and white touched by faint blue, gold, pink or violet undertones. The family also produces black, silver, gold, bronze, auburn, copper, red, rose-gold, pink, mauve, lilac, lavender, violet, plum, and opaline metallic tones depending on line, parental mixture, and resonance strength.

**Eyes** · Many Moto show **sigilic irregularities in the iris even before awakening**: ring-lines, shard-patterns, mirrored flecks, capillary lights, or vector-like spokes.

**Skin** · Metallic birth-sheens along the brow, clavicle, wrists, spine, temples, or hands, *as though the flesh remembers an older law than ordinary blood.*

**Presence** · Even untrained Moto often feel **too composed, too sharply outlined, or too heavy in bearing** to be mistaken for common mortals.

**Stature and voice.** Nothing the blood insists on: the house runs from slight road-surveyors to men broad as a door, and its power has never lived in the shoulders. What it shares is a stillness that carries weight even at rest, the look of someone who moved before they decided to, and a way of leaving a silence for you to fill. The old register has no contractions in it, so a Moto in full form sounds as though each word has been inspected.

### What they put on over that

The house dresses the way it reads, an outsider's formal law laid over a native people's practical toughness. The whole pattern is in [[Factions, Bloodlines & Institutions/Houses and Bloodlines/The Archaic Bloodlines/The Moto Bloodline/The Moto Bloodline — Material Culture|Material Culture]], and Mistress Hale of Thornhaven will sue your estate if you get it wrong. In short: a fitted war-harness under an open robe, magistrate and combatant at once; red enamel over blackened steel for the crown line, each other line in its own metal; a beast-face boss at the belt that marks the line before any name is spoken, moved to the shoulder on campaign; a knotwork belt in native Kharven interlace, the one thing on a Moto that is not Moto, earned or married in; and a fur mantle, ranked by wholeness of pelt, cut down to a collar in the field.

**Everyone looks, and everyone knows what it says.** The Tenrai dress as though a question has been raised about their claim, and it has. A Byakuya's white shows every mark, because she means not to be hit. Enamel is slow and costly and the full red is a claim on the throne, which is why a cadet who wore it to a feast lost a leg over it. A landless line carries its wealth on its body, so an Amagiri lord's earring is gold waystones and he can name every pass. A **poor cousin** wears the house's oldest trick: the plain field coat, replaced twice, iron-thread seams doing real work, one thumb's length of line-color inside the collar, and a boss cut from whatever a dead relation left. If their belt is old and tooled, ask whose mother it came from, and watch how they answer. And the Kurenai have one obscenity: a crimson eye turned outward, on a belt, at the hip. ## Lineage Traits

### [Trait] The Too-Sharp Outline — The Blood on the Skin

The body remembers an older law than ordinary blood, and strangers notice before they know what they are noticing.

- Birth-sheen, ring-lines and bearing show even in the unwoken and the unread.
- Clansmen's children are taught never to meet a Moto's eye before they are taught the forearm grip, and a clansman addressing the Hall looks at the chest, the hands or the floor.
- A trained room reads the Moto in it first, which is a problem for a family that would often prefer to be furniture.

*A Moto can buy a commoner's coat. Nobody has yet sold them a commoner's outline.*

### [Trait] The Heart-Eye — What the Weight Is Doing

The common Shingan, set out above. It is the house's only universal gift, and it is enough.

- Reads load and fault in matter, bodies and formations, and in the better eyes in a working or an oath.
- Sharpens under strain, and each forced opening stays a little wider.
- Runs without being asked. The supper table is included.
- Gives Gnosis Perception and Analysis their easy climb.

*Most people look at a wall and see a wall. A Moto sees the only part of it that matters, and then has to go on being polite.*

### [Trait] The Handed-Down Grip — Somebody Else's Hand on the Thread

A child's Attraction Layer comes with generations of practice laid into it, ready to close on one function.

- Thread Class may open before Glory, and the blood gives no help climbing it. Decades of use do the climbing.
- On their own Work, a pull lands far more often than a stranger's, at the same price.
- One Work is truly theirs, and which one is usually known by the time they are twelve, to the rejoicing or embarrassment of a whole household.
- The cap never lifts, and a Moto hand is not one bit lighter than yours.

*A Moto child starts with somewhere to put her hand. She is still lifting the same thing you are.*

### [Trait] The Ledger Reflex — Everything Entered

This one is no gift of blood. It is years of morning drill worn into the nerves, so deep that the house's physicians count it among the inheritance.

- Threat, cost, weakness and probable turn are ranked before a conscious thought fires.
- A Moto's first look at a room is a list, in order, and they would be able to recite it to you a decade later.
- In a fight it reads as two exchanges of advance. A stranger who does something truly unlikely gets one free blow while the list is corrected.
- It marks appetite as readily as malice, and a person who enters everyone at their table as a debt stops sitting at tables.

*A Moto will forgive you most things. They will never stop knowing the exact amount.*

### [Trait] Delegated Sight — A Chair Above the Stations

What the Moto see of Fate they see from above, on a leave nobody living has read.

- Moto read a blessing's line, an oath's terms and a thread's lean where no glyph or Warrant can name what they are doing.
- A Moto speaker of the Counted Speech opens on a sound the record writes down and leaves the value blank.
- It makes no one a better cutter. A Moto who speaks or cuts a sentence spends the soul's own share like anyone else, and glyphs and Seals never pull.
- A Fourth Seal, which ends a line instead of waiting for it, needs a grip on Fate or a Warrant that carries one. A Moto with a Work can sign it, and will mention this.

*Everyone else in the trade reads the law. The Moto have been told they are reading its handwriting.*

### [Trait] The Covenant Scar — Law in the Flesh

*Keiyaku-kon.* A dormant law-mark placed into flesh to store **inheritance triggers, military vows, marital obligations, or punitive backlash**, taught without comment in every line.

- It runs on recognition: the scarred person stops being lawfully recognized outside the terms, and no hunter need ever be sent.
- The Kurenai cut one into every headsman at nineteen, to hold the vow of no wrath. One such scar has since inverted, and glows black-violet through a coat.
- It binds at a Bind's price, as a condition set on the soul's own share, and it can be read by anyone with the eye. A removal is a job for the two-hand office.

*At the white shelf this was how a civilization ran. Today it is mostly marriages. The house sees no contradiction.*

## Doctrine and Arts

### Training and Cost

**Conceptual discipline** · The Moto must understand what a Work *means* inside existence.

**Operational discipline** · They must learn what that Work *does* when expressed through body, weapon, eye, or rite.

**Judicial discipline** · They must know **when a function is rightful, excessive, or forbidden.**
> **Every Work carries a corruption vector.**
>
> Ruins can become annihilation. Purity, fanatic sterilization. Spirit, soul-hoarding. Temporality, recursion. Knowledge, devouring certainty. Balance, suffocating stasis. **And Totality, tyrannical subsumation.**

The style that comes of this looks nothing like the showmen in the bracket towns. The founder's hatred of decorative combat is the house's whole aesthetic: a finishing move is the final legal consequence of everything the target has already failed to survive. A Moto bout starts with a long look, goes on to one or two very small cuts, and finishes with the other fighter discovering he has been losing for some time. The insult for a swordsman who fights any other way is *poet*. The children of the lines are nonetheless children, and every Stage bracket in the north has one in gold or silver bawling the name of a technique at the top of their lungs, and the elders deplore it, and pay for the ribbons.

Reinforcement (the visible coat of a practitioner's own aura) is a shared art everywhere, and the Moto have their own words and their own quarrel about it: *matoi*, the wearing, for the coat; *atsu*, the press, for Pressure; *kehai*, the sign of someone there, for sensing. ## The Seven Great Lines

The lines run very much like rival clubs, and no Moto will thank you for saying so. Each has its colors and its songs, its animals and its old grudges, and a fighting style a bracket crowd can pick out at fifty paces; in any Stage bracket town you will find the silver-ribbon Shirogane boys and the mourning-black Kurenai boys singing at each other across a street, and both lines deploring it, and both lines paying for the ribbons.

| Line | Shingan | Nature | Favored Works |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Byakuya** | Meigan | Cold revelation, hidden seams, exposed falsehood, clean structural breach. *The Owl, The White Fox, The Heron, The Stoat* | Knowledge, then Purity |
| **Shirogane**  | Meigan | A harder cadet revelation branch: immediate breach, anti-fortification work, **merciless structural punishment.** *The Lynx, The Snow Fox, The Hawk Owl, The Wolfcat* | Knowledge sharpened by Ruins |
| **Akagane** | Reigan | Soul-reading, curse anatomy, essence surgery, **intimate violence**, inner-system control. *The Fox, The Red Wolf, The Viper, The Red Hawk* | Spirit, then Knowledge |
| **Kurenai** | Reigan | Punitive soul-law, curse pressure, internal collapse, **execution through contradiction.** *The Crow, The Night Viper, The Jackal, The Black Fox* | Spirit under the shadow of Ruins |
| **Tenrai** | Tengan | War-command, elegant timing, convergence reading, **battlefield inevitability.** *The Hawk, The Falcon, The Stag, The Swift Fox* | Temporality and Balance, reaching into Knowledge |
| **Amagiri**  | Tengan | Route-reading, skirmish war, pursuit-breaking, escape law, **mist-like asymmetry.** *The Moth, The Kite, The Hare, The Grey Hound* | Temporality supported by Balance |
| **Kōkan** | Kamigan | Crown authority, unified judgment, triune command, **the ranking of outcomes by sovereign law.** *The Lion, The Dragon, The Raven King, The Crowned Serpent* | **Totality above all**, with natural access to the lower Works beneath it |

They sit in the Hall of Verdicts, a long black room on the crown of the rock, each in its own chair and its own metal, with the red chair a little forward of the arc. In law only the Kōkan hold a seat. In fact every line has its castle on the Castle Stair with loud opinions about the one above it, and each has a place it calls home by some means the law has not looked at closely.

**Byakuya, the white line.** The white house in the Shiraki hills, the Owl over the door, and a prayer room where every child swears the oath at nine with a grandmother's hand on her head: *purity before power, peace before the blade, and the red line before the white, until the end of the red line.* Their oldest office is the Kōkan's own guard, the **Samurai of Fate** (*Unmei no Samurai*), half a step ahead of the king and to his left. A Byakuya draw is taught behind a closed door and never shown, and they tie a white knot round the sword when they enter a house to keep the peace. Their daughters marry north and keep the oath over a lamp for forty years in someone else's castle. Their one rule: it has nothing to say when the king is the knife.

**Akagane, the copper line.** The Kurogane valley, the granaries, the Blood and Heart Arts that keep a heart and can stop one. *The Akagane never swore to the red. They only ever obeyed it.* The copper book holds every name they have buried, and every lord reads it through on taking the chair. They feed armies and sign for every sack, the best provisioning books in the north are theirs, and a provisioner who knows what the columns will do with the bread is the house's particular kind of monster. Copper comes out of a quench softer, as the Kurenai have reason to know.

**Kurenai, the black line.** The executioner's office, attached to the Hall. *Kurenai* means crimson and they wear it hidden, which is the line's one joke. The verdict kills and the headsman is its hand, and a hand that strikes in wrath has broken the law it serves, so they carve the vow of no wrath into every headsman at nineteen and walk with a wax-stopped bell, without a sound, until the sentence is done. They have carried out the Hall's verdicts for a thousand years, whatever the verdicts were. Their register struck one name and wrote the word *Sin* in its place. Thirteen went after him and none came home.

**Tenrai, the gold line.** The keep on the east of the rock with gold leaf on it, twelve hundred house-guard in gold and crimson, and the most polished manners in the north. War-command, go boards, and a line that sent its best to fill rafters for fifty years. The Tenrai took the Seat from the Kōkan three hundred years ago, and their ambition for the red chair is what the house politely calls its founding wound. The north's joke is that they are the only family that can lose a war for forty years and bill you for it. Anyone who has lived near them wears a collar.

**Amagiri, the indigo line.** Roads. Every pass, ford and herders' shoulder, written down in a line's ledgers for centuries and more of it unwritten; waystones cut with the Moth; masks left at crossroads shrines for whatever walks the roads at night. They thin the light around a party on the move, so a sentry looking straight at the track sees weather. Nobody takes them seriously in a hall, which they have been cultivating since before the crossing. A Moto host that gets out of trouble got out on an Amagiri road. So did the people running from it.

### House Within: The Kōkan Line

*The red line · the line of the red chair · the crown-line of the seven*

| | |
|---|---|
| **Eye** | The Kamigan, as an event; the Shingan with gold in it, otherwise |
| **Work** | Totality above all, with natural access to the lower Works. Most hold Ruin or Balance and are glad to |
| **Chair** | The red, plain lacquer over dark wood, with the mark worked into its back in fine black lines |
| **Seat** | The rock, by law; a long gray house deep-eaved and dark-beamed on the crown, used for three centuries to store Tenrai chests |
| **Look** | Red enamel over near-black steel, lion or dragon boss, gold or violet at the throat |
| **Word** | Their mark: a long down-stroke, a hook, a crossing and a small closed loop, like a knot in a rope, or a little like a closed eye |

One of the last Kōkan king's captains once asked him, at a fire on campaign, when he meant to sit the chair and wear the color. He gave the fire some thought and answered, "When I've made it worth wearing." He died in black, and at the Seat the servants swept round that chair for forty years, knowing exactly how wide to sweep.

Every other line reads what is there. **The Kōkan rank it.** The Crown Eye takes what the other three see, revelation, soul-law and fate-pressure, as subordinate offices awaiting coordination, and returns a list with the worst item first. In most of the line this shows as a hairline of gold running out through the iris, the first seam of the Kamigan, and it is why any Tenrai captain who sees it in a stranger's eye knows at once what she is looking at: any Moto brat can see where a wall wants to fall, and the gold in it is Kōkan. The whole eye appears less often in each generation, then rarely, then as an event. The founder's only remark on it is that those who wield it well develop an unfortunate resemblance to him.

**The Kōkan's two inheritances are a judgment and a page with no ink on it.**

**The Gold at the Edges.** The eye reads fault and load, and ranks them, and forced past its depth the gold spreads toward the rim and does not go back. Its great weakness is on record, and it is a good one: **Kōkan judgment requires clear fault to function.** It works on clean cases. The people who want a Kōkan gone come by the dirty ones, through channels the instrument has no vector against, and the line has been dying of that for a long time.

**The Unwritten Mark.** The line wrote its name in a glyph the Continuum has never been able to classify, in a First Epoch phonology no clerk can read, and held the register as family property. So a working that finds a person by the house written over them finds nothing on an unregistered Kōkan: tokens go dead in the hand, fate-rings slide off. It cuts the other way too. No seal will bind under the name until the mark is set on it by hand, and a register that cannot read you cannot protect you, as the Recension (a sincere and competent revision of the Church's archive) demonstrated to the Kōkan remnant on the coast, with bonded contracts. Plain eyes and a blade see them as well as anyone. Some hands in the line draw the mark true and some never can, whatever they are taught, and the house has no theory.

**Totality, and why they are watched.** The Kōkan are the line closest to the crown-function, too often resembling, in the founder's words, judges born without the useful softening of uncertainty. *He did not consider this a flaw.* Everyone else does. Totality's corruption is the hunger to gather all distinctions into one captive frame, and from inside Totality there is no difference between integration and subsumption, which was the six lines' whole charge against the Stay and was also, a very long time later, what the cellars at Halcrest were. Twenty years before the end of the long war the last king's own line told him no, in the hall, to his face, and he had them killed, and their children and their cousins. Four years later he went down into the cellars with a lamp after the last of his blood, with the gold coming up in their eyes as he cornered them. The Kōkan arts went into the ground with them, and the Kurenai closed the book. *The butcher of Hiroe* is the kinder of the things he was called, and when he fell at Shimane in the mud, the Moto began to eat themselves the same day. Naiser Yukari, who refused his riders twice, has called him a monster and, in his way, a truly great man. The line's own word for its worst temptation is **Singling**, and they say it of themselves.

*Later hand:* a boy came home from the ice with nobody, took the Seat in three days and broke only what was already dead, and sat the red chair his father never sat. In the Hall that spring the seven lines and six Banner Houses swore one by one: the entry reads *seven lines, and one blood, under one crown*, and the Hallenfeld counting-house has asked that it be noted who made it. They call him the Tokenless, and the ballads get the numbers wrong in at least four keys. Of the corridor, where the north says a king and two of his sons died in a night, this hand has no death of his to record. His son **Sodoku**, the Arctic Lion, came back from eleven years at the polar rift with Kamigan and frost together, a Titan's cold worked through the eye so that five ring-lines stand in it, and his father's beads on a cord, and he said the family record out loud at a kitchen table in a house with a scullery, which no Moto had done in living memory. *I know exactly what my family is*, he said. It is the most frightening thing any of them has ever said.

### House Within: The Shirogane

*The silver line · the rival branch · the Hard Second*

| | |
|---|---|
| **Eye** | The Meigan, sharpened and impatient |
| **Work** | Knowledge sharpened by Ruins, with a Purity in the family that nobody praises |

The Shirogane have a word for the great gate at Ōmagari, which held off a Tenrai host through two winters: *ornamental*. They were asked in on the third spring. A woman of the line, nobody's idea of important, walked its length at noon with two cold lunches and a clerk. She laid one silver hairpin against the base of the fourth buttress, and said in a dry little voice, "**SPLINTER ORDINANCE.**" Every small crack in that wall, each of which had been waiting its turn since the wall was built, was told when its turn was. They came along one at a time, in order, from north to south, over about a quarter of an hour, with a noise the Ōmagari people still tell their children about, and the gate stayed up, ornamental, with nothing on either side of it. She had the clerk enter the hour. She had a slight nosebleed, and finished her lunch.

**A thousand years ago the silver line was told it would always stand second, and believed it, and could not let it go.** That is the house in a breath, and everything else about them follows. The Byakuya read what is hidden so that it may be known. A Shirogane reads what is hidden so that it may be punished, and they read it faster, since a second who hesitates does not get to be first. They are the finest siege-breakers in the world, the kind of people who get sent for. Their chains have silver in the links, which drinks at a practitioner through the skin. They have the rival's claim, the rival's pride and the rival's ledger, and they are the one house in the Hall that the Tenrai have ever courted instead of bought, with the silver seal set on the left of a Tenrai letter in the place of precedence.

**What they hold.** The Hard Second is no Trait the coil can read, and every physician who has treated one knows it: an old wound carried from mother to daughter, with the same note under it in every generation, and a Temporality reader can follow it back along one thread the whole thousand years. The Shirogane's coin-bright rings lie in gray eyes, and most of their daughters look at a thing a moment longer than is polite and then tell you where it will come apart. Purity's Obsession face, which purges whatever does not fit the shape, is known across the north as the Shirogane way, and it is not meant kindly.

**The fostering.** For three hundred years the Shirogane and the Byakuya have sent each other a child, so that each house always has someone inside the other's walls. It is the only arrangement of its kind in the Hall, and it has produced more treason, more tenderness and more excellent silver embroidery than any other institution in the north.

**How to beat them.** They need a structure to punish. Soft ground, a camp with no wall, a host that does not care whether you broke its gate, and an enemy with nothing built gives them very little. A Shirogane has been known to spend an extraordinary amount of energy on a thing whose only offense is that it stands higher. Offer them first place, and watch the whole family forget what they were doing.

*Later hand:* what the silver house did in the last winter of the war, and what was done about it at the first bell in the Hall square, the songs have in four keys. The silver chair has a new occupant, a small careful woman who was raised in the white house and sends someone away in the night in her own voice, over and over. The boy in the red chair chose her for it because she had every reason to hate him. The whispering has not stopped, and this office does not expect it to.

## Culture

**Language.** The old register, full forms, no contractions, and honorifics where the old tongue is used, Japonic in its bones. Full names carry the line in the middle: Miku Tenrai Moto, Artemis Amagiri Moto. The register verb is *entered*. A person is *read*. The dead are *behind*, and behind is a direction, and a direction can be faced. Everyone in the house is literate, in the family's own hand and across the fourteen hands of the Archons, and a document in the wrong hand is void rather than incorrect. The Concord hand is read as a foreign script, accurately and without love.

**Greeting.** None on record. From senior to junior, a hand on the head and nothing said, and the nothing is enough. A guest eats the host's food for three days before business.

### Rites

- **The Reading.** The house's central sacrament, stripped down from what it was at the shelf: a thing read correctly has been given its Work, and a thing that has been given its Work has been praised. A newborn's irises are looked at by the oldest woman in the house, and she says one word, and it is written down and not repeated.
- **The Oath at Nine.** With a grandmother's hand on the head, at the hour of the evening bell, over a lamp in the Byakuya manner and over whatever is at hand in the others. Everything a Moto will later be held to is sworn at an age when the child is mainly thinking about supper. Keeping it counts as practice on the Fate road from that evening.
- **The Bow.** Fourteen inclinations at the hinge of the day, standing, before the bread. All fourteen, always. Cloth moves fourteen times and no voice is heard. A household that moves cuts its fourteen scratches in the new doorpost before it unpacks, and a guest is walked through the scratched door before business, on the folk belief that a man carrying a lie cannot pass it.
- **The Reading at Ice-out.** The winter's dead go into the ground together when it opens, and their names are read in the order they died. To read them alphabetically would be a desecration nobody could articulate. The Queen's Saishiki, the liturgy that made an oath structural instead of verbal, went with her, so what reads the names now is a man with a list doing his best.
- **The Revocation.** The terminal form, full register, correct contours, before a witness: *"I revoke the name Moto from you."* A man revoked is entered *elar-ama*, held by nobody, and the name cannot be given back by anybody. Retainers say the revoked die within the year.
- **The Guard Settling.** When a sentence is complete, the guard settles into the scabbard mouth with one small final sound, and in Kharven that sound means the verdict is complete. A Moto lord who wants a room silent need only touch his hilt.

**Who they marry.** Up and north and with an eye on the register. Lines marry lines (a Kurenai headsman once took an Akagane soul-surgeon, and the Hall's verdict on her is the worst page in either family's books). They marry the Yuno when they can, who keep the living interval and have married into the court. Byakuya daughters marry north to seal peaces nobody remembers, and a Moto's knotwork belt is always a specific belt tied to a specific alliance, oath or northern-born mother. Blood always mixes, as a Kōkan has observed to a furious son. The children get ring-lines anyway.

**Who they feud with.** The **Yukari**, the oldest quarrel in mortal history: the Moto see the threads as property, and the Yukari see them as reality, and a chancery fears a witness it cannot swear in. The **Zettari**, by forty years of war and the grudge of one king. The current settlement holds, so far, in the form of an oath that no Moto marches on Zettari land. The **Holy Sea**, as a tenant is feuded with by a landlord who has no way to collect. And each other, always, with enormous courtesy. The Tenrai and Kōkan feud is the kingdom's founding wound, the Shirogane's is a thousand years old, and the Akagane have a book.

**Who they owe.** Hallenfeld holds the Seat's paper, and never once lent the Kōkan a coin. Thornwall kept the corridors open for two hundred years in case. The Banner Houses swore their oaths to a person, and those oaths do not outlast the reign, so the house is forever one bad winter from having to earn them. And every Moto alive owes the Kōkan the window across the water, which is in the registers of all six northern lines, and none of them have ever taught it.

**What the commoners and servants get.** A retainer family gets a ration on a true scale, inspected in public, a name in a register they cannot read, and the certainty of being entered correctly. They read little, and the house's hand not at all. They bring a lord his beads before his water and know how wide to sweep. They get no vote and no explanation, and they have the cold comfort of a master who cannot be bribed about what they are. Northern clans have long since concluded that a Moto is weather, and arrange their lives accordingly, with the door shut when the jokes start. The house's whole faith lives, unacknowledged, in their kitchens: there are households in the Greenhollow who perform all fourteen labors daily and could not name an Archon.

The house's own small power is a trade. The **hakari-ban**, scale-watch, is a commoner's calling on Moto ground: a keeper who has weighed the ration every day for thirty years and whose beam will not settle on a false weight, by nothing more exalted than a Common skill grown past where it should have stopped. Her balance is why you trust the bread. She charges a day's ration a month, she can refuse, and in the Thin Weeks of 697 the scale-watch of the Castle Stair put down their beams in a body over a matter of back pay, and four cadet castles found out in nine days how much of their authority had been resting on a woman with a brass pan.

## Limitations and Soul-Costs

### [Curse] Devouring Certainty — The Instrument That Has Never Been Wrong

The corruption of Knowledge, listed in the house's own training register beside the other six, written by people who watched it happen and declined to name the century.

- A Moto's reading of what is there is usually correct, so they stop noticing where the reading stops.
- The eye shows load and fault, and the person supplies the meaning, and has been drilled since seven not to notice that they supplied it.
- *A man told he is unfree by a tyrant has a grievance. A man told he is unfree by an instrument that has never once been wrong has a diagnosis.*
- From inside, being right and being cruel feel exactly alike.

### [Curse] The Flat — What a Verdict Takes

- Eye bleed, a sternum ache, and twenty to thirty seconds of nothing at all after every hard verdict. Everyone who serves a Moto has learned the timing.
- Each forced opening widens the eye a thread and it stays wider, so the flat grows longer through a life, and in the old it is sometimes hard to tell when it has ended.
- The Ledger does not stop. People become entries, and the family becomes a very well-kept list of people.
- The founder recorded a mother who let her firstborn's ghost go with a palm on his brow, and approved. He did not record how she was at supper.

### [Curse] The Corruption Vector — Every Work Curdles

Every Work has an Attraction face and an Obsession face, and the Work presses the soul into a useful shape, so a long life in one grip tends to become a long life in its worse face. A breaker ends what was rotten and then what was near the rot, and then does not stop. A keeper will not let the dead leave, which is what the house means by *clogged*. A gatherer gathers. The bearer does not notice. The ground cracks around what they aim at and not only under it, and that is the tell, and by then the people who could tell them are in the cellar.

### [Curse] The Noticed Hand — Somebody Else's Thread

- A pull reaches up into the laws themselves, and any Throne with someone on it may look down. See Yorimasa, and his grocers.
- Removing a blessing tells the giver who took the pen, and the larger the giver, the heavier it runs.
- There is no appeal, since the house's faith forbids asking. A Moto may never petition the thing that has noticed them.
- *Nothing becomes more monstrous, more quickly, than a righteous fool with correct metaphysics.* A very old family has had a long time to test the sentence.

## How They Stand Against the Three Paths

**A Body enemy** is the one who kills them. A Body fighter gets there, offers no seam worth reading if well kept, and meets an ordinary body at the end of it. No Moto wants a straight exchange with a Zettari or anyone like one. Their answer is to have read the road, the ground, the straps and the man's knee three days ago, and to be somewhere else, or to have already cut it. High enough up a Body sovereign needs no road, and a Moto's armor grows too heavy to lift in front of her family.

**A Spirit enemy** reaches the hand on the thread. A pull shows on the Core before it lands, a wounded soul loses its grip, and a soul-hit goes past the coat as if it were not there. Their counter is that Spirit survives by being right, and being wrong is what the Ledger is for. One bad read and one spoiled stroke, and the Moto has entered it. A Yukari with a Regard is their nightmare: a witness who knows them whole, and cannot be sworn in.

**A Fate enemy** leans the other way, reads the weights and moves the same straps, usually while bleeding and usually in the baggage train. Such people are rare, and a Moto hunts them first, ahead of any champion. Most of the world's Fate walkers have Range, Aura Pressure and a kept word and no grip at all, and for those the house has a polite condescension and a very large body of case law. The one thing that gets a Moto's real attention is another bloodline's edit type, or a thread that turns out to be somebody's.

## Final Definition

> The Moto are a dynasty of **lawful perception and lawful function.** The Shingan lets them see the stratum of reality that matters. **Hataraki lets them work upon that stratum as fate's own labor.**

Their seven lines preserve different emphases of that inheritance, but all remain recognizably Moto: beautiful in an unnerving way, spiritually heavy, politically dangerous, and built around the principle that **reality is to be witnessed and then judged, assigned, and made to perform.**

They are the line Bara left behind him, handed seven grips on the hardest thing in the world by an ancestor who did not consider this a kindness. The grip lowers the failure and never the price. Their Path is whichever one they have lived, and it is usually the plain road of someone who reads a thing and then hits it. They read the world for a very long time, and it never once told them they were wrong, and the great question of the house is what a family owes to everyone it has been correct about. Some of them are starting to ask. The rest have entered that.

*"See before you act. Name before you sever. Measure before you reconcile. Release before you cling. End only what has earned its ending. Preserve only what remains fit to endure."*

And in the founder's hand, underneath, where only a child of the house will read it:

*"The Work is not that you saw it. The Work is that you returned."*

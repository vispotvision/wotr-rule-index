When the silk factor Dorrit Vance climbed to the Cedar Enclave in the spring of 548 to collect a debt, he brought two mules, three hired swords and a writ with twelve seals on it in twelve colors of wax, and a Yukari cousin in a black coat silvered at the cuffs handed him a cup of tea before he had got his hat off. It smelled of small white flowers. It was as bitter as a lawsuit. Vance set it down untasted, and since nobody in that house speaks a word of business until the guest has tasted the tea, for nine days nobody spoke a word of business to him. They were charming about it. They fed him trout and honey cakes and asked after his mother, while a hundred crows glossy as wet ink sat along the rail and watched him eat, and his hired swords drifted down to the village dance and did not come back. On the tenth morning he drank it, made the face everyone makes, and opened his writ. The cousin glanced once at the air beside his head, where the crimson had come up round the pupil of her eye, and told him kindly that the debt was his own brother's forgery, that the eighth seal had been cut in Thrum by a left-handed man, and that the brother had been sleeping with his wife since the autumn before last. Vance sat down on the gravel. The whole of it went into the Yukari record, with the date and the tea. He paid to have the cup, and it is on a shelf in his great-grandchildren's house, and none of them will drink from it.

That is the family at its most polite. Now the rest.

In the autumn of 633 Bartlemy Goss, Lord of the Ley Marches, a huge merry man in a gilded harness with a laugh you could hear across a river, came up the southern road to the Cedar Enclave with seven thousand Marrick spears on a season's contract and four hundred gray horses he had not finished paying the Yan for. He had been meaning to do it for nineteen years. The Enclave sent a cousin down to the road with a tray, and Lord Goss, in front of his whole column, took the cup off it and threw it at her head, and the column roared. He then read out his terms from the saddle, and they were very good terms, and when he had finished nobody on the Yukari side said anything at all. A woman got down from the sutler wagons at the back of his own army and came walking up the line past his bannermen. Everybody knew her. They called her Honey Nan, she was stout and fifty and had sold honey cakes in the Goss camps for eleven years, every campaign, and under the windows of the Goss hall between campaigns, and half the sergeants owed her money. She took off her apron as she came and folded it over her arm, and under it was a coat of crow black with the silver on its sleeve running all the way to the elbow, and it was about then that a bannerman of the Long Fields, a boy with nothing else to look at, noticed what his steward had been arranging for nine years that nobody should notice. It was noon. The banner threw a shadow. The horse threw a shadow. The road under Lord Goss's stirrup was clean.

"You had a lump on your liver the size of a plum, my lord, the winter your boy was nine," said Yukari Nagi, in the voice she used for selling cakes. "You'd have been dead by spring, and your brothers would have burned three valleys over the child. So I've kept you. It's been no trouble, you've good cooks. Your boy's twenty now and your brothers are dead, and you've come up here to my house." She looked at him a moment longer with real regret, for she knew him better than anyone ever had. Then a crow came down on the rail of the wagon, and she turned her head to look at it.

Eleven years of liver arrived in the time it takes to turn your head. The yellow came up out of his collar and filled his face as if it were being poured in, the whites of his eyes and then his teeth. His belly rose against the gilt until the buckles went, and he leaned out of the saddle with an expression of great surprise and brought up what looked like a bucket of black tar over his horse's neck, and more after it. While he was doing that his shadow came back, short and black, the first in eleven years, and he fell on it. The Marrick captain looked at the body, looked at the man who kept the pay chest, looked at the sun, and grounded spears. Seven thousand men walked home by supper. The Yan came for their horses. Yukari Nagi finished the tea that had been brought down for him, since it was poured, and went home, and to the end of her days she could tell you what Bartlemy Goss had dreamed on any night you cared to name, and she kept his day every year, and at the Tellings she told the whole thing against herself with the price left in. The lords of the South have walked their own courtyards at noon ever since, in front of witnesses. They call it taking the air.

The Yukari are the family of Ashura, the Breath Between Worlds, third and last of the Three Epochs. Yukari was her own name. When the Third Epoch came and the Soul Plane woke, Ashura walked the waking world for a time as a woman: she kept a house, sat at a table, laughed at it, and bore children, and the name she answered to at that table was Ashura Yukari. Every Yukari since is her kin, by blood or by the Telling, and the house is exactly as old as the Third Epoch, to the morning.

**Names in other mouths** · the Crow House, in the common tongue of the south · Domus Vinculi, the House of the Bond, in Accord filings · the Breath's kin, in the old Moto registers, which never write the word Yukari if they can avoid it

### What they want and what they hold

The Yukari say they want nothing more than the spiritual balance of the world kept, and the awful thing is that they mean it. They have sworn off more land, sworn off banners and sworn their knowledge out of every war. They will not rule you. They will only know you, for good, and know what you promised, and what it was tied to, and write it down.

Three things they hold that everybody needs. **The record:** in a quarter where every house has a price and a grievance, the only account of who did what that nobody suspects of being for sale. By that record, of the seven houses that turned a coin on the smuggler Tam Pennyroyal's funeral in 661, six made money and the seventh wrote down who did, and a crow rode the last twenty miles on the lid. **The gate posts:** the wards of half a frontier, cut by people who can hear stone. And **the spirit side of the world.** The war broke the Soul Plane, the dead have lost their road, and the only reason there is a road left to lose is that the father of this house went into a seam in the Epoch's own body and held it shut. Everything still alive over there is living on time a Yukari bought. Nobody can do without any of the three, and nobody at the table has ever enjoyed saying so.

## The Name

In the old Japonic of the house, yukari is the word for a tie between lives: kinship, affinity, the bond that makes two people related. The same root runs through Ketsuen, the old books' name for the thread-bound country the house holds. Her true name in the Celestial Proto-Tongue is Ashuraya, and the Empyreal Veil keeps it. Yukari was the name she chose for the world of tables and doorways, because it said what she had come to do. The Breath is what lets one life be related to another, and she named her family after the relation itself.

That is why the name is chosen and never simply inherited. A family named for the bond lets the bond decide who belongs. Ashura took foundlings into her house and gave them the name on the same evening she gave it to her own children, and the house has done the same ever since. *The tie makes kin. The blood only follows it.* It is the oldest thing the Yukari say about themselves, and they say it at every Telling, before the stone.

## Origin of the House

### The First Household

The Ketsumyōgan is Ashura's own sight, carried in a mortal body. The Breath knows the Soul Plane from inside, as recognition, and her children were born knowing a little of what she knew: the threads of all three planes, seen at once. Nobody taught the first Yukari to see. Their mother could, and they had her eyes.

The price came in the same cradle. A mortal Soul Crystal was never built to carry an Epoch's sight, and the triplanar load it takes in burns the lattice that holds it. The house calls that Threadburn, and every bearer pays it. The first children paid more. They were born in the first mornings of the Third Epoch, while the Soul Plane was still waking, and their eyes saw a plane half asleep and went dark under the weight of it, and came back, and went dark again. Those blindness cycles ended as the Plane finished waking and Temperance progression became possible. Threadburn never ended. The house learned to carry it.

In those first generations the eye was raw and undivided: every thread, every plane, every connection, all at once. Over the long run of the Third Epoch it settled in the blood into its eight types, Shisen and Teishi, Danshi and Orihi and the rest, one to each eye. The undivided eye still turns up once in an age, and the house treats that child the way it treated the first ones.

> *They asked who had taught them to see, and she said that nobody had; they had her eyes. They asked why the seeing hurt, and she sat down with them and told them, all of it, until the lamp burned out. Then she lit it again and told the next one.*
>
> the Empyreal Veil's echo-archive

That night at the hearth is the first Telling. Every Telling since is the same act repeated: a Yukari of age is sat down and given the whole account of what they carry, what it costs and what it means, because the mother of the house would not let one of her children pay for a sight they had not been told about. The Yukari do not worship Ashura and never have. They call her by the word a family uses, and they set a place for her at the long table on the first night of every year, and nobody sits in it.

The house also holds that she looked at a battle once, from a kitchen step, and that it stopped until she had finished what she was doing. It tells this as a story about her, and about how far every one of her descendants has fallen short of the kitchen step.

### Senri Yukari

**Son of Ashura · Father of the House · He Who Went Into the Seam**

Senri was the last child Ashura Yukari bore, late in the long run of the Third Epoch, and her blood ran fuller in him than in any Yukari since the first mornings. He was born with the undivided eye. It took his left eye before he was grown, and the left stayed dark for the rest of his life, a still black pupil ringed in old crimson; the right saw for both, and saw deeper than any eye in the house. People who met him remembered the dark eye first and the patience second.

He was a quiet man and a plain-spoken one. He said true things in the fewest words they would go into and then said nothing at all, which the house learned to read as the rest of the sentence. When the Moto offered to take his sight into the Loom's administration, to make it a lawful subset of their own judgment and him an officer of it, he refused them in one sentence and never discussed it again. The Moto remember the refusal. The Yukari remember the sentence.

#### Senri's stone

He cut it himself. It is a single slab of the valley's limestone taller than a man, gray with a warmth of honey where the light catches it, dressed flat on the face and left rough on the flanks so the quarry chisel's furrows still run down them. He took it out of the hill behind the gate over one whole winter and brought it down on a sledge, six men pulling and Senri walking beside it with one palm laid on the stone as if it were a horse that might bolt. There is one line on its face, set at the height of a seated man's eyes, so that whoever reads it properly has to sit down to do it:

> *Master the innards, and the outer world is yours to command.*

The stone stands at the back of the long hall of the Cedar Enclave, and every Telling is given in front of it. A child who asks for the name sits on the cold floor before Senri's stone, hears the whole account, and reads the line aloud. Children read it as a promise. Young men read it as a boast. The house says that anyone who has lived long enough reads it as a question.

#### His family

Senri married a daughter of House Lambert of Sallowgate, the counting house of the Holy Sea's land marches, and the match is why the Yukari record keeps its promises in figures as well as words. Their children are Naiser, the eldest; Naevra, a year younger; and Seradris, the youngest. He raised them in the cedar valleys through the worst of the forty-year war. When Naiser lost his first match in the yard at nine and the house's custom asked for a lock of his hair, it was Senri who knelt and cut it, very gently, and laid his hand on the boy's head after, and said nothing.

#### The seam

Four winters before the war ended, the Seikai forced a seam into the Soul Plane: into the place in Ashura where the dead are kept. Senri went into it. He left from the gate on a frost-white morning in his traveling coat with the pack already on his back, because a door like that will take only one, and he held it from the inside for four years. Everything that still lives on the spirit side of the world lives on the time he bought.

When Naiser broke the Seikai working at its root on the slope above the river, the seam stopped widening. Senri did not come back. The house has not given him a posthumous name, because a posthumous name is given to the dead, and the archive enters Senri as neither. The Yukari say it plainly to anyone who asks: *he went into his mother to hold her shut.*

Every soul has three layers, and every soul in this family has spent its life being told exactly how the three are doing. The soul is a Crystal: its outer Aether Shell meets the physical world, its inner Essence Core holds identity and works on spiritual reality, and its Attraction Layer is a soul's share of Fate. Aether (mana) comes in through the skin, and the Crystal refines it into Essence (spiritual energy), which is the soul's own stamped substance. Here is what the blood does to each.

## The Eye and the Look

There are three great instruments in the world, one to each Epoch's line. Bara's line tells the world its job, and Aegor's line asks what carries what happens next. Ashura's line looks at you. People outside the house confuse its two inheritances constantly, so this office will say it once, slowly. **The eye sees what everything is tied to. The look keeps what it sees.** The first is a matter of threads and can be turned into scissors at a terrible price. The second touches nothing at all, and that is a good deal worse.

### The Ketsumyōgan: she can see the strings, and she has scissors

Eleven hundred people paid the ferry toll to watch Yukari Hisame, in crow black with a folded paper fan and a head cold, let Ōtori Genba's finest cut finish on her neck with no consequence, and put the fan's guard-stick through his throat in the fraction of a second his body spent waiting for an answer. She spent most of what she had on that one heartbeat and did not fight again that year. That is the eye at its loudest. The Shingan is the eye of the throne, and this is the eye of the loom: a violet-black corona at the pupil's edge, crimson threads turning in it when it wakes, one type to the left eye and another to the right, permanent once they come. ### Null-Light: they look at you, and you stay exactly what they saw

Null-Light (無光, Mukō) is recognition with no light in it: a thing known completely by a light that never touches it. Two facts of looking make it. A thing can be found by the light that does not come back from it, which the Measurewrights call the null reading. And a thing watched closely and without a gap does not change, as the watched pot does not boil and Zeno's arrow never moves, while a thing watched unevenly changes faster than if it had been left alone. Put them together and you have the house's law: **to be known is to be held.** It gives no fire. No Regard has ever warmed a room or moved a pebble. Its [[The Disciplines/Schools and Bloodline Arts/Null-Light|full account]] is its own page, and every Yukari child hears the worst of it by name.

A bond with nothing wrong in it has nothing to finish, and so you find out which kind yours was, in front of people |
| **NA-YOBI!** The Calling-In | Sanshi and Ninshiki | Essence | The eye finds where a lost dead person is on all three planes, and the look says who they were. It is the house's real work since the split, done on hillsides at dusk, and it is why valley people let them in |
| **KIRI-NANORI!** The Cut That Names | Danshi and Ninshiki | Essence and Thread | She says in public what the tie is, and then cuts it. An heir stops being an heir, and every witness knows why. Two bills |

And there is the fragment. The eye carries a splinter of Null-Light, which is why a Yukari look leaves a room as it found it, and the splinter can be pushed. It will ride a good blade for one plane and one stroke, canceling a working it matches along the line of the cut. On the Kessen road Lord Naiser Yukari is said to have thrown it over a whole stretch of road at once, a man-shaped dark at the middle, with every cedar, carriage and crow around him gone shadowless, and the only shadow left in the valley lying under him and stretching toward the thing he meant to kill. The house has declined to discuss what that cost.

### The crows

They are not spies, and the crow-wards of Karasuyama will explain this to you at length while banding one. They are birds, and any Fair-day fool can learn that a crow knows a face, scolds it for years and teaches its chicks to scold it too. What the house has done, for as long as there has been a house, is feed them. Hundreds of thousands roost on the mountain above the Enclave and range the whole South, and each one of them is a small soul with a good memory that a Confessor can read whole, without touching it, from across a yard.

So a *karasu-yomi*, a crow-reader, sits on the Karasuyama steps of an evening with a bag of grain and takes in what the day's birds have seen. It is a lousy way to learn anything. A crow's account of the world is food, owls, and who threw what, and the Thread Standard, the Reach's law of what is tied to what, will not hear it without a second witness. But it does mean that the South has been watched from above, by birds with grudges, for some thousands of years, by a house that forgets nothing. It also means that a crow-reader who has kept ten thousand crows tilts her head when you speak and dreams about carrion, and has opinions about you based on an incident with a crust. Moto lords have taken to feeding them. It has been entered.

### What it costs, where it stops, who beats it

**The cost** is written out in the curses below, and it is the worst in the family's power to describe. What you hold you must keep looking at. What you have known you can never give back. The eye burns the lattice and flattens the heart.

**The limit.** The look needs a looker with the thing in mind and a thing to know. It starts nothing, mends nothing and moves no stone. The eye moves only what already has a way of happening. A Hold stands only until something else needs looking at, and a Domain past the moment of naming is out of its reach.

**Who beats it.** Reach her, since the body behind that eye is an ordinary body at its Stage. Arrange a second fight, so that she has to let go of the first. Bring more souls than she can hold, which is the old Vermilion answer. Stay in Thick Veil where the deeper planes go muted. Land the second blow after the heartbeat, because the world tolerates a Causal Disconnect for no longer than that. Be what you say you are. And be sound: a whole body has nothing for a Slip to finish, as Big Aldous Harrow proved at Sealhall in yellow boots with a surgeon's certificate on his chest, to the lasting benefit of the tooth-drawers' guild.

### What the House keeps

The Yukari keep [[Artifacts/The Immortal Cross|the Immortal Cross]], and they have kept it since the first morning: four arms around an empty heart, which close only for someone who understands everything each arm holds, Null-Light and the six teachings, the nature of the Seikai and the nature of the Zettari. Whoever closes it is held in true immortality. A hand with an Epoch in it does not seat a single joint, however much it understands. So the Yukari keep a test no Yukari born can pass, and they have kept it for the whole of the Third Epoch, watching for the one who can. That vigil is what the house means when it says the crow watches.

## Physical Appearance and Dress

**Stature** · 5'6" to 6'6", lean and long-limbed rather than broad, the body arranged to take in a room rather than to fill it. Narrow shoulders, long fingers, feet that sit flat and quiet.

**Eyes** · Dark irises from near-black to deep brown, with the faint violet-black corona of the Ketsumyōgan at the pupil's edge even at rest. Waking, the corona brightens to luminous crimson and thread-patterns turn slowly in the iris.

**Skin and thread-marks** · Complexions from dark earthen brown to pale cool ivory. Bearers grow thread-marks over the years: fine luminous lines under the skin of the forearms, the backs of the hands, the temples and the base of the skull, branching like roots seen from beneath the soil. At rest they are the color of old scars; under strain they glow silver going violet at the edges.

**Hair** · Most often jet black; blonde with notable frequency; deep crimson streaks in the lines that run closest to Ashura; now and then white from birth, which the house calls the Breath's mark. Active bearers carry Thread-Shimmer, a faint glow in the hair in low light.

**The Quiet Weight** · A Yukari voice settles instead of projecting, and a room it speaks in grows slightly more attentive, as though the walls had leaned in a fraction of an inch. People who sit across a table from one come away with the same feeling: of having been measured, thoroughly and without hostility, by someone who will remember the measurement for life.

### How they flex

Black and silver, silver threading through dark, and you can pick them out at fifty paces by how little they are doing. The field coat is crow black, cut long and plain and close in the arm so nothing swings, in good southern wool with a sheen on it like a wet feather, and the eye goes straight to the cuffs. **The silver at the cuff is a count.** One fine line of thread goes in for each soul the wearer holds or has held, and since a Yukari never stops knowing what she has known, nothing is ever unpicked. A young Confessor has a line or two and shows them off shamelessly. An old Shepherd's silver has climbed to the elbow. A Ferryman's sleeve runs silver to the shoulder, and the Long Fields sergeants who see one at a muster go quiet and start doing arithmetic. Forging it is a serious offense in the Reach, and fools do it anyway to get seated at dinner.

In the house it is Yukari gray, dark cloth with a thread of silver-violet down the seams, soft and heavy, and bare feet on the boards. The Thread Guard wear crow-black harness with no crest, since the wearer has already been told what you are. The bracket fighters of the house wear a crow-black sash and make a small polite bow, and it has become the most frightening sight on the Accord's sporting calendar. Ward-carvers go in chalk-white aprons with the mark of their station. The founder's mantle, high-collared, wide-sleeved and worked inside with sigils from the first household, is Legendary and worn by every head of the house, and no southern lord who has stood in front of it has failed to find something to look at on the floor.

Their weapons are a deliberate insult. A Yukari who means business has a paper fan, a kitchen knife or a very ordinary narrow blade in a scabbard with no fittings worth stealing, because the point of the exercise is to be holding something where you are about to be. Lord Naiser, a Grandmaster, goes about with a Rare longblade that assayers laugh at until they have seen it used. The house gives its steel no names until a thing is finished. What it does own in quantity is ink.

A poor cousin from the lower valleys wears homespun dyed in oak gall, black that has gone a little green, with the silver worked in one cuff by her mother and darned where it wore. She will read your entire inheritance before you have finished looking down on it.

## Lineage Traits

### [Trait] The Breath in the Blood — Ashura's Continuance

Ashura is the Soul Plane in some sense careful people decline to define, and a little of her runs in this blood and lengthens everything it touches.

- Spiritual decay reaches them slowly. Curses, soul-rot and the long wearing-down of a Core all arrive late.
- They stay young longer than most. The fuller the blood, the longer the line, and it has been thinning since the first mornings.
- Refined sensory filtering strips noise from what they take in, so they can read a room's real signal through a crowd, a glamour or a haze.
- It slows ruin and never stops it, and it does nothing whatever about being hit very fast.

*They will outlive most of the people who stood near them, and every one of those people will still be in there with them.*

### [Trait] The Long Memory — Retention as Birthright

The Core of this house does not put things down.

- Faces, debts, sums, the exact words of a promise and the hour it was made stay available for life, in bearers and in the merely Regarded alike.
- It works as a lean on Gnosis Retention that needs no training, and every clerk in the South would trade a hand for it.
- Whatever has been read by Mushō or the eye stays whole. A Yukari cannot be bribed into forgetting and cannot arrange it for herself.
- Below a tenth of reserve, where anyone's Core begins to fray, this works in reverse, and she may start getting your name wrong, or her own.

*Other families bury their dead. These ones have them all in the same room.*

### [Trait] Kin of the Threshold — The Dead Answer

Spirits and the dead know the family's smell, as a dog knows who feeds it.

- The Core starts to hear years early. A valley child who says there is a man in the pantry is taken seriously and asked what he wants.
- The dead reply more readily to a Yukari at every rung, and Echo Beasts, what was left of the dead with nothing to say who that was, hesitate when one looks at them.
- Since the split the drifting dead come to them unasked. Valley households put out a second bowl, and there are nights when the Enclave's gate-runners go down the road with lamps to bring in somebody's grandfather.
- This is a trade and not a safeguard. Whatever is drifting knows where you live too.

*Every house in the South has a door for the living. This one has been answering the other door since the first morning.*

### [Trait] The Quiet Weight — Presence Without Volume

Where another house's Pressure heats a room, a Yukari's turns the lamps down.

- A coat of Yukari aura dims the air around it, and the higher the Stage, the lower the room goes. A great one walks into a feast and every flame in the hall leans away and burns small.
- A room under it goes attentive. People find they have stopped chewing, and they hear themselves explaining.
- Lower Stages feel seen before they feel afraid, and the unwoken usually look away first, on a sound folk instinct.
- It shows nothing, roars nothing and intimidates no one who is too stupid to mind, and the Hot Tun brawlers have made a whole art of being too stupid to mind.

*The Moto make a room feel judged and the Zettari make it feel heavy. A Yukari makes it feel that somebody has opened a file.*

### [Trait] Thread-Marks and Shimmer — The Bill Worn on the Skin

The body keeps its own account of what the eye has cost.

- Thread-marks spread by years of use, so a stranger can read roughly how long a bearer has been looking and how hard.
- Under strain they light silver going violet, a tell that cannot be hidden or trained away, and good duelists watch the backs of the hands.
- Thread-Shimmer in low light marks an active bearer in any dark room, which makes the family poor at sneaking and very good at being noticed in the doorway at exactly the moment they meant to be.

*Everybody else's magic spends itself and goes. This family wears the receipts.*

### [Trait] The Taken Name — What the Telling Does

A name asked for after the whole account has been heard is a vow, and a vow is small Fate, spent from the soul's own share and pulling nothing down.

- It lays a binding on the Attraction Layer that every Reishi in the house can see and the Enclave's wards know, in blood kin and chosen kin alike.
- That is all it gives, and it is enough to get you into the long hall, the yard and the register. It gives no eye and no Regard.
- It holds. Kasane Byakuya took it in the valley at fourteen, before the stone, and the house has counted her its own ever since, whatever else she was.
- It can be refused with honor. Those who refuse are not pressed, and they get their dinner.

*The tie makes kin, and it is the one thing in this family anyone can walk in and ask for.*

## Doctrine and Arts

### Karasu no Michi, the Crow's Road

The fighting style of the Yukari yard comes down to two teachings: **stand where the outcome needs you** and **move a thread before you draw a blade.** Its whole curriculum is the patient removal of every occasion for a fair fight. Know the man first. Know his hurts, his temper, his seconds, what he owes and what he had for breakfast, and be standing with a very ordinary knife where he is about to be. A Moto reads a seam and puts a sword in it, and a Yukari has done much the same thing and only looks more bored about it.

It is a good deal louder than its reputation, because it has to be. The young ones shout their moves, because they are young, because a bracket crowd pays for it, and because the Moto next door have been bawling the names of their Works across battlefields for a thousand years and somebody ought to answer. The old ones do not raise their voices. There is no call to shout at something you have already got.

| The move | Meaning | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| **TOMARI-GI!** | The Perch | The yard's stance and first lesson: weight flat and quiet, head a little tilted, eyes closing for the deep read. The feet move early, to the ground the read says the other fighter will need |
| **KAGE-FUMI!** | Treading the Shadow | A valley children's game, played from the moment they can walk, in which you step on the other child's shadow and they are out. Grown up, it is footwork: put the sun behind him and your foot on the line he means to take |
| **TOKI-UCHI!** | The Taut-Hour Cut | An ordinary cut, delivered on the instant the other fighter's commitment hardens and not before. Anyone can learn it slowly, and a Teishi eye makes it unfair |
| **KIZU-YOMI!** | Reading the Scar | Every fighter alive has been hit in the last ten years. The blade or the thrown line goes to the old break, the mended rib, the knee he has told nobody about |
| **SENGAKI!** | The Drawn Line | The house's thrown working: one fine line of Essence that holds together across a yard or a field, goes past the Shell and arrives carrying a say on what the thing is. This hand is not holding that sword, for about as long as it takes to find out otherwise |
| **YOMIAGE!** | The Reading-Out | Mushō said aloud, at a carrying volume, for the whole bracket. Your debts and your wife's letters, and the fact that the left knee goes on the third exchange. It is no technique on paper, and it has ended more fights than the Slip |
| **IINAOSHI!** | Say That Again | A line thrown into a called name as it leaves the mouth. A working is partly what it is called, and in this world everybody calls them. Spoiled in the air, the name comes out wrong and the working comes out short. Bull Karl Ostrey of the Hot Tun called his HEAVEN-SPLITTING ANVIL in the Sealhall semifinal and what forty thousand people heard was MOTHER, and it hit about as hard |
| **ŌGI-TOME!** | The Fan Stop | Yukari Hisame's answer, taught as a drill: the guard-stick of a folded fan, or whatever is in the hand, to the hollow of the throat in the beat after the other fighter's result fails to arrive |
| **MI-MAMORI!** | Watching Over the Self | A Fixed Regard turned on your own wound. It stops bleeding and waits for you. It is half your looking gone from the fight, and it will all be there when you stop |

**The counter to the yard** is to be ugly. Fighters with no form to read, who do not know what they will do next and do not care, drag a Yukari onto ground where elegance buys nothing. And anyone who fights silent gives Iinaoshi no word to catch, at the price of a weaker working, which is why the great killers of the South have never much minded the Crow House and the showmen have never stopped complaining.

### At war, and what they swore

Three things a commander will pay nearly anything for: a line of the dying held in place until the surgeons reach them, every soul on a valley's field read and told over company by company, and the wounded of the other side slipped all at once, so that the enemy's line, which has spent the morning hearing them, hears them stop. That last one nobody puts in the song, and everybody puts in the terms of surrender. The Yukari did all three across forty years, and on the bean field at Ōmagari a quartermaster's clerk named Yukari Sayo sat nine days on a war drum with three hundred and twelve people's dying in her eyes. The muster book has her down as *a clerk*.

The house has since sworn that its knowledge goes out to mend and never to take, and the Academy has ruled in open session that a wyrm is not a war. It says nothing about what happens to people who come up the road to start one. Lord Naiser told the Sovereign of Kushara, in the long hall, that anyone who reached into the spirit side and pulled something out to throw at another house would be annihilated, and he told him over tea.

### The carvers and the Reach

Spirit work includes writing on things, and the craft the Reach is built on is a cousin of the eye: ward-cutting, in which a line on a stone carries the carver's say on what the stone is. A Cymorath-attuned carver hears the substrate before cutting, and completes a sentence the stone was already trying to say. Most carvers are not Yukari by anything but the Academy's letterhead, and that was the design. Niran Yukari, called the Crow of Fate, came up the coast at thirteen fixing gate posts for a meal, and every town he left an apprentice in found itself, some years later, asking to join. The arithmetic was the argument. The full account of the Reach, the Council of Threads and the Proving is [[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Silver Cedars of Yukari|the realm's own]].

## Culture

**Language.** The old Japonic of the house for formal work, plain Common for everything else, and a family habit of finishing other people's sentences that outsiders do not love. Names are two-element compounds on thread, crow, stone, water and grief, up to five in a life, and which one a person uses for you tells you exactly how close you stand. There is no worse manners in the valley than guessing up.

### Customs

- **The Telling.** The name is given only to those who hear the whole account of what it costs and then ask for it. Refusing is honorable and nobody presses. A person marrying in sits the same account, on the same floor, and is expected to have questions.
- **Five names.** A childhood name; an adult name chosen at the Telling; a calling-name for intimates; the true name, spoken only in Council, at a Proving or before Senri's stone; a posthumous name given at death.
- **The cut nape.** A fighter who loses cuts a lock from the back of the neck the morning after and lays it on the fire, and goes about with a bare nape until it grows back. Nobody speaks of it.
- **The tea.** No word of business is spoken in a Yukari house until the guest has tasted the tea, bitter with small white flowers. Whatever is said after, the tea was shared.
- **The dead.** The dead are laid in rows facing east with lamps between, and their names are read over them before the light comes. The house holds that death is a return.
- **The empty place.** On the first night of each year a place is set at the long table for the mother of the house, and nobody sits in it.
- **Grief.** The house treats grief as a known catalyst of deeper sight and says so plainly at the Telling, as a risk to prepare for.
- **The walk past the door.** Before their first Telling every child is walked past a clean room in the east wing with a good view of the cedars. In it Yukari Tadashi has been in the middle of a sentence for thirty-one years, with a stroke waiting on him to the hour, because his wife Hisano looked up and has not looked anywhere else. She is by every measure the kindest woman in the house. The child is asked what they would do. The house does not give an answer.
- **The breakfast round.** Every morning at the Enclave a child goes round with a bell and a list and says good morning, by name, to the people nobody else can remember. It is a chore, they draw lots for it, and they are told why. There are people in the house whose whole claim on the world is a nine-year-old with a bell.
- **The Looking-Away.** Nobody can hand on a Hold. So when a Holder grows old, the register names a day, and everyone she is keeping is told it, and their families, and their physicians. The surgeons come in. The house lays out a meal. She takes each one in the order the wounds stand, and she looks away, and the meal is eaten afterward, whatever is left of the party. The South's bookmakers have long since stopped pretending not to ask for the dates.
- **The relit lamp.** A child with the undivided eye is told everything every single night, by whoever is next on the list, until they can bear to hear it and go to sleep. The family takes it in turns, as Ashura did. Neighbors bring food, and the visiting courtesy is condolence.

**Words** · *The crow perches. The crow watches. The crow remembers.*

### The teachings

The Yukari teachings are short, and every one of them is something Ashura or Senri said or did in front of witnesses.

1. **The tie makes kin.** The bond decides who belongs; the blood follows it.
2. **Tell them everything.** Nobody in this house pays for a sight they were not told about.
3. **See without taking.** A thing looked at well is left as it was. A thing looked at badly is changed, and the looker is to blame.
4. **Master the innards.** Senri's line, the one on the stone.
5. **Stand where the outcome needs you.** Move a thread before you draw a blade.
6. **Knowledge goes out to mend.** What the house knows may leave the valley to heal and never to take.

The root of all six is [[The Disciplines/Schools and Bloodline Arts/Null-Light|Null-Light]], Ashura's own way of seeing.

### Who they marry, and what the valley gets

They marry whoever asks for the Telling and survives it, which has kept the bloodline a good deal wider than the Moto's. The Lambert match brought figures into the record. For generations the house swapped its children for a summer with the white Byakuya line, as if the war were weather, until the Hiroe burnings ended that, and there are old women on both sides who still write.

The valley folk pay the house a tithe and the house remembers who was late. They look away from a Yukari's eyes as a matter of course, and they believe that when a child's corona comes in a parent starts to weaken, that a true name pulls the thread, and that a child with a quiet life is to be pitied. What they get for this is that no stranger has come up their road unweighed in living memory, and that when somebody's grandmother will not go when she should, a thin person in black comes down the hill at dusk, sits with her until she will, and asks for nothing but a bowl. "You haven't eaten," says whoever loves you, setting it down beside tools gone cold. A Yukari says it to the woman's daughter. Servants of the Enclave go barefoot, get the house's long look in time, and know more than most lords. Nobody in the valleys has ever been known to steal from the Crow House, largely because there is no point.

## The House Now

The house is led by Senri's eldest, Lord Naiser Yukari, from the Cedar Enclave on its hilltop above the Yukari valleys, east of Sum-gol. Naevra keeps the archive and the register of every birth and death in the valleys, and reads the threads of a room so easily that they come out round her face unbidden, fine as cobweb in a barn door. Seradris is the youngest of the three.

The Enclave is the oldest standing seat of the family: cedar halls stepping down the slope on limestone footings, beams gone red-brown and resin-dark, bark-shingle roofs furred silver-green with lichen, a covered walk round a court of raked white gravel, and three standing stones in shawls of moss that throw no shadow at any hour. A stream runs under the floor of the long hall and talks to itself all night. The house's lamps burn a clear honey-gold in cedar hoods and do not flicker, and the crows that come down out of the cedars to sit on the rails are glossy as wet ink, their feathers taking a violet sheen in the sun. Below it on the drowned valley floor stand the shrines nobody has ever let go, red streamers renewed each year, and the Unlit Shrine, which goes on down past where any reflection stops. A thread-reader will not look at it for long.

### The wider family

Ashura's elder children founded lines of their own across the long Third Epoch, and their descendants are the cousins of the yard, the archive and the outlying valleys, each carrying the eye in one of its eight types or carrying none of it and taking the name anyway. Chosen kin stand beside them by the same right. A child of another house who hears the Telling and asks for the name receives it; Kasane Byakuya took it in the valley at fourteen, before Senri's stone, and the house has counted her its own ever since.

### After the war

The forty-year war ended with the Yukari standing and the Seikai gone, and it ended in the house's own courtyard. The Zettari struck the valleys in its last four years. The Seikai forced the seam. Senri went in, and Naiser broke the working on the slope above the river, and the house came home to lay out more than four hundred of its dead in rows facing east, with lamps between.

The wound in Ashura has not closed. Memory leaks out of her into the world: the dead of that day came back into the courtyard as faint silver shapes repeating one gesture each, and the living began forgetting the faces and calling-names of the people they had just lost. Springs come slowly in the valleys now. Midwives write down how long they wait for a child's first breath. The old die late, and some do not go when they should. In the south, springs that the Seikai drank from taste wrong to anyone with the tongue to taste them.

The house's terms to the other powers, given in the long hall, follow from all of it: no Yukari knowledge used for war; no Yukari name carried as a banner; no one touching the spirit side without Yukari counsel and teaching; every promise made in the hall entered in the Yukari record. The Yukari say they want nothing more than the spiritual balance of the world kept. They also keep [[Artifacts/The Immortal Cross|the Immortal Cross]], and they have kept it since the first morning.

The Cross cannot be closed now in any case. With the seam open the Soul Plane's recognition stutters, and the house holds that a Cross closed in these years would slip and kill its bearer quicker than if it had never been touched. So every family that has ever wanted a thing from the Yukari now has one more reason to care whether that wound heals, and the Yukari have noticed which of them have stopped asking.

## The Other Houses

### At the Table: the seventh house

Of the [[Factions, Bloodlines & Institutions/Houses and Bloodlines/The Southern Warlords|seven Southern Warlords]], the Yukari are the oldest blood and the least interested in ruling it, and they are the only house whose members are all volunteers. They held of the Mahou emperors for three hundred years at a tribute of one crow a year, and entered it. On the first day of autumn in 602, when the Emperor Rokju called his roll and the South stopped answering, the Yukari sent the crow, and it sat on the balcony rail for about as long as it takes to read a name, and left. After that they simply went on sitting where they were.

The warlords' table has sat at the Cedar Enclave since the war, because every house wanted every promise entered in a record somebody else was keeping. The southern rule is that a warlord has a price for everything and the price is never only silver, and the Yukari's is a promise put on the record. They tell the Lamberts' clerks where the figures are off, and the Lamberts tell them, since the record has to agree. The Yan sell a horse and keep its name, and the Yukari have a copy. Maud Corwen has not yet asked them to walk her springs from mouth to source. The Ryu ask very good questions about harbor dues. Gerrit Marrick considers them the only people in the South who have never once tried to hire him, and finds that restful, and checks his feet at noon.

### The Mahuo, who were carried

The Mahuo were carried at the front of other people's armies until there was nothing left of them but the carrying, the Yukari fought under the excuse of protecting them, and then the Yukari killed them. The house says so. It says the Moto stood back and let it happen, because a house turned into a symbol is very easy to kill and nobody weeps for a symbol, and it tells this in the long hall and at the Tellings, against itself, and tells it well. That is why it swore off banners. Lord Naiser has promised the Mahuo royal line its seat back, and Cozbi Mahuo, Head of the House, has declined to accept anything from anyone who ever carried his name. The couriers run between the two seats every week and the courtesies are perfect. Since the split the Sum-gol Post has also been laying hauntings for a fee, in olive coats, with no Yukari counsel asked or wanted, and the terms given in the long hall say what they say. Nobody at the table expects that to stay a matter of courtesy.

### The Seikai, who were never kin

The Seikai were a house that reached the Soul Plane alone. They had no Epoch in their blood and no Yukari to teach them, and they got there anyway, by discipline and by nerve, which is a thing no other mortal house has ever done. No Yukari carries Seikai blood, and no Seikai ever carried Ashura's.

Their nature was the Null. Their arts ran through Mortalis and Nihiloth, the cold of the Still Gate and the clean cancellation of a boundary, and they worked precise and still, with no wasted motion anywhere. They saw the spirit side by opening it. Every look they took was an incision, and every incision changed what they looked at. They called a Yukari's gaze the Crow's Audit and used the phrase the way other people use the word inquest, because they feared being seen through far more than they feared being beaten.

The Yukari tell the end of the Seikai against themselves. The house held its knowledge close and let it be known that nobody outside the family could reach the spirit side; when the Seikai reached it on their own, there was nobody to show them where the edges were, and a house that had already decided they were a threat. A Moto who reads fate came to them and told them what they could become. They reached for what cannot be arranged. They forced the seam in Ashura, drank from the southern Wellsprings to do it and put back what has fouled those springs since, and on the slope above the river Naiser Yukari broke their working and the people who held it. The house believes the Seikai were reaching for what the Immortal Cross keeps. While they lasted, the Seikai never said.

**How the house stands with them now** is the least comfortable thing it has to say, and it says all of it. Lord Naiser told the Sovereign of Kushara to his face that the Seikai were the Yukari's own making, by a refusal to believe anyone else could understand, and he called that insolence. Three things follow.

*The road they took is still in the house.* Null-Light with the Light taken out is Mugan, the Unseeing, the Seikai's own way of looking, and it is the sixth Regard. It is taught to no one, and it turns up about once a generation, unasked for, in somebody who has lost a great deal. The house has two rooms at the back of the Enclave for its own who tried, and they are well kept. Every Yukari knows that the worst thing the Seikai ever did can be done with the family eyes. Only one Seikai document survives in the Archive, and it lists six looks, with Mugan first and no Zenkō at all, and calls the Yukari's seventh a kitchen story.

*They are not all gone.* The house reads the slope as finished and the springs as not. Something still feeds what is wrong with the southern water, and the Enclave's terms name a *Seikai remnant* as one of the things it will annihilate. One name has come back to the Council's ears more than once, of someone who outlived the house Lord Naiser broke, and the Council does not say it aloud. A remnant of a people who reached the spirit side alone is the only thing in the South that this family is known to lose sleep over, and it will not say whether that is because of what such a survivor could do or because of what such a survivor might be owed.

*The left arm of the Cross is theirs.* Black glass, cold at every hour. Closing it asks for an understanding of what a mortal can reach alone, and why reaching alone ended in a cut. So the house's own immortality test requires someone to have understood the Seikai wholly and not to have followed them, and most of those who were brought to try could not seat that arm, since a mortal who has understood what can be reached alone has usually already reached for it. The second teaching says tell them everything, and the first says the tie makes kin. The register has a page ruled and headed for the day a child of that house walks up the road and asks for the Telling. There is nothing on the page, the elders have agreed to give the Telling if it comes to that, and they have not been able to agree on anything else.

### The Zettari, respect across the threshold

The Zettari were forged by Aegor in the Second Epoch, Path-born, dense in the body, their Crystals built for the translation of immense physical force, their blood answering to Matla and to the Sands of Kushara. Their Origin Eye reads the point where a thing began. The Ketsumyōgan reads what a thing is tied to. The Yukari eye is relational and the Zettari eye is sovereign, and each house has always understood exactly what the other's sees.

What the Zettari know is endurance by witness. A Zettari-forged thing is declared trustworthy once, before a witness older than the speaker, and it holds that law from then on; a Zettari body that survives a strain grows harder against that strain; the Sands keep a lineage's memory. When a Zettari wall began to fail they could feel the stress in the stone, and a Yukari could tell them why. The two houses gave each other respect, in the Zettari sense of the word: the acknowledgment that another's function is lawful and complements one's own. The Zettari struck the Yukari valleys in the war's last four years all the same, and the Sovereign of Kushara has since come to the long hall to talk terms over the tea. When a Zettari meets a Yukari, the response is typically a nod, and it is returned.

### The Moto, fear worn as policy

The Moto were chosen by Bara and carry the Scribe's mandate; their sight reads the threads as an administration to be kept. The fear between the two houses is as old as Ashura's first children and it is jurisdictional. The Yukari eye reads the same threads the Moto govern, with an intimacy the Shingan was never built for, and the Moto have the fear of a scribe who finds that someone else can read his hand and, worse, the words he chose not to write down. They tried to fold the family into their offices more than once. Senri refused them in a sentence. The Yukari hold that it was a Moto voice that talked the Seikai into the seam.

The worst insult in the valley is *ledger-eyed*, and it means somebody who sees people as entries, and it means the Moto, and every Yukari who has ever used it has then gone home to a family archive with a great many people in it. Both houses know this. At weddings where the two meet, they have been known to read each other's cards aloud.

## Limitations and Soul-Costs

### [Curse] The Long Look — What You Know, You Keep

The price of Null-Light, and the reason for the Telling.

- What you hold, you must keep looking at. When you look away, everything held slips at once, so the longer the Hold the worse the release, and all of that lands on someone.
- You cannot stop knowing what you have known. Every soul ever held stays in the Core, whole, for life, and nobody has ever found a way to give a known person back.
- An old Shepherd carries every deathbed she ever sat, entire, in the same place she keeps her own name. Below a tenth of reserve she is not always certain which of them she is.
- A holder of one soul learns to sleep the way a mother sleeps beside a sick child, with the one thing kept in mind, and she never has a whole night again. Nobody has managed it with a field, since a looker who truly sleeps has looked away.
- A hitter gets stronger and stays more or less the person who started. Each rung here means knowing more people more completely, for good.

*A Moto pays in blood and reserve and a job left undone. The reserve comes back and the rest does not.*

### [Curse] Going Shadowless — The Slow Vanishing

Holders grow shadowless themselves, and it comes on by inches.

- People forget their faces and do not feel their regard. A shopkeeper who has served one for thirty years will greet her as a stranger, and a husband will start to set one fewer place.
- By sixty, nobody outside the house could say what Yukari Sayo looked like, and the song has the color of her coat wrong.
- The end of the road is someone nobody but the house remembers is there. It keeps those people on its books with great care, since nobody else will. This is the real reason for the long table, the register and the child with the bell.

*The family that knows everyone ends by being known by no one. The stones in the court were the warning.*

### [Curse] The Threadburn — The Eye's Own Bill

A mortal Crystal was never built to carry an Epoch's sight.

- Prolonged use stresses the lattice where the three layers meet. Phantom threads show up that do not exist, and the mind starts sorting everything it sees into causal chains whether the eye is open or not.
- Emotional flattening is the part they warn you about. Continuous soul-thread data makes it steadily harder to feel a connection as opposed to seeing it. Yukari Hisame could see the thread between herself and her sister, amber to the last fiber, and could have told you its weight and its age, and she read her sister's letter twice looking for the part of it she was supposed to feel.
- Sustained paired use can empty a well-developed Crystal in minutes.
- At the very top Sovereign Weave and the deep Danshi severances burn the bearer's own fate-threads as fuel, and the Yukari who have used it have, without exception, died young.

*The more clearly you see the web, the less you belong to it. The family calls this the Paradox and sets a place for it at dinner.*

### [Curse] Keeping — The Kindest Cruelty

Obsession Force is what recognition becomes when a bond is no longer honestly witnessed. A Regard has its own version, with no thread in it, and the house says the word as other families say the name of a drinking uncle.

- A Hold begun for love can go on for no reason anyone could defend, and every day it goes on, the bill for stopping gets larger. Ask Hisano.
- Since nothing arrives and nothing comes back, a soul can be kept for years by someone it would not know on the road. Ask Bartlemy Goss.
- The holder is usually the kindest person in the room. The house has heard a great many answers to what it asks at the door, and it does not give one.
- See without taking is the third teaching because this is what taking looks like here.

*Nobody has ever been kept by someone who did not mean well. The house would like that noted, and it would like you to think about what it means.*

### [Curse] The Insolence — The Family That Knew Best

This one the house named itself, in front of the Sovereign of Kushara.

- A family that sees everything finds it very hard to believe that anyone else has understood anything. That belief made the Seikai.
- Its terms now say that nobody touches the spirit side without Yukari counsel and teaching. Other houses have pointed out what that sentence is, and the family has noticed.
- It offered the Mahuo a crown, and its own record says who slaughtered them. Good intentions are entered in the same ink.

*The Moto's error, they say, is to want to control the fate of other peoples. It is a very fine line, and they are drawing it themselves.*

## How They Stand Against the Three Paths

**A Body enemy** is the oldest answer, and it is a simple one. Get there. A spear does not care what you mean, a fragile body dies out of position, and a hitter who has never been hurt and is wearing good boots gives a Slip nothing to finish. But Armor and Durability do not cover the soul, a matter-reader has nothing to read on one, and the Crow has seen the charge from sixty paces and knows about the knee.

**A Spirit enemy** is the one person who can feel the Long Look on the Core and go for the hand behind it, and the only one who can spoil a Yukari's say with another say. This is the fight the house has trained hardest for, the one that ended on a slope above a river. Anyone who has come to the Soul Plane without Yukari teaching is by the house's own terms a matter of concern, and by its own confession the reason for the last war.

**A Fate enemy** can move what the look depends on, and every Regard depends on something: a wind that puts the smoke in her cousin's eyes, a messenger who arrives at the right moment. Spirit survives by being right, and one wrong read is enough. Then again, every pull is made by a soul, and it shows on the Core before it lands. The Moto can read a Yukari's threads as she can read theirs, and each house has spent a thousand years wishing it could not.

## Discrepancies of Record

*Attested, not reconciled. This office files them because the Yukari would.*

**The second blood.** The Enclave's register gives Lord Naiser's mother as a daughter of House Lambert, and his two bloods as Yukari and Lambert. Older southern accounts and a good many from Kushara give the other half as Zettari, and say he felt it stir on the road. The house has not commented.

**Seikai blood.** The register above is plain that no Yukari carries it. A story that has followed Lord Naiser since the yard says that there was Seikai blood in him, that the house never let him forget it, and that neither did the Seikai. Those who could settle the matter are dead on a slope or keep the register.

**A fourth child.** The register has three children of Senri's. Household talk from the war years speaks of a brother as well, also called Senri, who told Naiser more than once to work with other people. The keeper of the register is his sister and has been asked.

**The old recitation.** The house still recites blindness cycles at nineteen, twenty-six and thirty-one, and an old form of the Telling speaks of an eye that saw the warmth coming across a great many years and of a Senri who died against a stone facing east. It fits neither the first children nor the Senri who went into the seam. The Yukari say it is older than they can account for, which is not a sentence they enjoy.

**Numbers.** Seven Regards, one normal to a person, two rare. Eight types, one to each eye. Fewer than a dozen Zenshi in recorded history, and the undivided eye once in an age. Three stones. Six teachings. Five names. More than four hundred dead at the courtyard. No EU figures live here: a Hold is priced on its holder's card, as how long it lasts from full.

## Final Definition

The Yukari are the family of the Third Epoch, a house as old as the Soul Plane's waking, born to Ashura and kept up since by birth and by asking. Their bodies are lean and breakable. Their Cores hear early, keep everything and are answered by the dead. Their eye sees what everything is tied to, and for those who will pay for a second road it can cut. Their instrument, Null-Light, is the far end of the Spirit road, seven Regards under one law, and it can stop a death or finish one from across a valley without touching anything at all.

They will not rule you. They have sworn off your wars and your banners, they will mend what they can, and they will tell you everything, because the mother of the house would not let one of her children pay for a sight they had not been told about. Then they will write it down. They killed the Mahuo and say so. They made the Seikai and say so. A man of theirs is standing inside an Epoch's wound right now, holding it shut, and they have not given him a posthumous name. Somewhere in your market there is a stout woman with a good recipe for honey cakes, and you may wish to look at the ground.

*The crow perches. The crow watches. The crow remembers.*

Beneath it, on a slab of the valley's limestone set at the height of a seated man's eyes, the line that a child reads as a promise, and a young man as a boast, and anyone who has lived long enough as a question:

*Master the innards, and the outer world is yours to command.*

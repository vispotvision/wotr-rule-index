# The Yuno Bloodline

| | |
|---|---|
| **Type** | Archaic line with no Epoch behind it · hereditary house of kashira · keepers of the ma |
| **Origin** | The Eighty Isles, in a count that comes down from the Epochs and that no Measurewright will sign |
| **Founder** | None named. The *keizu* (the family's recited line) begins with the gods who first walked the isles and has not yet been recited to its end by anyone living |
| **Seat** | [[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/Isles and Archipelagos/Yasoshima — The Eighty Isles\|Yasoshima]], five island branches under *hyōjō* (councils of the house), with no king and no standing army |
| **Anchors** | Shinma · *keizu* · *chigiri* (witnessed covenant) · *kotoage* · the western gate at Nishimori |
| **Signature** | **Shinma**, the keeping of the ma: the interval between any two things can be measured, witnessed and made to bear load |
| **Maxim** | *The ma must be carried* |

> A hereditary house of **kashira**, an *ie* whose authority runs through **keizu**, through **chigiri**, and through the keeping of **ma** rather than through crown or conquest.
>
> In most provinces the sacred stillness that belongs to the *za* and the moving tongue that belongs to the *zetsu* sit in separate blood, separate halls, separate halves of the *hyōjō*. **The Yuno hold both in the same line.**
>
> *That alone is why other houses watch them the way a village watches a fire that has learned to sit still.*

Black Hob Turnstile had thirty-six ships, a monkey in a scarlet coat and the whole of the Kagura tribute, and in the summer of 478 he was the most famous pirate between the Old World's north coast and the western shield. The Eighty Isles sent no fleet after him, because they have none. They sent Tamaki of the Kagura branch, seventy-one years old, alone in a reed boat with one cup. She climbed the side of his flagship in a brocade the color of a ripe persimmon, sat down on his deck, and declined to say a word until the cup had gone round, and his crews, howling with delight, let her. She served the *sakazuki* (the ordered cup, where the serving order fixes who outranks whom) in its exact order, the captain second to last, and each man who drank woke the next morning already set in his place before a house older than his language. Then she spoke, in *kotoage*, the bound oration of the Yuno. The sentence took about as long as lacing a boot, and by its last word thirty-six crews stood obligated by its grammar to give back what they had taken, to carry their dead to Nishimori and bury them properly, and to pay their captain every honor owed him. She folded the brocade across his shoulder and went home.

He never got it off. He spent three years giving things back, thinner each season, and he died still wearing it, and the monkey outlived him and is in the *keizu* (the family's recited line), under a very respectable name. Tamaki never spoke again; the sentence had cost her the voice. She sat out her last eight years in silent watch on the western shore, and the house counts that as a bargain and tells its children the price at the same hour it tells them the story.

## Foundational Identity

Their identity centers on **shinma**, the sacred and unbreakable relational interval running between person and person, the living and the dead, the land and the sea, the *ie* and the gods who first walked it.

> Where other lineages inherit force that breaks, devours, commands, or foresees, **the Yuno inherit the harder portion.** They must keep the ma, mend it where it tears, **and answer, in their own bodies, for every silence a broken ma leaves behind.**

**Core inheritance** · Ancestral *keizu* recited without error **back through more generations than any living Yuno has counted aloud**, a discipline of *kotoage* sharp enough to bind a hearing crowd, and **the keeper's instinct for where a ma has thinned before it tears.**

**Reputation** · Revered, unsettling in their composure, and **politically almost impossible to move against**, since even rulers who fear nothing else fear the house that both blesses the *hyōjō* and speaks for it.

> **Yuno means gentle**, a temperament of calm water. **Nobody who knows them calls it a peaceful name.**
>
> It is a warning wearing a soft word, because a ma, once it is old enough and wide enough, **holds enough pressure to drown a village**, and the Yuno are the only hands trained to touch it without flinching.

**What they want and what they hold.** They want the ma kept, by whoever is standing in it, for as long as there are people. What they hold that everyone needs is the thing a realm cannot make for itself: an oath that stays sworn, a truce that outlives its signers, a funeral the dead will accept. Courts mock them in public and send for them in private.

## Origin of the House

The house's own account starts at Hatsuhi with the first light on the first isle, and it will give you the figure of two hundred thousand years without being asked. On islands with nothing anyone would cross the water to steal, a people had no use for walls and every use for knowing exactly what they owed each other. The other old lines were given something by an Epoch, and the Yuno were given nothing. On their mainland shrines, Kureha-in-Mist in the west the best known, the same house in time grew a whole clergy of bell-keepers, ash-recorders, vow binders and grave chanters, and still disagrees with the island branches about whether that was a good idea.

**The marriage.** Muken Moto of the Kōkan Line took Ayame Yuno of the Kagura branch as his queen. Kharven believes it gained an ally, and the Yuno believe they placed an obligation. The world has noticed that the Yuno endure because the Moto stand behind them.

### What the Blood Gives

The Yuno have no [[Factions, Bloodlines & Institutions/Houses and Bloodlines/The Archaic Bloodlines/The Archaic Bloodlines|Epoch behind them]], and so no instrument: everything they do with the ma, they built. Their blood gives what any old line's blood gives, four things and never a Path.

| Slot | The Yuno |

| **The grip** | [[The Magic System/The Soul and the Climb/Classes and the Three Ladders|Essence Class]] may open before Glory, in children marked at birth. The cap never lifts |

The Seven Tsutome are on the Spirit Path with Fate behind it, as a house's habit and never its blood. Their Forms are Rite for the great labors, Sing for *kotoage*, and Bind for the cup, the brocade and the witnessed *chigiri*. *Kotoage* spends the speaker's own share of fate and pulls nothing down, which is why it costs what it cost Tamaki.

**On the rungs.** A shrine attendant is Essence I, Listener. An ordained *kashira* seated in a *hyōjō* is II or III, Answerer or Confessor. Branch elders are III and IV, Shepherd. The Kagura officiants who hold a convergence reach V, Ferryman, and it empties them. Thread Class, the hold on Fate, stays at Unthreaded or at the Touched and Drawn that any long-kept vow will buy.

## Shinma — The Keeping of the Ma

**Every relation has a width, and a width can be made to hold weight.**

Shinma goes well past holy magic and past etiquette raised to the level of law. It is the discipline by which the interval between all living and unliving things is measured, honored, defended, **and, when torn, mended before the tear widens into catastrophe.**

**How it works, in plain words.** A rigged mast stands on ropes pulling against each other, and scholars call the principle *tensegrity*: a structure whose strength lives in the tension between its parts. The Yuno hold that a marriage, a truce, a hall or a kingdom stands the same way. Everyone keeps a distance from everyone: who speaks first, who may come near, what is owed and for how long. That web of intervals is already bearing load, and most of the time no one has ever looked at it. The gaze finds a line in it, and the rite names that line aloud before witnesses, and from then on the Core of each person in it holds that line as part of what they are. Breaking it costs them from the inside. Nothing moves the odds and no fire comes of it, since this is a Spirit art and the only thing it has ever touched is what a person takes himself to be.

> Their arts do not overpower a thing so much as **force it back into right relation with everything it stands beside.** Even their harshest workings carry the shape of ceremony: **closer to a verdict than a massacre.**
>
> Because the Yuno are a house of kashira rather than conquest, **their power always arrives dressed.** A working that could flay a man instead arrives as procession, as judgment, **as the folding of a brocade across a shoulder that will never be unfolded again.**

### The Seven Tsutome of Shinma

| Tsutome | Labor |
|---|---|
| **Kiyome** · Consecration | Sets apart a threshold, a hall, a relic, or a body as **sacred and no longer available to profane or unstable use** |
| **Akashi** · Witness | Seals a vow, a marriage, an accusation, an inheritance, or a confession **beneath a truth the speaker cannot later unmake** |
| **Yomiji** · Passage | Governs the funerary crossing, the vigil, ancestor continuity, and **the lawful movement of a soul from this shore toward the western gate at Nishimori** |
| **Wagō** · Reconciliation | Brings enemies, broken councils, and clashing Domains into a held space **where contradiction can be carried without collapsing into war** |
| **Yurushi** · Atonement | Answers curse pressure, inherited stain, and broken *chigiri* through confession, payment, **and the slow work of forgiveness** |
| **Muki-Naoshi** · Orientation | Turns a person, a hall, a relic, or a whole *ie* **back to face its correct center** when it has drifted from lawful balance |
| **Tōbō no Michi** · Far Wisdom | **The highest and rarest labor**: gathering memory, oath, passage, and reconciliation **into a single sacramental act** |

### The Saishiki

The greatest thing ever built on this discipline stood in the Ashen Hall for sixteen years. **Saishiki**, the Yuno rite of the woven oath, was carried out of Yasoshima by Ayame Yuno, whose title in the house was *Zenma no Mamori*, Keeper of the Whole Ma. The northern clans had a man's word before witnesses and nothing more. She took those oaths, and the ceremonies and formal occasions of a kingdom, and wove them into one liturgy that bound the loyalties of the Banner Houses into a single structure, so that an oath in Kharven carried spiritual weight and could not be broken without consequence. They say she once received thirty hostile clans alone in a courtyard and had them rethreaded into three coalitions by dawn, and the wards on the royal children's doors were the same weaving at a smaller scale. Her death removed the architecture and left the ceremony. The kingdom still performs it, and the load is no longer there, and what it was like to live under the Saishiki is the best argument for the Yuno and the best argument against any one person being able to do that.

### What it costs, what limits it, who beats it

**The cost is the keeper's body.** Every line a Yuno holds, she carries. A tear in a ma she has witnessed comes out of her as sleeplessness, blood from the nose, a limb gone cold, and a great *kotoage* takes something and does not give it back: a voice, a year, the use of a hand. **Hōkō is burden**, and it is written into how the art functions.

**Its limits.** It works only on what stands in relation. It needs witnesses and it needs time, because a sentence must be finished and a cup must go round. It binds what a person holds himself to, so it reaches the living, the dead and the places they have marked, and a plain falling rock is outside its jurisdiction. Nobody has reached Tōbō no Michi alone: it needs every branch that can be gathered under one roof.

**Who beats it.** Refuse the cup. Stop your ears, as sailors have done with wax since the Hob's day, or kill the speaker before the verb, since a bound sentence takes about as long as lacing a boot and a crossbow takes less. Bring something that has no relation to anyone: a beast, a Hollow thing, a person who has truly been cut off from every tie, and a sworn runaway of the Russet Crown, on whom a *kotoage* finds no party to obligate. A Yuno on the other side can unsay it through Wagō. Fate can lean on what the rite depends on, a cup that spills, an old man's cough at the wrong word. And a hard thing to say of a holy house: any plain soldier with a sharp blade, who has not been listening, is a very great danger to them.

## Sacred Bearing

**Skin** · Yuno children are **born already marked**: faint lines beneath the skin at the wrist, the sternum, and the base of the spine, tracing shapes that echo the finished *horimono* **before any horishi has touched them.** The family holds that **the ancestors mark the child first and the living carver only finishes what was already begun.**

**Gaze** · A Yuno's eyes do not settle on a face so much as on **the interval around it**: the exact width of ma a stranger keeps from kin, from land, from grief. *Villagers say a Yuno can tell what a person owes before that person has said a word.*

**Presence** · Even an untrained Yuno holds a stillness too exact to read as ordinary manners, the seated composure of *chinza* worn like a second skin. **Rooms quiet around them without being asked to.**

**Voice** · When a Yuno speaks in the register of *kotoage*, the sentence **arrives with the weight of something already decided**. The voice is rarely loud; the air in a *hyōjō* has been trained for centuries to receive that cadence **as close to law.**

**Adornment** · Ordained Yuno wear the *kanmuri* only at the gravest rites, hair bound close beneath it, skin oiled, **a** ***hossu*** **or** ***shaku*** **carried not as a badge but as a second limb.**

**Dress** · Brocade, always, and it says everything. At fifty paces a Yuno is one still figure in a very expensive color, folded and never flourished: persimmon for a Kagura elder, the pale silk and silver thread of a great ceremony, and the rest in the deep mineral dyes of the isles, every fold of it a stated rank and a stated debt. A *nishiki* of the old looms reads Legendary, and no one has ever seen a price, since a brocade that has been given cannot be sold. A poor cousin from a lime-burners' branch wears one narrow band of it at the collar, repaired by three generations, and carries herself exactly as if the rest were there. There are also Yuno who carry a blade and a light of their own. Those arts are theirs, and the house does not list them.

### [Trait] The Birth-Lines — Marked First by the Dead

- The lines come in before birth and are finished by a *horishi* at ordination.
- They warm when a ma is near tearing and when the wearer lies.
- They are the blood's claim on the Essence ladder, and a child without them may still be Yuno, and still be trained, and never quite believe it.

*The earned ink finishes what the ancestors began, and the ancestors do not ask.*

### [Trait] The Keeper's Gaze — The Width of Grief

- She reads who owes whom, who has wronged whom and who has stopped speaking, across a room and at a glance.
- Good ones read shrine geometry at forty meters.

*Children of the house learn early that a mother always knows, and they learn why.*

### [Trait] The Unbroken Recital — Keizu in the Body

- Layered memory of names, debts, pardons and broken oaths, recited into permanence.
- The list reads back as a defense, since a soul that can say everyone it comes from is very hard to unseat.

*Nobody has ever got to the end of it, and they keep the monkey in.*

## Doctrine and Arts

**Nasake before preference** · A Yuno is trained to ask what the *nasake* of the whole *ie* requires, **not what flatters one voice inside it.**

**The ma must be carried** · **Not every tension should be erased.** Some must be housed, named, and ritually held until true *wagō* becomes possible.

**Chigiri is architecture** · A sacred covenant, especially the *chigiri* between a brother and sister, is treated as **a structural force that reorders reality** once it is properly witnessed and kept.

**Hōkō is burden** · Sacred service always costs the one who performs it. **A kashira who takes on nothing has mediated nothing.**

> **No ma stands alone.** A tear in one interval echoes through the social, the spiritual, and the metaphysical alike, **and the Yuno are feared precisely because they act as though that were always true.**

### Arts Shared by the Whole House

**Ma-Jirushi**, the Marking of the Ma · A woven working of spoken *kotoage*, Aether pressure, and gesture that renders **the boundary of a sacred space visible, or half visible**, around people, graves, or chambers.

**Sakazuki**, the Ordered Cup · Mastery of the ceremony's exact serving order, used to **fix rank, precedence, and spiritual law within a rite before a single other working is spoken.**

**Kotoage**, the Bound Oration · Formal chiefly oratory turned technique: words shaped so precisely that **a hearing crowd finds itself already obligated by the sentence's structure.**

**Nishiki**, the Debt of the Brocade · The brocade exchange used as **a vessel that carries a sworn obligation across bloodlines and generations.**

**Chinza**, the Vigilant Stillness · Seated, silent, watchful intercession on behalf of absent kin, **patient toward an outcome it cannot yet see.**

**Naoshi-de**, the Mending Hand · A touch-rite of healing and curse extraction, named for the old proverb of the stonefish that **carries its own cure in its body.** Intimate, and **dangerous to whoever administers it.**

**Keizu**, the Remembered Line · A mnemonic art through which names, debts, pardons, and broken oaths are recited into permanence, **keeping the *ie*'s accounting honest across centuries no single memory could hold alone.**

### When It Comes to a Fight

The house does not fight, and it has made a long study of what to do when somebody starts. These are called in one low voice, and the name is usually the last thing you hear clearly.

| Move | Meaning | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| **Hikae** | "Hold back" | A half-visible line on the floor. Whoever crosses it owes a step before he may take another, and his leg knows this |
| **Oikaeshi** | "Returned to one's seat" | On anyone who drank her cup: the knees decide that this person has not been given leave to rise |
| **Shaku-Uchi** | "The tablet's stroke" | A hardwood slat across the wrist, delivered with a verdict in it. The bone takes the wood and the hand takes the sentence, and it opens |
| **Nishiki-Gake** | "The brocade laid on" | The Debt of the Brocade, in the field, across a living shoulder. Bookies will not take odds on anyone who has seen it done |

## Training and Cost

**Oratory discipline** · Layered *keizu*, *kotoage* structures, funerary law, and the exact serving order of the *sakazuki*, **all memorized before being trusted with public office.**

**Gestural discipline** · The body trained as an instrument of rite: seated posture, the angle of a *hossu*, the timing of a breath through *chinza*, **all held to account.**

**Emotional discipline** · A Yuno who cannot carry grief, shame, contradiction, or fury **without spiritual leakage** is considered **unsafe to seat in a hyōjō.**

> **Every Tsutome carries its own corruption.**
>
> **Kiyome** can harden into fanatic exclusion. **Akashi** into punitive surveillance. **Yomiji** into necromantic attachment. **Wagō** into a suffocating false peace. **Yurushi** into cruelty disguised as mercy. **Muki-Naoshi** into authoritarian fixation.
>
> And **Tōbō no Michi** into the tyrannical subsumption of every will beneath one.

## Cultural Customs

**Child naming** · A Yuno child receives **two names**: one spoken in the hearing of the whole *ie*, and **one whispered over them at the cup by the eldest *sōke* present, recorded only in the family's *keizu*.**

**Ordination mark** · Full ordination is marked by the completion of the *horimono*, worked by a *horishi* **who is shown where the birth-lines already run, so the earned ink can finish what the ancestors began.**

**Marriage law** · Marriage is a **witnessed** ***chigiri*** **between houses** as much as between two people. Dissolution is possible only through a formal, recorded *wagō* between both *ie*.

**Deathkeeping** · **No Yuno is buried unwitnessed.** The dead are washed, **named aloud through their *keizu***, and given three full nights of *chinza* before the western road is opened for them.

**Seasonal calendar** · A sevenfold cycle across the year, one season for each Tsutome, with the rarest convergence rites of Tōbō no Michi **reserved for the years when every branch can be gathered under one roof at once.**

**The tide pools** · The Yuno keep no walls at home, and they will tell you this is the most dangerous thing about them. On the islands, beastkin, humans, half-elves and demihumans of six lineages draw from one watershed and raise children in the same tide pools, and a lime-burner or a reef-pilot has a seat at a feast and a known place in the order of the cup. A Yuno queen once told her small son what she thought a kingdom was for, and he has never managed to forget it.

## Limitations and Soul-Costs

### [Curse] The Price Told at Bedtime — What a Sentence Takes

A great *kotoage* is paid from the speaker's own share, and it does not refill. Tamaki gave her voice. Others have given sight, a year of memory, or the use of the hand that poured.

### [Curse] Answering in the Body — Every Silence Left Behind

What they witness, they carry. A keeper of many intervals has a body like an old ledger: chronic pain with no wound behind it, a cold limb when a marriage fails two islands away.

### [Curse] Load-Bearing — When the Keeper Falls

An architecture held in one person goes when she goes. Kharven learned this. Nobody in the house has decided what the lesson is, and three separate branches have an opinion, and the convergence at Yasoshima goes on holding what it holds, by a very few hands.

### [Curse] The Hand That Stands Behind — What the Moto Are Owed

The house that bows to no crown endures because a very large crown stands behind it. The *chigiri* binds in both directions, and Kagura has now given Kharven a daughter and got back a grave, a ceremony and a long silence.

## How They Stand Against the Three Paths

**Body.** Their plain danger, because a spear does not care what you mean. A Yuno survives a Body fighter by getting him to sit down first, and if he will not, she has a *shaku* and a very short time. **Spirit.** A long, courteous and appalling contest, since Spirit is where they live, and a confessor of another school can read the same lines they do and pull at them. They win by having more witnesses. **Fate.** A pull moves what a rite depends on and they cannot stop it, though a working that leans on an oath finds the Yuno have already seen the thread thin, and they tend to know who is on the other end.

## House Thesis

> The Yuno are **a dynasty of sacred relation.** They pray over the world, and then **they force divided things to stand in right ma with one another**: force memory to answer *keizu*, force grief to answer Yomiji, force law to answer *nasake*, **and force every fracture to reveal the larger weave it never stopped belonging to.**
>
> Their power can wound and dominate when the ma demands it, and it is still best understood as **a gentle name worn over an enormous weight**: the labor of holding too many truths in one hand at once, **and refusing to let the world split clean where a deeper relation still calls to be kept.**

*The ma must be carried.*

## Yasoshima — The Eighty Isles

*The islands of the Yuno.*

An archipelago off the great land's shore, in the open water of the great bight between the Old World's northern coast and the Inner World's western shield, belonging to neither quarter and administered by no crown. The Accord's surveys record it, price its exports, and have never found a reason to send anyone twice.

**The ground** · Raised coral platform over karst, low, green and riddled with caves, with fresh water in lenses that go bad the moment anyone digs too deep. No metal, no coal, no timber worth a hull. This is the whole of the reason the islands have never been conquered. There has never been anything on them worth the crossing.

**Ambient Essence** · Moderate and, by every Measurewright reading taken, the most stable saturation recorded anywhere in the four quarters. Nothing drifts at Yasoshima. This is why Yuno rites hold across generations without correction, why the house has never built an array, and why the Accord's Research Division has filed four separate requests to seat a resident Measurewright there and been declined four times without explanation.

**The five branches** · Hatsuhi, Nishimori, Ishizue, Nakadachi and Kagura are islands before they are branches. The Kagura seat sits in the strait between the two largest, which is why the rarest branch is also the one every other branch must sail past.

**Standing** · A confederation of *ie* governed by *hyōjō*, with no king, no standing army, and no charter with the Guild Accord. Kharven's marriage to the house is the only formal tie Yasoshima holds to any mainland power, and the *hyōjō* ratified it as a **chigiri between houses**, which is not the same thing as an alliance between states and which the north has never fully understood.

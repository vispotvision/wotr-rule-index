# The Banner Houses of Kharven

*Six houses around the Seat, each holding land, troops, and the conditional loyalty that is the north's only currency. Lands and houses, all six, and never kingdoms.*

Nobody at Hollenmark has forgiven the pie. When a Stark daughter married a Thornwall in the hard winter of 592, House Ashmore sent the gift the whole north still talks about: a raised game pie six feet across, its crust glazed gold and painted with all six banners in the proper colors, with a bushel measure baked inside it in case anybody had forgotten who kept the granaries. A Hallenfeld courier captain brought it up the March road on a sledge, six hours ahead of the day he had promised, and was unbearable about it. A Greymane sat guard on it all night in the firelit hall, because no one had told him to stop. In the morning a Keld elder come for the bride's side tasted the crust and remarked that it would have made a decent meal for one, and the bride's Dire Icebear, which had been let into the hall out of plain fear, walked over and ate the remainder, along with the measure, the banners and most of the table, while the Thornwall groom priced the damage aloud and the bride laughed until she had to sit down. The Ashmore envoy, soft-voiced and clean-handed, said nothing whatever. That year Hollenmark's grain allocation came in short by exactly one bushel, and the Starks have sent an empty pie dish to Ashbury every midwinter since.

That is the north in one meal, and the rest of this page is the ledger behind it.

> **Two hands on this page.** The seats, lands, charges and tempers below stand as this shelf stands them, in Year 715, the twenty-fifth of the Dark Ages, when the six are old. What comes after runs past that seal, and the little of it set down here is in the later hand that also annotates the [[Cosmology & Metaphysics/Ages and Reckoning/The Concordance of Ages|Concordance of Ages]], marked *Later hand* where it begins. Read those lines as weather coming over the ridge.

## How the years run

The houses are far older than the crown that binds them, and older copies of this page have muddled which came first, so here it is in order.

**Before.** The Banner Houses carry northern names because their roots predate the Moto arrival by centuries. Kharven-Seat stands on its rock already. The Kōkan sit the red chair for a long time, with Thornwall as their forester and the others about their business, until the Tenrai take the rock three hundred years before this seal. The north's old count speaks of Seven Warlords, names six houses, and declines to discuss it.

**Year 715, the page's morning.** The forty-year war has just stopped. The red chair has stood empty all forty of those years. There is no sovereign to swear to, so no Banner Oath is running, and the houses answer under the old summons law to the seven lines in the Hall of Verdicts, which in practice means Tenrai silver, Tenrai factors and a Tenrai toll-post on the Kessen road. Each house is on its own land with its own lord, and each is working out what it is owed.

*Later hand:* within a few winters of the seal the Seat has a king again, and the [[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Inner World — The Northern Shield#The Northern Empire of Whitemere|Northern Empire of Whitemere]] is built out of the kingdom. The six go into it as they stand, holding their own lands. That is his story, and it is told elsewhere.

## 1. House Stark

**Realm:** [[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Cobalt March of Hollenmark|The Cobalt March of Hollenmark]]

**Seat:** Hollenmark, eastern highlands, toward the Ring

**Territory:** The Ironwood March and eastern frontier

**Identity:** Borders. House Stark defines where things end and begin.

**Lord in 715:** Ulf Stark, Lord of Hollenmark, Master of the eastern March, one of the Seven Warlords

The Starks carry an ice-aspected bloodline inheritance that predates the Moto and operates outside the standard Wellspring framework: a cold radiating from body and blood, intensifying under emotional pressure, silvering timber and stiffening snow in a radius around the bearer. The inheritance is northern, no part of the Moto, older than the kingdom and older than the Starks' own records, and the Starks do not discuss its origins with outsiders because the origins are Stark business and the Starks keep their business behind their borders.

Their mount tradition is the **Dire Icebear**, an enormous polar ursid whose white fur carries a permanent frost dusting and whose temperament is exactly what the name implies. Nobody has ever domesticated an Icebear; the arrangement is a partnership. A Stark rides one because the bear has decided to permit it, and the permission is withdrawn the moment the rider stops being worth carrying, and the withdrawal is not gentle.

*The full codex: [[Factions, Bloodlines & Institutions/Houses and Bloodlines/The Houses of the Inner World/House Stark|House Stark]].*

## 2. House Greymane

**Realm:** [[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Slate Ridge of Stormfold|The Slate Ridge of Stormfold]]

**Seat:** The Ridgewall, western highlands

**Territory:** The Stormfold

**Identity:** The shield. House Greymane holds the western border.

**Title:** The Shieldwarden, always, regardless of the lord's given name

**Words:** The Ridge Does Not Move

The north's defensive architecture in human form. Their seat is a fortress built into the ridgeline separating Kharven from Undaar-Keth, and they have held it, in their own words, since before there was a north to hold it for. The words describe the house, which has never once found them inspiring.

Greymane culture is military to the point of institutional. Children are raised to see themselves as components of a defensive structure rather than individuals with personal ambitions; the ones who reject the framing leave, and the ones who stay become the most reliable garrison troops in the Inner World. The Shieldwarden is addressed by title rather than name because the function matters more than the person, and the house has internalized this deeply enough that the personal name of the current Shieldwarden is known within the family and almost nowhere else.

*The full codex: [[Factions, Bloodlines & Institutions/Houses and Bloodlines/The Houses of the Inner World/House Greymane|House Greymane]].*

## 3. House Ashmore

**Realm:** [[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Golden Hollow of Ashbury|The Golden Hollow of Ashbury]]

**Seat:** Ashbury, southern lowlands, where the Kessen, the Marrow and the salt road meet

**Territory:** The Greenhollow

**Identity:** The breadbasket. House Ashmore feeds the north.

**Lord in 715:** Edric Ashmore, Lord of the Ashmore Marches

The least martial house in a martial kingdom and the most quietly dangerous. Their power is grain. Their leverage is the specific kind that comes from controlling what everyone else needs to survive the winter, and the Ashmores have never lost a negotiation with anyone who was hungry, which in the north means they have never lost a negotiation.

The house governs through a steward's competence rather than personal combat capability: soft voice, clean hands, and the danger that comes from knowing exactly how many bushels each Banner House needs to survive December and being willing to adjust the allocation on political grounds. The Ashmores do not threaten. Threats are for houses whose leverage requires explanation. Ashmore leverage explains itself every time the temperature drops. Oaths in the Hollow are sworn with the hand flat on the junction stone below Ashbury, and the Hollow believes the stone holds them.

*The full codex: [[Factions, Bloodlines & Institutions/Houses and Bloodlines/The Houses of the Inner World/House Ashmore|House Ashmore]].*

## 4. House Thornwall

**Realm:** [[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Scarlet Wood of Thornhaven|The Scarlet Wood of Thornhaven]]

**Seat:** Thornhaven, northern Ironwood March

**Territory:** The Thornwood, the ironwood stands, and the corridor to Marrowhold

**Identity:** Timber and trade. House Thornwall bridges the north and the Marrowkin.

**Ruler in 715:** Hisae Thornwall, the Lady of Thornwall

Merchant-warriors, comfortable with commerce in a way most northern houses find slightly undignified and the Thornwalls consider essential. Their wealth comes from the ironwood stands the [[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Ember Holds of Marrowhold|Marrowkin]] import for tool handles and ward-frames, and their function as the Marrowhold-facing house gives them an outsized voice in the north's trade. They read cargo rolls the way other houses read battle reports, and consider the ability to predict a supply shortage six months out a more valuable military skill than the ability to hold a shield wall, on the grounds that the shield wall is useless if the men behind it are starving.

When the Ore Council of Marrowhold cuts grain supply but keeps sending inscription stock, the Thornwalls notice first, because they have tracked the holds' trade behavior for generations and know the difference between a shortage and a message. Their standing reading of the Ore Council's supply decisions is that they are political rather than logistical: the Marrowkin are comfortable with an armed frontier buffer and uncomfortable with an independent one, and the grain cut is a statement about the limits of northern autonomy rather than a reflection of the holds' capacity. The reading is correct. The Thornwalls are almost always correct about trade, which is why the other houses find them slightly irritating.

*The full codex: [[Factions, Bloodlines & Institutions/Houses and Bloodlines/The Houses of the Inner World/House Thornwall|House Thornwall]].*

## 5. House Keld

**Realm:** [[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Aurora Reach of the Keld|The Aurora Reach of the Keld]]

**Seat:** None fixed. Seasonal camps on the Polar Reach, the greatest being the Whalebone Gather.

**Territory:** The tundra beyond the treeline

**Identity:** Survival. House Keld exists where existence is the primary occupation.

**In 715:** Old Harald leads the fires, and his grandson is coming up behind him fast

The smallest house, the hardest people, and the most conditionally loyal. They live beyond the treeline in conditions that would kill members of any other house inside a week and that the Keld consider baseline. A Keld fighter raised on the Polar Reach has already survived conditions that would kill the enemy before the fighting started, and the fighting itself is an afterthought beside the surviving. They swear to a person, never to a letter or a house, and only to one who comes out onto the tundra and sits at their fires.

*The full codex: [[Factions, Bloodlines & Institutions/Houses and Bloodlines/The Houses of the Inner World/House Keld|House Keld]].*

## 6. House Hallenfeld

**Realm:** [[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Painted Rivers of Hallenfeld|The Painted Rivers of Hallenfeld]]

**Seat:** Hallenfeld, the river junction

**Territory:** The river valleys and trade corridors between Kharven-Seat and the eastern provinces

**Identity:** Movement. House Hallenfeld controls roads, rivers, and communication.

**In 715:** Ilse Hallenfeld, Mistress of the Hallenfeld counting-house

The north's nervous system. They hold the junction where the three principal roads meet: the eastern route toward the March and the corridor to Marrowhold, the northern route toward the Polar Reach, and the southern route down to Ashbury and the Center. Whoever holds that junction controls the speed at which the north can respond to anything.

The Hallenfelds have no glamour and produce no legendary warriors or political masterminds. They produce quartermasters, road engineers, courier captains, and the category of administrator who understands that a message delivered six hours faster can save a thousand lives and treats the six hours as a problem worth solving with the intensity other houses bring to swordwork.

*The full codex: [[Factions, Bloodlines & Institutions/Houses and Bloodlines/The Houses of the Inner World/House Hallenfeld|House Hallenfeld]].*

# The Banner Houses of Kharven

*Six houses beneath the Ashen Crown, each holding territory, troops, and the conditional loyalty that is the north's only currency.*

Nobody at Hollenmark has forgiven the pie. When a Stark daughter married a Thornwall in the hard winter of 592, House Ashmore sent the gift the whole north still talks about: a raised game pie six feet across, its crust glazed gold and painted with all six banners in the proper colors, with a bushel measure baked inside it in case anybody had forgotten who kept the granaries. A Hallenfeld courier captain brought it up the March road on a sledge, six hours ahead of the day he had promised, and was unbearable about it. A Greymane sat guard on it all night in the firelit hall, because no one had told him to stop. In the morning a Keld elder come for the bride's side tasted the crust and remarked that it would have made a decent meal for one, and the bride's Dire Icebear, which had been let into the hall out of plain fear, walked over and ate the remainder, along with the measure, the banners and most of the table, while the Thornwall groom priced the damage aloud and the bride laughed until she had to sit down. The Ashmore envoy, soft-voiced and clean-handed, said nothing whatever. That year Hollenmark's grain allocation came in short by exactly one bushel, and the Starks have sent an empty pie dish to Ashbury every midwinter since.

That is the kingdom in one meal, and the rest of this page is the ledger behind it.

> **Two hands on this page.** The seats, charges and tempers below stand as this shelf stands them, in Year 715, the twenty-fifth of the Dark Ages. What each house did under Muken and after the Fall runs past that seal, and it is in the later hand that also annotates the [[Cosmology & Metaphysics/Ages and Reckoning/The Concordance of Ages|Concordance of Ages]], marked *Later hand* where it begins. Read those lines as weather coming over the ridge.

The Banner Houses carry northern names because their roots predate the Moto arrival by centuries. Kharven-Seat already stood when Muken came to it. He took its crown and did not build it, and he did not replace the people who were already holding the ground. Each house was bound by a different instrument: alliance, defeat, appointment, or an oath so conditional that accepting it was itself a statement of Moto confidence.

## 1. House Stark

**Seat:** Hollenmark, eastern highlands near the Ketsuen border

**Territory:** The Ironwood March and eastern frontier

**Identity:** Borders. House Stark defines where things end and begin.

**Current head:** Zaehaerys Stark, Warden of the Eastern March

The Starks were the first house to accept Muken's alliance and the only house to accept it without being defeated first. The Starks were competent, and competent people recognize competence, and Muken's competence was visible from the moment he crossed the March with a column moving faster than anything the Starks had seen move through boreal forest. Zaehaerys's ancestor met him at the old boundary stones, measured the man against the measurement system the Starks had been applying to the world for three hundred and seventy-six years, and found the measurement favored alliance over resistance.

The Starks carry an ice-aspected bloodline inheritance that predates the Moto and operates outside the standard Wellspring framework: a cold radiating from body and blood, intensifying under emotional pressure, silvering timber and stiffening snow in a radius around the bearer. The inheritance is northern, no part of the Moto, older than the kingdom and older than the Starks' own records, and the Starks do not discuss its origins with outsiders because the origins are Stark business and the Starks keep their business behind their borders.

Their mount tradition is the **Dire Icebear**, an enormous polar ursid whose white fur carries a permanent frost dusting and whose temperament is exactly what the name implies. Nobody has ever domesticated an Icebear; the arrangement is a partnership. A Stark rides one because the bear has decided to permit it, and the permission is withdrawn the moment the rider stops being worth carrying, and the withdrawal is not gentle.

The house's relationship to the Crown is the kingdom's oldest alliance and its most honest. The Starks serve because the Crown serves, and will continue for exactly as long as that holds. The distinction between this position and disloyalty is the distinction between a partner and a subject, and the Starks have never been subjects.

## 2. House Greymane

**Seat:** The Ridgewall, western highlands

**Territory:** The Stormfold

**Identity:** The shield. House Greymane holds the western border.

**Title:** The Shieldwarden, always, regardless of the lord's given name

**Words:** The Ridge Does Not Move

The kingdom's defensive architecture in human form. Their seat is a fortress built into the ridgeline separating Kharven from Undaar-Keth, held every year since Muken confirmed them in the post, including the three years after the Fall, when the rest of the kingdom collapsed and the Greymanes kept holding because the border was their function and they did not know how to stop performing it. The words describe the house, which has never once found them inspiring.

Greymane culture is military to the point of institutional. Children are raised to see themselves as components of a defensive structure rather than individuals with personal ambitions; the ones who reject the framing leave, and the ones who stay become the most reliable garrison troops in the Inner World. The Shieldwarden is addressed by title rather than name because the function matters more than the person, and the house has internalized this deeply enough that the personal name of the current Shieldwarden is known within the family and almost nowhere else.

*Later hand:* When the kingdom fell the Greymanes did not scatter, negotiate, or join a faction. They held the western ridgeline against Undaar-Keth's probing advance for three years on stored supplies and hunted game; when the supplies ran out they held another six months on discipline; when discipline was no longer sufficient to prevent starvation they withdrew to interior holdings and waited for someone to re-establish the Crown. The waiting is ongoing. The Greymanes have opinions about the current political situation, but their opinions do not affect their function, because their function is the wall and the wall does not have opinions.

## 3. House Ashmore

**Seat:** Ashbury, southern lowlands

**Territory:** The Greenhollow

**Identity:** The breadbasket. House Ashmore feeds the kingdom.

The least martial house in a martial kingdom and the most quietly dangerous. Their power is grain. Their leverage is the specific kind that comes from controlling what everyone else needs to survive the winter, and the Ashmores have never lost a negotiation with anyone who was hungry, which in the north means they have never lost a negotiation.

The current head governs through a steward's competence rather than personal combat capability: soft voice, clean hands, and the danger that comes from knowing exactly how many bushels each Banner House needs to survive December and being willing to adjust the allocation on political grounds. The Ashmores do not threaten. Threats are for houses whose leverage requires explanation. Ashmore leverage explains itself every time the temperature drops.

*Later hand:* When the kingdom fell they survived because food is the one currency that never loses value. They traded grain for protection, protection for political access, political access for favorable positioning in whatever structure emerged from the collapse, and the positioning ensured that whoever eventually re-established the Crown would need the Ashmores more than the Ashmores needed the Crown. This is the Ashmore method. It has not failed in three centuries because it cannot fail. People always need to eat.

## 4. House Thornwall

**Seat:** Thornhaven, northern Ironwood March

**Territory:** The ironwood stands and the Stannvaard trade corridor

**Identity:** Timber and trade. House Thornwall bridges Kharven and Stannvaard.

Merchant-warriors, comfortable with commerce in a way most northern houses find slightly undignified and the Thornwalls consider essential. Their wealth comes from the ironwood stands the Dawi import for tool handles and ward-frames, and their diplomatic function as the Stannvaard-facing house gives them an outsized voice in the kingdom's trade. They read cargo rolls the way other houses read battle reports, and consider the ability to predict a supply shortage six months out a more valuable military skill than the ability to hold a shield wall, on the grounds that the shield wall is useless if the men behind it are starving.

When Stannvaard cuts grain supply but keeps sending inscription substrate, the Thornwalls notice first, because they have tracked Stannvaard's trade behavior for generations and know the difference between a shortage and a message. Their current reading of the Ore Council's supply decisions is that they are political rather than logistical: the Dawi are comfortable with an armed frontier buffer state and uncomfortable with an independent one, and the grain cut is a statement about the limits of Kharven's autonomy rather than a reflection of Stannvaard's capacity. The reading is correct. The Thornwalls are almost always correct about trade, which is why the other houses find them slightly irritating.

## 5. House Keld

**Seat:** None fixed. Seasonal camps on the Polar Reach.

**Territory:** The tundra beyond the treeline

**Identity:** Survival. House Keld exists where existence is the primary occupation.

The smallest house, the hardest people, and the most conditionally loyal. They live beyond the treeline in conditions that would kill members of any other house inside a week and that the Keld consider baseline. Their oath to the Crown was the most conditional in the kingdom's history: *we follow because you are worth following, and we stop when you stop being worth it.* Muken accepted the condition because it was honest, and because Keld warriors, few as they were, were worth three of anyone else's. A Keld fighter raised on the Polar Reach has already survived conditions that would kill the enemy before the fighting started, and the fighting itself is an afterthought beside the surviving.

*Later hand:* When the kingdom fell the Keld withdrew to the tundra without ceremony or explanation. They took no part in the factional fighting and negotiated with no claimant. They returned to the life they had lived before Muken arrived, because the tundra does not care about politics and the Keld do not care about anything the tundra does not care about.

Winning them back will require someone to ride to the Polar Reach, sit with the elders, eat their food, sleep in their shelters, and demonstrate through action rather than argument that the Crown is once again worth the cost of membership. The demonstration cannot be faked. The Keld have survived too long to be fooled by performance.

## 6. House Hallenfeld

**Seat:** Hallenfeld, the river junction

**Territory:** The river valleys and trade corridors between Kharven-Seat and the eastern provinces

**Identity:** Movement. House Hallenfeld controls roads, rivers, and communication.

The kingdom's nervous system. They hold the junction where the three principal roads meet: the eastern route toward Ketsuen, the northern route toward the Polar Reach, and the western route toward Stannvaard's corridor holdings. Whoever holds that junction controls the speed at which the kingdom can respond to anything, and the Hallenfelds have held it since Muken assigned them the post, because Muken understood that logistics wins wars more reliably than courage and that the house controlling logistics needed to be competent above all other qualities.

The Hallenfelds have no glamour and produce no legendary warriors or political masterminds. They produce quartermasters, road engineers, courier captains, and the category of administrator who understands that a message delivered six hours faster can save a thousand lives and treats the six hours as a problem worth solving with the intensity other houses bring to swordwork.

*Later hand:* When the kingdom fell the Hallenfelds lost their function, because the thing they connected stopped existing. A nervous system without a body is a butcher's leavings. They have spent the years since maintaining the roads, the river crossings and the signal posts with the dogged persistence of people who believe the body will return and that when it does the nervous system needs to be ready.

**Osric of Hallenfeld**, a survivor of the house's collapse, is currently among the 'uncles' advising Hild Stark in the Ironwood March. His presence in her coalition represents the Hallenfeld interest in restoring central authority, because the Hallenfelds cannot perform their function without a center to connect things to.

# The Central Warlords

*Four houses of the Inner World's center, wedged between the North and the South, none of them human, each content that you have to look them up*

Quartermaster-General Tancred Hasketh of the second Aldwater League was the best-prepared man ever to march across the center, and in the spring of 709 he said so. He had paid the councils of the salt cities for the road. He had paid the Accord at Sealhall for the seals, and three separate petty kings of the Midmarch for three separate bridges, all of which turned out to be the same bridge. He had nine thousand men in orange, a wagon train a day long, four hundred licensed Beastkin porters on the Verath contract, and a ledger bound in red, and on the morning of the eleventh day he came out of his tent into a bright gold camp and found four things waiting for him on his folding table, none of which had been there when he went to bed.

The first was a basket of pears, with a note in a beautiful hand on birch bark, inviting him to go round. The second was an account from a surgeon he had never engaged, in rose ink, listing the names of the eleven men in his host who would be dead of the flux by the end of the month, the well they had drunk from, the price of the cure, and a line noting that his grandfather had camped in the same field and made the same mistake. The third fell out of a perfectly empty blue sky as he was reading it and stuck in the table, an arrow fletched with a gray feather, carrying a slip of paper that read *Rain Thursday, heavy, you are in a floodplain, four silver*. The fourth was no message at all. His ledger's Beastkin page was gone, sliced out with a very clean edge, and four hundred porters and their pack-beasts had left in the night without waking one sentry, and on the ledger's open spine lay a single sawn piece of iron, stamped with a thumb. He held all four up in front of his captains, the morning sun coming through the orange tent-silk and turning his face the color of a bad peach, and he bellowed, with nine thousand men listening, "Who in the name of hell are these people?"

Nobody could tell him. The Marrick sergeant who had been his guide said he believed there were four of them, and could name three. He went round by the second week, the rain arrived on Thursday, and he paid every one of those bills. He died an old man still unable to name the fourth, and that was the way all four of them preferred it.

The north counts its Banner Houses, and every soldier knows the words. The south has its seven, with a table and a chair kept empty, and every merchant knows the prices. Ask a northerner about the Central Warlords and you will get a shrug, a song that gets the details wrong, or a very good story about a pear. I have seen a man win a tidy sum in a Kharven tavern for naming three, and the bookie paid out on the spot, with no danger of anyone ever taking the fourth leg of the bet. The roads run through the salt cities and the Accord's seal-towns and the bridges of the Hundred Crowns, and the armies follow those roads, and so do the heralds, with the minstrels close after. The four Warlords live off to the side of all of that, around the rim of [[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Scourge of Hell|the Scourge of Hell]], in a mire, up a cliff, behind a posted price and under no name at all.

Each of them, for reasons of its own, likes it. A people the Accord would classify as stock has very little to gain from fame. The great powers march through the center every generation and take note of whatever they can name, so whatever they can name, they burn, tax or marry. The salt cities and the petty kings have paid for being on the map for as long as there have been maps. The four have spent a good many centuries staying off it, and they have discovered that an army with no idea you exist will walk into your country without a scout out.

> **The central rule.** A Banner House of the north serves for as long as the Crown serves. A Southern Warlord serves nobody and has a price for everything. **A Central Warlord would rather you had not heard of it, and will send you a bill on the morning you find out.** *The Moss Court's bill is in pears. The Scalebond's is itemized. The Caelwards charge for the weather. House Nobody has already been paid.*

## 1. The Moss Court

**People:** Verdant Orcs

**Seat:** Mosk-Vaal, grown into the roots of the great tree

**Territory:** [[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Moonlight Forest of Mosk-Vaal|The Moonlight Forest of Mosk-Vaal]], the glowing wetlands of the low center, with Rootgate, Glowwater, Ghurash Hollow, the Blooming Mire and Totemdeep

**Identity:** The ground. What marches through the Moonlight Forest stays there and comes up in the spring.

**Current head:** The Mire-Mother, Ssaaleth the Long Breath, chosen by the largest working spoken on one breath

**Words:** *We Will Keep It For You*

**Colors:** Moss-green and moth-white

A nation of wetland orcs who treat death as a phase, and who cannot leave. More than two days from swamp or kin a Verdant Orc begins to wither, and so the Court has never in its long history taken a yard of anyone's land. It has simply kept every army that tried to use its causeway, in rows, and sold the fruit. To a passing general this is the safest neighbor in the world up to the moment he decides to save eleven days.

## 2. House Scalebond

**People:** Serpentkin

**Seat:** Coilspire Den, under the statue of the man they kept

**Territory:** [[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Rose Coils of Coilspire|The Rose Coils of Coilspire]], the rose-stone hills with Stoneglass Hollow and Bothhands, and a disputed claim on the Pale Fang Archipelago

**Identity:** The record. They remember everything anyone has tried to forget, and they treat and poison both armies.

**Current head:** The Remembrancer, Zsovrath Oda-riss, for the Standing Record

**Words:** *It Has Been Tried*

**Colors:** Rose and gold

A country of gold-scaled scholars and surgeons, with a stair and a posted price. Every commander of means has a Scalebond at his bedside and has made his peace with the fact that the other side has one too. They take no side and forget nothing, and their testimony is admitted everywhere it can be afforded and treated as anecdotal by the Accord, which then asks them for the answer. The great powers know of them mainly as a surgeon's fee, which is the least frightening way to know them.

## 3. House Caelward

**People:** Avian Kin

**Seat:** Caelspire, on the twin crags, keeping the summit pool and the bridge

**Territory:** [[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Violet Peaks of Caelspire|The Violet Peaks of Caelspire]], a range of rival schools, with Windstair and Highroost

**Identity:** The height. No army has ever taken the high stairs, and most have not been noticed trying.

**Current head:** The Warden of the High Stair, Vessaly Caelward, called Nobody's Landing

**Words:** *Step Off*

**Colors:** Violet and white

A house that keeps order among a dozen schools run like football clubs, and that has never been the strongest on its own mountain. It holds the hoists, the Core's hours and the court of the Drop, and it sells the wind to whoever pays. Half the battles of the center have been timed off a Windstair almanac by someone who has never heard the name of the people who wrote it. The Peaks consider this a healthy arrangement, and their real enemy is whichever club beat them on Saturday.

## 4. House Nobody

**People:** Catkin, Wolfkin and Bearkin runaways

**Seat:** Brokencollar, at the foot of the Heap

**Territory:** [[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Russet Crown of the Unpapered|The Russet Crown of the Unpapered]], the forest of permanent autumn on the Concord's flank, with Shadowed Ridge, Nightveil Hollow, Greyfang Crossing and the Glade of the Slumbering Root

**Identity:** The refusal. A kingdom the Accord will not recognize, whose whole safety depends on a form it filled out wrong.

**Current head:** The Queen of Nobody, Sefa Tuen-zhiv, while the town allows it

**Words:** *Ashe*, which is "I remain"

**Colors:** Russet and gold

This is usually the one the man in the tavern forgets, and I have met people in that forest who bought a round on hearing so. Anonymity for the others is a preference. For House Nobody it is the law of the land: it keeps no list, because a list can be stolen, and the people on it can be found. A correction to the Accord's roll of 668 has been pending for nearly fifty years, since it must be served upon the party concerned.

## The Table Nobody Called

The Southern Warlords have a table and the north has a Crown. The center has neither, and nothing in it binds any of the four to the others. The one time all four are known to have sat in a room was the winter of 612, at Bothhands, in the long ward, when a sickness was going through the armies and the Mire-Mother's rot-wives, the Scalebond surgeons, a Caelward wind-reader and an old Bearkin from the Glade who had just come in with a cart found themselves over the same bed. They settled the matter of the sickness. They also came to one understanding, by all accounts without anyone proposing it, which is that none of them would ever point a marching army at any of the others. It has held for a hundred years, mainly on the strength of none of them being able to prove when it was agreed, apart from the Scalebond, who are not saying.

What the center wants, in one line each:

| House | Wants |
|---|---|
| **Moss Court** | The salt kept sealed, the board obeyed, and a good crop |
| **Scalebond** | The record complete, the exemption renewed, and everyone's accounts kept |
| **Caelward** | The stair uncut, the Core's hours kept, and the Peaks left to their own quarrels |
| **Nobody** | The correction never served, the Long Way open, and every last pen gate off its pins |

## The Center and Everyone Else

**The Accord.** The Sealed Charter of the Concord is the one neighbor all four must answer. Its Beastkin Classification Provision makes stock of three of them on any day it chooses, and the fourth has no entry at all. Its clerks count everything in the center except the people who live there, and all four Warlords owe their obscurity, in part, to the fact that a people the Accord refuses to write down is also a people no one else can look up.

**The other center.** The Amber Cities of Nalūn and the Hundred Crowns of the Midmarch are the part of the center that anyone has heard of, and they take the marching. The salt councils pay both sides and owe neither. The petty kings change sides each decade. The four Warlords sell to them all, and regard them, kindly, as the people who stand in the road.

**The North.** Kharven's Banner Houses buy Scalebond surgeons, Windstair almanacs and Vantry pears, and most of them could not tell you where any of it comes from. A clerk of the Hall of Verdicts at Kharven-Seat has lately sent inquiries. They are the first northern letters ever to spell all four names correctly, and all four houses have taken note of that with a good deal more alarm than they showed at any army.

**The South.** The great Dynasty of Sum-gol, when it held the whole South, sent its summoners and tax-collectors as far as the Rose Coils and no further, and the Coils will give you the date. Its breaking-up has left a generation of southern powers with their minds on each other. The Yan sell horses to anyone who can get a horse up a cliff, and the Marrick companies know the Moonlight Forest well enough to take their contracts' end dates into account.

> *In a later hand, in the margin:* The Northern Empire of Whitemere, in the years of its making, came down through the center by the roads like everyone before it, and it met the councils, the seal-clerks and the kings. Somebody in the Stack had at last read the right pages. Its columns were fed and guided and told about the rain, and few of the men in them ever knew how much of that was bought, or from whom. I have seen the ledgers, and the fourth column is blank.

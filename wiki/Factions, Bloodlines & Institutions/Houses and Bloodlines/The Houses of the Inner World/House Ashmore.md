In the bad autumn of 633 the Host of the Nine Crowns came up over the Kessen ford, twelve thousand spears out of the Midmarch in every color the Hundred Crowns have ever fought over, to take the harvest of the Greenhollow. The Ashmore Line was somewhere else. On the fourth morning the Host drew up on the stubble of Sheafton Field, under a tall gold sky with the great teal roof of the tithe barn standing behind the stooks, and a single horse came out to meet it at a walk.

Lady Aldith Ashmore was a spare woman of fifty in a brown dress, with clean hands. She stopped a bowshot off the front rank, sat on a folding stool and took an onion out of her pocket. She peeled it, in no hurry, and ate it, in front of twelve thousand men who had marched eleven days on short commons. Then she looked along the line the way you look along a shelf, and remarked in the soft voice that carries in the Hollow that the left wing had four days and the right had three, and that the Yan horse had started on their own oats on Tuesday. She got up and let it go.

What went across that field was Pressure, the plain weight of a stronger soul, as every lord in the north can lay it on a room. It landed as the second week of the Thin Weeks (the starving end of winter), in the stomach, all at once. They heard it at Sheafton, half a mile off: the whole Host's bellies turning over together. A captain of the Yellows was seen with his glove in his mouth. All along the front rank great bearded pikemen sat down in the stubble and wept for their mothers' bread, by name and by loaf, and a Stage VII duke, whom it hardly touched, stood in his stirrups shouting at a host that could no longer hear anything but its own insides.

The barn doors opened behind her. The carts came out, each with a clerk and a bundle of notched sticks, and every man who took a loaf had a stick split in front of him, one half for the barn and one for him. By sundown the Host had stopped being an army and turned into a very large number of customers. There are households in the Midmarch paying it off today.

Lady Aldith never finished another meal. That is the part the Hollow leaves out when it tells the story.

House Ashmore is the least martial house in a martial kingdom and the most quietly dangerous. They hold every sack between the Greenhollow and the treeline, and they know how many bushels each Banner House needs to survive December. The north assumes this comes from good clerks, and they have the best in the world. The clerks are not how Lady Aldith knew about the oats.

## Origin of the House

The house counts from Godric the Reeve, steward to a family nobody bothers to name, who kept the tally through a famine that killed his masters, and by the end of it everyone left in the upper Hollow owed him their next year. In a valley of plenty he found he could no longer sit down to dinner in comfort, because he knew where the dinner had come from and who had not had it. His descendants have made a dynasty of that discomfort.

## The Count of Mouths

**In a breath:** every sack under the boar carries a grain of Ashmore's own soul, so everyone who eats it goes on Ashmore's books, and an Ashmore can feel the whole hungry north as you feel your own stomach.

**How it works.** Essence is Aether refined in the Crystal and stamped with identity, and the stamp can be small. Every sack out of a barn takes a lead seal from an iron die on the steward's belt, in a line of Ashmore hands since Godric, and the die presses in a very little of whoever swung it. The tally stick does the rest: one stick split down its length, half to the barn and half to the farmer, a real bargain freely entered. Whoever eats from that sack has taken a share of a bargain, and a bargain is a tie on the Layer, the same kind of thing as a vow. Each tie is as thin as anything can be and fades within a season of the last mouthful. Ashmore grain feeds most of the north, so there are a great many.

That is why a sack under the boar sells a tenth higher in a famine, and why the marches are thick with forged seals. A forged seal is a blind spot, and when Edric has a forger's sacks weighed in the market square he is finding out where he cannot see.

**What it costs: the Hunger.** A tie goes both ways. Whoever holds the Count carries a share of the wanting at the far end of every thread, and food does not touch it. That is why the lords of the fattest country in the north are tall and spare, and why Edric at forty looks like his own clerk. It is also the truth of a habit the north takes for a bargaining trick: an Ashmore will not eat or drink at a table where he is weighing a bargain, because a cup taken is a tie entered on someone else's books. Edric looks at an offer for as long as it takes the wine to go cold, and he would very much like to drink it.

**Its limit.** It reads hunger and nothing else. It gives no thoughts and no orders, and no tie has ever moved a man's arm. It stops at the last sealed sack, so on the Reach, which runs on seal fat and buys grain third after iron and salt, it finds a few thin lines and a lot of white.

**Who beats it.** Feed your people, which is the whole answer to The Thin Weeks and any sergeant can do it. Buy elsewhere, or bring your own seal. A Stage gap works as it does against any Pressure. A Weaver moving the weather on the harvest will hurt them more than any army. And there is what Hayato Amagiri did: he drew new roads on a map, where grain could go up indigo at no price, with a Yukari witness counting sacks at each end, so that the north could eat without going on anybody's books. Edric looked at that map until the wine went cold and swore to the one thing that could end him. He has looked faintly amazed ever since.

## Physical Appearance and Dress

**Stature and coloring.** The commons are plump by northern standards and reminded of it. The ruling line is tall and spare, with black or brown hair, long pale faces and a level mouth. **Marks.** The die callus on the right palm. **Voice.** Very quiet. *"And Ashmore?"*

**The look at fifty paces.** In the hall, the plainest person at any table where they own the bread: black wool with a turned collar, a brown coat edged in pale tape, and the iron die on the belt. The Silver Boar at Edric's throat, a Rare clasp cast from the first purse of Tenrai silver the house took, is a receipt he wore to every table with a factor at it. In the field the show is the color: bright russet, picked so that anyone across five miles of snow can tell whose horse is coming, on big southern horses at about fifty gold each. Edric has priced Wallbiter plate twice against what the same money buys in seed. The poor cousin has a clean russet scarf, a tally-bundle and an onion.

## Lineage Traits

### [Trait] The Weighing — Nothing Moves Them, Then Nothing Stops Them

* An Ashmore considering a thing cannot be pushed, by bribe or threat, and once it is decided the house commits entirely. Edric's Untouched Wine is the finished version. Someone who needs an answer before the wine is poured gets none.

*Bargain with them in a hurry if you can. Few have managed to arrange it.*

### [Trait] Hand on Stone — What the Junction Holds

* Ashmore oaths are sworn hand flat on the black upright below Ashbury, older than the house, on ground that favors old bargains, and the Oath sets like the stone. It holds one promise cleanly, and two grind.

*A promise from an Ashmore is hard to get and impossible to return.*

### [Trait] Full Weight — The Honest Sack

* What they sell is what they say it is, since the tie carries a false measure back as a sour note. Short-weighting takes real intent, as Hollenmark found by one bushel in 592.

*That one bushel was an act of war, and everyone involved knows it.*

## Doctrine and Arts

Everything the Hollow needs killed is arranged by contract, usually by not sending someone their grain. When that fails there is the Line: five or six hundred big southern horses in a long ragged charge, lords' sons in the saddle, coming down on a flank like a landslide of money. It buckled the Keld south flank at the Skeldmere.

| Move | What it does |
| :---- | :---- |
| **THE ASHMORE LINE** | House art. It needs a field it can take at speed, and its counter is pikes, set |
| **THE THIN WEEKS** | Called in the soft voice |
| **WHICH SACK?** | The question, asked across a table or a battlefield. It has made generals check their supply |
| **SHORT MEASURE** | One man's tie, pulled on for a breath in melee: he feels empty and his arm is suddenly a long way off. It does nothing to a man who ate elsewhere |

## Culture

**Language.** Hollow Common, unhurried, with an unnerving number of idioms about what you have eaten.

**Rites.**

* **Sack Day.** First market after harvest, rent in coin and a sack, every stick checked against its half.
* **Stack-Count.** The allocation read in the Corn Hall, which has ended more friendships than adultery. The holder fasts through the reading.
* **The First Stick.** A child of the house splits her first tally at six with a farmer's child, and the two keep the halves for life.
* **Harvest Home.** A week of dancing in the empty barns and Stage brackets on the stubble under the moon, with bad luck to win the first night.
* **The Receipt.** Every midwinter, a very polite one to Hollenmark for an empty pie dish.

**Marriage, feud and debt.** They marry anyone, with care, as a form of bookkeeping. Thornwall has billed them for clearing the Malt Deeps since the 540s, and Hallenfeld carries their grain under mutual loathing. Ulf Stark said before the war that they would sell to whoever stood at the gate with silver when the snow came, and Edric considered it a fair summary. They took Tenrai silver for twenty years and came over at the Skeldmere.

**What the commons get.** Fresh bread every day, the only commons in the north to have it, for a tenth to the barn and a day a week to the manor. A clerk knows without asking that the widow on the end strip has nine days of barley left, and whether she gets a tenth off depends on the clerk. In 684 the hedge-workers walked out and there were no pears for two years, the only time anyone has seen an Ashmore surprised.

## Limitations and Soul-Costs

### [Curse] Never Finished a Meal — What Lady Aldith Ate

* Every mouth on the books is a little more hunger for the one holding them, and a great famine can kill a grain lord in a barn full of wheat, with a physician looking on.

*Her last entry in the Corn Hall's book, in a hand you could barely read, was a query about the price of onions.*

### [Curse] Free Bread — The One Thing They Cannot Count

* A gift with no price leaves no stick. Grain handed out at the cart-tail by someone who wants nothing makes a hole in the Count, and a fed people who owe nobody are a people they cannot find.

*"He told me my grain could go up the indigo at no price at all,"* Edric is reported to have said. *"Then I said yes. I would like someone to explain that to me."*

## How They Stand Against the Three Paths

**Body.** A well-fed hitter does not care, so they arrange never to meet him until his wagons have been late for a week. **Spirit.** A strong one reads the tie straight off them, finds the Hunger behind it, and has a very interesting lever. **Fate.** Their true rivals. Move a harvest or a frost, and it runs through the Count like a crack through a plate.

## Final Definition

House Ashmore keeps the north alive and has never let it forget. They are quiet people with clean hands, hungry all the time on account of everyone else's dinner, and they stopped twelve thousand men with an onion. They will send you a bill and you will pay, because the only thing worse than owing an Ashmore is having nothing for them to count.

*"Which sack?"*

# House Corwen

## Overview

| | |

| **Founder** | Jennet Corwen, cask-steward to the Dynasty, who took out the lease in 602 |
| **Seat** | Brightwater, at the head of the spring country |
| **Anchors** | The lease · the brass keys · the hum of the kept pools |

The Burning Ladder came up the Brightwater road in the spring of 689 in forty palanquins, because a sect of that standing does not walk. They were the coming power of the Midmarch, red lacquer and gold fringe and a marching band, nine core disciples and a Sect-Mistress at Stage IX, Invocation, and they had decided to have the use of the Wellheads' training pools without the tedious part. They took the Cask Yards by mid-morning. They threw the wardens' guard off the first terrace, roaring, because any practitioner on that ground fills faster than she has ever filled in her life. By noon the Sect-Mistress was sitting in the Warden's own hot pool with her boots off, dictating terms.

Around that time an old keeper named Hannet Corwen walked down the Hotwell Stair in a blue coat with a boiled egg, sat on the top step by the bronze ring at the pool's lip, and finished the egg. She looked up at the water clock. "Time, ladies and gentlemen, please," she said, in the voice of someone closing a public house, and took out a brass key the length of her hand, and turned it a quarter. Nobody felt a thing. It took about an hour to go through the chain, keeper to keeper, along the forks in the assay posts, up one terrace and down the next and out to the sites on the barge road, and the only sign of it was that a sound everyone had stopped hearing was slowly going out of the morning. The Sect-Mistress came out of the water at about the time her first disciple tried to light a lamp. She was still Stage IX, with a full reserve, and she went up that stair like the wrath of heaven and took off Hannet Corwen's left arm at the elbow. Then she stood there, on ground gone lean as a washboard, with the whole of a very long fight's worth of Essence in her and no way on this earth of getting any more, in the middle of two hundred miles of Cores whose keepers were all very sorry. Hannet Corwen, who was by then being carried off, is on record as saying that the arm would be on the bill.

The Burning Ladder walked home. It took them a month, carrying the palanquins, and it paid the arrears, and that was the last anybody heard of them as a power.

A chartered family, then, and the richest per acre in the South. Every worked Wellspring site under Guild lease from the Wellheads to the southern sea sells its hours through a Corwen keeper, and the keeper can close it. That is what they hold that everyone needs. What they want is the springs clean before anyone outside the Wellheads proves they are not.

## Origin of the House

There were Corwens in the spring country under the empire, keeping the casks. The Wellheads paid Sum-gol in water, and a stringy, unpleasant clan of cask-stewards saw to the sealing, and they were in nobody's histories. **Jennet Corwen** is the one everyone remembers. In the summer of 602 she read the salt-tax notice and went, at her own expense, to see a Guild factor at the barge basin. The Guild Accord had been trying for a century to get hold of the South's rich sites, and the Dynasty had been refusing for just as long. She sold him a proposition: that if there were ever to be no emperor, the springs would need a lawful holder, and that it would be cheap to have the wax ready. So when the Emperor Rokju called the Corwen from his balcony, a boy brought word that the casks had been sold elsewhere, and on the same afternoon a lease was sealed in Brightwater. It covered the entire spring country. The family has spent a century making sure nobody can find that piece of wax. The Guild says the term is in it. The Corwens smile.

Their one great defeat came from their own people. In 647 Ferris Corwen raised the cask by a third and the Dyers of Kingfisher Steps tipped out their vats and turned the river blue for nine days. The lease was redrawn around that settlement, and the color went on the banner before the river ran clear.

## The House on the Crystal

A Soul Crystal is the soul itself, in three layers: the **Aether Shell**, where Aether (mana) comes in and force goes out; the **Essence Core**, where it is refined into Essence (spiritual energy); and the **Attraction Layer**, a soul's own share of Fate.

| Layer | Plane | The Corwen form |
|---|---|---|
| **Aether Shell** | Physical | Grown huge in the intake and rather soft. Raised in the pools from birth, the Shell draws in Aether at a rate that embarrasses lowland Stages and has never had to work for it |
| **Essence Core** | Soul | Unremarkable. It does what the books tell it to |
| **Attraction Layer** | Fate | Keys, terms and a lease. A seal spends the soul's own share and never pulls, and that is the whole of a keeper's business here |

**Path.** Pool-trained guards lean to the Body Path. Keepers, after forty years of minding terms, bonds and who may enter, drift to the shallow end of the Fate Path, the way an old midwife does, without ever knowing it and without ever holding a thread. Corwens read Ø on Thread for life.

**No grip, no eye, and not much blood.** The family's gift is in the ground, and anybody who grows up in a kept pool gets most of it. A lamp-warden from the Steps with no Corwen in him at all fills nearly as fast. The stat lean comes from the water, and the authority comes from a document.

| Rung | Typical Corwen |
|---|---|
| Unwoken | Brass-boys, cask-men and the family's many cousins, with a bad knee that is always better up here |
| Aether I, Glory | Warden's guard in blue coats. Dangerous on their own ground, and ordinary off it |
| Aether II, Essence I | A keeper, with a key and a ring and the first understanding of what they are |
| Aether III and up | The Warden's chair. Very few, and they do not come off the ground much |

## The Hours

**What it is.** A practitioner pulls Aether from the world around her, and where she stands decides how fast she fills. Rich ground fills you and lean ground starves you. A worked site is a pool of one of the sixty Wellsprings (the types of Aether), with a bronze ring called a ferrule seated at its lip, which limits how wide anyone can pull. Whoever holds the ring decides who gets how much and when. Everyone else in the world treats that as plumbing. The Corwens took out a lease on it, sell it by the hour and carry the key on their belts.

**How it works, in plain words.** A hill full of water has a head on it, as any mill-hand can tell you: the higher the source, the harder it pushes at any opening below. A Core has a head of Aether, and the ring is a sluice. A keeper's key is cut for one ring, in brass, with a seal laid on it at the lease's signing and renewed in that keeper's Essence on the day she takes it up. Turn it, and the ring narrows or widens. Ground for a good way round goes fat or goes lean, for everyone on it, and with long practice it will favor the names in the hour-book. The tundish, a brass funnel beside the ring, shows from the door what color is passing. Keepers along a chain feel each other's turns through the tuned assay forks on every wellmouth, and pass them on, and that is the hum.

It does not take anything away. **A closed Core takes no one's reserve.** It stops the refill. Nothing happens to you until you need more, and you always find out late. Anyone who has ever been truly dangerous in a long fight has been dangerous because they were filling as fast as they spent, and most of them have never once thought about where that came from.

**The forms.** They have very plain names, as the family is proud of pointing out.

| Form | What it is | Who holds it |
|---|---|---|
| **Hot and Cold** | The daily turning. Widen or narrow a ring for the season's trade, and read the color in the tundish | Any keeper |
| **The Book** | An hour sold to a name. A sitter on the book fills as if she owned the place, and anyone else on the same terrace gets a trickle. Sects pay more for this than for anything | A keeper of ten years |
| **Time** | The whole site, shut. Up to an hour's turning to do it decently, and then every working in the district starts going thin | Any keeper with the nerve and the Warden's word |
| **Last Orders** | A quarter-turn in a fight, by hand, with the ring in reach. The ground under one terrace goes lean in about ten breaths. It kicks like a mule | An old keeper, and rarely |
| **The Eviction** | Every ring on the chain at once, by the Warden's word, from the Wellheads to the sea. It has never been done. It is the only argument Maud Corwen has ever needed to mention, and she mentions it with a broad smile | The Warden |

**What it costs.** A head of Aether does not go away when you shut the gate. **Close a Core and the pressure rises behind the ring**, and something has to be done about that. Tenders work in pairs at the lip at night, with a fork and a lamp, bleeding it a little each shift, unpaid, because they live there. Close too long and it rises and breeds, and the abandoned pools have the sump-toads to prove it. Last Orders kicks back through the key, up the arm and into the Shell, as scald and a bone-deep humming, and it is usually the end of a keeper's hearing. There is also the plain cost of being the person who turned it off. Every working in the district depends on that ring, and a keeper's whole family lives downhill of a great many angry people.

**Its limit.** It works on ground under lease, with a ring on it. A key opens one ring. An unlicensed Well like the Gold Mouth, or the bottom of the Long Copper, a long way past where any Corwen has ever got a key to, does not care at all. A Corwen on someone else's ground is about what her Stage says she is, with a bad sleeping habit. And all of it, every ring, stands on a piece of wax. The Guild could evict them, in theory, if anyone could find the document, and the Guild depends on Corwen keepers for every lease this far south, so it has not looked very hard.

**Who beats it.** Arrive full and finish fast, like the Burning Ladder's Mistress, who only went wrong by staying. A Body fighter with a good reserve and a short plan can walk up and kill the keeper. Bring your own: bottled Drafts, or a cask, at a price. Steal the key, since it is only brass and it will turn for anyone who has taken the seal, and that has made some very quick careers. Or hire a hedge-worker with a sack of salt, for less than the price of a spear, and have her lean out their fat ground the night before, as anyone can in this country. It is the first thing a sensible enemy tries.

## Physical Appearance and Dress

Big, pink and cheerful, most of them, gleaming a little, with the padded fingertips of people who spend a lot of time wet. Hair stays damp and curls at the temples. They tend to a comfortable weight, and their joints do not trouble them until they go down to the plain for a week and everything hurts at once. They talk in big comfortable voices, slightly too loud, as people do who have grown up beside a noise. A Corwen listening to you tilts her head a little to one side, and it means she is listening to something else as well.

Kingfisher blue and brass are the colors. Keepers wear a good blue wool coat, brass-buttoned, and the key at the belt on a brass chain, polished by small boys and displayed the way a Moto lord displays a grandfather. A senior keeper's coat is dyed on Kingfisher Steps in the violent blue that makes people stop and take their hats off, Rare and worth what a good horse is worth, and the Dyers charge the family full rate on principle, and tell you so. Blue coats and brass at fifty paces, walking downhill without hurrying. A poor cousin has a brass-washed iron key to the laundry overflow, wears it just as proudly, and will explain the terms.

## Lineage Traits

### [Trait] Soaked — Raised in the Fat

The body of the House, and it has no blood in it. Anyone raised here gets it.

- Intake far above their Stage on rich ground, and Aether seems to find them. On the Wellheads they fill as if there were a hole in the bottom of the world.
- Baths, steam and the pool's minerals do their skin, joints and lungs great good, and Vitality Regeneration runs high at home.
- On lean or ordinary ground the gift simply stops, and they fall to about what the Stage says.

*Every Corwen in a foreign war has written home to say the air there is thin.*

### [Trait] The Hum — Hearing the Pool

- A Corwen feels the ring's state from a long way off, the way you feel your own pulse.
- Keepers along a chain can pass a turning, a warning or a price down the forks, faster than a horse.
- It cuts both ways: see the Curses.

*Children of the house who leave home cannot sleep for the silence.*

### [Trait] The Tongue — An Older Way of Knowing

- Long before there were forks, keepers tasted. Someone raised on the pools can tell one Wellspring from another with a sip, and with practice can tell a good deal more.
- It is taught less than it was. The house reads forks now, as the Guild requires, and a fork reads what it reads.

*The Archives have asked why a house would give up that tongue, and have had a very polite reply about modern standards.*

## Doctrine and Arts

**Close a door.** A Corwen ends the terms on which you were having a fight. Her guard hold a terrace in blue coats with good crossbows, for as long as it takes the key to work. The fine points of the trade are all about ground: fighting uphill of an opened ring, topping up between exchanges, and being on the correct side of a book entry. Shouts are in plain Northern English and mostly learned in a tavern.

| Move | Meaning | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| **TIME, PLEASE!** | Closing up | The call before Last Orders, and the only warning you will get. Ten breaths, and the terrace goes lean |
| **ON THE HOUSE!** | The ring thrown wide | A keeper opens fully for one breath. Everyone on the book fills, a guard's coat flares, and the head of pressure dropping behind it makes the pool jump |
| **CASK!** | Portable ground | A sealed cask stove in underfoot, Wellhead water on the flags. For a minute or so a small circle of ordinary ground is fat, and she will fight in it |
| **SOAK!** | Reserve | A guard goes under for three breaths and comes up full. In the Hotwell raft bracket this is a foul, and everybody does it |
| **MIND THE STEP!** | A boiling-water trick | Terraced ground, a sluice kicked open, and the top pool comes down on the attackers in a sheet. The Stair has seen it, with eggs |
| **READ THE TERMS!** | Shouted with a key raised | A keeper's Command. A person on the book feels the line on their window, in the middle of whatever they are doing, and it is a poor moment for it |

## Culture

They speak the Wellheads' Northern English, with a good many lease terms in it. The children learn to read from the lease.

- **The First Soak.** A child's first hour in the home pool, on the day it is born, in the ring's shadow. A cousin writes down what color the tundish showed, and everyone pretends this means something.
- **The Silence.** At eleven, a child is carried down to the lean ground below the gorge and left for a night with a keeper. Most scream. Those who come back are told what the hum is.
- **The Sleeves.** Any Corwen who raises the Dyers' water must do it on Kingfisher Steps, in person, with sleeves rolled up. Nobody has hit one yet. It has been close.
- **The Fork-Tuning.** At the turn of each year the assay posts are struck in sequence from Brightwater to the barge road, and the family walks them, all of them, with a band. It is a wonderful party. The wardens read the forks, and every fork rings true.

**Who they marry, fight and owe.** They marry Guild factors, invalid fortunes and, very cautiously, each other. The Guild Accord is their landlord, partner and permanent threat. The ward-cutters of Kharven-Seat quench their chisels in Wellhead water at the price Maud Corwen sets, and say what they think of it. The Yan have shown her a count of sick foals, the Post buys nothing from a Corwen cask, and every month the Yukari repeat an offer to walk the springs, and every month it is declined with the same courtesy. Commoners get bath-money, the free overflow, keeper's wages for anyone who will sit on a step all night, and a Lease Court that hears anyone at all.

## Limitations and Soul-Costs

### [Curse] The Hum — A Soul That Needs the Noise

Off the ground the silence is a physical thing. They do not sleep. Their food tastes of nothing and their reserve fills at what seems to them a crawl. Corwen wardens abroad have gone home in the middle of very promising careers, and the family has a small quiet hospital for those who could not.

### [Curse] Pressure Behind the Ring — Every Closing Is Owed

The head rises whether you have the money for tenders or not. Close too long, or stop the night's bleeding, and something begins to breed down there, and a ring that has been shut in anger has to be opened by someone who then stands next to it. Most of what the Bestiary knows about a keeper's death comes from this.

### [Curse] The Wax — A Kingdom on Paper

The richest ground in the South can be ended by a Guild clerk with the right document. They have no blood claim, no conquest, and no answer to the question of what they are if the lease lapses, and it is the only House in the South where the heir's first lesson is on limitations of actions.

### [Curse] The Forks Ring True — Reading the Instrument

The whole house has trusted its whole power to tuned brass for a century, and since the war the water has been wrong in ways that brass does not report. Villages downstream are sick. The family reads the forks, and the forks are fine. The Warden has never in her life been seen to drink the water, and that is all this office can tell you.

## How They Stand Against the Three Paths

**Body.** A hitter on a good reserve with no interest in topping up is the classic answer, and the blue coats are there to slow him. Fine on their own terrace, and off it a poor match for anyone who has ever been hungry.

**Spirit.** The ground's wide-open gifts work fine for a Spirit fighter too, if they are on the book, and a Spirit art goes past a guard's fat Shell with very little trouble. A soul in a pool is nakedly easy to read. Their defense against this is mostly the law.

**Fate.** A Fate user might look at a lease and see what it depends on. A keeper is a poor Fate fighter herself, whatever she drifts toward, and a slipped key or a tender who misses a shift can bring on what no enemy could.

## Final Definition

House Corwen does not rule anything. It rents out the ability to be dangerous, with a brass key, on terms it knows by heart, to anyone at all, and lets the whole South fight its wars on ground it has already been paid for. A big cheerful woman at Brightwater could turn off the world for two hundred miles with a word to a chain of old people on steps, and no one with a Crystal in them has ever quite forgotten it, or ever been able to find the wax. They also live downstream of whatever has gone wrong, and the lease is silent on the point.

*The water keeps faith. After you, Maud.*

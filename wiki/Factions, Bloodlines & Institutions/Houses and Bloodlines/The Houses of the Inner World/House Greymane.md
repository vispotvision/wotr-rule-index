The spring slide at Harrowgill, in Year 658, is why nobody in the Stormfold will tell you a Greymane cannot do anything but stand there.

Harrowgill was a garrison town of fourteen hundred souls in the lee of the crest, under a long slate face that had been loading up with wet snow for a month. Nineteen hands of the Grey File had been sent up that morning to clear a drain. At first light, under a clean rose sky, the whole face let go. It came down about forty feet with the noise of a city falling over, well over two hundred thousand tons of snow and shale by the Masonwarden's reckoning, and it met nineteen rims locked across the only bench on the slope, because a section sergeant had looked up and said *lock*. It stopped. Every set of boots in that file went into the rock to the ankle, and nothing behind the line moved at all.

Then nothing happened for seven hours, because a Greymane under that kind of load does not speak. The Muster Aunt worked it out from the dust. She was a stout woman of seventy who had never held a shield. She cleared Harrowgill street by street, in the order of the roster, and when the last cart was over the bridge she climbed the slope with a stool and a ledger, set the stool in front of the first man, and relieved him. *You stand relieved. Who has the post?* He gave the answer, she entered it, and he walked off the line on legs that would not work again and fell down the hill. The load went into the other eighteen. She moved her stool. By dusk the last hand in the file, put at the far end because he was youngest, stood alone with the whole of the Harrowgill face resting on his shield, sixteen years old, with his eyes open and blood running out of his ears, and she sat down in front of him and opened the ledger.

*"You stand relieved."*

He had to ask. It is part of the form. *"Who has the post?"*

She looked back down the valley at a town with nobody in it. *"Nobody has the post, lad. There's no one behind you."*

He stepped back. The mountain came down and took Harrowgill to the river, every roof of it, and the Aunt and the boy were found the next morning on a shelf the slide had gone around. He was three inches shorter than the roster said. He served forty years more as the Third Sentry, his stone on the ridge says that and nothing else, and by the end he could not have told you his name.

House Greymane is the north's defensive architecture in human form. Children are raised as components of a structure, the ones who reject the framing leave, and the ones who stay become the most reliable garrison troops in the Inner World. What they hold that everyone needs is the far side of the Galecross road, and every other house pays a levy in grain and salt to keep the Mandate of Undaar-Keth over there. The outside world takes the titles for an old family's tic. They are a working art.

## Origin of the House

The Ridgewall is carved so far into the mountain that no surveyor can say where masonry starts, and under it runs Petralon, the Foundation Stone, the Wellspring of load taken and sent down. The oldest records are rosters, and the oldest roster lists posts with a stone mark for each holder. The First Warden appears to have struck his own name first.

West of the wall lies the ground the bloodline came from, which nobody holds, and the Greymanes look at it every morning. The Archives have twice asked why, and both replies said the post was filled.

**Path.** The lean is Body, with a Fate lean behind it for anchoring, oath and the gravity of a line: the usual Shieldwarden reads Body Mastered and Fate Walked at about a third. **Ladders.** Aether climbs, slowly, at the post. They do not seclude, and each Stage is met where they stand. **Grip.** None. The Post is a vow, which spends the soul's own share and reaches for nothing above it. Wystan died at Glory on Thread Ø having kept every word he ever gave.

## The Post

**In a breath:** a Greymane given a title and a place to stand holds that place with the whole wall behind them, for exactly as long as there is someone at their back, and nobody can relieve them but the proper hand.

**What it is.** A Post is a vow with a strict grammar: a **place** (this stair, this gate, this man), a **charge** (who is behind you and what you are stopping) and a **relief** (who may stand you down). A superior says it aloud before a witness, and the one posted answers with the title and nothing else. The vow goes onto the Layer and ties to the post above it, up the roster to the Shieldwarden, who is tied to the ridge. So a load landing on the youngest hand on the most distant section has a road to go down, through stone and through the chain of the garrison.

**Why they talk the way they do.** Every time a Greymane is addressed by title in front of someone, the vow is said again and sets a little harder. That is why the Shieldwarden is the Shieldwarden in his own bedroom, why a Greymane child calls the dog the Hound and the latrine the Lower Gate, and why the holder's personal name is known almost nowhere: it is the loose stone in the wall.

**Its limit.** It faces one way, holds the post and not the person, needs someone behind it and needs feet set on something one with the ground. Take away what is guarded and the art goes quiet: Amelia Greymane said out loud in a tower at the Seat that she does not know what she is with nobody behind her.

**Who beats it.** The failure is written into Petralon's own law: stone fails in tension. Never push the Shieldwarden. Hook the rim and haul, lift him by the belt, or fight him on ice, a deck or a moving cart. Come from the side of the column, or land two blows at once. Break the file's end and roll it up. Kill the officer who holds the relief and watch a section stand at a dead post until it starves, as the Mandate has done twice. Or use the name, which works for a breath, from someone who knew him before, as the load comes on: the Core answers to its own name whatever the Post allows. Godfrey Greymane has been called by his name in a king's household since the Foot, and the old men of the Stormfold do not like to talk about it.

## Physical Appearance and Dress

**Stature and coloring.** Long, all of them, with the wide even shoulders of a shield carried from childhood, and a stride you can recognize at a quarter-mile. Pale gray faces, hair anywhere from rye-stubble gold to dark, heavy lids, and gray eyes, though rowan-red turns up. **Marks.** Long still hands. The stance sinks with every fight, so an old Greymane stands lower than he did. **Voice.** Numbers before opinions, given as a report.

**The look at fifty paces.** A long gray file with the dents left in: Stormfold wool, the wolf's head in darker thread, iron clasps, and rims proud enough to lock to the next man's and also catch an axe-beard, which nobody in the house has ever filed down. The flex is what has been left alone. The old sun, Grade A and Very Rare, has a gold sun half worn off by sixty years of blows, it belongs to the post, and the Mandate's ridge reports list it among the fortifications. At court a Greymane wears navy closed to the jaw, cut by a tailor who regards this as a branch of masonry. The poor cousin is hardly different: a Common file shield painted over twice, a dead relative's plate let out by hand, and eleven small nicks inside the rim.

## Lineage Traits

### [Trait] The Ridge Does Not Move — The Load Goes Down

* With feet set on solid ground, a load that comes in does not move the bearer. Petralon routes it down through the bones in compression, the way an arch carries a roof.
* The ground takes it, cracking in a ring, and a set foot is a planted foot.

*The Mandate has a technical term for fighting a Greymane on solid rock. It translates as "elsewhere."*

### [Trait] The Ridgewall Stillness — Still, Then Fast

* A Greymane has a way of not moving at all and then moving very fast. The first thing out of stillness comes a Grade quicker than the sheet. After that it is only an old man with a sword.
* Its counter is never to be the first to move.

*Old Stormfold men have watched Wystan use his sword a dozen times, and none can tell you which hand moved first.*

### [Trait] The Steady Note — Nothing Rattles Them

* Those who hold Basilithe, the Stone Will, lock their rhythm so nothing drives it. Godfrey's Flat Hand spreads that a little way, a hand laid flat beside a frightened ally, until their pulse falls in with his. The one forcing that matches them rings them apart.

*Nobody's hand has ever been laid beside a Greymane's. Nobody in the house has remarked on this.*

## Doctrine and Arts

The wall first, and then very fast. The ridge sword runs on *taking the Before*: the one who strikes second spends the fight answering, so a Greymane who attacks does it first, from the high guard they call the Roof, and otherwise stands still. The wall does a third of the fighting, since an enemy on the crest in a storm is in the hands of the violet weather.

## Culture

**Language.** Northern Common, flat. Family offices run all the way down: the Gatewarden, the Masonwarden, the Muster Aunt, the Cook.

**Rites.**

* **The Posting.** At twelve, on the wall, with a witness.
* **The Look West.** Every morning, from every wall. A Greymane looks that way before looking at you.
* **Reading the Long Posts.** A Muster Aunt with a stool, trying the form on the stairs that still refuse people.
* **The Cutting.** Each new Shieldwarden's shield is cut new. Wystan hung the old sun on a peg at the Foot the morning he rode south.
* **The Sewing.** The women of the long houses make the cloak in one night, and the thread runs out near the left hem.

**Marriage, feud and debt.** They marry for the wall. Starks share the wolf on the breast, do not speak to them and would die for them. The two garrisons on the Galecross road know each other's relief schedules better than their own wives do, and the Ashmore clerk gets the same courtesy as a siege engine.

**What the commons get.** A Post. Nobody on the Ridge pays rent, because you hold your room by holding your post, and every trade has a title, and a title is a very small wall. Ragna the Cook's pot would never go off the boil. When you stop holding the post you stop holding the room, which focuses the mind of the elderly.

## Limitations and Soul-Costs

### [Curse] The Name Goes — What the Wall Is Mortared With

* Every address by title sets the Post and takes a little from the name. After forty years a Greymane may not answer to it, and at death the Layer leaves a Name that is often only a job.

*The Third Sentry's mother came up to the wall once, and asked for him by the name she had given him, and they could not find anyone who knew who she meant.*

### [Curse] With Nobody Behind — The Shape of the Absence

* The art needs someone to guard, and grief in them stays put and bears load. Rigidity is the usual end, and being made to abandon a ward can break the soul outright.

*They hold a silence the same way, and for the same reason, past the point of kindness.*

### [Curse] Creep — The Stance Sinks

* Petralon accepts slow settling as the price of never cracking. They get shorter and slower to turn, and a fighter inside their turning radius has a long time to work.

*The Ridgewall tailor measures them every year. He has a chart, and he will not show it to you.*

## How They Stand Against the Three Paths

**Body.** Their whole training, and the one who brings a hook has been reading. **Spirit.** A disaster: Fortitude does not cover the soul and Ward runs at almost nothing. **Fate.** A well-kept garrison offers few straps to give, so a Fate user moves the relief, the cart or the one officer, and waits.

## Final Definition

House Greymane is what happens when a family decides, so long ago nobody remembers deciding, that a person is a part of a wall. They gave up their names to it and the wall answers, and a boy of sixteen can stand under a mountain. They are the most reliable people in the Inner World and very nearly the saddest, and neither is a topic they have ever found inspiring.

*"You stand relieved. Who has the post?"*

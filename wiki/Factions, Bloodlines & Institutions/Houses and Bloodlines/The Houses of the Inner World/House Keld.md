Moriyasu Tenrai, a tax captain of the gold line, came out to the Whalebone Gather in the bright week of 689 with sixty lances, a fine coat of gold-trimmed black and a duelist's name he had earned fairly on the plateau, and had it read out at the fires that the Reach owed the Seat for eleven years. An old sled-woman told him he should talk to Thora. So they brought him Thora, under a green sky with the blue domes of the palace throwing long shadows over the racing course, and she was nineteen, low and wide and dense, with blue on her chin and a mouth full of seal fat, and when he asked how old she was to be speaking for the fires, she told him ninety.

He laughed. She finished chewing, looked for a while at how he had set his feet, and told him he had his grandfather's left foot. It was a good foot, she said. She had thought so at the time.

There was a ring cleared on the ice by then, with bets painted on a whale's jaw. Moriyasu drew, the blade gold in that low light and his aura coming up behind it, and Thora Sennet stood with a birch lance in a girl's two hands and took her breath in, and four hundred people stopped talking. She did not shout. **"The name stands."**

Nothing happened to her body. It stayed nineteen and short. But she settled into it the way an old woman settles into a chair she has owned for sixty years, her weight going onto a bad hip she did not have, and her aura rose the color of tallow smoke in the shape of someone a head taller. When he came in on the feint his family was known for, low to the left and then up, the one that had killed the first Thora Sennet on the Teeth seventy-one years before, she was already standing where it was not going. She said in a cracked old voice, *"I've had a long time to think about that one, boy,"* and put the lance through his throat. Blood came out of her own left side a moment later, through a coat with no hole in it, at the place a Tenrai sword had gone in before her mother's mother was born. She sat down on the ice and asked somebody to get her some fat. The sixty lances went home. Ask Thora how old she is now, and she will tell you ninety-seven. She is twenty-six.

House Keld is the smallest house, the hardest people and the most conditionally loyal. They live in conditions that would kill anyone else inside a week, and they swear to a person and never to a letter. What they hold that everyone needs is whale-oil, ivory and a tundra nobody else can use. What they hold that nobody else wants is a whole people's worth of the dead, in working order.

## Origin of the House

The Keld say their first ancestor came out onto the ice alone and was found dead by the second, who looked at the body for a while, took her name so that somebody would still be using it, and made it through. They had a word for one of the dead left without the Waiting, a corpse nobody sat with, long before they had a word for a lord: *kiilut*, the worst word they own. A name with nobody to carry it is the same thing to them.

Then there is the palace, the Kiilut House, which stood on the snowfield before their oldest song. Its third dome has no agreed number of sides, and Measurewright Ansell came out of it in 694 by the wrong door with his beard at his belt. They pitch their camp every year in its shadow, will not sleep inside it, and do not joke about it.

## The House on the Crystal

| Layer | Plane | The Keld form |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **Aether Shell** | Physical | Dense, low and economical. It runs on almost nothing, as a lamp runs on fat, and gives out very late |
| **Essence Core** | Soul | The bearer's own, with a lodger who cannot learn anything new and has very firm opinions |

**Path.** The soul leans Body, as any soul does that has hauled its own sled since it could walk, with a smaller lean toward Fate from the name on the Layer. **Ladders.** Aether climbs in the fighters. **Grip.** The Layer, and a very small one. Taking a name at the Gather opens **Thread at I, Touched**, long before Glory. A Touched Keld knows the ice is about to go, and on a rare day can lean on that for most of a reserve and a nosebleed. Nobody has gone past II on blood alone. **Whale-Caller** is the class that runs thick at the Gather, with later forms the Long Water and the Call Comes Back, and they will not discuss the third with strangers.

## The Carried Name

**In a breath:** when a soul dies its Layer leaves a Name behind, and the Keld pick these up and put them on their children, so a Keld fighter has one dead person's whole life standing behind her eyes, debts and all.

**What it is.** At death a soul comes apart by three roads: the Core goes to Echo, the Shell to residue through the body, and the Layer to Name, a shape left on the Plane of Fate of everything that person owed, promised and was owed. Everywhere else this is a matter for theologians. The Keld call out the names of their dead at the Gather and give them to children, freely, before witnesses, and the child's Layer takes it up. They call what she then has her *sauniq*, a word that also means bone. It is a vow in form, and it spends only the soul's own share.

The dead woman is dead, and nobody in the lodge is confused about it: rebirth moves pattern and never a person. What the child has is what that life left written on the Layer, and that turns out to be a good deal: how she held a lance, where she liked to camp, who she would not forgive. It is why a Keld will tell you she is ninety and not be joking.

**What it costs.** You take the debts with the skills. A Keld whose name died owing a shift on the rim will ride south alone in that heat, present herself at the nearest fort, and leave on the day the dead person would have gone home. A name that died in a feud is still in it, and this is the plain reason the Sennet and the Haldorssons have been killing each other over the Teeth: the same people keep being in it. Muken Moto read that line through the host at the Skeldmere as a crack waiting to run, and the Sennet grays turned their lances on the Haldorsson dark horses the moment the Iron Fist was busy.

**Its limit.** It cannot learn. The name knows nothing past the day its owner died, and its power is the bearer's own: a Stage II girl with a great warrior's name has a great warrior's habits in a Stage II body.

**Who beats it.** Find out how the name died. The south has been paying for this for a century, over seal fat: a death-wound is a flinch that opens under The Name Stands, on a side you can learn. Bring something the name has never met. Set its old debts against the bearer's living ones. Keep her fighting past a tenth of reserve, where any Core frays, and the wrong one may come back up. A Yukari who can say what a thing truly is can say it to a Keld in front of everyone, and the lodger hears it.

### The Kiilut House

I have asked every elder who would sit with me what the palace has to do with the names, and been fed well and told nothing. Here is what can be observed. The Calling is held nowhere else, at the edge of the palace's shadow, late in the day as the shadow lengthens toward the crowd. The unclaimed names are called in order of how long they have gone unclaimed, and the elders are visibly in a hurry. And a Keld hedge-worker, drunk, once told Ansell's old assistant that the third dome has as many sides as there are names nobody came for, and that this is why they keep the count down. I report this as gossip.

## Physical Appearance and Dress

**Stature and coloring.** Low, wide and dense, with barrel chests and necks like tree-stumps, broad flat faces burned red-brown, narrow black eyes and coarse black hair braided close. **Marks.** Three thin blue lines down the chin mark a killing in defense of the herds, and a fourth, thicker, a chief. Walrus-ivory rings go into the braids, one for each fight won. **Voice.** Whichever one it is today. They rise from a floor with no hand on it.

**The look at fifty paces.** Nothing, in a whiteout, until the white cloth comes down. Otherwise short men on short horses in two coats, reindeer hair turned in and sealskin turned out, a wolverine hood that takes no frost, horn lamellar sewn between. Arrowheads are black glass and bone, because iron cracks in the deep cold. They flex, in a country with no coin, by who sewed it, and a coat from a famous hand is worth a team of dogs. The most famous piece on the Reach was Aksel Keld's iron hand, black iron and walrus tusk and sinew on a stump a Tenrai sword left at thirteen, closing by a cord at the shoulder into a fist the size of a man's skull. The poorest Keld alive has the same two coats and a small ivory tooth at the throat with a dead person's mark on it.

## Lineage Traits

### [Trait] The Baseline — Conditions That Kill the Enemy First

* Near-immune to cold, hunger and bad air, with a body built to spend nothing it does not have to. Heat is another matter, and the rim keeps a list.

*Aksel Keld at eighteen sat his horse like a man of forty. Nobody on the ice has told me whose forty it was.*

### [Trait] The Worst of It — Meat-Sharing

* A hunter's kill is shared by a custom so strict that the one who killed it often gets the worst of it, and it has gone into the blood as a very high tolerance for going without.

*Ashmore's Thin Weeks has been tried on them. Their report was that it felt like Tuesday.*

### [Trait] Weather-Weight — Touched

* The grip above, in daily use: they know when the storm, the plate or the herd will turn, a little before it is reasonable to. A true lean costs what it costs anyone.

*They have one word for a soul's weight and a storm on its way. They have a low opinion of the southern argument about it.*

## Doctrine and Arts

Few as they are, they are worth three of anyone else's. They ride in the **long line**, which moves across open snow like weather, and in a wedge when something needs breaking. In the lifting a Keld child is taught to sit down and wait. A rider is taught the opposite.

| Move | What it does |
| :---- | :---- |
| **THE LIFTING** | Whiteout war. White cloth over rider and horse, hit and fade from three sides, and they die having seen nobody |
| **GLASS OUT OF WHITE** | Horn-backed bow at close range from nowhere, black glass that shatters in the wound |
| **QATUQ TAKE YOU** | Qatuq is the cold that takes the unready. A curse, and also a Keld with a dead fire-keeper's name walking a host in a slow circle until the wind turns |
| **THE IRON FIST** | Aksel's own. A fist of solid iron and his whole weight. It broke ribs through mail at the Skeldmere, and it was full of old cracks, which is how it ended |

## Culture

**Language.** Northern Common for strangers, and for themselves the old tongue of the carried-name peoples, in which most insults are about bowls: *hulluk* (wet wood), *numra* (an empty bowl held out).

**Rites.**

* **The Skin at the Door.** Sour milk in a horn cup. Refuse it and you are skin-refuser, said once, and then there is a fight.
* **The Waiting.** Lodge lamps turned down, the dead given to the sky or the shelf, and at the next Gather to a child.
* **Setting It Down.** When a name's debts are finally paid, the one who carries it can say so at the fires. Nobody can remember the last time.
* **The Boots.** A certain kind of Moto goes out on the shelf to practice Domains, and the Keld find them some weeks later, and return their boots.

**Marriage, feud and debt.** Marriages are arranged, in part, by whose names can stand to be in one lodge. Thornwall tried to take the whale-oil by force, and the song is long. Old Harald has been too old to sit a horse since Ulf Stark's father was a boy, and he moved those riders in the second year of the war, and if you find that hard to reconcile you should ask how many men have been Old Harald. His grandson Aksel won eleven fights, lost the twelfth to Muken Moto on the Skeldmere and was spared, and the Keld follow that king for as long as he stays worth following.

About forty Haldorssons walked off the Seat's square alive. That left several thousand names with nobody to carry them, and at the Gather after the war every clan on the ice stood up and took them. There are Sennet children now carrying the men their fathers killed. The Sennet elders call this an obligation, and the Haldorssons call it an occupation. It is the first new thing to happen in that feud in two hundred years.

**What the commons get.** There are no commons and no coin. Everyone has a name older than any lord's, and everyone's death will be sat with. They are the only people I have studied who do not seem afraid of being forgotten, and I think that is what makes them so hard to frighten about anything else.

## Limitations and Soul-Costs

### [Curse] The Wound That Comes With It — How the Name Died

* Each time the name stands its death-wound opens a little, and a little more each year. The old carriers wear rags where a blade never went, and there are lodges full of people coughing up water from a sea they have never seen.

### [Curse] Gone to the Name — Who Comes Back

* Below a tenth of reserve a Keld's Core has someone else to ask. Some do not come back: the girl is gone and the old woman walks around in her, asking after people. The lodge feeds her and keeps a Waiting for the girl.

*It is a hard thing to sit a Waiting with the deceased pouring your tea.*

### [Curse] It Is the Same Feud — Nobody Gets to Stop

* A carried debt does not fade, and a killing stays fresh for two hundred years because the people involved keep being born. Peace on the Reach is a matter of which children get which names.

*Muken Moto broke four thousand riders with a song and an old crack, and he did not have to invent the crack.*

## How They Stand Against the Three Paths

**Body.** On the ice a Body fighter is losing to the weather when he arrives. Off it, with their backs to a stair, the Keld die as well as anyone. **Spirit.** The real danger: two people in one Core, one of them dead, and a Spirit fighter can get between them. **Fate.** A very large unpaid bill on the Layer is a thing to lean on, and they are nearly alone in the north in feeling it coming.

## Final Definition

House Keld are the people who decided no one goes unsat-with. They took the bare ledger every soul leaves behind and gave it to their children, and it made them unkillably hard and unable to stop any quarrel their grandmothers began. A nineteen-year-old with a mouthful of fat will tell you she is ninety and put a lance through your throat. You should have asked her how she died.

*"How old are you?" "Which of us?"*

Moriyasu Tenrai, a tax captain of the gold line, came out to the Whalebone Gather in the bright week of 689 to collect, and he came with sixty lances, a fine coat of gold-trimmed black and a duelist's name he had earned fairly on the plateau. He had it read out at the fires that the Reach owed the Seat for eleven years. An old sled-woman told him he should talk to Thora. So they brought him Thora, under a green sky with the blue domes of the palace throwing their long shadows over the racing course, and she was nineteen years old, low and wide and dense, with blue on her chin and a mouth full of seal fat, and when he asked how old she was to be speaking for the fires, she told him ninety.

He laughed. He told her he had been warned about that. She finished chewing, and looked for a while at how he had set his feet, and told him he had his grandfather's left foot. It was a good foot, she said. She had thought so at the time.

There was a ring cleared on the ice by then, with bets painted on a whale's jaw. Moriyasu drew. He was very good, the blade gold in that low light and his aura coming up behind it, and Thora Sennet stood with a birch lance in a girl's two hands and took her breath in, and four hundred people on the ice stopped talking, and she said it. She did not shout. **"The name stands."**

Nothing happened to her body, and everyone who was there says the same. It stayed nineteen and short. But she settled into it the way an old woman settles into a chair she has owned for sixty years. Her shoulders dropped and her weight went onto a bad hip she did not have. Her aura rose the color of tallow smoke, in the shape of someone a head taller, with a long braid that was not hers. When he came in on the feint his family was known for, low to the left and then up, the one that had killed the first Thora Sennet on the Teeth seventy-one years before, she was already standing where that thrust was not going to be. She said in a cracked old voice, *"I've had a long time to think about that one, boy,"* and put the lance through his throat. Blood came out of her own left side a moment later, through a coat with no hole in it, from under the third rib, at the place a Tenrai sword had gone in before her mother's mother was born. It does that. She sat down on the ice, got her breath back, and asked somebody to get her some fat. The sixty lances went home and the Reach kept the silver. Ask the second Thora how old she is now, and she will tell you ninety-seven. She is twenty-six.

House Keld is the smallest house, the hardest people and the most conditionally loyal. They live in conditions that would kill anyone else inside a week, and the fighting is an afterthought beside the surviving. The south counts them as a Banner House because the south likes to count, and they swear to a person and never to a letter. What they hold that everyone needs is whale-oil, ivory, and a tundra nobody else can use. What they hold that nobody else wants is a whole people's worth of the dead, in working order.

## Origin of the House

The Keld say that their first ancestor came out onto the ice alone, in the first winter after the sky got cold, and was found dead by the second. The second looked at the body for a while, took her name so that somebody would still be using it, and made it through. Every Keld since has been a continuation of that sum. They had a word for one of the dead left without the Waiting, a corpse nobody sat with, long before they had a word for a lord: *kiilut*, the worst word they own. A name with nobody to carry it is the same thing to them. The whole of their society can be read as a people refusing to let anyone become that.

Then there is the palace, which they call the Kiilut House. It stood on the snowfield before their oldest song. Its third dome has no agreed number of sides, and Measurewright Ansell came out of it in 694 by the wrong door with his beard at his belt. The Guild calls it a Spirit War relic. The Keld do not say what it is. They say a name nobody carries goes somewhere, and they pitch their camp every year in the shadow of the most beautiful building in the world, and they will not sleep inside it, and they do not joke about it.

## The House on the Crystal

| Layer | Plane | The Keld form |
| :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **Aether Shell** | Physical | Dense, low and economical. It runs on almost nothing, as a lamp runs on fat, and gives out very late |
| **Essence Core** | Soul | Shared, in a way. It is the bearer's own and it has a lodger, who cannot learn anything new but has very firm opinions |
| **Attraction Layer** | Fate | Wider than any commoner's has any business being, because it carries a whole other life's worth of what was owed and promised |

**Path.** The soul leans Body, as any soul does that has hauled its own sled since it could walk, with a smaller lean toward Fate from the name on the Layer and the fires they swear at. **Ladders.** Aether climbs in the fighters. **Grip.** Theirs is the Layer, and it is a very small one. Taking a name at the Gather opens **Thread at I, Touched**, long before Glory, at an age when other children are reading their sums. A Touched Keld knows the ice is about to go and the bridge is a bad idea, and on a very rare day can lean on that for most of a reserve and a nosebleed. Their word for a soul's weight is the same as for a coming storm. All the centuries of carried names have never taken anyone past II on blood alone, and the few Keld who read higher have spent decades on it and paid. **Whale-Caller** is the class that runs thick at the Gather, with later forms the Long Water and the Call Comes Back, and they will not discuss the third with strangers.

## The Carried Name

**In a breath:** when a soul dies its Layer leaves a Name behind, and the Keld pick these up and put them on their children, so a Keld fighter has one dead person's whole life standing behind her eyes, debts and all.

**What it is.** At death, every soul comes apart by its own three roads: the Core goes to Echo, the Shell goes to residue through the body, and the Layer goes to Name, a shape left on the Plane of Fate of everything that person owed, promised and was owed. Everywhere else in the world this is a matter for theologians. It lingers, and it thins, and nobody does anything with it. The Keld call out the names of their dead at the Gather and give them to children, freely on both sides, before witnesses, and the child's Layer takes it up. They call what she then has her *sauniq*, a word that also means bone. It is a vow in form, and it spends only the soul's own share.

Rebirth moves pattern and never a person, and the Keld know that better than anyone. The dead woman is dead, and nobody in the lodge is confused about it. They sat with her. What the child has is what the dead woman's life left written on her Layer, and that turns out to be a good deal: how she held a lance, where she liked to camp, who she would not forgive. It comes up as dreams and habits, as knowing how the wind will go on a road you have never ridden. It is why a Keld will tell you she is ninety and not be joking.

**What it costs.** You take the debts with the skills. A Keld who carries a name that died owing a shift on the rim will ride south on her own, on a small shaggy horse, in that heat, and present herself at the nearest fort, and leave on the day the dead person would have gone home. A name that died in a feud is still in it. This is the plain reason the Sennet and the Haldorssons have been killing each other over the Teeth since their grandfathers' grandfathers: nobody in that quarrel has ever really been allowed to stop, because the same people keep being in it. Muken Moto read that line through the host at the Skeldmere as a crack waiting to run, and the Sennet grays turned their lances on the Haldorsson dark horses the moment the Iron Fist was busy.

**Its limit.** It holds a pattern and has no way of learning. The name knows nothing past the day its owner died. Its power is the bearer's own: a Stage II girl with a great warrior's name has a great warrior's habits in a Stage II body, and they are very likely to get her killed. A name's knowledge of a road, a fighter's family or a fort is seventy years out of date, and everyone in the lodge knows it. A name left with nobody to take it goes where they do not say.

**Who beats it.** Find out how the name died. This is the fair counter and the south has been paying for it for a century, in silver, over seal fat: a Keld fighter's death-wound is a flinch that opens under The Name Stands, on a side you can learn. Bring something the name has never met, a new school or a new engine, or a Tenrai captain whose family has changed its feint. Set the name's old debts against the bearer's living ones and watch her freeze. Keep her fighting past a tenth of reserve, where any soul's Core frays, and the wrong one may come back up. Reading souls is the deepest answer: a Yukari who can say what a thing truly is can say it to a Keld, in front of everyone, and the lodger hears it.

### The Kiilut House

I have asked every elder who would sit with me what the palace has to do with the names, and I have been fed well and told nothing. Here is what can be observed. The Calling is held nowhere else, always at the edge of the palace's shadow, always late in the day, as the shadow lengthens toward the crowd. The unclaimed names are called in order of how long they have gone unclaimed, and the elders are visibly in a hurry. Every Keld you ask will say that a thing nobody sat with is bad manners on a very large scale. It may hold Living Armor, and crews who cross the covered bridge do not come back onto any roll. And a Keld hedge-worker, drunk, once told Ansell's old assistant that the third dome has as many sides as there are names nobody came for, and that this is why they keep the count down. I report this as gossip. The Keld would prefer that I did not report it.

## Physical Appearance and Dress

**Stature and build.** Low, wide and dense, with barrel chests, short powerful legs and necks like tree-stumps, packed down until nothing is left on them but what the cold allows. **Coloring.** Broad flat faces wind-burned deep red-brown across the cheekbones, with narrow black eyes set deep against the glare and black coarse hair braided close in tight rows. **Marks.** Three thin blue lines tattooed down the chin from the lower lip mark someone who has killed in defense of the herds, and a fourth beside them, thicker, marks a chief. Walrus-ivory rings go into the braids, one for each fight won. **Voice.** Whichever one it is today. Long throat-songs in the dark months. They rise from a floor all at once with no hand on it.

**The look at fifty paces.** Nothing, in a whiteout, until the white cloth comes down. Otherwise short men on short horses that will not founder and can live a week on lichen. Each rider wears two coats, reindeer hair turned in and sealskin turned out, a wolverine hood that takes no frost, and horn lamellar sewn between them. Arrowheads are black glass and bone, because iron cracks in the deep cold. The lance is birch with a long leaf head, and every saddle has its twist of white cloth. Faces are painted in blue and red shelf-clay for joy and for war. They flex, in a country with no coin, by who sewed it: a lodge belongs to the woman who sewed it, and a coat from a famous hand is worth a team of dogs. A Clearbeam beryl as long as a man gets dragged south on a sled and sold cheap. The most famous piece on the Reach was Aksel Keld's iron hand, black iron and walrus tusk and sinew, strapped to a stump a Tenrai sword left at thirteen and closed by a cord at the shoulder, into a fist the size of a man's skull. The poorest Keld alive has the same two coats and a small ivory tooth at the throat, with a dead person's mark on it, which is what she would save from a fire.

## Lineage Traits

### [Trait] The Baseline — Conditions That Kill the Enemy First

* Near-immune to cold, hunger and bad air, and a whole body built to spend nothing it does not have to.
* A fighter raised on the Reach arrives at a battle having survived what would kill you before it started.
* Heat is another matter, and the rim keeps a list.

*They sit on bare packed snow. A southerner who needs a stool will get one and will never be asked anywhere again.*

### [Trait] Sauniq — The Bone Under the Name

* The carried name, working all the time at a low level: habits, knowledge of ground, old opinions.
* It makes a young Keld far steadier than she should be, and it means you may get a very strange answer to *how old are you*.

*Aksel Keld at eighteen sat his horse like a man of forty. Nobody on the ice has told me whose forty it was.*

### [Trait] The Worst of It — Meat-Sharing

* A hunter's kill is shared by a custom so strict that the one who killed it often gets the worst share. This is a law of the Reach, and it has gone into the blood as a very high tolerance for going without while watching someone else eat.
* A person who breaks trust is left behind at the next move, and nobody has to carry out that sentence.

*Ashmore's Thin Weeks has been tried on them. Their report was that it felt like Tuesday.*

### [Trait] Weather-Weight — Touched

* The grip above, in daily use. They know when the storm is coming, when the plate will go and when the herd will turn, a little before it is reasonable to.
* It is Thread I, and a true lean on it costs what it costs anyone: most of a reserve and it often fails.

*They have one word for a soul's weight and a storm on its way. They have a low opinion of the southern argument about it.*

## Doctrine and Arts

Few as they are, they are worth three of anyone else's. They ride in the **long line**, which moves across open snow like weather, and in a wedge when something needs breaking. A Keld rider is taught, in the lifting, the opposite of what a Keld child is taught. The child sits down and waits. The rider goes.

## Culture

**Language.** Northern Common for strangers. For themselves the old tongue of the carried-name peoples, in which most insults are about bowls. *Qatuq, kiilut, hulluk* (wet wood), *numra* (an empty bowl held out). A Keld is never the first holder of her name and her grammar assumes it.

**Rites.**

* **The Calling.** Above.
* **The Chin.** Three lines for a killing in defense of the herds. The fourth for a chief, thicker, and it can be taken back by the fires in a way that is not gentle.
* **The Skin at the Door.** Sour milk in a horn cup. Refuse it and you are skin-refuser, which is said once and then there is a fight.
* **The Waiting.** The lodge lamps turned down, the dead given to the sky or the shelf. A Keld sits with her dead and then, at the next Gather, gives them to a child.
* **Setting It Down.** When a name's debts are finally paid, the one who carries it can say so at the fires. Nobody can remember the last time. It is held to be the best death there is, and it does not happen to the bearer.
* **The Boots.** A certain kind of Moto practitioner goes out on the shelf to practice Domains, and the Keld find them some weeks later, and return their boots.

**Marriage, feud and debt.** They marry out at the Gather with great noise. Marriages are arranged, in part, by whose names can stand to be in the same lodge. Sennet and Haldorsson are the feud, and it has no end by design. Thornwall tried to take the whale-oil by force, and the song is long. Old Harald has been too old to sit a horse since Ulf Stark's father was a boy, and he moved four hundred riders through a whiteout in the second year of the war, and if you find those two things hard to reconcile you should ask how many men have been Old Harald. The fires will not say. His grandson Aksel won eleven fights, lost the twelfth to Muken Moto on the Skeldmere and was spared, and now the Keld follow that king for as long as he stays worth following. That is the only term anyone has ever got. About forty Haldorssons walked off the Seat's square alive. The rest had nowhere to go and did not know how to kneel, and that has left several thousand Haldorsson names with nobody to carry them. At the Gather after the war every clan on the ice stood up and took them. There are Sennet children now carrying the men their fathers killed. The Sennet elders say this is an obligation. The Haldorssons say it is an occupation. It is the first new thing to happen in that feud in two hundred years.

**What the commons get.** There are no commons. There is meat-sharing, labor-debt and salt, and no coin on the Reach. Every person alive has a name older than any lord's, every person's death will be sat with, and every one of them, when they die, will be given to a child at the next Gather. They are the only people I have studied who do not seem afraid of being forgotten, and I have come to think that is what makes them so hard to frighten about anything else.

## Limitations and Soul-Costs

### [Curse] The Wound That Comes With It — How the Name Died

* Each time the name stands, its death-wound opens in the bearer, a little, and a little more with each year.
* The old carriers wear rags under their coats where a blade never went. A burned name, a drowned name: there are lodges full of people coughing up water from a sea they have never seen.

*They are proud of these. A Keld with nothing to bleed from has been carrying somebody who died of nothing much, and she knows it.*

### [Curse] Gone to the Name — Who Comes Back

* Below a tenth of reserve the Core frays, for anyone. A Keld's Core has someone else to ask.
* Some do not come back. The girl is gone and the old woman is walking around in her with no idea how much time has passed, asking after people. The lodge feeds her, and keeps a Waiting for the girl.

*It is a hard thing to sit a Waiting with the deceased pouring your tea.*

### [Curse] It Is the Same Feud — Nobody Gets to Stop

* A carried debt does not fade. A killing is still fresh after two hundred years because the people involved keep being born.
* Peace on the Reach is a matter of arranging which children get which names, and everyone knows who is doing that arranging, and everyone hates it.

*Muken Moto broke four thousand riders with a song and an old crack, and he did not have to invent the crack.*

## How They Stand Against the Three Paths

**Body.** The enemy they understand. On the ice a Body fighter is already losing to the weather when they arrive, and off it, in a square with their backs to a stair, the Keld die as well as anyone. **Spirit.** The real danger. There are two people in that Core, one of them is dead, and a Spirit fighter can get in between them, though a Keld's Resilience makes it slower than you would think. **Fate.** They hold a very large unpaid bill on their Layer, which is a thing a Fate user can lean on. They are also, nearly alone in the north, able to feel it coming.

## Final Definition

House Keld are the people who decided that no one goes unsat-with. They took the thing every soul leaves behind, the bare ledger of what it owed, and they gave it to their children, and it made them unkillably hard and unable to stop any quarrel their grandmothers began. They live under a palace nobody built and will not explain it. A nineteen-year-old with a mouthful of fat will tell you she is ninety and put a lance through your throat. You should have asked her how she died.

*"How old are you?" "Which of us?"*

# House Marrick

## Overview

| | |
|---|---|
| **Type** | Warlord house of the Ochre Fields · the Wall for Hire · an elected captaincy over a country of spear companies |
| **Origin** | The Long Fields, levy country of the old Empire of Sum-gol |
| **Founder** | Old Marget Marrick is who they name; the house is older and has lost the receipts |
| **Seat** | Hammerford |
| **Anchors** | The Rank · the contract · the Widows' Chest |
| **Signature** | **The Shoulder:** sign one contract, stand in one file, and a blow that lands on one of you lands on all sixteen |
| **Maxim** | *Shoulder to Shoulder* |

Everyone in the Midmarch can tell you about the Red Bell of Tarn. She was Stage X, Realization, a tall woman in lacquered plate who had taken two castles alone, and at Wick Bottom in the summer of 691 she came down onto the left corner of the Sixth Blacks out of a clear sky, with a fist wrapped in enough force to stave in a gatehouse. The man on that corner was a sergeant named Joost Hallam, Stage IV, who had a wife in Musterfield and a bad back. What the field saw was Joost Hallam staying on his feet, with his teeth showing. And behind him, in file, a hard and terrible noise went back through fifteen men from front to rear, and every one of them, the whole sixteen, at the same instant, broke his left collarbone.

None of them fell. They could not; each was jammed against the next, shoulders locked, with a drum going, and the force had gone down through thirty-two boots and into the barley stubble, which was found afterward to have sunk a foot in a line as straight as a rule. She stood there with her arm out, looking honestly surprised. Sixteen spears came level, on the drum, in fifteen right hands and one left. It would not have mattered a great deal at Stage X, except that the crossbows on the wing had been waiting all morning for a target that could not be mistaken for a friend.

The Sixth Blacks held that line until sundown, singing, with sixteen broken collarbones in the left file alone. The contract expired at sundown. At the last edge of the light the drum stopped, and a clerk called the hour, and the entire file sat down in the stubble at once and began to scream. Sixteen men. You could hear it in the next parish. Gerrit Marrick was there, on the other side as it happens, and he tells that story at every Moot, and he always ends it the same way. *"You stand in it because the man on your left is standing in it, lad. Brave's got nothing to do with it."*

The north fights in knots and wedges and long Keld lines. The south fights in a wall, and that wall was Marrick's first. What they hold that everyone needs is the only infantry in the Inner World that will stand where it is put, to the hour, for anyone who pays, and fight a Stage X on a sergeant's wages. What they want is a long peace with a lot of small wars in it.

## Origin of the House

The Long Fields are barley and spearmen, and for three hundred years they were the foot of the Empire of Sum-gol, whose levies went where the Mahou told them. Nobody thought that made them a power. When the Emperor Rokju called the Marrick from his balcony in 602, the company in the square looked up at the sun, saw it was noon, and grounded arms, because the contract had that minute run out. The Fields keep it as a holiday. It was the first time anyone had noticed that a wall of men with a clause in it was a thing that could be owned by nobody.

The family has provided the elected Captain often enough that the name stuck to the country. **Old Marget Marrick** made it what it is: in 631, sitting on a barley sack on Dob Harkness's broken cart at the ford, she auctioned the whole levy for a day and a night, accepted both bids, and split it down the middle. Both armies had Marrick companies at their center at Sorley Bridge. Nine thousand went into the ground. The Marrick dead were paid to the end of the season, to the widows, on the nail, and both kings hired again next spring. Her clause is why the cart stays broken, and every recruit touches the tailboard on the morning of her first muster.

## The House on the Crystal

A Soul Crystal is the soul itself, in three layers: the **Aether Shell**, where Aether (mana) comes in and force goes out; the **Essence Core**, where it becomes Essence (spiritual energy); and the **Attraction Layer**, a soul's own share of Fate. On the Fields you will meet many with all three asleep.

| Layer | Plane | The Marrick form |
|---|---|---|
| **Aether Shell** | Physical | Most of the rank are unwoken and sealed. A corner is an ordinary woken fighter, and what is unusual is how far the coat goes |
| **Essence Core** | Soul | Solid and unlettered. Marrick ghosts are very well behaved |
| **Attraction Layer** | Fate | One line on the window, the contract, and a kept word that runs for a season. It spends the soul's own share and pulls nothing down |

**Path.** Body, and they would not know the word. A forty-year sergeant has leaned on the Aether Shell through his whole life, and he has very likely never been told. Any Marrick who keeps vows long enough reads Thread I or II from that alone, and that is the top of it. Nobody on the Fields has ever moved the odds on anything. They bring more spears.

**No eye, no grip, no blood.** The Marrick family has none of it, and their signature would work no better for a Marrick than for a Hobgoblin rope-seller. Anyone who signs gets the Shoulder. It is what the Long Fields sell, and the reason other armies who have copied the drill have never got the wall.

| Standing | Stage | What they do |
|---|---|---|
| **File** | Unwoken to II | Stand, push, level the spear. The great majority |
| **Sergeant** | III to V | A first coat and the file's whole weight behind it |
| **Corner** | IV to VII | Someone who can take a blow, on the outside angle of a rank, "shining for the whole file" |
| **Captain** | VII and up | Rare, expensive, and listed on the wall of the Hiring Hall |

Aether Class runs low and is generally slow to climb, since most of what a corner has ever done with her Shell is hold still. A Muridic sergeant with a bad back is the backbone of southern warfare.

## The Shoulder

**What it is.** A rank under one contract, touching at the shoulder, shares its load. Blows that land on one man spread down the file and go into the ground. It can be broken, and it hurts, and every person in that file feels every blow. That is what you are buying.

**How it works, in plain words.** Lock sixteen men front to back, each pressed to the next, each with a leg braced back, and now the blow has to move sixteen men and everything they are standing on. That much is plain mechanics, which scholars call spreading an impulse over a greater mass, and by the rear rank the blow is a shove.

What the Fields added is the coat. A corner's reinforcement, the coat of aura a woken fighter wears, can extend into held gear. In a signed rank it goes further. The contract is a small vow, one anybody can make, and it is written on the same terms for every soul in the company, so each signer sees the same line on the window (the soul's private report of its numbers). Those lines tune to one another like a set of bells cast together. With shoulders touching, the corner's coat runs along the file, thin as paint on the back row, and the unwoken get a share of something they could never make for themselves. It lets the force through without tearing anything too fast, and it goes on down to the boots and into the ground. The window shows it plainly, the moment you step in.

```
[Contract: Sixth Blacks · to the Lord of Oxley]
[Term: until sundown, August 19]
[Rank: Left file, 4th of 16 · Corner: Hallam, J.]
[Sharing: load, coat, a little of the gain]
```

**The forms.** They are drilled by number, like everything on the Fields.

| Form | How many | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| **The Pair** | Two | First lesson. Two recruits shoulder to shoulder, and a sergeant hits one of them with a plank until both can feel it |
| **The File** | Sixteen | The working unit. A corner can spread a very bad blow down a file, at the price of a broken bone apiece |
| **The Square** | A company | Files locked side by side. The ground under a good square has a settled look when they leave |
| **The Wall** | Several companies | The drums set the time, and what runs through it is mainly nerve: the coat is thin that far out. A Wall will take a charge, and a good many other things it has no business taking |

**What it costs.** Everyone pays. A blow that would have killed one person now hurts sixteen, and they all keep the injury. Whole Marrick files share the same limp. Essence in the coat comes from the corner alone, at a fast rate, and a corner who has shone for a whole file through an afternoon is gray, and shaking, and sometimes dead on her feet.

**Its limit.** The contract ends. That is the whole of the law and it has no appeal. At the hour named, the line goes off the window and the bells stop ringing, and it is just a lot of tired people standing close together, feeling every bone they broke at the same moment. So when a Marrick company grounds spears at noon, it is no point of pride. They have nothing left to stand in. Nobody touches a company walking off, because every commander in the South knows how many spears he would be offered afterward. Before the hour named, too, the Shoulder is not there. And it needs shoulders: a broken file is sixteen ordinary people, and most of them have never been anything else.

**Who beats it.** Do not hit it from the front. A wall sends force to the ground, and force from two sides at once has nowhere to go: surround a square and squeeze, and it kills from the inside, standing up. Ride around and shoot, as the Yan have done without rancor for three centuries, since an arrow in the throat is not a blow on the rank. Cut down the drummer or the corner. Go past the Shell entirely, with a working on the soul, since no one ever shared a soul's hurt with a shoulder. Fire over the top does nicely. Or look up the term on the Hiring Hall's wall, which is posted, and arrange your battle for the next morning.

## Physical Appearance and Dress

They come from every sort of family and look like it, all of them with a thick neck, a hard callus on the left shoulder and a stoop that the sergeants have spent years failing to correct. They stand very close to each other without noticing it, and stand too close to you as well. Old companies have a shared gait, and a stranger can tell which by the way they favor the same side. Scars run in lines. Voices are hoarse, and they have a habit of talking from the corner of the mouth, for use in the last minute before contact. Gerrit Marrick is sixty, loud, scarred, very funny and missing an ear, and he looks like a building someone has tried to knock down several times without getting a clear result.

Ochre and black in squares are the colors, a patch on every coat. Each company adds its own colors inside the squares and a song with disgusting verses, and you can tell them at a mile by the drum. Their gear is the point of pride, and it is entirely practical: a long ash spear shaved true by eye, a big-rimmed shield with the company's mark, a padded jack, a kettle helm and very good boots, Uncommon and well worn. A captain has plain black half-plate with an ochre sash and every buckle mended. At fifty paces it is just a straight line of spear-points, with shadows falling across the fields like a fence that goes on forever. A poor cousin looks exactly the same as a rich one, on purpose.

## Lineage Traits

### [Trait] The Corner — Shining for the Whole File

A fighter who can take a blow, at the outside angle of the rank, with her coat running down the line.

- Extends reinforcement through touching bodies under one contract, thinning fast with every rank.
- Lets a Stage IV hold ground that a Stage VII would like to walk through, once.
- Spends the corner's reserve at a frightening rate, and the tells show on her first.

*They say "good corner" on the Fields the way other people say "good wife," and they mean about the same thing.*

### [Trait] No Sight, No Fear — What a Rank Does to a Mind

- In the middle of a file you can see the back in front and a shoulder each side. It turns out to be a mercy.
- Pressure lands on a rank as on a crowd, but a file shares that as well, thinly: dread spread over sixteen is sixteen men feeling sick. The Stage gap still shows, and they still drop, in order.
- Morale in a good file runs well past anything an ordinary person would stand for.

*A rank stands because it cannot trust itself to stand anywhere else.*

### [Trait] A Little of the Gain — Crew on the Window

- A real crew bond shows on the sheet. Marrick files share a small part of each other's gain, and veterans of one file have very similar skills.
- Spear runs to a high Grade on some very ordinary people. Fifty years at one thing can give a soldier an alarming sheet with no Stage to speak of.

*A Long Fields sergeant past his marching years can name his price as a drillmaster anywhere between Kessen ford and the sea.*

## Doctrine and Arts

**Keep the line, finish the term.** They have no flourish, on purpose. Their doctrine is to arrive, stand, thrust, and go home at the agreed hour. Against high Stages it is older than anyone's grandmother: the rank opens, lets the great one in, closes, and every crossbow in reach looses at the one target that cannot be mistaken for a friend. A bolt costs a few coppers. They consider the exchange fair. Their shouts are in plain Long Fields, and they are all orders.

| Move | Meaning | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| **SHOULDER!** | Lock on | The first order of any fight. Every rank closes up, and the window shows the line |
| **LEVEL!** | Spears down | Sixteen ranks of ash come down as one, on the drum, with a noise like a building falling over when they start to walk |
| **LET HIM IN!** | The hole | A planned gap opens in front of a high-Stage fighter, closes behind her, and the crossbow companies start work |
| **TAKE IT!** | A corner's call | She has seen it coming, and the coat goes all down the file in one breath. Sixteen men brace, and pay what it costs in bone |
| **WHAT'S HE PAID?** | Parley | A sergeant walks out under a green branch to ask the terrible stranger what he is getting for this, and whether he would consider more. It has ended more sieges than the spear has |
| **IT ISN'T NOON!** | Contract's not started | Shouted across a burning town to the people screaming at you for help. Marrick is not proud of this and will not stop |

## Culture

They speak Long Fields, plain English and Dutch in the names and the swearing, and a good deal of the talk is out of the side of the mouth.

- **The Tailboard.** On the morning of her first muster, every recruit touches the broken cart at Hammerford's ford. The Guild of Wrights have repaired it nine times, and each new axle has been sawn through at the proper angle by a man with a sworn drawing. Dob Harkness's descendants still draw the rent.
- **The Signing.** The contract is read aloud to the company in a field, all the way through, and each soul signs or marks it. Anybody may leave before the last mark. The line comes up on every window at the same moment, and new recruits generally yell.
- **The Noon Holiday.** The noon of 602, kept every year. Everyone stands in the square, looks up at the sun, and when it is noon they put their drinks down. Then they pick them up.
- **The Widows' Chest.** A cut of every contract goes into a chest in the citadel, and a Marrick dead is paid to the end of the season, on the nail. Its keeper is the most frightening person in the country.

**Who they marry, fight and owe.** Everyone marries into the companies and everyone goes to all the funerals, because each family pays rent in sons and daughters, a set number to each muster, or in silver at a rate the Moot fixes. They have fought on both sides of every war, and a Marrick company will fight another to the hour and share a jug with whoever is left. The Yan brawl with them annually at Mashi, and neither has seen what a real war with the other would pay. The Holy Sea audits them, and Lambert officers are the only outsiders to get an honest head-count from a sergeant, as they would get it anyway. Commoners get a place, a pay day, a song with disgusting verses, and a country where any widow can walk up to the citadel and be paid.

## Limitations and Soul-Costs

### [Curse] The File Bleeds — Everyone Keeps the Wound

Being kept alive by sixteen people means owing sixteen people, in bone. There are old companies at Hotwell where the whole file walks with the one left leg. It is good for morale right up until the moment it is not, and whole villages on the Fields are crippled in the same place.

### [Curse] The Term — It Isn't Noon

They cannot be there early, and they cannot stay. A company will watch a town die at a quarter to, and a company will walk off a field with the job half done, because the strength stops with the hour. It stings every Marrick who has ever stood with her arms folded in front of someone she could have saved. There is a wyrm on the Kessen road wearing nine hundred of their spears, called Old Pincushion, and it is a joke because it has to be.

### [Curse] The Paid Dead — A Contract Outlasts the Man

Nine thousand men went into one trench at Sorley Bridge, on both sides, each one paid up to the end of a season. In the generation after, a Mortalis seep pooled under them (Mortalis being the Wellspring of slow death), and the ghouls that come up out of the Long Barrow come in companies, in step. Nobody on the Fields will say quite what is keeping them in step. Their children go down each spring to fight them.

## How They Stand Against the Three Paths

**Body.** They were made for this, as far as a good Stage gap lets them be. A Body master high enough to take a wall apart as a thing, by law, is another matter, and when one arrives they send a sergeant to ask what he is getting paid.

**Spirit.** Very bad. A working goes past every shoulder in the file and lands in the one soul it was meant for, and the rank cannot share that. Their answer is crossbows and a very strict rule about the first person you see standing on a wagon with her hands up.

**Fate.** A bad morning. A Fate fighter can move what a wall depends on, and a wall depends on rather a lot: a strap, a drum, whether the pay wagon arrived. A well-kept rank offers few straps to give.

## Final Definition

House Marrick sells the one thing in the world that a Stage X cannot buy: ordinary people standing next to each other. They have made a magic out of a shoulder, a drum and a signed sheet of paper, and it works exactly as long as the paper says. They have held a line against things out of nightmare for the price of a good pair of boots, and they have walked away from burning towns at the stroke of noon, and they have never told anyone that those two are different. On the Long Fields, the barley comes in and then the spears do, and a broken cart stands by the ford with its tailboard hanging, and if anyone asks them what it is for, they say the hire stands as long as it stands.

*Shoulder to shoulder, to the hour, and paid.*

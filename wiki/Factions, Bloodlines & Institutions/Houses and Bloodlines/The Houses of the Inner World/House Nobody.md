The Hearing of the Bell was held at Goodmorrow on the salt road in the autumn of 694, in a tithe barn hung with Accord blue, because a licensed returner named Bart Cray had finally caught someone. He had lost eleven men and forty good collars on the eaves of the Russet Forest that season, and he had a small Catkin called Tiss, in a coat of six colors with a stripe of red dye down her nose, chained to a post. The Accord had sent an examining clerk, and Cray had paid out of his own pocket for a truth-bell from Alabaster, worked to ring true on an oath and crack on a lie, the sort that has hanged bishops. A Scalebond notary sat in to witness. The whole town came, with pies. Tiss was sworn, and she swore very readily, and the clerk asked her who had cut the collars. "Nobody cut them," she said. The bell rang clear as a morning. He asked who had killed Cray's men. "Nobody killed them," she said, and it rang again, sweet and full, and somebody at the back began to laugh. He asked who had taught her to answer so. He asked who her king was, who was hiding the runaways, who had stolen the mules. It rang true at every answer, and by the mules the whole barn was chanting it along with her and beating on the benches, and the bell, a holy object of great age, was ringing without being asked. The clerk turned in despair to the notary, who was gold-scaled and in a very good coat, and who said only that she found the proceeding clarifying.

There was nothing to hold her on. They struck off the chain, and that was when the barn went quiet, because a small gray man had got up from the third bench with a folded paper, walking toward her with a pleasant expression and holding it out. "This is yours, miss, I think," he said. Tiss put both her hands behind her back. She went out of that barn backward with her hands clasped behind her, and the gray man followed her at a walking pace, smiling, for nine leagues. They say at Greyfang that she went into the ford backward, and that her witness carried her the rest of the way, not touching her hands.

## Who They Are

There is no blood in this house, and that is the entire idea of it. **House Nobody** is anyone who has arrived and spoken. They want the correction never served, the Long Way open, and every last pen gate off its pins. What they hold that everyone needs is forged papers, knife-hands, Bearkin lintels and the plain example of a hole in the law of the world, about the width of one person, that has stayed open for nearly fifty years.

An old line passes on an eye, a grip on one of the soul's ladders, a lean in the stats and an easy road to certain Wellsprings. The Crown's answer to the obvious question is painted on a wall at Nightveil Hollow, with a drawing.

| What a House gives | House Nobody |
|---|---|
| **The eye** | Nobody's |
| **The grip** | Nobody has one |
| **The stat lean** | Whatever you walked in with |
| **Lens affinities** | Ask your mother, if you can find her |

What passes down instead is one sentence, a witness, and a joke with a very sharp edge.

## Origin of the House

The people are older than the name. For centuries what they had was a forest, a heap of cut iron at the foot of a stolen fortress, and a rite: a person who arrives speaks her soul-name aloud at the Heap, and whoever is nearest answers *Ashe*. The fox-spirits call this the Name-Keeping, and the first of them through the Long Way left it behind as the only thing she had to pay with.

Then, in the autumn of 668, Provision Assessor Pell Wickham set up his table in the old sorting yard, and four thousand one hundred people gave their name and their owner as Nobody. He swore to record what was declared, and he sealed it under the Accord's green seal. What neither he nor the town understood until much later was that an Accord seal is a real oath, sworn by an institution that has spent centuries making sure its seals bear load, and that an oath is small Fate.

## The House on the Crystal

The Soul Crystal is the soul, refining Aether (mana) into Essence (spiritual energy). The Attraction Layer is the part that faces Fate, and a person's Name sits there. Each person keeps the layers of her own people, and the house adds one strange feature to the third.

| Layer | Catkin | Wolfkin | Bearkin |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Aether Shell** | *The lie*: no aura worth seeing. A reservoir of stored feeling, burst through one step | *The pelt*: the Shell runs with the moon and with the pack | *The weather*: vast, slow and not your business |
| **Essence Core** | Holds the reservoir and counts it before any act | Partly lives in the others. A Wolfkin alone goes "too quiet for too long" | Old-growth heavy. Cannot take in a wall that is not there |
| **Attraction Layer** | **In all three, for anyone who has spoken at the Heap:** the Name is held by a witness and no longer reads on the sheet | | |

**Path lean.** All three peoples lean Body, then Spirit, as a tendency of the untrained soul, and Aether climbs for most. **Thread** is the surprise. Thread Class opens only by blood, by blessing, or by keeping a vow for decades, and a very old runaway who has kept her *Ashe* for forty years reads Touched. Keeping vows stops at Drawn. There are a few hundred of them, and they sit at the long tables with chestnut bread and look like anybody. **Classes.** Nobody's Dog at Glory, the first offer. Off the Roll and Nobody Owns This for those who live.

## Unserved

**A writ, a curse, a brand, a summons or a blessing is a sentence with a name in it. Theirs is blank, by sworn and sealed record of the Guild Accord, and the Accord cannot fix it without admitting a seal can lie.**

**What it is.** Fate reaches along terms: a condition, a prerequisite, a named party. A collar-seal that drops a runaway to her knees at ten leagues, a family curse, a contract's penalty clause and a summoner's call all carry a slot for the party concerned. Those who have spoken at the Heap have sworn away what goes in it. They pull no thread to do this. They have made one vow, *I remain, and I own nothing written*, which spends a soul's own small share of fate, on top of a clerical error sealed under the most heavily trusted oath in the world. The harder the Accord swears by its own seals, the better it holds.

**On the window**, the soul's private report of itself:

```
[Name: Nobody]
[Owed by: nobody]
[To be served upon: nobody]
[Witness: 1]
```

| Form | From | What it does | What it costs |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The Bit** | Anyone | A collar-bit, sawn neck-iron stamped with a thumb, good for a day's work. Held in the teeth, it makes a name impossible to call for one breath, and rusts through. Left on an enemy's table it is a receipt: the House has already been paid | A day's work. Hoard forty and your window reads `[Owed: 40 days]` and the rest of this page stops applying to you |
| ***Ashe*** | Anyone, at the Heap | The rite. You speak your soul-name, and whoever is nearest answers. You are Nobody now, and that person is your witness for life | Everything you held on paper. Every deed, title, guild rank, inheritance and debt owed you is gone |
| **NOBODY HOME!** | Stage IV or so, shouted | Sets the vow against one incoming addressed working. Brand, seal, curse or summons arrives, finds no party, and falls off the skin like a wet letter | A share of reserve and a nosebleed, against anything of real strength. Contest of Resilience Oath and Persistence against whoever sent it |
| ***NON EST!*** | Stage VI | A writ's old return, "he is not found." What failed to be delivered goes back to the sender, with the sender's name on it, and most senders have written their own name very clearly | A heavy bite of reserve. Spoken wrong, it finds the witness instead |
| **Nobody Did It** | Any sworn Nobody | Testimony. Under any oath or truth-bell the sworn truth of a deed is that nobody did it, and it reads true. It hides a name and no other fact | Lose the *Ashe* and you lie under oath with every answer you have ever given |

**Domain: Nobody's Land.** A Domain is the territory where a high-Stage practitioner's own law holds, opened by a Second Release from Stage VIII, and every Domain is a claim on the ground. This one claims there are no claims. Law: *nothing here is anyone's*. A knight's bonded blade stops knowing him, a summoner's contract lapses and the thing he summoned turns round to look at him, and a sworn company becomes a number of men standing near each other. It takes no power from a fist or a Stage gap, it blanks the holder's own witness bond with the rest, and it has one famous fault: the holder may never say *mine*. Fights inside it become an extraordinary shouting match.

**The Russet Crown.** Somebody has to answer for the muster, and all three of the Crown's powers (to speak to outsiders, to call the muster, to be blamed) require a party. Whoever wears the rusted band is the one soul in the house with a slot on her, and every working aimed at *the Queen of Nobody* finds its way to her neck. A Guild Appraisal of the band reads Common Grade, Unique Rarity. Sefa Tuen-zhiv has held it eleven years.

**What it costs.** It costs exactly what it says. Nobody owes you. A contract binds in neither direction, so they take payment in advance and in coin. You inherit nothing, and hold no land, Guild title or sealed rank. No blessing lands, and bonded gear will not bond. And when you die the Layer goes to Name, as everyone's does, and yours goes to one person.

**Its limit.** It stops what is *addressed* and never what is *aimed*. A sword needs no paperwork, and "that one, in the coat" has killed a good many of them. Pressure, called the old man here, lands on anyone with knees. A raid by men who do not care about the fee is simply a raid.

**Who beats it.**

- **Serve her.** The Accord's correction needs only to be delivered. Put it in a Nobody's hand, have her take it, and `[Name: restored]`: every old writ with her name on it comes due that minute. Sealhall keeps a small corps of process-servers, polite and very hard to frighten, who have wrapped papers around pies and love letters. No child in Brokencollar will take a thing from a stranger's hand, and a correct Nobody takes a parcel off a table with her elbow.
- **Get her name.** A soul-name spoken aloud, with one witness to swear to it, fills the slot long enough for one working to land. Only her *ndoto* knows it, and her *ndoto* can be caught. This is why the Scalebond's list of Harrowgate names, at the posted price, keeps the Queen awake.
- **Move what it depends on.** A real Fate user needs no name, and one lean on the odds makes a strap give. It is ruinously hard and it works.
- **Fight her.** Light for a Catkin, a Lupine alone past a few days or under a Lunar Inversion, and for a Bearkin, go around.

## Physical Appearance and Dress

They look like everyone the Accord ever sold: slim pretty Catkin with a second conversation running under the first, dense Wolfkin with kin-cut palms shown open, enormous Bearkin in fir-green wool with a chair they brought themselves. Nearly all of them have a pale band at the neck where the fur has grown in wrong, and they do not cover it.

You can tell how long someone has been free by how loud they are: fur dyed in bars, stripes and handprints, a coat of nineteen colors if you can get it. None of their gear is bonded and all of it was someone else's. A Nightveil knife-hand wears three Rare pieces off three dead returners, guild marks filed down and her own thumb stamped over them, and she will tell you who she had each from. A new arrival wears a sack and a neck-sore and is the most honored person at the table.

## Lineage Traits

The first three are the lineages' own. The last is the house's.

### [Trait] The Reservoir — Before You Decided (Catkin)

- Feeling is stored in muscle and goes off as force through one precise step.
- The Emotional Covenant: before an important act, she stands still and counts up what she is carrying.

*Nobody in Brokencollar interrupts a Catkin standing still in a doorway.*

### [Trait] The Pack-Soul — One Gait (Wolfkin)

- Enough of them close together think as one, bound palm to cut palm under a full moon.
- A Howl sings the dead into the local lines of the earth.

*A Wolfkin can tell you how your brother is, by the weight of him, for a fee.*

### [Trait] Weight-Bound — Some Things Should Stay (Bearkin)

- A Bearkin tethers her soul to one mountain or glade and feels what it feels.
- Earthen Domains root in the geology and persist through her absence.

*They take hits that would end engagements for others, and do not track it.*

### [Trait] The Witness Line — The Only Family Tree

- A witness answers for you for life, and you may answer for others. You know one name above you and however many below.
- That chain of people, each holding one or two names, is the house's whole genealogy, and it exists in no single head.

*A child born here is nobody's until she first answers Ashe for someone else, usually at about nine, usually crying.*

## Culture

**Language.** Purrspeech, Moontongue and Urganic Growlscript on every bridge. Nothing is written in the country, and forgers are its most respected artists because they write for export. To keep a list of names here is the one crime for which the town will hang you without a hearing.

**Rites.**

- **The Tongs.** The Underhill Forge cuts any collar free, on any day. The only price is that you hold the tongs for the next one.
- **The Bad Day.** Someone stops on a bridge and stands shaking at a sound. Nobody remarks on it. Somebody comes and stands next to them, and in a while they go on.
- **The Saying Over.** At a witness's deathbed she speaks aloud every name she holds, once, to someone she has chosen. It is the only act of inheritance in the country, and it is why people are careful who they sit with.
- **The Feast of the Assessor.** Every autumn, in the sorting yard, a child in a paper hat sits at a table and asks the town its name. The hat is the only paper in the country and is burned at the end.

**Who they marry, feud with and owe.** They marry whoever they like and no one records it. Midmarch kings who offer the Queen a marriage are always surprised by the reply. No Scalebond is served a drink at the Heap. Some schools of the Violet Peaks run their high roads for love, and the Moss Court keeps a small garden of their dead.

**What commoners get.** A year and a day free, then one day in seven to the common work, then nothing else ever. It is the only country in the world where the poorest person at the table can tell the Queen she is wrong, and the Queen must then be blamed.

## Limitations and Soul-Costs

### [Curse] The Second Death — When the Witness Goes

You exist in the record of one head. Below a tenth of reserve a Core begins to fray, names first, and for a Nobody there is no file to read it back from. There are old people on the walkways of Brokencollar who no longer know what they were called, and they answer to Nobody with a great deal of dignity.

### [Curse] Owed by Nobody — No Way to Get Ahead

The house can accumulate nothing. Every generation pays its entire income down the Long Way at seventeen gold marks a head and starts again with chestnut bread. The Accord's accountants have calculated when this ends, and they are in no hurry.

### [Curse] Take Nothing From a Stranger — The Habit

They cannot be handed a cup or a child, or a bandage in a fight, without a flinch that good enemies have learned to read. Lovers pass things by setting them down.

## How They Stand Against the Three Paths

**Body.** The ordinary way they die, since a plain sword reads no paper. **Spirit.** A soul-reader can go past the blank slot and read the name off the Core, and a good one is the most dangerous thing a returner can hire. **Fate.** Most of Fate's cheap tricks slide off, and a real pull does not care what you are called. They console themselves that nobody who can do it would work for a returner.

---

## Final Definition

House Nobody is what is left when you take everything a House is and cut it off with a saw: no blood, no land, no paper, no name, nothing passed down but a chair, a witness, and the right answer to a clerk. It costs every one of them whatever they might otherwise have had, and they held a month-long feast about it.

Who's your king?

*Ashe.*

Marshal Quillon Aske was the most wounded man in the center, and he dined out on it. For thirty years he had a set speech for the second bottle, in which he took off his coat in front of the ladies and walked the table through his own body: the Threeways knee, the three ribs from Marl Bridge, the ax at Sedgeford that had opened him from the collarbone to the nipple and that he liked people to touch. In the winter of 688, flush from a good war, he disputed his surgeon's fee. It was a small sum. She was a narrow person in a coat of plum brocade named Zsevette Ir-riss, who had attended him for nineteen years, and she came to his victory dinner at Kingsbridge with the account in her head, and asked very civilly if she might take his hand. Nobody thought anything of it. They were all quite drunk, and the hall was hung with so many captured banners that the servants had to duck.

"The eleventh of March, 661," she said. "Threeways, the left knee. Eleven silver." The left knee went. It bent the way it had bent at Threeways, with no one near it, under a man who had not stood on it wrong in twenty-seven years, and he sat down hard among the sweetmeats with a noise I am told the back tables mistook for a toast. "Marl Bridge, the spring of 664. Nineteen silver, with the fever." Three ribs stove in under a clean silk shirt. He was still trying to get his breath when she mentioned Sedgeford, and at that point a very large man who had faced everything the Hundred Crowns could send began screaming for his purse, in front of the ladies, because he could see his shirt opening. She stopped there. She had the other hand on him by then, and his body was told, in the same dry voice, that it was this morning, and everything closed. She stayed to dress the sprain. He paid promptly for the rest of his life and never took his coat off at dinner again, and his son Hew, who had never been hurt in any way, and who went on to be a very bad general, was the only member of that family she could not have touched.

Every commander of means has a Scalebond at his bedside and knows the other side has one too. He tends not to think through what it means that she has read every wound he ever took, and has them all by date.

They want the record complete, the scholar-class exemption renewed and everyone's accounts kept. What they hold that everyone needs is the best surgeons in the world, the best poisons, the only antidotes to the best poisons, and a living memory of what you did. The house has never once agreed to forget anything. It holds that a thing with a price cut into the stair is no secret, and that distinction has got a good many people killed.

## Origin of the House

When the realms collapsed in the Dissolution Period, memory-essence came off them in sheets and etched itself into scaled bodies shaped to resist its erosion, and Serpentkin hold that the patience of Zseraxil the Coil runs in them. House Scalebond as a body came later, when the elders of the Rose Coils found the same families asking the same questions every two hundred years and began charging.

The surgery came from the ford. The first long ward at Bothhands was a shed, and it was there that an old notary, reading a dying man's scars for the sake of his will, discovered that the scars read back. The town took its name from what she did next, with each hand in turn.

## The House on the Crystal

The Soul Crystal is the soul, the organ that refines Aether (mana) into Essence (spiritual energy). It has three layers: the Shell toward the world, the Core toward what a thing is, and the Attraction Layer toward Fate. In the Serpentkin all three run through one strange fact, which is that memory lays down as structure. An old Crystal, read with enough skill, looks like sedimentary rock.

| Layer | How it runs in them | What shows |
|---|---|---|
| **Aether Shell** | Keyed to temperature and kept thin. They call the aura coat *the entry* and keep it pale gold | Comfortable at either thermal extreme, dull in between |
| **Essence Core** | Holds the layers, each a period of a life compacted into the substrate. The great elders stop consulting their own memory and begin consulting the world's | A faded violet behind the gold when a layer is opened |
| **Attraction Layer** | A very long list of names, kept without trying. Territories they have practiced in become living archives | Their word carries oddly far in a court |

**Path lean.** Spirit, then Fate, with almost no Body, as a tendency of the untrained soul, and the hands have to be good all the same. **The eye.** Chronos-Vision, on waking: past Aetheric events still standing in physical space, the last man's footwork in the air beside you. It reads and moves nothing. **The grip.** Thread Class, the ladder of a soul's hold on Fate, and only by time. A Scalebond of two centuries reads Touched without having tried, a very old one Drawn, the cap never lifts, and they do not pull. **On the rungs.** Essence Class is the ladder they climb: stair-wives are Listeners (I), army surgeons Answerers (II), long-ward physicians and memory-weavers Confessors (III), and the eldest of the Record Shepherds (IV), stalled at Transcendence and content.

## Both Hands

**Your body remembers everything that ever happened to it, and a Scalebond can remind it: your oldest wound with the left hand, this morning's health with the right, at the posted price.**

**How it works.** Flesh keeps its accounts. Bone knits thick at an old break, a scar pulls for life, and the blood that has met a sickness once keeps a standing guard against it. The physicians of the long ward call this last the *anamnestic response*: the second meeting is answered faster and harder than the first, which is why a man who survives the pox never catches it twice, and why a second bee sting can kill someone the first one only annoyed. Anamnesis, the Medium Wellspring of remembrance, teaches a soul that everything keeps its past, and a Serpentkin who tempers on it in the flesh can walk her Essence along that record by skin contact and find the day she wants. She then makes the tissue do again what it did then. The strength of it is the patient's own. A wound recalled comes from inside, so armor is no use, and the Aether Shell, which is built to stop things arriving from outside, has no one to stop. In the other direction, a fresh injury is told it is still the morning, and for as long as the tissue's record of this morning is strong, the wound closes.

| Form | From | What it does | Posted at |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Keeping Accounts** | Stage I | A fingertip on a scar gives the date, the weapon, and how the last man with that wound died | Two coppers |
| **The Twinge** | Stage III | One old hurt at a quarter of its force, for an hour | A silver, and you will want a receipt |
| **The Reminder** (left hand) | Stage VI | One old wound at full force. A healed break breaks again along the same line and a healed fever comes back for its second visit | By the wound |
| **The Recall** (right hand) | Stage VI | A wound under a day old closes, as far as the flesh recalls being whole. A severed hand, held on inside the hour, may take | By the wound, and double for the second side of a war |
| **Both Hands** | Stage VIII | The two at once. A surgeon holds a body between what it was and what it is, for interrogation, for a very hard amputation, or so that a man can be reminded of a thing many times | Not posted. Ask |
| **The Full Account** | Elders of the Record, Stage VIII and past | Every entry in a body, read back at once, in order, aloud | See below |

**The Full Account** is performed about once a century, both hands, skin to skin, with the door shut. Gorvaine the Unanswered, who wrestled a dragon, a serpent and a beast and raised a statue to say so, was brought up the great stair at the end of his conquest by a series of small accurate notes about his family, seeking a physician. The Reading of Gorvaine is performed on the plaza each year with the exact manner of his death, and it takes four hours, because he had a very long career, and children climb on him afterward. The reader who did it first lay still for eleven years, and every wound of that body is a layer in her Crystal for good.

**Poison, the second hand's cousin.** A Scalebond compound is rarely one dose. The first cup, *the introduction*, does nothing a soldier would notice except teach the blood to recognize it, and the second, perhaps from a different cask a month later, is answered by the man's own body at full fury. Widow's Rose works so, and the antidote they sell you is often a third cup.

**What it costs.** What she touches, she keeps. Every wound read back lays down in her own Crystal as a layer, an old physician carries a whole war from the inside, and that is the true reason so many stall at Stage VIII: Transcendence means restructuring, and there is too much in there she cannot bring herself to lose. The patient pays too. A wound recalled to health is filed and owes the body a hunger and a weight within the week, and it stays easier to reopen for anyone who has the date.

**Its limit.** Skin on skin, which is why a surgeon's first request is that you take off your gauntlet and why a duelist comes in on a short coiled strike. Only what this body has actually lived, and no stronger than it was, and the patient has by definition survived it once. It takes a breath to find each entry. It does nothing to the dead.

**Who beats it.** A body with nothing written in it: sheltered young heirs are close to immune, and easy to kill by other means. Letheveil or Absolution, which take an old wound's memory or its charge away. Negation, since the glyphs `Uur` and `No` leave a gap in a Serpentkin's filing logic and she stands for a moment at a loss. Long measure, a good gauntlet, a mild spring day. And the answer every student of the Coils learns first: do something to her that has never been done before in the recorded history of the art.

**Domain: It Has Been Tried.** A Domain is the territory where a high-Stage practitioner's own law holds, opened by a Second Release from Stage VIII. Theirs is passive: the past is present. Every technique used inside is entered as it fires, and its second use meets a holder who has already read it. It compels nothing, and a stranger with something new walks through it like weather.

## Physical Appearance and Dress

They are long and narrow, unhurried in every joint, with fine gold scale from brow to heel. The eyes are slitted and do not blink when they read you. The hands are what people remember: long, dry, perfectly steady and cold to the touch on any battlefield. Their voices are soft, exact and dated.

At fifty paces it is the coat. The Accord's exemption must be renewed in person in a good coat, so they have made the coat the house's flag: rose and gold brocade over bare scale, cut long and close, a notary's seal on a chain, a slit at the wrist so the hand comes out clean. A good surgeon's case is Very Rare work from a Den maker whose mark she will show you whether or not you asked. A poor cousin's coat has been turned twice, and he will tell you which grandmother's it was. Their gauntlets lace on the inside of the wrist so they can come off in one motion.

## Lineage Traits

### [Trait] The Coiling Memory — Sediment in the Soul

- Memory imprints accumulate as structural layers in the Crystal and do not degrade.
- They can harmonize with the residue of Wellspring states that no longer exist, and they do fine work at damaged sites because they remember what a site used to be.
- The layers feed glyph-chains passively, so a `Ma`-`Zo`-`En` chain (Memory, Echo, Lattice) stays live for a century.

*The young are told it is like learning you have always known a very long song.*

### [Trait] Chronos-Vision — The Last Man Beside You

- After-images of prior Aetheric events stand in the air for those who can see them.
- It reads a body against everyone who has ever stood where it is standing, and that read gets better with age.
- At high Stages it reaches the world's memory, and the seer loses track of her century.

*"When did you say that was?" is a point of grammar and it is also the most feared question in the center.*

### [Trait] Both Extremes — The Unwelcoming Corridor

- Aetheric sync holds them comfortable in great heat and great cold alike. Solfatara and the Titan Vohrin's cold ground are both workable.
- One great meal a fortnight, and a hot room and a cold room with a short corridor between at the temperature everyone else likes.

*A mild spring day in a shaded field is the nearest thing they have to a natural enemy.*

## Doctrine and Arts

The doctrine fits on the stair: know how it went last time, charge for it and sit down. A Scalebond fighter talks throughout, telling you what you are about to do and when you last did it. A name gets said once, clearly, in Common, and then it happens to you.

| Move | Meaning | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| **As Previously** (*Zsa-Veth*) | "The same again" | A coiled strike with the exact force that settled this same fight two hundred years ago, read off the place and repeated by her own shoulders |
| **When Did You Say That Was** (*Ess-Oda Riss*) | The question | A Coilspeech working on one spoken claim. Every assertion in the tongue must carry when it is true, and the speaker's own Core gets snagged on the tense of a lie |
| **An Introduction** (*Riss-Ani*) | The first cup | A scratch, and nothing else for a month |
| **Entered** (*Zsenn*) | "Noted" | A fingertip on the wrist in the clinch, and one old wound comes back in the hand holding the sword |

## Culture

**Language.** Coilspeech, in which a statement that does not say where it sits in time is incomplete. Soul-names run in two parts, and foreigners get a sealed copy of everything as a courtesy.

**Rites.**

- **The Coil-Rite.** The young meditate in an Anamnesis zone until the Crystal takes its first deep layer, holding the shape of the lineage and no one person's recollection.
- **The First Reminder.** No one may lay the left hand on a patient until she has done it to herself, with the worst thing that has ever happened to her body, in front of her teacher, who then waits a little while before using the right.
- **The Reading of Gorvaine.** Yearly, on the plaza, on the statue's steps. Bookies do not take bets on how long it runs, because the Record has the time.

**Who they marry, feud with and owe.** They marry inside the Coils, by a process closely resembling a merger of archives. The Concord is the standing fight. The Peaks owe an old fine over a bird. No Scalebond is served a drink at the Heap in Brokencollar, because the Record's list of everyone who ever passed through a Harrowgate pen is for sale. They honor Zsalla Underscale, who was theirs and spoke for the pen-breakers, and see no contradiction in this.

**What commoners get.** The same bed in the same ward at the same posted price, in no particular order. A dyer and a duke wait side by side at Bothhands, and their full accounts are kept alike.

## Limitations and Soul-Costs

### [Curse] The Crystal That Will Not Let Go — The Stall at Transcendence

Stage VIII demands genuine change and the Crystal is built to preserve. Most great Scalebond stay there for centuries, regarding the matter as settled by the evidence.

### [Curse] Nothing Filed Away — The Physician's Sediment

Old surgeons wake with other men's limps, and a bad campaign leaves a young one unable to eat at the fortnight. They have no word for the condition, because in their language to forget it would be tampering.

### [Curse] The Category Error — What Has Not Been Done

Clean negation leaves a gap where a backlash ought to be, and a truly novel situation sends her cautious at exactly the moment she needs to move. They lose to amateurs more often than they will tell you, and they can give you the dates.

---

## How They Stand Against the Three Paths

**Body.** A strong fighter with reach never lets the hands arrive, and has a lifetime of wounds he has survived. **Spirit.** A skimmer with Letheveil erases what they are made of, yet the Core is where they are strongest. **Fate.** They read a thread on contact and do not pull, and one lean on the odds, a gauntlet that will not come unlaced, sends the whole patient art past him.

---

## Final Definition

House Scalebond is the people who took the body at its word. They sit at every bedside with your whole history in their cold hands, they will mend you or end you for the same posted price, and they will tell you, once, clearly, that it has been tried. They hold no grudge against you, and they will keep an exact record of any you have earned.

*It has been tried.*

And cut at the bottom of the great stair, where you will read it on your way down: *Your account remains open.*

The note said *The north is reminded.* It came up the eastern road to Hollenmark in the Thin Weeks (the starving end of winter), in Kagenori Tenrai's own hand, tied to a cask of brine with Torvald Stark's head in it. Ulf Stark read it by the fire, set it down and did not raise his voice, and that is all anyone at Hollenmark will tell you about the evening.

By the next dusk every Stark on the March had walked to the nearest boundary stone. Nobody gave an order, and the Stone Roll, which enters who stood at each stone and what was decided there, enters only that they stood. They stood in their shirts with their boots off in the deepest cold of the year, old men and widows and children of nine, and they felt what they felt. The Rime runs harder under feeling. Lamps shrank in their brackets at Stonewatch, ironwood cracked like a thrown axe, and ravens came down out of the air. The white bears walked in from Bearhold unasked and lay in rings around the stones with their backs to them, and for nine days nothing else could go near.

On the tenth morning a Vessant factor on the supply road found a ring of wet black ground thirty paces wide around the first stone, smoking in the frost. Then he saw what was coming up out of it, and took off his hat, and could not have told you why. It was grass, ankle-high and thin and very green. On the next rise there was another ring, and another past that, dark green beads laid along the whole white length of the eastern line. "When the grass comes" is how the north says spring, and it has meant moss since the clans stopped riding, because the grass is on the list of things that are gone. Old women walked out of the long houses and knelt in it and put it in their mouths. It lasted six days. Ulf Stark cut one sod with his own knife, packed it in the same cask and sent it back to the Hall of Verdicts with no note at all, and the Hall's clerks filed it under soil.

The next winter the Winter Road killed ninety riders, on stones that had kept their grandfathers alive and had gone cold under them. Every Stark on the March knew that would happen before they took their boots off. Ask one, and they will tell you that is just the way this life is.

House Stark says where things end and begin, and it holds the eastern frontier against [[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Ember Holds of Marrowhold|the holds]], and it held the passes for forty years beside the Greymanes, and it imports nearly everything it eats from a house it despises. What they hold that everyone needs is an edge: the north knows where it stops because a Stark has gone and looked. What almost nobody south of Stonewatch knows is that the edge is warm.

## Origin of the House

The Starks did not set the boundary stones. Waist-high, lichen-green and older than any record of the house, the stones stood on every rise of the March before there was a March, and the first line of the Stone Roll, which surveyors will show you for a price and a promise, reads in full: *Came to the stone. It was already warm.*

The Rime itself sits outside the register of the Sixty. The Guild's best guess, which no Stark has confirmed, begins at the Cold Larder, a day from Bearhold, where the ground runs on Vohrin, a Titan's own cold, in which cold is a deficit and heat is let go of as a discipline. A family that lived on that ground long enough might come out with blood that lets go of heat as a habit. A Stark Measurewright asked about it in my hearing said that he had a stone to check.

**Path.** The soul leans toward the Body Path, with a second lean toward Fate for the line and the oath; *Body VI Mastered, Fate V Walked* at about a third is the family's usual shape. **Ladders.** Aether climbs, slowly. The great Warlords have stood at Glory (Stage VI) on the first rung, Muridic, and two of the best, Ulf and his young uncle Yusif, never put a class name on the slip at all. **Grip.** None. Stark blood opens no ladder early, and most read Essence Ø and Thread Ø to the grave, keeping every vow without ever reaching above the soul's own share.

## The Long Hearth

**In a breath:** every Stark steals the heat out of everything near them and cannot keep it, so it goes down through their heels into the ground, and after four centuries the whole border of the March is warm underneath.

**How it works.** Stone is very slow at passing heat. Warmth driven a hundred yards down into solid gneiss takes about three hundred years to work back up, and above it sits the permafrost, the best lid in the world. Under each boundary stone the surveyors have found what a northern digger calls a talik, a pocket of ground that never freezes, held open inside the frozen country like a coal in a fist. Heat goes in because a Stark has stood there. It stays because nothing up top can reach it, and because the stones, which were there first, hold what they are given. The Roll-Cellar under Stonewatch, a Fixatio Well (the Wellspring of commitment, where what is bound stays bound) beneath the oldest stone on the line, is the nearest thing scholars have to an explanation.

That is why the stones are lichen-green in January. It is also why the Winter Road exists. A rider who knows the line can lie down with his spine against a stone at thirty below and wake up, and a cache buried at its foot never freezes through. An army that has marched into the March is freezing to death in the middle of the largest hearth in the north, and nobody has told it.

**And none of it is for them.** A Stark's blood moves heat out and never in. A Stark can stand barefoot on four hundred years of his family's warmth and feel nothing. Ulf Stark's soul put it in two words the day his brother came home: *we give.*

| **Giving the Stone** | Any Stark, barefoot | The Rime follows feeling, so a grief can be stood out on a stone and paid in. It is the house's whole approach to mourning | A little of the feeling goes with it, for good. Starks get plainer with age |
| **THE FLOOR GOES** | Aether II | Through the ground by contact, what he takes from the air goes out along a line under a marching host. The crust rots from below as the air above drops. | **Reading the Line** | Aether III | Through his heels he feels a fire lit at the next stone along, which is much of how a Stark column has already moved | Standing still and barefoot on campaign |
| **HERE IT ENDS** | Domain, Stage VIII at least | Called twice in the Roll. A line is drawn, and past it heat goes where the Stark says | When it falls, everything it took comes up at the caster's feet |

**What it costs.** The bank runs down. The grass cost a third of the line's warmth by the Roll's own survey. Moving a stone breaks its lid, which is why the penalty is a hand, and a stone left unstood for a generation goes cold, so a house losing its sons in a forty-year war is losing its road as well.

**Its limit.** It moves heat and cannot make it. In deep winter there is little to take, against a great fire it is a bucket, and off the March a Stark is a cold man with no bank behind him. On the Hem of the Scourge his chief use is keeping beer cold.

**Who beats it.** A fire, a thaw and a scout who does not sleep. A thaw drowns the Winter Road in its own warm mud. A fire tells the Stark you are there and melts the snow that hides the stone, a fair trade if you came to find it. A captain who learns to look for green lichen can bed his men down on the Starks' own hearth, and those stones are entered in the Roll with a remark. And a Stark kept calm is a much smaller problem than a Stark kept angry.

### The bargain with the bears

A Dire Icebear stands nine feet at the shoulder in a coat built for standing still on ice, and she overheats within a mile of running, as any sealer on the shelf will tell you. So when the young of the house walk out to Bearhold in a shirt, which is called the Asking, the bear that comes up to them is checking how the heat leaves. A rider with the Rime running clean takes it off her as she goes, and she can run down a stag for as long as the rider lasts.

It also means she can feel his temper through her own spine, with nothing in the way. A rider who stops being honest about what he feels stops being worth carrying, and the withdrawal is not gentle. That is the true reading of what happened to young Sweyn in 668. No Stark wears an icebear, and the Bearhold graveyard is small.

## Physical Appearance and Dress

**Stature and coloring.** Big, rawboned and long-armed, going to doorway width by fifty, with dark hair graying early, a coppery line among the younger sons, and pale blue or gray eyes raw at the rims. **Marks.** The breath stops steaming first, then frost comes in the beard, and a tavern-keeper who sees that moves the glassware. **Voice.** Plain and short. Everyone under forty is *lad*.

**The look at fifty paces.** Wolf-fur and black wool in knots of men on foot, a heavy even walk, and the wolf of Hollenmark on the breast, silver for the Warlord alone and brass for the sons. They dress plain and carry one thing that is not. Marchstone, Grade A and Legendary, stands point-down before its bearer the way a boundary stone stands. Lorn's sword looks as if it cost a pig and is a Very Rare nickel steel that stays tough under his own cold, with the Hollenmark yard's small boundary stone under the guard. The poor cousin carries a market blade he will ruin in two years, since a Stark's rime breaks ordinary steel from the inside, as Yusif's did, and a pelt he will tell you he killed himself. Hollenmark women braid a son two cords of caged crystal, one for the going and one for the coming back, and the north can tell a lot from which riders still wear two.

## Lineage Traits

### [Trait] The Stark Rime — The Heat Goes Down

* Heat leaves everything near the bearer, timber silvers, snow stiffens, and all of it goes into the ground. It runs harder under feeling, and a tightened jaw on the left is the usual tell.
* Near-immune to cold and weak to heat in exact proportion. Its fair counter is heat, and calm.

*A Stark who is not angry is only a cold man. A great many people have died trying to work out how to arrange that.*

### [Trait] The Point Set Down — What the Soul Holds

The house's own Traits come out in one shape: this far, this thing, no further. Lorn draws a line in the snow. Mark's hand does not open on a sword whatever has hold of him. Rob stood over whoever he had claimed, and Yusif kept a silence for sixteen years until it killed him. Each has its limit in the same place as its strength, and the usual counter is to go around the end of it.

*You will notice how few of them involve going anywhere.*

### [Trait] Cold Steel — The Blade Goes First

* On the bind a Stark's cold can run through his own blade into the other man's steel and drop it below the point where iron stops bending and starts breaking. It ruins his own ordinary swords the same way, and bronze does not care.

*Yusif Stark bought a new sword every couple of years off a market stall. He could have carried a Kingsarm.*

## Doctrine and Arts

Stark war is winter war on roads nobody else thinks are open. The killing is done by men on foot in wolf-fur moving in knots, with the bears few and enormously loud in any chronicle. Great sword and axe in the north, boar-spear on the road, short sword and limewood shield for the young.

## Culture

**Language.** Plain northern Common, with an old tongue kept for shouting at bears. A debt is no debt until said aloud at a boundary.

**Rites.**

* **The Asking, the Laying-In and Giving the Stone,** above.
* **The Hand Under the Stone.** Whoever moves a stone loses the hand, and it goes under when the stone is reset. A bear at Stonewatch was once entered in the Roll as a party to the dispute.
* **The Dish.** Every midwinter, an empty pie dish to Ashbury.

**Marriage, feud and debt.** A Stark daughter married a Thornwall in 592, and it is still being discussed. A Stark and a Greymane will die for each other without having a conversation. They owe Ashmore for every bushel and resent every one.

**What the commons get.** They pay in days on the wall and split wood. And the long houses of the March are built where a surveyor says to build them, and in the worst winter on record not one child in them froze in its bed. The barley-cake woman under the twin towers has a warm stone, and has never asked where it came from.

## Limitations and Soul-Costs

### [Curse] Never Warm — One Way Only

* The blood moves heat out and never in. Food tastes of less. Beds are a formality. A Stark's child, held, does not get warmer.

*"A Stark has no use for a scarf,"* the north says of Lorn's, *"so somebody gave it to him."*

### [Curse] The Bank Runs Down — Nobody Pays It Back

* Every Stark who dies young leaves a stretch of line unwalked, and the forty-year war emptied Hollenmark of sons.
* Spend the Hearth in a season and the Winter Road kills your own riders the next year.

*A rider's widow at Stonewatch told me that her husband gave the stone their son, and came home, and asked what was for dinner.*

## How They Stand Against the Three Paths

**Body.** The fight they trained for. A great hitter on a hot Wellspring is the classic Stark death, and a Stark's cold is the classic death of a man with ordinary steel. **Spirit.** Their worst: Silent Cores and no Ward, though a Spirit fighter still has to cross a March that wants him dead of exposure. **Fate.** A Stark who keeps his oath offers little to lean on, but set two oaths against each other and he freezes between them.

## Final Definition

House Stark is the north's cold neighbor and its secret stove: a family that has poured every summer and every grief it ever had into the ground under a line, for people who will mostly never know it, and who cannot warm one hand at it themselves.

*"Don't take my word for it, lad. Go and look."*

In Year 631, at the midsummer fair at Raftwick, a Marrick captain named Aldric Voss, a large and cheerful man who had just been paid, stood in front of a Thornwall hedge at drill and laughed at the length of the spears. A forester called Sigrid Orm, who had come down to sell pitch, asked the name of the gentleman, and wrote it down. That summer she went up the Hale Ride, a straight red road through the deep stands, and at forty of the great ironwoods along it she took the small cross-headed hook from her belt, cut a palm's width of bark away at the height of her shoulder, and carved one short line in each, with his name in it and two guild marks. People who knew her said she was never quite as much after that summer. The bark closed over within a few years, and she died in 660 with her pitch accounts in order.

On a bright cold morning in the autumn of 695, under a canopy gone the color of an open furnace, eleven hundred Marrick lances on a Tenrai contract came up the Hale Ride to seize the season's convoy. Their captain was Aldric Voss's grandson, a large and cheerful man who had never heard of the fair at Raftwick. A woman in a red cap stood in the road ahead of them with a ledger under one arm. She read something out of it, walked to the nearest tree, a trunk wider than a house, set the hook into a place in the bark that looked like every other place, and pulled.

**"CALLED IN!"**

Sixty-four rings of red wood had grown over that line, and each had carried a little more of what Sigrid Orm put there, as a debt carries interest. It came out all at once. The great tree split from the shoulder down like a door opening and came over across the ride, two hundred feet of it, with the whole forest rising into the air as gold dust and needles, and fell into the next, which opened too. They went in order, left and right, forty of them, and when the resin settled there was a wall of fallen ironwood a mile long and as high as a keep, with a column under it, and a woman in a red cap making a note. A hundred and twelve men walked out over the next week. Young Voss was one, and she met him with the bill, itemized: forty great trees at the current rate, for the laugh, at interest.

He asked her what laugh. She told him it was all in the Ledger, and that he was welcome to come up and read it.

House Thornwall reads cargo rolls the way other houses read battle reports, and they will tell you what you will run short of six months before you know you need it. They hold the ironwood the [[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Ember Holds of Marrowhold|Marrowkin]] import for tool handles and ward-frames, and the corridor east to Collarbone. What they hold that everyone needs is wood that will outlast your grandchildren. What nobody asks is what else is in it.

## Origin of the House

The first Thornwalls found marks in the ironwood when they felled it, deep inside the trunk under centuries of rings, and being who they were they priced them. Thorold Hook's first bill in the cargo rolls is to an unnamed party for "one old debt, opened by accident, and the damage." The cross-headed hook on every forester's belt is copied from one he found in a tree. They inherited a practice from people they know nothing about, and got very good at it.

The Stage's ceiling still stands above that.

**Hisae Thornwall is the exception.** Born a Byakuya of the white house, she married in forty years ago, and her *Fate V Mastered, Spirit IV Walked* is Moto schooling kept over a lamp with the doors shut. Her husband's grandfather took one look at what the house had got and, the night of the wedding, gave her the hook. She has held the Ledger for most of the time since. Nobody knows what she has cut.

## The Long Score

**In a breath:** a working cut into a living tree does not fade, it grows, and it pays out to whoever holds the hook, a lifetime after the one who cut it has stopped caring.

**How it works.** A tree cannot heal a wound as flesh does. It walls the damage off and lays new wood over it, and you can saw into an old trunk and find a blaze cut a hundred years ago with the knife-strokes still sharp in the dark. Ironwood is the densest and slowest wood in the north, and it holds an inscription better than stone. So a line cut in a live ironwood, with some of the cutter's stamped Essence on the edge of the hook, stays. The tree goes on drawing Aether up from the ground to live on, and with each year's ring a little of that collects around the line. Verdantia, the green pulse, has a word for it: accumulation, one good day on top of another. Sixty years of good days is a great many.

Most of what a score does is simple. It breaks, drops, closes or holds. The cleverness is in the terms, and two guild marks make it a contract, good from here to the Center, because a lie in a cut splits the grain.

### How it pays

| Grade of score | Age | What it does | What it costs the cutter |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| **A Blaze** | Any | A trade trick anyone can do with a knife. A handle does not loosen, a lintel keeps a family's name | A moment's attention |
| **A Short Score** | Ten to thirty years | A limb that falls when told, a ride that closes behind a convoy | A day's reserve, gone for a month |
| **A Long Score** | Fifty years and up | CALLED IN. A tree that splits and comes down as a working, and can open its neighbors | A share of the reserve that never refills |
| **A Deep Score** | A century and up | Nobody living has opened one on purpose. The Ledger lists sixty-one, and three more it cannot place | The dead paid it |
| **Sold Timber** | Any | A felled tree keeps what it has and stops growing, and may go into a roof-tree or a ward-frame. The house has never said whether it has sold a score, and every gate in the north hangs on its timber | |

**The hook.** A score opens to a mark-hook of the house, set in the right place by someone who knows it, and that takes the Ledger of Rings: tree, height, year, cutter, terms and who it is for. It is the most dangerous book in the north. A score nobody has the page for is buried money.

**What it costs.** A forester of sixty may have a third of her reserve standing in trees she will never see open, given up for someone not yet born. The interest runs whether or not the debtor is alive, and young Voss paid for a laugh he never heard.

**Its limit.** It cannot be used in a hurry, and a score pays only where the tree stands. It is useless past the treeline, which is why the whale-oil warband lost as it did, and why Aksel Keld at fifteen broke another four times his size in a whiteout.

**Who beats it.** Fire, for a start, and the beetle as long as your thumb, which has cost the house more than any war. An Anamnesis reader (a soul that can lay a palm on a thing and read its past) can walk a road laying hands on trunks. Steal the Ledger or kill the foresters with the pages, and a century of savings goes dark. Or go around, since all of this needs you on one road on one morning.

### The Long Cuts

Everyone asks about the long cuts, the narrow straight rides walled in by trees so old you cannot see the sky. Hisae's foresters had kept them open for two hundred years when she sent twelve hundred pikes up them thirty at a time, and up the stair behind the frozen fall, and over the east wall of the Seat before the Tenrai knew there was a hole in it. A back door works once. I asked a forester whether the trees along them are in the Ledger of Rings. She told me that it was for the timber, in a forest where nobody has taken a tree off one in living memory, and she looked at me until I stopped.

## Physical Appearance and Dress

**Stature and coloring.** Northern commons, fair to fox-brown, strong in the forearm and short of a finger or two. **Marks.** Resin in the creases, a knife on every belt from the age of four, and the older foresters have a thinned look, like a good coat worn a long time. **Voice.** A Thornwall will price the damage aloud while it is happening.

**The look at fifty paces.** Oiled gray felt and an iron cap, in step, a thorn-sprig in dark red thread at the collar, on twelve hundred men who look like a hedge coming on at a walk. Raftsmen wear red caps and spiked boots. Everything is carved, in knots of beasts and leaves in red, ocher and verdigris blue, and guild marks are bragged about and sued over. Money shows in fire-enamel from the Hale workshop. The real flex is the hook: small, iron, on a long black cord, and the older the iron the less anyone wants to stand downhill of you. For forty years Hisae's passed for the keys to the linen. The poor cousin has plain felt and a borrowed hook.

## Doctrine and Arts

The house does not fight if it can bill instead. The Thornwall hedge is long ash-and-iron spears at a walk, to a drum, with the thorn-horn every hundred paces. Woodsmen fight with axes and short bows from behind things eight feet thick, and raft-crews with hook-poles and spiked boots. Technique names are named for the job.

| Move | What it does |

## Culture

**Language.** Northern Common with a raftsman's filth, and carved marks everyone can read.

**Rites.**

* **The Knife.** A child gets one before shoes.
* **The Birth Tree.** A forester cuts a score for each child of the house, in terms the child is never told.
* **Tying the Flag.** One faded cloth at the Red Stair for each great tree taken, by the raft-boss who took it. Anyone who has been down to the second landing drinks free in Raftwick.
* **Reading the Rings.** At a wedding a slice of the couple's tree is read aloud, with what the family expects to be owed by the time the grandchildren marry. Hisae's has not been shown.
* **Writing It Down.** When anyone insults the house, someone asks the gentleman's name.

**Marriage, feud and debt.** They marry for corridors: a Stark daughter in 592, a Byakuya peace forty years ago. Hallenfeld is the long war, over the log-jam of 688. The Keld still sing about the whale-oil. They were the Kōkan's foresters and kept the door for them, and Muken's price for Thornwall was the house free of the Ore Council.

**What the commons get.** A contract that holds, paid in a share of the drive, and it is not unusual for a raft-boss to own more than the lord she delivers to. And somewhere in the Thornwood a forester long dead will have cut a line so that a village not yet founded will have a roof. Most of what the Ledger holds is not revenge. They just tell that story more.

## Limitations and Soul-Costs

### [Curse] Paid in Advance — What Stands in the Wood

* A share of every forester is in trees, for good, and the great cutters end up thin and faded. Grandchildren visit them to ask where things are.

*Sigrid Orm's pitch accounts were perfect. Her family says that in her last years she could not always tell you which of them she was talking to.*

### [Curse] Interest Runs on the Dead — The Debt That Cannot Be Forgiven

* A score does not know the feud is over. Terms cut in anger will fire on a guest, an ally or the descendant you just married, and the only cancellation is cutting the tree down and taking the damage.

*Every forester of the house has been told, on her first day: cut it as though the person who will open it hates you.*

### [Curse] Standing Wealth — A Forest Can Burn

* A century of savings can be undone by a dry August. They cannot leave, and the Tenrai knew it and taxed accordingly.

*They paid on the day for forty years. People assumed that was fear.*

## How They Stand Against the Three Paths

**Body.** Bring them out of the wood and they lose. Fight them in it and you have walked into a sum done before your grandfather was born. **Spirit.** A soul that can read what a thing means can read a score. **Fate.** Something that moves what a road depends on can move you off it, and a score cannot chase anyone. Hisae, however, is a Byakuya with forty years of patience and a Ledger, and I would not.

## Final Definition

House Thornwall has made a magic out of the most irritating virtue there is. They cut their spite and their kindness into red wood with equal care, and they spend their own souls on people they will not meet. They are almost always correct. The bill will come. It may not come to you.

*"Six months. Or sixty years. I'll need the name of the gentleman."*

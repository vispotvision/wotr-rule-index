The Marrick sergeant Tam Ruddock walked home from the causeway in the spring of 683, with two hundred others, when the contract ran out, and told the first tavern he reached that it was the worst thing he had ever seen. Eleven years later, being a man with a bad back and a large family, he signed on for the same causeway under King Aldric Sorrel of Low Marl, whose nineteen hundred spears in orange and black had heard the story of the pears and took it to be about pears. On the fifth morning, in bright mist, a Totemdeep lodge came up out of the black water on both sides of the road: forty orcs in bark harness, eyes burning green, none of them doing anything an orc does. They dressed their ranks. They closed up by the left with the sergeants a pace out in front, and they came on at the Aldwater slow march, the exact hitching step of the Third Column, with a drummer on the flank beating the League's old cadence on a hollow log and all forty whistling, through teeth never shaped for it, a filthy hymn about a bishop of Kingsbridge that Ruddock had last heard eleven years before.

He had marched beside that hymn for six days. He knew the step and he knew the hitch, and he knew that Piers Vantry's column, drilled by a man with one good eye, had always wheeled wide to the right and left its flank open for the space of four breaths. "THEY WHEEL RIGHT!" he bawled, in front of his king and everybody, and he took his two hundred through that gap like a cart through a hedge, and kept going. That is how the Guild comes to have an eyewitness. The rest of Low Marl stayed, and it gives a small hard quince that a Kingsbridge cook will fight you for. Every autumn until he died a basket of it reached Ruddock's door with a note on birch bark, in a beautiful hand: *You were quite right about the wheel. We have spoken to the sergeants.*

The dead stay dead. I set that down first because every visitor to the Moonlight Forest comes away unsure of it. Nobody marched out of that silt in 694. Forty living orcs had drawn up through the root the drill of three thousand eight hundred men in rows, the way you might borrow a neighbor's ladder. The Moss Court buries everything that comes to it, friend or foe, keeps what the body knew, and puts it to work.

Every army in the center buys their physic and carts its dead to Rootgate, because the Court plants them free. Ask what it wants in return and you get the sentence every envoy has been given since the board went up: *Nuur ssaath*. The interpreters render it with care as "Nothing wants," and the salt councils have filed it for three centuries as a statement of no territorial claim. Press them and the Court will admit to wanting the salt kept sealed and a good crop. But I had the grammar explained to me at Ghurash Hollow by a rind-wife with her arms in a sump, and in Swamp Parun that sentence has a subject. *Nothing* is the bare silt under the gardens, the place where a thing goes once it has stopped being a thing. It is empty and it has an appetite. The whole of Verdant religion is that you had better feed it something specific, on purpose, with a tree on top, because it will take the unspecific on its own account. She thought this was very funny. She asked how ripe I was feeling.

## Origin of the House

The Verdant Orcs are Shard-kin, whose fragments of the First Impact came down in high-resonance wetlands, and when the expulsion scattered the goblinoid peoples they went into the mire for centuries and came out with a country, a tree and a very polite board.

The Court dates itself from the first Planting: a band starving in the wet buried its dead where it camped, and found next spring that the grubs on those graves knew where the eels ran. A trained soul's leftovers go to the lens (the Wellspring it tempered on longest) and an untrained one's feed the ground, and on the richest Manganthra and Verdantia silt in the center, lens and ground are the same mud. Nothing leaves.

## The House on the Crystal

Every soul has three layers in its Soul Crystal (the organ that is the soul, refining Aether, which is mana, into Essence, which is spiritual energy). The Shell faces the physical world, the Core faces the soul's own reality, and the Attraction Layer faces Fate. A people's lean is a tendency of the untrained soul. It settles nothing about what Path a person later walks.

| Layer | How it runs in them | What shows |
|---|---|---|
| **Aether Shell** | Thin and shed like a rind. They call the aura coat *ssuul*, and a good one is meant to be peeled off and left behind | Cold silver-green along old scars, dim at the dark of the moon |
| **Essence Core** | Wide open and shared. Spoor-Melds run Core to Core with no language required | The green mist at knee height |
| **Attraction Layer** | Held by kin and tree. A debt passes to the tree at death, and the living must come and hear it | Thin, and rarely climbed |

**Path lean.** Spirit, strongly, at some cost in strength and schooling, and Essence Class is the ladder most of them climb. **The grip.** Essence Class may open before Glory (Stage VI, where the ladders are first read) in a child who has sat her first Meld under a kin-tree. The cap never lifts. **The eye.** None in the sense the old lines mean. **On the rungs.** Rot-wives and lodge fighters are Listeners (I), garden-keepers Answerers (II), a Mother's circle Confessors (III), and a Mother in a good century a Shepherd (IV). Thread stays Unthreaded for nearly all of them.

## The Keeping

**Everything you bury works for you, and the Moss Court has been burying people for a very long time.**

**What it is.** A Soul-Tree is a tree grown on a corpse in worked silt, and it holds what the Court calls the keep: the residue that comes out through a dead body's Shell. The soul's other parts go elsewhere. The Core goes to Echo and the Name to the Layer, so the person is gone, and the Court's cheerful talk of grandmothers does not change that. What the tree keeps is what the *body* knew: the step, the guard, the hitch in a march, the way a pair of hands salts an eel, the sergeant's bark and the old man's grudge against a particular kind of fence. A Verdant Orc can draw that habit up through the root and wear it.

**How it works.** Foresters will tell you a wood is one animal. Under the leaf-mold a mat of fungus threads every root to every other, and it moves food along by a plain gradient, from a rich rotting log to the starving sapling a hundred paces off, so that the dead tree feeds the young one. That is nutrient cycling across a common mycelial network, and Verdant fungus runs it through the joints of living orcs as well. Manganthra, the go-between, lets two things that should never meet share one surface without tearing it, Verdantia gives the growth, and Rebirthine pays the bill. The keep comes along that mat, through a slip of ancestor wood under the wearer's shoulder-bark, and the living flesh takes up a dead flesh's habit.

| Form | From | What it does | What it costs |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The Sitting** (*Ssuth*) | Any Verdant | Sit against a kin-tree in a Meld and come away for a day with one old trade habit | A long afternoon and an argument |
| **The Cutting** (*Vaal-Ssek*) | Stage III, Ascension | A slip of ancestor wood under the shoulder-bark. It carries one body-skill at the tree's own Grade. It counts as a self-boost, under the usual cap of two Grades | A share of the reserve goes down the root each time it is used, as the bill for the tending |
| **The Row** (*Ssaarekh*) | Stage VI, in war-Meld, on a planted bed or with its fruit eaten that morning | A lodge wears a whole dead company's drill, as Ruddock saw. They hold the formation to a hair and do it in the dead men's own time | Everyone in the Meld shares every wound, and the drill is exactly as old as the men who died knowing it |
| **The Fruit** | Any | A tree's fruit carries a trace of its keep. A bought pear gives a stranger the words to a song he never learned. A dosed one, from a rot-wife, is a Draft for an hour's borrowed skill | Identity Contamination, in small and funny amounts, or large and otherwise. No Vantry has been on time since 683 |
| **The Second Spring** (*Uulm-Sseth*) | Stage IX or so, one breath, in Swamp Parun | The rot goes through leather, rope, bowstring and then men, across a field. Whatever falls there is planted where it falls, in rank, and from next spring the ground is the Court's | The speaker's whole breath and whatever she was keeping it for. A Mother who misjudges drowns on dry land |

**Domain: the Long Garden** (*Mosk-Ssaath*). A Domain is the territory in which a high-Stage practitioner's own law holds, opened by a Second Release from Stage VIII. Law: *nothing that falls here is lost, and nothing that falls here is yours.* A dropped blade, a spent working and a spilled pint of blood all come up as reserve for the holder, and a wound taken inside begins, gently, to garden. It starves on stone, fails under noon and dies on salt.

**What it costs.** A keep is a loan against your own body, and the Court holds the paper. Every cutting promises the wearer back to the bed, which is the plain reason a Verdant Orc withers past two days from swamp or kin: the graft is calling in the debt. Foreign scholars name this Isolation Degeneracy, and the orcs call it homesickness.

**Its limit.** A tree keeps what a body knew and nothing a Core authored. No Trait, Release, secret or name ever came up a root, as the Vantry family has established at some length. A habit is also dated, and knows the world as it stood on the day it was planted.

**Who beats it.** Salt, which is why the Court asks for yours at the landing. A Sun-aspected Domain, which costs them about a third of everything. Bled or stony ground, and a fight kept off the wet past the second day. Above all anyone who remembers the drill better than the tree does: Tam Ruddock did it with a bad back, a loud voice and no technique at all.

## Physical Appearance and Dress

They stand six foot four to seven feet, in long vine-like muscle, with eyes like solid lamps of moss-green or lunar white, skin the green of old forest, hard bark along the shoulders and luminous fungus at the joints, trimmed as a northern lord trims his beard. A cutting shows as a welt under the shoulder-bark with a different grain. An old wound pulses cold silver-green, and they show their scars the way other people show their children. Their voices are slow and sibilant, and a Mire-Mother has never needed to raise hers.

At fifty paces you see the glow first and the moths second, and you get the smell shortly afterward. A lodge fighter goes out in grown bark harness coaxed off her own family's tree, the one thing of Soul-Tree wood that ever travels, Uncommon by the Guild's measure and not for sale at any price. Ssaaleth's moth-wing mantle reads Legendary, and she will tell you what became of most of the former owners. A poor cousin wears a moss blanket with one thread of moth silk in the hem, and he is as loud about that thread as a Midmarch duke about his crown.

## Lineage Traits

### [Trait] The Silver Scar — The Rind That Remembers

- Hard bark grows over old damage and turns a blade better each decade.
- Scars light with the moon and read as a plain record of what the orc has survived.
- At full moon the reserve fills fast. At the dark of it everyone turns short with each other.

*They ask after your scars as you might ask after someone's mother.*

### [Trait] Spoor-Meld — The Shared Cloud

- Group meditation releases Aetheric spores into a green mist, and inside it memory and feeling pass without language for hours.
- A lie shows among kin, which makes Verdant testimony reliable and family dinners bad.
- Certain phrases can tip listeners into a Meld, so wedding speeches are cleared in advance.
- A war-Meld moves a lodge like one animal thinking.

*A decision of the Court is what the mist feels like when it clears.*

### [Trait] One Long Breath — Swamp Parun

- Swamp Parun, the Verdant hand of the script and speech that writes boundaries onto laws, runs on a single unbroken breath, so a working can only be as large as the speaker's chest.
- A Mother's lungs are the only credential the office asks for.
- Big workings arrive slowly, and they can be interrupted by anything that makes her cough.

*The bookies at Glowwater quote odds on chest measurements.*

## Doctrine and Arts

The doctrine is on the board at Rootgate. Ask them politely to go round. Let them come in anyway, let the water rise behind them, and plant what you get. The lodges fight in silence on Beast-Sign, some four hundred signals, and consider a battle cry a waste of breath, so the names below get said afterward, kindly, by people passing you the fruit.

| Move | Meaning | What it does |

| ***Mahaa-Sseh*** | **Mire-Mother's Yes** | Under the black water at one end of a fight and up behind you at the other, with friends |

## Culture

**Language.** Swamp Parun, with more words for kinds of rot than any other tongue has for weather, and Beast-Sign for the field.

**Rites.**

- **The Planting.** The dead go into a garden with drink and a great deal of cheerful advice. A family visits, quarrels with the tree and goes home feeling better, and nobody present thinks a tree is a grandmother. They will tell you that the argument is what the visit is for.
- **The Dive.** A new Mire-Mother is whoever speaks the largest working on one breath from the bottom at Glowwater, with the shore packed and the bookies on stilts.
- **The Salt Sentence.** Burial in salted ground, worked so that Aether will not pool and nothing will grow. The condemned gets no tree, and Nothing gets him for nothing. Nine in the record, each named as a warning to children.

**Who they marry, feud with and owe.** They marry at the Blooming Mire, and the speeches go wrong. They feud politely and forever with the salt cities of Nalūn, trade clients with the Scalebond, and have planted a small, well-tended bed of House Nobody's dead as a matter of neighborliness.

**What commoners get.** A tree. No Verdant dyer or eel-man has ever gone into an unmarked hole, and I have seen dukes envy it.

## Limitations and Soul-Costs

### [Curse] The Calling In — Isolation as Collection

Past forty-eight hours from swamp or kin they wither, stacking losses until half of what the body can bear is gone, and the more cuttings an orc wears, the sooner it starts. The Court has never held a yard of land outside the wet.

### [Curse] Borrowed Hands — Too Many Grandmothers

Wear a keep long enough and it stops being borrowed. Old lodge-masters have opinions on bishops they never met and walk with a dead man's limp, and a Mother who has drawn on too many ends up as a very good account of other people.

### [Curse] The Bed Wants — Nuur Ssaath

Feeding the silt has no end. A garden left unplanted through a hard decade begins to take the unspecific: livestock, a careless child, the far root-walks. Something below the Tarnish Pools drops the leaves off Soul-Trees at the dark of the moon, and the rot-wives plant a garden on top of it every generation, deeper each time.

## How They Stand Against the Three Paths

**Body.** A maul does not care how many grandmothers you have. Their answer is to refuse a straight fight until he is tired, wet and sunk to the thigh. **Spirit.** The worst of it: a reader goes past the rind into the Meld, and one poisoned Core hurts everyone in the cloud. **Fate.** A clever pull need only lean on what the Court depends on, a salt cart's axle at the right ford, a cloudless week. The Court has no answer except that Fate fighters also die, and the fruit is very good.

---

## Final Definition

The Moss Court is the people who took death at its word. They will not tell you the dead come back, whatever their manner suggests, and they will show you, with every courtesy, that nothing the dead knew has to go to waste. They have a tree for you. They have had it picked out for some time.

*We will keep it for you.*

And under that, from the rind-wife at Ghurash Hollow, drying her arms: *Nothing wants. Bring it something nice.*

# The Southern Warlords

*Seven houses of the Inner World's south, below the Center and beyond any living crown, what is left of the Empire of Sum-gol, each holding a thing the whole quarter needs and none of them holding it for anyone else.*

In the fat summer of 661, when the Mahou kings still sat at Sum-gol, the salt smuggler Tam Pennyroyal died owing money in every town between the Thousand Moorings and the Long Fields, and his widow, Goody Mordaunt, gave him the finest funeral the south had ever seen, in a coffin of painted cedar that took eight men to lift, because it held four hundredweight of untaxed sea salt and no Tam. The eight she hired from a Marrick company, who were paid by the day and asked no questions. The gray horses she hired from the Yan, who wrote down her name. Forty paid mourners in vermilion streamers went howling up the salt road behind it, and at the toll below Ashbury the Ryu toll-man took off his hat, charged the full toll on one corpse, and waved them through, because there are limits. A Corwen agent sold her Wellhead water to sprinkle on the bier, by the cask. A Lambert clerk at the ford looked once at the depth of the wheel-ruts, wrote down a number, and followed the procession for two days, weeping loudly, and a crow came down out of a Yukari cedar and rode the last twenty miles on the lid. At the graveside, before three hundred people and the priest, the clerk stood up and read the count: the deceased had gained a great deal of weight since market day. The sun went down at that moment, which ended the Marrick contract, and the eight men grounded the coffin and walked home, and the lid split, and the whole south stood in the road scooping up salt in its hats and singing. They found Tam in a Ryu barge three weeks later, thoroughly pickled. Sum-gol's post delivered every house's bill to the widow on the same morning. She paid them all, cleared seven silver on the day, and married the clerk.

By the Yukari record, which is the only one that has all of it, six of the seven houses made money off that funeral and the seventh wrote down who did. You may now read the rest of this page as commentary.

The north counts its warlords at seven and seats six. The south counts seven too, and seats six, and the empty chair is Sum-gol's. That is the first thing a northerner learns about the south and usually the only thing he believes: that the southern houses are softer, warmer and richer than his own, and fight with money. The second half is true. The rest is the kind of thing a man says about a country he has only ever seen from the far end of a spear.

Where the Kessen road and the salt road come out from around the Scourge of Hell, past the last of the Center, the Inner World stops being a shield of cold rock and turns green. The ground drops in long steps toward the southern sea: cedar valleys climbing into cloud on the east, the open grass of the Grey Plain in the middle, the spring country of the Wellheads steaming in the morning, iron hills and salt pans around Sum-gol, and at the bottom of it all the white shelf of the Holy Sea of Alabaster, where the cliffs are the color of milk and the water under them is so clear the fishing boats seem to hang in the air. It is loud country. Market bells, temple gongs, the hum of the kept springs under every terrace and lamp, big gray horses calling across the plain at dusk, cedar-smoke and orange blossom and hot iron. The north buys its horses here, its oil, its salt from the southern pans, half its sealed casks of Wellhead water, and every one of its grudges about southern courtesy.

None of the seven was ever bound by the Kōkan crown. The Moto held the North; the warlords hold what lies beyond the Center, and the long war of the last forty years was fought across their ground as often as across the north's. Some sold to it. Some bled in it. One was finished by it.

## How the Empire Fell

They were bound by another crown once. For three hundred years the Vermilion Dynasty of Sum-gol was the empire of the whole South, from Kessen ford to the southern sea, and every house below held its land of the Mahou emperors: the Holy Sea paid in records, the Long Fields in spearmen, the Wellheads in water, the Grey Plain in horses, the Thousand Moorings kept the fleet, and the Yukari, who were old when the Mahou were young, paid one crow a year and entered it. It ended on the first day of autumn in Year 602, when the Emperor Rokju Mahuo, who had doubled the salt tax to pay for his domes, walked out to call his yearly roll before the whole South under the vermilion banners, and the South stopped answering. A Lambert clerk read out what the throne owed the Archive. The Marrick company in the square looked at the sun, saw that its contract had run out at noon, and grounded spears. The Corwen casks had been sold elsewhere. The Yukari sent a crow. The Baengnok, the White Stag of the line, came when he called, looked at him, and walked out of the East Gate. The full account is on [[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Vermilion Dynasty of Sum-gol|the Dynasty's page]].

Only two houses stayed. **The Yan and the Ryu still answer to the name of Sum-gol**, which costs them a recitation and a letter each autumn. Lambert, Yukari, Corwen and Marrick stand apart, and the Mahou went on as kings at Sum-gol alone for three more generations, until the war. That is why the south has powers instead of provinces, and why every house on this page keeps an old imperial grievance in the same drawer as its new ones.

## 1. House Yukari

**Seat:** The Cedar Enclave, on a hilltop above the Yukari valleys

**Realm:** [[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Silver Cedars of Yukari|The Silver Cedars of Yukari]], the Crow's Reach

**Territory:** The Yukari valleys, east of Sum-gol: a country of cedar so tall and close that the light comes down between the trunks in thin bars of green-gold

**Identity:** The eye. House Yukari sees what the others are doing.

**Current head:** Lord Naiser Yukari

**Words:** *The crow perches. The crow watches. The crow remembers.*

**Colors:** Black and silver, silver threading through dark

The oldest blood in the south and the least interested in ruling it. The Yukari are an archaic bloodline before they are a house, and the house is the shape the bloodline took when the world forced it to hold ground: the valleys, the villages that pay the tithe, the long cedar hall with its covered walk and its raked white gravel and its three standing stones that throw no shadow, and wards laid along every approach that touch whoever comes up the southern road long before anyone at the gate has seen him. The Yukari name is chosen at the Telling, not inherited, which makes the house the only one of the seven whose members are all volunteers.

They came through the war harder than anyone in the north knows. The Zettari struck the Yukari valleys in the last four years, the Seikai turned, the Soul Plane broke, and Lord Naiser's father went into the seam and held it. The house came out of it having won, and has spent every month since telling everyone who will listen that it has no wish for more land. Its terms at the Cedar Enclave were plain: no Yukari knowledge for war, no Yukari name carried as a banner, no touching the spirit world without Yukari counsel, every promise made in the hall entered in the Yukari record. *And when Sum-gol is settled, the Mahuo royal line restored there, whatever is left of it, and the Yukari stepping back.*

In the south they are the house everybody wants at the table and nobody wants watching them across it. A Yukari reads a room the way a cartographer reads a coastline, and the warlords who have sat in the Cedar Enclave come out of it describing the same feeling: of having been measured, comprehensively, without hostility, by someone who will remember the measurement for the rest of their lives.

## 2. House Mahuo, the Mahou Line

**Seat:** Sum-gol, *vacant*

**Realm:** [[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Vermilion Dynasty of Sum-gol|The Vermilion Dynasty of Sum-gol]]

**Territory:** Sum-gol and its country, the iron hills and the salt pans at the end of the Kessen road

**Identity:** The crown that was carried. House Mahuo is what happens to a name used as a banner.

**Current head:** Cozbi Mahuo, Head of House Mahuo and Master of the Sum-gol Post, who refuses the crown and wears olive and white, leaving the vermilion on the banners.

**Words:** *Call, and It Answers*

**Colors:** Vermilion and white

Once the richest seat in the south, and for three hundred years the throne of all of it. Sum-gol sits where the Kessen road ends and the south begins, on iron and on salt, and for longer than the Moto have had a crown the Mahou line ruled it, as emperors of the whole South until 602 and as kings of Sum-gol after that: summoners whose arts called things across the Veil and bound them, and whose iron and salt went north in every cart that ever used the road. *The Mahuo's salt. The Sum-gol iron.* Every northern quartermaster still says the two together.

The war ate them. Their name was carried at the front of other people's armies until there was nothing left of it but the carrying; the Moto put Mahuo banners over Moto columns, the Yukari and the Zettari each fought under the excuse of protecting them, and when the time came the house was destroyed. **Every one of the three tells it differently.** The Yukari say the Moto stood back and let the Yukari slaughter them, because a house turned into a symbol is very easy to kill and nobody weeps for a symbol. The Moto blame the Yukari. Kushara blames them both. *All three are right about the other two.*

What is left is a post. The Mahuo post at Sum-gol still keeps its crows and its couriers, and every letter between the north and the Yukari valleys goes through it; Ulf Stark's last crows south went that way. A house that was a crown now carries the south's letters, by crow and relay rider, and carries them so well that nobody has thought to ask who is paying the couriers, or whom they answer to, or what a summoning line does with its arts when it has no hall left to call them into. The chair at the warlords' table is kept for Sum-gol. **It is the only one with nobody sitting in it, and every other house at the table has a reason to want it filled by somebody in particular.**

## 3. House Lambert

**Seat:** Sallowgate, the landward gate of the Holy Sea of Alabaster

**Realm:** [[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Holy Sea of Alabaster|The Holy Sea of Alabaster]], whose capital is the city of Alabaster

**Territory:** The Sea's land marches, from the white shelf north to the edge of the Grey Plain, and the Sea's courts wherever they sit

**Identity:** The count. House Lambert keeps the Holy Sea honest about its numbers.

**Current head:** Léonard Lambert, Warlord of the Lamberts, Keeper of Sallowgate

**Words:** *Count True*

**Colors:** White and ink-black, a balance on the field; the warlord rides in gilded harness under scarlet, with the Sallowgate bear on his breast

The Holy Sea of Alabaster is a theocracy on a white shelf above the southern sea, ruled by its Palatine from the Alabaster Court, hosting a Sancta Lux chapter hold that reports past him, and built from top to bottom to weigh one commodity: a sworn account. **The Lamberts are the hands that do the weighing.** For as long as the Sea has had courts there has been a Lambert at the bench beside the cleric, counting: tariffs at the gate, grain in the store, witnesses in the dock, the days between a lord's promise and a lord's delivery. The Sea measures testimony. The Lamberts measure everything the testimony is about.

They are the Sea's warlord house because a holy see needs somebody who is allowed to carry a sword, and the Lamberts have held Sallowgate and the land marches for the Palatines since before the current line of Palatines could count. Their levies are small, drilled, and paid on the day, and their officers are famous across the south for a habit no other house has: they will tell a general his own numbers before battle, out loud, in front of his captains, and will not soften them. **A Lambert who gives a false count is struck from the house.** The house has done it. It keeps the list.

There is an older thing in the blood that the house does not discuss. A great many Lamberts are born with Crystals that never wake, and some of those grow up able to fight anyway, reading a blow before it is thrown with no faculty to read it with, so that a spectator calls it luck and a practitioner calls it impossible. *Nobody outside the house has ever counted how many Lamberts there are.* The house has. It has not shared the figure.

Lamberts travel. Cadet branches have gone north on the Sea's business for generations and stayed: **Osbert Lambert**, tariff-master at the Hallenfeld junction, kept the ledger of Tenrai grain diverted from the starving provinces and sent a fair copy north, which is about as Lambert a thing as a man can do.

## 4. The Yan Hall

**Seat:** Qingyuan, on the Grey Plain

**Realm:** [[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Jade Plains of Qingyuan|The Jade Plains of Qingyuan]], in the Dynasty of Sum-gol

**Territory:** The Grey Plain, the open grass between the Wellheads and the iron hills, where the tall southern horses are bred

**Identity:** The horse. Every army in the Inner World rides out of the Yan pastures, and the Yan know which.

**Current head:** The Venerable Hall-Master of the Yan, First of the Grey Plain, Keeper of the Thousand Bloodlines, Elder Yan Shouyi

**Hall maxim:** *The Plain Remembers the Hoof*

**Colors:** Gray and jade, a running horse in silver

A lineage hall in the old southern style, with ancestral tablets nine rows deep and a title for every rung of the family, which the Yan recite in full at every introduction and which takes, for a senior elder, about as long as it takes to water a horse. Their wealth walks. The tall gray southern horse, a hand higher than anything the north can breed and dappled like river stones, comes from the Yan studs at Qingyuan and nowhere else, and the Yan keep the bloodlines in ledgers older than the Holy Sea's: sire, dam and grandsire for every animal they have ever sold, and the name of the man who bought it.

That last column is the Yan's real stock in trade. **They sold to everyone in the war.** Ashmore's big southern horses were Yan horses; so were the Tenrai's. The Hall-Master can tell you which side bought how many remounts in which season, which is to say which side was expecting to lose cavalry, and when. *The Yan take orders, in both senses, and never a side.*

Their own horsemen are few and superb, archers who shoot from the saddle at the full gallop, turning in the stirrups, with the gray horses running flat out under them like water poured downhill. When the Yan ride out in force, it is to make sure a buyer has paid.

*The full codex: [[Factions, Bloodlines & Institutions/Houses and Bloodlines/The Houses of the Inner World/The Yan Hall|The Yan Hall]].*

## 5. House Ryu

**Seat:** Haeseong, the sea-fortress at the mouth of the salt road

**Realm:** [[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Copper Coast of Haeseong|The Copper Coast of Haeseong]], in the Dynasty of Sum-gol

**Territory:** The Thousand Moorings, the warm southern coast east of the Holy Sea, and the salt road that runs north from it to Ashmore's junction

**Identity:** The sea. What comes into the Inner World by water comes in through House Ryu.

**Current head:** Admiral-Lord Ryu Ha-eun

**Words:** *The Tide Pays Twice*

**Colors:** Sea-green and copper

Once the Mahou line's admiralty and its southern ports. When the Mahuo were emperors and then kings, the Ryu kept their ships, their harbor dues and their salt fleet, and were the second family of Sum-gol; when the Mahuo fell, the ships, the harbor dues and the salt fleet were all still sitting at Haeseong, with nobody left to send the revenue to. **The Ryu left the Mahuo's wealth exactly where it sat, and simply stopped forwarding it.** The south has never settled whether that is theft. The Ryu would like it noted that the Mahuo have never asked.

Haeseong is the brightest city in the south: white walls, green copper roofs, a harbor packed with junks and coasting galleys and the long low salt barges, lanterns strung across the water every night of the year, and a company of lamp-wardens hired down from the Wellheads at great expense so the lighthouse can burn worked light that reaches forty miles out, who walk off the tower every few years over the fee and are paid before the fishing fleet can get home. The salt road begins at its quays and runs north to the junction below Ashbury, and every sack of southern salt that ever crossed the Ashmore marches paid a Ryu toll on the way.

They will be the first house to object if anybody restores the Mahuo royal line at Sum-gol, and the last to object out loud. Admiral-Lord Ha-eun sat at the Cedar Enclave and heard Naiser Yukari promise exactly that, and smiled, and asked a very good question about harbor dues.

## 6. House Corwen

**Seat:** Brightwater, at the head of the spring country

**Realm:** [[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Sapphire Wells of Brightwater|The Sapphire Wells of Brightwater]]

**Territory:** The Wellheads, the steaming upland of southern Wellspring sites whose sealed casks go north by river barge to the ward-cutters of Kharven-Seat

**Identity:** The springs. House Corwen keeps the south's power in the ground.

**Current head:** Maud Corwen, Warden of the Wellheads, Lessee of the Guild

**Words:** *The Water Keeps Faith*

**Colors:** Kingfisher blue and brass

A chartered family, the only one of the seven that holds its ground by Guild lease rather than by blood or conquest, a lease it took out the moment the empire broke, and the richest per acre. The Wellheads are a high, wet, luminous country of hot springs and cold springs side by side, terraced pools in every color from milk-jade to rust, an assay post on every wellmouth and brass-bound carts of sealed casks going off downhill toward the river, and the hum of the kept pools so constant that Corwen children who leave home cannot sleep for the silence. Every worked Wellspring site (a Core, in common speech) under Guild lease from the Wellheads to the southern sea sells its hours through a Corwen keeper, and the keeper can close it. The ward-cutters who re-cut the wards on the Seat's rock each spring quench their chisels in water out of these casks, at a price Maud Corwen sets, and have been known to say what they think of it.

Their trouble is the water. **Since the war the springs in the south have gone bad**, and nobody will say why. Some taste wrong to anyone with the tongue to taste; villages downstream of them are sick, and the physicians do not know what they are treating; the forks in the Corwen assay posts ring as true as they ever did, and the Corwen wardens read the forks. The Yukari say they can walk the springs from the mouth to the source and tell you where the harm is and whose hand is on it. *House Corwen has not yet asked them to.*

Maud Corwen is a big, cheerful, sharp-eyed woman who knows the lease terms of every spring in the south by heart, and who has never in her life been seen to drink the water.

## 7. House Marrick

**Seat:** Hammerford, on the Long Fields

**Realm:** [[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Ochre Fields of Hammerford|The Ochre Fields of Hammerford]]

**Territory:** The Long Fields, the levy country between the Grey Plain and the marches of the Center, and every muster ground in the south that will pay

**Identity:** The wall. House Marrick sells the southern rank.

**Current head:** Gerrit Marrick, Captain of the Long Spear

**Words:** *Shoulder to Shoulder*

**Colors:** Ochre and black, in squares

The north fights in knots and wedges and long Keld lines that move like weather. **The south fights in a wall**, a rank of men standing shoulder to shoulder because they cannot trust themselves to stand anywhere else, and charging in it with a sound like a building falling over. That rank was Marrick's before it was anyone else's. The Long Fields have bred spearmen for three hundred years, as the empire's levy country first and for hire since 602; the Marrick captains drill them in the stubble after harvest, sixteen deep, long ash spears and drums, and hire the finished companies out by the season to whoever has the coin.

Half the southern hosts in the war had Marrick companies at their center, and some years both halves. The house considers this a professional courtesy. A Marrick company fights for its contract to the last day of the season and not one day past it, and has been known to finish a battle at noon on the day its contract ran out by grounding spears in the middle of the field and walking home through both armies, unharmed, because both armies would be hiring next season.

Gerrit Marrick is sixty, loud, scarred, very funny, missing an ear, and the only warlord in the south who has fought in every war of the last forty years on at least two sides. He says the north never understood the rank. *"You stand in it because the man on your left is standing in it, lad. Brave's got nothing to do with it."*

## The Table at the Cedar Enclave

The seven have had no emperor since 602 and none of them wants another, unless it would be them. What they have instead is a table: a meeting called when the south's business outgrows any one house, sat at whichever seat the calling house names, with the chair for Sum-gol kept empty at it, and its decisions binding on nobody who did not agree to them. Since the war the table has sat at the Cedar Enclave, because the Yukari asked, and because every house wanted every promise entered in a record somebody else was keeping.

What the south wants, in one line each:

| House | Wants |
|---|---|
| **Yukari** | To be left to perform a lawful function, the Mahuo restored, and nobody's war fought with Yukari sight |
| **Mahuo** | Whatever is left of it, its own name back off other people's banners |
| **Lambert** | The count to come out right, and the Holy Sea's courts heard north of the border |
| **Yan** | Every army to need horses, and none of them to win too quickly |
| **Ryu** | Sum-gol left empty, the salt road open, and the harbor dues uncontested |
| **Corwen** | The springs clean before anyone outside the Wellheads proves they are not |
| **Marrick** | A long peace with a lot of small wars in it |

## The South and the North

**The Kessen road.** The western of the two trade roads between the north and the south, and the one the Post rides, runs from Kessen ford down the Scourge's west flank through the low country to Sum-gol and on past the foot of the Yukari valleys. When Kagenori Tenrai Moto put it under martial law he cut the Post's line between the north and the Yukari at once, and every southern house lost money on the same morning. *That was the first time in forty years the seven agreed on anything.*

**The empty red chair.** The north has had nobody in the red chair for forty years, and the war that emptied it has only just stopped. The Tenrai speak for the Hall of Verdicts and collect on the road, and the seven lines watch each other across it. Each southern house has made its own reading of who sits next. **None of them has yet sent a delegation**, because the first house to send one tells the other six what it thinks the chair is worth. *Later hand:* what comes out of it within these years is the Northern Empire of Whitemere, with the Kingdom of Kharven as its capital, and the south has been arguing about the price ever since.

**The Moto.** The Moto are eating inward: closing the roads between their own provinces, turning back their own grain convoys at their own posts. The Yukari believe the next blood the Moto spill will be their own. Every other house at the table is working out what a Moto civil war will pay.

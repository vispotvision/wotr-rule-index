# The Ten Great Houses of Drakyssia

*Vaer'Kharath — "The Ten Wings Beneath the Throne." Outer World imperial order.*
**Supreme House:** House Drakhalis · **Sovereign allegiance:** The Dragon Sovereign of Drakyssia
**Function:** Continental administration, military specialisation, resource stewardship, trade, breeding, infrastructure, diplomacy, Aether conservation, preservation of draconic civilisation
**Foundational principle:** No House possesses the Empire. Every House possesses something the Empire cannot afford to lose.

### The Structure

Drakyssia's dominance over the Outer World does not rest on the Dragon Sovereign alone. Beneath the imperial throne stand ten ancient draconic dynasties whose territories, bloodlines, armies, industries and institutions are the structural foundation of the empire. Each holds an **Imperial Charge**: a responsibility whose failure can endanger the whole Outer World.
The Houses deliberately overlap, and none is meant to become self-sufficient. The House with the greatest army does not hold the treasury. The House holding the treasury does not hold foreign trade. The House holding trade does not hold the Dragon Dens. The House with the greatest mines does not hold the forges. The House holding the forges does not command the Dragon Roads.
This was formalised under **Kain Drakhalis**, who understood that a dragon noble controlling every necessity of war eventually becomes a rival sovereign. Drakyssia therefore runs on **interdependence enforced by supremacy**.

### 1. House Drakhalis — The Royal House

**Formal title:** House Drakhalis, the Blood of the Eternal Throne
**Seat:** The Drakhalis Throne Spire, Kra'Vorrak · **Heartland:** Central Drakyssian Heartlands
**Charge:** Sovereignty, imperial command, succession, continental strategy, preservation of the Kan inheritance
Descending from **Kain Drakhalis**, nephew of Dova'Kan and Avo'Kan, the House carries one of the most prestigious draconic bloodlines in the Outer World. Its authority derives not from wealth alone, numbers alone or religious legitimacy alone, but from the historical unification of all three. Across a reign of more than seven thousand years Kain broke rival claimants, subdued rebellious Houses, shattered external invasions and reorganised the Great Houses around clearly defined imperial responsibilities.
The House holds the **Right of Final Command**. The Dragon Sovereign traditionally emerges from Drakhalis, though ancient law permits another sufficiently legitimate descendant of the founding blood to inherit under extraordinary circumstance.
**Controls:** the Imperial Throne · the War-Wing Synod · declaration of continental war · recognition of Great House succession · Dragon Bond permissions at the highest level · imperial treaties · recognition of foreign sovereigns · appointment of High Dragon Generals · continental Dragon Road law · final adjudication between Houses · recognition of legitimate bloodlines · imperial succession · mobilisation of the Ten.
It also keeps the **Throne Reserve**, a military force deliberately separate from every other House, answering only to the Sovereign.
What Drakhalis authority gives the continent is exceptionally rare among dragons: **predictability.** A human kingdom negotiating with House Dovaryn knows an imperial treaty outranks a Dovaryn lord. An Elven enclave threatened by an unauthorised dragon can appeal to imperial law. A merchant crossing four Houses' territory does not renegotiate sovereignty at every border. The throne turns draconic supremacy into government.
> All Wings Return to the Throne.

### 2. House Dovaryn — The Great Wing

**Formal title:** House Dovaryn, Lords of the Ten Thousand Wings
**Seat:** Vhar'Dova, the Sky Arsenal · **Heartland:** Northern Drakhal Spine and the Iron Roost corridor
**Charge:** Aerial warfare, Wyvern breeding, rapid deployment, sky patrol, continental interception
Dovaryn possesses the largest Wyvern air force in the Outer World, and no other House comes close. Its mountain territories hold nesting valleys, cliff hatcheries, aerial training grounds, military roosts, wind tunnels and breeding sanctuaries sustaining tens of thousands of Wyverns.
Dovaryn doctrine treats the Wyvern not as a lesser dragon but as the ideal military organism for rapid aerial war. True Dragons hold greater individual power; Wyverns hold **numbers, speed, manoeuvrability, formation discipline and replaceability**. That distinction lets Dovaryn place wings almost anywhere in the Outer World faster than conventional armies can march.
**Maintains:** the largest Wyvern military population · imperial aerial patrols · border interception wings · long-distance reconnaissance · Dragon Road security · emergency courier squadrons · anti-piracy patrols · search-and-rescue wings · rider academies · military breeding · airspace surveillance · rapid reinforcement. Its greatest formation is the **Grand Spearhost**, reserved for continental war.
Dovaryn makes distance irrelevant: a rebellion thousands of miles from Kra'Vorrak can be seen within days and reinforced soon after, and no enemy can assume mountains, rivers, forests or broken roads will protect them. The same wings protect civilian air routes, merchant caravans, isolated settlements and coastal shipping, and when flood or earthquake severs a region they are usually the first imperial force able to deliver medicine, food, messages or evacuation. They are feared as the first sign of invasion and respected as the first sign that help has arrived.
> Before the Enemy Sees the Mountain, We Have Seen the Enemy.

### 3. House Zhaeryn — The Golden Scale

**Formal title:** House Zhaeryn, Keepers of the Auric Depths
**Seat:** Zhaer-Khazad, the Golden Mountain · **Heartland:** Serrathan Highlands and Golden Escarpment
**Charge:** Gold, precious metals, gemstones, imperial reserves, mining law, monetary stability
Zhaeryn controls the largest concentration of gold mines in the Outer World, extending beneath the Serrathan Highlands through surface mines, Wyrm-dug shafts, underground settlements, fortified vaults, mineral rail systems and ancient extraction chambers. Beyond gold: silver, platinum, diamonds, emeralds, cobalt, manganese, nickel and several rare magical ores.
The House became enormously wealthy. Imperial law ensured it did not become enormously independent — the richest mines are **Crown Resources under Zhaeryn stewardship** rather than absolute private property.
**Manages:** imperial gold reserves · precious-metal extraction · gemstone certification · mining licences · coinage metals · strategic stockpiles · mine safety · deep-earth surveying · anti-smuggling · royal tribute assessment · mineral taxation · wartime reserves.
Its vaults keep the empire functioning through drought, war, failed harvest and trade disruption. Its mining towns employ enormous numbers of Humans, Elves, Dragonkin, Wyrms and smaller draconic peoples, and the House enforces extraction limits around Wellsprings — the Outer World having already learned what happens when a civilisation mines metaphysical resources without restraint.
> Gold may be exhausted. The land may not.

### 4. House Avarax — The Merchant Wing

**Formal title:** House Avarax, Masters of Coin and Crossing
**Seat:** Avar-Kesh, the City of a Thousand Contracts · **Heartland:** Eastern Sun Coast
**Charge:** Trade, markets, customs, foreign commerce, shipping, caravan law, commercial diplomacy
The commercial engine of the empire. Its dragons are stereotyped as negotiators, mathematicians, multilingual scholars, shipowners, financiers, contract-keepers and extraordinarily patient political predators. Avarax keeps commercial enclaves in nearly every major Outer World port and negotiates with human kingdoms, Elven sanctuaries, independent cities, foreign empires, and draconic Houses that refuse ordinary diplomacy.
**Oversees:** foreign trade · imperial customs · merchant licensing · port tariffs · continental trade standards · caravan protection agreements · maritime commerce · currency exchange · trade arbitration · merchant Dragon Roads · imperial warehouses · importation of Aether-rich resources.
The House answers one of Drakyssia's great weaknesses: **the empire cannot produce everything it needs on depleted land.** Avarax imports Aether-rich reagents, healing materials, Essence-bearing plants, specialised timber, catalysts, food during shortage and foreign magical technology. It also created standardised weights, shipping protections, commercial arbitration, recognised caravan marks and cross-House merchant law, so a human trader can cross draconic territory without personally negotiating with every dragon whose mountain shadows the road.
> What War Cannot Reach, Trade Already Has.

### 5. House Maelkar — Keepers of the Dragon Dens

**Formal title:** House Maelkar, Wardens of Egg and Ancient Sleep
**Seat:** Mael'Kara, the First Den · **Heartland:** Volcanic valleys beneath the Black Scale Ranges
**Charge:** Eggs, hatcheries, nesting grounds, hibernation chambers, wounded-dragon recovery, genealogy, population preservation
Maelkar holds perhaps the most sacred infrastructure in Drakyssia outside the Dragon Catacombs. The **Dragon Dens** are enormous protected environments built for every stage of draconic life: volcanic incubators, cold caverns, vast mountain chambers, artificial lakes, systems running for miles underground. Eggs are protected here. Hatchlings are raised. Injured dragons recover. Pregnant dragons receive sanctuary. Elders enter hibernation, and ancient dragons sometimes sleep for centuries. The largest Dens are effectively underground cities.
**Maintains:** imperial hatcheries · egg protection · nesting registries · bloodline genealogies · hibernation chambers · elder sanctuaries · recovery caverns · hatchling nutrition · breeding-health records · abandoned egg adoption · rare subspecies conservation · population censuses. Maelkar law makes theft or deliberate destruction of a recognised dragon egg one of the gravest crimes in the empire.
Armies can be rebuilt, fortresses reconstructed, gold mined elsewhere. A bloodline permitted to go extinct cannot be purchased again. The House also manages draconic population growth against ecological carrying capacity, which on an Aether-depleted continent means protecting dragonkind partly by preventing dragonkind from destroying its own habitat.
> Before There Is a Warrior, There Is an Egg.

### 6. House Ormyr — The Deep House

**Formal title:** House Ormyr, Wardens Beneath the World
**Seat:** Ormyr-Duun, the Buried City · **Heartland:** Wyrmdeep beneath the Drakhal Spine
**Charge:** Wyrmdeep infrastructure, tunnels, groundwater, subterranean mapping, seismic monitoring, deep Wellspring protection
Ormyr governs downward. The House keeps exceptionally close ties to Wyrm populations and counts numerous ancient Wyrm bloodlines among its nobility and client families. Its engineers built much of the underground infrastructure connecting the Heartlands — some tunnels large enough for mature dragons, others carrying water, heat, ore, freight or military forces.
**Oversees:** Wyrmdeep Cities · imperial tunnel networks · seismic monitoring · volcano pressure observation · underground aquifers · geothermal infrastructure · deep Wellspring surveying · mine stabilisation · subterranean fortification · evacuation tunnels · underground military movement · detection of unauthorised excavation.
Ormyr allows the empire to survive beneath its own volcanic homeland, detecting eruptions, earthquakes, collapse, groundwater loss, magma migration and Wellspring instability. Its engineers frequently assist mortal kingdoms with wells, aqueducts, mines, earthquake-resistant foundations and reservoirs. In the driest regions of the continent an Ormyr water engineer can be worth more than an army.
> Every Throne Stands Upon Something.

### 7. House Rhaegyr — The Iron Flame

**Formal title:** House Rhaegyr, Masters of Forge and Scale
**Seat:** Rhaegyr's Crucible · **Heartland:** Volcanic southern Drakhal Spine
**Charge:** Weapons, armour, metallurgy, siege equipment, Dragonfire forging, military engineering, strategic manufacturing
Rhaegyr manufactures the physical machinery of Drakyssian power. Its forge-cities surround volcanic vents, magma chambers, geothermal fields and controlled Dragonfire furnaces, and its smiths combine mortal metallurgy with draconic heat tolerance and magical material science. Its greatest workshops forge components no ordinary smithy can heat.
**Manufactures:** dragon-forged weapons · scale armour · Wyvern rider equipment · drake armour · siege weapons · fortress gates · Aether-resistant chains · military tools · Dragonfire glass · heat-tempered steel · war platforms · specialised dragon barding.
The separation is deliberate and structural: **Zhaeryn owns the ore. Rhaegyr owns the furnace. Drakhalis owns the final war stockpile.** No single House holds the whole military production chain. The same forge network also produces farming tools, bridge components, waterworks, mining equipment, ship fittings, earthquake braces and ordinary civilian metal goods, indirectly supporting millions of non-dragons.
> Ore Becomes Empire in the Furnace.

### 8. House Eredan — The Harvest Scale

**Formal title:** House Eredan, Keepers of Herd and Harvest
**Seat:** Ered-Vaelor, the Granary Citadel · **Heartland:** Southern Grass Sea
**Charge:** Food security, livestock, agriculture, dragon prey reserves, grain storage, ecological management, famine prevention
The least glamorous necessity of empire and perhaps the most important. Dragons consume enormous quantities of biological matter, and feeding Drakyssia through uncontrolled hunting would devastate the Outer World. Eredan therefore manages immense livestock territories, breeding plains, grain reserves, fisheries, controlled hunting preserves and agricultural tributaries.
**Manages:** imperial grain reserves · dragon livestock herds · emergency food stores · agricultural treaties · ranch territories · hunting quotas · fishery protection · drought planning · famine relief · soil conservation · migration corridors · ecological carrying-capacity records.
During mobilisation it supplies mobile herds and preserved stores to dragon armies. Its agricultural law also protects mortal populations from predatory overhunting: imperial dragons cannot simply descend on protected livestock and eat what they please, compensation law applies even to nobles, and a farmer whose cattle are legally requisitioned by a dragon army must be paid. A dragon who eats them without authorisation may discover that being three hundred metres long does not exempt one from imperial taxation.
> Even Fire Must Eat.

### 9. House Ishkar — The Wellspring Wardens

**Formal title:** House Ishkar, Keepers of the Last Breath
**Seat:** Ish'Kael, the Reservoir Citadel · **Heartland:** Distributed holdings surrounding major Aether Wells
**Charge:** Aether conservation, Wellspring stewardship, Essence reservoirs, Dead Zone mapping, environmental recovery, magical rationing
No House better represents the reality of the modern Outer World. Ishkar's responsibility is not wealth. It is **what remains**. The House monitors surviving Aether Wells, volcanic recharge systems, exhausted Wellsprings, environmental Essence flow, atmospheric Aether, Soul-plane stability and regions approaching metaphysical collapse. Its scholars include some of Drakyssia's finest Wellspring specialists, environmental arcanists, Wyrm geomancers and Soul Crystal researchers.
**Controls:** Aether reservoir management · Wellspring extraction limits · recharge-zone protection · Essence surveys · Dead Zone mapping · Hollow Wellspring records · stored Aether reserves · magical rationing during emergency · recovery projects · draconic population-impact assessment · Aether Station regulation · continental depletion maps.
Its authority is unusual: even Great Houses can be ordered to reduce magical consumption if Ishkar proves a territory is approaching irreversible depletion, with final enforcement belonging to the throne. It closes exhausted mines, restricts nesting near fragile Wellsprings, restores damaged river systems, rotates high-consumption populations, maintains emergency reservoirs and identifies regions where magic must temporarily cease. The House is sometimes hated because it tells powerful people no. It is respected because the alternative is eventually having nothing left to argue over.
> Spend Nothing the World Cannot Regrow.

### 10. House Akhazar — The Outer Wing

**Formal title:** House Akhazar, Keepers of Roads and Foreign Crowns
**Seat:** Akhazar Gate, the Continental Embassy-Fortress · **Heartland:** Eastern approaches between Drakyssia and the Five River Kingdoms
**Charge:** Tributary administration, diplomacy, frontier law, protectorates, Dragon Roads, intelligence, relations with non-draconic peoples
Akhazar governs Drakyssia's relationship with everyone who is not Drakyssian. Its members are diplomats, frontier commanders, interpreters, intelligence officers, treaty scholars, governors, road wardens and imperial representatives.
Where Avarax handles commerce, Akhazar handles sovereignty, and the distinction is carefully kept. Avarax can negotiate the price of grain. Akhazar negotiates whether the kingdom selling that grain owes military passage to Drakyssia.
**Oversees:** tributary kingdoms · protectorate agreements · human-draconic diplomacy · Elven treaties · imperial embassies · border law · Dragon Road permissions · foreign intelligence · territorial arbitration · refugee corridors · frontier forts · imperial census of subject territories.
Akhazar lets the empire rule without occupying every mortal settlement, translating imperial authority into treaties, obligations, taxes, protections, borders and enforceable law. Its treaty courts give human and Elven states a formal mechanism to challenge territorial violations by lesser dragon lords. This does not make Drakyssia egalitarian. It makes Drakyssia administratively functional. Fear can conquer a kingdom; it cannot efficiently govern one for seven thousand years.
> An Empire Ends Where Its Word Cannot Travel.

### The Imperial Charges

| Great House | Imperial Charge | Principal Continental Function |
|---|---|---|
| Drakhalis | Sovereignty | Keeps the empire politically unified |
| Dovaryn | Sky | Largest Wyvern air force and rapid-response network |
| Zhaeryn | Wealth | Gold, gemstones, precious metals, imperial reserves |
| Avarax | Trade | Connects Drakyssia to the Outer World and wider Continuum |
| Maelkar | Continuity | Eggs, hatchlings, Dens, hibernating elders, bloodlines |
| Ormyr | Depth | Tunnels, aquifers, Wyrmdeep infrastructure, seismic security |
| Rhaegyr | Forge | Weapons, armour, infrastructure, strategic manufacturing |
| Eredan | Sustenance | Feeds dragons and mortals, prevents ecological collapse |
| Ishkar | Aether | Protects Wellsprings, manages continental depletion |
| Akhazar | Dominion | Treaties, tributaries, roads, frontiers, foreign relations |

Drakyssian children learn these as the **Ten Necessities**: *Throne. Wing. Gold. Trade. Egg. Depth. Forge. Harvest. Aether. Road.*

### Why the Houses Cannot Simply Rebel

Several of the Ten could individually destroy mortal kingdoms. Dovaryn could put thousands of Wyverns in the sky. Zhaeryn could purchase armies. Rhaegyr commands military manufacturing. Maelkar holds infrastructure essential to the future of dragonkind. Ishkar can restrict Aether reserves whole territories depend on.
Yet the system was built so that **specialisation creates dependence**. Dovaryn can field the Wyverns, but Eredan feeds them, Rhaegyr arms their riders, Zhaeryn finances prolonged mobilisation, Ishkar determines where enough Aether exists to sustain deployment, Ormyr maintains the infrastructure beneath their roosts, Avarax supplies foreign materials, Maelkar maintains breeding populations, Akhazar keeps supply routes through tributary territory open, and Drakhalis coordinates all of it.
A House rebelling against the throne therefore risks frozen money, closed trade, restricted Aether, eggs removed from imperial protection, sealed supply roads, terminated forge contracts, stopped food shipments, and neighbouring Houses authorised to occupy its territory. Kain Drakhalis built the mature system around one understanding of dragon psychology: a dragon can be powerful enough to fight everyone, and still cannot manufacture, feed, mine, breed and govern everything alone.

### The Council of Ten Wings

Beneath the War-Wing Synod sits a more political assembly. Each Great House holds one principal seat; **House Drakhalis presides.** The Council handles matters touching multiple Imperial Charges — a proposed gold mine near a surviving Wellspring might involve Zhaeryn for the ore, Ishkar for the Wellspring, Ormyr for the geology, Rhaegyr for strategic value, Avarax for export, Akhazar if the mine lies beneath a tributary kingdom, and ultimately Drakhalis if the Houses cannot agree.
On an Aether-depleted continent, this restraint is not idealism. It is survival.

### The Houses and the Eighty Percent

Drakyssia's claim over roughly eighty percent of the Outer World is administered not as one continuous province but as overlapping systems of authority. Dovaryn holds much of the sky. Ormyr influences the depths. Akhazar maintains roads and political borders. Avarax connects markets and ports. Ishkar protects the remaining Aether geography. Eredan holds the food-producing landscapes. Zhaeryn manages mineral territories. Maelkar protects the future population of dragonkind. Rhaegyr turns continental resources into infrastructure. Drakhalis gives all of it one sovereign direction.
The empire is less a single colour painted across a map than a web laid over the continent. A human farmer in the Southern Grass Sea might live beneath a Veyran king, sell livestock through an Avarax-chartered merchant, receive Eredan agricultural protection, travel an Akhazar-recognised road, watch Dovaryn Wyverns overhead, use Rhaegyr iron tools, and never meet a representative of House Drakhalis. Every one of those systems still leads back to Kra'Vorrak.

### The Balance of the Ten Wings

The Ten are not friends. They are allies, rivals, relatives, competitors, creditors, political enemies, military partners, and occasionally ancient grudges wearing ceremonial robes.
Zhaeryn and Avarax dispute mineral export prices. Dovaryn demands greater military budgets from the gold reserves. Eredan complains that Dovaryn's expanding Wyvern populations consume too much livestock. Maelkar opposes breeding programmes that prioritise military traits over bloodline health. Ishkar restricts Rhaegyr's geothermal Aether when forge consumption runs excessive. Ormyr accuses Zhaeryn mines of destabilising deep strata. Akhazar objects when aggressive Houses threaten agreements with mortal kingdoms. And House Drakhalis listens to all of them.
The friction is intentional. Drakyssia does not expect dragons to stop being proud, territorial and ambitious; its political system tries to make those instincts useful. Competition produces stronger Wyverns. Rivalry produces better metallurgy. Pride produces better cities. Greed uncovers new deposits. Prestige keeps roads, hatcheries, reservoirs and armies worthy of the Names attached to them. The throne's purpose is not to eliminate the instincts but to ensure they serve Drakyssia before they destroy it.
Among mortals: *Drakhalis gives the command. Dovaryn brings the wings. Zhaeryn brings the gold. Avarax opens the market. Maelkar guards the egg. Ormyr knows what lies below. Rhaegyr lights the forge. Eredan fills the table. Ishkar saves what magic remains. Akhazar carries the word beyond the mountain.*
Among dragons the phrase is shorter:
> Ten Wings. One Sky. One Throne.

# The Tiered Path

*The Nine Titles of the Accord, the Same in Every Guild*
> *"The soul does not climb. It deepens."*
>
> *The Codex, on the sworn. The Mercers' version is shorter: "Show me the silver."*

The most famous duel in the history of the Waxgate road never happened. In Year 702 a Journeyman of the Scarlet Tally, seven feet tall in a coat of painted plates and very drunk, tried to take a Grandmaster's token off an old woman at the Thornbank ferry on the grounds that she obviously could not fight him for it. He was entirely right. Mistress Annis Fellowe of the Company of Leeches and Bonesetters had not hit anything since her wedding. She held the token out to him on her palm and told him, in a voice that carried across the whole landing, that he could take it, and that it would get him the right to amputate. She added that she had set his captain's femur in the spring, that the Tally's surgeon's bill stood on her ledger at four hundred marks, and that the next time anyone in scarlet came to her door with a hole in him she would be delighted to look it up. The ferry waited. The Journeyman's own crew, who had been cheering, had by then gone very quiet and were taking him by the arms. He gave her his coat before the week was out, and it hangs in the White Aprons' hall at Waxgate under a sign that reads *Paid in Full*.

**A title says how far your guild trusts you in your trade.** That is all it says, and it says it in every hall from the New World's arc to the salt roads. Nine words, the same nine everywhere: a stranger can read them on a bit of silver and know what he may safely hand you. Only in a fighting guild do they also say how dangerous you are, and that is a separate table, further down this page.

## The Nine Titles

| Tier | Title | A merchant may sell up to | A craftsman may make up to | An adventurer may take commissions up to |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Initiate** | Common | E (stamped Marked) | T1 |
| **2** | **Apprentice** | Common | D (Proofed) | T2 |
| **3** | **Journeyman** | Uncommon | C (Tempered) | T3 |
| **4** | **Adept** | Rare | B (Instrument) | T4 |
| **5** | **Expert** | Rare | A (Hallowed) | T5 |
| **6** | **Master** | Very Rare | S to SS (Storied) | T6 |
| **7** | **Grandmaster** | Legendary | SS to SSS (Found) | T7 |
| **8** | **Archmaster** | Mythic | X to EX (Numinous) | T8 |
| **9** | **Paragon** | Unique | EX+ (Proscribed) | T9 |

**Every hall writes its own line under the same nine words.** A courier's title says what he may be handed sealed: an Initiate carries letters, a Master carries a king's ransom, and nobody will tell you what a Paragon of the Long Relay has carried. A healer's says what she may be paid to treat. A notary's says the value of the instrument he may attest. A free company's captain's says the size of contract she may sign. The hall confers it by its own lights: a term and a masterpiece among the makers, a ledger that balanced for twenty years among the merchants, a clear record of cases among the White Aprons. **A title needs no woken Crystal**, and most of the merchants' Grandmasters have never so much as warmed a kettle.

**The token.** A title travels as a silver token at the belt, the numeral on one face and the hall's mark on the other, with a dab of the hall's wax set in a recess at its edge. It appears nowhere on your window. A window is the soul reporting its own numbers, and a title is other people's opinion, which is why it needs silver. **A forged token is a great crime in every realm that has signed, and the forger is often hanged.** Think through what it does. A false Journeyman on a T3 gets a crew killed. A false Master in a mercer's stall sells a Very Rare piece that turns out to be nothing of the kind, and every honest token on that road is worth a little less by morning. A title lost is a scandal of the same family: the hall strikes it, and the news goes down the road faster than the owner can.

**The stamps.** The old words on the table (Marked, Proofed, Tempered, Instrument, Hallowed, Storied, Found, Numinous, Proscribed) survive as the Accord's stamps on finished work. A smith's title caps the Grade she may lawfully make, and a merchant's caps the Rarity he may sell, and a piece that outruns either needs a second signature from someone whose token covers it. A Master Work, the single piece at the end of a lifetime that does what its Grade and material should not be able to, always Unique or Mythic and always named, is the one thing no title caps, and the assay rooms have never agreed what to stamp on one.

**The pure alchemist.** A Crystal that never woke has no transformation to read, so the Accord certifies such an alchemist at the bench, on the work alone, up to Tier Five, Expert, and no higher. A bench Tier is certified and never read off the Crystal, and it caps what its holder makes exactly as any maker's title does. For a pure alchemist the bench Tier stands in for the maker's Grade the Tier Ladders read; work he makes to stand is capped also by the Grade of the Crystal-bearer whose seal it goes out under.

## The Fighting Guilds' Table

Adventurers' guilds, Accord agents, free companies and the rest who are paid to be dangerous keep the old Stage table as their yardstick, because in that trade the thing they are trusting you with is the fight. For them the title sits on the Stages and measures nothing the Stage has not already measured, and an examiner confirms it with a coil (the Accord's assay instrument) and a fight in the yard. The last column is the honor the Lattice confers on the sworn among them.

| Tier | Grade | Stages covered | Threat Tier and seal | Sworn honor |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1 · Initiate** | E | Murmuring | T1, Green | — |
| **2 · Apprentice** | D | Welling | T2, Green | — |
| **3 · Journeyman** | C | Ascension | T3, Green | — |
| **4 · Adept** | B | Flourishing | T4, Blue | **Concord Agent** |
| **5 · Expert** | B* → A → S | Splintering, Glory, Refraction | T5, Blue | **Sealwright** (enforcement specialists) |
| **6 · Master** | S → SS | Transcendence, Invocation, Realization | T6, Gold | **Warden of Duty** |
| **7 · Grandmaster** | SS* → SSS | Dissonance, Emanation | T7, Gold | **Envoy of the Lattice** |
| **8 · Archmaster** | X → EX | Principality, Zenith | T8, Obsidian | **Bearer of the Concord Flame** |
| **9 · Paragon** | EX+ → Unbound | Revelation, Apex | T9, Obsidian | **Chosen of the Codex** |

**The paper Master.** Drafts and blessings can supply a Catalyst, so Stages can be bought, and the wealthy are stronger because they are wealthy. A bought Stage carries the title on this table without argument, since the coil reads what is there. It also opens the door and no more: a Release must still be found and mastered by fighting, and the techniques, the rites and the read come from a sect or from years on the board. A rich man's son at Stage IX may hold no Release at all, while a poor hunter at the same Stage holds three. Crews have a word for such a token, the paper Master, and a good hall gives the son a crew of hard people who are paid not to use it where he can hear. The poor come up by dungeon luck, guild patronage and blessings, and a hall that finds one early will sign her for seven years and feed her like a racehorse.

The sections below read each title on the fighting table, as the sworn are taught it.

## Tier 1 · Initiate

> *"Every oath begins with a single task."*

The newly awakened, walking the Stage the Lattice calls **Murmuring.** Initiates have bonded their first fragment of Soul Crystal to the Lattice, if they are sworn, and can perceive Essence currents, **though they cannot yet shape or direct them with meaningful force.** Their resonance is faint, *a candle's flicker against the Lattice's vast hum*, Grade E and rising no higher than the twenty-fifth level.

Training focuses on foundational discipline: courier assignments, ward observation, record transcription, and basic Essence meditation under supervised conditions. On the board this is the fox at the hens, which nobody files, so an Initiate mostly carries poles for someone else and gets shouted at.

## Tier 2 · Apprentice

> *"Small flames test the hand that holds them."*

Crossed into **Welling.** Initial Essence flow stabilizes and they can channel basic techniques (minor wards, elemental shaping, rudimentary combat applications) at Grade D, through the fiftieth level. **Their Soul Crystal develops its first true harmonic trace**, marking them as recognized practitioners within the guild system.

Assigned to experienced mentors. Beast culling, local relic retrievals, minor Aether leak containment, defensive patrols.

## Tier 3 · Journeyman

> *"The soul learns to endure consequence."*

The threshold of independence: **Ascension.** Journeymen have proven they can operate without constant supervision: regional scouting, mercantile escort, small-scale corruption cleansing at Grade C, through the seventy-fifth level.

Their Crystal's resonance deepens noticeably. **Other practitioners can sense a Journeyman's approach by the harmonic weight they carry.** *Many spend years at this Tier, building the breadth of experience necessary for the demands ahead.* Most of the crews on any hall's wall are captained at this title, and most of the songs are about them.

## Tier 4 · Adept

> *"The first true burdens of Concord law."*

**Flourishing**, the Stage where identity stabilizes and the first true Spirit Axis bonds take root. Essence has matured into a stable, directional force capable of sustained combat, Wellspring interaction, and inter-guild cooperation. Grade B, the hundredth level, **the close of what the Lattice reckons a soul's first true Band.**
> The Lattice formally recognizes the sworn among them as **Concord Agents**, practitioners authorized to act **with the Accord's name behind their decisions.**

Monster suppression, Wellspring stabilization, relic retrieval from ancient or contested ruins.

## Tier 5 · Expert

> *"Reality begins to bend beneath your will."*

What the Accord calls Expert spans **three Stages the Lattice itself distinguishes**, Splintering, Glory, and Refraction, the cluster the Codex names the **Awakened Band**, carrying a soul from its hundred-and-first level to its two-hundredth.
> **It does not begin gently.** Splintering is a crisis in the Crystal's own architecture, fracturing it along whatever emotional fault line the practitioner has spent a life avoiding.
>
> Those who do not break instead burn brighter for it, reaching **Glory**, where the self is noticed and costs itself gladly for what it loves. **Refraction** completes the climb: the soul learns to see itself from outside, **and reality answers that dual sight by bending.**

An Expert's Soul Crystal hums with enough harmonic density to influence the ambient Aether around them, and their techniques begin to impress upon reality itself: Domain intrusions, suppression of corrupted spirits, large-scale creature hunts.

Sworn Experts who specialize in enforcement are granted the title of **Sealwright**, authorized to deploy containment fields, barrier networks, and dimensional anchors.

## Tier 6 · Master

> *"Your decisions change the balance of a realm."*

The three Stages the Codex clusters as **Sovereign** (Transcendence, Invocation, Realization), from the two-hundred-and-first level to the three-hundredth. Their Essence has been tempered through crisis after crisis **until their Soul Crystal resonates with the clarity of struck glass.**

Anyone at Transcendence can have a Domain, their own concept made real, and fame, rivalries and bounties follow it. At Invocation, **Wellsprings and Titanic law answer them as co-authors rather than sources to be pulled from.** At Realization, the Continuum itself stops merely permitting their Domain and begins accommodating it.

They command veteran crews under direct supervision: containment of failing Wellspring sites, regional magical catastrophes, and the delicate work of political or divine mediation. The Lattice elevates the sworn among them to **Warden of Duty**, practitioners whose judgment is trusted to operate **within the spirit of the Codex as well as its letter.**

## Tier 7 · Grandmaster

> *"The air itself resists your passage."*

The boundary the Codex calls **Mythic**, spanning Dissonance and Emanation from the three-hundred-and-first level to the four-hundredth.
> **Dissonance is the second and more dangerous crisis a Soul Crystal endures**: power and identity in open clash, the practitioner deliberately meeting a force capable of ending them and choosing, coherently, how to be met.
>
> Those who emerge rather than shatter reach **Emanation**, where the fractures Dissonance left behind **stop being wounds and become architecture.**

Their Essence pressure warps the ambient Aether in their vicinity: *lesser practitioners feel their approach as a tightening in the chest, a ringing at the edge of hearing.* Deployed for inter-realm operations, Titan interference, and the neutralization of high-order paradox entities.

The sworn among them are named **Envoys of the Lattice**, recognized across every realm connected to the Gate Network. **Commissions at this Tier require Concord Council majority approval.**

Hold this beside the old woman on the Thornbank ferry. Mistress Fellowe's token and an Emanation-Stage Envoy's token carry the same numeral, and only one of them makes the lamps gutter when she comes in. Both will be seated at the same table, and both will be annoyed about it.

## Tier 8 · Archmaster

> *"Where gods falter, the Accord endures."*

Walking **Principality** and then **Zenith**, the four-hundred-and-first level through the five-hundredth, **the furthest reach of canonical mortal coherence.**

At Principality the practitioner's doctrine becomes a recognized law of local reality; biomes carry it, the Crystal writes itself. At Zenith, that doctrine grows recognizable to something greater still: **a Hollow Throne resonating in answer, because the practitioner was finally seen, with no breakthrough needed to earn it.**
> Their Soul Crystals no longer merely store resonance. **They generate it**, producing a constant harmonic output that feeds the Lattice passively. **They are living wellsprings in miniature, their very existence a stabilizing force.**

Realm-scale cataclysms, divine corruption events, and the collapse of entire Domains. The sworn among them are marked as **Bearers of the Concord Flame**, living arbiters between mortal and divine law. *Their missions are recorded in the Codex as events of historical consequence.*

## Tier 9 · Paragon

> *"Only those whose names are written into law itself may stand here."*

**The Codex has no name for what lies past its own fifth Band, so the Accord supplies one.** These practitioners have crossed past the five-hundredth level into territory the Codex calls the Apotheosis approach, walking Revelation and then Apex.

At **Revelation**, a soul first meets an Archon or Titan as participant rather than witness or casualty, its Crystal dense enough to hold that pressure without collapsing. At **Apex**, that same soul learns to hold a Principle without fracturing, even briefly, even imperfectly, **a state that does not end, pressing forever against what it has not yet become.**
> Paragons on this table are woven into the Lattice's architecture. Their Soul Crystals resonate at frequencies that mirror the Wellspring of Law itself, and their actions ripple through the harmonic fabric of reality **with the weight of natural forces.**
>
> Cosmic-scale operations: restoration of dying Wellspring sites, prevention of universal collapse, confrontation with Epochal or Archonic forces. **Authorization for Zenith Decrees rests with the High Concordant alone.**
>
> **Few who receive such decrees survive their completion. Those who do are rewritten into myth.**

A Paragon of the Mercers has, at last count, sold one Unique piece and retired. She will not discuss the buyer.

## Why Nine Titles Describe Sixteen Stages

> The Codex counts each transformation of the Soul Crystal with exacting precision. **But the old Accord was a bureaucracy, and bureaucracies compress what they administer.** The nine words were first cut for the sworn, and every other trade took them later because they already worked on a strange road.
>
> Below the hundredth level, where the overwhelming majority of hunters will live and die, the fighting table tracks every Stage individually: Murmuring, Welling, Ascension, and Flourishing each earn their own title and their own privileges.
>
> **Beyond that threshold, so few practitioners survive to climb further that finer distinction serves no administrative purpose.** The Codex's own Bands already do the sorting the Guild would otherwise have to invent.
>
> Revelation and Apex lie outside those Bands entirely, in territory the Codex itself declines to number, **and so the Accord folds them into a single Tier and calls it what it has always called such souls.**

The Measurewrights consider the whole arrangement an insult to measurement. Their own coil reads a soul to the last decimal, and they have spent four centuries watching the world ignore it in favor of a piece of silver.

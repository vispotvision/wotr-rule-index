# The Powers of the Dark Ages

*Crowns, great houses, orders, guilds, sects, a crown-chartered society, a cult and the chartered companies: what each wants, what it holds, how it fights, and who it is set against.*
> *The company takes the ground. The crown claims it afterward. The guild hunts whatever lives on it. All three send the bill to the same town.*
>
> *saying of the arc settlements*

---

In the ninth year of the Dark Ages a river drake forty feet long, the green of old copper from the jaw to the root of the tail, came up out of a new Well under Sallowford and ate the ferry, with the ferry's mule. A party of the Well-Hunters' Company killed it on the shingle at noon on a fair day, one licensed captain and a dozen men with no license between them, and before its blood had reached the far bank the carcass had three owners. The crown's Reeve of the Game rode down with forty men in madder red and a writ calling the beast royal venison. The Company's factor came along the towpath with a contract, a clerk and the crew in wasp yellow, who were paid by the carcass and wanted it counted. And down the hill from Sedgeby Hall, at the head of sixty licensed hunters in hall green, came Dame Ysmay Thole, called the Fishwife of Sedgeby by people standing a long way off, her coat the hot pink of boiled ham, bawling the license fee so that it carried to the fairground.

Nobody would give way, so each side yoked oxen to the part it had reached. The Reeve took the head, the factor took the tail, and Sedgeby Hall took both hind legs, and the whole fair came down to watch with its mouths full, while the outer disciples of two rival sects sold places on the bridge. It lasted until the fourth bell. When the Company's captain tried to clear the hall's yoke with an art he called GREAT BIG ARM, Dame Ysmay met him in the shallows with a boathook, called LAST ORDERS in a voice that dropped every gull on the sandbar, and put him through the side of his own wagon. Then the drake came apart. The head went up the hill behind eighteen frightened oxen and took the corner off a tithe barn, the tail went through the Company's baggage, and nine people were dead by sundown, four of them from a bench on the bridge. The ford stank for nine years. All that afternoon nobody on either bank looked at the Well itself, and in the week that followed a quiet factor from Altherion bought the water-meadow above it for less than the oxen had cost.

All three parties entered the day in their books as trade. I have copied those three entries out for every clerk I ever trained, because they teach the order of things: who came first, who claimed afterward, who licensed whom, who was paid by the piece, who was sitting on the bench, and who was buying the meadow.

## The Shape of the Scramble

The Dark Ages are the years in which everything that can be owned is being claimed at once. Crowns are expanding, chartered houses are expanding beside them, and the Withering has thinned the ground under all of them until five things are worth more than they have ever been. Every power on this page wants some of all five, and most want one of them badly enough to go to war for it.

| What is scrambled for | Why it matters now |
|---|---|
| **Wells and rich ground** | A practitioner fills fast on rich ground and starves on thin, and a worked Core (a Wellspring site, in the common word) sells hours at its pool. A keeper's contract written at old rates is an inheritance, and a Well is the only thing that writes new ones |
| **Materials** | Good steel, Cloudmetal from the eastern arc, Riftstone and gravity ore from the Old World's rifts, the Master Material Ledger's Essence-born materials. The forges and workshops eat them faster than any one realm digs them |
| **Markets and labor** | A workshop needs hands and a buyer. Guild marks and fair-day reputations have made a buyer out of every sealed household, and the un-Tempered laborer is worth less every year the arrays improve |
| **Routes** | Kept roads and their tuned waystones, the beacon line, sea lanes, Dragon Roads, caravan water and the Concord Gates. Whoever holds the route sets the price of everything carried on it |
| **The hunting trade** | Wells breed Well-spawn, a Well worked harder breeds more, and every mine, Core and settlement near a Well now pays somebody to clear it. Hunting has become a business, sold by the party and priced by the share |

**The order of events is nearly always the same.** A company goes in first, under a charter, and takes the ground by buying it, surveying it, fortifying it or simply standing on it. The crown that granted the charter claims the ground afterward, once there is something on it worth taxing. The guilds follow the company, because wherever there is a Well there are Well-spawn, and wherever there is a paid hunt somebody has to license the hunters. **Each layer arrives believing it is the one in charge.**

### Layered rule

Day-to-day rule in most of the four quarters comes from three authorities stacked on the same ground.

| Layer | Holds | Its instrument | Where it is weak |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The crown or great house** | The land, the levy, the tax, and the right to hang | Descent, the army, and the court of the realm | It cannot run a workshop, and it cannot hunt a Well |
| **The chartered company** (a sworn partnership of merchants, licensed by a crown, whose backers hold shares in its ventures) | The trade: the workshops, the warehouse, the route, the store and the scrip | The charter, the roll of partners and the company's own guards | Its charter can be revoked, and one lost venture can ruin its backers |
| **The guilds** | The hunters, the license, and local policing of magic | The license, the guild court, the trace examiner and the hunting hall | It holds no land and keeps no standing army, and outside a signed circle it is only a guild |

**Each layer fights the others over jurisdiction, and which one wins differs by place.** On the New World's arc the charter is the ground, so the company and the guilds win. In the Inner World heartland crown law and guild law have grown into each other until nobody can say which issued a given rule. In the Old World the great houses win, and a foreign company enters only through a house's port on a house's terms. In the Outer World the throne wins outright, and the Ten Houses are their own companies. In Kharven there is no working crown and no instrument that binds, so whoever arrives with the most men and the best grain wins that season.

### How the powers fight

| Means | What it looks like on the ground |
|---|---|
| **Proxy and company war** | Company guards, hired hunters and "survey parties" do what a crown cannot be seen to do. A company's private army burns a rival's way-station and the crowns behind both exchange letters of regret |
| **Open war** | Levies, fleets, Wyvern wings and practitioners in the field. Rare between the great crowns, because it is ruinously expensive, and common at the edges where one of them meets a smaller one |
| **Economic war** | Cutting supply, buying up a commodity, crying down a rival's shares at the fair, closing a port, raising a toll, withholding grain. The cheapest war there is, and the one that kills the most people who never see a soldier |
| **Intrigue** | Bought clerks, forged assays, concealed coil readings on an heir, a charter revoked because the right person heard about a problem three times in one season |

**Most conflicts in the Dark Ages are all four at once**, and the parties would describe every one of them as trade.

## The Crowns and Great Houses

### The Dragon Throne of Drakyssia and the Ten Houses

*Outer World · an empire claiming roughly eight parts in ten of its quarter of the world*

**Wants.** Wells and rich ground above everything, because Drakyssia's own ground is Aether-depleted and it cannot produce what it needs on it. Reagents, Essence-bearing plants, catalysts and food in the lean years. Predictable roads through its tributaries.

**Holds.** The largest Wyvern air force in the Outer World, the richest gold of its quarter, the Dragon Roads, the Dens, and a treaty court system that lets mortal kingdoms appeal against lesser dragon lords.

**Fights by.** Economic war first, through House Avarax's commercial enclaves and Avarax-chartered merchants in nearly every major port. Rule over tributaries through House Akhazar's treaties and tributaries. Open war through House Dovaryn's wings, which can reach almost anywhere in the quarter faster than an army marches.

**Set against.** Any power buying Wells it depends on. Foreign companies entering tributary kingdoms without Akhazar's permission. And itself: Zhaeryn and Avarax quarrel over export prices, Ishkar restricts Rhaegyr's forges when they pull too much, Eredan complains that Dovaryn's Wyverns eat the herds.

**Fears.** Depletion that no conservation can reverse. A House grown self-sufficient. The day the throne stops being predictable, because predictability is the whole of what it sells to the mortals beneath it.

**At the door.** A baker in a tributary port buys her flour in an Avarax sack at an Avarax price, and when she appeals the price she pays a treaty clerk to write the appeal, also in Avarax's pay. A herder's child in the Eredan grass can name a Wyvern wing by its shadow before she can count the cows it ate.

**The seam.** Every Imperial Charge depends on another. A party that can starve one House of what a second House supplies turns the second against the first, and the Council of Ten Wings exists precisely because that happens.

### The Kusharan Throne and the Seven Pillars

*Old World · regional rule beneath the throne's rule over the quarter*

**Wants.** To keep its law its own. The Old World's polities are older than the Accord and have no interest in being harmonized, and Kushara means to meet the age as a buyer and a seller, never as a charter.

**Holds.** House N'Kharu's bannermen levy, House Vokari's gold and Deep Surveyors, House Malani's food network, House Obaru's medicines, House N'Yari's rare magical minerals, House Jomari's Gulf Fleet and ports, and House Zembari's Hidden Water Network across the northern desert.

**Fights by.** Supremacy rather than mass in open war. Economic war through the Pillars, each of which can close what it holds. Intrigue through the caravans: armies see armies, caravans see everything else.

**Set against.** Foreign companies that want N'Yari's minerals or Vokari's seams without Kusharan law attached. The Accord, whose jurisdiction the Old World has never accepted.

**Fears.** One Pillar monopolizing what another needs. A foreign company buying a Pillar's loyalty with better prices than the throne can offer. Wellspring damage from reckless extraction, which Vokari's surveyors have walked away from fortunes to avoid.

**The seam.** A foreign company can only enter through House Jomari's harbors. Jomari profits from every cargo it lets inland, and the Pillars that lose trade to foreign goods blame Jomari first and the throne second.

### The Ashen Crown of Kharven and its Banner Houses

*Inner World, the far north · a kingdom without a working center*

**Wants.** A crown worth following again. Each Banner House wants the crown restored on terms that keep its own function: the border, the wall, the grain, the timber, the tundra, the roads.

**Holds.** Ground and custom and little else. House Ashmore's grain in the Greenhollow, House Thornwall's ironwood and the Marrowhold corridor, House Hallenfeld's river junction and signal posts, House Greymane's western wall against Undaar-Keth, House Stark's eastern March, House Keld's tundra.

**Fights by.** Open war on the frontier, oaths that bind only while they are worth keeping, and food. The north lives poorer and older than the Accord cities; its wars are fought with the tools of an earlier span, and it prices loyalty by the bushel.

**Set against.** Undaar-Keth probing the western ridge. Marrowhold, which cuts grain and keeps sending inscription substrate, because it wants a buffer state and not an independent one. Any company that comes up the road while the center is empty.

**Fears.** Being a buffer. Being bought one house at a time. A winter in which House Ashmore's allocation decides who survives.

**At the door.** A ferryman on the Hallenfeld junction pays the house's toll in the morning and the Northern Road Company's guards in the afternoon, and nobody has ever been able to tell him which one is the law, because nothing there is written. A child in a Thornwall timber camp eats what Ashmore's clerk allotted to a house that is presently quarreling with Ashmore.

**The seam.** The Accord has no instrument that binds in Kharven and the seat has never asked for one, so anything done there is lawful until someone strong enough says otherwise. That cuts both ways, and every outsider who arrives learns it.

### Eresse

*The calendrical principality, and the elven old-ground beside the New World's arc*

**Wants.** Its own law undisplaced. Four Eons of Eressean law run on the old-ground beside the arc and the Accord did not replace them there. Consecrated gold kept out of the melting pot.

**Holds.** Sacred Gold, which cannot lawfully be melted and trades at a premium over bullion. The appointed windows, outside which nothing is decided. The old-ground's titles, which predate every charter on the arc.

**Fights by.** Delay. Nothing is settled outside its ceremonial time, urgency is treated as a defect, and no polity in the four quarters can negotiate with Eresse at speed. Against a company that has promised its subscribers a return this year, a principality that answers next season is a siege.

**Set against.** Arc companies whose charters run up against old-ground titles. Sancta Lux, whose Eresse chapter hold the elven kingdoms tolerate on sufferance and audit every year.

**Fears.** Speed. A company that acts inside a closed window and dares the principality to answer before the window opens.

**At the door.** A ferryman on the old-ground who wants the right to a new landing petitions in one window and is answered in another, and his grandchildren collect the answer, which is yes.

**The seam.** A deal struck with Eresse in its window binds for Eons. A deal struck outside it binds nothing. Whoever knows the calendar controls the timing of every settlement on the old-ground.

### Korvaeth and the Sunroot Regency

*The New World · a far quarter of the one land, with its own calendar, and a war about names as property*

**Wants.** Title. The Korvaeth War is a fight over whether a name can be held as property, and every holding on its ground turns on whether its name was validly transferred. The Sunroot Regency wants to hold a regency that split name from office.

**Holds.** The Korvaeth seat and the roads its forces have opened, entered in its own records as assizes rather than battles. The Regency holds its vote, which has stood for eleven years.

**Fights by.** Open war conducted as litigation. Names taken in the field, witness-fees paid, validated names held in reserve to be used against a rival in any court.

**Set against.** Each other. And any arc company whose charter names land whose name is in dispute, because an Accord charter over a Korvaeth holding is only as good as the name it was granted under.

**Fears.** The challenge form, which is two words long and which any commoner may lawfully say. A finding that voids unassembled holdings, which would void every such holding in the quarter at once.

**At the door.** A baker on Korvaeth ground keeps the deed to her oven folded in her shoe and has twice paid a witness-fee to prove the street is still called what it was called. Children there learn the two words of the challenge form at about the age they learn to swear, and are slapped for both.

**The seam.** On this ground a charter is a claim on a name, and a name can be challenged. A party holding a validated name holds a weapon every court in the quarter must hear.

## The Orders

### Sancta Lux

*The chartered body of measurement and consequence · the Grand Church at Altherion*

**Wants.** Its Wellspring rights and its tax exemption kept. Its jurisdiction over oath, status, legitimacy and bad faith kept. The kneeling Grand Angel reached, and heard, before anyone else reaches him.

**Holds.** The Oracle, the only instrument in the four quarters that does not drift. The Grand Church inside a republic that cannot evict it. Chapter holds in Eresse and Foraye, a legation at the Concord Citadel, and the Ember Magistrates' circuit through towns that will never see the Church.

**Fights by.** Measurement. The Church can be asked for a reading and cannot be refused one. Mirror trials read the accuser against the same reference as the accused, which is why every guild that has brought it a complaint hates it.

**Set against.** The Holy Inquisition, which it calls apostate. Sōhai, which calls the Scale the largest Singling in the history of the Realms. The secular guilds, with whom its relations are procedurally correct and personally poisonous.

**Fears.** The Seventh Objection made public, which would show its authority to be one tile wide. Contradictory law, which stops its Arbiters dead. Workings that present nothing to weigh.

**At the door.** A baker accused of short weight by a rival may ask an Ember Magistrate for a reading, and the flame-lit court will read her accuser against the same reference in the same hour. Most complaints in those towns are withdrawn before the lamp is lit.

**The seam.** The order publishes its false-positive rate every year and nobody outside Arbitration reads it. A party that does has the one document in the four quarters that says, in the order's own figures, how often the Scale's servants have been wrong.

### The Holy Inquisition

*The Burning Mandate · Rogue Body 7-C in the Accord's filings*

**Wants.** Correction. It holds that the reading needs no Ladder and that purification is mercy delivered early.

**Holds.** No fixed seat. Legal standing where the crowns and churches that back it rule, and the Gate (the state's lock on who may learn magic) to enforce in that territory. Chapter markers and small garrisons left in the places it has declared corrected, and a catalog of everything it has kept from the places it has burned.

**Fights by.** Campaign. A Circuit Justiciar takes a reading, a declaration is read in the square, forty-eight hours pass, and what refused the mask is treated as structural corruption.

**Set against.** Sancta Lux, which exiled its founders. The Guild Accord, which outlaws it wherever a circle has signed and has authorized enforcement for six years without coming. Every settlement on a Circuit Justiciar's road.

**Fears.** The Accord deciding the cost is worth paying and sending practitioners of matching Stage. The world learning what the catalog is for.

**At the door.** In a town on a Justiciar's road the ferryman's forty-eight hours are the busiest of his life, and he charges accordingly, and the town remembers what he charged long after the riders have moved on.

**The seam.** It does not govern what it takes. Every planted chapter is a small garrison far from support, and every marker is a record of where the Inquisition has been and a line toward where it goes next.

### The Void Vanguard

*The host of the Void Regency · under the Void Regent, the Void Generals and a High Chancellor*

**Wants.** The Material Plane, as a new home for Aelor. Raiding has never interested it. It means to stay.

**Holds.** Whatever ground its column stands on, a palace dragged through the breach, and the Prayers of the Hollow, which answer to the same Chancellor.

**Fights by.** Open war when it must and slow campaign by preference. It would rather use a people than kill them, and it has looked at the Marrowkin of Marrowhold, whose metal arms half the Inner World, in exactly that way.

**Set against.** Everything native to the plane, in principle. In practice, whoever stands between it and the peoples it means to use.

**Fears.** Nothing the four quarters have found.

**The seam.** A power that means to use Marrowhold needs Marrowhold intact. Whoever understands that knows which of the Void's threats are real and which are leverage.

## The Guilds and the Accord Forming

### The guilds, and the circles of the Guild Accord

*A communion of guilds, forming now, circle by circle*

**Wants.** The hunting trade licensed and the license paid for. Anyone may practice privately; taking pay for a working or a hunt needs a guild license, and every unlicensed hunter is a fee the guild did not collect.

**Holds.** The hunters, the hunting halls and the license. Trace examiners who read a working's traces as evidence. Local policing of magic, and the guild courts that try it. Where a circle has signed, the Articles and the Commission framework bind. Elsewhere a guild is only a guild, and its writ runs as far as its members' reputations.

**Fights by.** License and exclusion: a man who cannot get an attestation cannot get credit. Guild courts against crown courts over who may try a crime done with a working. And locally, directly: the guilds hunt down those who do wrong.

**Set against.** Crown courts, over jurisdiction. Chartered companies that hire unlicensed hunters cheap. Crowns that register Crystals and control texts and want to decide for themselves who may learn which magic. Sancta Lux, whose jurisdiction overlaps theirs and will not define the boundary.

**Fears.** A circle breaking before it closes. A crown that makes its own license and refuses the guild's. The hunting guilds' own loophole, in which they train their own outside the state's gate, becoming the thing a crown uses as an excuse to shut them.

**Under one flag.** At Sedgeby Hall the Hall-Warden, Dame Ysmay Thole, wants every hunter on the river paying the fee, and her trace examiner, a dry stick named Aylwin Ansty, wants the fee waived for any crew that will bring him clean traces, because he has fourteen unsolved burnings and no witnesses. Both press the same green wax with the same seal, and neither has told the other what the last batch of attestations said.

**At the door.** A hedge-worker who takes an egg for talking down a fever has broken no schedule anybody has ever enforced, and one who takes a silver has. A boy of twelve with a newly woken Crystal can be bound to a hall for seven years for the price of his mother's signature, and the hall will feed him, which is the argument.

**The seam.** A guild court and a crown court will both claim a case they want and both refuse one they do not. A defendant who knows which court wants him can often choose the one that does not.

### The sects

*Everywhere · a thousand walls, four ranks, one argument the length of the world about whose style is best*

**Wants.** Students, ground, glory, and to see the people across the river beaten at the midsummer brackets in front of their own mothers.

**Holds.** A training ground and whatever rich earth lies under it. A style anyone can spot at fifty paces. Colors, songs and a grudge older than anyone can properly explain. The discipleship ranks everybody knows: **outer disciple** (the ones who sweep and fight in the first round), **inner disciple**, **core disciple** (the handful being raised to carry the art) and **elder**, with a grandmaster over all. A rise comes by ranking contests and the elders' word, and it opens the school's guarded secrets and its stores in return for duty. A disciple who leaves with the methods is hunted for what they know.

**Fights by.** Ranking days and brackets, mostly (the Copper Kites and the Iron Aunts of Hollin Ford have shut their whole town for it for fourteen generations): Stage brackets at every fair, yielding allowed, killing forbidden and still happening. Then bounties on each other's turncoats, and street fights on bracket eve that the watch has learned to wait out. A sect runs like a town's great love and greatest liability, and it has about the same relationship to the local baker's window.

| Sect | Where | Colors and song | The style at fifty paces | The grudge |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **The Scalded Cats** | River towns of the Concord heartland, commons to a man | Orange and soot; *Who's Got the Lid*, sung flat on purpose | Open hands, close work, a coat run so hot the sleeves scorch | The Pewter Yard, over a girl, a bridge and a disputed result in Year 671 |
| **The Thrawn Yard** | The Kharven Marches | Undyed wool and one red thumb; a song with a single line, repeated until someone stops them | Stand still, take it, hit back once | Everyone south of the Marches, on principle |
| **Kagerō-ryū** (陽炎流, the Heat-Shimmer School) | Moto country | White sash dyed to waver at the edge; a poem no outsider can sit through | Almost no footfall, a blade already somewhere else | The Thrawn Yard, whom they consider a weather event |
| **Baengnyeon-gwan** (백련관, White Lotus Hall) | The Silver Cedars, around the Sum-gol road | Rice-white with a pink hem; a drinking song in nine verses that gets ruder | Long pole and a low, wide stance, always in threes | Qīngshí Mén, over who taught whom the pole |
| **Qīngshí Mén** (青石門, Blue Stone Gate) | The lineage-hall country of the East | Ink-blue sleeves; a recited list of everyone they have beaten, with dates | Scholar's sword, and they tell you the page number while they do it | Baengnyeon-gwan, in writing, in three volumes |

Each people's fighters have their own word for the coat, the weight and the nose (see Part Six of [[The Magic System/The Soul and the Climb/The Color of Essence|The Color of Essence]]), and a sect's first lesson is usually why everybody else's word is wrong.

**Set against.** Each other, for the pleasure of it. The guild halls, when a sect's fighters hunt for pay without a license. Crowns that want to know who is teaching what behind that wall. Lords who post a bounty on a sect's champion and then find the whole town in the road.

**Fears.** A season without a champion. Thin ground. An elder who sells the third form to the people across the river.

**At the door.** A baker on Cooper's Row gives the Cats' outer disciples free heels on bracket day, because he has seen what happens to the bakers who do not, and he has a boy of nine he is hoping they will take. Heralds cry the results from the bridge, minstrels get them wrong by the next town, and the bookies pay out on the herald.

**The seam.** The outer disciples know everything the elders do not want told, and they are fifteen and hungry. A sect's whole style can be read from its laundry, and at least two famous upsets were planned by someone who paid the washerwoman.

## The Watch and the Cult

### The Night Watch

*The Night Watch Society · crown-chartered, older than the Guild Accord · illegal magic and unexplained phenomena*

**Wants.** Every illegal working attributed and every unexplained phenomenon explained before it spreads. It works beside the guilds and answers to the state, and it was investigating unlawful magic before any circle of the Accord had signed.

**Holds.** The crown's warrant, under the crown's charter to the Society. Its chapters: foresters, coil-carriers and other people who walk a line every week and file what they find on it. A bulletin office that has their inserts copied out by hand on the cheapest rag it can buy. And the Night Register, which takes up what the Lattice Classification Bureau, a separate office, closes: the desk where a filing is struck with a Warden's brass and becomes an exhibit.

**Fights by.** Paper and patience. A chapter's observer measures, seals a sample tin, and files. The Register builds its file off whatever is written down, and a single name in a private letter has been enough to hang a whole case on before.

**Set against.** Unlicensed practitioners, cults, and anyone working magic the state has not allowed. At the edges, the guilds, whose trace examiners it works beside and whose courts want the same cases. And the classifying desks that close a filing as weather because a closed filing costs nobody anything.

**Fears.** A working that leaves no author. Residue always carries its author, and the whole science of attribution rests on it. A method that makes the signature belong to somebody who cannot be charged defeats the Watch without a fight.

**The seam.** The Watch's eyes are ordinary people with notebooks, and none of them carries a signature on anything. A party that files an insert has put a fact on the Register, and a party that learns which observers are filing knows who the other side will be watching too.

### The Mother

*A cult, named for the mother of vinegar · founded by a lich*

**Wants.** To obtain the unattainable. To feed its founder's backlog, since he holds more than he can convert and cannot convert it fast enough. To break the Gate, the state's lockdown on who may learn magic. And profit and power.

**Holds.** Cultures, which is what it calls its cells, each small, each working in a cold place nobody is watching: a bled Core, a disused coldhouse, a street the company has struck from the round. Recruits it has inoculated. Whatever its buyers can divert from lawful supply.

**Fights by.** Intrigue, and workings that read as nature. A culture's best work leaves a residue signed by something that cannot be charged, so the classifying desk returns it as a bleed with no operator indicated, and is correct to. Liability under the article on the speaking falls on whoever paid, so the cult keeps its hands and its purse apart.

**Set against.** The Night Watch and its Register. The guilds' trace examiners. Every crown that keeps the Gate shut, because the Gate is where the cult recruits: it teaches what the state forbids to people the state has refused.

**Fears.** Edges. A working that stops at a boundary proves a hand chose the boundary. Its founder has a fear of his own, and it is volume: fed faster than he can convert, he does not grow stronger. He backs up.

**The seam.** The cult's buyers take their stock off lawful contracts and enter it as wastage, and wastage is a column in a Logistics return, and the Logistics Division publishes. Somebody reading the returns can see a line with years of flat wastage suddenly have a bad season.

## The Trading Powers Already Standing

### The Ore Council of Marrowhold and its merchant houses

*The Ember Holds · twelve guild masters, the extraction schedule, and the Accord's armory*

**Wants.** The extraction schedule kept as the realm's real law. The northern corridors quiet. Kharven armed and dependent.

**Holds.** The Ring's metal, which every Inner World economy runs on. Weapons procurement for the Accord's Strategic Division. The Merchant guild's great houses, which regulate a trade and make nothing.

**Fights by.** Supply. Marrowhold sets how fast the armory restocks and to whom, and it cuts grain while sending substrate when it wants to tell a neighbor where its autonomy ends.

**Set against.** Any power that would make Kharven independent. Any company that would buy the ore at the pithead before it reaches the Council.

**Fears.** Anything that makes its metal replaceable. A partner in Nalūn whose credit fails.

**Under one flag.** Twelve masters seal the schedule with one mark, the crossed hammers, and they agree about nothing else. The master of the smelters wants the northern quota raised and the master of the corridors wants it held, because he is the one whose carriers get robbed on it. They have voted the same way twice in nine years, both times against a third master.

**At the door.** A farrier in a Ring town pays for his iron by a schedule he has never read, and a bad quarter reaches him as a horseshoe that costs a copper more and nobody to complain to.

**The seam.** Marrowhold's governance is three legs: the Ore Council, the River Crown Council and the Grand Church. Set one leg against another and the whole Ring slows.

### The factor houses of Nalūn

*Fourteen recognized houses and the Council of Links · the Amber Cities*

**Wants.** Their supply books kept valuable, which means corridors open and contracts honored.

**Holds.** The instruments. Supply contracts are the most traded documents in the four quarters and the factor houses intermediate most of them, because their Parun-sealed contracts are the only ones counterparties trust to hold. The Greis Trading House runs eleven way-stations along the Iron-to-Salt Corridor under Nalūn-sealed contracts.

**Fights by.** Credit. Nalūn's credit finances Marrowhold's extraction and Marrowhold's metal backs Nalūn's credit. A factor house that withdraws credit starts a war without a single soldier.

**Set against.** Anyone closing the eastern corridors. Anyone promising delivery he cannot make, since the market in claims on a falling quantity rewards exactly that man until the day it ruins him.

**Fears.** A Parun-sealed contract failing in public, which the Council of Links has never recorded and the Measurewrights doubt has never happened.

**At the door.** A river carrier who signs for a load on a Nalūn contract feels the seal take hold of him, faintly, behind the breastbone, and will wade through a flood to deliver before he will learn what breaking it does. His wife knows the delivery date better than the anniversary of their wedding.

**The seam.** Rimward contracts carry a slight but measurable reduction in enforcement strength. A party that knows which city sealed a contract knows how hard it will hold.

### The Lamp and Ward Companies

*Chartered companies, one to a trade in each city or quarter · see [[The Magic System/Practice and Institution/Who Pays for the Lamp The Living Crafts|Who Pays for the Lamp]]*

**Wants.** The round-fee, the Core hours, the lead seal on every lamp stem, and no questions.

**Holds.** The rounds, the Core rights, the seals and the searchers (the company's man with a seal press, an assay fork and a book, who may strike a household from the round). The companies are licensed by the Accord and fund the body that licenses them.

**Fights by.** The strike from the round. A street struck dark fills its doorways within four minutes, and the companies schedule their work knowing it. Snuffers come after the arrears, and strangers are brought in from another town when the wardens walk off.

**Set against.** The botch, the unsealed work done with a stolen glyph and a nerve, which is illegal and universal. Their own lamp-wardens and ward-cutters, who can walk off the round, leave the last stroke uncut, or simply pass your door. The Lease-holders, who buy the dead legs (the streets a company has given up on) and feed the people there. Any surveyor who publishes what residue does to a tenement.

**Fears.** Their own records. What a company's surveys know about residue and fee band is the most dangerous paper any company holds. And a warden who has the street behind her.

**At the door.** A widow on a thin-ground stair pays three silver for a month of below-grade lamp, and she has never once been told that is what she bought. Her grandchildren will wake at the wrong ages or not at all, and the company has never commissioned a study of it.

**The seam.** A searcher is often the same man as the collector, which is why the exemption for a death-house, a physician's house and a working forge is claimed far more often than it is granted, and why a magistrate who does not drink with the company's factor is worth finding.

### The Company of Adventurers

*The venture company · the tables of mortality*

**Wants.** Returns on subscribed ventures. A Commission is a venture with shares, and a party that thinks it has wages has not read what it signed.

**Holds.** Subscribers' capital, bottomry (a loan against a voyage, repaid only if the voyage returns), underwriting, and the tables, which are the most honest document about mortality in the four quarters because they are written by people with money on the answer.

**Fights by.** Price. It decides which ventures get outfitted at all, and a party without a patron borrows on bottomry at a rate the Company sets.

**Set against.** Chartered hunting companies that sell the same risk cheaper by hiring hunters without licenses.

**Fears.** The tables leaving the room. A clerk showing a party what a Containment on a live Wellspring site actually costs in lives.

**The seam.** Losses fall on the subscribers and deaths fall on the party. A party that has read the tables can price its own lives, and can refuse a venture the Company has already sold.

## The Chartered Companies

*Three chartered companies stand out among the many that have risen with the scramble. Each is a company, as distinct from a crown or a guild: it holds a charter, sells shares in its ventures hand to hand before sworn notaries at the great fairs, and answers to its subscribers first.*

### The Eastern Arc Company · Societas Arcus Orientalis

*A chartered company on the New World's arc*

**Wants.** Cloudmetal, which only the eastern arc produces. Surface gold and silver. Wells, above all, on a coast where the Wellspring sites move as the ground shifts.

**Holds.** Settlements under Accord charter, company ports, company stores paying in scrip (company tokens good only at the company's own counter), and the survey maps its claims are filed against.

**Fights by.** Company war through its own guards. Economic war at the fairs: a rumor of a new Well lifts its shares, a rumor of a moved one crashes them, and both rumors have been bought and sold.

**Set against.** Eresse, whose old-ground titles predate its charter. Korvaeth and the Sunroot Regency, whose disputed names sit under half its land grants. Far crowns that granted rival charters to the same stretch of coast.

**Fears.** The Well leaving. A claim filed on a Well is a claim on ground, and when the Well moves off that ground the claim is worth what the rock is worth.

**At the door.** A child born in a company settlement on the arc is entered on the store's book at birth, against her mother's account, for the midwife.

**The seam.** Its survey maps date every claim. Anyone who knows where a Well has moved since the last survey knows which company holdings are already worthless and which rival claims are about to become valuable.

### The Well-Hunters' Company · Societas Venatorum Fontium

*A chartered hunting company, selling clearance by the party*

**Wants.** The hunting trade at scale: contracts with mines, lamp companies and crowns to keep Well-spawn off their ground.

**Holds.** Contracts, a roster of hired hunting parties, a few licensed captains, and a great many cheap unlicensed hunters working under them.

**Fights by.** Price, and proxy. It undercuts the guild halls by hiring hunters without licenses and putting one licensed captain over each party so the pay is lawful on paper.

**Set against.** The hunting guilds, whose license it is hollowing out. The Company of Adventurers, whose risk it sells cheaper. Any circle of the Guild Accord that closes the captain-over-a-party arrangement.

**Fears.** A signed circle in the district where most of its contracts are. A bad season in which the tables catch up with its prices.

**The seam.** Its hunters are paid by the carcass and hired for cheapness. They are the least loyal men on any contract, the easiest to buy, and the first to know which Well is breeding faster than the contract admits.

### The Northern Road Company · Societas Viae Septentrionalis

*A chartered road and timber company under a Concord crown*

**Wants.** Ironwood, the northern routes toward Marrowhold and the Polar Reach, and the right to set the tolls on them.

**Holds.** A charter from a crown with no writ in Kharven, company way-stations, hauling contracts, and grain bought forward from whoever will sell it.

**Fights by.** Being the center. Where Kharven has no working crown, the Company offers roads, pay and order, and a house that takes its money finds the Company's men guarding its bridges.

**Set against.** House Hallenfeld, which holds the junction and wants a crown, and no company, to connect. House Thornwall, whose ironwood trade it means to take. Marrowhold, which wants Kharven dependent on it and not on a company from the south.

**Fears.** A restored Ashen Crown. A northern winter that kills its hauliers faster than its charter can replace them.

**The seam.** It has no law in Kharven except its own guards. Anything it takes there is held only as long as it can be held by force, and the Banner Houses know it.

## The Jurisdiction Fights

*Where layers meet, somebody has to decide who decides. These are the standing disputes, and none of them is settled.*

| Dispute | The claimants | Why neither yields |
|---|---|---|
| **A crime done with a working** | The guild court, with its trace examiners, and the crown court | The traces are guild evidence and the victim is a crown subject. Each court takes the cases it wants |
| **Company land** | The chartering crown and the company that took the ground | The company paid for it. The crown granted the right to pay for it |

## What It Costs Below

The scramble is paid for at the bottom. **Displaced trades** lose their work to the charter: the carrier to the company's own wagons on the kept road, the hand-spinner to the spinning house, the hedge-worker to the warden with the company's seal. **Workshop labor** is paid on the coil and by the piece. A man who is slow or sick finds the shortfall entered as his problem on his account. **The colonized** find a charter granted over their ground by a crown they have never seen, and a company store where the market used to be. **Combinations and revolts** follow all three: weavers shutting a cloth town's gates, wardens walking off a round, riots when the snuffers come up a street, and the company guards sent to end them.

Take one baker. On a chartered street she answers to the assize on the loaf, sworn tasters who know her name, a beam she may be asked to prove at any hour, a lamp company for her oven's warm stone, a crown tax on the flour, a sect that eats free on bracket day and a guild examiner who wants to know why her bread has been rising so well. She has never been in a room with any of the people on this page, and every one of them is in her dough.

Take one ferryman. Article XXVIII of the Accord says his boat, the tool of his craft, cannot be taken in pledge. The crown's toll, the company's bridge up the river and the Well-spawn in the shallows have not read Article XXVIII.

Take one child. About one in ten wakes a Crystal, and the morning it happens four kinds of people are interested: the crown's registrar who keeps the Gate, a hall with an indenture, an elder scouting for outer disciples at the fair, and a soft-spoken stranger who has heard the state refused her family. The child wants to know whether she can still go out and play.

Every power on this page has a position on that unrest. Most of them would call it a matter of order.

## Where the Seams Are

*Every power above has an interest a player can find and use, and every one of them shows it in the open.*
- **Starve one House and turn another.** Drakyssia's Houses depend on each other by design. Cut what one supplies and a second House is hurt, and it knows exactly whom to blame.
- **Enter through Jomari.** Every foreign cargo into the Old World passes House Jomari's harbors. The Pillars who lose by it resent Jomari first.
- **Buy a Kharven house.** Ashmore sells grain, Thornwall sells timber, Hallenfeld wants a center. The Northern Road Company is buying all three, and none of the three has told the others.
- **Wait for the window.** Eresse binds for Eons inside its window and not at all outside it.
- **Hold a name.** On Korvaeth ground a validated name is a weapon every court must hear.
- **Read the rate.** Sancta Lux publishes its false-positive rate every year, and almost nobody reads it.
- **Follow the markers.** The Inquisition leaves a chapter marker wherever it has been.
- **File an insert.** The Night Watch's Register builds its case off whatever is written down, and it keeps what it is sent after a desk has closed it.
- **Read the wastage.** The Mother's buyers take from lawful supply as wastage, and the Logistics returns publish every column.
- **Choose the court.** A guild court and a crown court will both claim a case they want and both refuse one they do not.
- **Know where the Well went.** An arc company's claims are dated by its last survey, and the Wells have moved since.
- **Hire the cheap hunter.** The Well-Hunters' Company's men know which Well is breeding faster than the contract admits, and they are paid by the carcass.
- **Show the tables.** The Company of Adventurers' underwriting tables price a party's lives, and the party is not supposed to see them.
- **Find the magistrate who does not drink with the company's factor.** The strike-from-the-round exemption is real. It is granted by whoever collects the fee.
- **Pay the washerwoman.** A sect's style can be read from its laundry, and its outer disciples are hungry.

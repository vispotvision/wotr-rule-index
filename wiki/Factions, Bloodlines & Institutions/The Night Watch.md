# The Night Watch

*The Night Watch Society · crown-chartered, older than the Guild Accord · its walkers count, its bulletin office prints, its Register keeps*
> *"I checked the far side twice."*
> *Nol Tally, Timberline chapter, North Ferriby walk, in his insert*

---

## What the Night Watch Is

The Night Watch investigates illegal magic and the phenomena the law has no name for. It was doing that work before any circle of the Guild Accord had signed, and it goes on doing it while the circles form. **It is one body under two names.** In a chancery it is the Night Watch. On its own inserts, and in its members' mouths, it is the Night Watch Society.
**It holds the crown's warrant.** The Society is chartered by the crown, and the warrant it carries is the crown's warrant as the Society holds it. It works alongside the guilds and answers to the state, which is the whole of its difference from a hall. A coil-carrier who arrives somewhere "on the Society's warrant, not the Bureau's" means exactly that: the crown sent her, and the classifying office did not.
**Its eyes are ordinary people.** Most of the Society is foresters, counters and coil-carriers who cover the same ground every week and write down what they find on it. None of them needs a signature on anything to do the work, and most of them carry none.

---

## How It Is Organised

| Part | What it does |
|---|---|
| **The chapters** | The Society's local bodies. The Timberline chapter keeps the northern timber, two more chapters work out of the eastern timber towns, and a Society man files from the Marches. A chapter keeps a bench where coils are calibrated against the standard and samples are assayed |
| **The walks** | Fixed lines, each held by one walker. The walker goes out on the same walk every week, counts what the ground holds, and gives the place by the nearest stone. The North Ferriby walk is forty miles of timber a week |
| **The walkers** | Foresters and counters. A walker's count goes in as a filing under his name, his chapter and his walk: *Tally, N., Timberline chapter, North Ferriby walk* |
| **The coil-carriers** | A chapter's instrument hands. When a walker's find wants sealing, the coil-carrier goes out to the ground with a tin, seals the sample standing in it under the chapter's wax, and assays it at the chapter bench |
| **The bulletin office** | Takes in the chapters' filings and prints them as **inserts** on the cheapest rag it can buy, so the ink bleeds into the fibre. Inserts go out in bundles. The office cuts for length |
| **The Night Register** | Takes up what the Lattice Classification Bureau closes |

## The Night Register

A filing the Bureau closes does not end there. **It goes to the Night Register.**
The Register is a desk on Cutler Row, worked at night while the district main is off, from the eleventh division to the sixth. An unlicensed draw is inaudible at noon and unmistakable at three, and that is half the reason the desk exists. Its officer carries the address **Warden of Duty** and a token of brass rather than silver, because the Register is not a full desk. The Warden who keeps it now is Wystan Ashmore, who has held the title four years and has never once used it to get a document, a reading, or an hour of a scholar's time.
**A filing becomes an exhibit under the Register's brass.** A sample sealed in the field under the chapter's wax is a Society filing. When the Register takes it up, it goes into a fresh tin, the wax is struck with the Warden's brass, and from then on it is a Register exhibit. The assayer's full name goes at the head of the docket, lineage, generation character and courtesy, in the hand she was taught, because if the matter ends in a hearing the assay is where the other side comes first.
**Its jurisdiction is the purse.** A working's hand can seldom be charged. Article XV, which makes liability follow whoever paid, is the only jurisdiction the Register has, so a Register case runs from the ground to a supply contract, and from the contract to a purse. The Register builds its file off whatever is written down.

## What It Investigates, and How

Illegal magic, and anything the ground does that nobody can explain: carcasses that stand where they died, growth where there should be rot, ravens that will not come down. When the same thing turned up in four counties, it reached the Register as inserts from several chapters and a sketch from the Marches.
**The count.** A walker measures by count and by rule, and he cuts a section with his knife when a section is wanted. What makes his filing worth reading is that the same observer covers the same ground every week, so anything new on the walk stands against every week before it.
**The tin.** Samples go into Fixatio-anchored evidence tins, sealed inside the hour, standing on the ground. Fixatio holds what it is given. That is why an evidence seal is admissible.
**The assay.** Ambient is read before and after, as a number, not an adjective. The sample is loaded dry, with no wash, so nothing soluble is lost. Then four passes: a Signature sweep, a Family sweep, Category alignment, and a full sweep with the gate open. A gate-open pass on a null field burns the coil, and a coil-carrier who expects to use one carries two.
**Below the floor.** A regulation coil looks for Crystals above the Bureau's floor, because the Bureau pays for it and the Bureau prosecutes people. The Register sweeps below that floor, down to the bottom of the range. For a minute and a half it reads nothing but noise, and the carrier holds the head steady through the noise. That is where a residue signed by something other than a practitioner shows: a Crystal at the bottom of the tier table, with no Path, no doctrine and no Domain seed in it. It burns coils.
**The gap between a classification and what happened.** Residue carries its author, because coupled Aether keeps the signature of whatever coupled it. The Bureau classifies honestly off that signature and closes a filing when it finds no operator. The Watch works in the space between that honest classification and the ground. A bleed has no edge. A boundary means somebody chose one, and a choice means a hand. Where the Bureau asked the instrument *who signed this*, the Register asks *what is carrying the signature*.
**Contact.** Anamnetic contact off a live residue, bare fingers on the sample, gives the reader what the residue holds. The Register has used it. It is taken against advice when it is taken at all: an artificer out of a Measurewright line who took it off a coil housing from a foundry fire spent his last eleven months correcting people who called him by the wrong name.

## A Known Chapter: the Timberline

*The northern timber, a chapter bench, and the North Ferriby walk.*
| Member | Office in the chapter |
|---|---|
| **Nol Tally** | Walker on the North Ferriby walk. Forty miles of timber a week; counts deer for a living; measures what he finds with a rule |
| **Qiu Yinzhi** | Coil-carrier. Artificer's apprentice out of a Measurewright line, capped eight months; wears a regulation bracelet that pulls toward a suppressed practitioner |

**The filing.** On the ninth, eleven hinds and a stag dead where they stood in the Ferriby cut, a grey-green mass nine inches at the tallest come up through the hide, the tissue under it undecayed, four ravens in the birch that never came down. The coil-carrier sealed a section inside the hour. On the tenth the walker went north to the Ferriby stones and found the last carcass twenty-two paces short of the parish boundary marker, with clean ground on the far side. He checked the far side twice.
**The classification.** The Bureau's northern office returned it inside two days: Wellspring bleed, Verdantia, tertiary expression, no operator indicated. No further action and no file. The return did not mention the marker.
**What the Register found.** The chapter assay had come back clean four times. Swept below the Bureau's floor, the residue was signed by the hind: the operator had four legs and could not be indicted. The sample went under the Register's brass as an exhibit, with the coil-carrier as the assay of record.

## Its Limits, and What It Fears

**What it sends up can come back as weather.** A four-county summary went to the Grand Mage's clerks, who acknowledged receipt and returned the standard form, the one they return for weather.
**Its instruments are built for someone else.** A regulation coil reads what the Bureau pays it to read. Going below the floor costs coils.
**Its paper is cheap and cut.** The bulletin office prints on the cheapest rag it can buy and cuts for length. The last paragraph of the Ferriby insert, the one with the ravens and the far side in it, was nearly cut.
**Its Register is not a full desk.** A brass token, a title nobody in the districts uses, and its officer reading inserts at the third hour.
**Its eyes are exposed.** A walker's name, chapter and walk head every insert he files, and he walks a fixed line every week. Whoever reads the inserts knows where to find him.
**It fears a working with no author.** The whole science of attribution rests on residue carrying its author. A method that gives the signature to something that cannot be charged defeats the Watch without a fight, and a phenomenon with no edge reads as a bleed.

## The Tells

- **An insert on cheap rag**, the ink bled into the fibre, headed with a name, a chapter and a walk.
- **Chapter wax** across the join of a Fixatio-anchored tin.
- **A man with a rule** measuring something dead and writing the number down.
- **A coil walked slowly along a wall** at chest height, the carrier watching the wire and not the room.
- **A regulation bracelet**: brass bands wound with fine grey wire, seated against the inside of the wrist, guild proof-marks on the outer band.
- **A lamp lit at the third hour** on a street whose main is off.
- **Brass where a full desk would carry silver.**
- **A burned coil.**

## Where the Seams Are

*The Watch shows its interests in the open, and each of them can be found and used.*
- **File an insert.** A walker's filing reaches the bulletin office under his name, and what the Bureau closes goes on to the Register. The Register keeps what a desk has closed.
- **Ask the clerk.** The bulletin office clerk will say where a closed filing goes, and that the Night Register is a person.
- **Read the inserts.** They go out in bundles on cheap rag. Anyone who reads them learns which observer is walking which ground, and so does anyone on the other side.
- **Find the paragraph that was nearly cut.** The office cuts for length, and the part it cuts can be the part nobody else has noticed.
- **Sweep below the floor.** A clean assay means only that nothing signed above the Bureau's floor. A spare coil and the nerve to hold through the noise can find what signed below it.
- **Bring a ledger.** The Register can seldom charge a hand. It can charge whoever paid, and a party that brings it a purse brings it a case.
- **Mind the seal.** Under chapter wax a sample is a Society filing; under the Register's brass it is an exhibit. Which seal is on the tin decides whose case it is, and whose name heads the docket.

## Relations and Reputation

**The crown** chartered the Society, and the Society holds its warrant and answers to the state. **The guilds** are the Watch's neighbours on the same ground: it works alongside them, their trace examiners read a working's traces as evidence as it does, and their courts want the cases the crown's courts want. **The Lattice Classification Bureau** is a separate office. It classifies the Society's filings and closes the ones in which it finds no operator, and it is honest and usually right; Edwyn Brack of its northern office has never been wrong in a way that mattered. **The forming Accord** is younger than the Watch. Where a circle has signed, the Articles bind, and Article XV is the one the Register works through.
**Sancta Lux's Inquisitors** are the Church's lateral office, sent after unconsecrated high working wherever the Church holds standing, with authority to pronounce sentence in the field without an Arbiter. The Watch works the other way: it counts, seals and files, and its cases go to a hearing. **The Holy Inquisition**, which took the Inquisitor's name without the standing, claims the same work and prosecutes it by fire.
**The Mother** watches the Watch more closely than any other power, because its observers carry no signature the cult can follow. Its Greyshaft Nine culture knew the Ferriby walker by name, knew that the coil-carrier had not filed her clean return, and set its next work to leave no line anywhere a walker could stand at and check the far side of.
> Asked whether the Night Register was a person, the bulletin office clerk said it was.

# The Powers of the Imperial Age

*Crowns, great houses, orders, guilds, a crown-chartered society, a cult and the chartered companies: what each wants, what it holds, how it fights, and who it is set against.*
> *The company takes the ground. The crown claims it afterward. The guild hunts whatever lives on it. All three send the bill to the same town.*
> *saying of the arc settlements*

---

## The Shape of the Scramble

The Imperial Age is the age in which everything that can be owned is being claimed at once. Crowns are expanding, chartered houses are expanding beside them, and the draw engines have made five things worth more than they have ever been. Every power on this page wants some of all five, and most want one of them badly enough to go to war for it.
| What is scrambled for | Why it matters now |
|---|---|
| **Wells and draw** | The main and the meter run on harmonised veins. A supply contract written at old rates is an inheritance, and a Well is the only thing that writes new ones |
| **Materials** | Cheap steel, rubber and gutta-percha, Cloudmetal from the eastern arc, Riftstone and gravity ore from the Old World's rifts, the Master Material Ledger's Essence-born materials. The works eat them faster than any one realm digs them |
| **Markets and labour** | A works needs hands and a buyer. Branded goods, department stores and consumer Essence goods have made a buyer out of every metered house, and the un-Tempered labourer is worth less every year the arrays improve |
| **Routes** | Rail, wire, sea lanes, Dragon Roads, caravan water and the Concord Gates. Whoever holds the route sets the price of everything carried on it |
| **The hunting trade** | Wells breed Well-spawn, a Well worked harder breeds more, and every mine, main and settlement near a Well now pays somebody to clear it. Hunting has become a business, sold by the party and priced by the share |

**The order of events is nearly always the same.** A company goes in first, under a charter, and takes the ground by buying it, surveying it, fortifying it or simply standing on it. The crown that granted the charter claims the ground afterward, once there is something on it worth taxing. The guilds follow the company, because wherever there is a Well there are Well-spawn, and wherever there is a paid hunt somebody has to license the hunters. **Each layer arrives believing it is the one in charge.**

### Layered rule

Day-to-day rule in most of the four quarters is not one authority but three stacked on the same ground.
| Layer | Holds | Its instrument | Where it is weak |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The crown or great house** | The land, the levy, the tax, and the right to hang | Descent, the army, and the court of the realm | It cannot run a works, and it cannot hunt a Well |
| **The chartered company** | The trade: the works, the warehouse, the route, the store and the scrip | The charter, the share list and the company's own guards | Its charter can be revoked, and its shares can crash |
| **The guilds** | The hunters, the licence, and local policing of magic | The licence, the guild court, the trace examiner and the hunting hall | It holds no land and keeps no standing army, and outside a signed circle it is only a guild |

**Each layer fights the others over jurisdiction, and which one wins differs by place.** On the New World's arc the charter is the ground, so the company and the guilds win. In the Inner World heartland crown law and guild law have grown into each other until nobody can say which issued a given rule. In the Old World the great houses win, and a foreign company enters only through a house's port on a house's terms. In the Outer World the throne wins outright, and the Ten Houses are their own companies. In Kharven there is no working crown and no instrument that binds, so whoever arrives with the most men and the best grain wins that season.

### How the powers fight

| Means | What it looks like on the ground |
|---|---|
| **Proxy and company war** | Company guards, hired hunters and "survey parties" do what a crown cannot be seen to do. A company's private army burns a rival's way-station and the crowns behind both exchange letters of regret |
| **Open war** | Levies, fleets, Wyvern wings and practitioners in the field. Rare between the great crowns, because it is ruinously expensive, and common at the edges where one of them meets a smaller one |
| **Economic war** | Cutting supply, buying up a commodity, shorting a rival's shares, closing a port, raising a tariff, withholding grain. The cheapest war there is, and the one that kills the most people who never see a soldier |
| **Intrigue** | Bought clerks, forged assays, concealed coil readings on an heir, a charter revoked because the right person heard about a problem three times in one season |

**Most conflicts in the Imperial Age are all four at once**, and the parties would describe every one of them as trade.

## The Crowns and Great Houses

### The Dragon Throne of Drakyssia and the Ten Houses

*Outer World · an empire claiming roughly eight parts in ten of its continent*
**Wants.** Wells and draw above everything, because Drakyssia's own ground is Aether-depleted and it cannot produce what it needs on it. Reagents, Essence-bearing plants, catalysts and food in the lean years. Predictable roads through its tributaries.
**Holds.** The largest Wyvern air force in the Outer World, the richest gold of its continent, the Dragon Roads, the Dens, and a treaty court system that lets mortal kingdoms appeal against lesser dragon lords.
**Fights by.** Economic war first, through House Avarax's commercial enclaves and Avarax-chartered merchants in nearly every major port. Rule over tributaries through House Akhazar's treaties and tributaries. Open war through House Dovaryn's wings, which can reach almost anywhere on the continent faster than an army marches.
**Set against.** Any power buying Wells it depends on. Foreign companies entering tributary kingdoms without Akhazar's permission. And itself: Zhaeryn and Avarax quarrel over export prices, Ishkar restricts Rhaegyr's forges when they draw too much, Eredan complains that Dovaryn's Wyverns eat the herds.
**Fears.** Depletion that no conservation can reverse. A House grown self-sufficient. The day the throne stops being predictable, because predictability is the whole of what it sells to the mortals beneath it.
**The seam.** Every Imperial Charge depends on another. A party that can starve one House of what a second House supplies turns the second against the first, and the Council of Ten Wings exists precisely because that happens.

### The Kusharan Throne and the Seven Pillars

*Old World · regional rule beneath continental rule*
**Wants.** To keep its law its own. The Old World's polities are older than the Accord and have no interest in being harmonised, and Kushara means to meet the Imperial Age as a buyer and a seller, never as a charter.
**Holds.** House N'Kharu's bannermen levy, House Vokari's gold and Deep Surveyors, House Malani's food network, House Obaru's medicines, House N'Yari's rare magical minerals, House Jomari's Gulf Fleet and ports, and House Zembari's Hidden Water Network across the northern desert.
**Fights by.** Supremacy rather than mass in open war. Economic war through the Pillars, each of which can close what it holds. Intrigue through the caravans: armies see armies, caravans see everything else.
**Set against.** Foreign companies that want N'Yari's minerals or Vokari's seams without Kusharan law attached. The Accord, whose jurisdiction the Old World has never accepted.
**Fears.** One Pillar monopolising what another needs. A foreign company buying a Pillar's loyalty with better prices than the throne can offer. Wellspring damage from reckless extraction, which Vokari's surveyors have walked away from fortunes to avoid.
**The seam.** A foreign company can only enter through House Jomari's harbours. Jomari profits from every cargo it lets inland, and the Pillars that lose trade to foreign goods blame Jomari first and the throne second.

### The Ashen Crown of Kharven and its Banner Houses

*Inner World, the far north · a kingdom without a working centre*
**Wants.** A crown worth following again. Each Banner House wants the crown restored on terms that keep its own function: the border, the wall, the grain, the timber, the tundra, the roads.
**Holds.** Ground and custom and little else. House Ashmore's grain in the Greenhollow, House Thornwall's ironwood and the Stannvaard corridor, House Hallenfeld's river junction and signal posts, House Greymane's western wall against Undaar-Keth, House Stark's eastern March, House Keld's tundra.
**Fights by.** Open war on the frontier, oaths that bind only while they are worth keeping, and food. The north lives decades behind the Accord cities; its wars are fought with the tools of an earlier span, and it prices loyalty by the bushel.
**Set against.** Undaar-Keth probing the western ridge. Stannvaard, which cuts grain and keeps sending inscription substrate, because it wants a buffer state and not an independent one. Any company that comes up the road while the centre is empty.
**Fears.** Being a buffer. Being bought one house at a time. A winter in which House Ashmore's allocation decides who survives.
**The seam.** The Accord has no instrument that binds in Kharven and the seat has never asked for one, so anything done there is lawful until someone strong enough says otherwise. That cuts both ways, and every outsider who arrives learns it.

### Eresse

*The calendrical principality, and the elven old-ground beside the New World's arc*
**Wants.** Its own law undisplaced. Four Eons of Eressean law run on the old-ground beside the arc and the Accord did not replace them there. Consecrated gold kept out of the melting pot.
**Holds.** Sacred Gold, which cannot lawfully be melted and trades at a premium over bullion. The appointed windows, outside which nothing is decided. The old-ground's titles, which predate every charter on the arc.
**Fights by.** Delay. Nothing is settled outside its ceremonial time, urgency is treated as a defect, and no polity in the four quarters can negotiate with Eresse at speed. Against a company that has promised its subscribers a return this year, a principality that answers next season is a siege.
**Set against.** Arc companies whose charters run up against old-ground titles. Sancta Lux, whose Eresse chapter hold the elven kingdoms tolerate on sufferance and audit every year.
**Fears.** Speed. A company that acts inside a closed window and dares the principality to answer before the window opens.
**The seam.** A deal struck with Eresse in its window binds for Eons. A deal struck outside it binds nothing. Whoever knows the calendar controls the timing of every settlement on the old-ground.

### Korvaeth and the Sunroot Regency

*The New World · a separate continent, a separate calendar, a war about names as property*
**Wants.** Title. The Korvaeth War is a fight over whether a name can be held as property, and every holding on its ground turns on whether its name was validly transferred. The Sunroot Regency wants to hold a regency that split name from office.
**Holds.** The Korvaeth seat and the roads its forces have opened, entered in its own records as assizes rather than battles. The Regency holds its vote, which has stood for eleven years.
**Fights by.** Open war conducted as litigation. Names taken in the field, witness-fees paid, validated names held in reserve to be used against a rival in any court.
**Set against.** Each other. And any arc company whose charter names land whose name is in dispute, because an Accord charter over a Korvaeth holding is only as good as the name it was granted under.
**Fears.** The challenge form, which is two words long and which any commoner may lawfully say. A finding that voids unassembled holdings, which would void every such holding on the continent at once.
**The seam.** On this ground a charter is a claim on a name, and a name can be challenged. A party holding a validated name holds a weapon every court on the continent must hear.

## The Orders

### Sancta Lux

*The chartered body of measurement and consequence · the Grand Church at Altherion*
**Wants.** Its Wellspring rights and its tax exemption kept. Its jurisdiction over oath, status, legitimacy and bad faith kept. The six pieces of the divided Grand Angel found before anyone else finds them.
**Holds.** The Oracle, the only instrument in the four quarters that does not drift. The Grand Church inside a republic that cannot evict it. Chapter holds in Eresse and Foraye, a legation at the Concord Citadel, and the Ember Magistrates' circuit through towns that will never see the Church.
**Fights by.** Measurement. The Church can be asked for a reading and cannot be refused one. Mirror trials read the accuser against the same reference as the accused, which is why every guild that has brought it a complaint hates it.
**Set against.** The Holy Inquisition, which it calls apostate. Sōhai, which calls the Scale the largest Singling in the history of the Realms. The secular guilds, with whom its relations are procedurally correct and personally poisonous.
**Fears.** The Seventh Objection made public, which would show its authority to be one tile wide. Contradictory law, which stops its Arbiters dead. Workings that present nothing to weigh.
**The seam.** The order publishes its false-positive rate every year and nobody outside Arbitration reads it. A party that does has the one document in the four quarters that says, in the order's own figures, how often the Scale's servants have been wrong.

### The Holy Inquisition

*The Burning Mandate · Rogue Body 7-C in the Accord's filings*
**Wants.** Correction. It holds that the reading needs no Ladder and that purification is mercy delivered early.
**Holds.** No territory and no fixed seat. Chapter markers and small garrisons left in the places it has declared corrected, and a catalogue of everything it has kept from the places it has burned.
**Fights by.** Campaign. A Circuit Justiciar takes a reading, a declaration is read in the square, forty-eight hours pass, and what refused the mask is treated as structural corruption.
**Set against.** Sancta Lux, which exiled its founders. The Guild Accord, which has authorised enforcement for six years and has not come. Every settlement on a Circuit Justiciar's road.
**Fears.** The Accord deciding the cost is worth paying and sending practitioners of matching Stage. The world learning what the catalogue is for.
**The seam.** It does not govern what it takes. Every planted chapter is a small garrison far from support, and every marker is a record of where the Inquisition has been and a line toward where it goes next.

### The Void Vanguard

*The host of the Void Regency · under the Void Regent, the Void Generals and a High Chancellor*
**Wants.** The Material Plane, as a new home for Aelor. It is not raiding. It means to stay.
**Holds.** Whatever ground its column stands on, a palace dragged through the breach, and the Prayers of the Hollow, which answer to the same Chancellor.
**Fights by.** Open war when it must and slow campaign by preference. It would rather use a people than kill them, and it has looked at the Dawi of Stannvaard, whose metal arms half the Inner World, in exactly that way.
**Set against.** Everything native to the plane, in principle. In practice, whoever stands between it and the peoples it means to use.
**Fears.** Nothing the four quarters have found.
**The seam.** A power that means to use Stannvaard needs Stannvaard intact. Whoever understands that knows which of the Void's threats are real and which are leverage.

## The Guilds and the Accord Forming

### The guilds, and the circles of the Guild Accord

*A communion of guilds, forming now, circle by circle*
**Wants.** The hunting trade licensed and the licence paid for. Anyone may practise privately; taking pay for a working or a hunt needs a guild licence, and every unlicensed hunter is a fee the guild did not collect.
**Holds.** The hunters, the hunting halls and the licence. Trace examiners who read a working's traces as evidence. Local policing of magic, and the guild courts that try it. Where a circle has signed, the Articles and the Commission framework bind. Elsewhere a guild is only a guild, and its writ runs as far as its members' reputations.
**Fights by.** Licence and exclusion: a man who cannot get an attestation cannot get credit. Guild courts against crown courts over who may try a crime done with a working. And locally, directly: the guilds hunt down those who do wrong.
**Set against.** Crown courts, over jurisdiction. Chartered companies that hire unlicensed hunters cheap. Crowns that register Crystals and control texts and want to decide for themselves who may learn which magic. Sancta Lux, whose jurisdiction overlaps theirs and will not define the boundary.
**Fears.** A circle breaking before it closes. A crown that makes its own licence and refuses the guild's. The hunting guilds' own loophole, in which they train their own outside the state's gate, becoming the thing a crown uses as an excuse to shut them.
**The seam.** A guild court and a crown court will both claim a case they want and both refuse one they do not. A defendant who knows which court wants him can often choose the one that does not.

## The Watch and the Cult

### The Night Watch

*The Night Watch Society · crown-chartered, older than the Guild Accord · illegal magic and unexplained phenomena*
**Wants.** Every illegal working attributed and every unexplained phenomenon explained before it spreads. It works beside the guilds and answers to the state, and it was investigating unlawful magic before any circle of the Accord had signed.
**Holds.** The crown's warrant, under the crown's charter to the Society. Its chapters: foresters, coil-carriers and other people who walk a line every week and file what they find on it. A bulletin office that prints their inserts on the cheapest rag it can buy. And the Night Register, which takes up what the Lattice Classification Bureau, a separate office, closes: the desk where a filing is struck with a Warden's brass and becomes an exhibit.
**Fights by.** Paper and patience. A chapter's observer measures, seals a sample tin, and files. The Register builds its file off whatever is written down, and a single name in a private letter has been enough to hang a whole case on before.
**Set against.** Unlicensed practitioners, cults, and anyone working magic the state has not allowed. At the edges, the guilds, whose trace examiners it works beside and whose courts want the same cases. And the classifying desks that close a filing as weather because a closed filing costs nobody anything.
**Fears.** A working that leaves no author. Residue always carries its author, and the whole science of attribution rests on it. A method that makes the signature belong to somebody who cannot be charged defeats the Watch without a fight.
**The seam.** The Watch's eyes are ordinary people with notebooks, and none of them carries a signature on anything. A party that files an insert has put a fact on the Register, and a party that learns which observers are filing knows who the other side will be watching too.

### The Mother

*A cult, named for the mother of vinegar · founded by a lich*
**Wants.** To obtain the unattainable. To feed its founder's backlog, since he holds more than he can convert and cannot convert it fast enough. To break the Gate, the state's lockdown on who may learn magic. And profit and power.
**Holds.** Cultures, which is what it calls its cells, each small, each working in a cold place nobody is watching: a capped main, a disused coldhouse, a district the Board has cut. Recruits it has inoculated. Whatever its buyers can divert from lawful supply.
**Fights by.** Intrigue, and workings that read as nature. A culture's best work leaves a residue signed by something that cannot be charged, so the classifying desk returns it as a bleed with no operator indicated, and is correct to. Liability under the article on the speaking falls on whoever paid, so the cult keeps its hands and its purse apart.
**Set against.** The Night Watch and its Register. The guilds' trace examiners. Every crown that keeps the Gate shut, because the Gate is where the cult recruits: it teaches what the state forbids to people the state has refused.
**Fears.** Edges. A working that stops at a boundary proves a hand chose the boundary. Its founder has a fear of his own, and it is volume: fed faster than he can convert, he does not grow stronger. He backs up.
**The seam.** The cult's buyers draw their stock off lawful contracts and enter it as wastage, and wastage is a column in a Logistics return, and the Logistics Division publishes. Somebody reading the returns can see a line with years of flat wastage suddenly have a bad season.

## The Trading Powers Already Standing

### The Ore Council of Stannvaard and its merchant houses

*The Guild Seat · twelve guild masters, the extraction schedule, and the Accord's armoury*
**Wants.** The extraction schedule kept as the realm's real law. The northern corridors quiet. Kharven armed and dependent.
**Holds.** The Ring's metal, which every Inner World economy runs on. Weapons procurement for the Accord's Strategic Division. The Merchant guild's great houses, which regulate a trade and make nothing.
**Fights by.** Supply. Stannvaard sets how fast the armoury restocks and to whom, and it cuts grain while sending substrate when it wants to tell a neighbour where its autonomy ends.
**Set against.** Any power that would make Kharven independent. Any company that would buy the ore at the pithead before it reaches the Council.
**Fears.** Anything that makes its metal replaceable. A partner in Nalūn whose credit fails.
**The seam.** Stannvaard's governance is three legs: the Ore Council, the River Crown Council and the Grand Church. Set one leg against another and the whole Seat slows.

### The factor houses of Nalūn

*Fourteen recognised houses and the Council of Links · the Chain-Cities*
**Wants.** Their supply books kept valuable, which means corridors open and contracts honoured.
**Holds.** The instruments. Supply contracts are the most traded documents in the four quarters and the factor houses intermediate most of them, because their Parun-sealed contracts are the only ones counterparties trust to hold. The Greis Trading House runs eleven way-stations along the Iron-to-Salt Corridor under Nalūn-sealed contracts.
**Fights by.** Credit. Nalūn's credit finances Stannvaard's extraction and Stannvaard's metal backs Nalūn's credit. A factor house that withdraws credit starts a war without a single soldier.
**Set against.** Anyone closing the eastern corridors. Anyone promising delivery he cannot make, since the market in claims on a falling quantity rewards exactly that man until the day it ruins him.
**Fears.** A Parun-sealed contract failing in public, which the Council of Links has never recorded and the Measurewrights doubt has never happened.
**The seam.** Rimward contracts carry a slight but measurable reduction in enforcement strength. A party that knows which city sealed a contract knows how hard it will hold.

### The Draw Boards

*Chartered supply monopolies, one to a city or a quarter*
**Wants.** The tariff, and the mains, and the meters, and no questions.
**Holds.** The mains, the standpipes, the seals and the cut plate. The Boards are licensed by the Accord and fund the body that licenses them.
**Fights by.** The cut. A district cut is a district that fills its doorways within four minutes, and the Boards schedule their work knowing it.
**Set against.** The spur, the unmetered tap that is illegal and universal. The Lease-holders, who buy the dead legs the Boards abandon and feed the people there. Any surveyor who publishes what residue does to a tenement.
**Fears.** Their own records. What the Board's surveys know about residue and tariff band is the most dangerous paper any Board holds.
**The seam.** A Board inspector is often the same man as its collector, which is why the exemption for a death-house, a physician's house and a working forge is claimed far more often than it is granted, and why a magistrate who does not drink with the Board's factor is worth finding.

### The Company of Adventurers

*The venture company · the tables of mortality*
**Wants.** Returns on subscribed ventures. A Commission is a venture with shares, not a job with wages.
**Holds.** Subscribers' capital, bottomry, underwriting, and the tables, which are the most honest document about mortality in the four quarters because they are written by people with money on the answer.
**Fights by.** Price. It decides which ventures get outfitted at all, and a party without a patron borrows on bottomry at a rate the Company sets.
**Set against.** Chartered hunting companies that sell the same risk cheaper by hiring hunters without licences.
**Fears.** The tables leaving the room. A clerk showing a party what a Containment on a live Wellspring actually costs in lives.
**The seam.** Losses fall on the subscribers and deaths fall on the party. A party that has read the tables can price its own lives, and can refuse a venture the Company has already sold.

## The Chartered Companies

*Three chartered share-holding companies stand out among the many that have risen with the scramble. Each is a company, not a crown and not a guild: it holds a charter, trades its shares on the exchanges, and answers to its subscribers first.*

### The Eastern Arc Company · Societas Arcus Orientalis

*A chartered company on the New World's arc*
**Wants.** Cloudmetal, which only the eastern arc produces. Surface gold and silver. Wells, above all, on a coast where the Wellsprings move as the ground shifts.
**Holds.** Settlements under Accord charter, company ports, company stores paying in scrip, and the survey maps its claims are filed against.
**Fights by.** Company war through its own guards. Economic war through the share lists: a rumour of a new Well lifts its shares, a rumour of a moved one crashes them, and both rumours have been bought and sold.
**Set against.** Eresse, whose old-ground titles predate its charter. Korvaeth and the Sunroot Regency, whose disputed names sit under half its land grants. Overseas crowns that granted rival charters to the same stretch of coast.
**Fears.** The Well leaving. A claim filed on a Well is a claim on ground, and when the Well moves off that ground the claim is worth what the rock is worth.
**The seam.** Its survey maps date every claim. Anyone who knows where a Well has moved since the last survey knows which company holdings are already worthless and which rival claims are about to become valuable.

### The Well-Hunters' Company · Societas Venatorum Fontium

*A chartered hunting company, selling clearance by the party*
**Wants.** The hunting trade at scale: contracts with mines, Boards and crowns to keep Well-spawn off their works.
**Holds.** Contracts, a roster of hired hunting parties, a few licensed captains, and a great many cheap unlicensed hunters working under them.
**Fights by.** Price, and proxy. It undercuts the guild halls by hiring hunters without licences and putting one licensed captain over each party so the pay is lawful on paper.
**Set against.** The hunting guilds, whose licence it is hollowing out. The Company of Adventurers, whose risk it sells cheaper. Any circle of the Guild Accord that closes the captain-over-a-party arrangement.
**Fears.** A signed circle in the district where most of its contracts are. A bad season in which the tables catch up with its prices.
**The seam.** Its hunters are paid by the carcass and hired for cheapness. They are the least loyal men on any works, the easiest to buy, and the first to know which Well is breeding faster than the contract admits.

### The Northern Road Company · Societas Viae Septentrionalis

*A chartered road and timber company under a Concord crown*
**Wants.** Ironwood, the northern routes toward Ketsuen and the Polar Reach, and the right to set the tolls on them.
**Holds.** A charter from a crown with no writ in Kharven, company way-stations, hauling contracts, and grain bought forward from whoever will sell it.
**Fights by.** Being the centre. Where Kharven has no working crown, the Company offers roads, pay and order, and a house that takes its money finds the Company's men guarding its bridges.
**Set against.** House Hallenfeld, which holds the junction and wants a crown, not a company, to connect. House Thornwall, whose ironwood trade it means to take. Stannvaard, which wants Kharven dependent on it and not on a company from the south.
**Fears.** A restored Ashen Crown. A northern winter that kills its hauliers faster than its charter can replace them.
**The seam.** It has no law in Kharven except its own guards. Anything it takes there is held only as long as it can be held by force, and the Banner Houses know it.

## The Jurisdiction Fights

*Where layers meet, somebody has to decide who decides. These are the standing disputes, and none of them is settled.*
| Dispute | The claimants | Why neither yields |
|---|---|---|
| **A crime done with a working** | The guild court, with its trace examiners, and the crown court | The traces are guild evidence and the victim is a crown subject. Each court takes the cases it wants |
| **Company land** | The chartering crown and the company that took the ground | The company paid for it. The crown granted the right to pay for it |
| **The hunter on crown ground** | The crown's game law, the guild's licence, the company's contract | A hunter can be lawful under one, poaching under another and in breach under the third, on the same afternoon |
| **Oath and bad faith** | Sancta Lux and the Accord's courts | Neither will define the boundary, because both prefer being able to claim a case to being obliged to take one |
| **The arc's old-ground** | Eressean law and the Accord's charters | Four Eons against a charter from living memory |
| **A name in the New World** | Korvaeth, the Sunroot Regency and the arc companies | A charter is only as good as the name it was granted under |
| **The cut** | The Draw Board, the magistrate and the Accord's article on arrears | The exemption exists, is well known and is decided by the man who collects the tariff |
| **Anything in Kharven** | Whoever is standing there | No instrument binds, so nothing is unlawful until someone makes it so |

## What It Costs Below

The scramble is paid for at the bottom. **Displaced trades** lose their work to the works: the carrier to the rail, the hand-spinner to the mill, the lamplighter to the main. **Works labour** is paid on the coil and by the piece. A man who is slow or sick finds the shortfall entered as his problem on his account. **The colonised** find a charter granted over their ground by a crown they have never seen, and a company store where the market used to be. **Unions and unrest** follow all three. Combinations of workmen, strikes at the works, riots at the cut, and the company guards sent to end them.
Every power on this page has a position on that unrest. Most of them would call it a matter of order.

## Where the Seams Are

*Every power above has an interest a player can find and use, and every one of them shows it in the open.*
- **Starve one House and turn another.** Drakyssia's Houses depend on each other by design. Cut what one supplies and a second House is hurt, and it knows exactly whom to blame.
- **Enter through Jomari.** Every foreign cargo into the Old World passes House Jomari's harbours. The Pillars who lose by it resent Jomari first.
- **Buy a Kharven house.** Ashmore sells grain, Thornwall sells timber, Hallenfeld wants a centre. The Northern Road Company is buying all three, and none of the three has told the others.
- **Wait for the window.** Eresse binds for Eons inside its window and not at all outside it.
- **Hold a name.** On Korvaeth ground a validated name is a weapon every court must hear.
- **Read the rate.** Sancta Lux publishes its false-positive rate every year, and almost nobody reads it.
- **Follow the markers.** The Inquisition leaves a chapter marker wherever it has been.
- **File an insert.** The Night Watch's Register builds its case off whatever is written down, and it keeps what it is sent after a desk has closed it.
- **Read the wastage.** The Mother's buyers draw off lawful supply as wastage, and the Logistics returns publish every column.
- **Choose the court.** A guild court and a crown court will both claim a case they want and both refuse one they do not.
- **Know where the Well went.** An arc company's claims are dated by its last survey, and the Wells have moved since.
- **Hire the cheap hunter.** The Well-Hunters' Company's men know which Well is breeding faster than the contract admits, and they are paid by the carcass.
- **Show the tables.** The Company of Adventurers' underwriting tables price a party's lives, and the party is not supposed to see them.
- **Find the magistrate who does not drink with the Board.** The cut exemption is real. It is granted by whoever collects the tariff.

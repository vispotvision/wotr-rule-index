# The Bearing and the Holding

*A Survey of the Four Quarters, Their Substrate, and What May Be Made Of Them*
> Fourth revision. Compiled for the Logistics and Supply Division of the Guild Accord under attestation of the Guild of Measurewrights, Hexagonal Oath. Dates conform to the Concordance of Ages, sealed Year 715 of the Imperial Age. Kingdom names struck throughout. Circulated to Division heads, Accord assay houses, and the chartered mining companies of all four quarters. **Not for open sale.**

---

## Prefatory Note

This ledger exists because two men can dig the same hole and get different answers, and because the Accord has now lost four deep workings and one city block to the assumption that stone is stone.
It is arranged by quarter rather than by kingdom. Borders move. Bedrock does not.
> The fourth revision has struck the kingdom names from the deposit entries, and the reason is worth stating rather than burying. A ledger that names a seam for the crown that holds it must be reissued whenever the crown changes, and this one has now been reissued three times **without a single seam moving an inch.** Where a name was doing real work it has been replaced by the ground it referred to. Where a name was doing no work it is gone.
Every figure in this ledger was taken **at the face**, not from a chartered claim. Where a claim and a reading disagree, the reading stands.

## What Magic Actually Did to Industry

The common account is that Essence made industry possible. That is wrong in an instructive way. **Essence did not build the industry.** It removed four specific obstacles that had held mining and metalworking at a fixed ceiling for eleven centuries, and everything since has been the consequence of their removal.
| Obstacle | What it held | What removed it |
|---|---|---|
| **Water** | No working anywhere ran more than sixty fathoms below the adit line for longer than a dry summer. The deep ore was simply understood to belong to nobody | Phreatis-fed lift arrays · Fluxia, Fluid Dynamics |
| **Air** | Depth kills by the atmosphere long before it kills by the roof. Firedamp burns, blackdamp smothers, and the old answer was a man in a wet hood with a candle on a pole | Vectoria draught arrays, turning every deep working into a lung |
| **Haulage** | Rope has a limit and rock is heavy | **Weight Ablation glyphwork** — which does not lift anything. It lies to the rope about the weight |
| **Heat** | Charcoal and a good bellows will not take you past the point where iron becomes liquid, and everything interesting lives past that point | Ignivale and Pyraeon forge-arrays · Caloria, Thermodynamics |
| **Assay** | The bearing press is old. The holding coil is not | The coil, which is why the trade in Essence-active material stopped being a matter of a seller's word. A coil reading can be falsified, and **the Accord hangs people for it.** *What cannot be falsified is a coil reading set against a gauge, because the two instruments answer different questions and a forged lot will not satisfy both.* **The comparison is trivial, both reagents are indexed, and no procedure anywhere requires it** |

> **The single most important fact in the ledger.** A modern shaft is as deep as its array can be fed, and no deeper. Which means a mine does not die when the ore runs out. **It dies when the local Wellspring thins, and the ore is still down there when the water comes up.** The Old World is a graveyard of drowned workings that were still profitable on the day they were abandoned.
Weight Ablation halves the effective load on a winding rope for as long as the glyph holds. **When the lie fails it fails all at once.** Every winding house in the Old World has a bell for that and every man underground knows what the bell means. Spatial translation of ore was tried, costed, and abandoned; moving rock through Spatium remains more expensive per ton than moving it up a rope, and will remain so.
The heat ceiling's fall moved the constraint from the fire to **the vessel**, which is why refractory stock — wolfram and chromite and nothing else — is quietly one of the most strategic commodities in the four quarters and is priced accordingly.
> What follows from all this is the shape of the world as it now stands. **There is no steam engine in any mine, forge or mill in the four quarters and there never will be**, because every problem a stationary engine was going to solve had already been solved by other means before anyone thought to build one.
>
> There is instead a continent-wide dependency on Essence supply for the most basic industrial functions, pumping and ventilation, which means a Wellspring disruption does not merely inconvenience a mining district. **It drowns it, and then it suffocates whoever went down to see why the pumps had stopped.**

### The fifth obstacle, which fell last

*The four above were removed and are therefore invisible. The fifth held longest, and its removal is the reason the ledger has had to be revised.*
**Traction.** An array is fixed infrastructure. It is fed from a vein or a main, it is tended in place, and **nothing that moves can be plumbed.** Weight Ablation lies to a rope about a load and supplies no motive power whatsoever. Spatial translation of ore was tried, costed and abandoned. So the problem of moving a great weight a long distance overland stayed exactly where it had been for eleven centuries: horses, and the road, and the weather.
> **Which is why the railway is a main laid on its side.** Conduit is sealed into the bed between the rails, and the engine rides a hand's breadth above it and couples to it by Transference across the gap. Nothing burns and nothing boils, and there is no steam engine anywhere in the four quarters, on rails or off them.
> 
> *It is an Accord technology in every respect that matters: laid under a Board's charter, metered at every section, and cut from the signal box like a house at its junction.* **It solves the one obstacle Essence took longest to reach, and it solves it by carrying the tariff to every town on the line.**
> 
> The Logistics Division's position is that rail is a haulage convenience. **The Division has not yet costed what happens to a district when a line reaches it and the whole town goes on the meter in a single season.**
>
> It burns coal, it carries its own water, it answers to nobody's supply grade and it does not care what the local density is doing. *It is not an Accord technology and was not built by anyone the Accord would call an engineer.* **It solves the one obstacle Essence never touched, and it solves it for people who have never held a rank token.**
>
> The Logistics Division's position is that rail is a haulage convenience. **The Division has not yet costed what happens to a Board's tariff when a rail head reaches a district and coal arrives that can heat it without drawing anything at all.**
The tenders who work the arrays pay for this in the ordinary way. Long array duty produces the tremor the trade calls **the tender's shake**, a fine persistent palsy of the hands that arrives at about eight years of service and does not leave. Array houses hire young and they hire in numbers.

## Comparative Summary

| Quarter | Principal exports | Standing condition |
|---|---|---|
| **North** · Inner World | Iron, nickel, copper, salt, beryl, timber | Cheap array heat, expensive everything else |
| **West** · Old World | Wolfram, tin, lead, coal, lime, reworked slag | Total array dependency for survival, not production |
| **East** · New World | Gold, silver, sulphur, cement, Onceglass, Cloudmetal — the arc's; the elven old-ground beside it lies outside this ledger | Almost no array dependency outside the eastern arc |
| **South** · Outer World | Phosphate, nitre, chromite, fulgurite, serpentine | Extraction by hand at scale, value added elsewhere |

## Hazard Register, Abbreviated

| Hazard | Where | Character |
|---|---|---|
| **Array failure, pumping** | Western deep workings | Flooding within seventy to one hundred and ten hours depending on shaft. **The most likely mass-casualty event in the four quarters and the one the Accord is least prepared for** |
| **Array failure, ventilation** | All deep workings | Firedamp in coal, blackdamp in metal. Kills faster than water and is more easily concealed afterward |
| **Weight Ablation glyph failure** | All winding houses | Total and instantaneous restoration of true load. The bell is rung by hand because no automated warning has ever been fast enough |
| **Sulphide roast fume** | the Ring | Chronic. Vegetation loss to six leagues and rising |
| **Vitriol drainage** | Western old workings | Chronic, cumulative, irreversible at current practice |
| **Rock dust, silicate** | Western slate belts | Grinder's cough. Twenty year onset |
| **Rock dust, fibrous serpentine** | Southern coastal quarries | Twenty year onset, invariably fatal, no treatment recorded |
| **Lahar** | Eastern volcanic plains | Sudden, total, and faster than a horse |
| **Fumarole gas pooling** | Eastern sulphur fields | Silent, collects in hollows, **kills rescue parties second** |
| **Wellspring drift** | Eastern quarter generally | Economic rather than physical. Bankrupts rather than kills |
| **Tender's shake** | All array houses, all quarters | Onset at approximately eight years of service. Permanent. **No compensation scheme exists in any quarter** |
| **Crevice proximity** | Southern Crevice margin | Undescribed. Four-league exclusion, revised outward five times in the last sixty years |

## Unresolved Entries

*The Measurewrights record the following as open and decline to guess.*
**The provenance of Ghoststeel.** Attributed to the northern shield on ruin-siting evidence alone. No crucible reproduction has succeeded, and until one does the material's formation is unexplained.
**The provenance of Seerstone Resin.** Attributed provisionally to eastern cloud forest canopy species. Unconfirmed by Archives.
**Whether the Ring's interdimensional passages are caused by the impact structure or merely coincident with it.** The Measurewrights hold that the correlation is too exact to be accident and too unexamined to be asserted.

## The Names That No Longer Attach to Ground

*A separate class of open entry, and the one the Logistics Division finds most irritating.*
Certain materials in continuous trade arrive under provenance names that no current survey can attach to anywhere. **The material is real.** It is assayed, priced, shipped, and used. The place it is said to come from cannot be found by anyone the Accord has sent to look.
| Material | The problem |
|---|---|
| **Vellun Copper** | Trade-route evidence places the source hills in the western quarter. Four surveys have failed to confirm it. **The copper continues to arrive** |
| **Glassroot Fibers** and **Steelsilk Cord** | Two separate Accord records place the source in two separate quarters. Both cannot be right and neither has been withdrawn |
| **Willowblood Sap** and **Moonblight Ore** | Neither source appears on any current survey, and the older registers that name them give no coordinates — only a name and a road that no longer runs anywhere |

The Measurewrights offer three explanations and endorse none. That the names are **commercial fictions** protecting a real source, which is ordinary and would explain the shipping and not the age of the records. That the places **existed and have been depopulated** to the point of leaving no charter behind, which the northern quarter has seen twice within living memory. Or that the names are **older than the Concordance's count can reach** and refer to ground that has since been renamed by people who did not know what they were renaming.
> The Guild notes that the third explanation is the one the corrected chronology makes least available. Fourteen hundred years is not long enough for a country to be forgotten by everybody while its exports continue.
>
> **Something is still loading those ships.**

# The Bearing and the Holding

*A Survey of the Four Quarters, Their Substrate, and What May Be Made Of Them*
> Fourth revision. Compiled for the Logistics and Supply Division of the Guild Accord under attestation of the Guild of Measurewrights, Hexagonal Oath. Dates conform to the Concordance of Ages, sealed Year 715 In Concordance, the twenty-fifth year of the Dark Ages. Kingdom names struck throughout. Copied by hand and circulated to Division heads, Accord assay houses, and the chartered mining companies of all four quarters. **Not for open sale.**

---

## Prefatory Note

This ledger exists because two men can dig the same hole and get different answers, and because the Accord has now lost four deep workings and one city block to the assumption that stone is stone.

The block was Vat Row in Aldermarsh, and the two men were Simkin Kettleby and Osbern Tapley, who dug one trial pit together in the pale limestone behind the Dyers' new hall and fell out over it before they had climbed back up the ladder. Kettleby read thirty feet of sound rock and said so to the Dyers, who were paying him. Tapley read the same thirty feet and the hollow note under them, and said so to the Fullers, who hated the Dyers, and the Fullers nailed his reading to the hall door on the morning of the Feast of Vats. Nobody takes advice from a rival's door. Four hundred dyers came down the Row that noon in woad blue and madder red with the great vats carried shoulder high and the whole town leaning out of its windows to throw flowers, and at the corner by the tripe stalls the street folded in along its own length and went down into a cave that no survey had ever entered. Sixty-one people went with it, and nine hundred gallons of dye. The spring at Thornholt, three leagues off, ran blue for ten months and red for four more, and the Fullers charged a copper to see it. Kettleby's heirs and Tapley's still cross the street to avoid each other. Both readings, I am sorry to report, were accurate.

It is arranged by quarter rather than by kingdom. Borders move. Bedrock does not.

> The fourth revision has struck the kingdom names from the deposit entries, and the reason is worth stating rather than burying. A ledger that names a seam for the crown that holds it must be reissued whenever the crown changes, and this one has now been reissued three times **without a single seam moving an inch.** Where a name was doing real work it has been replaced by the ground it referred to. Where a name was doing no work it is gone.

Every figure in this ledger was taken **at the face**, not from a chartered claim. Where a claim and a reading disagree, the reading stands.

### Terms, for the new clerk

**The four quarters** are four quarters of one continent with isles off its shores, and a loaded mule could visit all of them without once getting its feet wet, though the Division does not recommend the mule. Inner, Old, New and Outer are chart-clerks' names from the Voyager Era, and they record the order in which the Guild's charts got there. They say nothing true about the ground, and the elves of the "New" World have been laughing at the word for seven centuries.

**Bearing** is what a material does under load: compression, tension, shear, thermal cycling, abrasion. **Holding** is what working it will keep, and for how long. Every entry gives both, a Tier from T0 to T9 (the Accord's ladder of what a material can stand and who can work it), and where it applies a Resonance (the Wellsprings it answers to) and a Domain (the branch of natural law that Resonance works through).

**Aether** is what lies in the ground and air, and how much of it lies there is **Aetheric Density**: rich ground and thin in a scholar's mouth, fat and lean in the street. It comes in sixty types that affect the Soul Crystal differently, and those types are **the Wellsprings**: sources of power that also represent alchemical principles and spiritual transformations. A practitioner pulls Aether out of the world, refines it in the Crystal into **Essence**, and drives it out through the Shell (the soul's outer skin). A place where one type of Aether pools is a Wellspring site, and a worked site with keepers on it is called a **Core** in common speech.

An **array** is a standing runed working cut for one duty, and it does nothing by itself. It is fed by **array-keepers**, practitioners on a shift who pull from the ground under the pit head, refine what they pull and drive it into the contacts. They are paid by the hour, and they may decline to come in. Everyone reading this ledger should keep that last clause in mind.

## What Magic Actually Did to Industry

The common account is that Essence made industry possible. That is wrong in an instructive way. Essence removed **four specific obstacles** that had held mining and metalworking at a fixed ceiling for eleven centuries, and everything since has been the consequence of their removal.

| Obstacle | What it held | What removed it |
|---|---|---|
| **Water** | No working anywhere ran more than sixty fathoms below the adit line for longer than a dry summer. The deep ore was simply understood to belong to nobody | Phreatis-fed lift arrays · Fluid Dynamics |
| **Air** | Depth kills by the atmosphere long before it kills by the roof. Firedamp burns, blackdamp smothers, and the old answer was a man in a wet hood with a candle on a pole | Cymorath draught arrays, turning every deep working into a lung |
| **Haulage** | Rope has a limit and rock is heavy | **Weight Ablation glyphwork**, which lifts nothing and lies to the rope about the weight |
| **Heat** | Charcoal and a good bellows will not take you past the point where iron becomes liquid, and everything interesting lives past that point | Ignivale and Pyraeon forge-arrays · Thermodynamics |
| **Assay** | The bearing press is old. The holding coil is not | The coil (the brass assay instrument), which is why the trade in Essence-active material stopped being a matter of a seller's word. A coil reading can be falsified, and **the Accord hangs people for it.** *What cannot be falsified is a coil reading set against an assay fork, because the two instruments answer different questions and a forged lot will not satisfy both.* **The comparison is trivial, both reagents are indexed, and no procedure anywhere requires it** |

> **The single most important fact in the ledger.** A pumped shaft is as deep as its array can be fed, and no deeper. Which means a mine does not die when the ore runs out. **It dies when the ground under its pit head thins and the keepers can no longer pull enough to feed the array, and the ore is still down there when the water comes up.** The Old World is a graveyard of drowned workings that were still profitable on the day they were abandoned.

Weight Ablation halves the effective load on a winding rope for as long as the glyph holds. **When the lie fails it fails all at once.** Every winding house in the Old World has a bell for that and every man underground knows what the bell means. Spatial translation of ore was tried, costed, and abandoned; moving rock by Transference remains more expensive per ton than moving it up a rope, and will remain so.

The heat ceiling's fall moved the constraint from the fire to **the vessel**, which is why refractory stock, wolfram and chromite and nothing else, is quietly one of the most strategic commodities in the four quarters and is priced accordingly.

> What follows from all this is the shape of the world as it now stands. **There is no steam engine in any mine, forge or mill in the four quarters and there never will be**, because every problem a stationary engine was going to solve had already been solved by other means before anyone thought to build one.
>
> There is instead a continent-wide dependency on hired wardens and the hours they buy at a Core for the most basic functions of a pit, pumping and ventilation, which means that a Wellspring site going thin or a Core's keepers closing their gate does more than inconvenience a mining district. **It drowns it, and then it suffocates whoever went down to see why the pumps had stopped.**

### The fifth obstacle, which fell last

*The four above were removed and are therefore invisible. The fifth held longest, and its removal is the reason the ledger has had to be revised.*

**Traction.** An array is a fixed work. It stands where it was cut, its keepers stand on the ground beside it, and **nothing that moves can bring its keepers' ground along.** Weight Ablation lies to a rope about a load and supplies no motive power whatsoever. Spatial translation of ore was tried, costed and abandoned. So the problem of moving a great weight a long distance overland stayed exactly where it had been for eleven centuries: horses, and the road, and the weather.

> **Which is why the kept road is a street that does not stop.** Ward-cutters keep its bridges and road-wardens its waystones, and a waystation's stones are cut in tuned pairs: the warden fills one in the station, and its twin under a carrier's cart-bed takes the heat across a hand's breadth of air by Transference (the Wellspring that passes energy between tuned things without contact). Nothing burns and nothing boils, a kept wagon rolls through the frost and mud that stop a plain one, and there is no steam engine anywhere in the four quarters, on a road or off it. The horses remain horses, and the Division has received complaints from them.
> 
> *It is an Accord work in every respect that matters: laid out under a company's charter, paid for at every waystation, and stopped on any morning its road-wardens choose to sit down on the steps.* **It beats the one obstacle Essence took longest to reach, and it does so by carrying the fee to every town on the road.**
> 
> The Logistics Division's position is that the kept road is a haulage convenience. **The Division has not yet costed what happens to a district when a kept road reaches it and the whole town goes on the fee in a single season.**

The tenders who work the arrays pay for this in the ordinary way. Long array duty produces the tremor the trade calls **the tender's shake**, a fine persistent palsy of the hands that arrives at about eight years of service and does not leave. Array houses hire young and they hire in numbers.

## Comparative Summary

| Quarter | In one breath | Principal exports | Standing condition |
|---|---|---|---|
| **North** · Inner World | Cold iron country with fire to spare and nothing else cheap | Iron, nickel, copper, salt, beryl, timber | Cheap array heat, expensive everything else |
| **West** · Old World | The richest holes on earth, and a whole people paid by the hour to keep the water out of them | Wolfram, tin, lead, coal, lime, reworked slag | Total array dependency for survival, not production |
| **East** · New World | Ground still being made, gold in the rivers, and every town ready to pick up its hall and run | Gold, silver, sulfur, cement, Onceglass, Cloudmetal, all of these the arc's; the elven old-ground beside it lies outside this ledger | Almost no array dependency outside the eastern arc |
| **South** · Outer World | The poorest quarter in the world, sitting on what the other three cannot do without, with dragons on half of it and Vampires on the rest | Phosphate, nitre, chromite, fulgurite, serpentine | Extraction by hand at scale, value added elsewhere |

A survey has no column for what a place looks like, and I will trespass here once. The clerk who has read only this ledger will arrive expecting a country of holes. He will find pale western towns with sect banners on every bridge and three schools' colors brawling at the bracket gate, lamps in amber and rose along the wharves, eastern markets selling river gold by the pinch under awnings dyed with the same sulfur that is killing the men who gather it, and coppersmiths in every quarter shouting the names of their own kettles. Rent, in every one of them, follows the ground. The ledger will tell him why. It will not prepare him for the noise.

## Hazard Register, Abbreviated

| Hazard | Where | Character |
|---|---|---|
| **Array failure, pumping** | Western deep workings | Flooding within seventy to one hundred and ten hours depending on shaft. **The most likely mass-casualty event in the four quarters and the one the Accord is least prepared for** |
| **Array failure, ventilation** | All deep workings | Firedamp in coal, blackdamp in metal. Kills faster than water and is more easily concealed afterward |
| **Keepers' stoppage** | All array houses | An array-keeper is a person with a wage and a grievance. A walkout starts the same clock as an array failure, and both sides of the dispute can count |
| **Weight Ablation glyph failure** | All winding houses | Total and instantaneous restoration of true load. The bell is rung by hand because no contraption built to give the warning has ever been fast enough |
| **Sulfide roast fume** | the Ring | Chronic. Vegetation loss to six leagues and rising |
| **Vitriol drainage** | Western old workings | Chronic, cumulative, irreversible at current practice |
| **Rock dust, silicate** | Western slate belts | Grinder's cough. Twenty year onset |
| **Rock dust, fibrous serpentine** | Southern coastal quarries | Twenty year onset, invariably fatal, no treatment recorded |
| **Lahar** | Eastern volcanic plains | Sudden, total, and faster than a horse |
| **Fumarole gas pooling** | Eastern sulfur fields | Silent, collects in hollows, **kills rescue parties second** |
| **Wellspring drift** | Eastern quarter generally | Economic rather than physical. Bankrupts rather than kills |
| **Tender's shake** | All array houses, all quarters | Onset at approximately eight years of service. Permanent. **No compensation scheme exists in any quarter** |
| **Crevice proximity** | Southern Crevice margin | Undescribed. Four-league exclusion, revised outward five times in the last sixty years |

## Unresolved Entries

*The Measurewrights record the following as open and decline to guess.*

**The provenance of Ghoststeel.** Attributed to the northern shield on ruin-siting evidence alone. No crucible reproduction has succeeded, and until one does the material's formation is unexplained.

**The provenance of Seerstone Resin.** Attributed provisionally to eastern cloud forest canopy species. Unconfirmed by Archives.

**Whether the Ring's interdimensional passages are caused by the impact structure or merely coincident with it.** The Measurewrights hold that the correlation is too exact to be accident and too unexamined to be asserted.

## The Names That No Longer Attach to Ground

*A separate class of open entry, and the one the Logistics Division finds most irritating.*

Certain materials in continuous trade arrive under provenance names that no current survey can attach to anywhere. **The material is real.** It is assayed, priced, shipped, and used. The place it is said to come from cannot be found by anyone the Accord has sent to look.

| Material | The problem |
|---|---|
| **Vellun Copper** | Trade-route evidence places the source hills in the western quarter. Four surveys have failed to confirm it. **The copper continues to arrive** |
| **Glassroot Fibers** and **Steelsilk Cord** | Two separate Accord records place the source in two separate quarters. Both cannot be right and neither has been withdrawn |
| **Willowblood Sap** and **Moonblight Ore** | Neither source appears on any current survey, and the older registers that name them give no coordinates, only a name and a road that no longer runs anywhere |

The Measurewrights offer three explanations and endorse none. That the names are **commercial fictions** protecting a real source, which is ordinary and would explain the shipping and not the age of the records. That the places **existed and have been depopulated** to the point of leaving no charter behind, which the northern quarter has seen twice within living memory. Or that the names are **older than the Concordance's count can reach** and refer to ground that has since been renamed by people who did not know what they were renaming.

> The Guild notes that the third explanation is the one the corrected chronology makes least available. Fourteen hundred years is not long enough for a country to be forgotten by everybody while its exports continue.
>
> **Something is still loading those ships.**

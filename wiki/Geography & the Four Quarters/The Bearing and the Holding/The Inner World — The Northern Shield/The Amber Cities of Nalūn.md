# The Amber Cities of Nalūn

**Say it** · nah-LOON

*The free salt cities of the Center, wedged between the North and the South*
> **Take the tunnels and you hold rock. The value is in the seals, and the seals answer to the Wellsprings, and the Wellsprings do not recognize occupation.**
>
> *Every serious military assessment of the chain-cities in the last two centuries, arriving at the same paragraph*

Bartlemy Quince of Ironlink Hold owed a wheel of Bridgemarch blue, and no cheese has ever cost anybody more. As a young man he had sealed a friendly instrument with a rival, one wheel a year on midsummer morning for as long as both their houses stood, as a joke, in the salt, with the glyphs done properly because he was showing off. Twenty-eight years later, at the height of his career, he forgot. It was his daughter's wedding in the Great Chain Hall, three thousand guests under the amber walls, wine from four kingdoms, lamp-dancers in gold leaf swinging from the chain overhead, when the rival's widow stood up on her bench and held out an empty cheese-board, and the iron disc on Quince's chest, the oldest active contract he owned, went dead in front of everyone.

The wedding instrument failed to seal under the scribe's hand three times running. By evening no calibrator in the Hold could read his credit. By the end of the month four more of his contracts had stopped answering and his carriers were leaving his loads at the roadside. He spent the next nine years walking cheese up and down the Iron-to-Salt Corridor in a borrowed coat, paying every back wheel by hand, and the widow ate each one in front of him. His daughter married anyway, in a Cutwall tavern, on a handshake, and she will tell you it was the better party.

Nalūn is seven free cities cut into the bed of a dead sea, where a promise is a working, every army that marches through pays for its bread, and nobody has ever worn a crown. How anyone dares live there takes the rest of this entry.

## A Country With No King In It

Nalūn is the name of a country. Its cities are free, each under its own council, and the fourteen factor houses that sit across all of them meet as the Council of Links. The old style, **the Kingdom of Nalūn**, lives in exactly one place, the salutation of its diplomatic instruments, because the Concord Codex's recognition framework requires a sovereign signatory and the Council refused to invent a new category. That is the name you will find on the first circle of the Guild Accord, signed in the lower hall at Kharven-Seat. Ask a Bridgemarch chain-hand who his king is and he will ask what you are selling.

The Center is where the North and the South meet when they want to hurt each other. Nobody marches through the Scourge of Hell, so all their traffic goes round it by two roads, and the eastern one, the salt road, climbs straight through the basin. The chain-cities have been marched through in every generation anyone has written down. They pay both sides, they owe neither, and they have outlasted most of the people who ever came to take them.

Seven cities, a surface port, six provinces. **Roughly forty-five thousand souls underground**, with a further eight thousand in surface settlements and corridor way-stations that depend on the chain-cities for their living.

## The Land and the Look

Come in at dusk, up the salt road, and the first thing you see of Nalūn is Ironlink Hold above ground: a walled rise of honey-colored stone under a deep blue sky, a great domed council hall at the top of it, tall slender call-towers around the dome like reeds around a pool, and one lamp-lit street climbing through the steep dark roofs. Halfway up that street stands a triumphal arch with no top, thick in green creeper. The upper city is the part of Nalūn that gets painted, and it is the smallest part. Below it the cities go down into the bed of the sea, and there the walls themselves glow honey and amber wherever a flame stands near them.

The whole country is a sealed evaporite trough (a basin where a sea dried out and left its salt) **ninety leagues by forty**, on the north side of [[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Scourge of Hell|the Scourge of Hell]]: bounded on the north and west by the basement rock of the northern shield, on the east by the boreal forest where the eastern timber begins, and on the south by a broken rim that looks out over the dry country the cutters call the Thresh. The floor sits three hundred feet below the surrounding shield, **drier, warmer and far steadier than the glaciated plateau above.**

The beds are **bedded halite of exceptional purity**, two hundred fathoms thick, **the preserved floor of an ancient sea that dried out in an era nobody alive can date with confidence.** Each band of salt holds a season of that sea's chemistry, and nobody has counted them to the end. The upper beds have been cut for centuries. **The lower beds, where the Aether pools thicker with every fathom, remain partially explored** and are reached only through the oldest tunnels, *which the cutting houses maintain with an obsessiveness outsiders find comic until they witness the first collapse.* Where a cup's flame catches a seam of clean halite the wall throws it back in pink and violet and a faint drowned green, and the gallery children lie on their backs and count the colors.

**Climate** · Underground it is cold at the top, dry and stable, and the deepest chambers hold a low constant warmth **that visitors describe as the temperature of a body at rest.** Above ground: short summers, five months of snow, **and a wind off the shield carrying the smell of wet granite and boreal resin.** North of the rim lie the chain-lakes, and Long Scratch, the greatest of them, is the road every salt smuggler dreams about in a hard winter.

**Marrowhold** · Outside the country and inseparable from it. The ore and the worked metal of Marrowhold, which older maps call the Ring and whose rim mountains are the ribs of something the Marrowkin carve up and sell, come down the Corridor and pass through Nalūn's supply contracts on their way anywhere else. What it was that died in there is an argument for another shelf.

## The Seven Cities

> Together they form a single organism: **a creature made of salt and contract and the accumulated weight of seven centuries of people who discovered that the most durable thing in the world is a promise made inside a mountain.**

| City | Character |
|---|---|
| **Ironlink Hold** *the seat of the Council* | Largest and oldest, where three salt horizons cross a fault that carried the Aether up from the deep. Above ground the honey-stone rise and the dome called the Hall of Links. Below, five levels, lift-cages, and the Great Chain Hall holding three thousand under lanterns that cast warm light across walls of translucent amber salt. **The most significant Parun-bound contracts in the four quarters are sealed here.** *The air smells of mineral warmth, lamp oil, dried ink, and the sharpness of active resonance that visitors feel as low pressure behind the eyes. The oldest walls glow faintly during a sealing, and that glow is called the Chain Light* |
| **Halcrest** *the gate city* | Uppermost, where the beds thin against the northern rim. **Customs house, weigh-house, lazar-house and the hiring ground of half the free companies in the north.** Goods are weighed and classified here before they go down. See below |
| **Cutwall** *the extraction district* | **A city only loosely**: a shifting complex of work chambers and temporary barracks **that moves slowly through the basin as the cutting houses exhaust one face and open another.** Permanent structures: the stamping houses, the assurance chambers where array-grade Chainsalt is tested, the offices of the six recognized cutting houses. *The rest is scaffolding, sled-track and dust.* **The air tastes of powdered halite and lamp soot**, and career workers develop a dry cough the trade calls **salt-throat**, which never quite goes and which the older cutters wear like a rank |
| **Vaultmere** *the counting city* | Where the salt is exceptionally dense and dry, **ideal for the long keeping of Parun-bound instruments.** Sealed chambers hold the physical record of every significant agreement in the country's history, kept dry by the salt itself. Roughly three thousand: archivists, contract scribes, and the calibrators who verify authenticity. A dead merchant's token is carried here and shelved beside the instrument it stood for. *Visiting factors describe it as the place where you can feel the country's memory pressing against your skin, and they tend to lower their voices and then resent having done it* |
| **Bridgemarch** *the junction* | The central void, where the tunnel network converges and the largest chain-bridges span two hundred feet across open caverns. **Built upward rather than outward**, houses hanging from the ceiling and climbing the walls in tiers. **The sound of chain is constant, a low rhythmic clanking that becomes, for residents, a form of silence**, and on a still night it carries all the way to Halcrest. *More egalitarian, more physical, more inclined to solve problems with engineering than with contracts, a disposition the factor houses regard with the wariness of people who understand that the person who maintains the bridge decides whether the bridge stays up.* The blue cheese is theirs |
| **Deepvein** *the Wellspring floor* | Lowest of the seven, where the beds are thickest and the Oblation pools richest. **Contracts sealed here hold tighter, last longer and enforce with a specificity instruments sealed elsewhere do not match, and the difference is measurable.** Roughly two thousand: the most senior contract specialists in the country, the Measurewright station, and the handful of scholars studying how Contraxis and Fixatio work on each other. *Warm and glowing. The Chain Light is constant here, a faint amber radiance that does not flicker and needs no fuel, and the oldest residents claim they feel the Wellspring the way other people feel weather* |
| **Rimward** *the eastern frontier* | Newest and most vulnerable, founded about 140 years ago to service corridor trade. Not the Holy Sea of Alabaster's harbor of the same name, and letters go astray about once a month. Thinner salt, weaker resonance, **and contracts sealed here carry a slight but measurable reduction in enforcement strength.** **The factor houses call this a scandal. The Rimward merchants call it a selling point**, *because a contract that holds slightly less hard allows slightly more give, and give is what frontier dealing needs* |

### Halcrest

Halcrest is where most people meet Nalūn, and it is the city the songs get wrong. The upper town climbs the basin wall in pale terraces roofed with salt slab, furred white by the frost through its five months of snow, and the bridge-chains strung from terrace to terrace carry worked lamps along their length like beads on a cord. Up in the factor quarter the lamps burn behind panes of clear salt-glass, rose and amber and a clean cold white, and snow falling past them goes pink and gold for the space of a breath. Brass assay posts stand along the shaft-heads, capped in snow, slate-backed and under Guild seal. The salt here is the pale Halcrest kind, its bedding planes showing like the grain of a board, and a lamp cut from it gives a light the color of weak honey.

A worked lamp costs what a sergeant's pay cannot cover, so the town runs on oil above the kitchen. The salt-tea houses rent their upper rooms by the week, and those rooms smell of lamp oil, wet wool and the butter-and-brine fug of the tea boiling below. Merchants wait here for clearance, couriers for dispatches, and free companies for anybody fool enough to pay them: the Lanternless, the Grey Lamps and Fennick's all keep a table at one tea-house or another, and a captain's word on who is a liar will travel the length of the terrace before he has finished his cup. Pay for a season's escort is quoted in stamped bars, and a factor's man will look at your belt for a token before he looks at your face.

Under the town lie the Cuttings, gallery below gallery, three hundred feet and more of steady warmth. The lift-cages go past the galleries with their worked lamps lit and their gates shut, carrying factor men to the stamping floor and up again, where the presses strike the house seal into every bar. The cutting families burn fish-oil in tin cups. They cut for the houses that hold the galleries, Orrel among them, and they are entered by name or number in the drainage book, because the drainage book is the one roll in the salt that everybody keeps honest.

## The Six Provinces

*Each governed by a Provincial Chainwarden, appointed by the Council and bound by Parun-sealed terms specifying their authority, their obligations and the conditions of recall.*

| Province · Chief city | Character |
|---|---|
| **The Ironward** *Ironlink Hold* | Political and financial core. Provincial and Council business are in practice the same thing, **and the Ironward's Chainwarden is traditionally the most junior Council member, given the post as a formality because the Council governs the province directly** |
| **The Upper Basin** *Halcrest* | The province of arrival and departure, **the one outsiders see first and often the only one they see.** Its Chainwarden holds the outer perimeter, the defenses of the surface port on the lake road, and the Chain Gate garrison. *The most militarily significant province of a country that has no military, a distinction the Chainwarden carries with the cheerful competence of someone defending a thing that refuses to believe it needs defending* |
| **The Cuttings** *Cutwall* | Extraction, from the galleries under Halcrest out to the moving face. The six cutting houses operate under their own internal governance, **which the Council regulates and does not administer**, and they defend that distinction with an obstinacy that is itself a provincial trait. **The most physically dangerous district in the country.** *The safety record is good by any standard except the standard of people who have never been underground* |
| **The Vaultlands** *Vaultmere* | Archival. **Deliberate, precise and suspicious of haste in any form.** Home to the largest concentration of trained scribes in the Center, each of whom completed **a seven-year apprenticeship before being permitted to draft an instrument that would be sealed.** *A factor without a scribe is a man with opinions. A scribe without a factor is a scribe with a full calendar* |
| **The Span** *Bridgemarch* | Transit. Defined by engineering more than commerce. **Has produced more Chief Engineers than Council members**, a figure its residents cite with pride and the Council cites with concern. *The factor houses decide where things go, but the Span decides whether they get there, and the difference matters more than anyone in Ironlink Hold is comfortable admitting* |
| **The Eastern Reach** *Rimward* | Frontier. **Bears the full weight of the corridor closures**: supply disruptions, stranded convoys, and mountain settlements that have received nothing for months. Deepvein sits inside its borders as a special zone under direct Council authority, administered by a Council-appointed Steward, producing exactly the friction one would expect between a frontier province and a carve-out in its middle |

## The Wellsprings and the Oath

> Nalūn sits on a great site of **Oblation, the Wellspring of what is given up**, one of the sixty types of Aether that affect the Soul Crystal each in its own way, a Fate Wellspring, and a contract is two parties giving something up in front of a witness. Worked this way, on this much ground, it is **able to seal an agreement into metaphysical law.** This is the single fact upon which the entire country is built. A site where such Aether pools is a Core in common speech, and Nalūn's has its keepers in the factor houses, who have never once closed it, because they know exactly what they would be closing.

**Oblation** · *Contract and agreement, as the salt towns read it.* It produces few practitioners in the conventional sense and **a great ambient field in which spoken and written agreements, properly sealed, acquire metaphysical weight.** A Parun-bound contract (one written in Parun, the glyph script that sets boundaries on law) executed under Contraxis resonance **is a working**, with no court behind it and none required, and breaking it has consequences that arrive **without anyone needing to be convinced, summoned or bribed.**
> The consequences are structural. The breaker's Essence (the refined power a practitioner holds and spends) becomes unreliable, their instruments fail to seal, **their credit becomes physically unreadable to anyone attuned to the field.**
>
> ***The punishment for breaking a Nalūn contract is that Nalūn stops working for you**, and in a world where Nalūn's instruments are the backbone of long-distance credit, that is a punishment worse than any court could impose.*

**Fixatio** · Concentrated in the salt itself, making Chainsalt **an Essence dielectric of exceptional quality**, which in plain words means a stuff that will not carry Essence, will not keep it and will not wear down under the strain of holding it back. *The interaction gives the chain-cities their character: agreements made inside the salt are bound by the one Wellspring and shielded from tampering by the other.*

**Parun-bound instruments** · A document inscribed with specific glyph sequences, sealed in a linked token, executed inside the resonance field. **Each party holds a token, a small iron or bronze disc stamped with the chain-mark, which hums faintly in the metal while the contract is sound and goes silent and cold when it is breached.**

**The seal-token** · Every significant commercial actor carries one, representing their oldest active contract, and "What's your oldest?" comes before the name. *A merchant without a token is a merchant without a history, and a merchant without a history is a merchant without credit.* A Nalūn seal binds to a house name. A tokenless man in the chain-cities cannot sign for a sack of meal.

## Who Rules

**The Council of Links** · The senior partners of the fourteen recognized factor houses, sworn in by Parun-bound contracts specifying obligations, term, authority and the consequences of exceeding any of the three.
> **Four Council members in the recorded history of the Links have been removed by the mechanism of their own oath**: their tokens going inert mid-session when the Wellspring determined they had breached the terms under which they held their seat. The chairs were refilled. The four are named in the record only by what they broke.

**The High Chainlord** · The one who signs for the country, with a chair and a seal and no crown, chosen by **the Trial of Ten Deals**: ten days, ten increasingly complex commercial problems, judged by retired factors, active Measurewrights (the Guild's sworn assayers of Aether) and one representative of the Accord's Arbitration Division, with odds posted on every terrace from the first morning. **The candidate who resolves the most without breaching any existing contract, creating any new liability, or insulting any party whose goodwill the country requires** takes the office. *Held for life, or until the holder's oath-token goes inert, whichever arrives first.*

**The factor houses** · Fourteen recognized, each a partnership bound by Parun-sealed articles. **The articles are public documents and anyone may read them.** Orrel and Vessant are the two a stranger most often hears named: Orrel for its cuttings under Halcrest, and Vessant for the oldest supply contract into the eastern timber, iron stock and lamp oil out, furs and resin back, every mile of it through Stark country.

**The city councils** · Each of the seven runs its own streets, its own watch and its own quarrel with the Hold. Bridgemarch's sits on a platform slung over the drop, and a member who talks too long is said to find the chain being let out.

## Wealth and Trade

**Salt** · Cast into standard bars of fixed weight and fineness, stamped by the cutting house, circulating as money across the northern quarter and well into the west. **At once a food, a preservative, an array-maker's isolation stock (arrays being standing runed workings) and a currency.** *A salt bar passes for a silver mark at the Accord rate, more in a bad winter, and it can be eaten, which no other money can.*

**Credit** · Parun-bound enforceability solved the problem that defeated every other economy of the age: **long-distance trust.** A bill drawn on Ironlink Hold is accepted at the furnaces of Marrowhold, in the Old World's western ports and in trade posts across the frontier, and **those places need feel no respect for Nalūn, because the instrument enforces itself.** A bill issued by a factor house can be carried by an unarmed man across three jurisdictions and presented for payment. ***Metal does not need to travel if claims on metal can.***

**Supply contracts** · A commitment to deliver at a rate, for a term, from a named source. **The most traded document in the four quarters**, and the factor houses broker the majority of them because their instruments are the only ones the counterparties trust to hold. A river carrier who signs for a load feels the seal take hold of him, faintly, behind the breastbone.

**The Iron-to-Salt Corridor** · The principal overland route from the furnaces of Marrowhold to the chain-cities and on to the coast. Serviced by independent merchants under Nalūn-sealed contracts, **most prominently the Greis Trading House, which operates eleven way-stations along the route.**

**Interest** · **Where enforcement is physical rather than moral, lending stops being a sin and becomes a schedule.** *The Holy Sea of Alabaster maintains that this is a distinction without a difference, and has maintained it for four hundred years while keeping its money at Ironlink Hold.*

## Rent, Bread and the Street

Underground has never meant hushed. Bridgemarch at change of shift is a roaring market hung over a two-hundred-foot drop, with bread-and-salt sellers working the chain-bridges on ropes, lamp-wardens' boys running amber and rose lanterns along the tiers, and the cutting-house bracket crews in their house colors fighting on a salt floor while half the district bets sealed slips on them. Every tunnel has its petty trade: a woman at Halcrest who can find a short-weight bar with her tongue and charges by the lick, a drain-walker who hears standing water through a wall and has twice struck over late fees, a Vaultmere copyist whose ink stays wet until the signature is honest and who is booked for three years.

**Rent** goes to the tunnel's holder with a drain-share on top, and a household behind on the drain-share is out faster than a household behind on the rent, for the reason Salt Law gives below. Property is measured in salt, so a family's standing is the volume its ancestors cut.

**Supper** is salt fish and hard bread, salted meat when the surface has sent it, and grain and medicine that were weighed at Halcrest before anybody ate them. A host at his threshold shaves a curl off a stamped bar onto a heel of bread and hands it over, and a guest who eats it has eaten the host's money. Salt tea (black tea boiled with salt and butter) is sold by the bowl on every terrace in Halcrest, and in the private chambers of the factor houses it is where the real positions get stated.

**The classes** · Three, defined by relationship to the instruments. **The factor class**, partners and senior employees of the fourteen houses. **The cutting class**, miners, processors, engineers. **The agent class**, everyone operating under a sealed instrument without being a partner: independent merchants, bonded couriers, ward searchers, scribes, calibrators. Beneath all three lies what the rolls decline to call a class: the tokenless, the people who came down into the galleries with no paper and no house, whom the factor houses take in the way they take in everything that arrives without a claim on it. A party without standing is not a party. They cut, they are entered in the drainage book under a number, and no instrument in the country can be broken against them, a fact the Measurewrights have noted and the Council has not been asked to discuss.

**The dead** · The salt keeps them. The cutting class lays its dead in a worked-out face, and the salt takes them and does not let them go soft. A merchant's funeral is also a reading of his open contracts, because they do not die with him.

**Words for the shared arts** · The aura coat is **the surety** here, Pressure is **the call**, and sensing is **reading a man's seal**. A Guild roll uses the proper words and everyone in the salt thinks those are the affected ones. A practitioner's First Release is "called," like a note, and a release held too long is **standing water**, which is the rudest thing you can say about anyone's technique.

**Language** · Concord Common spoken with a precision that reflects the culture's relationship to binding language. **Contracts are drafted in a register so exact it has become a technical dialect.** A loose word in Ironlink is a small liability.

**Religion** · The Holy Sea maintains a chapel in Ironlink Hold, **staffed by a single cleric whose primary function appears to be changing money.** Devotional life centers on the Wellspring, and the rites around sealing a great contract carry a ceremonial gravity that does the work of worship. Nobody drinks in a sealing room, and scribes blot with salt, because a spilled cup is held to void the deal.
> ***The chain-cities do not pray. They negotiate, and the negotiation is the prayer.***

## Law

**The Honor Challenge** · Insulting a merchant's honor is a binding challenge, **because a merchant's honor is a load-bearing financial instrument.** Resolution: retraction, arbitration, or **a formal counter-negotiation in which the accused must demonstrate, through prescribed transactions under full Wellspring scrutiny, that their conduct meets the standard the accuser claimed it did not.** *The loser pays all costs.* These draw bigger crowds than most executions and the Bridgemarch bookies run a book on the third transaction.

**Salt Law** · Property is measured in salt: **the volume a family's ancestors cut, the tunnels that cutting produced, and the right to maintain them against collapse.**
> **The Drainage Obligation** is the single most important provision. Every tunnel owner must maintain their drainage to a standard sufficient to prevent intrusion into adjacent tunnels, **because salt fails in standing water.**
>
> **It is enforced by the Wellspring.** *A tunnel owner who neglects their drains finds their contracts going unreliable before the water arrives.*

## How It Fights

The **Chain Guard** is roughly eight hundred professionals responsible for tunnel security, the surface perimeter and the physical safety of the factor houses, in harness and amber surcoats, with crossbows on every terrace. **Competent, well-equipped and entirely insufficient to resist a determined assault.**
> **This does not matter. Taking Nalūn by force accomplishes nothing.**
>
> An invading army would find itself responsible for maintaining drainage works that require three centuries of institutional knowledge, **and would fail at it within a season.**
>
> The real defense is economic. Every crown, lending house and war-chest in the four quarters depends on these instruments. ***A war against Nalūn is a war against one's own account-books.***

The proof stands in the upper city at Ironlink Hold. Marshal Han Do-yun, when Sum-gol was still the empire of the whole South, took the rise in eleven days with thirty thousand men and sat down in the Hall of Links, and he ordered a triumphal arch to be raised on the street below it, in honey stone, with his campaigns cut into it in letters a yard high. He paid the masons in bills on Ironlink Hold, because that was what his war-chest held. His quartermaster stopped the drains on the third level to billet a regiment. Six weeks later the water reached the first contract, the marshal's bills went dead one after another in the pockets of his own masons, and they put down their tools at the top of the piers, with the keystone in its cradle, and went to dinner. He was out of the country by autumn. The arch stands as they left it, under its creeper, and the Council has it entered as a forfeit against an unpaid account, on which interest continues to run.

So the cities have a custom for armies. A marshal coming through is met at Halcrest or Rimward by a scribe with a **march contract**: a named road, a term, a schedule of what bread and fodder cost, and a token for his own belt. Sign it and the bakers open. Loot, and the war-chest paper that pays your army stops being money by the end of the week. North and South have both signed it, frequently in the same month, and the cities pay each side a quiet fee against the quartering, which each side enters as tribute and the factor houses enter as insurance.

Where it comes to blows the fighting is hired. Free companies sign under seal out of Halcrest and fight like men whose wages depend on the instrument holding, and the factor houses keep contract specialists at Deepvein who can lay a clause across a doorway that a man will feel behind the eyes before he crosses it. Those are the people the bracket crews are afraid of.

## What They Joke About

The arch, always. "Paid in full," said of anything left half built. Rimward seals, a promise with give in it, and "a man with opinions" for a factor with no scribe. Every child in Bridgemarch can do the cheese-board, holding out both empty hands with a widow's face. They say Nalūn has been conquered eleven times and billed for it twelve. They tell northerners that the chain is what holds the South off them, and they tell southerners the same, and in the Cuttings they say a factor has two kinds of friend: the ones under contract and the ones in the salt.

## Monsters on the Roads

Water is the great enemy, and what comes with it: Sump-toads (T5 on the Bestiary's Danger Tier, which runs from T1 to T9 and prices every bounty in the world) in any neglected gallery, the kind of creature a drainage book exists to prevent. Lamp-drinkers (T6, found only as the darkest place on a dark shape) have ended whole shifts in the older tunnels, and the lamp-wardens charge accordingly. Blind white fish in the old flooded cuts and the bats of the upper galleries are the only wild animals most of the forty-five thousand will ever meet, and a blind fish at a wedding is a boast.

Above ground the beasts are the plain northern kind, and hungry. Goats on the salt-bush bring wolves, the wolves work the ice of Long Scratch in a hard winter and have learned the hour a smuggler's sled goes out, and the ice-road taverns will sell you a pelt with the hole in the right place, for those who wish to have been brave. In bad years, stilts and greywalkers come up off the Thresh to the southern rim, a stilt being a herd animal on legs as long as a ship's mast, thousands together, and a greywalker the upright, long-armed thing that follows them in files at dusk. In dry years haze-kites the color of an old copper pot ride the outward wind as far as the rim road and take mules.

**Deepvein** is the worked Core, the great Oblation site (the Wellspring of what is given up, and here of contract), and its keepers in the factor houses have never closed it. The dungeon is **the Lower Beds**, below the deepest maintained gallery, reached only through the oldest tunnels and only partially explored, where the Aether pools thicker with every fathom and a whole cutting house, Merrow's, went down in 590 and has gone on sending up its drainage fee on time, in bars, by a lift nobody is working. The top of it is a country, the bottom is partly explored, and it is the only dungeon a nation's law depends on. What the cutting houses have walled off down there they will not say for any fee. The Accord holds a bargain with a speaking spawn (the rare deep creature that talks) void at law, and no Measurewright has been able to say whether the Contraxis at that depth has read the Accord. The Bridgemarch bookies run a long book on the day Merrow's payment fails to come up, and they have been paying out to the patient since 590. ## Beside the Scourge

The southern rim of the basin is the north side of the worst neighbor in the world. [[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Scourge of Hell|The Scourge of Hell]] begins past it, across the dry country called **the Thresh**, then the Hem (its outermost belt, fifteen leagues of black-barked thornwood and pale dust), then the Black Roots, and in high summer the wind comes up hot and dry off all of it, smelling of iron and pollen, and sets the salt-slab roofs ticking. Two trades here answer to the word cutter: the cutting class, who cut salt, and the free-company hunters on Thresh contracts (monster bounties, paid by anyone with a monster problem), who cut up whatever the bounty names. Each takes the confusion as an insult.

Halcrest, on the far side of the basin from the Thresh, is where every crew signs on, with cutters' taverns and a map trade, and everybody on its terraces will sell you a copy of a copy of the good map. Two of those taverns face each other across one lamp-chain, the Ordinary Sword and the Other One, under the same picture on the sign: a lone figure on a ridge under a purple sky, looking up at a colossal bronze-hilted sword driven into the mountains, with a field of violet stars where its blade should be. On perhaps nine days a year you can see the real one from the Hem, a dark upright line on the sky, and the crews come back up the south road and argue about it over salt tea.

Nalūn's one contribution everybody admires is **the Salt Line**: a strip of salted ground, worked by hedge-workers with Chainsalt so that Aether will not pool on it, some sixty miles along the Scourge's north rim and a hundred paces deep, renewed each spring. Whatever crosses it comes off its Well (ground thick with one kind of Aether) and comes weaker. It costs a fortune and it holds about as well as a fence. The work is let under seal, and the cutters of Halcrest will tell you which stretches were salted honestly and which were let under a Rimward seal, with give in it.

Then there is [[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Scourge of Hell#The Turn|the Turn]]: North, then South, then Center, nine years each, with whoever holds it keeping the Line of forts around the Hem, paying the door bounties and bleeding, while the other two watch their borders and count what it costs. The Center holds it now, from 712 to 721, and on the Center's Turn Nalūn pays in salt and complains in writing. A Vaultmere scribe drafts its Count, the bill each party presents when a Turn ends, in a hand so exact that the Accord's auditors at Sealhall have twice tried to hire her. On the North's Turn or the South's, the host bound for the Line comes up the salt road like any other army, right of passage in hand, and is met by a scribe with a march contract, and it signs, because a right of passage has never yet made a baker open. The difference carried forward between the three Counts is now larger than all the coin in the world, and claims on it trade at Ironlink Hold for a sliver of a copper on the mark. They have never once failed to find a buyer.

Every other town on the rim builds on stone footings with no cellars, for fear of what comes up through the ground. Nalūn is all cellar. No black root has ever been found in the salt, the drainage book keeps a column for it, and on the day a Bridgemarch engineer writes something in that column I should like to be elsewhere.

## Neighbors and Grudges

**The North** · The Banner Houses buy on credit and resent the rate. Stark country carries the Vessant contract, Marrowhold's metal comes down through Thornwall's corridor, and the Painted Rivers of Hallenfeld fight the Corridor for every load that could go by water.

**The Guild Accord** · The Accord handles disputes between parties who did not seal under Nalūn authority. Nalūn handles disputes between parties who did. **The arrangement has never been codified, because codifying it would require acknowledging that Nalūn's Wellsprings constitute a rival jurisdiction**, and the cities' name on the first circle has only made the silence more expensive. The Sealed Charter next door regards Ironlink Hold as an unlicensed court with a very good roof.

**The Holy Sea of Alabaster** · *The theological reconciliation required to condemn the interest and keep the deposit is an achievement of ecclesiastical reasoning that the chain-cities regard with genuine admiration and no small amount of professional respect.*

**The Hundred Crowns of the Midmarch** · Owe Nalūn more than any other people in the Center, jointly and severally, and a good third of their wars are about who inherits the debt.

**The Moonlight Forest of Mosk-Vaal** · The Moss Court's law makes a spilled cask of salt a capital matter, and every salt carter on the Kessen road is a walking insult to it. The Court takes the coin and forgives nothing, and the city councils keep a clerk whose only work is apologizing.

**The Marrowkin** · The best customers and the worst, since no instrument has yet settled who owns what is being sold.

**The Zettari** · Commercial rather than political. Corridor merchants operate under Nalūn instruments in Zettari territory, **creating a jurisdictional complexity both parties manage by pretending it does not exist.**

## The Corridors Now

**The Withering, the slow thinning of the world's Aether, has not touched Nalūn's Wellspring in any measurable way.** The cities function. The instruments seal. The credit flows.

**What has changed is the world around the country.** The war that ended in the mud at Shimane collapsed the authority that kept order between Nalūn and the frontier. The eastern corridors are partially closed. **The factor houses sit behind their Parun-sealed doors. The mountain settlements have received no salt, no iron stock and no medicine for months.**
> **The High Chainlord's position** is that the restoration of outside order is no business of Nalūn's. **The Eastern Reach's Chainwarden disagrees, loudly and on record**, and calls the policy of waiting an absence of policy.
>
> **The factor houses' position**, expressed less officially and over salt tea in private chambers, is that a world in which the corridors stay closed is a world in which their supply books lose value, **and a factor house whose supply book has lost its value has lost the only thing it cannot replace.**
>
> *The tension between these positions brought a woman named Alkia of the Thornridge settlements to speak with a merchant named Torven Greis about roads, and wards, and what a man with wagons owes to the people who depend on them.*
>
> **A man who owns the ore is rich until the road closes. A man who owns the road is rich until the road falls apart. A man who owns the wards on the road is rich until he dies, and if he dies, the road falls apart, and then nobody is rich. That is the whole of the business.**
>
> *Torven Greis*

*Later hand, from the last winter before the turn, running past the year these shelves stand in:* the Northern Empire of Whitemere, when it came down from the north with a name and a capital, was met at Halcrest by a scribe with a march contract. The rate had gone up.

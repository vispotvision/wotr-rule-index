# The Aurora Reach of the Keld

**Say it** · KELD

*House Keld · The Polar Reach · Beyond the Treeline*

Cuthbert Ansell, Measurewright of the Third Hand, came north in Year 694 with four mules, a set of brass assay forks and a theory, and he arrived at the Whalebone Gather in the bright week after the sun comes back, when the whole Reach is out on the ice for the sled-races with their faces painted and walrus ivory clicking in their braids. The palace stood over the camp the way it always does, white and ice-blue, its spiral towers and blue-glass domes throwing long shadows on the cracked snow. Ansell announced to the fires that it was a perfectly ordinary Spirit War relic and that he intended to spend the night in it and measure it, and an old sled-woman asked him what he would bet. He put up the mules. She put up a reindeer team, and by evening the whole Gather had put up something, the odds were painted on a whale's jaw, and four hundred people watched a small man in a good coat walk across the covered bridge under the green aurora with a lantern, singing to keep his spirits up.

In the morning he came out by the far tower, on the wrong side of the snowfield, in excellent spirits. His beard had grown to his belt. He had, he said, had a most interesting evening, and he asked if the race had started. He spent the next eleven years writing nine hundred pages on the dimensions of the third dome, and the Guild has three copies, and no two of them agree on how many sides it has. The sled-woman took his mules and gave him back the assay forks, on the grounds that they had stopped being his. And the Keld, who had told him all of this would happen, went on doing what they have done every year for as long as there have been Keld, which is to pitch their camp in the shadow of the most beautiful building in the world and refuse to sleep inside it.

The Aurora Reach belongs to the hardest people in the north, living beyond the last tree under a sky on fire, around a palace nobody living built, and they will follow you exactly as long as you are worth it.

## The Land and What It Looks Like

Past the treeline there is nothing to govern. North of the Thornwood and the Stark bears the last spruce gives out, and then it is tundra and permafrost to the polar shelf, which is a geological fact more than a border. It is also, against all reasonable expectation, one of the most beautiful places alive. Rose mountains stand far off along the horizon. The snow is white and blue and pale gold according to the hour, cracked into enormous plates where the frost has heaved it. The aurora comes out most clear nights in green and violet and hangs from one side of the world to the other, and a pale moon sits up there in it looking cold. In summer the moss goes green and scarlet and there are more birds than you would believe, and the herds come through in rivers.

**The palace** stands on a cracked snowfield toward the western end of the Reach: a white and ice-blue thing of spiral towers and blue-glass domes, with a long covered bridge running out to a far tower that stands alone. Snow-bent firs grow around it, curling over like breaking waves, the only trees for a hundred miles and alive for no reason any forester can give. No one living built it. It is old work, the Guild says, out of the days of the Great Spirit War or earlier, though the Guild says that about most things it cannot get into. The Keld have called it the **Kiilut House** for as long as they have talked, *kiilut* being one of the dead left without the Waiting, a corpse nobody sat with, and the worst word they own. They camp beneath it, trade beneath it and race around it. They will not go in, and they will not cut the firs.

Around it, and for a thousand miles, are hide camps and sleds. A Keld camp is long lodges of reindeer hide over whalebone, nine fat-lamps to a war-lodge, sled-dogs staked out in rows, drying racks of windmeat, and smoke going straight up. There is no wood on the tundra, so the Keld burn fat, seal and reindeer and the yellow tallow of the great horned musk-beasts that come down off the high ice in the Thin Weeks. It burns low and smoky and very hot, the deep yellow of butter, with a smell like a butcher's yard on a summer afternoon.

### The major places

The Keld have no city, and consider this an advantage in several ways.

- **The Whalebone Gather.** The one great seasonal camp, in the shadow of the palace: ten thousand people for two bright weeks, jaws of whales set up in rows as gateposts, and sled-races, marriages, feuds settled, children named, a market in ivory and beryl and furs, and the only time in the year a southern factor can buy anything without being looked at as if he had brought his own weather. Then the camp goes, and there is a large stain in the snow and a palace.
- **The Teeth.** The high summer pastures, fought over by the Sennet clan and the Haldorssons since their grandfathers' grandfathers.
- **The shelf.** Where the land stops. Sealers go out on it. A certain kind of Moto practitioner goes out on it as well, because the emptiness is the only space large enough to practice Domain-scale techniques (a Domain being a working that makes a whole piece of the world obey its user) without killing bystanders. The Keld find them, some weeks later, and return their boots.

**Roads.** There are none. You come by sled, north from the Thornwood or along Threeways' northern road and past the Stark bears, in the hard months only, since the thaw turns the whole country to bog.

## House and Rule

**House Keld** is the smallest house, the hardest people and the most conditionally loyal. They live beyond the treeline in conditions that would kill members of any other house inside a week and that the Keld consider baseline. They are low and wide and dense, built by the tundra, with barrel chests and short powerful legs, and they count their years differently from the south, or decline to.

They have no seat and no lord in the way the others mean it. The clans lead their own fires, and over the fires there is whoever every fire will follow. In Year 715 that is **Old Harald**, too old now to sit a horse; the talk on the ice is about his grandson, who is about seventeen and has never lost a fight he chose. The Keld are counted among the Seven Warlords, by the old count, by anyone who has met them.

Names are carried. A Keld child is given the name of someone dead and is held to carry that person, and the dead person is held to be in some sense still about; the Keld release, when a fighter's whole power rises, is called **the name stands**. Ask a Keld how old she is and she may tell you ninety, and she is not joking, and she is nineteen.

**The Keld swear to a man.** Never to a letter, and never to a house. The oath goes to whoever comes out onto the tundra in person, sits at their fires, eats their food and stays worth following. *We follow because you are worth following, and we stop when you stop being worth it.* Nobody has ever got a better term out of them, and quite a lot of silver has tried.

## Wealth and Trade

Whale-oil, seal-fat, walrus ivory, whalebone, reindeer hides, white-fox and bear furs, musk-beast tallow, salt fish, windmeat, and from the pegmatite dikes on the northern margin the odd crystal of **Clearbeam beryl**, sometimes longer than a man, which they drag south on a sled and sell for less than the Guild would have paid if the Guild had got there first. They buy iron, salt and grain, in that order, and have a settled opinion that everything else is a way to get you killed. The whale-oil trade has drawn envious southern warbands past the treeline more than once. Those warbands are the subject of a popular song cycle on the ice.

There is no coin on the Reach. Exchange is meat-sharing, labor-debt and salt, and a hunter's kill is shared out by custom so strict that the one who killed it often gets the worst of it.

## Rent, Food and the Street

Rent is a meaningless word here and you will get a blank look. A lodge belongs to the woman who sewed it. Food is seal, reindeer, fish, whale when there is whale, blood-soup, frozen fat cut in curls, windmeat, stonecurd and the sour fermented milk in horn cups, served at the door. Refuse it, and you will be called skin-refuser, which is said once and then there is a fight. Keld sit on the bare packed snow of the lodge floor, cross-legged, and rise from it all at once with no hand on the floor, and a southerner who needs a stool will get one and will never be asked anywhere again.

Street life is camp life, and it is louder than you would expect: dogs, drums, racing bets, the long throat-songs that go on all night in the dark months. Faces are painted for joy and for war in blues and reds from the shelf-clays. Practitioners are a plain matter of who can keep a lamp lit in a whiteout or sniff out a herd, and Keld hedge-workers will talk the rot out of a stored seal for a share of the seal. Their word for a soul's weight is the same as for a coming storm, and they have a low opinion of the southern argument about it.

**What they joke about.** How tall southerners are. Horses a hand too high for anything. What a Measurewright says when his forks stop working. The Kiilut House, never: you can joke about your mother on the Reach, and about the dead, and you can tell any story you like about what is in that palace, but a joke is a thing you say to something that is listening. The curse is *Qatuq take you*, Qatuq being the cold that takes the unready; the insult is *numra*, an empty bowl held out.

## Law and How It Fights

**Law.** The elders of the fires. A dispute goes to them and they talk until the lamp runs down. A killing costs a killing or a great many reindeer. A person who breaks trust is left behind at the next move, which on the Reach is an unusual kind of sentence, in that nobody has to carry it out. The dead are given to the sky or the shelf, and the Waiting is kept in the lodge with the lamps turned down.

**The fighting.** Keld warriors, few as they are, are worth three of anyone else's. A fighter raised on the Polar Reach has already survived conditions that would kill the enemy before the fighting started, and the fighting itself is an afterthought beside the surviving. The Keld ride small shaggy tundra horses and fight in the **long line**, which moves across open snow like weather, and in a wedge when something needs to be broken, with birch lances with leaf-shaped heads and short horn-backed bows firing black-glass arrowheads out of the white. Their great trick is the whiteout. They can move through snow-blind fast and quiet by reading the wind and the feel of the ground, and southerners die without having seen who killed them. Tenrai tax columns were sent onto the ice for years, until the Tenrai learned better than to do that.

## Monsters on the Roads

**The beasts** are the whole economy and they know it. Reindeer come through in rivers, the great horned musk-beasts come down off the high ice in the Thin Weeks, white fox work the edges of every camp, and on the shelf there are seal, walrus and whale, any of which will turn over a boat. Everything the Keld eat, burn and wear has at some point tried to kill them, and they consider this good manners.

**The monsters.** Monsters move, and they live across the whole of the Inner World, even out here. **Droval**, the Frost-Howlers or Rimegaunts, are the Reach's own terror, spirit-touched hunters of the snowfields that lie along a ridge until the ridge is suddenly not there. Wild bears come next, and packs of what the Mandate's northern wall calls an interesting category.

### The Kiilut House

The [[The Bestiary/Dungeons|dungeon]] (what a Wellspring Core makes around itself, or what grows up around one) is **the palace**, which the Keld call the **Kiilut House**, a *kiilut* being one of the dead left without the Waiting: white and ice-blue, spiral towers and blue-glass domes, a covered bridge to a far tower, old work no one living built. It may hold Living Armor (T5 on the Bestiary's Danger Tier, which runs from T1 to T9) and worse. No one has cleared it, since no one can agree how many sides its third dome has. Measurewright Ansell spent one night inside in 694 and came out by the far tower with his beard at his belt. The Keld camp beneath it, trade beneath it, race around it, and will not sleep inside. The register enters its kind as unknown. Crews who cross the covered bridge to the far tower are not on any roll afterward, and Ansell is the only name that has ever come back that way, which the Keld put down to the palace not having finished with him. In a Keld mouth a dungeon is a house nobody sat with, which they regard as bad manners on a very large scale, and you do not go in. More on the [[The Bestiary/Dungeons|Dungeons]] shelf and in the [[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Inner World — A Gazetteer|Gazetteer]].

### The Scourge and the Turn

The [[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Scourge of Hell|Scourge of Hell]] lies most of a continent to the south, and the Seat's clerks count the Keld in the North's share of it anyway. The Turn is the old rotation by which North, South and Center each spend nine years keeping its monsters off everybody else, and on the North's Turn a summons reaches the fires by letter, and the Keld, who swear to a person and never to a letter, feed it to the lamp. What the Line (the chain of forts and beacon towers round the Scourge's outer belt) gets from the Reach is fat. Those beacon towers burn whale-oil and musk tallow on anybody's Turn, because it burns hot and low in a hard wind, so oil sold at the Whalebone Gather to a southern factor turns up on the rim a year later at a price the sled-woman who sold it would find very funny.

Now and then the Line also gets a fighter. A Keld who carries the name of someone who died on the rim is held to have a shift unfinished, and she will ride south on her own on a small shaggy horse, in hide, in that heat, and present herself at the nearest fort, and whoever holds the Turn had better feed her. They are worth three of anyone else's, and they leave on the day the dead person would have gone home. The Center holds the Turn until 721 and the Keld have no view on the matter. The Count, the running bill the three parties present to one another, has never yet managed to charge them anything, since there is no coin on the Reach.

- **[[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Cobalt March of Hollenmark|The Cobalt March]].** The wild white bears of the Reach and the ridden ones of the March are the same animal, and the two houses agree on one thing, which is that nobody can tell the bears anything. A Keld elder tasted the crust of that famous pie and remarked it would have made a decent meal for one.
- **Each other.** The Sennet and the Haldorssons, over the Teeth, without intermission.

# The Cobalt March of Hollenmark

**Say it** · HOLL-en-mark

*House Stark · The Eastern March · Where Things End*

Young Sweyn Stark was nineteen in the spring of Year 668, freshly made a rider and in love with the sound of himself, when he led forty Starks on white icebears up to the old boundary stones to explain to a survey party from Marrowhold whose moss they were standing on. It was a fine morning, the sky hard blue, the snow pink with low sun, every bear's fur dusted in frost and every rider's breath going up like a banner. The holds' rib-sounders had brought a table and were eating lunch. Sweyn rode out ahead of the line on a sow called Mistress Knife, nine feet at the shoulder and the most celebrated bear in the March, stood up in the stirrups, let his hide go blue-white so the whole hillside could admire it, drew his grandfather's great sword and screamed FROST HOLDS THE LINE in a voice they heard at Stonewatch.

Mistress Knife stopped. She turned her head and regarded him for about as long as it takes to read a short letter. Then she reached round, took him by the thigh, lifted him out of the saddle, shook him twice, put him down in the snow with the leg broken in three places, ate his saddlebags, and walked home. The other thirty-nine bears, on reflection, sat down. The rib-sounders laughed until one of them had to be thumped on the back, sent a bonesetter over with the rest of the lunch, and the border held quiet for thirty years on the strength of that afternoon. Sweyn lived to be seventy and limped all of it, and he told the story himself at every midwinter, because in Hollenmark a rider who is put off his bear has been told the truth by someone with no reason to lie.

The Cobalt March is a blue fortress at the edge of everything, held by people who ride animals that can fire them, and who have been deciding where the world stops for almost four centuries.

## The Land and What It Looks Like

**Hollenmark** is a cold blue stone city under a snow mountain, and in a clear dusk the whole of it is the color of a good enamel. Its heart is a pair of heavy round twin towers joined high up by an arched bridge, with snow forever blowing through the streets under them. Blue ice grows on every battlement and nobody chips it off, since it does more for the wall than the wall does for it. Beasts are carved all over the dark walls, bears and wolves and things the masons had heard about, and high up on the right a pale citadel looks down on the town, catching the last of the light long after the streets have gone dark. The wolf of Hollenmark is on the banners, the gate and the coin-weights.

Outside the walls is the March proper, the Ironwood March on older maps: boreal forest giving way to shield upland, ironwood and black spruce under deep snow, standing water frozen blue, and at every rise a **boundary stone**, waist-high and lichen-green and older than the house's records, with a Stark rider on a white icebear somewhere within sight of it. The light is extraordinary. Autumn sends the broadleaf stands up in scarlet and gold against the blue ice, for about three weeks, and then it is over and everyone goes back to work.

### The major places

- **Hollenmark.** The seat. The twin towers, the bridge, the pale citadel, the hall where the Lord of Hollenmark seals the house's business, and a street of sword-cutlers who will tell you what is wrong with your grip before they take your money.
- **Stonewatch.** A hard little garrison town on the eastern line with a view of the old boundary stones, the Roll-house where the Stark surveyors keep the record, and the training yard of the Thrawn Yard, who wear undyed wool and one red thumb and have a song with a single line.
- **Bearhold.** A high stockade and a great deal of open ground in the northern March, where the white bears come in the autumn of their own will to look over the young of the house. A Stark who wants to ride walks out there unarmed, in a shirt, and stands still. This is called the Asking. There is a small graveyard.

**Roads.** The eastern road runs in from Threeways, timber goes out down the eastern rivers, and the Vessant supply line comes in from the Amber Cities under a tithe, with a bronze disc to show at the gates. East, past the stones, is Marrowhold. West is the plateau and the Seat, and north along the head of the March lies the Thornwood.

## House and Rule

**House Stark** defines where things end and begin, and has done so with a straight face for three hundred and seventy-six years by its own measure. The Roll, or Stone Roll, records the position of every boundary stone on the March, who last stood at it and what was decided there, and a Stark quarrel is settled by going and looking.

The lord in Year 715 is **Ulf Stark**, Lord of Hollenmark, Master of the eastern March and Warlord of the house by the right of his father and his father's father: fifty-seven, broad as a doorway, wearing the head and pelt of a great brown wolf and calling everyone under forty *lad*. By most counts he is the worst of the north's Seven Warlords to have against you, and he has spent most of his life proving the count correct.

The house carries the **Stark Rime**, an ice-aspected inheritance that predates the Moto and operates outside the standard Wellspring framework: a cold radiating from body and blood, intensifying under emotional pressure, silvering timber and stiffening snow in a radius around the bearer. An angry Stark's breath stops steaming and his beard goes white, and an experienced tavern-keeper will start moving the glassware at that point. The inheritance is northern, older than the kingdom and older than the Starks' own records, and they do not discuss its origins with outsiders, because the origins are Stark business and the Starks keep their business behind their borders. The fair answer to it is heat, and keeping him calm.

The **Dire Icebear** is an enormous polar ursid whose white fur carries a permanent frost dusting and whose temperament is exactly what the name implies. Nobody has ever domesticated one; the arrangement is a partnership. A Stark rides one because the bear has decided to permit it, and the permission is withdrawn the moment the rider stops being worth carrying. The withdrawal is not gentle, as Sweyn could tell you.

*Later hand:* a cousin of the house, Zaehaerys Stark, carries the title of Warden of the Eastern March a generation on, and copies of this shelf that name him lord in 715 are reading the wrong winter.

## Wealth and Trade

Timber, furs and road. The March sells ironwood and spruce down the eastern rivers, white-bear furs from animals that died of something else (ask no further), and above all safe passage. A Nalūn factor house, the Vessant, has run a supply line into the eastern timber for thirty-one years, every mile of it through Stark country, and for thirty-one years it has paid Hollenmark a tithe for the road and the contract has run sound. Hollenmark is the only town in the north whose smiths reckon in Nalūn seal-tokens as readily as in copper, and the bronze disc with the wolf on one face and the chain-mark on the other is a thing a merchant shows at a gate and a soldier never expects to be handed.

The house imports most of what it eats, by Ashmore's allocation, and has resented every bushel of it since before the pie.

## Rent, Food and the Street

A tenant in Hollenmark pays in days on the wall and split wood, and a house with a son in the riders pays nothing and is reminded of it. Lodging is a long bed over the bakehouse if you know someone. The town eats windmeat, bone broth, smoked fish from the blue lakes and whatever Ashmore sent, with a cup of the skin (fermented milk in a sealskin) at the door. There is a market under the twin towers where snow blows through the stalls year-round and a woman sells hot barley cakes off a warmed stone, and she has a hedge-worker's trick that keeps them hot exactly until you have paid.

Street life runs on the road and on the bears. A bear in the street has right of way over the Warlord. Children play the Asking in the lanes, one of them standing very still in a shirt while the others growl. The hide, in Stark country, is expected to have frost on it, and a practitioner with a warm-colored aura gets stared at. Heralds come through from the Seat and from the holds and are made to stand at the same fire.

**What they joke about.** Everything south of them. Greymanes, who have the same wolf on their breast and no sense of humor about it. Ashmore's arithmetic: every midwinter, for a hundred and twenty years, Hollenmark has sent an empty pie dish to Ashbury. Being put off your bear, which is a joke only the person it happened to may start. A Stark's idea of a warm welcome is telling you exactly where you are standing.

## Law and How It Fights

**Law.** The March keeps its own laws, and they are about stones. Move a boundary stone and you lose the hand, in theory; nobody has been stupid enough in living memory, not counting the bear that scratched its back on one at Stonewatch and was entered in the Roll as a party to the dispute. Disputes go to the nearest stone, with witnesses, and then to the Lord. A debt is not a debt until it has been said aloud at a boundary.

**The fighting.** Great sword and axe in the north, boar-spear on the road, short sword and round limewood shield for the young. The heavy walk is the signature, weight down through the heels, even and unhurried, and it frightens people who have seen where it ends. Stark war is winter war on roads nobody else thinks are open: a Stark column has already moved before you knew it was coming, and the fair answer is a fire, a thaw and a scout who does not sleep. House riders on icebears are few and enormously loud on the page of any chronicle, and most of the killing is done by men on foot in wolf-fur, moving in knots. First Releases in the family are named for frost and called in the old northern tongue, and the first person to tell a Stark they are shouting is generally the bear.

## Monsters on the Roads

**The beasts.** Wild bears are the first danger and the most respected. An icebear with no rider on it has made its own decision about you, and the Dire Icebear that carries a Stark is the same animal on a better day. Elk and boar keep the ironwood, the great brown wolves follow the elk, and the hunting hall at Hollenmark will post a bounty on a wolf and none on a bear, on the grounds that the Roll would have to enter the bear as a party.

**The monsters.** Monsters move, and they live across the whole of the Inner World, and the March gets the ones that come down off high ground or out of the east. Goliaths (T5 on the Bestiary's Danger Tier, which runs from T1 to T9; giantkin who want to be left alone) hold the snow mountain above the city and throw rocks at surveyors. The surveyors go up anyway, and each rock is entered by location. On the eastern line, Stark riders and Marrowkin survey parties both hunt the odd Greater Wolf pack and whatever comes off the Ring's fume-belt with too many opinions.

### Two dungeons

The March talks about two [[The Bestiary/Dungeons|dungeons]] (a dungeon being what a Wellspring Core makes around itself, or what grows up around one). The first is **the Roll-Cellar** under Stonewatch, a Fixatio Well (the Wellspring of anchoring) that opened beneath the oldest stone on the line. Starks cleared it three generations ago, going down with surveying chains and entering each dead thing in the Roll by location. It is a worked Core now, with Stark keepers, and practitioners pay by the hour to sit in amber light that makes a stance very hard to shift.

The second is **the Cold Larder**, a day from Bearhold, a Vohrin ground (Vohrin being a Wellspring of Caloria, the Family of heat, with the heat run backward): blue galleries under the boreal and a cold that drinks your lamp. Renderers hold it on a Stark lease, and the winter trade cures down there what cannot be warmed. Its masks have to be run to the hall in a sack of snow, since a hall pays only while the color holds. A Stark with the Rime up is charged double at the door, because the renderers have seen what happens to their stock when the cold in the room acquires an opinion. More on the [[The Bestiary/Dungeons|Dungeons]] shelf and in the [[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Inner World — A Gazetteer|Gazetteer]].

### The Scourge and the Turn

The [[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Scourge of Hell|Scourge of Hell]] is about as far from anything blue as the continent allows, and the March goes there anyway when the wheel says so. The Turn is the old rotation by which North, South and Center each spend nine years keeping the Scourge's monsters off everybody else. A Northern Turn takes the March's spears in long columns, by the eastern road to Threeways, the southern road to Ashbury and the Kessen ford, already gone before you knew they had left. The riders go with them, and it is the one campaign a Stark dreads. An icebear on the Hem, the Scourge's outer belt, in that hot dry wind sheds its frost, goes off its food and sits down in any water it can find, and the rider goes slowly mad beside it. More Starks have been put off their bears south of the Kessen ford than anywhere on the March, and the bears then walk home, which takes a season and frightens a great deal of barley. A Stark with the Rime up, on the other hand, is the only cold drink for a hundred miles, and the rim taverns will stand him free beer all night to keep him sitting beside the cask, angry.

Hollenmark blue was on the field at Lastpenny in 676, at the handover the whole Center still spits about, drawn up beside the Stormfold slate and watching a wyrm eat the town because the host had been stood down an hour before. The March does not dispute it. "It isn't noon" is a joke in Hollenmark that only a man who stood there may start, and there are fewer of them every winter. The last Northern Turn ended in 703 and the Center has it now, until 721, so the March's share for the moment is a clerk's copy of the Count (the running bill the three parties present to one another), some remarks on what Ashmore charges for bread on the way down, and a list of which boys will be old enough.

# The Copper Coast of Haeseong

**Say it** · HEH-sung

*The Thousand Moorings · The Salt Road's Mouth · In the Dynasty of Sum-gol*

The lamp-wardens walked off the great tower at Haeseong at dusk on the fourth night of the mackerel run in 671, in their sea-green coats, in good order, over a matter of eleven coppers a night. It was the sixth such strike in living memory and they timed it, as always, for when it would hurt. The lamp they tended burned worked light that reached forty miles out, and every boat of the fishing fleet was out past the Sawbacks with a southerly rising and no other mark on that whole black coast to steer for. On the quay the wardens' captain, Gim Dol-soe, stood with his arms folded, explaining the terms to an under-steward who was explaining them to Admiral-Lord Ryu Seok-ju, who was at dinner.

Nobody explained anything to old Deok-sun the net-widow. She took the paper lantern off her own door, lit it, walked to the end of the mole and hung it on a boathook. The woman next to her did the same. Inside the hour every house along the Moorings was stripping its lamps and every child was running with an armful, and they strung them on fishing line from mast to mast and roof to roof clear across the harbor, red and gold and copper-green, thousands of them, and rowed more out on sampans, until the whole bay stood lit up and shaking on the water like a city on fire that was enjoying itself. The fleet saw it from nineteen miles out and came in on the backs of the waves with the men screaming with laughter. Only three boats were lost. The Admiral-Lord paid the wardens the eleven coppers, because the tower is the tower, and fined the town for the use of the fishing line, because the line was harbor property. Deok-sun paid the fine in mackerel. The lanterns have gone up across the water every night of the year from that day to this, and they say that a Haeseong child, set down anywhere in the world, cannot sleep in the dark.

The Copper Coast is the brightest city in the South, the Dynasty's old admiralty, sitting on the Dynasty's money, answering to the Dynasty's name, and asking very politely whether anyone would like to come and collect.

## The Land and the Look

Warm, wet and shining, from the white cliffs of [[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Holy Sea of Alabaster|Alabaster]] eastward. The coast is called the **Thousand Moorings**, a run of inlets, sandbars and pine-topped islets, and the water goes from sea-green to copper at evening with the whole shore smelling of salt, tar, charcoal and frying fish. Old copper workings in the hills behind it stain the creeks green and the roofs are sheathed in the same metal, bright as a kettle when new and going sea-green along the oldest eaves, so that a Moorings town at sunset looks as though someone had tipped a purse out on a hillside.

**Haeseong** itself climbs from a long stone quay at the mouth of the salt road in hundreds of copper-red and gold roofs, tile and upturned eaves stacked up the hill, over a harbor of deep blue water packed with fishing boats, junks, coasting galleys and the long low salt barges, with those lanterns on every line and huge white clouds standing behind it all. Two citadels look down on it. The dark stone fortress on the sheer green crag is the Ryu's, and the white-and-gold palace on the next rise is the old Dynasty's governor's seat, swept and aired and kept for Sum-gol by the people who have stopped sending Sum-gol its money. Behind the city the salt road runs north, a raised causeway at first, between paddies and salt-ponds, then a long straight highway through the low country, past the Rose Coils at the ford of Bothhands, across the Sealed Charter and up the east flank of the [[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Scourge of Hell|Scourge of Hell]] to the salt basin, and so to the junction below Ashbury. There are camellias on the walls and it rains hard for ten minutes every afternoon, and everyone stands in doorways making remarks about it.

## The House

**House Ryu** was the Mahou line's admiralty. When the Mahuo were emperors, and later when they were kings, the Ryu kept their ships, their harbor dues and their salt fleet, and were the second family of Sum-gol. They did not leave at the last Calling: they sent a superb letter. They have answered to the name ever since, and they are by every form of law still its loyal officers.

When the Mahuo fell, the ships, the harbor dues and the salt fleet were all still sitting at Haeseong, with nobody left to send the revenue to. **The Ryu left the Mahuo's wealth exactly where it sat, and simply stopped forwarding it.** The South has never settled whether that is theft. The Ryu would like it noted that the Mahuo have never asked. The counting-rooms keep the whole sum in a separate set of books under the heading *held for the Throne*, correct to the copper and growing, and they will show you the heading.

The present head is **Admiral-Lord Ryu Ha-eun**. She is fifty-three, stout, sunburned and merry, wears sea-green silk with copper at the throat, and has been at sea since she was eleven, and the fleet would follow her onto the Sawbacks. She sat at the Cedar Enclave and heard Naiser Yukari promise to restore the Mahuo royal line at Sum-gol, and smiled, and asked a very good question about harbor dues. House Ryu will be the first to object if anybody restores that line and the last to object out loud. Its words are *The Tide Pays Twice*. Its colors are sea-green and copper.

## Cities and Towns

**Haeseong**, seventy thousand souls and the largest living city in the South, has its fortress on the crag, the governor's palace on the next rise with nobody in it, the Admiral's Hall above the harbor with its copper dragon-ridge, and the Long Mole running out to the great lighthouse. At the mole's root is the **Salt Quay**, where the barges load in a white haze that gets into your teeth, and where the salt road begins, every sack of southern salt that ever crossed the Ashmore marches having paid a Ryu toll on its way. A company of lamp-wardens hired down from the Wellheads at great expense keeps the tower lit. They still walk off every few years over the fee, and are still paid before the fishing fleet can get home.

**Geumpo**, "gold harbor," lies a day east along the coast, a smaller, prettier, more dishonest place, where the foreign and island ships unload away from the toll-clerks' eyes. It got the name for the river-gold once washed down there or for the color of its mud at sunset, depending on who is selling you the house. Lacquer, pearls, dried sea-cucumber and a great many things without papers pass through its warehouses. Its Guild of Pilots has been in a state of cold war with Haeseong's for two hundred years, and it fields the better bracket team.

Along the rest of the Thousand Moorings lie some hundreds of salt-ponds, fishing villages and customs posts, each with a shrine to the drowned and a Ryu toll-man who takes his hat off to funerals and charges for them anyway.

## Wealth and Trade

Everything that comes into the Inner World by water comes in through House Ryu, by their own account, and the account is close to the truth. Harbor dues, salt tolls, the salt fleet, wreck-right on the whole coast. Copper, hammered and sheathed; green copper sheet goes up the salt road for half the roofs in the South. Fish in every form. Sea-silk, pearl, and the deep southern dyes come in from the isles and go out to wherever a lord wants to look richer than his neighbor.

The salt itself comes in part from the Dynasty's pans at Yeomjeon, carried by Ryu barge along the coast and sold north by the Ryu at a Ryu price, which is how one sack can make its way through three lawsuits before it reaches a pot. The money that should have gone to Sum-gol has mostly become ships. Ask to see them. They are magnificent.

---

## Rent, Food and the Street

Rent is high on the rock, ruinous on the Salt Quay and a matter of family on the water, where several thousand people live on sampans and pay mooring-fee by the lunar month to a harbor-mother who knows who is sleeping with whom. Grilled mackerel, rice, cold noodles in broth, salted shrimp with everything, sea-urchin at a stall if you have just been paid, and a clear rice liquor that visitors take for water exactly once.

The evening is the point of Haeseong. When the lanterns go up the entire city comes out on the mole, the sampans turn into floating kitchens, and the sound of the place rises to where you can hear it at the Sawbacks. Fortune-sellers will tell you the tide of your marriage. Hedge-workers sell net-charms, knot-charms, a wind in a bag that has never yet produced a wind and always produces a story about the last customer. Bracket schools wear their harbor's color and row out to fight on barges lashed together in the middle of the bay, with the betting done by boat: the Haeseong Green Tide, the Geumpo Gilders, and a fisher school from the outer Moorings called the Bad Weather, who have no style anyone can name and are very hard to knock off a barge. On the quays the aura coat is gabot and Pressure is nureum, as at Sum-gol, and you sense a stranger by nunchi, which here also means knowing when to change the subject.

## Law and How It Fights

**The Tide Court** sits on the Long Mole in the shade of the tower, and it hears wreck, salvage, toll and mutiny in an afternoon, with an admiral's officer on the bench and a harbor-mother beside him to say who is lying. Goods from a wreck belong by old right to the Ryu, unless a living creature from the ship came ashore, so an unusual number of ship's cats have been very carefully thrown overboard in time. Everything on the road pays. A man may bribe a toll-man, and it is considered polite, but it does not reduce the toll.

**At sea they are the best in the South** and know it: coasting galleys with marine crossbows along the rail, fire-pots, and big war-junks that carry a company of boarders in copper-scaled harness. A Ryu line takes the windward, lets the current do the work and shoots the rowers first. House practitioners stand on the fighting platforms and do what they can with wind and water, and the lighthouse wardens, on the rare day the fee is paid in advance, can throw that worked beam across an enemy's eyes at a mile. Ashore they hold the salt road with little walled toll-forts every thirty miles, and rent spears from [[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Ochre Fields of Hammerford|Hammerford]] for anything larger. They have never in their history fought a land battle they could avoid by raising a price.

## What They Joke About

Money, loudly, and the Mahuo, quietly. "Held for the Throne" is what a Haeseong man says about his wife's jewels, his brother's loan, or whatever he has just put under his coat. A sailor who is late with what he owes says he is waiting for the Mahuo to ask. They say a Yan will make you wait through his grandfather's name to sell you a horse and that a Lambert will count your fingers after shaking your hand, and that the Yukari already know what you had for breakfast and disapprove. Of Geumpo they say that its children are born with a false bottom. Of Haeseong, Geumpo says only that it pays its pilots on the wrong tide.

## Monsters on the Roads

Sea Serpents (T6) follow the mackerel, and every Ryu fleet has lost hulls off the Sawbacks. Sirens (T4) work the outer islets, and everyone on the water has a story and a net-charm. Kraken (T9) are named in old wreck-right and nobody has lately had the nerve to file a claim. Beasts are the daily nuisance and have their own column at the Tide Court: sharks under the sampans in the mackerel weeks, otters robbing the creek traps, wild boar in the paddies, and cormorants that have learned to work for a rival family.

**The dungeons.** The respectable one is **the Green Galleries**, the old copper workings in the hills behind Geumpo, which broke into a Basilithe Well (the Wellspring of pure pitch and clean crystal, ochre-gold). Tolling Crabs (T5) hold the Upper Workings, and a careful delver strikes a tuning bar and rings a crab's own note back at it until the carapace falls apart. Geumpo's pilots hold a lease on the Mouth. The Ryu dispute it annually. The other is **the Gold Mouth**, a sea cave east of Geumpo, an Oblation ground (a warm Wellspring of the Fluxia family, whose law runs on what is given up to it), unlicensed and very busy: a tide cave that pulls. Smugglers use it, and House Ryu claims wreck-right on anything that washes out, crews included. More on the [[The Bestiary/Dungeons|Dungeons]] shelf and in the [[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Inner World — A Gazetteer|Gazetteer]].

**The Scourge and the Turn.** The [[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Scourge of Hell|Scourge of Hell]] is a long way up the road from the Salt Quay and nobody on the Long Mole has ever smelled it, but this is where the eastern corridor starts, and every carter who dies on that flank had a Ryu toll-stamp on his sacks. [[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Scourge of Hell#The Turn|The Turn]], the old wheel by which North, South and Center each spend nine years keeping the Scourge's monsters off the other two, is the one thing that goes up the salt road free. Whoever holds it may march through anyone's land by right of passage, and House Ryu regards nine years of spears passing its toll-forts unbilled as a natural disaster. The toll-men stand at the gates regardless, hats off, writing down what each company would have owed. When the wheel comes round to the South and the old Dynasty's realms are made to pay, the Ryu answer as they answered the Calling, with a superb letter, salt barges at a fixed price and the carters' losses entered to the copper, and the whole unbilled sum goes into the South's Count with the Admiral-Lord's compliments.

In every other season the traffic comes back down the road in the other direction. Wyrm cores and bagged masks off the Hem pass the Salt Quay on their way to any crucible that will pay, a good deal of ghoststeel leaves through Geumpo with no papers at all, and a cutter with a season's bounty and one ear short is a familiar sight on the mole, under the lanterns, eating sea-urchin.

## Neighbors and Grudges

**[[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Vermilion Dynasty of Sum-gol|Sum-gol]].** Their sworn liege, in olive now and refusing the crown, and the accounts grow in their column every month. The letter the Ryu send to each Calling is much admired, and is copied out in a fair hand all along the coast.

**[[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Holy Sea of Alabaster|The Holy Sea]].** Rimward is the only harbor on the southern sea that can hurt them, and the Lamberts' tariff books are the only ones in the South that match theirs. Rivals by water and colleagues by temperament.

**The Golden Hollow of Ashbury.** The salt road ends at Ashmore's junction, with grain coming back the other way. The north eats what Ashmore sells, seasoned with what the Ryu sell, and neither has ever been able to stop the other.

**[[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Jade Plains of Qingyuan|The Jade Plains]].** Fellow servants of the empty chair. They dispute the salt toll annually, and the Hall's elder recites at it.

**[[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Silver Cedars of Yukari|The Silver Cedars]].** Lord Naiser's promise to restore Sum-gol is the one sentence in the South that could cost the Ryu everything, and it is in the Yukari record, with the date, and Ryu Ha-eun's very good question next to it.

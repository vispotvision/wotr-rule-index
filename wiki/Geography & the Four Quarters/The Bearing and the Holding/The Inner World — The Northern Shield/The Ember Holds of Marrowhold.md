# The Ember Holds of Marrowhold

**Say it** · MARE-oh-hold. In its own tongue *Luuthruk*, LOO-throok, the bone-hold.

*The Ring · The Ore Council · The Lender's House · The Seat of the Scale · Eastern Shield*

King Hobart of Pennycrown, whose realm was four villages, a ford and a very high opinion of itself, decided in the spring of 651 IC that a true king ought to sit on a god. He sold his ford-tolls eleven years ahead, rode to the Cutting Fair at Collarbone in a borrowed scarlet cloak, and outbid an abbot of the Holy Sea and two northern factors for one slab of old ivory off the Twelfth Rib, nine feet tall, warm as a new loaf, with a gold grain in it that moved when the light did. Forty Marrowkin hauled it south on ox-sleds with kettledrums and pans of copper-green fire, and half the Midmarch walked out to watch it go by. In Pennycrown's square a hold-clerk named Vappu Tarrekk Ollan, four feet tall, with furnace-colored eyes behind a slit visor of carved horn, read the sale aloud in meter, all of it, for three hours, down to the last line: *returnable on the Lender's demand, carriage at the holder's charge.* Hobart signed with a flourish and bellowed that the Lender was welcome to come and try. The crowd adored him.

The throne leaned. By the first winter a marble set on its seat rolled north. By the fifth year the court had wedged it with lead, by the ninth it was held down with ship's chain, and visiting kings were learning to pour their wine at an angle. On midsummer night of 662, with the hall full of guests and Hobart on it in his crown, the chain let go and the throne went north, through the dais, through the kitchen wall, through the queen's pigeon-house, and across four hundred paces of bean field with the king clinging to the armrests and roaring for his guard, until it met the town wall and stopped there. It broke his pelvis and his reign. He sued at Breastbone. The tribunal sat for two years and found that the throne had done nothing the sale had not said it might, and the Carriers' Guild sent a courteous note offering to take it the rest of the way at the usual rate per league. A sworn Measurewright inspected the furrow and blamed bad footings and a spring under the beans, and that remains the Guild's opinion. Pennycrown built a new stretch of wall around the thing rather than touch it. It is still leaning.

Marrowhold is a nation of smiths and clerks who live inside the ribcage of something that fell out of the Great Spirit War, cut it up, sell it to everybody, and write down every ounce, because someday the body will want it back.

## Overview

A **Marrowkin kingdom built into and through the bones of the Ring**, the ninety-league crater holding the northern quarter's richest sulfide ore, at the eastern end of the northern shield, past the Ironwood March and the Scarlet Wood, extending outward to take in the territories and trade routes that depend on what comes out of it. Older rolls and most of the songs call the people Dawi and the country the Guild Seat of Stannvaard, the Metal-Warden, a Concord filing name built from *stannum*, tin or worked ore. The Guild's clerks have kept using it, the Marrowkin have stopped answering to it, and no state of that name is on any roll now.

Among its holdings is **Altherion**, the multiracial republic whose River Crown Council governs the largest surface settlement on the rim, **and whose constitutional architecture provides the political stage on which Sancta Lux has chosen to seat the Grand Church.** This makes Marrowhold the most politically complex sovereign entity in the Inner World: a forge, a republic and a court of universal jurisdiction, stacked one inside another, with the friction managed by a people who treat every problem as an engineering challenge and refuse to be impressed by anyone else's theology, having a much larger one under their feet.

**Roughly eighty thousand souls**: about 35,000 Marrowkin, about 20,000 human, the remainder elven, beastkin, goblinoid and other, concentrated primarily in Altherion.

> *A man who owns the ore is rich until the road closes. A man who owns the forge is rich until the fuel runs out. A man who owns the court inside the republic inside the forge-kingdom is rich until the mountain disagrees, and the mountain has not disagreed with us yet.*
>
> Guildmaster Thordren, Ore Council address, Year 698 IC

### What died

Nobody can tell you, and you will meet plenty who try. **What fell into the Ring stays disputed.** Every school has a candidate and every candidate has a priesthood prepared to explain, at length and with witnesses, that their god is alive and well, thank you. The Guild's rolls of Archons and Titans show no empty chair that all the rolls agree on. One school holds that the thing was never on any roll, and another that it never had a mind and the word god is a sales term. The Marrowkin enter no opinion. They call it the Body when they are selling and *Lainur*, the Lender, when they are frightened, and a stranger who presses the question will be asked what he proposes to buy.

### Why the Church is here

**Darius Ignatius Sanctus Sanctorum**, who wrote the order's modern apparatus between the ages of twenty-six and thirty-five, understood that a court seated inside a republic seated inside a guild-state **produces a jurisdictional complexity so dense that no single power can evict the court without dismantling the republic, and no power can dismantle the republic without disrupting the guild-state, and no power can disrupt the guild-state without collapsing the metal trade that every army and every forge in the four quarters depends on.**

> ***A court that cannot be evicted is not a court. Darius made sure his court was a court.***

## The Land and What It Looks Like

Come at it from the north road at sunset and the first thing you take for mountains are mountains, and the second thing you notice is that they lean. The rim of the Ring is a wall of sheer peaks all curving inward over the basin at the same angle, each one a single bone the color of old ivory wherever the red has not climbed it, and the red has climbed most of them. Everything that can grow in the Ring is ember red. Moss, bracken, thicket, forty-foot ferns on the bone terraces, all of it the same range of rust and coal and hot copper, running up the ribs in great burning shawls and pouring down into the basin, so that the place looks at every hour of the day like a forest in the last week of autumn, with the leaves refusing to fall. The holds call this growth **emberwort**, and it lives on what seeps out of the bone. Far off across the basin a silver river goes through it like a dropped chain.

**Shocked quartz and an inverted rock sequence at the rim confirm the Ring as an impact scar rather than a volcanic caldera**, so something came down here from a very great height and very fast, whatever else it was. The sheet of melted rock beneath the basin floor is where the ore concentrated as it cooled, **producing sulfide deposits in concentrations that make every other mine in the four quarters look like a surface scraping.** The ore seams follow the faults, which puts the richest deposits along the crater walls rather than the basin floor, and that is why the cities are built into the walls. The metal is in the cliffs and the Marrowkin followed it. A Guild surveyor who walked the rim in 702 counted twenty-nine standing ribs. The holds say twelve to a side, because that is what a person has, and they told him to enter a number that scans.

Through the seams and under the floor runs what the holds call *hehkuveri*, the glow-blood: a slow heavy melt, dull orange, thick as cold tar, that has not finished cooling since the fall. A Measurewright will tell you it is impact melt saturated with Aether. A Marrowkin will tell you it is blood. Neither has moved the other in a very long time, and the furnaces work either way. **Climate inside the Ring is warmer** than the surrounding shield, from that heat and from the thermal mass of the walls, and the snow that lies a yard deep on Altherion's roofs turns to steaming rain halfway down the inner slope. **The air carries the taste of sulfur, hot metal, and the dry-mineral smell of stone that has been heated and cooled thousands of times**, with something under it like a tooth that has just been drilled.

## The Cities

### Breastbone, the capital

The picture on every guild seal is the upper city, and it is as good as they say. **Breastbone** is a walled citadel of slender pale spires and tiered towers rising against the sheer face of the ribs, every one carved out of the Body's own bone and left standing where the fall snapped it upright, each tower narrowing by stacked terraces to a needle. Thin bridges with no visible means of support run from spire to spire. Inside the white wall a great ribbed dome houses the Ore Council, and below the wall lie the round terraced halls, each a wheel of pale stone with an ember garden blazing in its hub, and below those the low town, a spill of pale roofs furred with red moss. Everything that was not carved is red, because the emberwort has climbed every surface the glow-blood can reach as it cools in the bone, and the whole ember-colored country is, by the holds' own account, feeding on the thing they are cutting up. A traveler who has crossed the gray shield for a month generally stops at the last turn of the road, and the road's beggars work that turn for a reason.

All of that is the part you are shown. Under it, going down through the crater wall along the ore seams, are the seven working levels the old rolls call **Thane-Gorr**, joined by shaft-lifts, chain-bridges and tunnels, the lowest reaching the base of the impact melt sheet. **Thane-Gorr's forges burn continuously, set over the glow-blood in furnaces that hold temperatures no fuel achieves and hold them steady for days.** The glow is visible from the rim at night, **a dull orange pulse against the walls** that travelers steer by and **that poets have been comparing to a heartbeat for centuries because the comparison is accurate and nobody has found a better one.** The holds enter it as furnace-light and decline to discuss the timing.

A small Tinker Goblin quarter keeps the hoists and chain-lifts running, and has been petitioning for a guild seat for over two centuries with increasing volume and decreasing patience. In 669 IC they concluded that the Ore Council had not been reading it, and cast it in bronze. Every letter stood as tall as a goblin. The whole text, fifty-three words and four tons, was strung on one chain down the great shaft with pans of copper-green fire burning on every landing, so that seven levels of Marrowkin coming off shift could watch their neighbors' grievance descend past them in order, slowly, to horns and kettledrums, while Mag Cotterpin, master of the works, rode the top letter in her good leather cap and bellowed the name of her invention, THE SELF-ANSWERING PETITION. The brake she had designed held until about the word *respectfully*. Four tons of bronze then went down that shaft faster than any appeal has ever moved in the Ring, through the roof of the Assay Guild's counting room and the floor beneath it. Cotterpin rode it as far as the fourth level, and it cost her both legs below the knee and a Guild balance worth more than her street. Nobody else died, because a Marrowkin hears a chain going wrong through the soles of the feet before it goes. The arbitration tribunal sat for two years, found that a petition delivered through the ceiling counts as received, denied it on the merits, and billed the Tinker quarter for the ceiling, which the quarter is still paying off, one letter at a time.

### Collarbone

The northern hold and the front door. **Collarbone** runs along one enormous horizontal bone at the head of the rim road, a mile and more of galleries with windows cut through to the open air on both sides, so that its long market gets the wind from the shield in one ear and the heat of the Ring in the other. This is where the Thornhaven corridor comes in and where the outside world is permitted to buy. The **Cutting Fair**, held for nine days after each year's schedule is read, fills the bone end to end with relic-hunters, sect quartermasters, abbots, smugglers, armorers from four quarters, and the bookies who will give you odds on what the top lot fetches and whether its buyer gets it home. Every piece sold is read aloud by a clerk before the money moves, so the whole place sounds like a very large liturgy being interrupted by a very large fight.

### The Twelfth Rib

*Ykt-kaht Kylki* on the hold's rolls, written as two words because the numeral will not harmonize. The longest bone anyone has cut into, hollow for most of its run, lying on the eastern wall. The Twelfth is the oldest cutting face still working and the source of the best ivory, and it holds **Kegshard Hold**, seat of the Cask Line, with its grand hall, its Oath-Breakers' wall, its red and copper banners and the Deepvault beneath. The galleries run so long that a bowl of soup carried from the kitchens to the far shift arrives cold, and the far shift has a song about it.

### The other holds of the Upper Ring

**Kolmthruk**, the Third Hold, cut into the Third Rib on the west, is a town of furnace-tenders who consider Breastbone soft. **Barkstone** on the northern wall takes its name from the scaled, weathered outer layer of its rib, and gives the tongue its standard dialect and its most insufferable metricians. All four cities of the Upper Ring are **workings first and settlements second**, with civic life organized around the guild shifts that decide when people work, eat and sleep.

### Altherion, the republic on the rim

**Altherion** sits on the northern rim where the three principal roads meet, outside the bone and very pleased about it. It was not planned. Merchants needed a place to sleep, the place needed a market, the market needed law, and by the time anyone decided to write a charter the city was already there. What makes it different is that **the charter it eventually received was drafted by every race that lived there, at the same table**, and the table, **round, without a head, one chair per delegation and no chair larger than another**, is still in the River Crown Council's chamber and is still used. Marrowkin built the shafts, and everyone else built Altherion, timber-framed over stone with rooflines pitched against snow, in the visual chaos of seven racial building traditions practiced in the same streets across three centuries. The Accord's northern chancery and its Arbitration Division sit up on the high ground called the Seat, where it always seems to be raining.

## Three Histories That Became One

**The Ore** · By the Tally the Marrowkin have been inside the Body since the first entry, and the Guild's Errata would cut the Tally's count down hard. What is on firmer ground is the climb. A High Line prospecting expedition came up out of the deep halls, assessed the crater, and sent word back down that **the richest mineral deposit in the northern quarter was sitting in the open on the surface, unguarded, unmined, and waiting for someone with a forge hot enough to work it.** The halls sent smiths. The smiths brought families. The families became guilds. **The guilds became a kingdom**, by the accumulated weight of people who never meant to found one and stayed because the work was there, and who discovered over generations that a community organized around a forge has already solved most of the problems governments are designed to solve.

**The Republic** · The holds offered to govern the incoming population under guild law. **The newcomers declined, on the reasonable ground that guild law was designed for guilds and that most of them were not guild members.** The compromise was the founding charter: a multiracial republic, the **Essence Resource Sharing Act** preventing any body from monopolizing Wellspring access, and the **Trade Equality Act** preventing any race or guild from monopolizing extraction or trade. The charter was, in essence, a promise that Altherion's governance would measure every community the same way, which is why Sancta Lux eventually chose it as a home. The **Echo Elves** maintain that Altherion's confederated structure owes more to Echo Elf political philosophy than the republic's official histories credit. Altherion's official historians maintain the resemblance is coincidental. The Echo Elves repeat their claim verbatim, once, every time it is made, which is their way of recording that it has been said without conceding that it is true.

**The Court** · The Grand Church is a consecrated stone compound within Altherion's walls, **built on rock that Sancta Lux's Measurewrights consecrated themselves, using methods they declined to share with the republic's architects and that the republic's architects declined to ask about.** It includes the Sanctified Hall where Arbiter-grade readings are taken, the training facilities, the offices coordinating four-quarter operations, **and the chambers in which the Arbiter-Militant and Arbiter-Lector wings are institutionally required to disagree, and do, loudly, in a room built for the purpose.** The Hall of Equal Breath, where the Oracle is housed, is elsewhere. Sel-Ur is at Meridian Hold, whose location does not appear in any Accord filing.

## The Wellsprings

| Wellspring | Function |
|---|---|
| **Petralon** *Stone / Reinforcement* | The Ring's primary territorial Wellspring, the Aether of stone pooling in the crater walls and the bone alike. **Tunnels carved through fault-stressed rock that should collapse and do not, because the stone has been reinforced by centuries of sustained exposure until the rock is stronger than the load above it.** *Petralon-attuned Marrowkin fighters are the hardest opponents in the Inner World to move, because their connection to the ground is literal. Breaking it requires either lifting them off the ground or destroying the ground beneath them* |
| **Judicium** *Judgment / Priority* | The Sancta Lux installation's primary Wellspring. The Measurewrights calibrated the compound's stone to Judicium and Fixatio simultaneously, **producing a substrate that responds to the Scale's readings with the same fidelity that Nalūn's salt responds to Oblation.** *A reading in the Sanctified Hall carries more weight than a field reading. The Hall amplifies nothing: the substrate holds the reading steady while the Lectors work, the way a good bench holds a workpiece steady while the craftsman cuts* |
| **Fixatio** *Anchoring / Stability* | Present in both the geology and the installation. **The same substrate stability that holds an inscription in a tunnel wall also holds a finding in a consecrated hall**, *and the fact that both rest on the same Wellspring is either a coincidence or a theological statement, depending on whether you ask a Marrowkin or a Lector* |
| **Ignivale** *Flame / Collapse* | The collapse from finding into execution. **The fire that falls**, the moment a measured finding becomes a physical consequence. **The forges of the holds do not use Ignivale.** *The distinction between forge-heat and judgment-fire is one the kingdom maintains with the deliberateness of people who understand that a fire that melts metal and a fire that executes people should never be confused, because confusing them produces a civilization that cannot tell the difference between manufacture and murder* |

## Who Rules: Three Institutions

**The Ore Council** · Twelve guild masters (Smelter, Forge, Tunnel, Assay, Carriers, Inscription, Stone, Fuel, Merchant, Archive, Healer, Ward), chaired by a Council Chair elected for a ten-year term whose authority is administrative. The present Chair is Mirja Hiilekk Solisan, called Sixty Tuns, a former Tunnel master with arms like bridge-timbers, who once stopped a Council session to correct the scansion of a death threat. The Council governs everything that touches the ore and everything cut from the Body. **The extraction schedule is the kingdom's true sovereign instrument, and everything else is administration.** It sets how much comes off which rib, how fast the world's metal moves, and who waits. Its reading each year opens the Cutting Fair.

**The River Crown Council** · Representatives elected by every racial community, sitting in a circular chamber whose design reflects the founding principle: **no delegation is larger than another, no seat is elevated above any other, and the speaking floor belongs to whoever holds it.** The Trade Equality Act's provisions occasionally conflict with the extraction schedule's requirements, and the conflicts are resolved through negotiations that take longer than the Marrowkin consider reasonable and shorter than the humans consider sufficient.

**The Grand Church** · Does not vote on the Ore Council and does not sit on the River Crown. It maintains its own compound, staff, training, consecrated substrate and chain of command, and its relationship with the kingdom it sits inside is defined by one principle: **the Church measures.** It measures the Ore Council's trade practices, Altherion's civic governance and the kingdom's military operations **with the same instrument and the same standard it applies to every other realm in the four quarters.** The government can request a measurement and cannot decline one. It cannot evict the body that performs them either, because that would require the cooperation of every kingdom that depends on the metal, and those are the same kingdoms that depend on the court.

> **The three coexist because the cost of separating them exceeds the cost of managing their contradictions.** The Ore Council controls the economy. The River Crown controls the politics. The Grand Church controls the measurement both are judged by. The resulting governance has neither elegance nor efficiency. It is stable the way a three-legged stool is stable: remove any leg and the structure falls, and with all three in place it holds more weight than its construction would suggest.

## Wealth and What It Trades

**Marrowhold's economy is the northern shield's economy.** Every Inner World realm's economy connects to the Ring's output, **and the Ore Council sits at the center of that web with the patience of a people who know the web cannot function without them.** Smelting happens at the Ring because that is where the sulfide is, and the smelters run on forge-arrays (standing runed workings) that pull directly from the ambient saturation, so a Ring furnace costs a fraction of what a western one does. In the west you pay a Core keeper for your hours, and here it is simply in the air.

Two kinds of goods come out of the Ring, and the difference matters.

**Metal** is the bulk of it and the part that moves armies: above all the alloys made with **Blackcap Sulfide**, the massive nickel and copper ore of the melt sheet, which **keeps its temper inside a forge-array's working field** where ordinary steel goes soft and stupid. Every crucible tong, array clamp and furnace door hinge worth owning is Blackcap alloy. Every bar carries the assay stamp, and the stamp counts for more than the bar.

**The Body** is the part that made them famous, sold by the piece, by hand, each piece cut by a named person and entered under that name:

| Piece | What it is | What it is bought for |
|---|---|---|
| **Old ivory** | Rib bone, warm to the touch, gold-grained under moving light | Sword hilts, ward-frames, reliquaries, thrones of petty kings. A rune cut in it does not drift |
| **Sinew** | Cord drawn from the great pale sheets that line the older galleries | Crossbow strings and bow-cord. A bolt costs a few coppers, and a Marrowhold string under it is why a sect elder should watch the windows |
| **Marrow-tallow** | Fat rendered from the core of the bone, dull gold, slow-burning | Lamps that burn orange and last a season, and a base for Drafts. It smokes, and every Marrowkin thinks an honest lamp should |
| **Bloodslag** | Black-red glass skimmed off a glow-blood furnace | Alchemists, ward-cutters, anyone wanting heat that stays where it is put |
| **Ember dye** | Boiled emberwort | The hot red that no northern dyer can match and every northern tailor lies about |
| **Relics** | A joint-cap, a splinter that hums, a hand-sized thing nobody can name | Sects and kings, and people have killed for less. No tooth has ever been sold, and two hundred are on offer in the Midmarch at any time |

**The Return Clause** closes every sale of the Body: *returnable on the Lender's demand, carriage at the holder's charge.* Nobody reads it, and it is read aloud anyway. That means every old-ivory hilt in the four quarters is out on loan, every buyer's name is in the Tally beside it, and the clerks at Breastbone can tell you where most of the Twelfth Rib currently is. Sold pieces are reputed to lean, a hair, toward the Ring, and sailors hang splinters on threads to prove it. Guild assayers have measured the lean on some pieces and found nothing on others, and a Measurewright will explain it by the cut of the grain until you leave. The holds have one comment, which is that they keep the record for when it is asked for.

**The Accord relationship is the deepest of any Inner World state.** Enforcement maintains a permanent legation at Altherion. Research sources experimental metalwork from the Inscription Guild. Strategic's weapons procurement runs through the Forge Guild. **The Skyglass Archive sources its substrate materials from the Deep Reach.** Marrowhold is the Accord's armory, and the Ore Council decides how quickly the armory restocks.

Altherion runs on trade, services, and the revenue of hosting the Grand Church: pilgrim traffic, arbitration fees, legation rent and diplomatic delegations, **making it the most commercially active surface settlement on the northern shield despite producing nothing itself.** The merchants have a saying: Altherion makes nothing and sells everything, accurate in the way all self-deprecating merchant humor is accurate, describing a real condition and pretending to be embarrassed by it.

### The dead mines of Anguz

Out past the northern roads lie the workings of **Anguz**, which were once a real power, with the guilds setting the price of Aethersteel and Bloodsilver on the coil and gold running second to both. The mines collapsed and took the power with them. Nobody holds Anguz now except whoever is down there this week, and what comes out is **Dustquartz**, from shattered veins, sold by the sack for cheap decorative charms and treated with less caution than it warrants, since in bulk its shards resonate to discordant Aether under compression. Marrowkin use the place as a proverb. A mine that owes nobody, they say, ends up owing everyone.

## Rent, Food and the Street

**Rent in the holds is reckoned by warmth.** A gallery near a furnace shoulder costs about twice what the same room costs on the cold side of the bone, the rent goes in the Tally like everything else, and people will tell you their rent before they tell you their trade, with the same pride. Rooms in Altherion are let by the quarter, and rent on the river side has been a live question before the River Crown for three hundred years.

**Food is bought in, and it shows.** The Rimlands feed the crater and never quite enough, so bread is dear, grain prices make every other kingdom wince, and the stores that matter are salted, dried, cellared and packed in bulk. Underground the meal is the **crib**, a pastry case of meat and root carried down in a tin and eaten at the shift's break, with a mark pressed into the corner of the crust so that a man gets his own back up. Sour barley beer in the lower levels, which every visitor calls an acquired taste and every Marrowkin calls beer. Cheap and everywhere are **redcaps**, the fat emberwort fungus off the bone terraces, fried in tallow, tasting strongly of iron. Marrow itself is never sold to be eaten. A child gets one smear of it on bread at the First Entry, and it goes in the Tally as that child's opening debt.

**Come in a market week and bring ear-stoppers.** The low town under Breastbone's wall is pale houses, red moss on every roof, copper-green fire in pans at the corners, and shift horns going over the top of everything. A rib-sounder will press her jaw to your party wall and tell you, for a copper, whether your neighbor is really out. Hoist-goblins in too many pockets are arguing with a Tunnel journeyman over the last chain-lift. On the rim it is louder: seven peoples' building traditions lean over the same Altherion streets in seven colors of paint, a Bugbear porter and an elf factor are arguing the price of one mule in two languages, cookshops sell cribs to pilgrims who have walked a season to see the Church, and the fighting clubs of the rim wards march to the River Stairs brackets behind their own drums and their own insulting songs. Down in the bone the shift clubs do the same, with the First Shift Reds and the Third Shift Coals holding a grudge so old it has its own column. Lamp-wardens, ward-cutters and a thousand petty trades work these streets on their own account, and they strike when they please. One old ratcatcher will clear your cellar for a copper and leave you the receipt carved on the rat.

**The Weighing Festival** · Sancta Lux's festival, held in Altherion's streets. The senior Measurewright performs a public reading of the Grand Church's operational integrity, and **the annual reading of the false-positive rate is treated with the ceremonial gravity other kingdoms reserve for coronations.** Altherion attends in numbers that surprise the order every year, because it is the one time most people see the Scale's instrument in operation, and most attendees describe the experience as the most intellectually honest thing they have ever witnessed and also the most frightening.

**The Assay** · More than a test: **the Marrowkin way of saying that a thing that has been measured and found correct carries a truth no salesmanship can replicate and no counterfeiting can fake.** The measurement is the thing, and the thing without the measurement is just rock.

**Language** · The Tally-Tongue in the holds. Concord Common in Altherion. **Lattice Tongue** for High Line sacred and technical inscription, *where the grammar of the utterance mirrors the grammar of the universe, and outside listeners describe the experience as hearing a proof being recited rather than a sentence being spoken.* See [[Races & Peoples/The Tongues of the Realms/The Grammars/The Tally-Tongue of the Marrowkin]].

## Law

Three parallel systems **whose jurisdictions are defined by function rather than geography.**

**Guild Law** · Practical, specific, enforced by arbitration tribunals **whose appeals process is slow, thorough, and famously humorless.**

- **The Drainage Obligation**, borrowed from Nalūn's model: structural integrity sufficient to prevent collapse into adjacent workings.
- **The Tender's Obligation**: lifetime support for array-tenders who develop **the tender's shake**, the fine persistent palsy that arrives at about eight years of forge-array service **and does not leave.** You will see it in a cup that will not sit still. The guild that shook a tender keeps him for life and pays for his funeral, and a hold that defaults on this has never been found, because the clerks would have to enter it.
- **The Assay Standard**: counterfeiting a stamp is a capital offense. The last person who attempted it was found, tried and sentenced within three days, which is the fastest judicial process in the kingdom's history.
- **The Cutting Right**: nothing comes off the Body without an entry naming who cut it and how much. An unentered cut is theft from the Lender, tried like murder, and the bone-runners who carry such pieces out over the rim at night are the most romantic criminals in the north and have an average career of about eighteen months.
- **The Reckoning**: a life is settled by nothing but a life, a theft by nothing but its own return. Toll-right is honored by every branch of the people.

**Republican Law** · Applies to every person within Altherion's territory **regardless of guild affiliation, racial origin or institutional membership.**

**Sancta Lux Jurisdiction** · Operating on the principle that **the Scale measures everywhere and that the measurement does not require the measured party's consent.** The Ember Magistrates hold flame-lit equilibrium courts in towns that will never see the Grand Church, **and their jurisdiction is one of the few legal authorities neither Council can override.**

The three intersect constantly. The complexity is managed by a class of professional arbitrators trained in all three, known informally as **the Three-Tongued**, who make their living translating between legal traditions and who are, by universal agreement, the most exhausted professionals in the Inner World.

## How It Fights

**The Forge Guard** · Roughly four thousand, **built from the same material as the Ring's walls: dense, patient, and extremely difficult to move.** They fight in harness of Blackcap alloy under red-enameled rib-ivory shields, with hammers and short heavy spears, a line of squat figures with hot eyes behind slit visors who plant their feet and let you arrive. Every tunnel in the Ring is defensible and every road narrows where a formation benefits them. Crossbowmen on sinew strings hold the galleries above. A Forge Guardsman's signature move is called **Worked Ground**: he sets both feet, the floor for three paces round him answers to Petralon, and the man who charges him finds he has charged the mountain.

A Marrowkin with his aura coat up is *on shift*. He calls Pressure *the load*, and he calls sensing a man *sounding* him, the way a rock face is sounded for a bad seam. A human from Altherion who tries the same words will be corrected, in detail, for as long as he stands there.

**Sancta Lux** maintains its own capability within the compound: the Arbiter-Militant wing, the Inquisitors with their gauntlet-etched Judica rites, and **the Mask, of which there is only ever one, whose mere presence in a room suppresses invocation.** It answers to the Will of Judger, **currently held by Darius, who refuses to be invested in it and has been pending investiture for eleven years.**

The strategic defense is economic. **A war against Marrowhold is a war against your own account-books, your own armory, and the judicial body that every other kingdom's legal system references when its own authority is insufficient.** Nobody has been willing to pay all three costs at once. An invader also has the small additional problem of a Tally that will record his name, what he took, and who is to be asked for it back.

## What They Joke About

Marrowkin are flat about everything except the current seam, which they will boast about at the top of their lungs in a meter you can feel through the floor. Their humor is mostly accounting.

- The oldest joke in the holds holds that the god died of being invoiced.
- A Marrowkin who has cut very little is teased by cut-name for life. *Thimble* is a real name on a real clerk, and she has outlived three people who laughed.
- Altherion is "the lid." Humans are "the weather." The Church is "the people who check our sums," said very quietly, in the shaft, with a look at the ceiling.
- They tell a whole cycle about King Hobart's throne, and they tell it to buyers, at the signing, with a straight face.
- Everyone else says that you cannot insult a Marrowkin because he will only write it down, and that you cannot lie to one because he will read it back, and that if you want to know how a Marrowkin feels about you, check whether the entry scans. Kharven soldiers will tell you the Marrowkin sold the god its own coffin and are still waiting on the balance. Nalūn says the Marrowkin have a heart and it is on the schedule.

## Monsters on the Roads

Everything red is slightly wrong to eat if you are not used to it, including the deer. The Ring's ordinary beasts are those deer, red-stained goats on the bone terraces, and pale bats that roost in the galleries and come out at dusk against the furnace glow like smoke going the wrong way. Out in the Rimlands the timber holds the usual northern company of wolves and bears, and they are no respecters of a Carriers' Guild escort. Bone-runners on the rim at night are the most romantic criminals in the north, and rib-lice, pale cat-sized things living on the older galleries, are the least, and every shift keeps a terrier and a low opinion of both.

Basilisks (T5 on the Danger Tier, the hunting halls' scale from T1 to T9) hunt the fume-belt, the six leagues downwind of the smelters where the smoke has killed everything else and nothing will trouble them. Along the boundary stones west of the rim, Marrowkin survey parties and Stark riders both hunt the odd Greater Wolf pack and whatever comes off the fume-belt with too many opinions, and each side enters the kill on its own side of the stone. Monsters move, here as on every road in the Inner World, and a bill for one goes up on the door at Collarbone beside the Fair's lot list, in meter, with the reward in the last line where you have to hear the rest to get to it.

### Dungeons

Petralon (the Aether of stone) pools in the walls, a good part of the Ring is worked Core, and the holds will sit you beside one for an hour at a price. Marrowkin do not use the word dungeon. They say *a cavity*, which is a butcher's question: what was in it, and who has already had the good cuts. Three get talked about, under whatever name, and the full account of what such a place is sits on the [[The Bestiary/Dungeons|Dungeons]] shelf.

**The Deep Reach** below the basin floor is a Steward's territory, with the Deepkin in an advisory role that the upper holds find unnerving, and nobody who knows what lies at the bottom has shared it in full.

**The Cold Marrow**, under the Twelfth Rib, is a gallery through the god's bone that the Marrowkin stopped working one winter, in a great hurry, leaving their tools. The Ore Council closed it. In a country that will read you the entry for a splinter, nobody will say what kind of ground it is, the halls at Altherion have stopped asking to post it, and no Marrowkin goes down there. The far shift's song about the cold soup has one more verse, on why the soup goes cold, and they stop before they reach it.

**Anguz**, out past the northern roads, is dead mines, once a real power, now held by whoever is down there this week: a Wild ground of stone and ore, Dustquartz by the sack, collapses, shards that resonate to the wrong Aether under pressure. Marrowkin use it as a proverb, that a mine that owes nobody ends up owing everyone.

### The Scourge and the Turn

[[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Scourge of Hell|The Scourge of Hell]] lies well to the south, with the whole salt basin between, so no Marrowkin has ever had a stilt walk through his barley, and the Ring has done extremely well out of the place. The Turn is the old rota by which North, South and Center each keep the Scourge's monsters off everybody else for nine years, and the Ore Council's position is that Marrowhold is nobody's holding and so no party to it. On the North's Turn, Kharven and the Banner Houses march in Ring metal and enter it in their Count as a northern neighbor's contribution. The Ore Council enters the same metal as a sale, and the Accord's auditors at Sealhall have looked at both columns and taken their fee. The South's Turn and the Center's buy from the same schedule at the same price, and the present Turn, the Center's until 721, pays by Charter instrument, which the Assay Guild accepts and reads very slowly.

So the Forge Guard has never stood on the Line, the chain of forts around the Scourge's edge, and most of the spearheads that do carry an assay stamp. Heavy loads go down the Iron-to-Salt Corridor to the basin and then by the salt road along the Scourge's east flank, where a dry year's haze-kites (T4, membrane and hunger, riding the outward wind) take the Carriers' mules on the last stretch, and the Guild bills for the mule. What comes back up that road is the other half of the trade. Wyrm cores, the glassy hardened hearts of very large creatures, sell to any crucible at Breastbone that can pay, and the relic hunters bring ghoststeel out of the Shambles to the Cutting Fair, where the Inscription Guild buys it to find out, again, why it cannot be made. A young Marrowkin who has cut a wyrm core out on the Thresh may not take it as a cut-name, because the Tally has only the one Body, and several have asked. Shown the sword itself, on one of the nine clear days, the holds' surveyors look at the Ring's ribs, look at the blade, and suggest it is a butcher's pin marking a cut.

## Neighbors and Grudges

**[[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Amber Cities of Nalūn|The Amber Cities of Nalūn]]** · The defining trade partnership of the Inner World, now that the cities have no crown to argue with. **Nalūn's credit finances extraction. The Ring's metal backs Nalūn's credit**, and supply contracts are sealed at Ironlink Hold, never here. It is structural the way a bridge and its piers are structural: remove either and the span falls, and nobody describes a bridge as being friends with its foundation.

**[[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Kingdom of Kharven — The Ashen Crown|The Kingdom of Kharven]]** · Imports metalwork and supplies military protection for the northern corridors, and is the most critical economic relationship the holds have and the most complicated political one. The Ore Council has long treated Kharven as a useful armed frontier, and when it wants to say so it cuts grain and keeps sending inscription substrate. **Moto Fractura-attuned practitioners provide fortification assessments the holds' engineers find useful and irritating in equal measure**, because they can see stress in stone the holds' own instruments do not detect, and being shown a flaw in your own masonry by a human warrior with a blood-inherited eye is the kind of experience Marrowkin process by building a better instrument rather than by expressing gratitude. Kharven-Seat's crown ironwork, set with garnet, came from a Ring smith whose descendants still hold the commission.

*Added in a later hand: when the banners of the north came together as the Northern Empire of Whitemere, the Ore Council entered the new name at the head of the same column and kept the same grain prices. Marrowhold is nobody's holding. The empire is its largest buyer, and both of them know what that makes it.*

**[[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Scarlet Wood of Thornhaven|The Scarlet Wood of Thornhaven]]** · House Thornwall's corridor runs to Collarbone. Grain and worked metal go west along it, ironwood comes east for tool handles and ward-frames, and **the Thornwalls know the difference between a shortage and a message**, having tracked the Ore Council's supply decisions for generations. Their reading is that the holds are comfortable with an armed buffer and uncomfortable with an independent one. They have been saying so, correctly and unheeded, for a long time.

**[[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Sealed Charter of the Concord|The Guild Accord]]** · An essential supplier, an intractable negotiating partner, and the host of the most powerful judicial body the Accord has to answer to. All four divisions submit to the same Sancta Lux jurisdiction every other visitor submits to, producing the friction of a supra-national body being measured by one of its own chartered members inside a territory it nominally oversees. There is also the matter of the great curved bones around the arena at Sealhall. The Accord bought those somewhere and has never produced the receipt. Hold-clerks have gone to look, more than once, with a measuring cord and their mouths shut, and the Tally says only that the bones are not in it.

**[[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Holy Sea of Alabaster|The Holy Sea of Alabaster]]** · **The Holy Sea measures testimony, Sancta Lux measures everything else, and both hold that a measurement that does not drift is worth more than a judgment that does.** Warm, distant, mediated through the chapter hold that reports back to Altherion. Abbots keep turning up at the Cutting Fair and keep describing it as a pastoral visit.

**[[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Silver Cedars of Yukari|The Silver Cedars of Yukari]]** · Commercial, functional, defined by mutual utility, a relation the Crow's Reach had with the holds and the Yukari land carried on. Its wards protect corridors both sides depend on.

**[[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Iron Mandate of Undaar-Keth — The Closed Fist|The Iron Mandate of Undaar-Keth]]** · A cold peace at a distance, and an unspoken embarrassment. Philologists have laid out the cognates between the Tally-Tongue and Old Vaross, down to the low numerals and the word for hand. Both peoples have read the table, and neither has commented.

**[[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Hundred Crowns of the Midmarch|The Hundred Crowns of the Midmarch]]** · Very good customers. Roughly one in five of its kings owns something that leans.

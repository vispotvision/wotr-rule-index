# The Inner World — A Gazetteer

*Every realm of the one continent, from the Polar Reach to the Holy Sea: its weather, its ground, its monsters, its people, its towns and its roads*

> *A traveler wants four things from a page: what will fall on me, what will bite me, who will cheat me, and where I can get dinner. This page answers in that order, and it has been wrong about all four in living memory.*
>
> Note on the flyleaf of the Guild's circuit copy, in an archivist's hand

In the bright week of Year 691, at the Whalebone Gather, a clerk of the Holy Sea made an offer to the fires. The old Palatine, the one before this one, had read that polar ice tastes of nothing at all and wished to be certain, so he would pay two hundred silver for one block of it on his table at Alabaster at midsummer. Everyone laughed except Ikka Sennet, a sled-woman nineteen years old and, by the name she carried, ninety. She cut a block off the shelf the size of a lodge door, wrapped it in reindeer hide and whale fat, whistled up thirty dogs and two cousins, and went south past the last tree with the green aurora on her back. At Thornhaven the raftsmen packed it in red sawdust and priced the melt aloud at every bend. On the Hallen a reeve chained it across the bow and billed it as grain, since the wall had no rate for ice. Ashbury gave it barley straw and a receipt. The weigh-house at Halcrest weighed it twice, a day apart, with the whole terrace betting on the second figure. In the Midmarch the tolls came every mile until she reached Pennycrown, where two kings and a bribed Measurewright attended while she crowned the thing for a penny, and King Block the First crossed the rest of the country as a reigning monarch, untaxed, with a hired trumpeter walking ahead of him.

At Rootgate an orc seven feet tall hung a basket of pears on the sled and asked her to go round. She went round, eleven days along the flank of the Scourge of Hell, and the dry wind out of the haze took a third of His Majesty in one afternoon. On the eighth evening a wyrm with scales the size of shields came up through the black roots to look at the only cold thing in a hundred miles, and it took one cousin and nine dogs, and Ikka drove on with her jaw set and the blood freezing on the block. The olive riders of Sum-gol kept her company past the iron hills, and the Yan lent two tall grays and an elder who recited his titles at the ice all the way across the grass. On midsummer morning she climbed the eleven hundred steps of the Stair of Testimony in her hides, with half of Alabaster roaring on the landings and the sardine smoke going up, holding what was left in her two bare hands. It was the size of a goose egg. The Palatine, nearly fainting with delight, put it in his mouth and pronounced that it tasted of nothing. A Lambert clerk noted that the contract said one block and named no size, paid in full, and fined her for water on the Stair. The Keld sing it still, with the fine as the last verse.

That block went through every weather this continent owns. So will you, and you may as well know what is coming.

## The Shape of the Land

The Inner World is one continent, long from north to south and pinched in the middle by the Scourge of Hell, and this page covers all of it and nothing beyond. A reader standing anywhere on it in Year 715, the twenty-fifth of the Dark Ages, with the forty-year war only just stopped, should be able to find his feet from what follows.

**The North** is a shield of old cold rock, planed flat by ice and dimpled with chain-lakes. At the very top lie the tundra and permafrost of the Polar Reach, past the last tree. Below that Kharven holds the high plateau at the center. West, the Slate Ridge closes the border, with Undaar-Keth beyond it in black basalt and boreal forest. East, the Ironwood March and the Scarlet Wood run out to the Ring, where the rib mountains of Marrowhold stand. South, the land drops into the Greenhollow, and the Painted Rivers carry everything between.

**The Center** is the Scourge of Hell and whatever can live on its edges. Nobody marches through the middle, so all traffic between North and South goes around by two corridors, the **Kessen road** on the west flank and the **salt road** on the east, and those corridors are where the armies go and where the Center's people live. The Amber Cities of Nalūn lie on the north side, in a dead sea's salt basin, with Halcrest at the gate where the beds thin against the northern rim. The Hundred Crowns crowd along both roads, with Kingsbridge on the Kessen road. The Sealed Charter sits at the east road's crossing. The Moonlight Forest lies on the west flank around its great lake, the Violet Peaks climb on the northwest, the Rose Coils lie on the old ruins of the southeast edge, and the Russet Crown holds the east around an inland sea.

**The South** turns green and drops in long steps to the water. The Long Fields come first, just below the Center. The iron hills and salt pans of Sum-gol lie west at the Kessen road's end, the Yukari valleys climb into cloud on the east, the Jade Plains open in the middle, and the spring country of the Wellheads steams in the uplands between them. At the bottom stands the white shelf of the Holy Sea, with the Copper Coast warm to its east, where the salt road starts.

**Offshore**, the Crimson Isles lie south, past the sea.

### What the sky does, north to south

The weather on this continent runs in three broad belts, and a Measurewright can explain every one of them with a globe and an orange.

- **The cold shield.** Dry, bitter air off the polar ice sits on the North for half the year. The ground under the Reach stays frozen all the way down, summer scarcely thaws the top hand of it, and water cannot drain, so the summer moss stands in bog. Thirty below is an ordinary night on the plateau. Storms come in off the western shield and break on the Slate Ridge, which is why the Ridge gets the lightning and Kharven gets the clear hard cold behind it. The aurora goes over most clear nights.
- **The continental interior.** The Center is a long way from any sea, so it swings hard: hot, bright summers with towering thunderheads, cold winters with snow in the north of it and iron frost everywhere. The Scourge makes the swing worse. Its bare ground heats fast and sheds a dry wind in every direction, its haze hangs copper and rose on the horizon, and rain that comes near it tends to fall on the mountains and forests around its rim, so the Moonlight Forest drowns while a day's ride inside the rim nothing has seen a cloud burst in a decade.
- **The warm south.** Below the Center the land falls toward a warm sea, and the sea sets the terms: mild wet winters and long summers, lemons and rice and camellias, with fog on the white shelf, a hard ten-minute rain every afternoon on the Copper Coast, and big autumn gales that come off the water and take roofs. High ground stays cold. Cloud sits on the Yukari valleys all year, and above the shelf the frost hardly breaks.

### The two roads and the rivers

The **Kessen road** comes up out of the low country to the Foot, at the door of the north. Going south from the Greenhollow, it takes the Kessen ford and runs through the Midmarch, over the great bridge at Kingsbridge, down the Scourge's west flank past the Moonlight Forest, and ends at Sum-gol, and the warded track carries on across the South as its last stretch in all but name, to the foot of the Yukari valleys. The **salt road** starts at the Salt Quay in Haeseong, runs north as a raised causeway through the paddies and then as a long straight highway through the low country, skirts the Rose Coils at the ford of Bothhands, runs up the Scourge's east flank across the Sealed Charter, climbs through the salt basin and finishes at the junction below Ashbury, where it meets the Kessen and the Marrow.

The rivers of the North gather on that same junction. The **Marrow** comes down past Kharven-Seat, the **Hallen**, the **Ruddle** and the **Sleeve** meet at Hallenfeld, and below Ashbury their water comes out as the broad brown **Aldwater**, the river of the Midmarch, which feeds the Moonlight Forest's mire through a hundred channels before the rest of it runs on west, off this map.

### Monsters, beasts and Cores

Three things want to eat you, and the hunting halls keep them apart. **Beasts** are animals with an ecology, some of them very large, and they are everywhere: wolves and bears and worse, on every road on the continent. **Monsters** are the things beyond that, and they move, following prey, Aether and the marching of armies, so a wyvern bred on the Slate Ridge may turn up over Ashbury's barley in a dry year. A Well-spawn is a creature born inside a Well and masked in its color. The Bestiary rates all of them on a Danger Tier from T1 to T9, and a lord's bounty and a hunter's funeral are both priced off that column.

A **Core** is what common speech calls a site where one of the sixty Wellsprings (the sixty types of Aether) pools. Most lie in dungeons, and some are said to lie out in the open in vast territories nobody has walked. A **[[The Bestiary/Dungeons|dungeon]]** is what a Core makes around itself or what grows up around one: caves, drowned stairs, old workings, a ruin the ground has begun to rearrange. The Measurewrights argue about which comes first and have been wrong in writing on both sides. Delvers read a dungeon from the top: the Mouth, the Upper Workings, the Lower Workings, the Deeps and the Heart, each band richer and worse. A worked Core with keepers is a dungeon somebody cleared and now holds, and those keepers can close it. Every realm below has at least one dungeon that people talk about in taverns, and the taverns have usually got the depth wrong.

Monsters live across the whole continent. The [[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Scourge of Hell|Scourge of Hell]] is where they are extreme.

## The North

*Later hand, from the last winter before the turn:* these lands become the Northern Empire of Whitemere, with Kharven its capital and the Banner Houses holding lands inside it. On the morning this shelf stands, there is no such empire. Kharven is the one true kingdom, the six Banner Houses are lands and never kingdoms, and Marrowhold and Undaar-Keth answer to nobody on this list. On the North's nine years of the Turn (see the Scourge, below), Kharven and the Banner Houses take their long columns down the Kessen road; the North last held it from 694 to 703.

### The Kingdom of Kharven

*House Moto · full page: [[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Kingdom of Kharven — The Ashen Crown|The Kingdom of Kharven]]*

**Climate.** The cold is the constitution. Winter runs six months at the Seat, the growing season four, and heating eight, and thirty below is a working temperature. Snow comes dry and squeaking, the wind off the plateau finds any gap in a coat, and the first frost that will not lift opens Stack-Count. The hungry end of winter is the Thin Weeks, a date on the calendar. Spring arrives all at once with Ice-out, the morning the river breaks, followed by mud, then a short bright summer of long pale evenings and biting flies. The sky is enormous, storm stacked on storm with orange sunset at the edge, and aurora goes over most clear nights, a slow green river with violet at its hem. In the hour after sunset the snow turns blue.

**Habitat.** A high granite plateau scarred by ice, with boreal forest of spruce, pine and birch on thin acid soil, moss in the shade, standing water everywhere, and permafrost in patches where the ground lies open. Rye and root crops grow in the sheltered valleys and barely. The **Marrow** runs under the Seat. The **Skeldmere**, the great frozen lake, hums in the deep cold over the water beneath it. North of the Seat lies **the white**, a mere that goes opaque by the second week of frost and never freezes through, because something keeps the water moving down there. In these woods live elk, wolverine, brown bear, lynx and shaggy little horses, and in the rivers char and pike.

**Monsters and Cores.** Greater Wolves (T3) work the Castle Stair in packs and will flank a column in a whiteout. In a bad Thin Weeks a Wendigo (T6) may come down off the shelf road, and the clans say it was somebody once, and they will tell you whom. Droval, the Frost-Howlers, stray south of the treeline in the hardest winters. The dungeon everyone talks about is **the Hum**, a drowned stair going down from the Skeldmere's north shore under an ice-cutter's hut, a Well of Abyntheus (a Wellspring of deep naval blue, whose slow throb is felt in the teeth before it is heard). Its spawn wear masks of deep naval blue. It has never been cleared. The Scarlet Scarves and the Wolf Grays each claim to have reached the Lower Workings, and each has the funerals to prove it.

**Peoples and culture.** Two traditions that coexist without merging: the Moto, Japonic in name, seven Great Lines each in its own metal, and the northern clans, Nordic in their pragmatism, who were here first and were steppe-riders once, before the ground froze through. "When the grass comes" still names the spring. Clan names run Norse and northern English (Sigga Tolleifsdottir), Moto names run given name, Line, Moto. They eat bone broth, windmeat (dried hard in the cold air), stonecurd (a dried cheese like a pebble) and the skin (fermented milk in a sealskin, and refusing it can get you killed), with the first bowl going to the one who cannot fill it. They wear wool and hide, and paint their doors ox-blood and ocher to find them in a whiteout. The greeting is a forearm grip and "How's your stack?" The dead lie first in the unheated death-house, and street and family sit through the Waiting; then the Seat buries and the clans give to the sky. Sōhai (the Moto's own faith) lives in household labors done correctly, among people who would be astonished to hear they were religious. They value competence, a full woodpile and showing up, and despise wet wood (a liar) and an empty saddle. Never meet a Moto's eye. The north fights in knots and wedges with great swords, axes and boar-spears, and a bracket fighter (brackets being the public fights, sorted by Stage, the sixteen rungs of a practitioner's climb) on every lane. The hide is the aura coat, the visible coat of a fighter's own color. The low sky is Pressure, the weight a stronger soul puts on a room, and smelling a man's stack is sensing. Festivals: the Midwinter Brackets on the river ice, Stack-Count, Ice-out, and the nameless day in the dead of winter, when no oath holds. They joke about southern coats, anyone with a third pair of boots, and the Tenrai, "the only family that can lose a war for forty years and bill you for it."

**Cities and towns.**
- **Kharven-Seat.** A towering dark castle of many spires and verdigris roofs on four hundred feet of rock, with a thousand orange lights in the lower town at its foot, and about the only place in the north where you can buy something you do not need.
- **The Foot.** The door of the north, square gray stone, two towers and a toll-post, where whoever holds it holds the Kessen road.
- **Harlsey Landing.** A river town that turns out in its best at Ice-out to watch the ration scale inspected in public.
- **The Castle Stair.** A long frozen valley with a cadet castle at every bend, each with loud opinions about the one above it, and the hooded stone torch-bearers on the last mile.

**Getting there.** Up the Kessen road from the Greenhollow to the Foot, then the valley of the Castle Stair. The shore road runs south to the Seat from the Skeldmere. West is the Slate Ridge, and past it the Mandate's country and the ground the bloodline came from, which nobody holds. East lie the Ironwood March and the Thornwood. South are the Painted Rivers and the Hollow, and past them the two roads around the Scourge. North there is the Reach, and nothing to approach from.

### The Cobalt March of Hollenmark

*House Stark · full page: [[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Cobalt March of Hollenmark|The Cobalt March of Hollenmark]]*

**Climate.** Colder and snowier than the Seat, because the March catches what wet air gets past the plateau. Snow blows through Hollenmark's streets all year in some fashion, and blue ice grows on the battlements and stays there. Winter is long, still and glittering, with a hard blue sky and pink snow at dawn. Autumn lasts about three weeks and it is glorious, the broadleaf stands going up scarlet and gold against the ice, and then everyone goes back to work. Summer is short, cool and full of mosquitoes.

**Habitat.** Boreal forest giving way to shield upland: ironwood and black spruce under deep snow, peat and bare gneiss, lichen on everything, and standing water frozen blue. A boundary stone stands on every rise, waist-high and lichen-green. There is timber and fur here and not much else that grows, so the March eats by Ashmore's allocation. Elk, boar, great brown wolves, and the **Dire Icebear**, enormous, frost-dusted and never domesticated, who carries a Stark for as long as the Stark is worth carrying.

**Monsters and Cores.** Wild bears are the first danger and the most respected. Goliaths (T5, giantkin who want to be left alone) hold the snow mountain above the city and throw rocks at surveyors. On the eastern line, Stark riders and Marrowkin survey parties both hunt the odd Greater Wolf pack and whatever comes off the Ring's fume-belt with too many opinions. The dungeon is **the Roll-Cellar** under Stonewatch, a Fixatio Well (the Wellspring of anchoring) that opened beneath the oldest stone on the line. Starks cleared it three generations ago, going down with surveying chains and entering each dead thing in the Roll by location. It is a worked Core now, with Stark keepers, and practitioners pay by the hour to sit in amber light that makes a stance very hard to shift. The other is **the Cold Larder**, a day from Bearhold, blue Vohrin galleries (Vohrin being the Wellspring of heat run backward) held by renderers on a Stark lease, where the winter trade cures what cannot be warmed and masks have to be run to the hall in a sack of snow.

**Peoples and culture.** Northern folk of the old clan stock, Norse and northern English in name (Ulf, Sweyn), in wolf-fur and wool, with the wolf on banners, gate and coin-weights. Food is windmeat, bone broth, smoked fish from the blue lakes and whatever Ashmore sent, with hot barley cakes in the market under the twin towers. They value the line, and a debt said aloud at a boundary, and being told the truth, even by a bear. They despise anyone who moves a stone. The dead go through the Waiting as elsewhere in the north. The Asking is their great rite: a Stark who wants to ride stands unarmed in a shirt at Bearhold while the bears look him over. Children play it in the lanes. A hide is expected to have frost on it, and the house's own inheritance, the Stark Rime, silvers timber around an angry Stark, and the tavern-keeper starts moving the glassware. They fight with great sword and axe, the heavy walk through the heels, winter war on roads nobody else thinks are open, in knots of men on foot with a few enormously loud bear-riders. Every midwinter for a hundred and twenty years they have sent an empty pie dish to Ashbury. They joke about everything south of them, and being put off your bear is a joke only the person it happened to may start.

**Cities and towns.**
- **Hollenmark.** A cold blue stone city under a snow mountain, twin towers joined by a high bridge, a pale citadel, and sword-cutlers who criticize your grip before they take your money.
- **Stonewatch.** A hard garrison town with the Roll-house, the old boundary stones in view, and the Thrawn Yard's training ground and its one-line song.
- **Bearhold.** A high stockade and open ground where the white bears come in autumn to look over the young of the house, with a small graveyard.

**Getting there.** The eastern road from Threeways. Timber goes down the eastern rivers. The Vessant supply line from the Amber Cities runs through here under a tithe, and a bronze disc with wolf and chain-mark gets you past most gates. East is Marrowhold, past the stones.

### The Slate Ridge of Stormfold

*House Greymane · full page: [[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Slate Ridge of Stormfold|The Slate Ridge of Stormfold]]*

**Climate.** The Ridge makes its own weather, and the locals say it does so out of spite. Thunderheads come in off the western shield and catch on the iron-banded rock most afternoons from thaw to first snow, so violet lightning walks the crest over wet slate that shines like a plum, the rain goes sideways, and a band of clean bright sky opens behind it. The storms pass as fast as they come, every gully runs silver and the heather smells of hot iron. Winter is a wall of driven snow, and in spring the keeper at Galecross ropes himself to his door. The children can tell a storm's arrival by the taste of the air.

**Habitat.** One long fold of slate and greystone, wind-hammered, with thin peat, heather, bilberry and dwarf birch below the crest and bare rock above. Stillband iron lies bedded in it. Gray-faced sheep too stubborn to fall off graze the shelves, with ravens and mountain goats, and snow hare in the gullies. Nothing else grows that anyone would call a crop, so grain comes up by levy.

**Monsters and Cores.** The Ridge has a plague of storm-things. Raiju (T5, lightning in the shape of a beast) run the crest in bad afternoons, and a coat of aura up there is regarded as asking for it. Wyverns (T6) nest on the western faces and take sheep and sentries. Harpies (T4) work the Galecross road. The dungeon is **the Ninth Gallery**: a Masonwarden's crew cutting a new gallery through the living slate broke into an older one that nobody had cut, full of blue-white light and Glarewings, Well-spawn with arc-blue lightning trees across their brows. It is a Well of Aurevane (hard-edged electric light). The Greymanes walled it, put a door in the wall, and set a post on the door, and a sentry has stood it for three hundred years, because no one has told him to stop. It has never been cleared. Every Greymane who has been sent inside was sent by title and has been replaced by the next one of that title.

**Peoples and culture.** Gaunt, long, gray people raised as components of a defensive structure, Norse and northern English by descent and addressed by function: the Shieldwarden, the Gatewarden, the Masonwarden, the Muster Aunt, the Cook. They eat at the mess: mutton, barley, bone broth, rind-water in the Thin Weeks, and hard cheese you could shoe a horse with. They wear gray, with a wolf's head in darker thread. They value the post and despise leaving it, and you hold your room by holding your place on the wall. The Waiting is kept in shifts in a cold death-house on the crest. Their one festival is an accident, the singing at the Dry Sock in Galecross on nights too bad for the wall, generally the song about the Cook, thirty verses. They fight in the Grey File, shields locked rim to rim, and the ridge sword strikes first from the high guard they call the Roof. Releases (a fighter's whole power rising into a higher form) are called in earth-words, Stonefast and Setting the Stone. A Greymane child will address the family dog as the Hound and the latrine as the Lower Gate. Everyone else tells the one about the Greymane who sat guard over a pie all night.

**Cities and towns.**
- **The Ridgewall.** A fortress carved into a sheer mountain face, one arched door at the end of a snowfield and leagues of curtain wall on the crest, with a lightning-mast on every tower and a garrison town in the lee whose whole economy is boots.
- **Galecross.** The great pass town, a knot of slate roofs where the wind comes from four sides, with a famous mess, a smoked-mutton trade and a tavern called the Dry Sock.

**Getting there.** From the Seat by the ridge road, west, on foot in winter. Galecross is where the road comes up from the Mandate's country, and the two garrisons know each other's relief schedules better than their own wives do. Past the wall lies the ground the bloodline came from, which the Greymanes look at every morning and which is nobody's business. Grain comes by Ashmore clerk each autumn, received with the courtesy due to a siege engine.

### The Golden Hollow of Ashbury

*House Ashmore · full page: [[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Golden Hollow of Ashbury|The Golden Hollow of Ashbury]]*

**Climate.** The one warm valley in a cold country, and cold by anyone else's measure. Sheltered from the plateau wind and open to the south, it is green from thaw to first frost with a four-month growing season to work in. Winters bring deep soft snow and fog on the water-meadows, summers bring warm rain, thunder at harvest and evenings the color of honey. Late summer is when northerners come down the road and find themselves crying at a hedge.

**Habitat.** Forested river valleys and deep dark soil, the only ground in the old kingdom where farming comes first: barley and rye to the tree-line, oats, pears, plums, onions, red cattle in the water-meadows, alders along bright rivers, golden birch and beech on the slopes. Eels and herons in the ditches, and mice in quantities that have shaped the region's history. Tithe barns stand over the wheat in every parish like cathedrals to a religion of bread.

**Monsters and Cores.** The Hollow is the softest ground in the North, so everything hungry eventually comes to it. Reaching Briars (T4, Well-spawn hedges with a green knot over the root-crown) creep out of the woods and are burned by the parish. Greater Wolves come down in the Thin Weeks, and in a dry year a wyvern from the Ridge will take a red cow in front of the whole village. South of the valley the barns have arrow-slits, for what walks up from the Thresh as well as for armies. The dungeon is **the Malt Deeps** under Barleyford, old malting caves that went down too far and turned out to be a Verdantia Well (the Wellspring of growth). Hired Thornwall axes cleared them in the 540s at a cost Ashmore still disputes. It is a worked Core now, kept by brewers on an Ashmore license, its hours sold to bracket schools, and the air down there will sprout a dropped crust while you watch.

**Peoples and culture.** Northern commons, English and Norse in name (Edric, Perkin, Wat), plump by northern standards and reminded of it. They eat fresh bread every day, pease pottage, barley cakes with butter, dark rye, smoked eel and plum brandy in a good year. They wear russet and yellow wool and carry a clean onion in the pocket. Many households perform all fourteen Sōhai labors correctly, in order, and would be insulted to hear they were doing anything other than keeping a house. They value the tally-stick and a promise on the junction stone, and despise waste and being asked "which sack?" Harvest Home runs a week: dancing in empty barns, sheaf-tossing, cloth-of-gold scarecrows on poles, and Stage brackets on the stubble under the moon, where it is bad luck to win on the first night. Peasants fight with billhooks and grievance, and the Ashmore Line comes on as a long ragged charge of lords' sons on big southern horses, very pretty. Everything else is arranged by not sending someone their grain. They joke about thin people, the rest of the north, and Perkin Oatley, always.

**Cities and towns.**
- **Ashbury.** The seat, a fat town of honey stone and gold thatch with tiered verdigris roofs on its great halls, the Corn Hall where the allocation is read, and the black junction stone below it, which holds one oath at a time.
- **Sheafton.** The barn town of the upper Hollow, with the rebuilt Great Tithe Barn, a regulation about tallow, and a Harvest Fair that draws fighters for the food.
- **Barleyford.** A malting town on a shallow crossing, smelling of sweet smoke, with one famous roof slate in the church wall and the best small beer north of the Center.

**Getting there.** The junction below Ashbury is where the Kessen, the Marrow and the salt road meet, and nearly everything on this continent passes it sooner or later. North by the Marrow road to the Seat, through the last of the spruce. South the North stops and the marching country begins: the salt road to Halcrest, the Kessen ford and the road to the Midmarch.

### The Scarlet Wood of Thornhaven

*House Thornwall · full page: [[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Scarlet Wood of Thornhaven|The Scarlet Wood of Thornhaven]]*

**Climate.** Cold, snowy and sheltered, with less wind than the plateau because the trees take it. Snow lies bright as linen for six months, and under the canopy it hardly thaws until the rivers are already running. Thaw to midsummer is the great season, loud with raft-horns and ice going out, and mist hangs in the stands most mornings. Summer is green, damp and gold-barred. Autumn fills the air with scarlet leaves that blow across everything.

**Habitat.** Hills of giant ironwood, red-barked, the color of a fox at the crown and old dried blood at the root, wider than a house and two hundred feet to the first branch. The dense stands are too dark for anything but moss and bear. Bright towns sit in the clearings along the rivers, with barley in small fields and birch for sap. Furs, pitch, tar, resin and charcoal come out of it, and the rivers run thick with log rafts. Bear, marten, boar and red deer, and beetles that a hedge-worker must talk out of a stand.

**Monsters and Cores.** Sasquatch (T3) move through the deep stands masking their scent, and most foresters leave them food. Dryads (T3) guard the oldest groves and have started two wars with the axe crews. Reaching Briars take the edges of the stump fields. Hedge-workers refuse to work on the far side of a **long cut** (a narrow straight ride through the deep stands) after dark and give no reason, and I set that down without comment. Crews call the Long Cuts a *lying-down*, a dungeon with no roof, and have offered to walk one end to end, and the foresters have been very civil about refusing. The dungeon anyone will admit to is **the Red Stair**: a ruined carved archway older than the house, at the head of worn steps, with long strings of faded cloth flags running off between the trees. The loggers' tally flags are tied there, one for each great tree taken. The steps go down among the roots into a Well nobody has reached the bottom of, and a raft-boss who has been down to the second landing drinks free in Raftwick.

**Peoples and culture.** Northern commons, Norse and English in name (Aud Rannsdottir), with a lady from the Shiraki hills at their head. They carve everything, in knots of beasts and leaves painted red and ocher and verdigris blue, and a child gets a knife before shoes. Food is smoked river fish, venison and bear in dark gravy, birch-sap beer in spring and flat barley bread bought dear; every raft has a pot that has not been washed since the raft was built. Raftsmen wear red caps and sing dirty songs in rounds, charcoal burners are the Black Hands, house soldiers wear gray felt and iron caps. They value a contract cut in wood with two guild-marks, and despise people who think a forest is scenery. Raftsmen's brackets are fought on moving logs in spiked boots. They fight with the Thornwall hedge, long ash-and-iron spears at a walk to a slow drum, and woodsmen with axes and short bows behind things eight feet thick. Technique names are named for the job. A Thornwall will price the damage aloud while it is happening, and "six months" is their punchline.

**Cities and towns.**
- **Thornhaven.** Carved red halls where the Thornwood meets the water, the Raftmen's Hall with a famous boot on a shelf, the cargo-roll house, and the best fire-enamel in the north.
- **Raftwick.** The great raft-town at the head of the river-drives, half of it afloat, which shifts its streets every spring and has no reliable map.

**Getting there.** By raft in the thaw, down to the Hallenfeld junction and beyond, with a toll fought over every spring. House Thornwall holds the corridor, the road and river-chain east to Collarbone in Marrowhold, timber one way and worked metal the other. North past the last spruce is the Aurora Reach. Starks next door, in the habit of checking the stones.

### The Aurora Reach of the Keld

*House Keld · full page: [[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Aurora Reach of the Keld|The Aurora Reach of the Keld]]*

**Climate.** Polar. Past the treeline the winter is a months-long dark at the top of the Reach, dry and windless until it is not, and then a whiteout comes across the plates and a person cannot see his own hand. Cold takes the unready, and the Keld have a name for it, Qatuq. The sun comes back to one bright week of sled-races, and summer is a brief endless day, moss going green and scarlet, more birds than you would believe, the top hand of soil thawing into bog over ice that never melts. The aurora hangs from one side of the world to the other in green and violet, with a pale moon in it looking cold.

**Habitat.** Tundra and permafrost to the polar shelf. The snow is white, blue and pale gold by the hour, cracked into enormous plates by frost heave, and rose mountains stand along the horizon. No trees except the snow-bent firs around the palace, curling like breaking waves, alive for no reason any forester can give. Moss, lichen and dwarf willow. Reindeer in rivers, the great horned musk-beasts that come down off the high ice in the Thin Weeks, white fox, wild white bears, seal, walrus and whale on the shelf. Clearbeam beryl comes from the pegmatite dikes on the margin, some crystals longer than a man.

**Monsters and Cores.** **Droval**, the Frost-Howlers or Rimegaunts, are the Reach's own terror, spirit-touched hunters of the snowfields that lie along a ridge until the ridge is suddenly not there. Wild bears come next, and packs of what the Mandate's northern wall calls an interesting category. The dungeon is **the palace**, which the Keld call the **Kiilut House**, a *kiilut* being one of the dead left without the Waiting: white and ice-blue, spiral towers and blue-glass domes, a covered bridge to a far tower, old work no one living built. It may hold Living Armor (T5) and worse. No one has cleared it, since no one can agree how many sides its third dome has. Measurewright Ansell spent one night inside in 694 and came out by the far tower with his beard at his belt. Crews who cross the covered bridge are not on any roll afterward, and his is the only name ever to come back across it. The Keld call a dungeon a house nobody sat with. They camp beneath this one, trade beneath it, race around it, and will not sleep inside.

**Peoples and culture.** The smallest house and the hardest people: low, wide, barrel-chested, in reindeer hide, faces painted blue and red from the shelf-clays for joy and for war, walrus ivory in their braids. Far-Northern names are carried: a child is given the name of someone dead and is held to carry that person, so a nineteen-year-old may tell you she is ninety. They eat seal, reindeer, fish, whale when there is whale, blood-soup, frozen fat cut in curls, windmeat, stonecurd and sour milk in horn cups at the door. They sit on bare packed snow, rise with no hand on the floor, and burn fat for light and heat because there is no wood. The dead go to the sky or the shelf, with the Waiting kept in the lodge and the lamps turned down. They swear to a person who comes in person, sits at the fires and stays worth following, and never to a letter or a house. Long throat-songs run all night in the dark months. They fight in the long line on small shaggy horses, with birch lances and horn-backed bows with black-glass heads, and their great trick is the whiteout. A fighter's Release is "the name stands." They joke about how tall southerners are and what a Measurewright says when his forks stop working, and never, ever about the Kiilut House. There is no coin here. Exchange is meat-sharing, labor-debt and salt.

**Cities and towns.**
- **The Whalebone Gather.** Ten thousand people for two bright weeks under the palace, whale-jaw gateposts, races, marriages and feuds, then a large stain in the snow.
- **The Teeth.** High summer pastures fought over by the Sennet clan and the Haldorssons since their grandfathers' grandfathers.
- **The shelf.** Where the land stops, where sealers go, and where a certain kind of Moto practitioner goes to practice Domains (a working that makes a whole piece of the world obey its user) without killing bystanders. The Keld return their boots.

**Getting there.** By sled, north from the Thornwood or along Threeways' northern road and past the Stark bears, in the hard months only, since the thaw turns the whole country to bog. Hill clans come off the Shiogane in bad years to raid the reindeer. Tenrai tax columns were sent onto the ice for years, until the Tenrai learned better.

### The Painted Rivers of Hallenfeld

*House Hallenfeld · full page: [[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Painted Rivers of Hallenfeld|The Painted Rivers of Hallenfeld]]*

**Climate.** Northern lowland weather with a river's damp in it: long cold winters that freeze the barges in and shut the realm's whole income for three months, a thaw that floods everything and is the great event of the year, and short warm green-gold summers with fog on the water at dawn. Everything smells wet and looks freshly painted, because rain gets at the paint and a house without a fresh coat is presumed to be in debt.

**Habitat.** Low green river valleys south and east of the Seat, running down from the plateau and the March to the Greenhollow, with red rock at the falls, misty blue mountains behind, deep silt along the banks. Three rivers (the Hallen, the Ruddle and the Sleeve) meet here. Beyond them are farmland, alder and willow, mills every half mile and long straight causeways, with firs on the high ground. Eels, river perch, otters and herons, and swans that once turned the color of whichever river they picked. Sheep on the water-meadows; a Hallenfeld sheep's left side in a wet year can still tell you about 699.

**Monsters and Cores.** Things come up the water. Sump-toads (T5, Well-spawn with blued-steel rings on the brow riding level with the surface) turn up in mill-races after floods. Greater Wolves take the causeways in winter when the river is walkable. The realm has one dungeon with two names, because its top is a business and its bottom is a debt. The top is **the Fall Cellar**, a dripping stair behind the falls on Phreatis ground (a Wellspring of the Fluxia Family, fluids and pressure), worked, famous for it and kept by the robed reeve on the lip, where barge-masters top up by the hour and come out remembering other people's childhoods. The bottom is **the Drowned Toll**, a flooded stair behind the middle of the triple falls below Hallenfeld castle, leading to a toll house far older than the house, with its rate still painted on the wall in a script no clerk can read, and a Well under it where Mourning Pike (T5, bred in Fluxia water, teal over the skull) grow as long as a barge. It has never been cleared. A Hallenfeld clerk has entered the rate as owing.

**Peoples and culture.** Quartermasters, road engineers, courier captains, bargefolk and sign-painters, English and Dutch in name (Ilse, Wat Pennyquill), in loud stripes, with blue and ocher for the house. They eat standing up: eel pie, river perch fried in barley meal, black pudding, pickled cabbage, small beer, hot spiced ale from a boat that comes alongside your boat, and the Toll Bun, shaped like a fist. They read Concord, as almost no one else in the north can. A child knows the rate on a sack of barley before her letters. They value delivery and honest books and despise whoever cuts a chain. Faith here is the northern kind with a saint on every toll house, painted by whichever guild has the contract, the Limners of Threeways in gold leaf or the Daubers of Chainbridge in whatever is cheapest. Sects are bargemen's clubs and bridge companies, and midsummer brackets are fought on the chain bridges, where one fall is a loss and two a very cold swim. They fight reluctantly, with boathooks and short spears, and the real weapons are the chain, the halt and the courier captains. "Six hours" is the whole of the house's humor. Thornwall raftsmen, Stark bears on the subject of paying a toll.

**Cities and towns.**
- **Hallenfeld.** The junction itself, a pale towered castle on a green rise above a triple waterfall that stops all river traffic, so every barge unloads and hauls around under the castle, and below it a damp rich town of counting-houses on stilts.
- **Threeways.** Where the roads actually meet, a day's ride from the water, where you can buy anything and sell it back an hour later at a profit.
- **Chainbridge.** Four hundred yards of chain bridge over the Hallen in flood, the Daubers' town at each end not speaking to itself, and a middle span that lifts when it does not jam.

**Getting there.** You are probably already here. From Threeways the eastern road goes to the March and the corridor for Marrowhold, the northern road to the Aurora Reach, and the southern road to Ashbury. South by water to Ashbury and the upper Aldwater. Relay riders change horses every twenty miles, and the beacon line can carry a yes or no from the March to the Seat in a night. A chartered carrier, the Northern Road Company, posts guards at bridges a house can be paid to give it, so check who you are paying.

### The Ember Holds of Marrowhold

*The Marrowkin · full page: [[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Ember Holds of Marrowhold|The Ember Holds of Marrowhold]]*

**Climate.** The rim keeps the shield's weather, a yard of snow on Altherion's roofs and rain up on the Seat, where it always seems to be raining. Inside the Ring it is warmer, from the heat under the floor and the thermal mass of the walls, and that same snow turns to steaming rain halfway down the inner slope. The basin floor is a warm, sulfurous, red-lit fog for much of the year. The air carries sulfur, hot metal and heated stone, with something under it like a tooth just drilled. Downwind of the smelters the fume has killed everything for six leagues.

**Habitat.** A crater some ninety leagues across at the east end of the North, whose rim is a wall of sheer peaks all curving inward, each one a single bone the color of old ivory wherever the red has not climbed it. Everything that can grow in the Ring is ember red: moss, bracken, thicket, forty-foot ferns on the bone terraces, the growth the holds call **emberwort**, living on what seeps from the bone. Redcaps, the fat emberwort fungus, grow everywhere and taste strongly of iron. A silver river crosses the basin. Under the floor runs *hehkuveri*, the glow-blood, thick as cold tar. The Rimlands outside grow timber and thin grain, never quite enough. Blackcap sulfide, old ivory and sinew come out of it, along with red-stained goats and pale bats that roost in the galleries.

**Monsters and Cores.** Everything red is slightly wrong to eat if you are not used to it, including the deer. Bone-runners on the rim at night are the most romantic criminals in the north, and rib-lice, pale cat-sized things living on the older galleries, are the least. Basilisks (T5) hunt the fume-belt, where nothing else will trouble them. Petralon (the Aether of stone) pools in the walls, and a good part of the Ring is worked Core. Three dungeons get talked about. **The Cold Marrow**, under Twelfth Rib, is a gallery through the bone that the Marrowkin stopped working one winter, in a great hurry, leaving their tools; the Ore Council has closed it, and they do not go down there. **The Deep Reach** below the basin floor is a Steward's territory, with the Deepkin in an advisory role that the upper holds find unnerving, and nobody who knows what lies at the bottom has shared it in full. **Anguz**, out past the northern roads, is dead mines, once a real power, now held by whoever is down there this week: Dustquartz by the sack, collapses, shards that resonate to the wrong Aether under pressure. Marrowkin use it as a proverb, that a mine that owes nobody ends up owing everyone.

**Peoples and culture.** About 35,000 Marrowkin, chest high to a human and door wide, furnace-eyed behind slit visors of carved horn, with ridges of ivory bone pushing through from about thirty; humans, elves, Beastkin and goblinoids make up the rest, mainly in Altherion. The Tally-Tongue in the holds, Concord Common on the rim, Lattice Tongue for sacred inscription. A name runs given name, oath-name, line, cut-name, the last being the largest thing you ever took from the Body in plain words, so a clerk may be Thimble and a Chair Sixty Tuns. They eat the crib (a meat-and-root pastry with your mark in the crust), redcaps fried in tallow, sour barley beer, dear bread, and one smear of marrow on bread at the First Entry, entered as the child's opening debt. They value the stamp and the true entry and despise a counterfeit above murder. The dead and the god are both on account; what died in the Ring stays disputed, and they call it the Body when selling and *Lainur*, the Lender, when frightened. Festivals: the Cutting Fair at Collarbone and, on the rim, the Weighing Festival, the Church's public reading of its own error rate. The Forge Guard plant their feet in Blackcap harness under rib-ivory shields, and Worked Ground means the man who charges one has charged the mountain. On shift is the aura coat, the load is Pressure, sounding is sensing. The oldest joke holds that the god died of being invoiced.

**Cities and towns.**
- **Breastbone.** The capital, a walled citadel of pale spires carved from the Body, thin bridges between them, an ember garden in every round hall, and seven forge levels under it pulsing orange at night.
- **Collarbone.** A mile of market along one horizontal bone at the head of the rim road, the front door, home of the nine-day Cutting Fair.
- **The Twelfth Rib.** The longest bone anyone has cut into, the oldest cutting face, and Kegshard Hold.
- **Kolmthruk.** The Third Hold on the west, a town of furnace-tenders who consider Breastbone soft.
- **Barkstone.** On the northern wall, source of the standard dialect and the most insufferable metricians alive.
- **Altherion.** The republic on the rim, seven peoples' building traditions in one street, a round table, the Accord's northern chancery on the Seat and the Grand Church in its compound.

**Getting there.** Thornwall's corridor comes into Collarbone from the west with ironwood. The Iron-to-Salt Corridor, under the Carriers' Guild and the Greis way-stations, runs south to the Amber Cities, and the salt road takes the heavy loads on down the Scourge's east flank to the coast. Three principal roads meet at Altherion. The north road brings you at sunset to the last turn, where you stop, and where the road's beggars work for that reason.

### The Iron Mandate of Undaar-Keth

*Hobgoblins under the Ironfold · full page: [[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Iron Mandate of Undaar-Keth — The Closed Fist|The Iron Mandate of Undaar-Keth]]*

**Climate.** Winters longer, colder and more brutal than at Marrowhold or the Kharven uplands, with river ice thick enough to march reserves across and snow from early autumn. Summer is short and shadowed under the canopy, with black flies. Autumn is brief, and the whole forest goes up in rust and copper like a banner nobody ordered. The northern wall looks out on the polar waste's edge and gets its wind directly. At thirty below, Stone-Blood runs slow and every goblinoid fighter moves about three-quarters of a beat late, and they know it as they know the sum of their grain.

**Habitat.** West beyond the Slate Ridge: black basalt and boreal forest, river valleys and low granite ridgelines running north to south. The forest is dark, resinous and thick enough to swallow a formation within fifty paces of the treeline. The Ashenmoor valleys grow seasonal grain, rye and roots. The ground runs thin, lean in the street's word, so a practitioner fills slowly here. Elk, gray wolves, bear and fur-bearers in the woods, salmon in the braided rivers, and in the Ashenmoor interior giant Bugbear farmers wading the shallows.

**Monsters and Cores.** The northern wall faces the creatures that breed on rich tundra ground and consider a garrison an interesting food source. Gray wolf packs are a matter of record, and one served as an officer. A dragon has been seen far overhead above the Ashenmoor, and it is entered in watch-reports as a dragon. Concentrated seeps are largely absent here, so a dungeon is a rare event, and the one everyone mentions is **Vorrak's Standing Grove** on the western frontier, where the third Stone-Carver spent her retirement cutting practice decrees into living trees. A thin Chthonica seep (a Wellspring of dark iron-oxide, all grip and ground) lay under the wood. The things in that grove now obey clauses, and a patrol that knows the wording can walk through. A company stands around it. The Ironfold has it annotated "monitor, do not engage."

**Peoples and culture.** Of the Roll, about 55% hobgoblin, 25% Bugbear, 12% Ironblood Orc, 5% Tinker Goblin and 3% other, with a good many bench goblins off it. Hobgoblins command, Bugbears feed, Ironblood fight and Tinkers build, and nobody notices until someone from outside points it out. Old Vaross is the language of law, with a commander's name taken from the post and given back; garrison towns are numbered and never named; Gorr-Vaross runs in the Ashenmoor in long chains joined by *and*. Smoked elk and barley by the barrow, Nalūn salt on every table, roots and pickled things under a Bugbear roof, the soup in the Gorge. Crimson lacquer, iron and doors lacquered in company color. They value the count and despise noise. There is no faith but the law: the Decree Reading runs fifteen minutes daily at every garrison and non-attendance is noted. Festivals are the Forge-Circle, monthly, at the top of the Gorge, and the Wall Games on feast days. They fight with the Resonance Lattice (soldiers' souls set like the stones of an arch), Ironblood assault, crossbows on cleared lanes, and The Red Wall Takes One Step. The aura coat is *ak-gresh*, the survived skin. "Report to the wolf" is what a soldier says of an order that makes no sense, said with a straight face because it might be lawful.

**Cities and towns.**
- **Undaar-Gorr.** The capital on a basalt plateau at three rivers, a grid laid for the dead run, and the Decree Stone as tall as a keep.
- **Vreth-Kaan.** The northern wall, coldest and most fought, with the Fortress-Monastery of Haldrek at its back and Echohold of Stoneblood Vale a day south.
- **Gorr-Shal.** The eastern march, a customs post and war camps a morning's march up the road from the wool scales, with sutlers working both.
- **Keth-Vorruk.** The southern line, three centuries of holding back younger sons with a charter and a cart.
- **Tinker's Gorge.** A mile-long crack three hundred feet deep, hung with lamps in lime and amber and pink, painted doors at the top and quota benches at the bottom.
- **The Ashenmoor.** The farming heartland, steep-roofed timber towns on stilts along braided rivers and a long bridge railed with tusks, with Za'tarch in its southeastern corner, a village of two hundred with ideas.
- **Vorrak's Reach.** The western frontier, held for depth, with a coast where Isles brimstone comes ashore.

**Getting there.** Through Galecross if you are invited. South across Keth-Vorruk from the Hundred Crowns, with two tally-sticks, one for each bank's law. By sea to the Vorrak's Reach coast. Salt comes up from Nalūn under Ironwall escort, the one arrangement nobody has tried to change. A caravan with six hobgoblins on its flanks pays more at the start and less at the end.

## The Center

Wedged between North and South and marched through every generation by somebody on the way to somewhere else. Much of it is not human, and the Accord's Beastkin law and its neighbors' very existence are its standing fight. The Central Warlords, four of the realms below, are less known than either side, and a northerner may not be able to name all four. Summers here are hot and loud with thunder, winters cold and clear, and the dry wind from the middle reaches everyone. The Center holds the Turn at present, from 712 to 721.

### The Scourge of Hell

*No ruler, and no shortage of opinions. Also called Hell's Scourge · full page: [[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Scourge of Hell|The Scourge of Hell]]*

**Climate.** A vast wasteland at the center of the continent, with perpetual haze standing over it, copper at noon and rose at evening. The wind is dry and never quite stops. Summer days are furnace-hot on the bare ground and the nights drop hard, so rock splits with the change. Winter is bitter, windy and nearly snowless, and what rain reaches it falls as a sudden violent burst that fills the dry beds for an afternoon and drowns anything in them. The sky over the deep Scourge has a purple cast that no Measurewright has explained to the satisfaction of a second Measurewright.

**Habitat.** Cracked plains, dry river beds and broken ridges, with mountains deep inside that few have seen from the near side. Black roots run across the ground and go down, belonging to no tree anyone has found. Thornscrub, salt-bush and gray lichen hold on at the rim, and the ambient Essence reads wrong, so growing things do poorly and practitioners from fat ground feel a faint tiredness they cannot locate. Hobgoblins hold some of the ridges above it. Herds move through it in numbers nobody has counted, followed by what eats them.

**Monsters and Cores.** This is the one area known across the continent for extremely dangerous monsters, filled with them long ago. Things come up through the ground without warning. Wyrms (T7) with scales the size of shields run under the plains, and whole companies have been lost to one in an afternoon. The great herds are known by cutters' names: **stilts**, tall as a keep's wall and thin as famine, walking in thousands; **greywalkers**, who follow them; and the things that hunt in the dry beds, which nobody has named because nobody has come back to name them. Hellhounds and Bicorns (T5) work the near rim, and a Hydra (T8) is reported every decade and disputed by the survivors. Cores are said to lie here in the open across whole territories, with no dungeon around them, simply a stretch of ground that kills you differently, and it has more roofed dungeons than it has surveyors: the Lamplit Stair, Nine Wells Gulch, the Upside Abbey, the Dry Harbor, the Long Muster, and the Wound at the foot of the sword. At the center, by every account and no survey, a colossal bronze-hilted sword stands driven point-down into the mountains, its blade a window onto a violet field of stars. It is old work of the Great Spirit War, who drove it in is an open argument, and the Measurewrights who suspect the monsters are what leaks from that wound have never been able to go and check. The long ridge where a person can first see it is called **the Last Look**, and small parties go there on purpose.

**Peoples and culture.** Nobody lives here, and a good many people work here. **Cutters** are free-company hunters on Thresh contracts (monster bounties paid by anyone with a monster problem), a loud, scarred, superstitious trade in gray wraps and lucky teeth, drawn from every realm and mostly from the Midmarch and the Long Fields. They eat what they carry and drink what they can find a hedge-worker to clean. They name each other by what they lost. Their dead stay where they fell, with a tally cut on the nearest root. The great fact of their lives is **the Turn**: North, then South, then Center, nine years each, deal with the Scourge's monsters in rotation, by an arrangement old enough that nobody can produce the first copy. Whoever's turn it is bleeds, and the other two watch their border and count what it costs. Forty years of war never stopped the wheel, though free companies on Thresh contracts now do most of the dying, and each region can prove with documents that the bill is someone else's.

**Cities and towns.** No city has ever lasted.
- **The Thresh.** The monster-haunted southern approaches of the salt country and the first belt of the rim, more a contract than a place, marked by burned camps and cairns of mask-shards.
- **The Turnstone.** A black pillar on the western rim off the Kessen road, where a commander handing over the Turn is supposed to hand his successor a broken spear, and generally sends it by courier.
- **Cutters' camps.** A hundred muddy names, none lasting three seasons, each with a surgeon, a bookie and a board.
- **The edge towns.** Lastpenny on the Kessen road, Goodmorrow on the salt road, Halcrest at Nalūn's gate and Rootgate on the west flank stand outside it and live off it, painted, expensive and very loud.

**Getting there.** Walk inward from anywhere on the Center's map and it will find you. Nobody marches through it. Every army on this continent has gone around, by the Kessen road or the salt road, and the armies that tried otherwise are why the rim has so many cairns.

### The Amber Cities of Nalūn

*City councils, no crown · full page: [[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Amber Cities of Nalūn|The Amber Cities of Nalūn]]*

**Climate.** Above ground: short summers, five months of snow, and a wind off the shield carrying wet granite and boreal resin, with a dry hot breath from the Scourge side in high summer that makes the salt-slab roofs tick. The basin floor sits three hundred feet below the shield and is drier, warmer and far steadier than the plateau. Underground it is cold at the top, dry and stable, and the deepest chambers hold a low constant warmth that visitors describe as the temperature of a body at rest. Nobody down there has ever been rained on, and they are obsessive about it.

**Habitat.** A sealed evaporite trough (a basin where a sea dried out and left its salt) ninety leagues by forty on the Scourge's north side, bounded by shield rock on the north and west, by the boreal forest where the eastern timber begins, and on the south by a broken rim above the Thresh. Two hundred fathoms of bedded halite, where a flame throws back pink and violet and a faint drowned green. On the surface, little that grows: salt-bush, tough barley in a few lee valleys, goats. North of the rim are the chain-lakes, Long Scratch the greatest, with perch and char and the salt smuggler's road across the ice. Below, blind white fish in the old flooded cuts, and bats.

**Monsters and Cores.** Water is the great enemy, and what comes with it: Sump-toads (T5) in any neglected gallery, the kind of creature a drainage book exists to prevent. Lamp-drinkers (T6, found only as the darkest place on a dark shape) have ended whole shifts in the older tunnels, and the lamp-wardens charge accordingly. In bad years, stilts and greywalkers come up off the Thresh to the southern rim, and the Salt Line, sixty miles of salted ground along the Scourge's north edge, holds them about as well as a fence. **Deepvein** is the worked Core, the great Contraxis site (the Wellspring of contract), and its keepers in the factor houses have never closed it. The dungeon is **the Lower Beds**, below the deepest maintained gallery, reached only through the oldest tunnels and only partially explored, where the Aether pools thicker with every fathom and a whole cutting house, Merrow's, went down in 590 and has gone on sending up its drainage fee on time, in bars, by a lift nobody is working.

**Peoples and culture.** Forty-five thousand underground, eight thousand on the surface, mostly human with English and Common names (Bartlemy Quince, Torven Greis), in amber surcoats, chain-mark discs and good boots. Three classes: factors, cutters and agents, and beneath them the tokenless, entered in the drainage book under a number. They eat salt fish, hard bread, salted meat when the surface sends it, Bridgemarch blue cheese and salt tea (black tea boiled with salt and butter). A host shaves a curl off a stamped bar onto bread at his threshold, and a guest who eats it has eaten the host's money. "What's your oldest?" comes before your name, since a seal-token's age is your credit. Concord Common here is so exact it has become a technical dialect. The cutting class lays its dead in a worked-out face, the salt keeps them, and a merchant's funeral is also a reading of his open contracts. They do not pray; the rites around sealing a great contract do that work, and nobody drinks in a sealing room. The Trial of Ten Deals, with odds on every terrace, chooses the High Chainlord, and Honor Challenges outdraw executions. Hired free companies do the fighting and the Chain Guard holds the terraces with crossbows; a march contract does the rest. The surety is the aura coat, the call is Pressure, and reading a man's seal is sensing. They joke about the arch and "paid in full," and every Bridgemarch child can do the cheese-board.

**Cities and towns.**
- **Ironlink Hold.** The Council's seat: honey-stone rise, domed Hall of Links, a topless triumphal arch under creeper, and the Great Chain Hall below, where the oldest walls glow during a sealing.
- **Halcrest.** The gate city where the beds thin against the northern rim, pale salt-slab terraces and lamp-chains, customs, weigh-house, lazar-house, and the hiring ground of half the free companies in the north.
- **Cutwall.** Barely a city, a shifting camp of work chambers following the cutting face, tasting of powdered halite, where the older cutters wear salt-throat like a rank.
- **Vaultmere.** The counting city, dense dry salt, three thousand archivists and calibrators, and a place people lower their voices and then resent it.
- **Bridgemarch.** The central void, chain-bridges over two hundred feet of drop, houses hung from the ceiling, engineers who decide whether anything gets anywhere, and the blue cheese.
- **Deepvein.** The Wellspring floor, warm and glowing, where contracts hold tightest and the Chain Light never flickers.
- **Rimward.** The newest, on the eastern frontier, thin salt and contracts with a little give in them, and no relation of the Holy Sea's harbor of the same name.

**Getting there.** From the north by the salt road out of Ashbury to Halcrest. From the south by the same road, through Rimward or the south road to Ironlink Hold. The Iron-to-Salt Corridor comes in from Marrowhold. A lake road serves a surface port. Any army gets met by a scribe with a march contract: sign it and the bakers open.

### The Sealed Charter of the Concord

*The Guild Accord, which holds no territory · full page: [[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Sealed Charter of the Concord|The Sealed Charter of the Concord]]*

**Climate.** The kindest weather in the Center: hot blue summers, afternoon thunder that clears by supper, long golden hay-weather, and snowy winters that are hard but short. Bee-weather, they call it, and they plan around it. The wind from the Scourge reaches the west of the country a few weeks a year and dries the hay on the stalk.

**Habitat.** Good low country on the Scourge's east flank, where the salt road crosses the roads to the Russet forest and the Bearkin hills, with the salt basin to the north, green and blue and yellow in summer: hay meadow, clover and lavender grown by the mile for the hives, barley, brown rivers, white towns. Deep river soils. Oxen and sheep, bees beyond counting, kites over the meadows. Bearkin hills lie to one side and the Russet forest to the other.

**Monsters and Cores.** Everything here has a form. Cockatrices (T5) get into henyards along the river towns and a warden has to be sent for. Harpies raid the wax wagons. Stilts have been sighted from the western hay-country in bad years, and the Concord Legion drills for them on the plain. The hiring halls run their boards from here, and the Company of Adventurers keeps its underwriting tables, the most honest document about mortality on the continent. The dungeon is **the Tail**: the spine of the dead beast of Sealhall runs out through the city wall and away into the barley, each great bone bigger than a chapel, and a stair goes down at the fourteenth vertebra into a Well the survey has never identified. Enforcement holds the gate on it, the Company rates it, and past the Lower Workings the tables simply carry a dash. The other is **the Heart Vault** in the Concord Citadel, a true Wellspring Core held by the Accord, and who cleared it is not a question the Accord has a form for.

**Peoples and culture.** Scriveners, clerks, chandlers, wax-carriers and everyone who has come here on business, mostly human with Common and English names (Corbin Ashlar, Isolde Averell), with Court Latin in the formulary. Beneath them are Beastkin, chiefly Catkin and Bearkin, who carry the wax and cut the hay and are entered under stock: the Beastkin Classification Provision sorts the living into free-status, bonded-status and labor-stock, and a petitioner without papers is now written *nemo*, nobody. Everybody can read. Food is honest and exact, with an assize loaf of fixed weight, pulse porridge, ale passed by two sworn tasters, meat on feast days and honey on everything, and the children have bad teeth and very good manners. Titles come before names at any door. They wear good wool in sober colors with wax colors over the hall door, and strangers walk around with a dab of it on the hand. They value the instrument, *coram testibus*, and despise an unwitnessed anything. Their festivals are Veyra's Day and Irath's Day and the Stage brackets at every fair, the nearest thing to a shared religion, bookies in green hats and all. The Concord Legion in blue and white is bound by resonance, an oath cut into the bone, and Null Wardens with sealed throats bring the worse news. Reinforcement, Pressure and sensing are the Guild roll's own words, used proudly, and the crowds say shine, weight and nose to annoy them. They joke about paperwork, with love. Cory and Tess, the statues, have been married since the Second Eon with their spears crossed.

**Cities and towns.**
- **Sealhall.** A vast walled city of sixty thousand around one white hall, obelisks, blue and orange banners, crossed spears over the road, and at the center the Ribs, a court of judgment inside the bones of a dead beast.
- **Waxgate.** A loud yellow river town of hives, rendering sheds and pigment yards, with everyone's thumbs stained and a mystery shed for the obsidian wax.
- **Verath.** A clean, inspected township on the far western edge, at the crossing of two trade corridors, with the largest licensed pens in the Center. Past its two iron posts with red-painted caps the Accord's jurisdiction ends and the Verath Expanse begins, the Charter's own name for its stretch of the Scourge's rim.
- **Aldersey and Stowmarch.** Market towns of the kind the Charter is full of, where the Chandlers and Saddlers both ride eighth and bakers are buried with the loaf.
- **Harrowgate.** A great fair town, with cherry and cream banners on the horse lines, a Long Market, a Fair Court and the pens where the gate came off its pins.

**Getting there.** The salt road crosses it, and armies cross on a Writ by the named road, with their bread paid at the assize. Most marshals take the Kessen road on the far flank, across the Midmarch, to save the fee. The older Concord Gates, one in Sealhall's forecourt, admit only a soul bound to the Lattice, and every year someone tries it on a dare. On the east, the forest eaves of the Russet Crown begin where a returner's paper stops being paid.

### The Hundred Crowns of the Midmarch

*Petty kings, changing sides every decade · full page: [[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Hundred Crowns of the Midmarch|The Hundred Crowns of the Midmarch]]*

**Climate.** Big stormy skies over a wide river plain, and Kingsbridge's weather appears to be a local product. Hot wet summers full of thunderheads, gray-gold autumns for marching, cold muddy winters that send the levies home, floods on the Aldwater most springs. The dry wind from the Scourge makes a hard week or two in late summer, when the barley goes up in somebody's war.

**Habitat.** River plain and low green hill along both roads, thickest on the Kessen down the west flank and thinner on the salt road up the east, with more castles per mile than anywhere else in the world. Hedgerows, water meadows, barley, onions, apple orchards, hill pasture for hams, and a chain across every lane with somebody sitting beside it. The **Aldwater** runs down the middle of the crowded western half, broad, brown and full of traffic. The **Harl Hills** and the **Stitch Hills** break it up. Pigs, geese, eels, and the fat red cattle of whichever king has not yet lost them.

**Monsters and Cores.** The great herds south of the salt, the stilts and greywalkers and the things in the dry beds, come up into Midmarch barley some years, and a bounty on a Midmarch door is cried for ten leagues. Greater Wolves follow armies. Manticores (T5) have been crowned in at least one valley, by a herald who needed the fee. The dungeon is **Nether Marl**, the only kingdom in the world that is wholly a dungeon: a labyrinth under a hill with a Minotaur (T5) at the Lower Workings, whose title King Hamo the Undefeated bought at Pennycrown along with a stamped copper crown. He has never gone below the Mouth. He sells licenses at the door, charges a toll going in and a much larger one coming out, and has legally been at war with whatever is down there for twenty-six years. **The Orchard Under Goosegrass** at Hollowhay, nine galleries of apple trees on Verdantia ground, was lost to its own keepers over back pay and is booked for weddings until 719.

**Peoples and culture.** Human kings are the most common and the loudest, descended from some northern sergeant or a southern marshal's bastard. Tinker Goblin kings hold the workshops and passes and rarely live past forty, the Essence (the refined power a practitioner holds and spends) running too hot for the frame. Chimeric-Born hold the Stitch Hills, with gilded Binding Scars at court. A few Beastkin hold crowns too, and the Charter enters them as *nemo, rex*. Names run English and Common, with comic ones the rule among commons and kings alike (Gerent Tuppence, Tizz Copperwick, Ambrose Fivefold). They eat better than the Charter: barley bread, river eels, smoked ham from the hill kingdoms, big onions and apple beer. Everybody dresses loud, herald-painters are paid before the masons, and scarlet bulls, blue-and-yellow checks and gold geese fly over every keep. A peasant keeps a chest of old tax receipts from dead kings. They greet with "Who's king today?" and toast "Long live whoever." The bishop's court at the Scale Minster hears crown claims, Sancta Lux (the order that measures oath and legitimacy) sends its Ember Magistrates on circuit, and otherwise faith is a saint per bridge. They value a good song and a lawful coronation (three neighboring crowns attending, or two and a Measurewright) and despise being asked which king it is by anyone with a cart. The Coat-Turning at Pennycrown, every tenth summer, is the great fair. War is a sport with casualties, forty days and home for harvest, with spears, crossbows and goblin contraptions that everyone stands well back from. Colors is the aura coat ("his colors have run"), the crown is Pressure, the herald is sensing. They will not joke about the Moss Court.

**Cities and towns.**
- **Kingsbridge.** A river city under a storm sky, a bridge of huge pale arches with a Standing Army of two hundred who are home for dinner, a green-roofed minster on the height, and a king whose kingdom ends at the last stone of each pier.
- **Tollgarth.** A castle-town shut across the one good pass in the Harl Hills under Queen Tizz Copperwick the Fourth, where every gate is a contraption and watching a wagon go through is free.
- **Pennycrown.** Where you go to become a king: goldsmiths, heralds, notaries, Crown Row, and a very old throne still leaning north into the town wall.
- **The Stitch Hills.** A kingdom in many pieces under King Ambrose Fivefold, who has won every case and every field on whether he was assembled.
- **Upper Marl.** Nine miles wide, the Gambrel kingdom, bareheaded since 704.
- **Lastpenny.** The edge town on the Kessen road that the whole country claims, rebuilt in stone after the wyrm of 676, with the awning kept and the Noon Fair every Midsummer.

**Getting there.** The Kessen road runs right through it and the salt road grazes its eastern patches, so you will arrive by accident. The Aldwater goes by longship and barge. North are the salt basin and the Banner Houses' southern marches, and Keth-Vorruk's wall on the northwest. South lie the old roads to Sum-gol and the Ochre Fields. The Windstair rises from the western farmland, and at Rootgate a board says GO ROUND.

### The Moonlight Forest of Mosk-Vaal

*Verdant Orcs, the Moss Court, a Central Warlord · full page: [[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Moonlight Forest of Mosk-Vaal|The Moonlight Forest of Mosk-Vaal]]*

**Climate.** Warm, wet and windless, the lowest and wettest ground in the Center, where the storms that the Scourge pushes off its rim come down as rain on the mire. Winter is mild and foggy, with a skin of ice some mornings at the edges. Summer is a steam bath, full of insects and enormous soft moths. The real seasons here are the moon's: at the full you can read a contract by the moss and the black water may rise a hand with no rain falling, and at the dark the whole forest sinks to a dull ember and the people turn short with each other.

**Habitat.** Fen and flooded forest on the Scourge's west flank, nine days across by causeway, all deep black silt and water like black glass. At the middle a colossal tree shines ice-blue over a still teal lake, tall stalks carrying their own light stand thick as temple columns along the shore, tree-houses sit under magenta blossom and soft lights drift with no one carrying them. Moss-green glow on every trunk, white moths in drifts, alder, pear and quince, luminous fungus, eels. The ground is rich in Selunite (the lunar Wellspring), with Manganthra (rot) and Verdantia (growth) thick beneath.

**Monsters and Cores.** Very little here will bother you if you have been polite. Mourning Pike and Sump-toads hold the deep channels, Reaching Briars are regarded as a kind of pet, and swamp-grown Hydra (T8) stories are told to children and also to generals. The orchards are not, strictly, monsters. The great tree stands on the richest worked site in the Center, and the Court keeps it; the delvers' tables enter it as a dungeon never cleared, which has joined the government. The one crews talk about is **the Tarnish Pools** below Ghurash Hollow, Manganthra sumps where rot-wives cut their reagents from the upper levels and from which, at the dark of the moon, something older sends up a smell that makes the Soul-Trees drop their leaves. The Mire-Mother's divers have cleared it four times, in the sense of planting a garden on top.

**Peoples and culture.** Verdant Orcs, six foot four to seven feet, vine-muscled, eyes solid lamps of moss-green or lunar white, bark at the shoulders and luminous fungus at the joints, tended like a lord's beard. Swamp Parun runs on a single unbroken breath, names are long and sibilant (Ssaaleth), and Beast-Sign runs four hundred silent signals. Decisions come out of the Spoor-Meld, a shared cloud of green mist at knee height. Cooking is the finest and most dangerous in the Center: black-water rice turned for three moons, eel smoked over punk-wood, mushroom beer the color of old brass, a cheese that has to be held down, fried moth-grubs in bark cones. They wear moth silk and moss-dye. Death is a phase. The dead go into the Ancestor-Rot Gardens, a Soul-Tree carries each one, and people visit their grandmothers and quarrel with them. Funerals are merry and long. Salt is under law, spilling a cask is capital, and the Salt Sentence, burial in salted ground with no tree, is the worst penalty they have. They value the cycle and despise hoarding a corpse. The Mire-Mother's succession dive at Glowwater is the great festival. A Verdant Orc more than two days from swamp or kin begins to wither, so they have never taken a foot of ground outside the wet, and at home Mire-Mother's Yes takes them under the water at one end of a fight and up behind you with friends. *Ssuul*, the rind, is the aura coat, the damp is Pressure, and to sense a stranger is to ask the moss. The toast is "See you later." They ask a guest how ripe he is feeling.

**Cities and towns.**
- **Mosk-Vaal.** The seat, grown in shining green and gold into the tree's roots, its streets water and root, where you leave your salt at the landing.
- **Rootgate.** The northern head of the causeway, the board, the pears, innkeepers and nervous factors, the one town where outsiders sleep uninvited.
- **Glowwater.** The lake port, dyers with luminous forearms and little ships with lamp-stalks at the bow.
- **Ghurash Hollow.** The oldest settlement and the greatest of the Ancestor-Rot Gardens.
- **The Blooming Mire.** The marriage town, a square league of flowering fen that stinks gloriously, where wedding speeches go wrong every year.
- **Totemdeep.** Half under water, carved trunks standing in the flood, the fighting lodges' home.
- **The Orchard.** Three thousand eight hundred pear trees in company order, with a gatekeeper.

**Getting there.** Go round. The Kessen road's long loop around the forest, between the eastern eaves and the Hem, adds eleven days and is what every sensible carter takes, and the forest calls it the salt road, because that is what comes along it. The causeway from Rootgate has a fee that is cheap for a carrier and ruinous for a regiment. Do not bring a marching column, do not bring an open cask, and take the pear.

### The Rose Coils of Coilspire

*Serpentkin, House Scalebond, a Central Warlord · full page: [[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Rose Coils of Coilspire|The Rose Coils of Coilspire]]*

**Climate.** Warm, dry and bright on the ridges, with hot blue summers and the dry wind off the Scourge on the northwestern slopes. Winter brings cold clear nights and a little rain to the valleys. Hot springs on one flank of each ridge and cold seeps on the other make two climates a hundred paces apart, which suits the Serpentkin, who are comfortable at either extreme and weakest in between. Their one enemy is a mild spring day in a shaded field.

**Habitat.** Long curved ridges of rose-colored stone, turning back on themselves like a sleeping snake, with narrow green valleys wound between them. Terraced vines give a pale pink wine, and plums, rose gardens, almond and thin wheat grow there. Old battlefields lie in those valleys like pressed flowers. Basking lizards, river-fish, peppered snails. It is rich ground of a peculiar kind: a visitor with any training feels the afterimages before he has found an inn.

**Monsters and Cores.** Nine centuries of armies have left this country well supplied with Ghouls (T4) and Skeletal Folk (T4) on the old fields, and the Hollow's guides know which ones you can tour. Basilisks (T5) and Lamia (T5) keep to the far valleys, where they are entered by name. The dungeon is **the Lower Reading** beneath Stoneglass Hollow, an Anamnesis Well (the Wellspring of memory, faded violet, smelling of old paper) under the fused rose-black glass. It breeds Roll-callers (T6), Well-spawn of the Deeps who speak in the voices of people the Well has killed, and has never been seen above them. The house has not cleared it and regards it as a source. Scholars go down on a rope to hear a dead witness and pay the posted price per question. The Hollow above it counts as a dungeon in its own right, a town living inside one, and nobody has tried to clear it, since no one can say what there is to kill.

**Peoples and culture.** Gold-scaled scholars in good coats, cold-handed surgeons and exact, patient poisoners, formed near the Scorchline Wellsprings in the Second Eon, their Soul Crystals (the organ that refines Aether into Essence) laying down memory in layers like sedimentary rock. Coilspeech carries when-it-was-true in the grammar, so "When did you say that was?" is the most feared question in the Center. Soul-names run in two parts (Zsovrath Oda-riss). A Serpentkin eats one great meal a fortnight and lies still after it; the street food is for everyone else: hot peppered snails, iced rosewater, flat bread with river-fish and pickled plums. Every house has a hot room and a cold room and an unwelcoming corridor between. The dead are read before they are buried, and the Coil-Rite gives the young their first deep layer. They value the record and despise tampering, and the second-worst crime is asking someone to forget. Each year they hold the Reading of Gorvaine on the steps of their conqueror's statue, with his full titles and the exact manner of his death, and then children climb on him. They fight rarely and as a slow horror, with As Previously, a coiled strike with the force that settled this same fight two hundred years ago. The entry is the aura coat, the objection is Pressure, and to sense a fighter is to look him up. A Scalebond's great joke may wait a hundred and forty years. Everyone else says that a man with pink teeth has been to school.

**Cities and towns.**
- **Coilspire Den.** The seat, up the finest stair in the world, a carved gray plaza and serpent lamps and pink spires, with the giant's black statue in the middle and miles of living archive underneath.
- **Stoneglass Hollow.** A bowl of fused rippling glass where past events show if you stand in the right place at the right hour, and tourists pay to see the battle of their choice.
- **Bothhands.** A hospital town at the ford, one long ward for each direction of the march, and the only street where both sides of a war share a cookshop.
- **Lamp Stair.** The Den's cheapest lodging quarter, a bed for a few coppers or a true story, where half the spies in the Center began.

**Getting there.** The salt road skirts the country and crosses the ford at Bothhands, so every army comes through with its wounded. South of the salt country, on the old ruins of the Scourge's southeast edge, and a fine view of the haze from any ridge. The Pale Fang Archipelago is claimed and disputed, a long way off.

### The Violet Peaks of Caelspire

*Avian Kin, House Caelward, a Central Warlord · full page: [[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Violet Peaks of Caelspire|The Violet Peaks of Caelspire]]*

**Climate.** High mountain weather on the northwest of the Center: thin bright air, strong steady wind, lilac cloud lying below the crags in a flat sea. Snow on the summits most of the year, a cold clear summer, and spring gales that make Highroost eat standing up. The Kin read a front two days out on the skin, and the Windstair almanac sells it. A dead calm inversion morning, with fog lying flat, grounds half a school, and those are the only days everyone is polite.

**Habitat.** A broken line of violet-shadowed summits above farmland like a map on a table. Hanging meadows, small barley terraces, lilac scrub, goats, one huge old tree at Caelspire's summit beside a pool that spills off the edge as a waterfall so long that it blows away before it lands, with a rainbow in the spray. Flights of white birds go around it all day. The pool is a worked Core of Cymorath, the Air of Ascent. Lower down are pine and rhododendron, with bear on the northern faces.

**Monsters and Cores.** Griffons (T5) nest on the east faces and will leave a fight to keep the nest. Hippogrifs (T4) are caught and sold, Harpies are a nuisance on the Windstair, and a Wyvern taking a fighter out of the air is a thing that happens during fixtures. The dungeon is **the Chimney** inside Highroost: a shaft up through the hollow tooth of the peak, an Ascensio Well (the Wellspring of rising and hoist, steel gray) where the Mouth is at the bottom and the Heart at the top, so delvers climb and everything else falls on them. The Shrikes claim to have cleared it in 588. The Old Mews, four very elderly women, send them a note every year correcting the date and the word. The Summit Pool at Caelspire is the other on the Guild's list, worked by the Caelwards for longer than the house can document, and every other school on the mountain has to come and shout at them to buy its hours.

**Peoples and culture.** Avian Kin, light-boned and hard-eyed, feather crests from brow to shoulder and long stiff vanes down the forearm, in every color from sparrow to flame. Names are light and old (Vessaly Caelward). Current-Reading lets them feel the air's whole shape on the skin. They eat goat cheese and lilac honey, barley cakes, dried fish that has climbed four thousand steps and resents it, and thin sharp wine. Do not order eggs. A family of means leaves its front door opening onto nothing. They wear school colors with loud loyalty, like football clubs, and a child is born owing love to one. They give their dead to the wind from the bridge, feathers first. They value the step off and despise a fowler, and a fowler taken on any stair is walked to the bridge in the sight of the schools, who for that one hour wear no colors. The Long Step, the great match, runs one week a year and is fought for a dented crown called the King's Hat. They fight with the sky as a floor: the Highroost Stoop, the Long Afternoon, Nobody's Landing. The lift is the aura coat, the downdraft is Pressure, and to sense someone is to feel the draft go round him. They call anyone without feathers a groundling, with pity.

**Cities and towns.**
- **Caelspire.** Twin crags and a green-hung bridge on no pier anyone has found, the Warden's domed hall, the Stair Wardens' violet coats, and a lower town on ropes and iron pins.
- **Windstair.** The working town along the top third of four thousand cut steps, home of the Gray Kites, hoist-lodges, feather-sellers and century-old arguments.
- **Highroost.** The far peak, narrow as a tooth and brutally cold, home of the Shrikes, who hang losers up by the coat on a thorn tree.
- **The little peaks.** The Longdrop Swifts, the Old Mews and the Lilac Stair, each with a peak, a goat and a song.

**Getting there.** Eight days up the Windstair from the Midmarch farmland, a road one man wide at its worst, or baskets on the hoisters' ropes, when they are not on strike. The high passes are the only roads between the salt country and the west never cut by war, and the Caelwards take a toll. Below are the Hundred Crowns, whose kings send demands.

### The Russet Crown of the Unpapered

*Catkin, Wolfkin and Bearkin runaways, House Nobody, a Central Warlord · full page: [[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Russet Crown of the Unpapered|The Russet Crown of the Unpapered]]*

**Climate.** It has been autumn here for as long as anyone can say. Cool bright days, gold light, smoke and apples in the air every month of the year, sudden floods off the hills and a cold breeze off the inland sea, which keeps the heat of the interior away. New leaves bud, turn and fall at once on the same branch, and winter never arrives. Three surveys have explained this. The Bearkin were asked and said that it was fine.

**Habitat.** Hill country on the east of the Center, with the salt road and the Concord's uneasy flank on its west, oak, beech and great old chestnut in every shade from rust to bright gold, with leaves knee-deep on the slopes, around an inland sea of bright water. Chestnut, blackberries, apples and strong honey. Trout, deer, boar, and the fat acorn-hogs that fight back. The soil is black and sweet under a thousand years of leaves.

**Monsters and Cores.** Sasquatch (T3) in the high glades, Tanuki (T2) who pick the same pockets the Catkin do, and in the leaf-drift the long low things locals call drift-eels, that hunt by the shiver in the leaves and are the reason nobody walks at ground level after dusk. Returners, licensed hunters of runaways, are entered in the same list by local habit. The dungeon is **the Seventh Fold**, up in the hills beyond the Glade, where a Domain collapsed around a Bearkin line in an old siege and left a pocket that will not settle. The ground weighs more the further you go in and the fast get slow. Its Mouth is three days from anywhere. The Glade's people keep it, in the sense of standing near it. Crews call the whole forest **the Autumn Country**, and the Guild's list enters it as a *lying-down*, a dungeon with no roof, which the Unpapered do not care about and keep closed to surveyors.

**Peoples and culture.** Catkin, who store feeling as a reservoir and turn it to speed; Wolfkin, who run on the Pack-Soul and die slowly alone; Bearkin, Weight-Bound to their mountains, who were here first and declined to move. House Nobody is anyone who has arrived and spoken. They wear bright cloth with fur dyed in bars, stripes and handprints, and you can tell how long someone has been free by how loud they are. Chestnut bread, smoked trout, blackberries stewed with venison and cider come out on long tables, and people take a second helping on principle. Three languages share every bridge: Purrspeech, with a second conversation underneath, Moontongue, with its growl, and Urganic Growlscript, in which breakfast takes a while. Soul-names are the name of your birth and your charge (Sefa Tuen-zhiv). To become a citizen you stand at the foot of the Heap, speak your name and whatever you have lost, and whoever is nearest answers *Ashe*, I remain, and is your witness for life. Nothing is written down; keeping a list of names is the one crime they will hang you for without a hearing. The Wolfkin sing their dead into the ground at the Shrine of Nine Howls. The Bearkin keep a three-day silence for the Seventh Fold. They fight in order, Catkin first (Before You Decided), then the Nine-Howl Run, then a very large Bearkin in an apron in the middle of the ford (The Mountain Has Thought About It). The lie, the pelt and the weather are the aura coat, by people; Pressure is the old man, for whoever held the whip. Toast: "To the health of nobody." Do not ask at any stall for furs.

**Cities and towns.**
- **Brokencollar.** The seat: a great walled city of russet roofs on a headland over bright water, built by the Accord as a holding fortress with walls made to keep people in and taken by the people inside, and at its landward foot the stilt-town of nine thousand around the Heap, a rust-red hill of cut collars.
- **Shadowed Ridge.** The Catkin town, very hard to find and instantly obvious once you are in it, its map carried in the residents' heads.
- **Nightveil Hollow.** The den of thieves, forgers and retired assassins, where the master penmen walk with bodyguards.
- **Greyfang Crossing.** The Wolfkin town at the river ford on the Concord road, which holds the border.
- **The Glade of the Slumbering Root.** The Bearkin heart, stone halls among the oldest occupied places in the world.

**Getting there.** By the Long Way, a chain of safe barns, friendly ferrymen and bought clerks from Verath to Harrowgate and beyond. By the Concord road to Greyfang, with papers from Nightveil. By boat across the inland sea. Below the Charter's edge the salt road itself runs with the forest eaves on its east and the Hem on its west. Some of the high roads over the Violet Peaks run runaways too, for love or for a fee, depending on the school. Inside the eaves the law of 668 holds and a returner cannot be paid, so the border is a matter of feet.

## The South

The land turns green below the Center and drops in long steps to a warm sea. Sum-gol was once the empire and dynasty of the whole South, and it broke up in an afternoon in 602. The Yan and the Ryu stayed with the Dynasty; Lambert, Yukari, Corwen and Marrick stand apart. It is loud country, all market bells, temple gongs, cedar smoke and orange blossom, and northerners call it soft until they have fought in it. The South's nine years of the Turn run by the season, on contract, with subscription lists at Alabaster and the Marrick companies on the Hem; it last held them from 703 to 712.

### The Ochre Fields of Hammerford

*House Marrick · full page: [[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Ochre Fields of Hammerford|The Ochre Fields of Hammerford]]*

**Climate.** The northern edge of the warm belt, and the coldest of the South: hot, dry, barley-gold summers with a big sky, a dusty wind from the Center in August, and thunderstorms that walk across the country in plain sight. Autumn is long and fine, the campaigning season's last and the drill-season's first. Winter brings hard frost, mud and a little snow, and fifty thousand people crowd inside Hammerford's walls to wait it out.

**Habitat.** Flat and enormous, deep loess soil between the Grey Plain and the marches of the Center, with the Scourge's rim a long ride north of the last company's stakes, green country and blue mountains on the far edge around the city. Barley, the color of a lion in summer, with onions and rows of ash poles grown for spears. After harvest the stubble is laid out in ochre and black squares, each a company's drill ground, marked with limed stakes and tarred rope. Hares, bustards, foxes, swifts over the tent lines, and kites following the drums for scraps.

**Monsters and Cores.** Stilts come down off the Scourge rim in bad years and walk through the barley, and the companies take the contract at a rate fixed by the Moot. Neiman Lions (T4) cross from the plains after horses. Great flocks of crows make a nuisance of themselves at any muster. The dungeon is **the Long Barrow at Sorley Bridge**: nine thousand dead went into one trench there in the autumn of 631, on both sides, and in the generation after it the trench turned, a Mortalis seep (the Wellspring of slow death, venous black-red) pooling under so many men paid to the end of the season. Ghouls (T4) and Skeletal Folk (T4) come up out of it in companies, in step. Every spring the Moot lets a clearing contract, recruits do their first real fighting there, and it has been cleared sixty times and never stayed cleared.

**Peoples and culture.** Spearmen and barley farmers, English and Dutch in name (Gerrit, Marget, Dob), in ochre and black squares, with company colors inside them. Land is held by company, and a family pays its rent in sons and daughters to each muster. They eat barley bread, onions, sour beer, blood sausage, hard yellow cheese and a stew called *the sergeant*. They go to all the funerals, because everyone knows someone in every company, and a cut of every contract goes to the Widows' Chest. Every recruit touches the tailboard of the broken cart at the ford on the morning of her first muster. They value the contract, to the last day of the season and not one past it, and they despise whoever breaks a rank under contract, and whoever touches a company walking off. The holiday of the last Calling, the day in 602 when their company grounded arms at noon in front of the Emperor, is the greatest in their year; the Moot at midsummer is the next. They fight in the rank, sixteen deep with long ash spears and drums and crossbows on the wings, and you stand in it because the man on your left is standing in it. A high-Stage fighter who cuts in is let in, closed on and shot. The shine and the weight are aura and Pressure. They joke about everything and the dead most of all, told out of the side of the mouth in the last minute before contact. They say a Lambert will tell you there are four thousand and eleven of you and a Marrick will tell you it's plenty.

**Cities and towns.**
- **Hammerford.** A great walled city of rose-brown stone, tall pale citadel, the Hiring Hall with this season's prices on its wall, and the broken cart by the ford, repaired nine times by sworn drawing.
- **Spearmoot.** At the center of the Fields, a large meadow with a ring of old stones and a town of inns, where captains vote, fix rates and break each other's noses in the traditional order. Every Marrick and most maps call it Pikemoot.
- **Musterfield.** A square league of beaten earth with a permanent street of sutlers, raw, loud and cheerful, with very few old men in it.

**Getting there.** The cobbled south road comes up to Hammerford's gatehouse. The Kessen road passes on the west on its way to Sum-gol, and the northern roads run up into the Midmarch. A line of boundary stones and an annual brawl at Mashi mark the Grey Plain.

### The Vermilion Dynasty of Sum-gol

*House Mahuo · full page: [[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Vermilion Dynasty of Sum-gol|The Vermilion Dynasty of Sum-gol]]*

**Climate.** Warm and windy, drier than the coast, with a long hot summer that makes the pans work and short mild winters with sour rain. The evening wind off the salt tastes of what you would be paying for anywhere else. In the iron hills it is colder and bare. Up the river in Breath Hollow the air is soft and wet over green shelves. At the Stubble-Burn, after harvest, the whole hollow smokes for three days.

**Habitat.** The southwest corner of the Scourge's rim country: rust-red, bare-backed iron hills to the north, salt pans shining flat to the south in rows of white and rose, and rice terraces climbing a river valley in green shelves behind the city. Ore and cinnabar (the red ore that made the vermilion) lie in the hills, and a dye works stains the river for a mile. Crows on every cornice, herons on the pans, wild goats on the hills, and somewhere up there a great white stag.

**Monsters and Cores.** Since the Soul Plane broke, hauntings have spread across the South and the dead drift, and the Post will lay a haunting, exorcise a house or clear Well-creatures where the dead have soured a site, each job priced and recorded. Ghouls work the empty quarters. Brine-walkers, pale long-legged things that follow the rakers at dusk, are the salt pans' own contribution. The **Baengnok**, the White Stag, bound power of the old line, walked out by the East Gate in 602 and has not been summoned since, and hill people leave it alone. The dungeon is **the Answering Vaults**, under the Palace square, where the great summoners of the old line kept whole bestiaries in bound vessels against the next Calling. Nobody living has the roll. Scavengers in painted hoods go down for the gold and bring up reports of a host still standing in rows, waiting to be called. **The Ninth Adit** at Cheolsan is the other, a worked Chthonica site (grip and ground, in the Materia Family) under the iron mountain, with the Eighth sealed and its accounts carried under seal, and the smiths have stopped asking what the Ninth is being fed.

**Peoples and culture.** Fifteen thousand in streets laid out for a hundred and fifty, Korean in register, given name and generation-syllable with the family name before or after (Kwon Mu-jin, Cozbi Mahuo). They eat rice and barley with salted radish and fermented bean, blood soup at the Post-House gate, grilled river eel and sour greens. A nursemaid teaches a child to count the ox's breathing before she teaches it the copper. Everyone but the Post-Master wears vermilion, a sash or a rag or a dyed hat, and the Post wears olive and white. Mahuo burial law stands alone in not requiring the family to keep the body. The dead are the house's business now: "We keep the dead. Nobody else would take them." They value a sealed bag and despise a robbed courier, and Post law is the only law everyone respects. The autumn Calling still runs, an old herald on the Balcony of Answers reading the roll to a cheerful crowd that has come for the food. Summoning is the old art. Riders carry the hwando, a short curved saber across the shoulders, and pour rings of salt. Gabot is the aura coat, nureum is Pressure, nunchi is sensing. Every joke comes round to attendance: a husband home late "sent a very good letter."

**Cities and towns.**
- **Sum-gol.** Gold domes stripped on their north faces, pale twisting towers, a palace still hung with vermilion and white, and a journeyman living in a duke's kitchen for the price of keeping it standing.
- **Cheolsan.** "Iron mountain," a red stone hill town of smiths and fire-setters and a vile black beer.
- **Yeomjeon.** "Salt field," a low white town on stilts at the pans, rakers with linen on their feet.
- **Breath Hollow.** The old valley the name comes from, sixty roofs and a teaching house.

**Getting there.** The Kessen road ends here, and everything moving between North and South passes this gate or goes a long way round. The warded track runs to Senri's Gate, one hundred and forty leagues. Olive-coated riders change horses at the old imperial stations and never close. East and south lie the Wellheads and the Grey Plain, and the salt goes along the coast by Ryu barge.

### The Sapphire Wells of Brightwater

*House Corwen · full page: [[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Sapphire Wells of Brightwater|The Sapphire Wells of Brightwater]]*

**Climate.** High, wet and luminous, and warm underfoot whatever the air is doing. Rain and mist are the ordinary weather, steam stands over everything each morning and goes gold when the sun comes through, and snow on the upper terraces melts as it lands. Winter is damp, cheap and full of invalids. Summer is the season, bright between the showers, with the gorge a hard blazing blue.

**Habitat.** Upland spring country at the heart of the South, between the iron hills and the Jade Plains, of hot springs and cold springs side by side, pale stone terraced down the hills, every pool a different color from milk-jade to rust. A drowned gorge at the head of it is filled by the springs and colored by their minerals, with white stacks and arches standing in the water. Below, the land falls to the river, the cask road and the western edge of the Grey Plain. Watercress, plum, fat sheep, trout and shameless kingfishers.

**Monsters and Cores.** Every kept pool is a worked Core, and most were cleared dungeons once, which is what the coin in a keeper's purse is really paying for. A keeper can seal a site in under an hour. Sump-toads breed in the abandoned pools, Glarewings are rare and much bet on, and Hotwell's boiling bowl has a resident Sea Serpent's smaller cousin that nobody admits to. The dungeon the keepers show you is **the Hotwell Stair**, boiling springs going down beneath the bath town, worked for so long that invalids boil eggs in it. The one they do not show you is **the Long Copper**, a sapphire pool at the gorge's head so deep and clear you can watch a dropped copper fall for a long time, and then keep watching. A Phreatis Well (the Wellspring of water under the ground, copper-green) lies under it. Divers from the Steps have gone down on a rope for two centuries, and a Corwen keeper holds a key to the top landing and no idea of the bottom.

**Peoples and culture.** A chartered family, big, cheerful and sharp-eyed, English in name (Maud, Odo, Annis Blewitt), over coopers, dyers, brass-founders, bath-keepers and hedge-workers. Everything is cooked in the springs since the fuel is free: eggs boiled in a net, trout steamed over the vent, sulfur-tasting black bread baked in hot sand, pickled plums, a whole sheep lowered into a pool in a basket on feast days. They wear kingfisher blue and brass, with brass keys at the keepers' belts, and "blue to the elbow" means someone who has fought the management and lived. They value the lease and despise a false assay, the worst crime in the country. Their dead go in the hot ground, with no record of anyone in Hotwell ever having been sober at a funeral. A wellmouth is their church, and they run on the hum of the kept pools, so that their children cannot sleep elsewhere. The Blue Bracket on the Hotwell raft fields the Steps Dyers, the Brightwater Coopers and the Hotwell Dregs, who have never won anything and have the best songs in the South. They rarely fight; they close a door, or hire from Hammerford, and a hedge-worker salts the ground under you the night before. Since the war the springs in the south have gone bad, nobody will say why, and everyone knows the joke, which is to raise a cup and say, *After you, Maud.*

**Cities and towns.**
- **Brightwater.** Twenty-five thousand on a white stair of red-tiled houses, rock stacks and arches over sapphire water, with the Warden's brass doors, the Cask Yards and the barge road.
- **Hotwell.** The bath town, cheap and bawdy, a fighting ring on a raft and eggs boiled in the gutters.
- **Kingfisher Steps.** Thirty-one tiers of dyers, glassmen and invalids in chairs, gutters running in stripes.

**Getting there.** The cask road and the river go down to the Plain by barge, and from there the casks go north by brass-bound cart, round the Scourge by one corridor or the other. A wet road goes up through mist from the Grey Plain, with brass-bound carts in both directions. Iron hills lie to the west of the uplands.

### The Jade Plains of Qingyuan

*The Yan Hall, in the Dynasty · full page: [[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Jade Plains of Qingyuan|The Jade Plains of Qingyuan]]*

**Climate.** Big-sky temperate grassland: warm and windy, rain coming across in long gray curtains you can watch for a day before it arrives, hot bright summers and cold clear winters with a dusting of snow that blows off. The wind turns the blades over and the plain goes pale, which is why northerners wrote *the Grey Plain* on their maps. In the low sun after rain it turns the exact color of the stone in the spring at Yuquan.

**Habitat.** Silver-green grass, waist high by midsummer, in long swells from the Wellheads across to the foot of the cedar country, with still pools, wildflowers and low stone walls on the valley floors, and waterfalls off vast curved mountains at the far rim. Deep black soil. There are no trees to speak of, a great many larks, and more sky than most visitors can stand for the first week. Tall gray horses, sheep, felt and mare's milk. Foxes, marmots, wild asses, steppe eagles.

**Monsters and Cores.** Neiman Lions (T4) hunt the plain with a stunning roar that has cost the Hall a great many foals, and every stud has a bounty standing. Hippogrifs come down from the rim mountains and are shot on sight or sold. The cold spring at Yuquan is a small worked Core, kept by the Elder of Tablets. The dungeon is **the Ninth Paddock**: in the Hall's old ledgers there was a stud east of Qingyuan whose compound sank into the plain in a single night, walls, roofs and tablets, into a hole no one had known was a Well. On a still dusk, tall horses can be heard calling from the ground. The Hall enters the stock every year as *held*, has sent three parties to read out their names, and got back one of the horses.

**Peoples and culture.** A lineage hall in the old southern style, Chinese in register, nine rows of ancestral tablets with a title for every rung. A Yan states his rung before his business. They eat hand-pulled noodles, mutton on the bone with cumin and salt, steamed buns the size of a fist, tea with butter and salt from a bowl at the saddle, and sour mare's milk spirit at the fall gather, which everyone regrets. They wear gray and jade felt, with a running horse in silver. Their dead go to the tablets, and the ancestors watch every dispute. They value the bloodline and the ledger, and they despise a poor seat. A horse will come home if the right person reads out its name, and the penalty for stealing one turns on how well you rode. The Lantern Fair at midsummer is their great feast, the fall gather their debt-paying and drinking. They fight as horse archers shooting backward at the full gallop, riding around you and taking you apart from the edges. Hùtǐ, wēiyā and shénshí are the scholars' words for coat, Pressure and sensing; a boy with a good coat is "shining like a wet gray." Stable-sects run the brackets: the Silver Bridles, the Black Girths, the Yuquan Waders. They joke about titles and tell one against themselves, in which the gatekeeper of the next world gives up around the third row.

**Cities and towns.**
- **Qingyuan.** Forty thousand in a square of tamped-earth walls faced in pale stone, streets wide enough for sixty horses abreast, the pale many-towered Great Hall of Tablets on its rise, and an empty stall swept yearly for the Emperor's horse.
- **Yuquan.** "Jade spring," where the whole town wades in with sieves after every flood and carvers cut the small jade horses every Yan child gets at its naming.
- **Mashi.** "Horse market," a wide empty field for most of the year and for nine weeks a mile of tents, with races at noon and sunset and on any provocation in between.

**Getting there.** Salt and grain come across from the salt road on the east, paying at every toll. The old imperial road comes in from Sum-gol, and the Sallowgate marches lie on the south. Boundary stones on the north with the Long Fields. Buyers come from four quarters. Horses sold from here have a habit of walking back.

### The Silver Cedars of Yukari

*House Yukari, the Crow's Reach · full page: [[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Silver Cedars of Yukari|The Silver Cedars of Yukari]]*

**Climate.** The ground climbs into cloud and the cloud stays. The valleys are cool, soaking and green-gold, with mist most mornings, rain most evenings and silver lichen furring every north face. Winters bring a wet heavy snow on the hills and frost in the valleys. A warm basin at Vaeloris runs off an old vent. The Reach proper is another country: it runs out north and east toward the salt road and the old ruins of the Rose Coils, cold upland with long winters, sharp autumns and new mud.

**Habitat.** Cedars so tall and close that light comes down in thin bars on moss and black water, limestone that goes gold at evening, and the Heartstone valleys six hundred feet above the country around them. On the low valley floor below the Enclave lies a drowned shrine country, mossy dark pagodas and carved spires standing in still water under pale waterfalls, lily pads and long red streamers. Further out are long lakes, birch, cold upland and raw timber towns with sap weeping from the boards. Lake-margin grain, bitter tea, bark paper and high-altitude herbs grow here. Crows in hundreds of thousands, otters, lake fish.

**Monsters and Cores.** The wildlife of the Thornback March has been marinating in high-density Aether for centuries and has opinions about territorial boundaries. Tanuki (T2) cheat travelers on the valley roads. Psy-Apes (T4) troop in the upland cedars and everyone in earshot suddenly knows what they ate. A perimeter reads an entrant's intent through the rock, which tells you when something is coming and not what to do about it. The Uplands hold the land's highest natural seeps. The dungeon is **the Drowned Shrines**, that whole flooded country below the Enclave, kept and never cleared, the house declining all commissions, and the water a good deal deeper than the valley. Inside it stands **the Unlit Shrine**, a pagoda whose lower stories go on down under the water past where any reflection stops. A Yukari thread-reader will not look at it for long. The house has not told anyone what is in it, and what the Yukari know may leave the valley to mend and never to take.

**Peoples and culture.** An archaic bloodline first and a house second, its members all volunteers after the Telling, the whole account of what the blood carries and what it costs, heard on the cold floor before Senri's stone. Names are two-element compounds, up to five forms in a life (Senri, Naiser). More than half the Reach is human of mixed frontier stock, about one in six Yukari by blood or name, about one in seven Beastkin, otter-folk prominent among them, with Marrowkin and Mahuo besides. They eat broth with root vegetables and dried lake fish, dumplings, forest game and bitter tea, and "You haven't eaten," said by whoever loves you, does most of the work in a household. Black and silver for the house, crow-black harness for the Thread Guard, chalk-white aprons for the carvers' boys. The dead are laid in rows facing east. They value proof of work; every post re-Proves every six years in public with odds on every seat, and they despise "absent," a neglectful holder. The greeting on the road is "Who keeps your posts?" Festivals: the Proving Festival at the solstice, the Ward-Lighting at the autumn equinox with chalk thrown in the street, and the midwinter Thread-Reading. Courts ask what was connected and what must be restored. They fight quietly, long fingers and flat feet, moving a thread before drawing a blade. Their jokes are about being looked at: "ledger-eyed," "blind crow," and how many carvers it takes to fix a gate.

**Cities and towns.**
- **The Cedar Enclave.** The seat on its hill, cedar halls, raked white gravel, three standing stones that throw no shadow, and since the war the warlords' table.
- **Karasuyama.** "Crow mountain," a bare limestone peak and rookery town where the sky goes black for an hour on autumn dusks.
- **Sugidani.** "Cedar valley," the market town of sawpits, ink-grinders and bark paper, with the only decent inn.
- **Senri's Gate.** Capital of the Reach, grown around the old standing stones, with the Council, the Ward-Carver's Academy and the Archive of Provings.
- **Vaeloris.** A warm stone-built basin, the proving-house for new ward-work.
- **Reedgate.** A station on the Long Water in the Lake Districts, famous for a pike.
- **The Thornback Ridge.** A forward post at the end of the perimeter, on the northern frontier.

**Getting there.** The warded track from Sum-gol, the Kessen road's last stretch in all but name, with a way-station every twenty to twenty-five leagues, three weeks by wagon, to the foot of the valleys. The Corridor runs north beside the salt road toward the Nalūn basin. Wards touch whoever comes up the southern road long before the gate has seen him.

### The Holy Sea of Alabaster

*House Lambert and the Court · full page: [[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Holy Sea of Alabaster|The Holy Sea of Alabaster]]*

**Climate.** Warm-temperate coast. Long dry bright summers with a sea breeze every noon, mild wet winters, autumn gales that come off the water, and huge clouds piling gray-blue and rose over a ring of misty mountains. Fog sits on the shelf at dawn. Inland and up, it gets colder fast, and at the top of the land marches the frost hardly breaks.

**Habitat.** White cliffs the color of milk dropping into water so clear the fishing boats seem to hang in the air, and drowned country on either flank of the bay where on a still morning sponge divers can show you the tops of walls. Behind the shelf, lemon terraces, goat hill, olives and thin chalky barley climb into the land marches and north to the edge of the Grey Plain. Sardines, sponges, gulls, sallow willows in the ditches. It is poor in grain and rich in everything that can be written down. Marrowchalk barges (bone-rock that doubles a wheat yield and also raises the dead) come through its lawyers.

**Monsters and Cores.** Zombies (T2) get up in fields dressed too heavily with Marrowchalk, and the parish has a form for it. Sirens (T4) work the rocks off the point, and two men hold the Lone Clerk's lamp and watch each other. Sea Serpents (T6) are a bad year's news. The ground under the shelf is the great working site, which the Court calls *Attestis*, a word on no Guild list, and which Measurewrights read most respectably as Thaloré, the Wellspring of Depth. It is why a true oath wets the stone. The dungeon is **the Drowned Parishes**, the sunken low land on both sides of the bay since the blow of about 310 IC. The church bells are still in their towers. Mourning Pike (T5) hold the naves, the souls of tens of thousands died mid-oath on that ground, and the Veil-Saints will tell you why they charge what they do. Nobody has cleared a single parish, though the Archive still holds the deeds.

**Peoples and culture.** The most mixed crowd on the continent, because the ground will not distinguish one race's oath from another's: Serpentkin scribes, Catkin sailors, Tinker Goblins and Eressean pilgrims on one landing. Court Latin is the working tongue and French-sounding names are the fashion (Aurélien Cassard, Léonard Lambert), and every lord who comes before the Court gets a Latin name he did not ask for. They eat sardines off a skewer, goat cheese with lemon salt, chickpea cakes, barley soup with fish heads, and almond paste molded into a balance on feast days, eaten from the pan that holds the lies. White and ink-black are the Court's colors, and a black balance on a white door means someone inside can ask your name. Faith is the Church of the Holy Sea, its saints and its Archive. Veil-Saints perform Sacraments over the dying so that the soul crosses whole, and you can hire one by the night. They value the sworn account and the true count and despise a dry foot; to be *dry-footed* is to be a liar. The Manumission Doctrine protects the counted, and only the counted. Festivals: the Feast of Saint Ebb with pennons and frying sardines, and bracket days between the Whites of the Upper Stair and the Inks of the Laundry Steps. Small drilled levies paid on the day, crossbow companies on the Stair, Hammerford spears hired and audited, and paper for the larger wars: excommunication from the registers. Dockers say the shine and the weight. They tell the one about the Palatine turned back at the gate of the next world for a discrepancy.

**Cities and towns.**
- **Alabaster.** Sixty thousand on eleven hundred steps, pale walls and red roofs stacked from a blue bay, the Sounding Walk, the Bridge of Notaries, the Archive cut into the shelf, and the Lone Clerk on its rock.
- **Rimward.** The harbor on the bay's western arm, sail lofts, slave-blocks and sponge boats, a loud salt town that thinks the upper city is a pack of hypocrites and supplies it with fish.
- **Sallowgate.** The fat walled landward gate where the salt road's western branch comes in, a stone bear over the arch, the South's most accurate water clock, and Lamberts learning the Sallowgate Answer.

**Getting there.** By the bay, if you can afford the dues. By the salt road's western branch to Sallowgate. The Copper Coast lies east along the shore, and Rimward is the only harbor on the southern sea that can hurt it. Inland and up, broken walls, a well and a gatepost mark the old Kōkan seat, where pilgrims are advised not to go, and go anyway (see [[#Where the Maps Quarrel]]).

### The Copper Coast of Haeseong

*House Ryu, in the Dynasty · full page: [[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Copper Coast of Haeseong|The Copper Coast of Haeseong]]*

**Climate.** The warmest and wettest on the mainland, subtropical and shining. It rains hard for ten minutes every afternoon and everyone stands in doorways making remarks about it. Summers are sticky and salt-hazed, with autumn typhoons that come up off the warm sea and wreck the outer Moorings, and winters so soft that camellias bloom on the walls. The water goes from sea-green to copper at evening.

**Habitat.** The **Thousand Moorings**, a run of inlets, sandbars and pine-topped islets east of the white cliffs, with paddies and salt-ponds behind and old copper workings in the hills staining the creeks green. Rice, sea-silk, mackerel, shrimp, sea-urchin, pearl-beds. Cormorants, herons, otters in the creeks, and a shore that smells of salt, tar, charcoal and frying fish.

**Monsters and Cores.** Sea Serpents (T6) follow the mackerel, and every Ryu fleet has lost hulls off the Sawbacks. Sirens work the outer islets, and everyone on the water has a story and a net-charm. Kraken (T9) are named in old wreck-right and nobody has lately had the nerve to file a claim. The dungeon is **the Green Galleries**, the old copper workings in the hills behind Geumpo, which broke into a Basilithe Well (the Wellspring of pure pitch and clean crystal, ochre-gold). Tolling Crabs (T5) hold the Upper Workings, and a careful delver strikes a tuning bar and rings a crab's own note back at it until the carapace falls apart. Geumpo's pilots hold a lease on the Mouth. The Ryu dispute it annually. **The Gold Mouth**, a sea cave east of Geumpo on Oblation ground (a warm Fluxia Wellspring whose law runs on what is given up to it), is unlicensed and very busy: a tide cave that pulls, used by smugglers, with House Ryu claiming wreck-right on anything that washes out, crews included.

**Peoples and culture.** Korean in register (Ryu Ha-eun, Gim Dol-soe, Deok-sun), harbor people in sea-green and copper silk or tar-stained cotton, thousands of them living on sampans and paying mooring-fee by the lunar month to a harbor-mother who knows who is sleeping with whom. They eat grilled mackerel, rice, cold noodles in broth, salted shrimp with everything, sea-urchin if just paid, and a clear rice liquor visitors take for water exactly once. Each village keeps a shrine to the drowned, with a toll-man who takes his hat off to funerals and charges for them anyway. They value a good letter and despise being shown the wrong set of books. Every evening the lanterns go up on fishing line from mast to mast and roof to roof across the harbor, red, gold and copper-green, as they have since 671, and the city comes out on the mole to eat. Haeseong children set down anywhere in the world cannot sleep in the dark. They fight at sea, with windward, currents and marine crossbows, shooting the rowers first, and they have never fought a land battle they could avoid by raising a price. Bracket schools row out to fight on lashed barges: the Haeseong Green Tide, the Geumpo Gilders and the Bad Weather. Gabot, nureum and nunchi, as at Sum-gol, and nunchi also means knowing when to change the subject. "Held for the Throne" is what a Haeseong man says about whatever he has just put under his coat.

**Cities and towns.**
- **Haeseong.** Seventy thousand, the largest living city in the South, hundreds of roofs in copper new and old climbing from a stone quay, a dark fortress on a green crag, the white-and-gold palace of the old Dynasty's governor on the next rise, and the Long Mole with its great lighthouse.
- **Geumpo.** "Gold harbor," a day east, a smaller, prettier, more dishonest place where things without papers unload, with the better bracket team.
- **The Moorings.** Some hundreds of salt-ponds, fishing villages and customs posts, each with a toll-man.

**Getting there.** The salt road begins at the Salt Quay and runs north, past Bothhands, across the Sealed Charter, up the Scourge's east flank and through the salt basin to Ashmore's junction, with walled toll-forts every thirty miles. By water from anywhere, since everything that comes into the Inner World by sea comes through House Ryu by their own account. Foreign and island ships unload at Geumpo. Ryu barges carry the Dynasty's salt in from Yeomjeon along the coast.

## Offshore

### The Crimson Isles of Vorruk-Khal

*Hobgoblins of the Crimson Order, no king · full page: [[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/Isles and Archipelagos/The Hobgoblin Expanse|The Crimson Isles of Vorruk-Khal]]*

**Climate.** Warm, maritime and volcanic. Steady trade winds and tropical rain on the windward slopes, a dry lee, and fine warm seas going from ink to jade over the shelves. Ash falls some years, the sky over Gorr-Keth goes smith's-apron orange and bruised violet when the mountain has a mind, and nobody has ever approved an awning. Deep cold is rare enough that the Isles' soldiers learned about it from a Greymane winter.

**Habitat.** Forty-three islands in a rough crescent. Seven High Islands with peaks above 2,000 feet and deep volcanic soil, terraced with taro, breadfruit, volcanic-ash grain and thurr-kep, a root tasting of charcoal mixed with honey. Twenty-two Shelf Islands of forge, quarry and sulfur crust. Fourteen Low Reefs of kelp and fish. Black beaches, yellow vents, green terraces up every caldera wall, sea birds in millions, turtles, reef fish.

**Monsters and Cores.** Sea Serpents hunt the kelp lines and are entered as fishing disputes. Ash-crabs, shore-walking things with hot shells, come out after an eruption and eat what the ash killed. The Isles' Essence runs on what Hobgoblin scholars call the arc's own magmatic source, and the Low Reefs have vent-forges the Marrowkin taught them to build. The dungeon is **Skaal-Mol**, one of the three forbidden islands where the ground shakes at a frequency that makes Stone-Blood vibrate at the wrong harmonic, and nobody enforces the prohibition because nobody needs to. Tinker crews and foreign delvers land there anyway for the glass, down a lava tube that goes on past any rope, and a Bugbear can tell you from the hair on her scalp that they will not be back. **The Moot Vent** at Khal-Serrat is the worked one, a Pyreveil site (banked-coal heat, in the Caloria Family) that young hobgoblins of the Order walk down to earn a voice at the moot, its keepers being whoever is left.

**Peoples and culture.** Hobgoblins of the Crimson Order in scarlet, geometric etchings on the skin, under Vow of Silence below Stage VII, arguing in Silent Sign-Glyphs with a sign-caller speaking for them; Bugbears, seven to eight feet, charcoal-dark, reef families who regard the moot with tolerant disinterest; Tinker Goblins with four guild-seats and no vote on war; Fleshshapers with their coats left open to show the work. Goblin bellows-gangs in the deep yards under Gorr-Keth hold no seat and no hearth. Old Vaross is law and Gorr-Vaross runs at sub-bass. Names are hard Vaross compounds (Vaal-Gorrum, Sahl-Venn). They eat taro, breadfruit and Shelf fish, and strangers are offered thurr-kep so that everyone can watch. A Bugbear taking on a responsibility carries a stone to the nearest summit, and when a known Bugbear dies every Bugbear in earshot stops speaking for a day without meaning to. They value the roll and the Rock and despise a king. Every spring the Speaker recites the standing law and reads the Toll Entry, the old bill against the North. They fight almost never first, with geography and fresh water, then glass-sheathed hulls, the Phalanx of the Soul, The Red Wall Takes One Step, and Bugbears planted on a beach. *Ak-gresh* is the aura coat. All their jokes are about procedure, and "May your motion carry" is a curse.

**Cities and towns.**
- **Khal-Serrat.** The moot-isle, a great terraced city of rust-red and black rock, every wall ending in spikes, red banners with nothing on them, and the Rock's sheer carved cliff above a moot floor under open sky.
- **Krev-Mol.** A dawn fish market under strings of blue-glass floats, and the Fleshshaper quarter, and the Breakwater brackets.
- **Mokk-Vesh.** The great shipyard, volcanic-glass hulls and Tinkers being told to stop helping.
- **Gorr-Keth.** The oldest caldera towns on a mountain that glows at night, with the slag-yards underneath.

**Getting there.** By ship only, south past the sea, and the shipmasters of Rimward and Haeseong make the crossing for brimstone and glass, paid in weight rather than coin. On the Kharven side of the Greymane wall they sell to nobody, and Mandate loads land on the Vorrak's Reach coast. Rooms on the Shelf are rented by the season and on moot days every room is let twice.

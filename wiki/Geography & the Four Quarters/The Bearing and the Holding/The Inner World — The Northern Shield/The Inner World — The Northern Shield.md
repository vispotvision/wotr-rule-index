# The Inner World — The Northern Shield

**Say it** · the IN-er WURLD

*One long continent, pinched in the middle by the Scourge of Hell: the North on its old cold rock, the Center on the two roads around the wound, and the South going down green to the sea*

> *Go north until the ground stops wanting you and south until the sea does. If anyone offers you the short way through the middle, count your fingers before you shake on it.*
>
> Painted on a map-seller's board at Halcrest

A smuggler called Long Inga Rawe took forty sledges of Nalūn salt out across the ice of Long Scratch, the greatest of the chain-lakes, in the hard winter of 661 IC, to dodge the weighing at Halcrest. She went by night with the northern lights running green and rose overhead, every driver singing to keep the dogs steady, and she had paid a hedge-worker two hams to charm the ice. The charm held for eleven miles. The twelfth mile had no interest in hams. A seam opened from shore to shore, wide enough to swallow a chapel, and twenty-six sledges, nine hundred bars and fifteen men went down into the black water with their lanterns still lit, so that for some while the whole lake glowed from underneath.

Rawe walked ashore with frostbite and a sled-dog under each arm. All that salt melted where it lay, the perch of Long Scratch came up pickled for three winters, and the lakeside villages held the finest fish feast in their history and then took her to law for fouling their water. She paid them off in installments, in salt, legally weighed at Halcrest, until she died.

The Inner World in a breath: the oldest ground there is at one end, soaked in Essence, worth a fortune and trying to kill you on the way to market; a warm green country falling to the sea at the other; and between them a Center that lives on two roads around a wasteland nobody crosses, and gets marched over by both. Bring a sledge and a lawyer. The shelf still files all of it under the Northern Shield, after the rock its first surveyors stood on. That rock accounts for the top third of the map, and the Guild has been meaning to change the label since before the ink dried.

## The Lie of the Land

The Inner World is one continent, long from north to south and pinched in the middle by the [[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Scourge of Hell|Scourge of Hell]]. Read it from the top down.

**The top** is the Polar Reach, tundra and permafrost past the treeline, which the heralds call the Aurora Reach.

**The North** is a shield of old cold rock. Kharven holds the high plateau at its center. West, the Ridgewall of the Slate Ridge closes the border, and beyond it lies Undaar-Keth in black basalt and boreal forest. East, the Ironwood March and the Scarlet Wood run out to the Ring, where Marrowhold's rib mountains stand. South, the land drops into the Greenhollow, and the Painted Rivers carry everything between.

**The Center** is the Scourge of Hell, which fills the middle of the continent, and whatever can live on its edges. Nobody marches through it, so all traffic between North and South goes around it by two corridors, the **Kessen road** on the west flank and the **salt road** on the east. Those two corridors are where the armies go and where the Center's people live. The Amber Cities of Nalūn lie on the Scourge's north side, in the dead sea's salt basin, with Halcrest at the gate where the beds thin against the northern rim. The Hundred Crowns crowd along both roads, with Kingsbridge on the Kessen road. The Sealed Charter sits at the east road's crossing. The Moonlight Forest lies on the west flank around its great lake, the Violet Peaks climb on the northwest, the Rose Coils on the old ruins of the southeast edge, and the Russet Crown on the east around the inland sea.

**The South** turns green and drops in long steps to the sea. The Long Fields of the Ochre Fields come first, just below the Center. The iron hills and salt pans of Sum-gol lie west, at the Kessen road's end, the Yukari valleys climb into cloud on the east, the Jade Plains open in the middle, and the spring country of the Wellheads steams in the uplands between them. At the bottom stands the white shelf of the Holy Sea of Alabaster, with the Copper Coast warm to its east, where the salt road starts.

**Offshore**, the Crimson Isles lie south, past the sea.

**What will eat you.** Monsters move, and they live across the whole of this map, with beasts more widespread still, so every realm below has its own bad road and its own song about it. The Scourge of Hell is the one area known for extremely dangerous monsters, and the North, the South and the Center each take a nine-year [[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Scourge of Hell#The Turn|Turn]] at keeping them off the other two, which all three resent in writing. Wellspring Cores (sites where one of the sixty kinds of Aether pools) mostly lie in [[The Bestiary/Dungeons|dungeons]] and are also said to lie in vast territories, and every realm has at least one dungeon people talk about, cleared or not.

For the weather, the roads, the towns and who will cheat you, see the [[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Inner World — A Gazetteer|Gazetteer]].

## The Ledger of the Shield

> The oldest exposed rock in the Material World, and it looks it. **Superb bearing stock, high ambient Essence, and not one road you would call finished.**

This is the Guild's old materials ledger, and it covers the rock under the North. The Center and the South keep their ground on their own pages.

## Formation

Beneath the soil, where there is soil, lies a continuous basement of gneiss and granite that was already ancient when the western mountains were first raised. It has been planed flat by successive ice, scoured down to bare rock across enormous stretches, and left dimpled with chain-lakes lying in the scratch-marks the ice left behind.

Two features break the monotony and both of them matter.
> **The Ring.** A circular structure some ninety leagues across whose rim mountains are the only relief on the eastern shield, out where the North runs out. The Measurewrights hold, on the evidence of shocked quartz and an inverted rock sequence at the rim, that this is **an impact scar and not a volcanic caldera**, and that the sheet of melted rock beneath the basin floor is where the northern quarter's richest sulfide ore was concentrated as it cooled.
>
> The [[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Ember Holds of Marrowhold|Marrowkin]], who live there, do not dispute the quartz. They add that the rim mountains are the color of old ivory and curve in a line a butcher would recognize, that they are ribs, and that what struck the ground that day was already dead when it landed. The Guild keeps three accounts of what the body was, none of them agreeing with the Marrowkin or with each other. The Yukari record, kept by people whose carvers once minded wards all over the shield, adds that the **passages** (the thin places in the Veil) of that country occur only within the ring, and invites the reader to draw the obvious conclusion about what a strike of that magnitude does to the local thinness of the Veil.
>
> *This ledger records the correlation and declines to assert the cause.*
**The northern shield Basin** on the southeastern margin, a sealed evaporite trough where an ancient sea dried out under salt beds two hundred fathoms thick. This is where the North gives out and the Center begins, and the Amber Cities of Nalūn are cut into that salt. **Its wealth is what the tunnels are made of.**

## The Ground Itself

Bare rock the color of old pewter, moss as green as a bottle, and standing water that throws back the whole sky in the interior. Boreal forest across the southern third, dense, resinous, broad-leafed, going up in scarlet and gold every autumn and growing back hard over the stump fields the frontier left. The roads are new and mostly bad.

Settlements arrive as clusters of raw timber with sap still weeping from the wall boards, ringed by fifty yards of cut stumps, with crude three-layer ward work on the gate posts **that any journeyman Engraver can maintain and no journeyman Engraver is proud of.** They have a bracket ring before they have a chapel, two rival sects recruiting at opposite ends of the same tavern, and a herald who comes through once a month and is believed about half the time.

Ambient Wellspring saturation across the shield runs high and steady, which visitors feel as a low pressure behind the eyes within a day of arriving. A scholar calls this rich ground and the street calls it fat, and a practitioner standing on it pulls Aether straight out of the rock and refills faster than almost anywhere else in the world. It is the reason the northern quarter can afford to run its arrays (banks of glyphs cut by hand into stone and metal to hold a working steady) at all on a frontier tax base.

## Bearing Stock

**Shieldgneiss** · *T2 · Stone · Basement outcrop across the whole quarter*

Banded, coarse, brutally hard, and effectively without Holding. Inert to nine Wellsprings in ten. **This is the reason a Measurewright array laid on the northern shield stays exactly where it was put for a century**, and why the Accord sites its most sensitive grid-work here rather than anywhere more convenient. Bearing exceptional, Holding negligible. Resonance: Petralon, weakly. Family Materia, Domain Material Science.

**Greystone** · *T2 · Stone · Quarrylands of the western marches*

The standard fortification stone of half the realms on this map. Minimally resistive to magic, entirely adequate against everything else. Bearing good, Holding poor.

**Stillband Ore** · *T2 · Metal ore · Bedded across the western shield*

Banded iron, magnetite alternating with gray chert in finger-width layers. Smelts to sound structural iron. The chert bands scatter Essence badly, so anything made from unrefined Stillband takes an inscription and then loses it over a season. Cheap, abundant, and the backbone of every bridge and gate in the quarter. Bearing good, Holding poor.

**Sunkwood** · *T3 · Wood · Recovered from the beds of the chain-lakes*

Oak and black spruce drowned when the ice dammed the northern drainage, buried in anoxic mud, and lifted out after two thousand years black to the heart and dense enough to sink. Takes an edge tool badly and a polish beautifully. Essence-quiet, which makes it the preferred bench and bracket stock in workshops where a stray resonance would spoil the piece being made. Bearing very good, Holding negligible.

## Holding Stock

> **Chainsalt** · *T2 · Mineral · the northern shield Basin, bedded halite*
>
> **The most important dull material in the four quarters.** Bedded rocksalt of exceptional purity acts as an Essence dielectric: it will not conduct, will not retain, and will not degrade under sustained potential. Every gasket, every isolation collar, and every separator plate in a serious glyph array is cut from northern shield salt, because nothing else in the ledger refuses Essence so completely and so cheaply.
>
> It also happens to preserve food. **The northern oath-bound trade houses have never needed to lie about the value of what they sell.**
>
> Bearing poor, Holding deliberately nil. Resonance: Fixatio. Family Materia, Domain Material Science. Fails in standing water, which is why the chain-cities are obsessive about their drainage in a way outsiders find comic until the first collapse.
**Blackcap Sulfide** · *T3 · Metal ore · Melt sheet beneath the Ring*

Massive nickel and copper sulfide with a platinoid fraction, concentrated where the impact melt cooled and settled. Alloyed into iron it produces a metal that **keeps its temper inside a forge-array's working field**, where ordinary steel goes soft and stupid. Every crucible tong, array clamp, and furnace door hinge worth owning is Blackcap alloy. Bearing very good, Holding moderate. Resonance: Manganthra. Family Materia, Domain Material Science. Roasting it releases a sulfur fume that has killed the vegetation for six leagues downwind of the Ring smelters and shows no sign of stopping.

**Clearbeam Beryl** · *T4 · Crystal · Pegmatite dikes on the northern margin*

Optically flawless beryl in crystals occasionally longer than a man. Ground into lenses it passes Essence-light without the scatter that quartz introduces, which makes it **the only acceptable stock for holding coils, assay glasses, and long-range Aetheric instruments.** The Skyglass Archive is glazed with it. Bearing brittle, Holding high and clean. Resonance: Luminalis, Mirithane. Family Fulguria, Domain Electromagnetism.

**Ghoststeel** · *T3 · Metal · Recovered from Spirit War ruins across the shield*

**Salvaged, never mined**, off the dead battlefields of the Great Spirit War, where it lies among things better left alone, and people have killed each other for a good piece of it. Partial phasing quality, reflects spiritlight, and nobody has yet reproduced it in a crucible. The Measurewrights record it here on the argument that the ruins it comes from are all sited on the shield and the shield is therefore its provenance. *This is contested and should be read as provisional.*

## The Works

Northern work is extractive, young and short of coin. **The ore is superb and the way to move it is a muddy cart track through second-growth forest.**

Smelting happens at the Ring because that is where the sulfide is, and the Ring smelters run on forge-arrays that pull directly from the ambient saturation, which is the one genuine advantage the quarter has over anywhere else. A Ring furnace costs a fraction of what the same furnace costs in the west, because in the west you must pay a Core keeper for your hours and here **it is simply in the air.**

Everything else about northern working is worse. The shafts are shallow, the shield being so hard that sinking is slow and fire-setting is the only practical method: you build a bonfire against the rock face, quench it with cold water, and pick out what cracks. Haulage roads freeze and thaw and destroy themselves annually. Skilled Engravers are scarce enough that ward maintenance on frontier settlements is done by traveling men paid in meals, *which is precisely as reliable as it sounds.*

The salt basin is the exception and operates on a wholly different logic. **Salt cutting requires no smelting, no array heat, and almost no capital.** It requires only the tunnels, which the cutting itself produces, and the oaths, which the northern oath-houses enforce with a thoroughness that has become the quarter's chief cultural export.

## What It Costs

> **Frontier ward failure** is the ordinary northern death. A moisture barrier thins past coherence in a well-stone, nobody reads it because nobody in the settlement can read it, and the water goes bad before anyone connects the two.
>
> **Ring smelter fume** is the second. **The ice** is the third, which is not magical at all and which kills more men annually than the first two combined.

## Trade Posture

Exports raw sulfide matte, structural iron, timber, salt, and beryl blanks. Imports finished cloth, worked tools, and Engravers. **Chronically indebted to Old World money-lenders against ore it has not yet dug.**

The Accord's Logistics Division regards the northern quarter as the single greatest untapped bearing-stock reserve in the Material World and has said so, in writing, in language that has made the oath-houses **extremely careful about who they let sign anything.**

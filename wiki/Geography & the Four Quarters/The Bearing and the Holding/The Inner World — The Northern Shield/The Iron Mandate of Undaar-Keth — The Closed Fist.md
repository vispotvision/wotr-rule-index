# The Iron Mandate of Undaar-Keth — The Closed Fist

**Say it** · oon-DAHR-KETH

*The Closed Fist · The Decree-State · Western Shield*

One stroke of a chisel is the most faithfully obeyed mistake the northern shield has ever made. In the midwinter of 634 IC an apprentice to the Stone-Carver Hesh-Kaal, cutting the season's muster on the Decree Stone before the whole plaza, let his hand slip on a single vowel, and the order that every reserve company of the capital district shall report to the wall came out in Old Vaross as shall report to the wolf. The Stone at any moment holds exactly and only the law that applies, so by the next morning's Reading it was the law. Nine hundred soldiers of the Undaar-Gorr reserve in red-lacquered harness, banners up and drums going, their breath smoking in a pink dawn, marched across three frozen rivers into the forest and reported.

A pack of nine gray wolves, unused to this, took the forward rank of the Third Company and one sergeant's throat before the line could be dressed. After that the companies stood at attention in the snow while their commanders cut boards of inquiry as to which wolf. A superseding decree needs the Ironfold's assessment and a public re-carving, and it took nine days, in which the reserve drew rations, posted sentries around the pack it now surrounded, and entered a she-wolf on the Third Company's roll as officer commanding. Forty men lost fingers or feet to the cold. Hesh-Kaal laid down his chisel; the apprentice was assessed, found to have the best hand of his year, and made to carve the correction himself. The she-wolf drew a soldier's ration until she died, because nobody could find the decree that stopped it, and every child in the Ashenmoor can give you her name and rank, which were Shaarn and kaan (an officer).

Picture a fortress that grew a country, where the law is whatever is cut in one black stone this morning and nobody has ever told it no. The Mandate would like it noted that it has never lost a border war.

## Overview

A **hobgoblin decree-state run by a military order**, the Ironfold, occupying the western reaches of the northern shield beyond the Slate Ridge, and the oldest enemy the North has. Its banner is iron-red cloth with a black basalt fist on it, closed, knuckles forward. Every goblin who ever worked a bench knows that banner before they know their own mother's face. The goblins at those benches call the Mandate the Empire in the North when no hobgoblin is in earshot, and the Imperium when one is and they want him to smile, and the Ironfold, which has no sense of humor on the subject, has never decreed against either.

> ***It is the answer that the other answer refused to give.***

When the Scattering broke the goblinoid peoples out of the Old World, **the Crimson Order sailed south and built a war-moot on volcanic rock. They opened their palm.** They asked what rule looked like when force was set down at the door, and have spent their centuries refining the answer. **[[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/Isles and Archipelagos/The Hobgoblin Expanse|The Crimson Isles of Vorruk-Khal]] are that answer.**

**Undaar-Keth is the other answer.** It was given by the hobgoblins who did not sail, who stayed on the land, dug into the western shield's boreal forest and granite ridgelines, **and held ground against human settlers, Ironblood raiding, Marrowkin indifference, and the specific hostility of a frontier that did not want them and that they refused to leave.**

> The founding doctrine is one sentence carved into the basalt of the Decree Stone:
>
> **"The hand opens when the border is safe, and the border is not safe."**
>
> *The sentence has been there for six hundred years. The border has not been safe for any of them.*

**The Ironfold Council does not debate policy.** It receives the watch-reports, assesses threats, issues decrees, and enforces them. The efficiency of this system is its strength. The rigidity is its weakness. **The Mandate has never lost a border war.** It has also never produced a body of law that a farmer would describe as humane, and the Isles' moot-historians consider this a related phenomenon.

**The name** · Old Vaross, and argued over. The Mandate's own heralds take *Undaar* from **undaal**, platform, elevation, the throne that sits above, and *Keth* as **the open palm**, the universal goblinoid gesture of peaceful intent (*keth-ur*), carried here as clan-signifier **and bitter irony**, the open palm offered by a state that rules through closed fist. The grammarians read *undaar* as the standing thing, that which does not fall, and *keth* as a seal, which makes the whole name a seal of the standing kind, since in Vaross the second element of a compound governs. Nobody on Decree Street minds which.

> *The Mandate is named for the peace it intends and has never been permitted to practice.*

**Roughly fifty-five thousand souls on the Grain Roll**, predominantly hobgoblin, with significant Bugbear, Ironblood Orc and Tinker Goblin populations, and a good many more off the Roll, of which more below.

> *The Isles ask. We answer. The difference is that we do not wait for the question to finish.*
>
> Ironfold Commander Vreth-Kaan, address to the border garrisons, Year 702 IC

## Geography and the Western Shield

Black basalt and boreal forest. River valleys, low granite ridgelines separating drainage basins, **and the specific quality of terrain that military surveyors call defensible and everyone else calls miserable.** The forest is resinous and dark green, the canopy thick enough to block sky in midsummer, the undergrowth dense enough to swallow a formation within fifty paces of the treeline, and in the short autumn the whole of it goes up in rust and copper like a banner nobody ordered. Black pines run down to the roads, and on the far side of nearly every road stands a watchtower of basalt blocks on a ridge of gray granite, squat and square and ugly as a fist, with the iron-red cloth snapping on top. Against all this the crimson ranks march in lines you can see from the next valley, which is the idea.

The ridgelines run north to south, creating natural defensive corridors **the Mandate has fortified with a thoroughness that visiting envoys describe as excessive and that the Mandate's own commanders describe as adequate.**

Winters are longer, colder and more brutal than at Marrowhold or the Kharven uplands. The population has adapted the way all northern populations adapt: by developing a tolerance for cold that outsiders find alarming and an attitude toward comfort that outsiders find depressing. The tolerance has a hard floor, and the floor is in the blood: see The Ironwall, below.

The Ashenmoor interior is the picture the Mandate does not hang in its offices: steep-roofed timber towns on stilts along braided rivers under a snow mountain, smoke going straight up, long bridges railed with tusks bigger than a cart, goblinoids round a campfire arguing about the fish, and some mornings a giant Bugbear wading the shallows with a net over her shoulder. Far overhead, more often than the watch-reports care for, there is a dragon.

### The capital

**Undaar-Gorr** stands at the confluence of three rivers on a basalt plateau chosen for **its defensive geometry and its stone.**

> **Basalt is the goblinoid's native substrate.** The Crimson Order's asteroidal inheritance (the mineral of the First Impact, carried in the blood) responds to dense igneous rock as nothing else: **the ground that feels correct, the stone that holds weight the way weight was meant to be held.**

Dark, heavy, angular, with walls thick enough to absorb siege-engine impact and **streets laid out in a grid that moves soldiers, grain and spears from any quarter to any gate at a dead run.** Under the plateau are the dry Undaar deeps, cut vaults kept bone dry on purpose, where a fluid-worker (a Fluxia adept, who cannot act without a medium) finds nothing to work with and is shown the way out.

> **The grid is doctrine.**
>
> *A city whose streets are laid for the rapid march is a city that has decided, at the level of its foundation stones, that defense is the architecture of rule itself.*

### The Decree Stone

A basalt monolith as tall as a keep, in the central plaza, carved in Old Vaross with six centuries of active law. High on its face runs the one founding sentence in cuts longer than a man, legible from the far bank of all three rivers. Below that, at the height a scaffold can comfortably reach, is the working face, twelve feet by eight, where the law actually lives.

> **It is a legal instrument, and anyone who calls it a monument gets corrected.**

**Every decree currently in force is carved into the working face, and every superseded decree is chiseled off and the surface re-carved**, so that the Stone at any moment contains **exactly and only the law that applies.** The boards in the decree archive keep what the Stone has let go.

Both acts are performed in public by the Ironfold's designated **Stone-Carver**, and both carry the ceremonial weight other realms reserve for coronations: ***the law arrives in stone because the stone is the law's body, and the law departs when the stone releases it.*** Crowds bring their children and their lunch. A clean carving of a long clause gets applause, and the old men in the front row, who have opinions about crossbars, are worse than any tournament crowd.

## Origin · The Ones Who Stayed

**Not every hobgoblin followed the fleet.** The territories held populations who could not leave, would not leave, or **chose not to leave because the land was the only home they had and abandoning it felt, to them, like a second defeat dressed in the language of a strategic withdrawal.**

**The Ashenmoor Period** · Reckoned at two centuries after the Scattering by the Mandate's chroniclers and at nearer twelve by anyone with a calendar, and named for the character of the land they held: **ashen, marginal, and wanted by nobody else.** They survived because the territory was poor enough that no major power considered it worth conquering, **and because the Stone-Blood inheritance works just as effectively in a forest as it does in a fortress.**

> *They could not build a moot because they were too scattered and too threatened. They could not build a kingdom because they had no capital and no surplus. What they could build was a network of garrisoned settlements joined by defensible corridors, and what they built was exactly that.*

**Vreth-Undaar** consolidated three garrison settlements onto the basalt plateau, carved the first decree, and declared the enclosed territory sovereign ground **ruled by decree rather than by council, because a council requires the luxury of time and time was the thing the western shield had never provided.**

> **Three founding principles the Ironfold has never amended.**
>
> **The border defines the state. The decree defines the law. Every mouth that eats the state's grain fights for the state's border.**
>
> *These produced a realm that is, by design, an armed order with a farming population attached, rather than a farming population with an army attached.*
>
> **In Undaar-Keth the army is the state**, and agriculture, trade, schooling and craft exist inside the military's perimeter **because the military's perimeter is the only reason any of them survive.**

### The Ironblood

Some came as refugees from inter-clan wars. **Some came as raiders who discovered that raiding the Mandate's border was the military equivalent of headbutting a wall**, and who, after sufficient headbutting, **accepted the wall's offer of a place on the Roll in exchange for standing on the other side of it.**

The **Ironblood Clause** was enacted under Commander Gorath-Keth, who argued with the pragmatism of a commander who had spent eight years fighting orcs on the northern perimeter that **the Ironblood were going to be inside the borders one way or another, and that a people on the Roll have a stake in the border's survival while a people off it have a stake in its collapse.**

> *It passed five to two, and the two dissenters were reassigned to the northern perimeter for the following season, which the Ironfold's records describe as a coincidence and which the dissenters described as a very specific kind of hobgoblin humor.*

## The Seven Districts

*An ashfold, which is the Mandate's word for a district, is drawn along defensive lines rather than geographic or cultural ones. A ridgeline that makes a good defensive position makes a good district border, regardless of whether the farming village on one side has more in common with the village on the other than with the garrison that governs it.* A garrison settlement is a *kaadre*, numbered and never named, and the street Vaross of Kaadre-Six is what foreigners are taught to imitate when they want to be understood and laughed at. A commander takes the district's name with the post and gives it back with the post, which is why there has been a Vreth-Kaan on the northern wall for as long as there has been a wall, and why the name in the epigraph above belongs to a chair.

> The Ironfold regards it with **official indifference and unofficial attention**, too small to threaten anything and too philosophically interesting to ignore. Watch-reports are cut with the annotation **"monitor, do not engage"**, which is the Mandate's way of saying it does not yet know whether the settlement is a threat or a future, and that it intends to find out by watching rather than by asking.

### The Gorge, top and bottom

Tinker's Gorge is a crack in the western shield a mile long and three hundred feet deep, and it is the brightest place in the Mandate. No daylight reaches the floor, so the goblins who live there hang their own: lamps on chains from every ledge and gallery, strings of brass and green glass by the hundred, kept burning by lamp-wardens on a garrison stipend, and the light runs down the walls in lime and amber and pink and copper-gold and pools where the workshops are. The shops are cut into the rock in tiers, joined by ladders, rope-bridges and little iron cage-lifts on counterweights. Every door is painted a different color, the goblins having nothing else that is theirs to paint.

The top of the Gorge is the Mandate's pride, with its sheds, its guild marks shouted across the galleries, and its monthly Forge-Circle. The bottom is the Mandate's money. At the head of the road stands a tower of black basalt with the fist on its banner and a bell in it, and when the bell rings four thousand goblins put down one set of tools and four thousand more pick them up, and the benches do not stand empty. On the floor tiers they file valve-seats and bellows-fittings and the small brass hearts of half the contraptions in three realms, true to a hair, by eye and by feel, to a daily quota set by a hobgoblin foreman on a raised platform with a ledger. The rank-cut on his shoulder plate, four cuts and a crossbar, can be read at forty paces. The pay is bunk space and soup.

Readers who can add will have noticed that eight thousand goblins do not fit in a district of three thousand. The bench goblins are hired by contract out of the Gorge's own families and the slag-yards of the Isles, and a contract hand does not eat the state's grain in the Roll's sense, so the Roll does not count them. The Iron Code says no one on the Roll is exempt from anything regardless of species. The decrees, on the rare occasions they mention bench goblins, use a compound that comes into Common as *the lesser*. The Archive offers this without comment and suggests you not offer one in Undaar-Gorr.

## The Wellsprings

**The western shield's profile is sparse.** The ground runs thin, lean in the street's word, thinner than at Marrowhold, the Kharven uplands or the Nalūn salt basin, and concentrated seeps are largely absent. A fighter pulling Aether (the raw magic of the world) here fills slowly, so Mandate practitioners learn thrift early, and their aura coats run close and quiet until the moment they do not. They call the coat *ak-gresh*, the survived skin, and hold that the Ironblood wear it too loud.

> This has shaped the Mandate's magical culture. Where other realms build rule around Wellspring-backed instruments (Nalūn's contracts, the Holy Sea's testimony, the Thread Standard of the Silver Cedars), **the Mandate rules through its order alone.**
>
> *The decrees carry no Wellspring enforcement. They carry the Ironfold's enforcement, which arrives in the form of soldiers rather than metaphysics.*

**Petralon** · Diffuse rather than concentrated, providing the ambient reinforcement that makes the fortifications hold. **A formation of hobgoblin soldiers standing on Petralon-bearing stone locks into the substrate in a way that makes the formation functionally immovable without destroying the ground beneath it.**

**Stone-Blood Resonance** · **A racial Aetheric signature that all goblinoid peoples carry**, rather than a Wellspring, producing measurable effects when populations concentrate. The Mandate's practitioners describe it as the weight of the stone remembering what it was before it was people. **The practical consequence is a passive defensive field that makes a settlement harder to disrupt through Aetheric means,** which is one reason the fortifications have held for centuries against opponents with superior magical capability.

> **The Decree Stone's Question. The single most politically sensitive Wellspring question in the goblinoid world.**
>
> The Isles' Rock at Khal-Serrat is openly Engraved: the inscriptions carry Aetheric weight, **and a law written into the Rock is a fact about the stone.**
>
> **If the Decree Stone carries the same property, the Mandate's decrees are metaphysically enforced, which would mean the Ironfold rules through Wellspring-backed authority whether or not it acknowledges doing so.** If it does not, the decrees are enforced purely by soldiers, which is the Mandate's official position **and which the Isles' scholars consider unlikely, given that the Stone is carved from the same asteroidal-resonance basalt and inscribed in the same script.** The nine days the reserve spent guarding a wolf are cited by both sides.
>
> ***The question is unresolved. Neither side has tested it. Both sides suspect the answer and neither side wants to be the one who confirms it.***

## Governance · The Ironfold

There is no house here, no crown and no heir. The Ironfold is a sworn military order that happens to own a country, its officers bound by the Crimson Order's Engraving (an oath carved into the Soul Crystal itself, so that breaking it does immediate harm to the breaker). At its head sit seven operational commanders, one per district, holding **both military and civil authority.** The Ironfold does not vote in the Isles' sense. It reaches agreement through assessment: **commanders present their counts and reports, the Mandate-Holder sets them side by side,** and the decree that results is the decree the counts support.

> **Dissent is recorded but not deliberated.** A commander who disagrees may cut a formal objection, **which the Stone-Carver annotates beneath the decree's text on the Stone in a smaller hand**, so that the law carries its own criticism visibly and permanently.
>
> *This is the Mandate's only concession to loyal opposition, and it is one the Isles' moot-speakers regard as both insufficient and deeply honest.*

**The Mandate-Holder** · Selected from the Ironfold by **the Assessment**, judged on published, specific and merciless criteria: border integrity, how much grain reaches the line, how quiet the villages are, how fast the line answers an alarm, **and the ratio of decrees issued to decrees enforced.** The bench goblins call the holder the Imperator, for the same reason they call the place the Imperium. The holder in 715 IC is Drekka Vreshen, a hobgoblin of the Gorr-Shal command, known on every wall as Drekka Never-Late, an epithet her soldiers mean as praise and the Greymane scouts mean as a dare.

> *A commander who issues many decrees and enforces few is not considered decisive. They are considered noisy, and noise is the Ironfold's cardinal sin.*

**Three Mandate-Holders have been reassessed out of office. All three accepted the verdict.** The Mandate's own chroniclers describe this as the order's maturity. The Isles' historians describe it as the consequence of knowing what happens to people who refuse, a characterization the Mandate does not dignify with a response.

**The Stone-Carver** · The official whose chisel creates law. **The quality of the carving, the precision of the script, the depth of the grooves, the clarity of the letterforms, is treated as a measure of the law's own quality.**

> *A poorly carved decree is a poorly made law, and a decree carved sloppily into stone is a decree that has already confessed it was not worth the effort of doing properly.*

## Law and the Iron Code

**The Iron Code** · Amended only by unanimous vote of the Ironfold. Mandatory service between sixteen and forty-five, a levy assessed on what each field and shop can yield, collective responsibility for border upkeep, **and the principle that no one on the Roll is exempt regardless of rank, species or guild.**

> *It reads as the standing orders of a garrison: clear, imperative, without ornamentation, concerned exclusively with function.*

**The Decree** · The Code is the bones; the decrees are what you are actually told to do this morning. Decrees are composed in *Thruum Vaross*, the reading register, with clauses ordered by what must be done first, and they refuse in a form with nobody in it: *Skaal vashk*, there shall be no breaching. The Concord's clerks enter the Mandate as the type-case of the polity they call a Mandate: rule by standing decree, read aloud daily at every garrison, with no assembly and no appeal outside the decree archive. Its strength is that everybody knows the law because everybody hears it every day. Its weakness is that the law is whatever was read this morning, as the she-wolf's descendants could tell you. A ferryman on the southern line keeps two tally-sticks, one for each bank's law, and his children learn which to show before they learn to row.

**The Ironblood Clause** · Produces the specific friction of shared standing between peoples **whose traditions are fundamentally incompatible and whose ways of war are terrifyingly complementary.**

> **A hobgoblin formation provides the Resonance Lattice (soldiers' souls set together like the stones of an arch), the structured defensive anchor. Ironblood soldiers provide the assault, the raw impact force the formation channels rather than generates.**
>
> Together they produce **the most effective ordinary way of war on the northern shield**, and the effectiveness is why the Clause survives: the military argument for integration is so strong that the cultural arguments against it have been losing for four centuries.

**The Petition Process** · The only road for anyone below an officer to be heard. Slow, cut on boards, carried up through three commands, **and surprisingly effective**: the commanders discovered over centuries that **a people who are heard, slowly, are a people who do not revolt, and putting down a revolt is expensive.**

> *It produces stability, which the Mandate considers a prerequisite for justice and which the Mandate's critics consider a substitute for it.*

## Economy

**Military and farming, in that order.** Boreal timber, furs, limited minerals, seasonal grain and roots in the Ashenmoor valleys, brass and fittings from the Gorge, **and the austere productivity a people living inside a permanent perimeter develop because they cannot afford waste.**

**Soldiers are the unofficial export**: frontier settlements and caravans occasionally hire Ironwall units for escort, a practice the Ironfold officially discourages and unofficially permits, because the fee feeds the war-chest and the road teaches the soldiers. A caravan with six hobgoblins walking its flanks in iron-red pays a little more at the start and a good deal less at the end.

> **The Mandate does not use Nalūn's credit paper.** It has tried to make its own, **through decree rather than Wellspring**, and every attempt has produced documents that are **enforceable within the borders and metaphysically inert outside them.**
>
> *The result is a realm whose internal money is the split ration-tally and whose external money is barter, weight-for-weight exchange, and the grudging acceptance that the salt bars of the Amber Cities are the only universally trusted medium on the northern shield and that refusing to use them is a principle the Mandate cannot afford.*

From the Crimson Isles the Mandate buys brimstone and volcanic glass, at the weight, with a great deal of mutual contempt. From the Marrowkin it buys as little as it can, and the Ironfold's decree forbidding any officer to own a piece of god-bone (the ivory stuff Marrowhold cuts out of whatever it lives inside) is among the best enforced on the Stone.

### Rent, food and the street

Black stone does not make it a dull town. Every door on the Undaar-Gorr grid is lacquered in its company's color, oxblood and yellow and a shouting blue, so that a runner can find a billet at a dead sprint, and the regimental messes on Decree Street turn out smoked elk and barley by the barrow, with Nalūn salt on every table. Out in the Ashenmoor it is roots, pickled things and rye under a Bugbear roof, low and wide, with a bench that will hold anybody; in the Gorge it is the soup. Housing is by billet, and a family that wants a better one files a petition, which is slow, and then pays a sergeant, which is faster.

Small practitioners are everywhere and every one of them is on a roll: wall-wardens who keep the watch-fires burning a true color, ration-cooks whose pots never scorch, and the Ironwall's famous farrier who can shoe a warhorse while it is kicking him and bills the horse's company for the bruises. Each company has its song, its color and its grudge against the company next door, and on feast days they run the Wall Games on the plaza, carrying sandbags up a scaffold in full harness before a crowd that knows every sergeant by name. Betting on the Wall Games is unlawful by no decree whatever, because the Ironfold has done the sum on the ratio and knows it could not enforce one.

## Defense · The Ironwall

Roughly **eight thousand**, the largest standing army on the northern shield and the readiest, **in the specific sense that a force defending the same borders for six centuries has honed every procedure for exactly one purpose: holding the line.**

> **Ironwall doctrine produces companies that function, baggage trains that arrive, and fortifications that hold, and has no place for individual heroes.** It does post a monthly list of officers commended, which every soldier in the Ironwall reads as a ranking and bets on accordingly.

**The tactical approach is the phalanx of spears, remade for a world with magic.** The Resonance Lattice absorbs chaotic Aether and channels kinetic energy; integrated Ironblood companies break the attacker's formation once the Lattice has absorbed the first impact. The move the crowds and the enemy both wait for is **The Red Wall Takes One Step**: a whole formation advances a single pace, in silence, and the field changes hands. Ironblood on the Mandate's wall add **Vorruk-Da, Shard Comes Home**, standing still for the first blows on purpose and handing the stored force back through one fist, and the companies that drill the two together call the combination the Door and the Knock and argue over which of them is the door. Crossbows hold the cleared lanes, because a bolt costs almost nothing and does not care about your Stage if you do not see the loose.

> Devastating against ordinary forces. **Less effective against Domain-scale practitioners** (those who can make a stretch of ground obey their own law), because a Lattice that functions by absorbing chaos is vulnerable to an opponent who applies order, and a Domain is the most organized expression of magical authority that exists.
>
> **The cold is the other way in.** Stone-Blood runs slow in deep cold, and at thirty below every Stone-Blood fighter moves about three-quarters of a beat late. The strength is all there and arrives behind the count, and a formation drilled to keep time loses its time before it loses its strength. In such weather the Silent Sign-Glyphs fail and the Ironwall falls back to a horn count, which can be heard by the people it is fighting. The Ironfold knows this as it knows the sum of its own grain.

**The fortification line** is the most extensive military work on the shield with no Wellspring behind it: redoubts at regular intervals, cleared shooting-lanes, Tinker-kept warded perimeters, **and a chain of beacon towers and Tinker mirror-posts that carries a border alert from the northern wall to the Mandate-Holder's table in approximately forty minutes**, fast enough that the response is usually in motion before the attacking force has finished crossing the perimeter.

> The strategic weakness is every military state's: **it can hold its borders and cannot grow beyond them without turning from a defensive posture to an offensive one**, and the turn requires a surplus of grain and of will the Mandate has never possessed at the same time.
>
> *The borders do not expand. The borders do not contract. The Mandate exists inside its walls, and the walls are its argument that existence is sufficient.*

## Culture

**Martial, direct, and functional in the way a well-kept fortification is functional:** everything serves a purpose, and the one exception to the absence of ornamentation is color, which the Ironfold permits on the ground that a banner is a signal. **The rest of the plainness is itself a statement about what the culture values.**

**Composition** · Of the Roll, about 55% hobgoblin, 25% Bugbear, 12% Ironblood Orc, 5% Tinker Goblin, 3% other, predominantly human frontier families who crossed the southern line going the wrong direction and were offered a place on the Roll rather than expulsion, on the pragmatic ground that a family who voluntarily enters a military state has already selected for the characteristics the military state values.

> **The peoples' roles are functional rather than harmonious: the hobgoblins command, the Bugbears feed, the Ironblood fight, and the Tinkers build.** The roles are not prescribed in the Code, but six centuries of cultural gravity have produced a division of labor that newcomers absorb within a generation and that the Mandate's own people regard as natural in the way breathing is natural, meaning they do not notice it until someone from outside points it out. Orcs get the walls and the northern perimeter, to be spent against whatever comes off the tundra hungry. Goblins get the benches.

**Language** · Old Vaross for administration and law. **Gorr-Vaross**, the Bugbear dialect, in the Ashenmoor: long coordinated chains joined by *and*, resisting subordination, idiom drawn from water, rot, growth and things that sink. The Ironblood speak Old Vaross with **heavier consonants, shorter vowels, and the sub-bass frequencies that physically vibrate the iron content of nearby listeners**, which the hobgoblin population has learned to filter and visitors have not. A Vaross speaker in a rage gets quieter and slower, and emphasis is carried by how long a vowel is held. A commander who starts holding his vowels has told you everything you need to know about your next few minutes.

**Architecture** · The Bugbear instinct is horizontal while the hobgoblin instinct is vertical, and the contrast between the capital's angular height and the Ashenmoor's spread-out sprawl is the most visible expression of the realm's inner character.

**The Decree Reading** · A daily ceremony, roughly fifteen minutes, at every garrison, market square and settlement hall. A *thruum*, in Vaross, is a decree read aloud. **Attendance is not mandatory. Non-attendance is noted**, and in a culture where being noted is functionally equivalent to being assessed, the distinction between mandatory and expected has collapsed into a category the Mandate calls observed and everyone else calls mandatory with extra steps. Every child can recite the Third Ashfold Decree by seven, for its last lines: *A thousand years. A thousand years. And we bore it.*

**The Forge-Circle** · The monthly Tinker exhibition, held at the top of the Gorge and attended by commanders, engineers, **and whatever farmers are brave enough to attend an event where the exhibits occasionally achieve unplanned combustion.** The makers shout their names and guild marks. The Mandate's closest equivalent to a festival, and its atmosphere, watching a Tinker demonstrate a device whose stability the proposal boards described as provisional, produces the only occasion in the calendar when hobgoblin commanders are observed to smile.

> **Za'tarch's cultural influence.** A position official doctrine does not accommodate and that the younger generation finds increasingly interesting: **that goblinoid peoples can live together through soul-work rather than decree.**
>
> **The Mandate's younger soldiers visit during leave rotations and return with questions the Ironfold has not prepared answers for.**
>
> *Whether Za'tarch represents the Mandate's future or merely a village of two hundred with ideas above its station is the question the Ironfold has been avoiding for a decade, and the avoidance is itself an answer the Ironfold prefers not to examine.*

### What they joke about

The she-wolf, first and always: "Report to the wolf" is what a Mandate soldier says when an order makes no sense, and he says it with his face perfectly straight, because it might be lawful. An officer who is out of her depth is *Kaan Shaarn*. A *kaanith* is a jumped-up officer, and the diminutive is the only insult the Ironfold will overlook on a feast day.

Of the Isles they say a decree is a motion that got on with it, and that an Islander asked the time will call a roll. Of the Greymanes they say the house has one idea and that it is correct. Of the Kharven they say a lord's oath is good until the thaw. Of the Marrowkin they say that you will be sold your own teeth and given a receipt. The bench goblins say a number of things about all of the above, quickly, in the Goblin dialect, with the syllabic *r* dropped so that the foreman has to ask.

## Monsters on the Roads

The woods are full of the honest kind of danger: elk that will not move for a column, bear in every drainage, wolverine in the ration stores, salmon thick enough in the braided rivers that the Ashenmoor bears fish beside the Bugbears and neither side files a complaint. Gray wolf packs are a matter of record, and one served as an officer.

The northern wall faces the creatures that breed on rich tundra ground and consider a garrison an interesting food source, and Droval (the Frost-Howlers of the polar snowfields, which lie along a ridge until the ridge is suddenly gone) head the watch-reports in any bad winter. Monsters move, and the Mandate's are entered by the post that first saw them. Wyverns (T6 on the Danger Tier, the hunting halls' scale from T1 to T9) nest on the western faces of the Slate Ridge and take sentries from both garrisons with perfect impartiality, the one subject on which Gorr-Shal and Galecross have exchanged notes in a civil tone. A dragon has been seen far overhead above the Ashenmoor, and it is entered in watch-reports as a dragon.

### The dungeon

Concentrated seeps are largely absent here, so a dungeon is a rare event, and the one everyone mentions is **Vorrak's Standing Grove** on the western frontier, where the third Stone-Carver spent her retirement cutting practice decrees into living trees. A thin Chthonica seep (a Wellspring of dark iron-oxide, all grip and ground) lay under the wood. The things in that grove now obey clauses, and a patrol that knows the wording can walk through. A company stands around it, and the foresters who say the forest has been ignoring her law make one exception, in a lower voice. The Ironfold has it annotated "monitor, do not engage." More on the [[The Bestiary/Dungeons|Dungeons]] shelf and in the [[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Inner World — A Gazetteer|Gazetteer]].

### The Scourge and the Turn

[[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Scourge of Hell|The Scourge of Hell]] lies south and east of Keth-Vorruk, past the Midmarch and the foot of the Violet Peaks, and the Mandate holds no ground on its rim. The Turn is the old rota by which the North, the South and the Center each keep the Scourge's monsters off everybody else for nine years. The Ironfold's whole recorded view of it is two words on a board in the decree archive, *not party*, and it follows the thing more closely than some of the parties do. On the North's Turn, Kharven and its Banner Houses march down the Kessen road with Stormfold slate in the column, the Greymane wall stands thin by however many spears went south, and every commander at Gorr-Shal knows the count by the first thaw. The Center holds the Turn until 721 and the North's comes next, and that sum has been done on every wall in the Mandate.

The other end of it arrives on the southern line. In a dry late summer the Scourge's herds push outward and what follows them comes too, so the Midmarch empties northward on its carts, and Keth-Vorruk sees more younger sons with charters in a bad herd season than in any other, some of them followed up the road by Hellhounds (T5) off the near rim. The Ironfold shoots both on the same principle. Caravans bound for the Kessen road hire Ironwall units for the flanks, and the fee goes in the war-chest.

Then there are the ridge-men, who are the Mandate's quietest export. On the western ridges above the Black Roots, the long-service hands of the Scourge trade are hobgoblins, stripping carcasses and hiring out as guides, and a good many came from here. Service under the Iron Code ends at forty-five, and a hobgoblin may see two centuries if the world allows it, which leaves a discharged sergeant about a hundred and fifty years to fill and no decree to fill them. They keep their old company's color on a rag somewhere about the harness, and they will tell a Midmarch boy the worst of it in the same voice they used for the morning Reading.

## Relations

**The Slate Ridge of Stormfold and the Greymane wall** · The Mandate's whole eastern sky. The Ridgewall, a fortress grown out of a ridge of slate under violet lightning, stands on the line between the shield's two great militaries, and House Greymane has held it for longer than the North has had a name. Both sides have been looking at each other across the same passes for centuries; in the present year it is a cold peace, which on that ridge means raiding parties, burned watch-posts, stolen horses and a great deal of formal notice served by arrow. Hobgoblins hold that those passes are goblinoid ground, lost in the Scattering and never paid for. Greymane children are raised as components of the wall they stand on, and the Ironfold, which raises its own the same way, has never quite been able to bring itself to despise them.

**Kharven and the Northern Empire of Whitemere** · The cold peace across the Greymane wall, and **the one border the Mandate has pressed and not taken.** Both states believe borders must be held, threats must be answered, and rule is serious work done by serious people, which is precisely why neither can leave the other alone: **Moto sovereign judgment and Ironfold decree-authority arrive at similar conclusions through different mechanisms**, and the conclusion they have both arrived at is that the other is the threat worth the winter. *Later hand, from the last winter before the turn:* when Kharven was built out into the Empire of Whitemere, with the Banner Houses holding lands inside it, the new empire inherited the wall, the cold and the enemy, all three, and the Mandate's reading of the news took fifteen minutes and changed nothing on the Stone.

**The Crimson Isles of Vorruk-Khal** · The most politically charged relationship in the goblinoid world. **Two states, one people's two answers to the question of rule. The Isles opened their palm. The Mandate closed its fist.** Both respond to the same founding wound, and both believe the other's response was wrong. The Isles consider the Mandate a tyranny. The Mandate considers the Isles naive. Both are correct. They agree on exactly one thing, and it stands on a ridge: no human lord has a good title to those passes, and every load of Isles brimstone landed on the Vorrak's Reach coast is a vote on the subject.

> *Dealings are conducted through observers who are at once envoys and spies, and both states are aware of this and consider it the healthiest possible arrangement, because an observer who is also a spy cuts comprehensive reports, and comprehensive reports are what both states require to maintain the mutual assessment that has prevented a war between them for four centuries.*

**Marrowhold** · The whole of Kharven lies between them, and a joint trade moot still meets annually in the customs post at Gorr-Shal, **discusses the same three issues, reaches the same compromise, and adjourns until next year.** It has performed this exact ritual for two centuries. Both sides consider it a model of efficient rule. Both sides are correct. The Marrowkin enter each year's compromise in their tally of what has been taken, and the Ironfold has asked what that means, and has been told it will be informed.

**The Center** · South of Keth-Vorruk lies the Hundred Crowns of the Midmarch, whose petty kings change sides every decade and send their spare sons north with charters. The Ironfold deals with whichever of them has most recently stopped being a nuisance. The Concord's Sealed Charter does not run past the southern line, and its clerks' opinion of the Iron Code has been received and entered. Salt comes up from the Amber Cities of Nalūn under Ironwall escort, the one arrangement nobody has ever tried to change. Nalūn's factors are the only foreigners a commander will stand up for.

**The Silver Cedars of Yukari** · The land the old maps call the Crow's Reach lies far to the south, and what passes between them is defined by **mutual incomprehension**: the Mandate cannot understand a realm that rules through perception rather than decree, and Yukari cannot understand a realm that rules through decree rather than perception. Each keeps an observer at the other's seat, and each observer's reports are read with the deep suspicion that they might be accurate.

**The Holy Sea of Alabaster** · **The Manumission Doctrine and the Ironblood Clause are two radically different approaches to the same problem: how to extend legal protection to a people the surrounding world considers disposable.** The Holy Sea does it through theology. The Mandate does it through military pragmatism. The clerics find the Mandate's approach morally inadequate, and have lately asked about the Gorge. The commanders find the Holy Sea's approach militarily irrelevant. Both assessments are accurate. Both are incomplete.

## Flagged Gaps

| Gap | Standing |
|---|---|
| **The Decree Stone's Aetheric status** | **The most politically sensitive Wellspring question in the goblinoid world.** Open, and it will stay open until someone tests the Stone's authority and lives |
| **Ironfold names** | The Archive has the Mandate-Holder and the post-names. The commanders turn over too fast to be worth cutting into a board, and the present Stone-Carver has asked not to be named, which the Archive respects because of what she could do to its entry |
| **Za'tarch's trajectory** | Whether it grows into a political force or remains a monitored curiosity **decides a good deal of the realm's inner politics**, and nobody in Undaar-Gorr will say which way the wind is blowing |
| **The Blackened Orcs** | Whether any Void-corrupted bands (orcs whose Stone-Blood has turned against itself) live within or near the borders, **and how the Mandate would respond to them**, is more than the Archive's sources will say |
| **The Drow** | If Drow territory exists on the western shield, the jurisdictional overlap could produce a situation **neither model of rule is equipped to resolve**: decree-based authority meeting inverted-Parunic courtesy, the irresistible force meeting the impeccably polite immovable object |

> *The mountain does not negotiate. The mountain holds. Everything that has tried to move this mountain is now part of its base, and the base is wider than it was. That is how we treat with neighbors. That is our history. That is the whole of the explanation, and if the explanation is insufficient, the mountain is still here, and you are welcome to try.*
>
> *Stone-Carver Vorrak, unofficial remarks, preserved in the Archive despite her objections*

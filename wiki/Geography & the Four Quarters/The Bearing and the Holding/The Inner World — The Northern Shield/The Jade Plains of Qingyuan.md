# The Jade Plains of Qingyuan

**Say it** · ching-YWEN

*The Grey Plain, on northern maps · The Thousand Bloodlines · In the Dynasty of Sum-gol*

Lucky Bao stole the greatest horse in the South on the night of the Lantern Fair in 694, and he did it beautifully. Rain-on-Slate was a seven-year-old stallion of the tall gray Yan breed, a hand higher than anything the north can raise and dappled like river stones, with a stud fee that would buy a village, and Bao walked him out of the Qingyuan paddocks with his hooves wrapped in silk while nine rows of the Yan family were up at the tablets toasting their ancestors. He rode north for eleven days and sold nothing, because he was a professional and meant to do it properly. In the great horse market below Ashbury, with half the Ashmore gentry bidding and a real northern lord holding up a purse the size of a cheese, Bao was counting his fortune when a child of about nine came through the crowd on a fat pony. She wore a jade sash and carried a ledger as big as she was. She looked the horse over, opened the book, and read out his name, his sire, his dam and his grandsire in a clear, bored voice.

Rain-on-Slate turned his head. Then he turned the rest of himself, went through the market fence with Bao still in the saddle, and set off for home. They say there has never been a ride like it. He ran flat out for six days, down the salt road with the copper haze of the [[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Scourge of Hell|Scourge of Hell]] standing on his right hand, past a string of Ryu toll-forts without paying, through two weddings and somebody's war, with the whole country laying money on it from the hedges and Lucky Bao hanging on with both arms because by now he could not get off. The Elder of Stables was waiting at the paddock gate when the gray came in at a walk, not even blown. Bao slid down, fell over, and held out his wrists. The Elder looked at his seat and his legs and the state of the saddle, took out a brush, and made an entry: *One thief, sound, excellent hands, sire unknown.* Bao rode for the Yan for thirty years, and they buried him with his name in the book, which he considered the best thing anybody ever stole.

Qingyuan is a green valley and an ocean of silver-green grass, a family that has bred every good horse in the Inner World and written down who bought it, and the quiet certainty that a horse, a debt or a man will come home when the right person reads out his name.

The Yan sell a horse and keep its name. You may read everything below as a footnote to that.

## The Land and the Look

It is grass from here to the sky. Silver-green, waist high by midsummer, running in long swells from the steam of the [[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Sapphire Wells of Brightwater|Wellheads]] in the west, with the rust-colored [[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Vermilion Dynasty of Sum-gol|iron hills]] beyond them, across to the foot of the [[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Silver Cedars of Yukari|cedar country]] in the east, it goes pale whenever the wind turns the blades over, and that is why northerners who have only ever ridden across it in a cold wind wrote *the Grey Plain* on their maps. The Yan call it the Jade Plains, because in the low sun after rain it turns the exact color of the stone in the spring at Yuquan, and they will correct your map for you if you leave it unattended.

Toward its middle the plain narrows into the valley everyone paints: a wide green floor of wildflowers, still pools and low stone walls under an enormous blue sky, with long waterfalls coming down off vast curved mountains on either hand, and on a rise in the midst of it Qingyuan, a pale many-towered hall under slate roofs.

Out of the grass rise the stud-halls, each a walled compound with lacquered roofs of black and sea-green turned up at the corners, and on the rise behind every one, jade and silver banners as tall as ships' masts. Big gray horses call across the plain at dusk, and sound carries a long way. There are no trees to speak of, a great many larks, and more sky than most visitors can stand for the first week.

This is the middle of the South. Boundary stones mark the [[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Ochre Fields of Hammerford|Long Fields]] on the north, the Sallowgate marches climb away on the south toward the [[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Holy Sea of Alabaster|white shelf]], the old imperial road comes in from Sum-gol, and salt and grain come across from the salt road, paying at every toll. Buyers come from four quarters, and horses sold from here have a habit of walking back.

The war came through here more than once, and the dead of the Grey Plain include a Warlord of the Lamberts. Grass grows back. The Yan have a column for that as well.

## The House

**The Yan Hall** is a lineage hall in the old southern style (a clan ruled by its elders and its ancestral tablets, rather than by a lord). Its tablets stand nine rows deep, with a title for every rung of the family, which the Yan recite in full at every introduction and which takes, for a senior elder, about as long as it takes to water a horse. The present head is the Venerable Hall-Master of the Yan, First of the Grey Plain, Keeper of the Thousand Bloodlines, **Elder Yan Shouyi**. He is ninety, he rides every morning, and he has personally inspected the teeth of most of the lords you have heard of. The hall maxim is *The Plain Remembers the Hoof*. Its colors are gray and jade, with a running horse in silver.

Below the Hall-Master sit the Elders of Stables, Ledgers, Remounts and Tablets, the core disciples of each stud, and the outer cousins, who do the shoveling and are reminded of their fortune at every meal. A Yan is born to a rung, and the only way off it is a horse. A cousin of the lowest row who breeds a filly of the first class goes up three rows that evening, and the tablet-carver is sent for.

The Hall answers to the name of Sum-gol, and has done without interruption since before the empire broke. Every autumn an elder goes to the Calling and recites his titles from the square. The Hall keeps the Emperor's stall swept at Qingyuan and enters it each year as *held*. As for what the Dynasty has received from the Grey Plain since 602, the Hall will be delighted to show you the ledger, and you will need to bring a lunch.

## Cities and Towns

**Qingyuan** is the seat and the only thing on the plain you could call a city: forty thousand people inside a square of tamped-earth walls faced in pale stone on the rise, with the Great Hall of Tablets at its center, many-towered and slate-roofed, its nine rows lit by a thousand oil lamps, and the home paddocks stretching out through its north gate. The streets are laid wide enough for sixty horses abreast and smell like it. Saddlers, farriers, tack-lacquerers and ledger-binders each hold their own guild street. The **Lantern Fair** at midsummer fills the walls with paper light and buyers from four quarters.

**Yuquan**, "jade spring," sits where a cold spring comes out of the eastern rise over beds of milky green stone. River jade comes down it after every flood and the whole town wades in after it with sieves, while the carvers who buy their finds cut bridle-bosses, ledger-weights and the small jade horses every Yan child gets at its naming. The water is the sweetest on the plain, and since the war the town has been watching very closely whether it stays that way, given what has happened to the springs upstream.

**Mashi**, "horse market," is a town that exists for nine weeks a year and is a wide empty field for the rest of it. Then the tents go up in rows a mile long and everyone comes: northern quartermasters, Marrick paymasters, Lambert clerks to check the Marrick paymasters, Moto stable-lords in red, horse-copers with a dozen ways of making an old mare look young, hedge-workers who will gentle a colt or lame a rival's for the same fee, and bookies on every rail, because there are races at noon and at sunset and on any provocation in between.

## Wealth and Trade

Their wealth walks. The tall gray southern horse comes from the Yan studs at Qingyuan and nowhere else, and the Yan keep the bloodlines in ledgers older than the Holy Sea's: sire, dam and grandsire for every animal they have ever sold, and the name of the man who bought it.

That last column is the Hall's real stock in trade. **They sold to everyone in the war.** Ashmore's big southern horses were Yan horses, and so were the Tenrai's. The Hall-Master can tell you which side bought how many remounts in which season, which is to say which side was expecting to lose cavalry, and when. *The Yan take orders, in both senses, and never a side.* Lords pay well for that column, and pay better to have their own line of it left unread.

Beyond horses: river jade, felt, hides, mare's milk spirits, lacquer, and the leather of a hundred thousand saddles. Grain and salt come in by the cart, the salt at a Ryu toll that the Hall disputes annually, by reciting.

---

## Rent, Food and the Street

The grass belongs to the Hall. A herding family rents its stretch by the head of stock, in foals and days' labor, and pays it at the fall gather with a great deal of drinking; a Qingyuan townsman rents a room over a stable at about the ordinary [[Geography & the Four Quarters/Value, Coin and Trade/What Things Cost|city list]] and gets the warmth free. They eat hand-pulled noodles, mutton roasted on the bone with cumin and salt, steamed buns the size of a fist, and tea with butter and salt from a bowl that hangs at the saddle. Everyone drinks the sour mare's milk spirit at the fall gather, and everyone regrets it.

A Qingyuan market is all elbows and titles. Two men who meet in the street state their rung before they state their business, the lower one bows the correct number of degrees, and a Yan grandmother can reduce a horse-coper to tears from behind a tea stall by asking who his people were. Stable-sects run the bracket fights, each with its own colors and its own songs and a stud to be loyal to: the Silver Bridles of the home paddocks, the Black Girths of the eastern halls, and the Yuquan Waders, who are not taken seriously until they break your jaw. The hall's scholars say hùtǐ for the aura coat, wēiyā for Pressure and shénshí for sensing, and they will explain to you that the Guild's words are translations, poorly done. On the plain, a boy with a good coat is simply said to be *shining like a wet gray*.

## Law and How It Fights

Hall law runs the plain. The Elder of Ledgers hears disputes in the Great Hall in front of the tablets of the plaintiff's own ancestors, which keeps testimony short. Killing a man is a matter for his family and a blood-price. Stealing a horse is a matter for the Hall, and since the horse will come home regardless, the penalty turns on how well you rode it. No one on the plain has ever been able to explain to a Lambert why the ledger is not perjury when it says what it says about the buyers; the Yan hold that a true record of who bought what cannot be a lie, whoever it embarrasses.

**Their own horsemen are few and superb**, archers who shoot from the saddle at the full gallop, turning in the stirrups, with the gray horses running flat out under them like water poured downhill. They never stand and they do not charge. They ride around a force at a distance from which it cannot reach them and take it apart from the edges, starting with whoever is shouting. When the Yan ride out in force, it is to make sure a buyer has paid. A wall of good spears gives them nothing to charge and they have no interest in trying, which is the entire history of relations between the Yan and the Marrick, and both sides consider it a professional friendship. A crossbow in the right hedge has killed more than one elder of the third row. That goes in the ledger too.

---

## What They Joke About

Titles first. A Yan will tell you about the elder who introduced himself at a siege and the siege was over when he finished. Of the Marrick they say they have spent three hundred years walking everywhere to prove it could be done. Of northerners they say a northern lord will pay anything for a tall horse and then fall off it in a very brave manner. Of the Ryu they say that the tide pays twice and the Yan three times, and the Hall still does not know who the third payment went to. There is one joke they tell against themselves. A Yan dies, comes to the gate of the next world and is asked his name, and the gatekeeper gives up and lets him in around the third row.

## Monsters on the Roads

Neiman Lions (T4) hunt the plain with a stunning roar that has cost the Hall a great many foals, and every stud has a bounty standing. Hippogrifs come down from the rim mountains and are shot on sight or sold. The rest of the country's teeth belong to beasts: steppe eagles take lambs, foxes take everything smaller, wild asses break the hay-fences, and marmot holes have lamed more good horses than the lions have killed, a figure the Elder of Ledgers will show you.

**The dungeon.** The cold spring at Yuquan is a small worked Core, kept by the Elder of Tablets. The dungeon is **the Ninth Paddock**: in the Hall's old ledgers there was a stud east of Qingyuan whose compound sank into the plain in a single night, walls, roofs and tablets, into a hole no one had known was a Well. On a still dusk, tall horses can be heard calling from the ground. The Hall enters the stock every year as *held*, has sent three parties to read out their names, and got back one of the horses. More on the [[The Bestiary/Dungeons|Dungeons]] shelf and in the [[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Inner World — A Gazetteer|Gazetteer]].

**The Scourge and the Turn.** The plains lie too far south to see the [[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Scourge of Hell|Scourge of Hell]], and it is on every page of the ledger anyway. The stilts (T4, herd animals on legs as long as a ship's mast) that walk through the Marrick barley in a bad year do not all stop for the boundary stones, and whose contract covers a stilt past the stones is one of the things the annual brawl at Mashi is for. Greywalkers (T5) come behind them at dusk, and those the stud archers shoot on sight. When [[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Scourge of Hell#The Turn|the Turn]] comes to the South, the old wheel's nine years of keeping the Scourge off everybody else, it falls first on the old Dynasty's realms. The Hall answers with remounts by the season, the Elder of Remounts going north with a ledger and a bill for every animal, and a few score horse archers who ride the southern Hem shooting greywalkers off the flanks of the herds from a distance nothing there has yet closed on a gray. In the other two regions' Turns it sells to them instead, and reads each order as advance notice of how much cavalry that side expects to lose.

Bounty crews from every realm buy grays for the Hem at any price. In late summer one will come walking in at the north gate of Qingyuan with an empty saddle, thin and unhurried, having crossed half a continent alone, and the Elder of Ledgers makes a line in the book and sends the buyer's heirs a courteous note about the balance. A good many families in the Midmarch have heard their news that way.

## Neighbors and Grudges

**[[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Vermilion Dynasty of Sum-gol|Sum-gol]].** Sworn. The Hall answers at every Calling and keeps the stall, and has written down how many relay horses the Post bought this year, and how many it did not.

**[[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Sapphire Wells of Brightwater|The Sapphire Wells]].** Upstream. Bad water means sick foals, the Hall has started counting them, and Maud Corwen has been shown the count.

**[[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Copper Coast of Haeseong|The Copper Coast]].** Fellow vassals of the same empty chair, and they have been quarreling about the price of salt since before it was empty.

**[[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Ochre Fields of Hammerford|The Ochre Fields]].** Their border is a line of boundary stones, a shared love of being paid, and an annual brawl. The Yan want every army to need horses, and none of them to win too quickly. The Marrick want a long peace with a lot of small wars in it. They could not be better neighbors.

**The north.** Their best customer and their least attentive one. Every lord in the north who has ever bought a gray has a line in a book on the Grey Plain, and that book is what the Hall offers the next buyer.

# The Moonlight Forest of Mosk-Vaal

*The Moss Court of the Verdant Orcs, one of the four Central Warlords, on the wet west flank of the Inner World's center*

> *GO ROUND.*
>
> Painted on a board at Rootgate in four tongues, with a basket of pears hung under it. The basket is refilled daily, and the board has been repainted more often than any royal portrait in the Midmarch.

Every autumn the fruit-sellers on the bridge at Kingsbridge cry a fat red pear called a Vantry, and every buyer over forty knows why it tastes the way it does. In the wet spring of 683 Captain-General Piers Vantry brought the Third Column of the Aldwater League, a coalition of Midmarch kings that lasted three years, to the edge of the Moonlight Forest, four thousand men in blue and yellow with a hired Marrick company at the center, because the Kessen road round the forest was eleven days longer and he was late for a battle. At Rootgate an envoy seven feet tall, in a cloak of white moth-wings, with eyes like two green lamps, handed him a basket of pears and said in excellent Common that the forest would much prefer he went round. Vantry ate a pear on horseback in front of his staff, remarked that it wanted salt, threw the core in the water, and ordered the column onto the causeway with every drum going. By all accounts it was a glorious sight for six days, blue and yellow banners under a tree the size of a mountain, the lake lit teal from below, and the men singing. At noon on the sixth day the Marrick contract ran out. Two hundred spearmen grounded their ash spears, faced about and walked home between a great many green eyes that watched them go and did nothing, and they told the first tavern they reached that this was the worst thing they had ever seen. On the seventh night the moon came up full, the black water on both sides of the causeway rose by the width of a hand with no rain falling, and the Third Column's rear rank discovered that the people behind them had come up out of it. It took until morning. The water ran red as far as Glowwater, and the moths came down on it in such numbers that the fishers say the lake looked snowed on.

The Court buried the column where it had stood, in its ranks, in the good black silt below the causeway, and planted a pear whip on every man. The orchard is there still: three thousand eight hundred trees in company order, sergeants a pace out in front, the drummers in a neat line on the flank, and on a little rise the largest tree of all, which fruits a week ahead of the others and has the sweetest crop. Dame Emmot Vantry sued for it. She brought her claim before the Fair Court at Harrowgate, arguing that the fruit of her husband was his estate, and the Moss Court replied on a sheet of birch bark that it had no objection whatever. The Fair Court awarded her the pears. A basket has gone to the Vantry family every autumn since, with an itemized bill for the tending, and the family, I am told, has never once been ahead on the transaction.

> **The premise, in one breath.** A nation of wetland orcs for whom death is a phase, living in a shining city grown into the roots of a glowing tree, who ask every army politely to go round and then bury and farm the ones that do not.

I catalog this page with some care, as an archivist of the Guild who has been asked to go round and did.

## The Land

Come down the Kessen road from Kingsbridge and the first thing to change is the color of the night. The Moonlight Forest lies on the west flank of [[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Scourge of Hell|the Scourge of Hell]], in the lowest, wettest ground of the center, where the Aldwater goes to pieces in a hundred channels and the rain that will not fall on the Scourge comes down instead, nine days across by the causeway when the causeway is dry, and it glows. At its middle stands a colossal tree whose whole height shines an ice-blue, reflected whole in a still teal lake, and in the roots of that tree the city of Mosk-Vaal is grown in shining green and gold. Around the shore rise tall stalks that carry their own light, thick as temple columns. Tree-houses sit in the crowns under masses of magenta blossom, and small soft lights drift between them with no one carrying them. On any clear night a single small ship can be seen crossing the lake on its own reflection, and the people aboard are generally selling something.

Out past the lake the forest turns to mire: moss-green glow on every trunk, white moths in drifts, and water like black glass, which is lovely until you have stepped in it. These are the most beautiful places a goblinoid has ever lived in. The orcs will tell you so while something unpleasant happens to your boots.

The light follows the moon, which earned the forest its name. The ground here is rich in Selunite, the lunar kind among the sixty Wellsprings (the sixty types of Aether, the raw power of the world, each of which works on a Soul Crystal in its own way), with Manganthra, the Wellspring of rot, and Verdantia, the Wellspring of growth, lying thick beneath it. At the full moon you can read a contract by the moss. At the dark of the moon the forest sinks to a dull ember and the whole population turns cautious and short with each other. In the street's words the forest is fat ground, which fills a practitioner quickly, and a fighter who has fed on it often comes up short when he leaves.

The Measurewrights cannot agree on what the great tree is. The Court says it is the eldest of the Soul-Trees, and that it knows whose. The Guild survey of 640 calls it a tree that happens to be growing on the richest worked site in the center, a Core in common speech, and notes that the surveyor was asked to go round before he finished. Two later readings dispute both. I have touched the bark. I can report that it is warm and that I felt I was being remembered.

## The House and Who Rules

The Verdant Orcs are Shard-kin, a people descended from fragments that struck the world in the First Impact, and theirs landed in high-resonance wetlands. Where the Ironblood carry the violence of that impact, the Verdant carry its aftermath: stone turning to soil, decay feeding growth. When the Crimson Order made its Refusal and some forty years of grinding expulsion scattered the goblinoid peoples to every quarter, the Verdant bands slipped into the deep wetlands of the interior and were not seen for centuries. When they were next seen they had a country.

They stand six foot four to seven feet, with long vine-like muscle built for moving silently under water. Their eyes are solid lamps of moss-green or pale lunar white. Their skin is the green of old forest, with hard bark along the shoulders and luminous fungus at the joints and collarbone, which they tend and trim as a northern lord tends his beard. An old wound on a Verdant Orc pulses cold silver-green, and they show their scars the way other people show their children.

**The Moss Court** is the house, and it is grown and never built. Its hall is a chamber of living bark in the great tree's roots, widening by about a hand each century, with bark-shapers on retainer to argue with it. The Court has no fixed roll of lords. It sits in a Spoor-Meld, the group meditation in which participants release Aetheric spores into a shared cloud of mind, visible as green mist at knee height, and inside that cloud the tribe shares memory and feeling directly, with no language required. A decision of the Court is what the mist feels like when it clears. Outside envoys are seated upwind and told the result.

The one fixed office is the **Mire-Mother**, the voice that speaks for the Court to anyone who does not have spores. The office goes by lungs. Swamp Parun, the Verdant hand of Parunic (the script and speech that writes boundaries onto laws), runs on a single unbroken breath, so a Verdant working can only be as large as the speaker's chest. When a Mother dies, or as the Court prefers to say, moves on, the candidates go under the black water at Glowwater on the first full moon, with the whole shore packed, the bookies on stilts and three days of fried eel, and each speaks the largest working she can on one breath. The present Mother, **Ssaaleth the Long Breath**, took the office by speaking a full Third Seal (a working of the third and heaviest order of Parunic speech) from the bottom of the lake, and the moths rose off the water for a mile in every direction when it took. She is enormous, cheerful and very old, wears a mantle of moth-wing she has personally eaten most of the former owners of, and has never been known to raise her voice. The Court's words are *We Will Keep It For You*. Its colors are moss-green and moth-white.

## Cities and Towns

**Mosk-Vaal** is the seat, in the roots of the tree, and the name means roughly "the moss that stays awake." Its streets are water and root. You go by punt or along the high root-walks, past fungus-lit doorways and market rafts, and the glow changes hue block by block, as each quarter's gardeners feud over whose moss is finer. Foreigners may enter by leave of the Court, and they are asked to leave their salt at the landing.

**Rootgate** stands at the northern head of the causeway, and it is the one town where outsiders sleep without an invitation. It has the board and the pears, along with innkeepers, money-changers, guides of varied honesty and a fee-house for the causeway. It will also sell you a burial plot before you go on down the rim, at a discount for paying in advance, and it is the only place on the Scourge's edge where the prices fall as your chances do. Half the people on the street are orcs, and the other half are carriers and factors from the Amber Cities pretending not to be nervous.

**Glowwater** is the lake port, a town of dyers and moss-gatherers whose forearms are stained luminous for life, and of little ships with lamp-stalks at the bow. The succession dive takes place off its main stair.

**Ghurash Hollow** is the oldest Verdant settlement on the Guild's roll and holds the greatest of the Ancestor-Rot Gardens. The Verdant dead are laid there in sacred groves, and their decomposition feeds the growth of Soul-Trees, each tree carrying the compressed Aetheric signature of the person it grew from. People come to visit their grandmothers and quarrel with them. Walking through it is walking through accumulated lives.

**The Blooming Mire** is the marriage town, a square league of flowering fen that stinks gloriously in season. Certain phrases of Swamp Parun, spoken on saturated ground, can tip Verdant listeners into a Spoor-Meld they were not attempting, so the wedding speeches here are cleared in advance, and they go wrong every year anyway.

**Totemdeep** is half under water, a town of carved trunks standing in the flood, each one a school's standard. It is where the Verdant fighting lodges train, and its children learn Beast-Sign before they learn to swim, a very short gap.

**The Orchard** is no town at all, yet it appears on every map, and it has a gatekeeper.

## Wealth and Trade

Mosk-Vaal sells what comes up. Its fruit goes north and south by barge and carrier, and the Vantry pear is only the most famous of the cuts; the orchard of the Second Battle of Rootgate gives a small hard quince that a Kingsbridge cook will fight you for. Moss-dye that keeps its glow for a season is the next great line, and it dresses half the festival banners of the center. Moth silk is white and light as breath, and every thread of it is someone's patient life's work. Physic is the third, and it is the one the armies come for: a green rot-wife can pack a wound with a mold that eats the poison from it and leaves the flesh, and the molds cost dear and spoil if taken far from the wet. Tarnish-reagents, cut from the Manganthra pools, go to every alchemist from Sealhall to the Wellheads. Living lamp-stalks, cut green, burn a whole season on a little water, and the lamp-wardens of the Amber Cities have twice gone on strike against them.

The Court also takes two fees. The causeway fee is paid by anyone who insists on crossing, and it is cheap for a carrier and ruinous for a regiment. The reclaiming fee is paid by families who would like a body back, and it rises each year the tree has been growing, for the Court considers by then that the tree has rights.

Soul-Tree wood is never sold. A foreigner found with a piece of it is not asked where he got it.

## Rent, Food and the Street

No one in Mosk-Vaal rents a room, because no one in Mosk-Vaal built one. A household asks a grower for a chamber, waits about nine years while it comes in, and owes that grower's line a share of its work for as long as the room lives. Young couples sleep in a cousin's root. A foreigner at Rootgate pays ordinary inn prices in plain coin, and the blanket is moss, and in the morning it is a little bigger.

The Verdant cook is the finest and most dangerous in the center. Swamp Parun has more words for different kinds of rot than any other tongue has for weather, and a menu reads like a calendar: black-water rice left to turn for three moons, eel smoked over punk-wood, mushroom beer the color of old brass, a cheese that has to be held down, and moon-pears poached in something you would rather not have explained. Dark-moon food is plain and sulky, and full-moon food is an event. Street stalls sell fried moth-grubs in paper-thin bark cones, and on feast nights the entire lake is ringed with cook-fires, each one a slightly wrong color.

The street is small powers from end to end. For a couple of coppers a hedge-worker will ripen your fruit overnight or put a rot in your neighbor's fence that can be traced to no one, and a rind-wife will take the fungus off a sore joint and tell you your future from it. All of them can refuse you, and they often do, because the moon is wrong. Funerals here are merry and long, with drinking at the graveside and a great deal of cheerful advice for the deceased. Mention that you are grieving and someone will ask what you plan to plant.

## Law

Death is a phase, so death is no punishment, and a foreigner who has been told this usually takes it for a comfort until the matter is explained further. The great Verdant crime is against the cycle: hoarding a body, keeping a corpse from the ground, poisoning a garden. The great Verdant penalty is the **Salt Sentence**, by which a convicted orc is buried in salted ground, worked by hedge-workers so that Aether will not pool on it and nothing will grow. The condemned gets no tree, and the family gets nobody to quarrel with for the rest of time. It has been carried out nine times in the record, and every one of the nine is named as a warning to children.

Salt itself is under law. A sealed cask may pass under Court license; a spilled one is a capital matter for the carter, and the carter's employer will get a very polite letter.

A debt does not die with the debtor. It passes to the tree, and a creditor may stand in the garden and demand payment from the tree's living kin, who must come and hear it. In a Spoor-Meld among kin a lie shows, which makes Verdant testimony very reliable and Verdant family dinners very bad. An outsider has no spores to speak with, and so has no standing unless a native speaks for him, for a fee.

Toward everyone else the whole of the law is on the board at Rootgate.

## How It Fights

The Moss Court has never in its history taken a foot of ground outside the wet, and the reason is written into the people's bodies. A Verdant Orc more than forty-eight hours from swamp or kin begins to wither, losing strength by stacking measures until half of what the body can bear is gone, which is what scholars call Isolation Degeneracy and what the orcs call homesickness. In Radiant, Fire or Sun-aspected Domains (a Domain being the territory in which a practitioner high on the sixteen Stages of the climb makes his own law hold) they lose about a third of everything. They cannot go conquering, and no general has ever managed to explain this to the survivors.

At home they are a nightmare with excellent manners. Their fighting lodges go out in grown bark harness over the bark they were born with, moss-green eyes burning under the brow. The move the crowds wait for is **Mire-Mother's Yes**: go under the black water at one end of a fight and come up behind you at the other, with friends. Beast-Sign, the Verdant gesture-language of some four hundred signals, runs a whole company in dead silence, and Crimson Order operatives who have studied its basics call it the most information-dense speech they know. A war-Meld lays green mist along the ground, and every orc in it feels every other, so an ambush moves like one animal thinking. Their big workings arrive slowly, on one enormous breath, and when one lands on an army the rot goes through leather, rope, bowstrings and then men, and the whole field begins to bloom.

They have their own quarrel with the shared arts. The aura coat, the visible coat of a fighter's own color, is *ak-gresh*, the survived skin, in Low Goblintongue, and the Verdant call it *ssuul*, the rind, and insist that a good one should be peeled off and left behind. Pressure (the weight of a higher Stage felt in the room) they call the damp. To sense a stranger is to ask the moss.

The counters are known, and I set them down for fairness. Bring a Sun-aspected Domain and the strength to hold it in a forest that hates it. Take the fight onto salted or bled ground, which means carrying a great deal of salt across nine days of people who object to it. Keep your enemy from kin past the second day, if you can catch one. Or fight at the dark of the moon, when they are at their weakest and most irritable, and bear in mind that everything you kill goes straight back in the ground and is on their side by summer.

## What They Joke About

They joke about everything, at length. The Verdant consider themselves the only funny people in the center, and on the matter of Ironblood they may be right: "Ironblood rings are a very heavy way to remember your own name." They ask a guest how ripe he is feeling, and they tell the Hundred Crowns that they have a lovely spot picked out. The standard Verdant toast translates as "See you later," and it is a threat only if you are not family.

The others joke back. Nobody in the Midmarch will take a pear from a stranger, and "going round" is street talk in six towns for a sensible surrender. The Scalebond note that the Verdant have been making the same joke for eleven hundred years. Everyone agrees that the smell of a moth-wing cloak is one you get used to, and everyone agrees that this is the frightening part.

## Monsters on the Roads

Very little here will bother you if you have been polite. Mourning Pike (T5 on the Bestiary's Danger Tier, which runs from T1 to T9, bred in Fluxia water with teal over the skull) and Sump-toads hold the deep channels, Reaching Briars are regarded as a kind of pet, and swamp-grown Hydra (T8) stories are told to children and also to generals. The orchards are not, strictly, monsters. The wild things are mostly food: eels, frogs and the enormous soft moths whose grubs turn up fried at every stall. Nothing on four legs crosses the mire without someone's leave, and the deer of the outer gardens have a thoughtful look.

The great tree stands on the richest worked site in the Center, and the Court keeps it. The Guild's list of famous dungeons enters it as never cleared, with the note that it has joined the government. The dungeon people talk about is **the Tarnish Pools** below Ghurash Hollow, Manganthra sumps where rot-wives cut their reagents from the upper levels and from which, at the dark of the moon, something older sends up a smell that makes the Soul-Trees drop their leaves. The Mire-Mother's divers have cleared it four times, in the sense of planting a garden on top. The Verdant word for any such place is a garden gone to seed, on the reasoning that something ought to be growing on all those adventurers, and something is. ## Beside the Scourge

The rain that will not fall on [[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Scourge of Hell|the Scourge of Hell]] comes down here, and that one fact draws the forest's eastern edge as if with a ruler: moss and black water on one side, and a day's ride the other way a country that has not seen a cloudburst in a decade. The **Kessen road** runs down that seam. Every sensible carter takes its long loop round the forest, eleven days between the eastern eaves and the Hem (the Scourge's outermost belt, fifteen leagues of black-barked thornwood and pale dust), with the dry wind on his left shoulder going south and green lamps among the trees on his right. The forest calls this stretch the salt road, because that is what comes along it, and letters meant for the real salt road on the Scourge's far flank have gone astray on that account. Wyrms (T7) with scales the size of shields run under the black roots along here, and Hellhounds and Bicorns (T5) work the near rim. The Rootgate board has no opinion on any of them.

The Court's answer to all of it is the one it gives everybody. It lets things come in, and it plants them. A stilt, one of the great herd animals on legs as long as a ship's mast, goes down to the barrel when it wades past the third channel and stays there, and the greywalkers following it in their dust-colored files find the footing going soft and the water coming up behind them. The **Scourge bed** of the great garden runs a very long way down the eastern eaves on what has been fed to it, in trees I could not name and the rot-wives would not, and its fruit is kept for family. As for the colossal bronze-hilted sword at the Scourge's center, with a field of violet stars where its blade should be, the Court holds that it is a stake, holding up something that has not finished growing, and that it can wait.

[[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Scourge of Hell#The Turn|The Turn]] runs North, then South, then Center, nine years each, with the holder keeping a Line of forts around the Hem and the other two counting what it costs. A host bound for the Line on the North's Turn or the South's reads the board, goes round, and gets a pear. The Center holds the Turn now, from 712 to 721, and the Court's share is set by its people's bodies: an orc two days from swamp or kin begins to wither, so it mans no fort past the wet. It takes the dead. Every cart of bodies that reaches Rootgate from the Line is planted free, whoever sent it, and in the Center's Count (the bill each party presents when a Turn ends) the Moss Court's contribution appears as three lines on birch bark, with a cutting attached, which the auditors at Sealhall have to water.

## Neighbors and Grudges

**The Amber Cities of Nalūn** are the great grudge, and the great customer. The Kessen road runs along the forest's edge, and every salt carter on it is a walking insult. The Court buys their coin and their silver and has never forgiven them a single spilled cask, and the councils of the salt cities keep a clerk whose only work is apologizing.

**The Hundred Crowns of the Midmarch** march through. One petty king in every generation decides that the causeway is the short way, and most of the fine fruit trees of the outer gardens have a coat of arms on a little plaque.

**The Rose Coils of Coilspire** treat the same armies and keep a precise record of how many the Court has planted. Relations are polite, and the Scalebond are the only foreigners ever invited to read a garden.

**The Sealed Charter of the Concord** has no good law for a people who are neither Beastkin nor quite on its rolls. It sends a surveyor once a generation, and formal notices rather more often. The surveyors are asked to go round, and the notices come back with things growing on them.

**The Ironblood and the Crimson Order** regard the Verdant as cousins who have gone strange. The feeling is warm and mutual.

**The North and the South** know the forest as the place on the map where armies go missing, and the Moss Court is content with that.

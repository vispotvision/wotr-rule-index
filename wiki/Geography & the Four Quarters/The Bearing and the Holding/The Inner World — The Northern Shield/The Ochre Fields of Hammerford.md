# The Ochre Fields of Hammerford

**Say it** · HAM-er-ford · MARE-ick

*The Long Fields · The Wall for Hire · Shoulder to Shoulder*

Go to Hammerford by the south road and the first thing you will see, before the gatehouse, is a broken cart. It sits by the ford at the little bridge, tipped on one snapped axle, with its tailboard hanging, in a neat cobbled square of its own, and the most dangerous infantry in the Inner World take their hats off when they go past. It has been there since the hiring fair of 631. The carter was a barley-man named Dob Harkness, who broke his axle that morning in the worst possible place, with the only ford for ten miles blocked behind him and, on either bank, the paymasters of two kings who had come to buy the same eight thousand spears. Neither would go around, and each sent men to shift the cart, and both sets of men got into a fight on the bridge. The Captain of the day, Old Marget Marrick, climbed up onto the load in her ochre and black, sat down on a barley sack, called for a jug, and offered the whole levy to the higher bidder on the spot.

It took a day and a night. Every soul in the Long Fields came down to watch, with drums and sausages, and the bookies set up on the bridge. The kings' men shouted their offers across the barley with their voices going, and Dob Harkness, who had grasped his position by about noon, began charging them rent for the use of his cart. The price went past anything a spear had ever been worth. At dawn Old Marget accepted both, split the levy down the middle, had the contracts signed on the tailboard, four copies, and added a clause in her own hand: this hire stands as long as the cart stands at the ford. Both armies had Marrick companies at their center at Sorley Bridge that autumn. Nine thousand went into the ground on the two sides. The Marrick dead were paid to the end of the season, to the widows, on the nail, and the two kings each hired again next spring.

So the cart stays. It has been repaired from end to end nine times by the Guild of Wrights, with every new axle sawn through and the break reset at the proper angle by a man with a sworn drawing. Nobody now living could tell you whether the clause still binds anyone. Every Marrick recruit touches the tailboard on the morning of her first muster, and Dob Harkness's descendants still draw the rent.

The Long Fields are a land that grows barley and spearmen, sells the second by the season to anyone who pays, and has fought on both sides of every war for three hundred years without once taking it personally.

## The Land and the Look

**Hammerford** is a great walled city of rose-brown stone, its long curtain wall and gatehouse facing the cobbled south road, its houses climbing a hill to a tall pale citadel, with that broken cart by the ford and its small bridge below the wall, and green country and blue mountains all around. On a clear evening the wall goes the color of a good loaf. Marrick does not care for decoration, but it does care for a wall, and the masons have been patching this one for three centuries and sign their work.

Beyond the walls the **Long Fields** run out flat and enormous between the [[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Jade Plains of Qingyuan|Grey Plain]] and the marches of the Center, with the rim of [[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Scourge of Hell|the Scourge of Hell]] a long ride north of the last company's stakes. The cobbled south road comes up to the gatehouse, the Kessen road passes on the west on its way to Sum-gol, and the northern roads run up into the Midmarch. It is muster country. After harvest the stubble is laid out in ochre and black squares, a patchwork a day's ride across, each square a company's drill ground, marked with limed stakes and tarred rope. The tent cities go up in rows, cook-smoke over them, drums going from before sunrise, and the spears: long ash spears in the thousands, standing in racks along every road with their shadows falling across the fields like a fence that goes on forever. In summer it is all barley, the color of a lion. Spearmen are one of the two crops, and they come in after the other.

---

## The House

**House Marrick** sells the southern rank. The north fights in knots and wedges and long Keld lines that move like weather. The south fights in a wall, a rank of men standing shoulder to shoulder because they cannot trust themselves to stand anywhere else, and charging in it with a noise like a building falling over. That rank was Marrick's before it was anyone else's. The Long Fields have bred spearmen for three hundred years, first as the levy country of the Empire of Sum-gol, whose foot they were. On the day of the last Calling in 602 the company in the square looked up, saw it was noon and that their contract had expired, and grounded arms. Marrick considers this the greatest day in its history and has a holiday for it.

Its head is **Gerrit Marrick, Captain of the Long Spear**. He is sixty, loud, scarred, very funny, missing an ear, and the only warlord in the south who has fought in every war of the last forty years on at least two sides. He has been asked to wear a crown a number of times, generally by people who wished to have someone to blame, and he says there is no room for one under a helmet. The land is a kingdom by every test but the coronation; the Captain is chosen by the assembled company-captains, for life or until he loses their pay. *"You stand in it because the man on your left is standing in it, lad. Brave's got nothing to do with it."*

The words are *Shoulder to Shoulder*. The colors are ochre and black, in squares.

## Cities and Towns

**Hammerford** holds fifty thousand in a hard winter, and half that in the campaigning season. The citadel is plain, tall and pale, and it holds the Captain's Hall and the strongroom with every contract the house has ever signed. On the hill below are armorers, drum-makers and spear-shavers, who take an ash pole and bring it to true by eye in a long curl of white wood. The Hiring Hall sits on the Ford Square, and on the wall by its door anyone can read what any company costs this season. Rose-brown houses, red tile, and a tavern at every corner named for a battle in which its landlord was on the winning side, or the other one.

**Spearmoot**, which every Marrick and most maps call Pikemoot, stands at the center of the Fields and is mostly a very large meadow with a ring of old stones and a town of inns around it. The Scourge contracts are signed here. The company-captains meet here at midsummer in the Moot to vote for the Captain, settle precedence, fix the season's rates, and break each other's noses in the traditional order. The Moot Law, kept by rote and shouted once a year from the stones, is the closest thing the Fields have to a constitution. A stranger may buy a place on the ring at Moot for about the price of a good meal, and it is worth it.

**Musterfield** is the great drill-ground town to the north, a square league of beaten earth with a permanent street of sutlers, where recruits learn the rank in the stubble after harvest, sixteen deep. It is a raw, loud, cheerful place with very few old men in it. The Midmarch is a few days' march away, and people in Musterfield tend to know which of its kings owes them money.

---

## Wealth and Trade

Companies by the season: that is the crop, the export and the religion. Half the southern hosts in the war had Marrick companies at their center, and some years both halves. The house considers this a professional courtesy. Barley goes out by the cart, with beer, ash poles, drums and very good boots. There is also a brisk trade in veterans, because a Long Fields sergeant past his marching years can name his price as a drillmaster anywhere between Kessen ford and the sea. A cut of every contract goes to the Widows' Chest in the citadel, and the most frightening person in the country is its keeper.

---

## Rent, Food and the Street

Most land is held by company, and a family pays its rent in sons and daughters, a set number to each muster, or else in silver at a rate the Moot fixes and everyone complains about. Those who send a child pay nothing for that year, and the same system means that everyone on the Fields knows someone in every company and goes to all the funerals. In town a room costs about the ordinary [[Geography & the Four Quarters/Value, Coin and Trade/What Things Cost|city list]], and a pitch in the tent lines costs a few coppers a week. People eat barley bread, onions, sour beer, blood sausage, hard yellow cheese, and a stew called *the sergeant*, because it is thick and it has been around for a long time and nobody knows what is in it.

The companies run the place like rival parishes at a ball game. Each has its colors within the squares, a drum-pattern you can tell at a mile, a song with disgusting verses and a long grudge. The Late Arrivals, the Stubble Boys, Mother Hallam's Own, the Sixth Blacks, who are the fourth company of that name and do not wish to discuss it. Men fight in the street over a drummer's honor and share a jug afterward. Hedge-workers cure blisters at a copper the foot and charm a shield-strap for two, and the blister-wives who follow the tent lines are the only people in the country a sergeant will salute. Anyone with an aura coat has a place on the corner of a rank, where one fighter who can take a blow makes the line behind him braver: they say the shine and the weight on the Fields, and a good corner is said to be "shining for the whole file." Bookies take odds on the musters, the Moot and the weather. Minstrels get everything wrong, and the companies pay them to.

---

## Law and How It Fights

**The contract is the law.** A Marrick company fights for its contract to the last day of the season and not one day past it. It has been known to finish a battle at noon on the day its contract ran out by grounding spears in the middle of the field and walking home through both armies, unharmed, because both armies would be hiring next season. Nobody touches a company walking off. That custom has held for three centuries, and a commander who broke it would find no spear for hire anywhere in the South, ever, including his own. Breaking a rank while under contract is death by the file, carried out by the file. The rest is settled by the sergeants, then the captains, then the Moot, and the fines go to the Widows' Chest.

**The rank** is sixteen deep, long ash spears and drums. It works because no one in it can see anything but the back of the person in front and the shoulder at each side, which is exactly why it holds when any sensible person would run. A cavalry charge stops at it. The Yan know this and never try, and go around, and shoot, so the Marrick carry crossbow companies on their wings and big shields in the second rank. A high-Stage fighter can of course cut a hole in sixteen ranks, and the drill for that is older than anyone's grandmother: the rank opens, lets him in, closes, and every crossbow within reach looses at the one target that cannot be mistaken for a friend. A bolt costs a few coppers. They consider the exchange fair. The other drill, equally old, is to send a sergeant over to ask what he is being paid and whether he would consider more.

---

## What They Joke About

Everything, and the dead most of all. A Marrick joke is told in the rank, out of the side of the mouth, in the last minute before contact. They say a Marrick was once offered a place in the world to come and asked what it paid. They say a Lambert will tell you there are four thousand and eleven of you and a Marrick will tell you it's plenty. Of the Yan: they've been running away for three hundred years and calling it a tactic. Of the Moto: you can tell a Moto lord's about to lose because he's stopped looking at you. Gerrit Marrick lost his ear to a Tenrai officer at a battle in which the two were on the same side, and tells that story at every Moot with a new ending.

## Monsters on the Roads

Stilts (T4) come down off the Scourge rim in bad years and walk through the barley, thousands together on legs as long as a ship's mast, and the companies take the contract at a rate fixed by the Moot. Greywalkers (T5) come after them in files at dusk, dust-colored until they rouse. Neiman Lions (T4) cross from the plains after horses. Beasts are the everyday trade: hares and bustards in the barley, foxes in the tent lines, kites following the drums for scraps, and great flocks of crows that make a nuisance of themselves at any muster.

**The dungeon.** It is **the Long Barrow at Sorley Bridge**: nine thousand dead went into one trench there in the autumn of 631, on both sides, and in the generation after it the trench turned, a Mortalis seep (the Wellspring of slow death, venous black-red) pooling under so many men paid to the end of the season. Ghouls (T4) and Skeletal Folk (T4) come up out of it in companies, in step. Every spring the Moot lets a clearing contract, recruits do their first real fighting there, and it has been cleared sixty times and never stayed cleared. More on the [[The Bestiary/Dungeons|Dungeons]] shelf and in the [[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Inner World — A Gazetteer|Gazetteer]].

**Beside the Scourge.** The Fields are the south side of the [[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Scourge of Hell|Scourge of Hell]], and they live with it the way a farm lives with a river that floods. In August the dusty wind from the Center goes hot and starts to smell of iron and pollen, and the farmers north of Musterfield look at the sky and cut their barley green. That is the herds' season, when thirst pushes them outward and what follows them comes too. Every stretch of northern stakes has a drum on a pole for it, with its own pattern, and a company a day's march away that has already asked what the farmer is offering. Cutters, the free-company hunters who work the rim on Thresh contracts, come mostly from here and the Midmarch, and half the funerals everybody goes to have nothing in the coffin but boots.

**The Turn.** The great harvest is [[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Scourge of Hell#The Turn|the Turn]], the old wheel by which North, South and Center each spend nine years keeping the Scourge off the other two. A Southern Turn runs by the season, on contract, with subscription lists at Alabaster paying the bill and Marrick companies standing the Line. It is well run and it stops at sundown on the last day, which the North holds against them, accurately. The South last kept the Line from 703 to 712, and the Center holds it now, which is better still, since the Center pays by instrument and hires nearly all of its rank.

The Fields' own story of it is Lastpenny, on the Kessen road, Midsummer Day of 676. Five thousand of the companies stood in the square in ochre and black with their arms folded while the biggest earth wyrm anyone living had seen ate the Corn Hall and the better half of Butter Row, because their contract began at noon, and the instant the clerk called it the drums came in. They drove the thing back down by lamplight and left nine hundred spears standing in its hide. It still comes up about every eleven years along that stretch, wearing them. Everyone calls it Old Pincushion, a sergeant can tell you by the marks whose pay each spear was, and there is a standing bet at Spearmoot on which company gets the next one in. In the Center a Marrick who declines to help you has said *it isn't noon*, and you are entitled to spit. On the Fields it is what they say when the jug goes round before the contract starts.

## Neighbors and Grudges

**[[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Jade Plains of Qingyuan|The Jade Plains]].** A line of boundary stones, a shared love of being paid and an annual brawl at Mashi. They have never once fought a real battle against each other on their own account, as neither sees what it would pay.

**[[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Holy Sea of Alabaster|The Holy Sea]].** A good steady customer who audits. Lambert officers are the only outsiders to whom a Marrick sergeant will give an honest head-count, since they will get it anyway.

**[[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Vermilion Dynasty of Sum-gol|Sum-gol]].** Old employer, and the holiday still stings there. The Post pays on time when it can.

**[[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Sapphire Wells of Brightwater|The Sapphire Wells]] and [[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Copper Coast of Haeseong|the Copper Coast]].** Both rent the rank by the season. Both have tried to buy a company outright, and been told a company is only for sale by the year, and that the year ends.

**The Moto.** Up the Kessen road past the whole of the Center, and the most lucrative customer on the map. The Fields want a long peace with a lot of small wars in it, and every house at the warlords' table is working out what a Moto civil war will pay; Gerrit Marrick has been heard to say he has already sent in his rates. *Later hand:* the north becomes the Northern Empire of Whitemere in these same years, and the rank has been quoted to it too.

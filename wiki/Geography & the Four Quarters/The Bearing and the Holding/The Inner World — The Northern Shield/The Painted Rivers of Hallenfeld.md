# The Painted Rivers of Hallenfeld

**Say it** · HALL-en-feld

*House Hallenfeld · The Junction · Movement*

Every toll house on the Hallenfeld rivers is painted, and for a hundred and fifty years two guilds have fought over who gets to do it. The Limners of Threeways use gold leaf and paint saints. The Daubers of Chainbridge use whatever is cheapest and paint the reeve, unflatteringly, with his wife. In the spring of Year 699 the Limners, having lost the Long Toll contract, climbed the Chainbridge toll house by night and gave the reeve's portrait a donkey's hindquarters, and the Daubers replied by repainting the Threeways guild-hall door with a depiction of the Limners' master that a passing bishop had to be helped away from. It came to brushes in the street, then to boathooks, and on the morning of the eighth day a Dauber barge carrying the guild's entire year's stock, ninety barrels of ultramarine, ocher and madder rose, was rammed amidships at the junction itself by a Limner lighter, with the crews going at it on the decks in full view of three towns.

It went down in the exact middle of the meeting of the rivers. For a week the Hallen ran blue, the Ruddle ocher and the Sleeve rose, three bright ribbons winding side by side for eleven miles below the junction, and all the swans of the lower valley turned the color of whichever river they had picked. Every washerwoman for a day's walk took it to law. Five men had drowned and two were knifed. A Hallenfeld clerk, Wat Pennyquill, came down to the bridge with his book, looked at it for a while, entered ninety barrels of pigment at the river toll rate, and billed both guilds for carriage. The heralds tell you the realm gets its name from the toll houses. The people on the rivers know better, and if you look at a Hallenfeld sheep's left side in a wet year you can still see why.

The Painted Rivers are three rivers, three roads, and a house of clerks who can stop the whole north with a chain and a sharp remark, and who would prefer to be thanked.

## The Land and What It Looks Like

The river valleys south and east of Kharven-Seat, running down from the plateau and the March to the Greenhollow, low and green-gold and busy, and in thaw or harvest more crowded than any place in the north has a right to be. The realm is a junction: three rivers (the Hallen, the Ruddle and the Sleeve) coming together at the meeting of the three principal roads of the north, and the whole of it packed with barges stem to stern, poled and sailed and hauled, loaded with grain and salt, ironwood, furs, ore and every kind of person. Above the water run the great **chain bridges**, plank roadways hung from iron links as thick as a man's arm between stone piers, swaying under carts. And everywhere on the banks are the **toll houses**: tall narrow buildings with jettied upper stories, painted from the eaves to the waterline in stripes and saints and beasts and scenes of what happens to people who do not pay, blue and ocher and rose and a very great deal of red. The realm looks like a fairground that has been running a tax office for three hundred years.

Above the junction the Hallen comes down off the high country over red rock in a triple waterfall, three white drops one below the next with firs on both sides and misty blue mountains behind, and on the green rise over it stands **Hallenfeld castle**, pale and towered. On the lip of the falls, most mornings, stands a robed figure with a long staff, the house's river-reeve, sounding the water and calling the day's rate up to the castle. The same office keeps the stair behind the falls, and no holder of it has ever been seen to fall. Nothing floats past those falls but loose timber. Every barge unloads at the top or the bottom, and its cargo is hauled round by the portage road under the castle wall, and everything hauled pays. The portage came first and the tolls came after. It is where the house's power comes from, and all that paint is just what the money turned into downstream.

Beyond the rivers it is farmland and alder and willow, with mills every half mile, long straight causeways, and on every high point a **signal post**, a tall timber frame with a basket for the beacon and a hut for the woman who lights it.

### The major places

- **Hallenfeld.** The seat: the pale castle over the falls, the portage road, and below them the junction itself. A loud, damp, wealthy town of counting-houses and warehouses on stilts, the greatest toll in the north, a Fish Market rebuilt with some bitterness since 688, and the Hallenfeld counting-house, a plain ugly building that every courier, quartermaster and road-toll between the Seat and the southern marches answers to. Under its stair is the room where the house's couriers wait, with their boots on.
- **Threeways.** Where the roads actually meet, a day's ride from the water, a town of inns, smiths, wheelwrights and the Limners' guild-hall: the eastern road goes off toward the March and on through the Scarlet Wood to the corridor for Marrowhold, the northern road climbs past the Seat toward the Aurora Reach, and the southern road goes down to Ashbury and the two great roads round the [[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Scourge of Hell|Scourge of Hell]], and the water goes the same way, by barge to Ashbury and the upper Aldwater. People say you can buy anything at Threeways, and sell it back an hour later at a profit.
- **Chainbridge.** The biggest chain bridge in the realm, four hundred yards from pier to pier over the Hallen in flood, and the town that grew up at each end of it and does not speak to itself. It is the Daubers' town. A contraption at the eastern pier raises the middle span in a siege; a guild-master built it, it has jammed twice, and the guild-mark on it is the most boasted-about in the valley.

## House and Rule

**House Hallenfeld** is the kingdom's nervous system. They hold the junction, and whoever holds that junction controls the speed at which the north can respond to anything. They have no glamour and produce no legendary warriors or political masterminds. They produce quartermasters, road engineers, courier captains, and the category of administrator who understands that a message delivered six hours faster can save a thousand lives and treats the six hours as a problem worth solving with the intensity other houses bring to swordwork. They count among the Seven Warlords only in the sense that a heron counts among the great beasts of the river; try taking a fish from one.

The house in Year 715 is run from the counting-house by **Ilse Hallenfeld**, its Mistress: a fine cold hand, a ledger, and a settled refusal ever to leave the junction. The house's river corridor is run under her by a tariff-master, who is expected to keep honest books and is sometimes made to suffer for it. "Clerks and couriers," Ulf Stark says. "They'll carry anybody's letters." They will, and they read Concord, which almost no one else in the north can, and they do not need to open your letter to know what it says from who sent it and how fast.

## Wealth and Trade

Tolls, carriage and paper. A barge pays at the first toll house it passes and every one after that, a rate painted on the wall in pictures for those who cannot read and a second, better rate available from the clerk upstairs. Hallenfeld holds carrying contracts on every road north of the Center: grain for Ashmore, timber for Thornwall, salt bars up from Nalūn, sealed Wellspring casks from the Wellheads, which come round the Scourge by road and go north from Ashbury by river barge for the Seat's ward-cutters. It runs the beacon line (a chain of those signal posts, each lit from the last, that can bring a yes or no from the March to the Seat in a night) and the relay riders, who change horses every twenty miles and will tell you what they think of your road.

The counting-house also holds paper. A Hallenfeld note of hand, written by a sworn notary and witnessed, is good from here to the Sealed Charter, and half the debts of the north are held in that ugly building, each in its own pigeonhole. Lords who have forgotten what they owe can expect a very courteous reminder at a very poor moment.

## Rent, Food and the Street

Everything on the rivers is rented by the quarter and by the foot of frontage, with the rate going up the nearer you are to the water. Bargefolk rent nothing and pay everything. Eel pie, river perch fried in barley meal, black pudding, pickled cabbage, small beer from Barleyford and a hot spiced ale sold from a boat that comes alongside your boat. The whole realm eats standing up. Food-sellers pole around between the barges calling out, and the most famous item in the valley is the Toll Bun, sold at every toll house, in the shape of a fist.

Street life is water and argument. A Hallenfeld child knows the rate on a sack of barley before it knows its letters. Sign-painters are everywhere with a brush behind the ear and a grudge, and a house without a fresh coat is presumed to be in debt. Sects here run as bargemen's clubs and bridge companies, in loud stripes, with brackets fought on the chain bridges in midsummer, one fall into the river being a loss and two being a very cold swim. Lamp-wardens work the bridge lanterns by the night and strike by the season, and when they do the night traffic stops and the toll goes up and everyone pretends to be surprised. Heralds cry the news from the Hallenfeld bridge first, and the minstrels who pass through rewrite it into a song by the second bridge.

**What they joke about.** Delivery. "Six hours" is the whole of the house's sense of humor, and it can be told as a joke by anyone at any time. Thornwall raftsmen. Stark bears, on the subject of paying a toll. Anyone who thinks they can get to the Seat by some other way. The rest of the north jokes about Hallenfeld as well, and those jokes all have clerks in them, and in all of them the clerk gets paid.

## Law and How It Fights

**Law.** The Toll Law: pay or be chained. A barge that has not paid has a chain put across its bow, in plain sight, and a boatman who cuts one loses his boat. The reeve sits on a bench on the bridge itself on market days, in view of the river, and hears whatever comes along. Written contracts are enforced here as nowhere else in the north, because here someone can read them. A sworn notary is a respectable trade and has been known to be knifed.

**The fighting.** Reluctantly. House men wear blue-and-ocher stripes and carry boathooks, short spears and the weight of the law. The house's real weapons are the **chain** (every span and river-crossing in the realm can be closed in an hour), the **halt** (no grain moves, no message moves, and an army a week away starves on the road), and the courier captains, the only people in the house who are any use in a fight, because they have spent their lives getting through other people's fights with a satchel. Where there is real killing to be done, the house pays for it, and the company in question is paid on the day, in full, in the most terrifying way possible, with a receipt.

## Monsters on the Roads

**The beasts** are the river's, and they pay nothing. Eels and perch fill the water, otters raid the fish-weirs, herons stand on every toll house roof with the expression of a clerk, and the swans have been in a bad temper since 699.

**The monsters.** Monsters move, and they live across the whole of the Inner World, and here they use the same rivers as everyone else. Things come up the water. Sump-toads (T5 on the Bestiary's Danger Tier, which runs from T1 to T9; Well-spawn with blued-steel rings on the brow riding level with the surface) turn up in mill-races after floods, and a miller keeps a horse-hoist and a bait lantern for them the way other towns keep a fire bucket. Greater Wolves take the causeways in winter when the river is walkable.

### The Fall Cellar and the Drowned Toll

The realm has one [[The Bestiary/Dungeons|dungeon]] (what a Wellspring Core makes around itself, or what grows up around one), with two names, because its top is a business and its bottom is a debt. The top is **the Fall Cellar**, a dripping stair behind the three waterfalls below the castle, on Phreatis ground (a Wellspring of Fluxia, the Family of fluids and pressure). It is worked, famous for it, and kept by the robed reeve on the lip. Dew beads on your sleeve as you go in, the air smells of cellar stone, and barge-masters top up there by the hour and come out remembering other people's childhoods.

The bottom is **the Drowned Toll**, a flooded stair going on down from behind the middle of the triple falls, leading to a toll house far older than the house, with its rate still painted on the wall in a script no clerk can read, and a Well under it where Mourning Pike (T5, bred in Fluxia water, teal over the skull) grow as long as a barge. It has never been cleared. A Hallenfeld clerk has entered the rate as owing. A chain hangs across the head of that stair, the only one in the realm that the house has never tried to collect on. ### The Scourge and the Turn

The [[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Scourge of Hell|Scourge of Hell]] is four or five days past Ashbury for the fastest rider the house owns, and every Northern Turn on record has gone through this junction on the way. The Turn is the old rotation by which North, South and Center each spend nine years keeping its monsters off everybody else. On the North's Turn the columns come down the roads to Threeways, the grain comes down the water, and the house carries all of it under contract, unloads it at the portage, hauls it round under the castle wall and charges it at the rate, since the old terms give a host the right of passage and are silent on the subject of its bread. For the nine years the beacon line is carried on past Ashbury as far as Halcrest, so that a herd sighted on the north rim reaches the Seat in a few nights, and that is "six hours" at its most expensive.

The North's Count, the running bill the three parties present to one another, is written here. The Count for 694 to 703 fills a wall of pigeonholes in the ugly building, and one of the three hands on its cover sheet is Wat Pennyquill's. The Center holds the Turn until 721, so the North's part for now is to watch and write it down, and the clerk it sends down the Kessen road to Lastpenny for the purpose is a Hallenfeld clerk with a satchel, whom the whole town spits at on principle. He enters that too.

## Neighbors and Grudges

- **[[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Scarlet Wood of Thornhaven|The Scarlet Wood]].** The log-jam of 688 took the bridge, the fish market and the reeve's counting-house. Each year the rate on logs is negotiated as a continuation of that war.
- **The Northern Road Company.** A chartered carrier out of the south with its eye on the northern roads and tolls, and its men standing guard at any bridge a house can be paid to give it. A ferryman on the junction pays the house's toll in the morning and the Company's guards in the afternoon, and nobody has been able to tell him which one is the law. Hallenfeld means to outlast it.
- **[[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Golden Hollow of Ashbury|The Golden Hollow]].** Mutual necessity. Ashmore has the grain and Hallenfeld has the barges, and a wise person does not come between them or try to work out who is using whom.
- **[[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Kingdom of Kharven — The Ashen Crown|Kharven-Seat]].** A customer, and a customer who has a Hallenfeld officer in its cells for keeping honest books. The house has written that down.

# The Rose Coils of Coilspire

*House Scalebond of the Serpentkin, one of the four Central Warlords, physicians and poisoners to both armies*

> *"When did you say that was?"*
>
> The most feared question in the center. In Coilspeech it is a point of grammar.

The war of the Rose Teeth lasted nine days in the autumn of 697 and killed three hundred men, none of them with a weapon. Two armies, one under Marshal Corbel Aumery in northern pay and one under the southern captain Merrin Penhale, had camped on either side of Stoneglass Hollow for the harvest, and on the first morning Aumery's quartermaster climbed the great stair at Coilspire Den with a heavy bag and bought a cask of Widow's Rose, the finest well-poison in the center, at the posted price, from a gold-scaled apothecary in a very good coat. That afternoon Penhale's quartermaster bought the antidote, from the same apothecary, at the posted price. The next morning Aumery's man bought the thing that spoils the antidote. By the fourth day the two quartermasters were climbing the stair together, in step, with porters, and the whole plaza under the great statue had turned out in its colors to watch, with bookies working the steps and rose-sellers doing the trade of their lives. Neither side thought to ask whether the apothecary had any preference. On the ninth day she came out onto the top step and observed, once, clearly, in Common, that these same two families had done this same thing two hundred and twelve years ago, and that the casks now in both camps had been compounded on each other so many times that they contained, in effect, rosewater, a strong purge and a dye. She then went in to lunch.

Both armies had already been dosed. Thirty thousand men sat down where they stood, in full harness, in the sight of each other, for three days, and the battle was conducted entirely in insults. The dye was fast. Every soldier on that field had rose-pink teeth until midwinter, and in the taverns of two quarters men are still pointed out for it. Three hundred died of the purge, and they were treated before the end in the same long ward at Bothhands, on the same terms, in beds that ran in no particular order.

> A country of gold-scaled scholars who remember everything anyone has tried to forget, who treat and poison both armies at a posted price, and who keep their conqueror's giant statue in the main square because it reminds them who lost in the end.

Every archivist in the Guild has been sent to Coilspire at some point to be made to feel ignorant. I went at twenty-six. It worked.

## The Land

The name comes from the ground. On the southeast edge of [[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Scourge of Hell|the Scourge of Hell]], where the salt road comes down toward the Long Fields, the hills run in long, curved ridges of rose-colored stone, turning back on themselves like a sleeping snake, with hot springs on one flank and cold seeps on the other and narrow green valleys wound between. Old ruins and old battlefields lie in those valleys like pressed flowers. The Serpentkin chose this country for that reason: they settle at historically significant sites, Wellspring ruins and event-dense lines of the earth, anywhere the Coiling Memory (their name for the principle that the echo of a thing persists in the world's Aether longer than the thing itself) can be reached. It is rich ground of a peculiar kind. A visitor with any training feels the afterimages before he has found an inn.

**Coilspire Den** is the seat, and you approach it up the finest stair in the world. It opens on a grand ornate plaza of carved gray stone, sweeping steps on every side, glass-globed lamps burning on posts cast as rearing serpents, and pink cathedral spires standing up beyond it against the sky. The crowds on those steps are gold-scaled scholars in good coats, reading, arguing, and being consulted by anxious people from elsewhere. And in the middle of it all stands the statue, colossal and nearly black: a giant wrestling a dragon, a serpent and a beast, all at once, and winning.

The giant is Gorvaine, called the Unanswered, who took this city with an army and raised the statue to tell the world what he had done to it. The serpent is them. His people kept no record of him and his name appears in no history outside this country. The Serpentkin have never moved a stone of it. They keep it spotless. They hold the Reading of Gorvaine on its steps each year, with his full titles and the exact manner of his death, and then children climb on him. Ask any scholar there why they left it standing, and she will ask you who else knows his name.

## The House and Who Rules

The Serpentkin, who go by Scalebond on every Accord roll, were formed in the Dissolution Period of the Second Eon, near the Scorchline Wellsprings, when memory-essence from collapsing realms etched itself into scaled bodies shaped for it. Their function was the condition of their creation. Their spiritual lineage runs to Zseraxil the Coil, ancient guardian of the Anamnesis site called Ancestral Echoes.

A Serpentkin Soul Crystal (the organ that refines the world's Aether into the Essence a person spends) lays down memory in structural layers, and an old one, read with enough skill, looks like sedimentary rock. What they touch, they remember, and what they remember, they can use. On waking they gain Chronos-Vision, the sight of past Aetheric events still standing in physical space, and the great elders stop consulting their own memory and begin consulting the world's. Anamnesis is their native Wellspring, with its faded violet and its smell of old paper and attic dust, and Mirithane, the Wellspring of the mirror, stands close behind. The cost is written into the same stone. A Crystal so full resists all restructuring, and so most of their great ones stall at Stage VIII of the sixteen Stages of the climb, where going further would mean Transcendence, unwilling to pay for it with anything they have kept. I have met four of them, and each regarded the matter as settled by the evidence.

The young take the Coil-Rite, meditating in an Anamnesis zone until the Crystal receives its first deep layer, which holds the shape of the whole lineage and no one person's recollection. I am told that it is like learning you have always known a very long song.

**House Scalebond** rules by the Standing Record, a body of the hundred eldest Crystals in the Rose Coils who are consulted on all matters and who decide very little. Its working head is the **Remembrancer**, chosen by the Record to go and speak to people. The present Remembrancer is **Zsovrath Oda-riss**, whose soul-name means "born into plenty, the coil that remembers," a narrow, unhurried person in a coat of old gold brocade, who is said to have corrected a Palatine's grandmother's birthday during a peace negotiation, in passing, and to have ended the negotiation by doing so. The house words are *It Has Been Tried*. The colors are rose and gold.

## Cities and Towns

**Coilspire Den**, described above, is the capital, the house of the Record and the seat of every notary in the center worth the seal. Under the plaza the living archives run for miles, and they consist mostly of people: rooms of scaled elders, warm or cold by preference, who may be asked things, by appointment.

**Stoneglass Hollow** is the second city and the older one, a bowl in the hills whose walls were fused to rippling rose-black glass by some working no one will discuss for less than a very large fee. A Memory Domain has accumulated there across so many centuries that the town's practitioners live inside it (a Domain being the territory in which a high-Stage practitioner's own law holds, and this one the residue of many such lives). Past events show in the glass if you stand in the right place at the right hour, and the Hollow's guides know the hours. Tourists pay to see the battle of their choice. Armies have camped outside it for nine hundred years, because the water is good and no general believes the stories.

**Bothhands** is a hospital town at the ford below the Den, where the salt road crosses and every army crosses with it, with one long ward for each direction of the march. It has the only street in the center where men from opposite sides of a war routinely share a cookshop, because the physicians enforce it, and no one here has yet been willing to anger the person who is about to set his leg.

**The Pale Fang Archipelago** is claimed by the house, and disputed, and it lies a long way off. The Record will tell you who disputed it first.

## Wealth and Trade

Coilspire sells three things, each at a posted price cut into the stair.

**Physic** first. Serpentkin surgeons are the coldest-handed and the most experienced in the world, and a great many of them can tell you how the last person with your wound died. Every commander of means in the Inner World has a Scalebond surgeon on retainer, and every one of them knows that the other side has one as well.

**Poison** second, compounded by the same people in the next room, and the antidote is always for sale. They consider this an ethical position. A household that buys both is said to have "kept its accounts."

**Memory** third, and the rest is small change next to it. A notary's testimony from living Crystal is sought by every court that will admit it. A Stage VII practitioner standing on a contested field can query it: what happened here, in what sequence, by whom. Heirs pay for that. So do widows, who come in great numbers for memory-weaving, in which a practitioner shares a Crystal layer through sustained touch and matched breathing, and the recipient receives the feeling of a lost time with none of its content. It is the center's great grief-trade, and I have seen hard captains weep on those steps and go home lighter. And then there are the people who come to pay for silence on what they have done. The house has never once sold a secret to a third party, and it has never once agreed to forget one. Its discretion and its menace are the same service.

Rose stone goes out by barge in small amounts. Rose-dye, after 697, has an ironic following.

## Rent, Food and the Street

The Serpentkin are comfortable at either thermal extreme and at their weakest in between, so every Scalebond house has a hot room and a cold room and a short, unwelcoming corridor at the temperature everyone else likes. Rents are set by thermal comfort, and a good steam-room near the plaza costs what a house would elsewhere. The hard part of a Coilspire lodging is the bill: every stair-side lodging-house will take the usual few coppers for a bed in the room of twelve, and the cheapest of them, in Lamp Stair, will also take a true story you have told nobody, at the same value. Half the spies in the center started their careers on Lamp Stair, and they all know the other half did too.

A Serpentkin eats one great meal a fortnight and lies still after it, so the street food is entirely for the benefit of everyone else: hot peppered snails, iced rosewater, flat bread with river-fish and pickled plums, and a pale pink wine that comes from the terraces and is better than it needs to be. Food-sellers on the great stair know their customers' grandfathers' orders. Children sell rhymes giving the kings of the Midmarch in order, and the rhyme takes four hours and has never once been accurate for more than a season.

The petty crafts run to memory too. A stair-wife will find your lost ring by walking you backward through your afternoon, and a hedge-worker will take a stain out of a garment by persuading it of an earlier time. The lamp-wardens who tend the serpent posts are a hereditary company, and they have been on strike exactly once, in 512, and the Record is still discussing it.

## Law

In Coilspeech, a statement that does not say where it sits in time is incomplete, as a sentence without a subject is incomplete in Common. Every assertion carries the speaker's judgment of when it is true, in relation to what came before. So a Coilspire court does not begin with what happened. It begins with when you knew, and a good many foreign liars have talked themselves into a corner at that point, just by trying to say the sentence correctly.

Law here is precedent and nothing else, and all of it is living. A contract needs no paper among the Serpentkin; a notary witnessed it, and the notary will outlast you. Foreigners get a sealed copy as a courtesy. The worst crime is tampering, which covers altered records, a falsified boundary stone or a bought witness, and the second-worst is asking someone to forget. Poisoners cannot be prosecuted for selling. They can be prosecuted for mislabeling.

The Accord's law hangs over all of it. Under the Beastkin Classification Provision a Scalebond is contestable stock unless she carries the scholar-class exemption, and the exemption has to be renewed, in person, in a good coat, at Sealhall. The Accord's historical division treats Serpentkin records as anecdotal, because their source is living memory and not inscribed record. Then, at the end of a long winter, its clerks come quietly up the great stair and ask for the answer. The Serpentkin find this classification clarifying. If you ever hear a Scalebond use that word about you, I would make arrangements.

## How It Fights

Very few armies have thought to find out. House Scalebond keeps a small, exact guard in rose and gold, and otherwise regards a siege as a kind of case. Any host that takes the Coils finds its wells have been considered in advance, its surgeons want to discuss their fees, and its best officers have started receiving small, accurate notes about their families. Gorvaine is the only conqueror who ever got as far as raising a monument, and the notes were how they finished him.

A Scalebond fighter is a slow horror. The move the crowds wait for is **As Previously**: a coiled strike with the exact force that settled this same fight two hundred years ago, against someone who fought like you. They read your body against everyone who ever stood where you are standing, with Chronos-Vision showing them the last man's footwork in the air beside you, and they talk throughout. Their glyph-chains in `Ma`, `Zo` and `En` (Memory, Echo and Lattice, units of Parunic) feed on the Crystal's own layers and never fade, so a chamber they have prepared stays prepared for a century. At Stage VII and past, a fighter's Domain can be queried in the middle of a duel, and it will tell him every feint you tried on the way in.

They call the aura coat (the visible coat of a fighter's own color) *the entry*, as in an entry in a record, and they keep it thin and pale gold. Pressure, the weight of a higher Stage felt in the room, is *the objection*. To sense another fighter is to look him up.

Now the fair counter, which every student of the Coils learns first. Negation breaks them. `Uur` and `No`, the glyphs of Disruption and Negation, produce a gap in the Crystal's filing logic where a backlash ought to be, and a Scalebond hit by a clean negation will stand very still and be, for a moment, truly at a loss. Moderate temperatures dull them; fight on a mild spring day in a shaded field. And the only other reliable answer is to do something that has never been done before, in the whole recorded history of the art, which is harder than it sounds.

## What They Joke About

They joke dryly and with dates. A Scalebond's idea of a great joke is to wait for the right occasion, and they have been known to wait a hundred and forty years. "A Bearkin is the only person who is older than my grudge" is as near as they come to affection. Humans they regard with no contempt at all, as a civilization that consistently fails to consult the available record before repeating a documented mistake, and they will tell you which one. A favorite story among them concerns a young human who said "that will never happen," and it has no other content.

Everyone else has one joke, in many forms. Ask a Scalebond the time and he gives you the year. "A Scalebond has been wrong before, and can give you the date" goes around every market in the center. North of the salt country they say a man with pink teeth has been to school. In the Midmarch, "keeping your accounts" means sleeping with a knife, and in the Moonlight Forest they consider the Scalebond a people who have never once allowed anything to be finished, and they say so kindly, while passing the fruit.

## Monsters on the Roads

Nine centuries of armies have left this country well supplied with Ghouls and Skeletal Folk (both T4 on the Bestiary's Danger Tier, which runs from T1 to T9) on the old fields, and the Hollow's guides know which ones you can tour. Basilisks (T5) and Lamia (T5) keep to the far valleys, where they are entered by name, with dates. The ordinary animals are basking lizards on every warm flank, river-fish in the valleys and peppered snails, which are dinner. Serpents of the plain kind are left strictly alone, and the house will not discuss them.

The dungeon is **the Lower Reading** beneath Stoneglass Hollow, an Anamnesis Well (the Wellspring of memory, faded violet, smelling of old paper) under the fused rose-black glass. It breeds Roll-callers (T6), Well-spawn of the Deeps who speak in the voices of people the Well has killed, and has never been seen above them. The house has not cleared it and regards it as a source. Scholars go down on a rope to hear a dead witness and pay the posted price per question. Stoneglass Hollow itself is the odd case on the Guild's list, a dungeon with a town inside it and guides who know the hours, and nobody has tried to clear it, since no one can say what there is to kill. The Serpentkin call such a place a long memory with a bad temper. They have the names of everyone it ever ate, and their rates are reasonable. ## Beside the Scourge

Every ridge in the Coils has a fine view of [[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Scourge of Hell|the Scourge of Hell]], and the northwestern slopes get its dry wind along with the view. The haze stands along that side of the sky all year, and the vines beneath it give the sharper wine. A survey taken from Stoneglass Hollow reckons the colossal sword at the Scourge's center, bronze-hilted, driven point-down into the mountains, with a field of violet stars where its blade should be, at about three leagues above the peaks. A rival survey from the Windstair makes it four, and the two authors did not speak afterward. The Record holds both, with dates. The Scalebond say they remember what the sword was, and they will tell you for a sum nobody has ever been able to raise. It is posted on the stair, and children recite it.

This corner of the rim takes the Scourge's survivors. Crews and soldiers off the southeast Hem (the outermost belt, fifteen leagues of black-barked thornwood and pale dust) come down to Bothhands by the salt road, and in every edge town on the rim a Scalebond surgeon has a door by the hunting hall, between the renderers and the core-buyers, and knows how the last man with your wound died. Whoever comes out alive is treated at the posted price, and the house keeps a record of everything anyone says in fever. Half of what crews rave about is lies. The other half is the best map of the Black Roots and the Haze that no surveyor ever drew, the only one with the dead marked by name, and you may guess how much it costs. In the late summer of a dry year the great herds push outward and what follows them comes too, and the far valleys fill with greywalkers, long-armed and dust-colored, who have no idea whose old battlefield they are standing on, and are corrected.

[[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Scourge of Hell#The Turn|The Turn]] runs North, then South, then Center, nine years each, with the holder keeping a Line of forts around the Hem and the other two counting what it costs, and the Coils treat all three as they treat both armies. A Northern or Southern commander on his Turn has his Scalebond surgeon on retainer, like any commander of means. The Center holds the Turn now, from 712 to 721, and the house pays its share in surgeons, each one entered in the Center's Count (the bill each party presents when a Turn ends) at the posted price. The Record will recite every Count ever presented, by all three parties, with the arithmetic corrected. No party has asked twice.

## Neighbors and Grudges

**The Sealed Charter of the Concord** is the standing fight, polite and without end. The Record holds the true date of every Accord promise and it reads them out at inconvenient moments.

**The Holy Sea of Alabaster** weighs sworn accounts, and the Lamberts count everything, and each regards the other as a very good amateur. The Sea admits Scalebond testimony on alternate centuries, and this is one of the off ones.

**The Moonlight Forest of Mosk-Vaal** plants what the Coils have failed to save. They get on well, in the manner of two tradesmen who keep sending each other clients.

**The Russet Crown of the Unpapered** gets a quiet fair hearing here. Zsalla Underscale, the Scalebond advocate of the Harrowgate gate, was of this house, and the Record has kept the name of every soul in those pens, including the nineteen who were caught. The Crown would like the list. The Record will sell it to anyone at the posted price, and that includes the Crown, the Accord and the men who hunted them, which is why nobody at Brokencollar will share a drink with a Scalebond.

**The Violet Peaks of Caelspire** owe the Record a very old fine over a bird that fell on the plaza. It accrues.

**The Marrowkin** of the Ember Holds keep a tally of everything cut from their dead god, and the Scalebond keep a parallel count of how the tally has been altered, in a mutual respect grounded in preservation, material on the one hand and Aetheric on the other.

**The Hundred Crowns of the Midmarch** consult the Record on matters of succession and then ignore the answer. It is the most reliable income the house has.

> *In a later hand, in the margin:* The Northern Empire of Whitemere sent a Scalebond surgeon its bill of thanks while it was still being built, and the surgeon sent it back corrected. The great stair has cut no new posted price for an empire. The last one was Gorvaine's, and they are holding it open.

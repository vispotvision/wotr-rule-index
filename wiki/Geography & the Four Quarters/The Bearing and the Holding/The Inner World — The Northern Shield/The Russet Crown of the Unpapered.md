# The Russet Crown of the Unpapered

*House Nobody, the runaway kingdom of Catkin, Wolfkin and Bearkin, one of the four Central Warlords, which the Guild Accord does not recognize*

> *Holder of the lands aforesaid: nobody. Owed by: nobody. To be served upon: nobody.*
>
> The Assessor's return for the Russet Forest, sealed at Sealhall, 668 IC. It is the most expensive clerical error in the history of the Accord, and the Accord has never admitted that it was one.

A Provision Assessor named Pell Wickham rode up to the landward gate of Brokencollar in the autumn of 668, with a clerk, a roll-book and forty coated bravos of the Red Bar school in their red and white, to enumerate the unlicensed beastkin of the Russet Forest for the purposes of the Labor Provision, which classes any Beastkin without sealed papers as available for sale. He set his table up in the old sorting yard, at the foot of the great heap of cut iron collars, which even then stood higher than a mill. He called the first person forward, an enormous Bearkin in a fir-green apron with a chair she had brought herself, and asked for her name and her owner. "Nobody," she said. The clerk asked her to repeat it, and wrote it down. The Catkin behind her was named nobody and belonged to nobody. So were the four Wolfkin after that, in chorus, and by noon the whole town had come down off the terraces in its painted fur and its brightest cloth, four thousand one hundred people in a line that went twice around the heap, with cider going along it and fiddlers working, each stepping up in turn to give the same answer to the same tired man. Wickham's bravos looked at the line and then up at the ring-walls, where a great many pairs of eyes were looking back, and they asked him quietly whether it might be a good idea to finish the job.

He finished it. He was a conscientious man. He carried the roll back to Sealhall and entered it, and sealed it, because an Assessor swears to record what is declared. It took the Accord's lawyers about a year to understand what he had done. By the Provision's own wording, a writ for the recovery of property must name the holder, and the holder of the whole country, in a good clear hand under a green seal, was nobody. Nothing could be claimed from them. No one could be sued. A raid on the forest was now an assault upon no one's land by no one's warrant, and the licensed returners who hunted runaways for the bounty could not get paid for it. The Accord has spent the better part of fifty years trying to file a correction, and a correction must be served upon the party concerned.

The town held a feast that lasted a month. They hammered the first collar ever cut there into a band and put it on the head of the oldest runaway they could find, and it had gone a fine deep red by the first rain, and they have refused to clean it since.

> A kingdom of runaway Beastkin in a forest of permanent autumn, whom the Accord will not recognize and whose own rolls file them under "nobody," so they took the name and put the rust on as a crown.

I keep this page at the request of no one, and the Guild's Beastkin desk has twice asked me to stop.

## The Land

The Russet Crown holds the east of the Center, around a bright inland sea, with the salt road and the Concord's uneasy flank on its west and [[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Scourge of Hell|the Scourge of Hell]] beyond them. Inland from the water lies the Russet Forest, in hill country, and it has been autumn there for as long as anyone can say. Oak and beech and great old chestnut stand in every shade from rust to bright gold, the fallen leaves lie knee-deep on the slopes, and the air has smoke and apples in it in every month of the year. New leaves bud, turn and fall at once, on the same branch, without any winter ever arriving. The Measurewrights have sent three surveys to explain this. One blames a Verdantia site gone out of its season, one blames the Bearkin who have been Weight-Bound here since before the count, and one blames the surveyor before it. The Bearkin were asked and said that it was fine.

The forest towns stand on stilts over the leaf-drift and the floods that come down off the hills, walkways and ladders and rope bridges strung between platforms, everything carved and painted because nobody here was ever allowed to own a painted thing before. People go about in bright cloth with their fur dyed in bold bars, stripes and handprints, in colors that would earn a flogging in a licensed pen. You can tell how long someone has been free from how loud they are.

## The House and Who Rules

There is no blood in this house, and that is the entire idea of it. **House Nobody** is anyone who has arrived and spoken. Its people come in three kinds, with old law and older habits between them.

**Catkin** (Feline, in the Accord's terms) make up the largest number, and they are among the most frequently enslaved people in the world, because human buyers find their faces pretty. They descend from the Clawbound Accord, formed during the Watcher's Eclipse, and their Soul Crystals (the organ that refines the world's Aether into the Essence a person spends) store feeling as a reservoir and turn it to speed. In the Spirit War their ancestors were the Concord's preferred assassins, and they hold no parades for that. Their discipline is the Emotional Covenant: before any important act a Catkin stands still and counts up what she is carrying, and decides whether it is ready. **Wolfkin** (Lupine) are the most hunted, under the "wild beastkin clearance" provision, and their tether-bonds and the slow death of Isolation Decay make it easy to run one down alone, so they do not go alone. They run on the Pack-Soul, in which enough of them close together think as one, bound palm to cut palm under a full moon by the Kinbinding, and they keep their history in the ground by the Howls of Mourning, sung into the local lines of the earth. Their packs guarded collapsing Wellspring sites during the Verdant Siege, under the Moonwake Accord, and received formal recognition for it and no membership, and they understood the difference. **Bearkin** (Ursine) were here first, and they are generally free-status where documented, which puts them in little danger and makes their staying a very pointed thing. A Weight-Bound Bearkin has tethered her soul to one mountain or glade and feels what it feels, and a Stillness Trial is two of them standing under pressure to see who breaks stance first, which can take up most of a festival. When the first runaways came through in the last days of the Third Eon the Bearkin of the Glade of the Slumbering Root looked at them, looked at the hunters coming up behind, and declined to move. They have been declining since. Catkin and Wolfkin share the oldest beastkin alliance there is, and the Wolfkin and the Bearkin have an old saying between them: *Lupine move; Bearkin stay.*

**The Russet Crown** itself is that hammered band of collar-iron, rusted the red of the forest, and whoever wears it is the Queen or King of Nobody for as long as the town allows. The Crown holds three powers: to speak to outsiders, to call the muster, and to be blamed. The one requirement is a scar at the neck. The present Queen is **Sefa Tuen-zhiv**, whose soul-name means "born in flight, charged with quickness," and who was sold twice as a grown woman, first as "skilled labor" and then at a rather better price to a man who wanted a rival's accounts stolen, and who describes that second owner's death in detail to visiting envoys while they are eating. She is a small, gorgeous, furious Catkin in a coat of nineteen colors, and has held the Crown for eleven years. She can vanish in the middle of a sentence and finish it behind you. The house words are the one word of the Name-Keeping, *Ashe*: I remain. Its colors are russet and gold, in any combination the wearer can afford.

## Cities and Towns

**Brokencollar** is the seat, and you can see it from a day out on the water: a great walled city of russet roofs terraced up a rocky headland over the sea, ring on ring of high walls, a domed hall, needle spires on the summit and a sheer stone platform jutting out from the cliff, all of it peach-colored in the low sun. The Accord built it, as the holding fortress of the labor trade, and every one of those rings faces inward, because they were made to keep people in. The platform is where the barges loaded. The runaways took it, on a night Sealhall has never entered, and they live in it now, with their washing on the inner walls and doors knocked through wherever anyone wanted one. When the rings filled they spilled out of the landward gate, and at the fortress's foot a stilt-town of nine thousand stands around the old sorting yard and around the Heap. The Heap is what it sounds like: every collar, shackle and neck-ring ever cut off a runaway in that place, thrown down in one spot since the Third Eon, grown now to a rust-red hill with a stair up it and small shrines and flowers tucked into it by people visiting their own. At its foot stands the **Underhill Forge**, kept by the descendants of Orsa Underhill, the Bearkin smith who lifted a pen gate off its pins at Harrowgate. They will cut any collar free of charge, on any day, and the only price is that you hold the tongs for the next one.

**Shadowed Ridge** is the Catkin town, and its map is carried in the heads of those who live there. It is very hard to find from the outside and instantly obvious once you are in it, since it is built around the shadow-map its residents share and has no visible landmarks whatever. The Clawbound Trial is held on the Ridge, where a young fighter must shadow-walk through ground her elder has anchored, following a map written in darkness, and come out bound to the elder for life, with the burden running both ways.

**Nightveil Hollow** is the second Catkin town and the Crown's great den of thieves, forgers and retired assassins. Its people go to the Dusk Quarter of Altherion and back by roads nobody else knows. Most of the good false papers in the center start here. Fine penmanship is the most respected art in the Hollow, and the master forgers walk around with bodyguards.

**Greyfang Crossing** is the Wolfkin town at the river ford on the Concord road, set where it is for reasons of lunar sight-line that no outsider can follow, and it holds the border. **The Shrine of Nine Howls** stands in the hills above it. A pack goes there to sing their dead into the ground, and it can be heard in Brokencollar on a still night.

**The Glade of the Slumbering Root** is the Bearkin heart, among the oldest continuously occupied places in the world, its stone halls unchanged since the Age of Glacial Memory. Its people keep a three-day silence every year for the Siege of the Seventh Fold, where the Bearkin held the final line for three days while a Domain (the territory in which a high-Stage practitioner's own law holds) collapsed around them, so that other peoples could escape, and none survived intact. They call the silence recognition, and they will not have it called grief.

## Wealth and Trade

The Crown is poor and proud of it, and it has more money than it admits. Chestnut flour, smoked trout, strong honey and russet cloth go out by the sack and the bale, with a good deal of information folded in the cloth, along with apple spirit that the Accord's excisemen have been unable to tax because they cannot identify the owner. Bearkin workings are the great export; glyph-chains in `Ir`, `Tar` and `Ky` (Endurance, Weight and Structure, units of Parunic) last for decades without renewal, the only inscribed beastkin work that outlasts its maker, and a Glade-cut lintel on your door is the best ward money can buy. Catkin scouts, spies and knife-hands hire out by the season, to both armies, with a clause that no one on the job will wear a collar or guard anyone who does. Those fees are large.

Then there is the **Long Way**, which is both the Crown's reason for being and its worst drain. The Long Way is a chain of safe barns, friendly ferrymen and bought clerks running from Verath to Harrowgate and beyond, down which runaways come. A pen-breaker's work is expensive. It costs far more to buy one person out of a licensed pen with good papers than to buy a good horse, and the old price at Verath Township is seventeen gold marks a head. The Crown spends every bit of what the fighters and forgers earn on that, and it has never yet paid for anyone who could not be stolen instead. Forged free-holder papers go out by the hundred, and they are good enough that the Accord now charges a fee to check its own seals.

Do not ask at any stall for furs. They will think you are joking, and you do not want them to find out otherwise.

## Rent, Food and the Street

A runaway owes nothing for a year and a day. After that a household owes one day in seven to the common work of the town, on the stilts, the roads or the watch, and that is the whole of the rent. Coin from outside is good everywhere, and locally people pay each other in **collar-bits**, small sawn pieces of cut neck-iron stamped with a thumbprint, each good for a day's work and no use at all beyond the first ford. They are considered unlucky to hoard.

The eating is loud and generous. Bearkin bring their own chairs to it. Chestnut bread and smoked trout and blackberries stewed with venison come out on long tables on the platforms, and cider is passed around in anything that holds it, by people who remember a pen ration and take a second helping out of principle. Above the leaf-drift the walkways are full all night, because a Catkin's day runs from dusk. Pickpockets practice on each other and return the takings. You will hear three languages on any bridge: Purrspeech, with a second conversation running under the first that is usually about you, Moontongue with its growl even in a greeting, and Urganic Growlscript, in which ordering breakfast takes a while. Small powers are for sale at every ladder-foot: a Wolfkin who can tell you how your brother is, without being told his name, by the weight of him; a hedge-worker who takes the smell of you off a road, for a price, and will refuse you if the hounds are too close and she has her own children to think about.

The other thing you will see on the street is the bad day. A woman in her thirties stops walking in the middle of a bridge and stands shaking at a sound. A man cannot sit with his back to a door. Nobody in Brokencollar remarks on it. Somebody comes and stands next to them, and in a while they go on.

## Law

The whole constitution is one rite. A person who arrives stands at the foot of the Heap and speaks her soul-name, the name of her birth and her charge, aloud, and whatever else she has lost: *Fen Tuen-zhiv, Baru*, "the last thing, Tuen-zhiv, spoken aloud." Whoever is nearest answers *Ashe*: I have heard, you remain. That person is now her witness, her *ndoto*, for life, responsible for her first year and answerable for her. That is the whole of citizenship. Nothing is written down.

The refusal of writing is the deep law of the country, and it is meant. Paper is how they got you. No birth, debt, marriage or land in the Crown has ever been recorded, and to keep a list of names here is the one crime for which the town will hang you without a hearing, because a list can be stolen, and the people on it can be found. A dispute goes to the witnesses of the parties. Bearkin elders hear what the witnesses cannot settle, slowly, at a pace that encourages settling. Theft from a Crown neighbor is fined and theft from an outsider is admired.

The Accord's law applies, in the Accord's view, to every inch of this. Any Catkin or Wolfkin here without sealed papers is contested stock, and a hunter who catches one beyond the forest eaves may lawfully bring her to a licensed pen. Inside the eaves the law of 668 holds, and a returner cannot be paid by any owner. Sealhall keeps a standing Enforcement line for its own collectors to go in anyway, on forfeiture, and their iron comes out cut and goes on the Heap. So the border is a matter of feet, and there are people on both sides of it who know exactly where it runs.

They do lose people, and I record it, as the Crown will not. A bad decade sells off two or three hundred at the fairs. A raid by men who do not care about the fee is simply a raid, and there have been children taken from the outer platforms, and a whole Wolfkin hamlet at Lower Sedge run down in a single Lunar Inversion, when the moon had them half out of their minds. There is a Howl for it. Every year the Crown's knife-hands go out to find the names of those responsible, and the Accord's rolls show a higher than usual mortality among licensed returners in the weeks that follow.

## How It Fights

House Nobody has no army, and it keeps three thousand people at the ready who would tell you the same. What comes at you is a tactic that the Hobgoblin staffs have studied with envy.

The Catkin go first, and you will not see them. **Before You Decided** is the reservoir going off through one precise step, and the knife was already in you when you thought about parrying. Then the Wolfkin: the **Nine-Howl Run** puts a whole pack in one gait, and the distance you were relying on stops existing, since inside a Lupine Pack-Hunt Domain the space between predator and prey obeys the pack. Then you turn around to retreat to the ford, and there in the middle of the ford is a very large Bearkin, in an apron, who was not there this morning and has no plans to leave. Inside her Earthen Domain things weigh more and fast techniques cost more, and **The Mountain Has Thought About It** waits on the far side of a lifetime of patience. The muster is called by Howl, and from the time of calling, the first knives are moving before the sound has crossed the valley.

The words for the aura coat (the visible coat of a fighter's own color) start fights here: Catkin call it *the lie*, since the skill is in not showing one; Wolfkin say *the pelt* and ask whose; Bearkin call it *the weather* and do not understand why you are worrying about yours. Pressure, the weight of a higher Stage felt in the room, is called *the old man* by all three, in memory of whoever held the whip. To sense someone coming is to smell the iron on them.

The counters are known to every licensed returner. Light hurts them: a Catkin's lattice is built for the space between light sources, and in a Radiant Domain she loses nearly a third of everything, which is why the good raiders march at noon with priests. Structure backlashes on Lupine, for `Ko` and `Al`, Order and Edge, land in the Pack-Soul like a wall appearing in a field. Run a Wolfkin alone past a few days and Isolation Decay will finish what you started. Wait for a Lunar Inversion and Bloodmoon Corruption will turn the pack's great strength into a screaming thing with no plan in it. As for Bearkin, go around them; everyone does, and they will still be there when you come back.

## What They Joke About

They joke about their name, mostly, and they have heard yours already. "Who's your king?" is the oldest joke in the center and every child in Brokencollar has a better answer to it than you do. A toast goes "To the health of nobody," and a curse goes "May you be somebody." When a thing goes badly, they say *the Assessor will hear of this*. A Catkin will tell you that a Lupine cannot go to the privy alone, and a Lupine will tell you that a Catkin will swear she was never there, and for once you should believe her. Everybody tells jokes about the Bearkin, slowly, in the hope one will reach them in time for the next feast.

Outside the forest, an Accord clerk who has made a very stupid mistake is said to have "enumerated." Market thieves all over the center give their name to the watch as Nobody, and it does not work, and the watch writes it down anyway, out of a sort of tired love.

## Monsters on the Roads

Sasquatch (T3 on the Bestiary's Danger Tier, which runs from T1 to T9) in the high glades, Tanuki (T2) who pick the same pockets the Catkin do, and in the leaf-drift the long low things locals call drift-eels, that hunt by the shiver in the leaves and are the reason nobody walks at ground level after dusk. Returners, licensed hunters of runaways, are entered in the same list by local habit. The beasts are deer, trout, boar and the fat acorn-hogs that fight back, and a Brokencollar child's first real fight is generally against a hog.

The dungeon is **the Seventh Fold**, up in the hills beyond the Glade, where a Domain collapsed around a Bearkin line in an old siege and left a pocket that will not settle. The ground weighs more the further you go in and the fast get slow. Its Mouth is three days from anywhere. The Glade's people keep it, in the sense of standing near it. Crews have another name for the whole forest, **the Autumn Country**, which the Guild's list enters as a *lying-down*, a dungeon with no roof, with an edge you can walk across without noticing and a center nobody has mapped, and as Verdantia (the Wellspring of growth) by one survey of three. It is inhabited and closed to surveyors. The Unpapered do not care what it is, and have reasons to dislike people arriving with forms. More on the [[The Bestiary/Dungeons|Dungeons]] shelf and in the [[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Inner World — A Gazetteer|Gazetteer]].

## Beside the Scourge

Below the Charter's edge the salt road runs with the forest eaves on its east and the Hem of [[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Scourge of Hell|the Scourge of Hell]] on its west, the Hem being the Scourge's outermost belt, fifteen leagues of black-barked thornwood and pale dust. The breeze off the inland sea keeps the worst of the heat out of the chestnut woods. Nobody here has signed anything about the Scourge, and the things that come out have never asked to see a roll, so the Crown fights them anyway. In the late summer of a dry year the Howl goes up, the Catkin go out along the eaves ahead of whatever is coming, the Wolfkin run it down, and in the morning there is a very large Bearkin in the ford.

Catkin out of the Russet Crown run some of the best-regarded bounty crews on the eastern side, working out of Goodmorrow on the salt road, and they are paid in actual money, which goes down the Long Way. A good many of their people learned the trade at the back of someone else's line. A licensed labor-stock porter hired out of Verath is equipment on a clearing's instrument, written *nemo* along with the rope, and a porter who comes over the wall has generally seen more of a dungeon than the captain who hired him. That is why Brokencollar has so many people who can read a fork, the brass assay instrument that rings truer as the ground thickens. The Crown's crews sign under the same clause as every Crown hire: no one on the job wears a collar or guards anyone who does.

[[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Scourge of Hell#The Turn|The Turn]] runs North, then South, then Center, nine years each, with the holder keeping a Line of forts around the Hem and the other two counting what it costs. The Center holds it now, from 712 to 721, and "the Center" means everybody who lives along the rim, which is a story in itself here. The Unpapered are on no roll. The Center's Count, the bill audited at Sealhall when a Turn ends, must carry this stretch of the rim as held by nobody and owed nothing, because the Crown fights regardless and bills nobody, and I am told it is the most aggravating line in the document. On the North's Turn or the South's a host may march through anyone's land by right of passage. Every marshal who has come up the salt road has been advised by his lawyers that the forest is nobody's, and by his scouts to stay on the road.

Asked about the colossal sword at the Scourge's center, with a field of violet stars where its blade should be, an elder of the Glade thought about it for most of a festival and said that it was a long way off.

## Neighbors and Grudges

**The Sealed Charter of the Concord** is the whole of the matter. The Accord does not recognize the Crown and has a standing committee on how to do so without having recognized it. Sealhall's lawyers, every generation, produce a new form of the correction, and every generation someone goes through the wall of their counting-house.

**The Rose Coils of Coilspire** gave them Zsalla Underscale, and the Crown honors her name at Brokencollar. They also hold a complete list of everyone who has ever passed through a Harrowgate pen, for sale, at the posted price. The Crown has been trying to buy it, and burn it, for a century, and has also tried to steal it, and this is why no Scalebond is served a drink at the Heap.

**The Violet Peaks of Caelspire** sell the Accord its wind and shelter the fowlers' victims, depending on which school you ask. Some of the Long Way goes over those high roads, and the Crown keeps a list of its friends up there in the only place it keeps any list, which is in the head of one old Catkin.

**The Moonlight Forest of Mosk-Vaal** accepts Crown dead when asked, on the Moss Court's terms, as a matter of neighborliness, and has planted a small and very well-tended garden of them. Not a single returner has ever crossed the causeway in pursuit.

**The Hundred Crowns of the Midmarch** sometimes offer the Queen a marriage alliance and are always surprised by the reply.

**The Iron Mandate of Undaar-Keth** has Hobgoblin staff officers who hire Catkin scouts, and who call the pairing the most uncomfortable military alliance that consistently wins engagements.

**The Ember Holds of Marrowhold** have an honor-bound kinship with the Bearkin of the Glade, forge-culture and endurance-culture having enough in common that the two need no contract, and a good deal of the iron that the Crown does not get from the Heap comes down that road.

**The Amber Cities of Nalūn** buy forged papers, Crown knives and honey, and pretend not to. **The North**, for its part, has a Banner House or two that hold their own Beastkin under older words, and a few northern lords quietly send coin to the Long Way for reasons that are their own.

> *In a later hand, in the margin:* The Northern Empire of Whitemere, in the years it was building, sent the Crown a letter. It was addressed to Nobody, and they thought that was quite good. The Accord's correction was still pending in my time, and I am told that the file now fills a room.

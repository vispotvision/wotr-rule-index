# The Sapphire Wells of Brightwater

**Say it** · BRITE-wah-ter · the WELL-heads

*The Wellheads · The Leasehold Kingdom · The Spring Country*

The Warden's cask-men caught Annis Blewitt drawing water out of the Hotwell overflow for her dye-vats in the spring of Year 647, and took her to Brightwater for it. Nobody could have called it stealing, in the ordinary way, since it was running into the ditch at the time. But the lease said water from a kept pool was sold by the cask, and the Warden then, a long stringy man named Ferris Corwen, had just raised the cask by a third. Annis Blewitt was the mistress of the Dyers' Guild of Kingfisher Steps. That overflow did something to woad that no other water in the world would do, and it made a blue so violent that people had been known to stop and take their hats off to a bolt of it. She paid the fine on the steps of the Warden's Hall in clipped silver, told him he could keep his ditch, and walked home.

On the third morning, every dyer on the Steps tipped out her vats into the top pool at once. It went down the terraces in a sheet. It went through the bathhouses and the laundries and the cheese-cellars of Brightwater, it went into the river and the barge basin, and it dyed every cask-man's breeches where he stood. It dyed the Warden's white mare, four hundred sheep, a bishop on retreat, the whole of a Guild assay party that had been wading in to find out what was happening, and the river itself, a clean roaring kingfisher blue, all the way down to the barge-towns for nine days, with people running along the banks in their thousands with buckets and bedsheets and a hired band of pipes and drums. Six dyers were held under to the shoulders in the last vat by Corwen's men and came up with arms that never faded. Then the Guild's own factor at the barge basin, a man of practical mind, sent up to ask what the Warden wanted per bolt.

The settlement took two years and the whole Wellhead lease was redrawn around it. To this day the Dyers pay a third less for their water than anyone else, and any Corwen who raises it must do so on the Steps, in person, with his sleeves rolled up. Ferris Corwen, who was no fool, put the color on his banner before the river ran clear. You will still meet old families on the Steps with one blue-armed ancestor in the portrait, and "blue to the elbow" is what the spring country says of anyone who has been in a fight with the management and lived.

The Wellheads are a high steaming upland of pools in impossible colors, the richest ground per acre in the South, held on a Guild lease by a cheerful family who keep the keys to everyone's power, and whose water, since the war, has been going wrong.

## The Land and the Look

Up through mist, on a wet road, and you hear the place before you see it. The Wellheads steam in the uplands at the heart of the South, with the iron hills of Sum-gol off to the west and the Jade Plains opening to the east. They are a high, wet, luminous country of hot springs and cold springs side by side, terraced down the hills in step after step of pale stone, every pool a different color from milk-jade to rust, from a green that hurts to a sapphire so deep and clear you can watch a dropped copper fall for a long time. Steam stands over all of it in the morning and goes gold when the sun comes through. Brass is everywhere, brass-bound carts, brass wellmouth rings, brass stair-rails kept polished by small boys, an assay post on every wellmouth with its tuned fork humming. Kingfishers are real here and shameless. They dive through the steam and come up blue as the banners, and the house has them on its spoons.

Under it all is the hum of the kept pools, so constant that Corwen children who leave home cannot sleep for the silence. A scholar calls this rich ground, and the street calls it fat. Any practitioner standing here pulls Aether from the air and the water far faster than down on the plain, and within a day feels twice the size she is and is liable to pick a fight about it. Below them the springs have drowned an old gorge to the brim, with the minerals in the water giving it that color, and past the gorge the land falls away to the river, the cask road and the western edge of the [[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Jade Plains of Qingyuan|Grey Plain]].

## The House

**House Corwen** is a chartered family, the only one of the seven southern houses that holds its ground by Guild lease rather than by blood or conquest, and the richest per acre. It left the Empire of Sum-gol in the most Corwen way possible, sending word to the last Calling that the casks had been sold elsewhere, and then took out a lease from the Guild Accord on the entire spring country the moment there was no emperor to object. It is a kingdom of its own by any reckoning a southerner would use, with its own law, levies and table at the warlords' council, and it could in theory be evicted by anyone holding the right piece of wax. The house has spent a century making sure nobody can find that piece of wax.

Its head is **Maud Corwen**, Warden of the Wellheads, Lessee of the Guild, a big, cheerful, sharp-eyed woman who knows the lease terms of every spring in the south by heart, and who has never in her life been seen to drink the water. She takes no crown, says the lease does not provide for one, and has a very fine brass one in a box. The words are *The Water Keeps Faith*. The colors are kingfisher blue and brass.

Every worked Wellspring site (a Core, in common speech) under Guild lease from the Wellheads to the southern sea sells its hours through a Corwen keeper, and the keeper can close it. That is the true crown. The keepers, in blue coats with brass keys at the belt, can seal a site in under an hour if paid and willing, and a keeper in a bad mood has ended more arguments in the South than Marrick ever did.

## Cities and Towns

**Brightwater** stands at the foot of the spring country, twenty-five thousand people in a white town with red-tile roofs, stacked on sheer rock stacks and arches over the brilliant sapphire water of the gorge. Stone and rope bridges run between the crags, sailboats work the water, boats are drawn up under a great arch, and horses stand about on a sunny terrace looking pleased with the view. In it are the Warden's Hall with its brass doors, the Cask Yards where coopers hammer from dawn with a noise you can hear on the Plain, the Lease Court, and the barge road going down to the river. Every morning the sealed casks go off downhill toward the north, and everyone comes out to count them, on principle.

**Hotwell** is the bath town, cheap and bawdy, built around a boiling pool in a bowl of the hills. Sick men, old soldiers, Marrick companies between contracts and anyone who has been told by a physician that it might help come to sit up to their necks in water the color of tea and tell lies. There are eggs boiled in the street gutters, there is a fighting ring on a raft, and there is no record of anyone in Hotwell ever having been sober at a funeral, including the corpse.

**Kingfisher Steps** is the terrace town, thirty-one tiers of pools and workshops down the side of a steep green valley. Here are the dyers, the glassmen and the brass-founders, every wall hung with drying cloth in blue, and the richest invalids in the Inner World being carried up and down in chairs by men with calves like bedposts. Dye-water runs down the street gutters in stripes. Anyone who has argued with the Warden gets a free drink on the top landing.

## Wealth and Trade

Power in the ground, by the hour and by the cask. Sealed casks of Wellhead water go down by barge and north by brass-bound cart, round the [[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Scourge of Hell|Scourge of Hell]] by one corridor or the other, to the ward-cutters of Kharven-Seat and half the alchemists of the world; the ward-cutters who re-cut the wards on the Seat's rock each spring quench their chisels in water out of these casks, at a price Maud Corwen sets, and have been known to say what they think of it. Every practitioner wanting hours at a rich site pays a keeper. Every sect that wants a training pool for its core disciples pays a keeper more. Add bath-money, blue cloth, brass work, physicians' fees, and a thriving trade in small sealed vials sold by persons with no connection to the house to pilgrims who will not know the difference.

## Rent, Food and the Street

Rent is steep in season and steep out of it, with a room in Brightwater running above the [[Geography & the Four Quarters/Value, Coin and Trade/What Things Cost|city list]] and a lower price in winter for anyone who can stand the damp. Everything is cooked in the springs because the fuel is free. People eat eggs boiled in a net, trout steamed over the vent, watercress, sulfur-tasting black bread baked in hot sand, pickled plums, and on feast days a whole sheep lowered into a pool in a basket and hauled out with a good deal of shouting. The poor bathe for nothing in the overflow, and the rich pay a hundred times that to be alone.

Hedge-workers work every ditch here, reading pools for a copper and charming a bad knee for two. Brass-boys polish all day and fight all evening. Lamp-wardens from this country are hired all over the South, because a person raised on this ground takes to the craft young, and they carry the habit of the strike wherever they go; the wardens of the tower at Haeseong are Wellhead men. The Blue Bracket on the Hotwell raft fields the Steps Dyers, the Brightwater Coopers and the Hotwell Dregs, and it is said that the Dregs have never won anything and have the best songs in the South.

**And the water.** Since the war the springs in the south have gone bad, and nobody will say why. Some taste wrong to anyone with the tongue to taste. Villages downstream of them are sick, and the physicians do not know what they are treating. The forks in the Corwen assay posts ring as true as they ever did, and the Corwen wardens read the forks. Street life goes on around this the way it does around a family member nobody mentions. The Yukari say they can walk the springs from the mouth to the source and tell you where the harm is and whose hand is on it. *House Corwen has not yet asked them to.*

## Law and How It Fights

**Lease law** is the law, and a wellmouth is its church. The Lease Court, in brass and blue, hears water rights, hours, contamination, and who touched whose fork. A false assay is the worst crime in the country, and a Corwen warden will tell you this with great sincerity while looking you directly in the eye. Assay-searchers walk the pools by rota. Water may never be taken from a kept pool without payment, a rule from which only the Dyers' Guild has an exception, and they will show you the arm.

The house does not fight, in the sense of owning an army. It keeps a small warden's guard in blue coats, good crossbowmen and pool-trained practitioners who are dangerous on their own ground and ordinary off it, and it hires everything else from [[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Ochre Fields of Hammerford|Hammerford]]. What it actually does when threatened is close a door. A besieging force finds that its own practitioners have no hours at any Core within two hundred miles, that its enemy's stand open, and that the keeper is very sorry. To fight in the Wellheads at all is to fight on ground the other side has paid for: come here with a sect behind you and you may find that a hedge-worker was out the night before with a sack of salt, and that the lovely fat ground under your feet has gone lean as a washboard, with Aether that will not pool. Anyone can hire that done. It is cheaper than spears.

## What They Joke About

The lease. Every Wellhead joke ends with someone checking the wording. They tell the one about the man who drowned in a kept pool and whose widow was billed for the water he drank. They say a Lambert clerk bathes with his hat on so as not to miss anything, and that a Marrick company will fight for you all day and then sit in your bath till the contract runs out. And there is one joke that everyone in the spring country knows and nobody tells the Warden's people to their face, which is just to raise a cup of the water and hold it out and say, *After you, Maud.*

## Monsters on the Roads

Every kept pool is a worked Core, and most were cleared dungeons once, which is what the coin in a keeper's purse is really paying for. A keeper can seal a site in under an hour. Sump-toads (T5, Well-spawn with blued-steel rings on the brow riding level with the surface) breed in the abandoned pools, Glarewings are rare and much bet on, and Hotwell's boiling bowl has a resident Sea Serpent's smaller cousin that nobody admits to. Beasts come for the free lunch: hill wolves after the fat sheep, boar in the watercress, otters in the trout runs, and kingfishers that have taken rings off bathers in broad daylight.

**The dungeons.** The one the keepers show you is **the Hotwell Stair**, boiling springs under Brightwater's lease going down beneath the bath town, worked for so long that invalids boil eggs in it. It is the proof that a dungeon can be tamed, as the Corwen keepers will tell you with a brass key in their hand. The one they do not is **the Long Copper**, a sapphire pool at the gorge's head so deep and clear you can watch a dropped copper fall for a long time, and then keep watching. A Phreatis Well (the Wellspring of water under the ground, copper-green) lies under it. Divers from the Steps have gone down on a rope for two centuries, and a Corwen keeper holds a key to the top landing and no idea of the bottom. More on the [[The Bestiary/Dungeons|Dungeons]] shelf and in the [[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Inner World — A Gazetteer|Gazetteer]].

**The Scourge and the Turn.** The dry wind of the [[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Scourge of Hell|Scourge of Hell]] gives out a long way north of the steam, and the Wellheads meet the wasteland through the books. A Southern Turn, the South's nine years in the wheel of [[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Scourge of Hell#The Turn|the Turn]], runs on hours. Every practitioner a host takes north has to fill somewhere, this is the fattest ground on the way, and keepers charge what they like. Captain-generals write very long letters about it. Right of passage covers roads and victuals at a fixed price and, the Lease Court has found, does not cover hours, and the Warden replies each time that the lease makes no provision for patriotism. The bill then goes into the South's Count, the other two regions read it, and it is the one item all three have agreed to resent.

What comes home comes to Hotwell. Marrick companies off the Line and bounty crews off the Hem sit up to their necks in water the color of tea, coughing up rim dust and lying about the Black Roots, and a one-eared cutter on the raft will fight anybody in the Blue Bracket for his supper. The small sealed vials sold to pilgrims have lately begun to promise protection from wyrms, by persons with no connection to the house.

## Neighbors and Grudges

**[[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Jade Plains of Qingyuan|The Jade Plains]].** Downstream. The Yan have started counting sick foals and have shown the Warden the count.

**[[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Silver Cedars of Yukari|The Silver Cedars]].** They have offered to walk the springs. Every month the offer is declined with the same courtesy and every month it stands in the record.

**The Guild Accord.** Their landlord, partner and permanent threat. The Guild's assay is the house's legitimacy, and the Guild depends on Corwen keepers for every lease this far south, which is a marriage both would like to see written down more clearly.

**[[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Vermilion Dynasty of Sum-gol|Sum-gol]].** The Post buys nothing from a Corwen cask. The Corwen have noticed. Cozbi Mahuo likes to put the Corwen first in the list of houses that do a job the world cannot do without, and the Warden has not decided how she feels about that.

**The Kingdom of Kharven.** The north's ward-cutters depend on these casks every spring and resent every copper, and the Warden sets the price each year with a broad smile. *Later hand:* a Northern Empire pays the same price as a kingdom. She checked.

# The Sealed Charter of the Concord

*The heartland of the Guild Accord, in the Center, where the wax comes from*
> *The Accord holds no territory. I have read that sentence many times, in a room it owns, on a street it owns, in a city it bought.*
>
> *Marginal note in the open shelf's copy of the Articles, in an archivist's hand, unsigned*

On the second morning of the Veyra's Day fair in the year 677 a Catkin named Whitlow Sixtoes, who carried wax for a chandler at Waxgate and had been sold at Verath for seventeen gold marks when she was nine, stood in line at a market-court window in Sealhall with six copper in her fist. She reached the front at about the hour the clerk reached his seventieth petition. He was a man named Corbin Ashlar with forty years on that window, and she told him she wished to bring an action of detinue, the plea for the return of goods wrongfully held, against the chandler, for one Catkin. He took the coin, wrote out the form, entered her in the party line, and struck it in green wax without looking up. She had read the formulary aloud to herself on the wax wagon for eleven years, and she took the instrument from his hand with both of hers and walked outside and held it up.

A party is a person. Nothing in seven centuries of chancery text has ever let a chattel plead, and only formulary wordings bind, and the wording was perfect. By noon the wax-carriers of every quarter in the city had downed their baskets and were carrying her through Long Spine Street on their shoulders, under the blue and orange banners, with Catkin on every roof and the bookies giving nine to one. For three days she was the only Beastkin in the Charter with standing nobody could dispute, and on the third they heard her in the Ribs, under the great curved bones, with more people on those benches than the Measurewrights will swear can fit. A Scalebond advocate spoke for her for free, from memory, for six hours. Enforcement wanted the clerk's head. It fell to an Examiner of Arbitration, Dame Isolde Averell, to find the way out, and she found it in the accounts. She rose and ruled that the fee had been accepted in error. She walked down to the beam in the middle of the floor, in front of all of them, counted out six copper and the interest due on them, to the fourth part of a copper, and put them in Whitlow's hand, and the instrument was void from the moment of the refund, before witnesses, because a person whose fee has been returned has never been a party to anything.

Nobody in the Ribs made any noise at all for about as long as it takes to breathe twice. Then Whitlow Sixtoes threw the coins at the bench, and the riot lasted until Irath's Day. The Legion took the carriers' quarter back street by street, and forty-one people died. Corbin Ashlar refused to say he had made an error and was struck from the roll at seventy-one. Whitlow went over the wall of the Ribs that night, up the bones, in a way no Measurewright has since been able to reproduce, and Brokencollar says she reached it. Brokencollar says many things. Every window in the Charter now carries a standing instruction that a petitioner without free-holder papers be written in the party line as *nemo*, nobody, so that it can never happen again. In the market towns a bad bargain with a smile on it is called a Sealhall refund.

The Sealed Charter is the country the Guild Accord swears it does not have: a bright white land of wax, weights and windows, where the law is faster than anywhere in the world and everybody who can pay for it adores it. The rest of this entry is how that works, and who it was written against.

## The Land and the Look

The Charter lies in the Center, on the good low country on the east flank of [[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Scourge of Hell|the Scourge of Hell]], where the salt road crosses the roads to the Russet forest and the Bearkin hills, with the salt basin to the north of it. It is green and blue and yellow in summer, all hay meadow and bee-country, with lavender and clover grown by the mile for the hives, and white towns on brown rivers, each with a beam in the yard and the bronze reach (the Accord's standard length) set into the market wall at hand height. Every town flies its wax colors over the hall door. A kept street burns its lamps in the color of whoever keeps them, amber, rose or kingfisher green, so the country at dusk looks like a jeweler's tray.

Sealhall dominates it, and you will see Sealhall from a day away. It is a vast walled city around a single vast white hall, with obelisk towers standing along its roofline and blue and orange banners the size of mainsails running down them. Two great statues stand on the wall above the south gate, each ninety feet of pale stone in a long robe, with their spears crossed over the road, and over the crossing hangs a horned skull bigger than a barn. Inside the walls, at the center of everything, there is a round arena ringed by the colossal curved bones of a dead beast, and the beast's spine trails away from it across the city and out through the wall into the barley.

## The Three Places You Will Be Sent

### Sealhall

Sealhall is a city of perhaps sixty thousand who all claim to be there on business. **The Hall of the Seal** is where a Regional Envoy's business comes to be finished: the high windows of the chancery, the rolls of the formulary, the great water clock that keeps the Guild hour, which older rolls place at Stannvaard and which every clerk in the Charter will now tell you has always been here. The obelisks carry the head of the beacon line, and the beacon line coming down out of the Concord Citadel is read by more people than any other line in the world, so that a change in the lamps on the third obelisk will move the price of bread by evening.

**The Citadel itself is elsewhere.** The City of Latticeglass stands between planes, visible only to those bound to the Lattice (the living web of Essence signatures that records every oath), and Sealhall is the place on the ground from which the bound look up at it. A sworn agent crossing the Hall's forecourt can see a crystal spire and bridges of light standing in the sky over the skull. Everybody else sees weather, and pays a fee to have it explained. One of the older Concord Gates stands in the forecourt under a Sealwright Prime's guard, and the Gate recognizes oath: only a soul bound to the Lattice survives the fold, and every year someone tries it on a dare.

**The Ribs** is what everyone calls the arena. The Accord bought those bones from a bankrupt town council in the fourth century of the count, on a bill of sale that reads, in full, *one carcass, condition as seen*, and it has been the best-attested purchase of an unidentified object in the history of commerce. It raised its court of judgment inside them. The tiers go up between the ribs, the great bones curve over all of it the color of old cream, and in the middle of the floor stands the First Beam (a beam being the public scales), on which any person may sound a weight at any hour without leave. **The Neutral Chambers** run under the tiers, and that is where the Trials of Resonance are heard, where lies are held not to survive and the Lattice convicts or clears. The Chambers' armory is in the third rib. The door of **the Bench of Attribution** opens off the Chambers, with the Guild motto carved over it, where it is understood to be a joke.

What the beast was stays open. Research and Archives holds to three accounts and the Measurewrights to a fourth, and the assayers who have worked on the bone have returned figures nobody can place. The Marrowkin send an assessor every ten years, who taps it, looks thoughtful, and offers a price. It has never been established that anyone was offering to sell.

The two statues are **the Witnesses**, named for the Article that makes an instrument good only *coram testibus*, before witnesses. The town calls them Cory and Tess, and brides leave them ribbons.

### Waxgate

Everything in the Charter is closed with colored wax, and that wax comes through Waxgate, a loud yellow river town of hives, rendering sheds and pigment yards that smell of honey and scorching and paint the river four colors in a day. Green comes from verdigris scraped off copper in the sheds, blue from ground smalt, gold from leaf worked in by the small hammer, and the whole town has stained thumbs. The Worshipful Company of Waxmakers holds the charter, and the wax-carriers who walk it out on their backs in baskets are the poorest people in the place, Catkin and Bearkin for the most part, papered or otherwise. Nobody in Waxgate will tell you who makes the obsidian wax. The carriers say there is a shed. Waxgate's gate-toll is a dab of hot wax on the back of your hand, in the color of your business, and strangers walk around town for a week with it on.

### Verath

Verath is a township at the crossing of two trade corridors on the Charter's far western edge, the last clean street before the Scourge, and it exists for the reason the page opened on. **Beastkin law belongs to this country.** The Verath Township Labor Authority runs the largest licensed pens in the Center, under a provision that bears its name, with enforcement men in gambesons carrying its seal, a crossed hammer and chain in copper thread. It is a clean town. The pens are inspected, the brass on the assay post is polished, and the going price at Verath the last time anyone published it was seventeen gold marks a head, less than a horse. *"Those aren't people, boy. Those are labor stock. Licensed and documented."* That sentence, from a Verath enforcement man called Darvo, has been repeated in more songs than he would have enjoyed.

## The Law That Lives Here

The rest of the four quarters takes the Articles as a custom or a circle. Here they are the weather. The market court sits in the market, three times a week in a big town and monthly in a village, and **Titles I and V between them are ninety percent of what any court in the Charter ever does.** Short loaves, weak ale, heaped measures, men who bought the carrots before the carts reached the gate. **The dusty foot** gets a traveler heard before the market closes, which is the reason merchants will walk three days out of their way to be sued here. Amercement, the Accord's standard fine, is set to the offender's means, and the factor house budgets for it. For the full text, see [[Factions, Bloodlines & Institutions/Powers and Polities/The Articles of the Accord, and the Kinds of Polity|The Articles of the Accord]].

### The Beastkin Classification Provision

It was written in the Third Eon, in a good clear hand, on purpose, and it divides the living peoples it groups together into **free-status, bonded-status and labor-stock** on criteria the Accord's own practitioners consider inconsistently applied. A Beastkin who cannot show free-holder status through guild membership, a sealed indenture of service or bloodline recognition is **available for labor contracts**: purchased, held in licensed pens, sold at Accord-adjacent fairs. Read the *licensed and documented* qualifier closely, because it protects nobody. It is the mechanism by which the transaction is made legal. Demihumans default to free-status pending investigation of their lineage; Beastkin default to labor stock pending documentation. Bearkin are generally free where documented, Catkin and Lupine are contested, Serpentkin may claim a scholar-class exemption, and the Chimeric-Born have no coherent policy at all, which is a worse thing to live under than it sounds. See [[Races & Peoples/The Peoples/The Beastkin and Demihuman Peoples/The Beastkin and Demihuman Peoples|The Beastkin and Demihuman Peoples]].

This is the Center's standing fight. Half the Charter's neighbors are peoples its statute has opinions about. It will not recognize the Russet Crown, whose subjects are by its own rolls nobody and who took the name. It trades with the Serpentkin through the exemption and the Avian Kin by the territory, and it sends the Moss Court formal notices that come back with things growing on them.

### Who actually rules

**On paper, the Crown in Charter.** The Concord is the oldest composite monarchy of the age: several countries under one crown, each keeping its own law. In the ruin of the Great Concord Splintering its crowns pledged their regalia to the chancery against their debts, and the instrument that did it, the Sealed Charter itself, is the document the country is named for. The present holder keeps the land, the law, the levy, the tax and the right to hang, as crowns do, and is permitted to borrow the crown for coronations, on signature. The Accord holds nothing but a pledge, and so it may say with a straight face that it holds no territory. It holds the assay, the rank, the credit, the writ and the arrays, and every Mage Royal or Gauger Royal in the Charter enters a room together with a Guild officer who loathes him. Other crowns are further from the chancery's chest, and some, Kaetran among them, have fought the Bench and come away on terms nobody has published.

**In fact, the Guild Accord.** Sealhall's Regional Envoy (the Accord's senior officer in a realm) outranks anyone within a week's ride who is not a Divisional officer, and the Divisions fight each other for the rest. Strategic and Enforcement vote together and are called the hard hand. Arbitration and Research are called the long table. Logistics holds the purse and the Gates and swings. Enforcement eats by forfeiture, which is why a miller here can have his stones seized on a Tuesday and his fine reduced by a different officer of the same Codex on the Wednesday. The High Concordant cannot leave the Citadel and has not seen any of this. See [[Factions, Bloodlines & Institutions/Powers and Polities/The Guild Accord/Structure and Rank/The Guild Accord in the Dark Ages — Divisions, Companies, Academies and Offices|The Guild Accord in the Dark Ages]].

## Wealth

The Charter makes very little and charges for everything. Its fortune is fees: attestation, registration, the entry in ink, the proof-mark, the license, the seal. Under that sit wax and honey, parchment and iron-gall ink, standard weights cast at the Founders' Yard in Sealhall, and scriveners by the regiment. The great hiring halls run their boards from here, and the Company of Adventurers keeps its underwriting tables here, the most honest document about mortality in the four quarters. The alchemical economy runs through the Bench's Standing Index, and a formula not entered cannot be bonded, shipped, pledged or financed. *The Legion has never taken a district as cheaply as the Bench has.*

Beastkin labor, sold at Verath and the fairs, carries the wax, cuts the hay, works the rendering sheds and is entered in the accounts under stock.

## Rent, Bread and the Street

Rooms are by the month in tenement, with the warden's fee on top, and a house on a kept street pays its bill to a chartered company in a sealed household grade; the lead seal on the lamp stem by your door says who did the work, what was paid and until when. "Still on the round?" is how neighbors say good morning. The poor boil their pots at the waist-high parish stone in the road, warm, iron-bound and quarreled over, and the water tastes of it.

Food is honest and exact and there is not quite enough of it. The assize loaf has its weight fixed against the price of grain, white for those with money and dark for the rest; pulse porridge; ale at the fixed price, passed by two sworn tasters, who are chosen each year, unpaid, may not refuse, and are visibly unsteady by the sitting. Meat is a feast-day matter. Honey is everywhere, on everything, the one cheap sweet thing the country has, and the Charter's children have bad teeth and very good manners.

Everybody here can read. A window clerk may be the most powerful person you meet in a year, and he knows it, and his ink is on his cuff. Titles come before names at any door, without exception. Across the street from any hall sits a sect in its colors, whose elders consider the establishment a tax on talent and whose core disciples carry Guild tokens anyway. The Scalded Cats hold the river towns in orange and soot, open hands, and sing *Who's Got the Lid* flat on purpose, and the Stage brackets at every fair are the nearest thing the country has to a shared religion, bookies in green hats and all. In Aldersey the Chandlers and the Saddlers still both ride eighth. In Stowmarch they still bury bakers with the loaf.

The Charter's words for the shared arts are the Guild roll's, reinforcement, Pressure and sensing, and its people use them proudly and correctly, and the bracket crowds around them say shine, weight and nose to annoy them.

## How It Fights

Its first weapon is the charter, and the real sanction is exclusion. A town that crosses Sealhall finds that its weights no longer pass, its letters no longer clear and the rider has stopped coming. *The Accord cannot hang you. It can make it impossible for you to sell anything, anywhere, ever again, and it prefers that.*

The Articles say the Accord keeps no standing army, and the **Concord Legion** drills on the plain below Sealhall's walls every morning, in blue and white, bound by resonance where other men are bound by pay, every soldier's oath a working cut into the bone. Its doctrine is measured force, and every engagement is reviewed by Arbitration afterward. Enforcement brings the worse news: Vanguard strike teams; Sealwrights (the Accord's specialists in binding glyphs) laying containment circles that can pin down things no one should have to pin down; the Null Wardens, their throats sealed by vow, who track a violator through the dissonance he makes and have never been heard to say they are sorry. **A High Warden's presence in a realm is martial law**, and the sight of one on the Waxgate road will empty a market.

And the spear-points are crossed over the road for a reason. Armies do cross the Charter, in every generation, because nobody marches through the Scourge, the salt road is one of the two ways round it, and the salt road runs under those spears. They cross on a Writ, by the named road, with their bread paid for at the assize, and the ones that did not bother with a Writ are why Sealhall has walls. Most marshals save the fee and the argument by taking the Kessen road on the far flank, across the Midmarch, which is one of the things the Midmarch holds against the Charter.

## What They Joke About

Paperwork, with love. "Before witnesses," said of anything done in the dark. "Nullum et irritum," of a husband. Both companies ride eighth. Nobody dreams in Latin, and a clerk who talks in his sleep is reported to his Warden. Cory and Tess are said to have been married since the Second Eon and to have stood with their spears crossed the entire time. The beast, they say, was the last creature to dispute a bill in Sealhall. The Beastkin who carry the baskets have jokes of their own in Purrspeech, with a second conversation running underneath the first, and it is usually about you. They ask each other who is coming to dinner, and the answer is nobody.

## Monsters on the Roads

Everything here has a form. Cockatrices (T5 on the Bestiary's Danger Tier, which runs from T1 to T9) get into henyards along the river towns and a warden has to be sent for. Harpies raid the wax wagons. Stilts, the great herd animals of the Scourge on legs as long as a ship's mast, have been sighted from the western hay-country in bad years, and the Concord Legion drills for them on the plain. The ordinary beasts are kept in good order. Wolves on the sheep-walks carry a fixed bounty under the assize, paid by the pair of ears at any hall door, and a bear in the clover must be reported within the week by whoever owned the hive, with the hive's number.

The hiring halls run their boards from here, and the Company of Adventurers keeps its underwriting tables, the most honest document about mortality on the continent. The dungeon is **the Tail**: the spine of the dead beast of Sealhall runs out through the city wall and away into the barley, each great bone bigger than a chapel, and a stair goes down at the fourteenth vertebra into a Well the survey has never identified. Enforcement holds the gate on it, the Company rates it, and past the Lower Workings the tables simply print a dash. Farmers on either side plow round the vertebrae and let the shade to drovers.

The chancery rolls call such a place a *domnio*, an asset with a defective title, and the Articles never use the word dungeon at all, saying *a pool*, which the courts have had to stretch one lawsuit at a time. The greatest of them is **the Heart Vault** in the Concord Citadel, a true Wellspring Core (Fountainhead on the survey, the top of the ladder) held by the Accord, and who cleared it is not a question the Accord has a form for. The darkest line in the whole ledger is the porter clause. A licensed labor-stock porter, bought or hired by the season at Verath, is equipment on a clearing's instrument: he cannot hold a share, cannot be named in the saving clause, and goes on the roll as *nemo* along with the rope. He carries the poles at the back of the line. He is generally the first to say that something down there is wrong, and nobody is obliged to listen. More on the [[The Bestiary/Dungeons|Dungeons]] shelf and in the [[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Inner World — A Gazetteer|Gazetteer]].

## Beside the Scourge

The west of the Charter lives with [[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Scourge of Hell|the Scourge of Hell]] the way a good house lives with a bad neighbor, by fixing the boundary exactly. Its dry wind reaches the western hay-country a few weeks a year and dries the hay on the stalk. Past Verath the guild's jurisdiction ends at two iron posts with red-painted caps, and beyond them lies the **Verath Expanse**, the Charter's own name for its stretch of the Hem (the Scourge's outermost belt, fifteen leagues of black-barked thornwood and pale dust). On this side of the posts you get an assize loaf, two sworn tasters and a court that will hear you before the market closes. On that side nothing holds. Crews going out touch the red caps for luck, and the paint is renewed every spring at public expense, on a form.

The dungeon every young hunter in the Charter has a story about lies out there. **The Lamplit Stair** is an Aurevane stair (Aurevane being the Wellspring of hard white-blue light) going down from a thornwood gully, its walls a hard blue-white, cleared twice and lost twice, and it is where half the Green Sigils in the country, Green being the first license color a hunter can carry, get their first bad night. **Goodmorrow**, on the salt road under the Charter's edge, is the great edge town of that side, painted stilts over a sinkhole country, every bridge a different color so you can tell them apart through the haze. It has the best mask-clerks on the east side, Charter-taught every one (a mask being the colored plate a [[The Bestiary/The Corrupted and the Risen/Well-spawn|Well-spawn]] wears, and the thing a hall pays on), and the worst beer.

[[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Scourge of Hell#The Turn|The Turn]] comes to Sealhall in the end, whoever holds it. North, then South, then Center, nine years each, the holder keeping the Line of forts around the Hem and paying the bounties while the other two count. The copy of the arrangement at Sealhall refers to an older copy, and that one refers to "the ancient custom." Every Turn ends with each party presenting a Count of what it cost and what is owed, and the Arbitration division audits them, for a fee, and has never produced a figure any party accepted. It was an Accord water clock, on a trestle under a green awning, that eleven thousand soldiers stood and watched in 676 while a wyrm ate Lastpenny, and Sealhall maintains that the instrument performed correctly.

The Center holds the Turn now, from 712 to 721, and the Charter's share raises the prettiest point of law on the map. The Accord holds no territory, so by its own argument it owes nothing. The Crown in Charter owes, and the Crown's regalia are in pledge to the Accord. So the Charter pays by instrument, a bill drawn on one pocket and accepted by the other, which buys free companies and keeps the eastern forts in bolts, and the Legion in blue and white crosses the posts only on a Writ. On the North's Turn and the South's a host may march through anyone's land by right of passage, which would seem to dispose of the Writ and the fee together, and Enforcement has spent some centuries on the question of when an army under those crossed spears has stopped being on its Turn. As for the colossal sword at the Scourge's center, with a field of violet stars where its blade should be, one star-clerk at Sealhall has spent his career recording those stars from borrowed reports, and he holds that they have moved twice in three centuries. His office is funded by a fee on something.

## Neighbors and Grudges

**The Amber Cities of Nalūn** · A rival court with a very good roof. The arrangement between Sealhall and Ironlink Hold has never been written down, because writing it down would admit it exists.

**The Russet Crown of the Unpapered** · The runaways' kingdom, unrecognized by every roll in Sealhall and the subject of a standing Enforcement line item. The Charter's collectors go in. Their iron collars come out, cut, and go on a heap.

**The Hundred Crowns of the Midmarch** · The Charter sells them law at a markup, and the worst of the armies go across their ground so as to stay off the Charter's.

**The Rose Coils of Coilspire** · The Serpentkin take the exemption, argue every case, and remember the clerk's grandfather. No Scalebond advocate has ever needed to look anything up. Two chairs at the Bench have wanted their archives entered for a hundred years, and Research treats what they hold as anecdotal, since the source is living memory.

**Sancta Lux and the Holy Sea** · Sancta Lux hears oath, status and legitimacy, the Accord hears everything else, and neither will define the boundary. Both prefer being able to claim a case to being obliged to take one.

**The North** · The first circle was signed at Kharven-Seat, over a custom that has never been written. Sealhall regards that as its greatest triumph, and Kharven regards it as a thing that was signed.

*Later hand, from the last winter before the turn, running past the year these shelves stand in:* the Northern Empire of Whitemere found the crossed spears still over the road, and sent up its own clerks, in their own wax.

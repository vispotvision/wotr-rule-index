# The Silver Cedars of Yukari

**Say it** · yoo-KAH-ree · keh-TSOO-en

*The Crow's Reach · Ketsuen, in the old books · Domus Vinculi, in Accord filings*

Three summers ago a wedding barge came down the Long Water with the bride's whole family aboard, a hundred and twenty otter-folk of the Lake Districts in scarlet and reed-gold, with drums, a roast pike the length of a door, and the bride's four brothers up in the bow singing louder than anyone. At the Reedgate station the perimeter read them and called a threat. The Thread Guard turned out sixty in crow-black harness to meet a party of drunk fishermen at the landing, and Maren Ottertail, in a crown of marsh-marigolds, broke the sergeant's arm with a boat pole for laying a hand on the pike. Nobody died. The pike was a total loss. Within the week the Corridor was laughing at the Guard and at the Academy that trained it, and the bookies at Senri's Gate were giving odds on which carver would be disgraced for cutting a station that could not tell a bride from a bandit.

Then the case came up under the Thread Standard, and a reader went through what was connected to what, and found that the ward had been right. The four brothers had come to kill the groom over a debt of nets, and each of them had a filleting knife under the scarlet. The court traced the debt to a bad sale made by the groom's father twelve years before, ordered the groom to make it good, set the brothers to mending the Reedgate landing every spring under the sergeant's eye, and gave the sergeant a seat at the table on every anniversary. All of them still come. The sergeant says the pike has improved.

This is the oldest blood in the South on a cedar hill, all around it a frontier that asked to be governed because its gateposts were failing, and every promise anyone makes written down by people who can see what it is tied to.

The old maps call the outer country Ketsuen, "Binding Bond," and give it a border and a flag. It never was much of a state. What it has always been is the Yukari valleys and the settlements that hired the Yukari to mind their wards, and the South calls the whole of it the Silver Cedars for the trees and the Crow's Reach for the people who watch from them.

## The Land and the Look

On the east of the South, past the Wellheads and the whole width of the Jade Plains, the ground climbs into cloud, and the cedars begin. They stand so tall and close that the light comes down between the trunks in thin bars of green-gold, on moss, on black water, and on the silver lichen that furs every north face, and a person walking under them lowers their voice for no reason they could give you. Crows go over all day, high up and talking. The house colors are black and silver, silver threading through dark, and they are the colors of the place at any hour after noon.

The **Heartstone valleys** sit in limestone six hundred feet above the country around them, walled on three sides by ridges whose faults have been exhaling Essence into the soil for centuries, so that the ground presses on your temples within a day of arriving and does not stop. Scholars call this rich ground, and so does everyone else after the first headache. The valley's entrance is a pair of standing stones under a carved lintel, bearing one unbroken line of thread-notation in a language older than Parunic; the stones stood before the house came, and nobody will say who set them. The limestone goes gold at evening. Above the valley on its hill stands the Enclave, with raked white gravel in its court.

Below the Enclave the valley floor drowned long ago, and nobody has ever let it go. Mossy dark pagodas and towering carved temple spires stand up out of still water that gives the whole of them back, lily pads crowd their steps, huge pale waterfalls come down behind them, and long red cloth streamers run from spire to spire across the sky, renewed each year by people who row out to do it. The shrines are still kept. A robed figure in red can generally be seen on the shore, and the valley will tell you that is a keeper and will not tell you which one.

Beyond the valleys, north and east, the Reach proper runs out through long lakes, birch and cold upland toward the salt road and the old ruins of the Rose Coils: raw timber settlements with sap still weeping from the wall boards, ward-posts cut last season, new roads and mostly bad ones. A market town out there is loud in the way of any place where everyone arrived recently and has an opinion, with carvers' apprentices in chalk-white aprons racing each other to a failed gatepost for the fee, Lake District fishers selling dried fish off the tailboard, and a hedge-worker on the corner who will tell you the Academy's standards are a racket while charging you half of what the Academy's graduate would.

## The House

**The Yukari** are an archaic bloodline before they are a house, the family of Ashura, the Breath Between Worlds, and the house is the shape the bloodline took when the world forced it to hold ground. The name is chosen at the Telling, when a child of age sits on the cold floor before Senri's stone and hears the whole account of what the blood carries and what it costs, and is never simply inherited. That makes this the only house in the South whose members are all volunteers. Their words are *The crow perches. The crow watches. The crow remembers.* The [[Factions, Bloodlines & Institutions/Houses and Bloodlines/The Archaic Bloodlines/The Yukari Bloodline|bloodline's own page]] has the rest, and you should take the tea.

**Lord Naiser Yukari**, the Bearer of Weight, is head of the house, a man of near thirty, Senri's eldest and half Lambert by his mother, known across the South as the lord who won the war and asks for nothing. His father went into the seam when the Soul Plane broke, and held it. The house came through the last four years of the war harder than anyone in the north knows and laid out more than four hundred of its dead in rows facing east. It has spent every month since telling everyone who will listen that it has no wish for more land. Its terms at the Cedar Enclave were plain: no Yukari knowledge for war, no Yukari name carried as a banner, no touching the spirit world without Yukari counsel, every promise made in the hall entered in the Yukari record. His sister Naevra keeps the archive and the register of every birth and death in the valleys. Seradris is the youngest.

**The Reach and how it came.** The Reach accreted, and there was no conquest in it. Frontier settlements joined because the alternative was worse, and the thing that made the alternative worse was what Yukari carvers could do that nobody else could: maintain the ward-craft that kept the frontier from collapsing in the night. The story everyone tells starts with **Niran Yukari**, called the Crow of Fate, who came up the long coast to the valley gate at thirteen with salt in his pack and a ward-carver's tools at his hip, able to hear a failing bridge by listening to its footing. He fixed wards as he traveled, a cracked gate post or a thinned well-stone, in twenty minutes for the price of a meal and a dry place to sleep, and each time he left a trained apprentice behind. If the valley maintained your wards, the valley set your ward-standards, and if it set your standards it was already minding your gates and wells. The eighth settlement petitioned on birch bark, carried by a merchant who had observed that towns under Yukari maintenance had not lost a building to structural Essence failure in three years, while towns outside it had lost an average of four per season. *The arithmetic was the argument.*

**The Council of Threads** runs the Reach, under the house and at arm's length from it: seven Thread-Wardens, one per province, and the **Crow's Seat**, a post Niran designed for himself with no executive power at all, carrying only the right to speak last, to call an emergency session, and to place before the Council any crisis his Thread-Sight shows him. Roughly eighty thousand souls live under it. The charter says the Seat passes to whoever holds the most comprehensive Thread-perception and otherwise stays empty. *A blind Crow is worse than no Crow at all.* The house stays out of Council business as loudly as it can, and everybody knows who owns the stones.

**The Proving** is how anyone gets anywhere. It is a public review of demonstrated performance, with no examination in it. Candidates present what they did, what came of it, what they learned from the failures. The panel sits in public, the deliberations are recorded and the criteria are posted in advance. Incumbents re-Prove every six years and about one seat in three changes hands. A Proving draws crowds that would shame a tournament, since it is the one contest in the South where a sitting lord can lose in front of his own tenants with his own numbers, and the Corridor's bookies post odds on every seat.

## Cities, Towns and Provinces

**The Cedar Enclave** is the seat, on its hilltop above the valleys: cedar halls stepping down the slope on limestone footings, beams gone red-brown and resin-dark, bark-shingle roofs furred silver-green with lichen, a covered walk around a court of raked white gravel, three standing stones in shawls of moss that throw no shadow at any hour, and wards along every approach that touch whoever comes up the southern road long before anyone at the gate has seen him. A stream runs under the floor of the long hall. Senri's stone stands at the back of it. Since the war the warlords' table has sat here, because everyone wanted every promise entered in a record somebody else was keeping.

**Karasuyama**, "crow mountain," is the bare limestone peak above the Enclave and the rookery town on its shoulder. Hundreds of thousands of crows roost there, and the crow-wards who feed and band them will tell you, without being asked, which ones have been reading your mail. Every autumn the sky over it goes black for an hour at dusk, and people walk in from three valleys to see it.

**Sugidani**, "cedar valley," is the house's market town and the only place in the valleys with a decent inn: sawpits and shingle-makers, the great cedar rafts going down the river, ink-grinders, and paper made from bark, which every clerk in the South prefers and complains about the cost of. Its tea tastes of small white flowers and is as bitter as a lawsuit.

**Senri's Gate** is the town that has grown up around the standing stones, at the mouth of the valley, and the capital of the Reach. It holds the Council, the Ward-Carver's Academy and the Archive of Provings. Old books use its name for the whole Enclave, and are wrong in a way that has annoyed three generations of the family. The warded track runs out from here to **Sum-gol**, one hundred and forty leagues, three weeks by wagon and nine days by mounted courier, with a way-station every twenty to twenty-five leagues, each with a Yukari-standard perimeter and a resident carver. The Kessen road proper ends at Sum-gol, and this track is its last stretch in all but name, carrying on across the South to the foot of these valleys.

## The Wellsprings and the Ward

The land has no single dominant territorial Wellspring. What it has is more diffuse and in practice more versatile, a composite ward-network running on several types of Aether at once.

| Wellspring | Function |
|---|---|
| **Cymorath** *Vibration / Resonance* | Underlies the entire ward-net. Lets a practitioner **hear the substrate's internal frequency before carving**, find the paths of least resistance, and design inscriptions that work with the material's architecture. *A Cymorath-attuned carver completes a sentence the stone was already trying to say* |
| **Fixatio** *Anchoring / Stability* | The network's holding power. A properly sealed ward holds its function for years if the substrate remains intact |
| **Monolithion** *Thread / Causality* | Feeds the Ketsumyōgan. The Thread Standard depends on it, the Council is named for it, and the valley sits on a fault system that concentrates it. *The Yukari settled there because the valley was the place where Thread-Sight worked best* |

The valley's limestone walls carry a Boundary function that predates any inscription system. Whether this is a local Wellspring phenomenon, a geological artifact, or evidence of a tradition too old to identify is undetermined, and three Measurewrights have stopped speaking to one another over it. The wards function. The Reach maintains them. The explanation will arrive when it arrives.

## Wealth and Trade

**Ward-craft** is the defining product. A Yukari-standard ward carries a mark that frontier settlements recognize, Accord searchers accept, and rival ward-cutters have been unable to copy, because the glyph formulae are posted on the Academy door and the quality lies in the attuning of the substrate, which requires Cymorath perception most practitioners do not have.

**Practitioners** are the second. The Academy trains anyone who can pass the entrance Proving, and a land whose graduates keep the wards on half the frontier's settlements is a land everyone depends on and nobody wants to antagonize. Cedar, shingle and bark paper come out of the valleys, and herbs from the Uplands. The land does not feed itself and buys its grain and preserved goods through the free cities of Nalūn.

The house itself trades in what it sees and never sells it outright. Its wealth is its archive. What the Yukari know may leave the valley to mend and never to take, and they are the first to admit that this makes them very expensive friends.

## Rent, Food and the Street

Land is held by stewardship rather than ownership. Neglect forfeits a holding through the Proving of Absence: unmaintained ward-craft degrades, a Cymorath-attuned searcher can hear it, and the finding triggers a review of whether the holder is still competent to hold. Nobody in the Reach pays rent, exactly: they pay dues to their station, which keep the carver in dumplings. Under the valley's cedars the tenants pay the house a tithe, and the house remembers who was late.

Broth with root vegetables and dried lake fish tastes, the Heartstone says, like the country. Dumplings at a family table, lake fish, forest game, bitter tea. The sentence that does most of the work in a Yukari household is "You haven't eaten," said by whoever loves you, as a bowl is set down beside tools that have gone cold.

"Who keeps your posts?" is the greeting on the road; it says which carver maintains your settlement's wards and which station answers for you. Every building sits on a ward-carved foundation, tied to the ward-line around it, so that every house is also a knot in the net. Ward-carved foundations do not glow and do not advertise their presence, and the only way to know one is to listen to it. The Proving Festival runs every six years at the summer solstice. The Ward-Lighting falls at the autumn equinox, when the Academy's graduating carvers cut their first permanent station in a public ceremony the whole district treats as the great day of the year, with chalk thrown in the street. At midwinter the Thread-Reading is given from the flat stone above the gate, and people walk for days to hear what the web shows.

## Law and How It Fights

**The Thread Standard.** Other courts ask what happened and who is guilty. These courts ask what was connected, what was damaged, and what needs to be restored for the connections to hold. It takes in the threads that bound the parties before, the threads the act damaged, and what happens to the community if the damage is left unrepaired. Outsiders find the outcomes either profoundly just or deeply confusing, depending on whether they understand that a fine paid to the wrong party can leave the real damage unrepaired. It is slower than conventional adjudication and more accurate. No other land has copied it, because no other land has a population of Thread-readers.

**Citizenship** is earned through service. Any person of any race contributing demonstrated capability for three continuous years may petition through the Proving, and race, bloodline, wealth and origin are not factors. The population divides into the Proven and the Unproven, and the Unproven are protected by law and may work and earn. More than half the Reach is human of mixed frontier stock, about one in six Yukari by blood or by name, about one in seven beastkin, and the rest Marrowkin, Mahuo and a small growing goblinoid population in the Marches. The Telling goes to every new citizen on completing the Proving. *Not gentle, not abbreviated, not optional.*

**The Thread Guard** is roughly three thousand strong, in crow-black harness, organized around the ward-network, and holds no fortified positions. **The perimeter detects entry and makes no attempt to prevent it.** It classifies an entrant's intent through vibration in the substrate and alerts the nearest post. A bandit crossing is read as hostile before she clears the treeline, an army before it finishes forming up, and a wedding party according to its brothers. The land is never surprised, and a prepared answer is cheaper than an improvised one. Its strategic weakness is concentration: the perimeter is only as strong as its worst station, and an attacker who understands the inscription architecture could open a gap. Yukari fighting is quiet and unnerving, with long fingers, flat feet and eyes that close for a deep read, and the house's own teaching says to move a thread before you draw a blade. The house has sworn no Yukari knowledge for war. It says nothing about what happens to people who come up the road to start one.

## What They Joke About

Being looked at. A valley child learns early to look away from a Yukari's eyes, on the belief that the Crow's Audit sees what you have done wrong, and the valley makes all the jokes you would expect about it. "Ledger-eyed" is the worst thing you can call someone who sees people as entries, and that means the Moto. "Blind crow" is someone holding a post his sight cannot carry. "Loud" covers any unnecessary motion, and Yukari visit Haeseong in order to feel it. "Absent" is a neglectful holder. Out in the Reach, the commonest joke runs: how many carvers does it take to fix a gate? One to fix it, one to listen, and a panel to review them both.

## Monsters on the Roads

The wildlife of the Thornback March has been marinating in high-density Aether for centuries and has opinions about territorial boundaries. Tanuki (T2) cheat travelers on the valley roads. Psy-Apes (T4) troop in the upland cedars and everyone in earshot suddenly knows what they ate. Beasts are plentiful and well informed: crows in their hundreds of thousands, otters who know the fishers by name, bears on the upland herb slopes, and in the Long Water pike the length of a door. A perimeter reads an entrant's intent through the rock, which tells you when something is coming and not what to do about it. The Uplands hold the land's highest natural seeps.

**The dungeon.** Everyone's name for it is **the Drowned Shrines**, the low valley floor below the Enclave, Fluxia by the look of it, kept and never cleared. The Yukari row out to tend the pagodas and renew the streamers, they decline all commissions, and the water is a good deal deeper than the valley. The worst of it is **the Unlit Shrine**, a pagoda whose lower stories go on down under the water past where any reflection stops. A Yukari thread-reader will not look at it for long. The house has not told anyone what is in it, and what the Yukari know may leave the valley to mend and never to take. More on the [[The Bestiary/Dungeons|Dungeons]] shelf and in the [[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Inner World — A Gazetteer|Gazetteer]].

**The Scourge and the Turn.** The Corridor's merchants go north beside the salt road along the east flank of the [[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Scourge of Hell|Scourge of Hell]], and that is as close as the Reach comes to it by choice. The South's share of [[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Scourge of Hell#The Turn|the Turn]], nine years in every twenty-seven keeping the Scourge's monsters off the other two regions, falls first on the old Dynasty's realms, and the Yukari stood apart from the Dynasty long before it fell. Every captain-general since 602 has come up the road to ask anyway. The house has sworn no Yukari knowledge for war, and the Academy ruled in open session that a wyrm is not a war, so Academy carvers go north by the season at the posted rate to cut perimeter stations on the southern arc of the Line, and answer afterward at a Proving for any that failed. A station on the Hem reads a herd and reads what follows the herd. Carvers who have stood one report that what comes up through the stone from under the Black Roots is nothing they can enter as an intent, and that the edge towns' ground-bells, on an iron stake at a copper's worth of hedge-work, gave the same warning first. The Academy has reviewed this finding, in public, with odds.

## Neighbors and Grudges

**[[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Vermilion Dynasty of Sum-gol|Sum-gol]].** The Yukari tell the story of how the Mahuo were killed against themselves, and tell it well. Lord Naiser has promised the Mahuo royal line its seat back and the Head of House Mahuo has declined the gift. The Post carries every letter between the north and these valleys, and the courtesies are perfect.

**[[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Holy Sea of Alabaster|The Holy Sea]].** The Sea arrives at equality through theology and the Reach through empiricism, the disagreement is genuine, respectful and permanent, and it constitutes approximately forty percent of the content of tavern arguments in both Rimwards. Kin as well, through Lord Naiser's mother, which is why the Yukari record keeps its promises in figures as well as words.

**The Moto.** Fear worn as policy. Their sight reads the same threads the Yukari read, and they have been watching each other across a table for two hundred thousand years. The Moto believe governance requires sovereign judgment, a crown that decides, and the Yukari that it requires distributed competence and a council that measures. Both have been wrong in public. The whole Center lies between them, and the peace is quiet because both know what testing it would cost.

**The free cities of Nalūn.** The Reach's wards protect the corridor and the chain-cities finance it. Neither cares about the other's philosophy. They care about whether the wards hold and whether the bills clear.

**The Ember Holds of Marrowhold.** The Marrowkin keep a tally of what they have cut from what they live in, the Yukari keep a record of everything else, and each suspects the other's book of being the one that gets read out at the end.

**The Iron Mandate of Undaar-Keth.** The Mandate governs through decree and the Reach through measurement. No direct trade, no military cooperation, and a mutual understanding that the distance will not be tested, because neither is foolish enough to fight a war it cannot model.

**The Zettari** struck the valleys in the war's last four years, and the Sovereign of Kushara has since come to the long hall to talk terms over the tea. Their page is their own.

# The Slate Ridge of Stormfold

**Say it** · STORM-fold

*House Greymane · The Western Wall · The Ridge Does Not Move*

The garrison cook at Galecross in Year 671 was Ragna Ottarsdottir, and her whole gift as a practitioner was that a pot under her hand would never go off the boil, for which the Greymanes paid her a soldier's wage and a second blanket. In the first week of that spring, a column of three hundred and eleven hobgoblins of the Mandate came up the Galecross road in crimson lacquer and iron, in step, under a thunderhead the color of a bruised plum, with the violet lightning walking along the ridge above them the way it does there most afternoons. The pass garrison was sixty and most of those were eating. Ragna looked at the sky, looked at the column and looked at her kitchen, and had the eight great iron cauldrons of the Galecross mess dragged up onto the crest by mule, with every chain in the fort run from them down the rocks to the road, and the last of the chain she laid across the road herself, in an apron, with the head of the column in plain sight. Then she stood on the rocks and shouted, in the voice that had called sixty men to dinner for thirty years, COME AND GET IT.

A storm looks for the easy way down, and iron is a far easier way than air. The first stroke took the crest cauldrons and came down the chain in a violet rope you could see from the Ridgewall, and the second and third came right behind it, and it went along that column from the front, from iron to iron, so that for about as long as you could hold your breath a hundred men in crimson stood lit from the inside, every buckle a star. The rest ran. The Mandate entered it as an unforeseen loss, Ragna lost her left arm at the shoulder and all the hearing she had, and the Galecross mess went without a hot meal for a month. The Greymanes do not do parades. They gave her a title, as they do for everything, and for the eighteen years she had left she was addressed by the entire ridge as **the Cook**, and no one in Stormfold has used her name since, which is the highest honor they know.

The Slate Ridge is a fortress cut out of a mountain, under its own private lightning, held by a family who have turned being a wall into a religion and do not know they have done it.

## The Land and What It Looks Like

The Stormfold is the western highlands of the north, exposed and wind-hammered, one long fold of slate and greystone running between Kharven and Undaar-Keth. The ridge makes its own weather, and the local belief is that it does so out of spite. The rock is banded with Stillband iron, and the thunderheads that come in off the western shield catch on it most afternoons from thaw to first snow, so that violet lightning walks along the crest over dark wet slate, which shines like a plum, with the rain going sideways and a band of bright clean sky behind it. The storms pass as fast as they come, and then every gully runs silver and the heather smells of hot iron.

**The Ridgewall** is a fortress carved straight into a sheer mountain face, so thoroughly that no surveyor has been able to say where the mountain stops and the masonry starts. You come to it across a white snowfield in the usual blizzard, toward one arched door, with great ice-sheathed pillars standing up on either side of it, a single tall tower cut from the rock above, and gold light in its tall windows, the only warm thing for ten miles. Galleries run back through the living slate, and from the fortress's shoulders the curtain wall goes on for leagues along the crest in the same stone, with watchtowers rising out of the outcrops like the outcrops' own idea. Gray banners with a wolf's head worked in darker thread hang wet on every tower, and the old shields of the house, steel-faced and green-rimmed with a gold sun rising on them, are worn half blank by centuries of blows. The whole thing has an iron lightning-mast on every tower, and the storm hits those instead of the sentries as a rule.

West of the wall lie two things. Down the Galecross road is the Mandate's country, black basalt and boreal forest under crimson ranks. Past the ridge lies **the ground the bloodline came from**, which is not Greymane business and not anyone else's. Nobody holds it, and the Greymanes look at it every morning from the wall. The Holy Sea's copies of the map put that ground somewhere else entirely, and the [[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Inner World — A Gazetteer#Where the Maps Quarrel|Gazetteer]] enters the quarrel.

### The major places

- **The Ridgewall.** The seat, the long wall, the galleries, and a garrison town in the lee of the crest whose whole economy is boots.
- **Galecross.** The great pass town, a knot of slate roofs at the crossing where the wind comes through from four sides and the road comes up from the Mandate's country. It has a famous mess, a smoked-mutton trade, and a toll-house whose keeper has to rope himself to his door in spring. Its children can tell a storm's arrival time by the taste of the air.

**Roads.** East to the Seat by the ridge road, on foot in winter. West and down from Galecross into the Mandate's country. Grain comes up by levy each autumn, in the care of an Ashmore clerk.

## House and Rule

**House Greymane** is the shield, military to the point of institutional. Children are raised to see themselves as components of a defensive structure rather than individuals with personal ambitions; the ones who reject the framing leave, and the ones who stay become the most reliable garrison troops in the Inner World. Their words are **The Ridge Does Not Move**, which describe the house, and which the house has never once found inspiring. They have held the ridge, by their own telling, since before there was a north to hold it for.

The lord is **the Shieldwarden**, always, regardless of given name, because the function matters more than the person. Lord of the Ridgewall and one of the Seven Warlords of the north, addressed by title in his own bedroom, gaunt and long and gray like all of them: the Greymane stride is a thing you can recognize at a quarter-mile, and so is the Ridgewall stillness, by which a Greymane does not move at all and then moves very fast. The personal name of the current holder is known within the family and to the kind of person who reads cards. Family offices run the same way down the ladder. There is a Gatewarden, a Masonwarden, a Muster Aunt, and the Cook.

## Wealth and Trade

Very little. The Ridge sells greystone (the standard fortification stone of half the realms in the north, bearing good and holding poor), Stillband ore off the western shield, wool from gray-faced sheep too stubborn to fall off, smoked mutton, and soldiers. Its real income is a levy that every other house in the north grumbles about and pays, in grain and salt, because everyone south of the crest would like the Mandate to remain on the far side of it. Mountain companies from the Ridge hire out for a season or two; they are expensive, humorless and worth it. The long war has cost the house more sons than it has ever admitted on paper. It imports grain, and the Ashmore clerk who arrives each autumn with the allocation is treated with the same exact courtesy as a siege engine.

## Rent, Food and the Street

Nobody pays rent on the Ridge. Everyone holds their room by a post on the wall, and when you stop holding the post you stop holding the room, which focuses the mind of the elderly. Food is the mess: mutton, barley, bone broth, rind-water (the honest northern name for thin broth) in the Thin Weeks, and hard cheese you could shoe a horse with. Galecross has the only real tavern on the ridge, the Dry Sock, and its one boast is the name. There is singing there on the nights the storm is too bad for the wall, generally the song about the Cook, which runs to thirty verses, and it is the only time you will hear a Greymane raise his voice for a reason other than an enemy.

Local aura-talk runs the same as everywhere in Kharven (the hide, the low sky, smelling a man's stack), with the addition that a coat of aura on the crest in a storm is regarded as asking for it. Hedge-workers here do a brisk trade in charming sentries' boots dry, and lamp-wardens have refused the upper towers altogether. A signal post on the ridge is kept by a garrison hand and costs nothing, and it is the only free service in the country.

**What they joke about.** Hardly anything, in front of you. Among themselves, the joke is titles: a Greymane child will address the family dog as the Hound and the latrine as the Lower Gate. The other Banner Houses tell the jokes about them, and the best known is the one about the Greymane who sat guard over a pie all night because no one had told him to stop. Greymanes consider that a fair description of a good soldier and do not see why it gets a laugh.

## Law and How It Fights

**Law.** Garrison law, from the Shieldwarden down: post, relief, watch, and what happens if you leave one. A dispute goes to the senior hand on the section, and from him to the wall. Greymanes have their own version of a death-house on the crest, cold and wind-scoured, and the Waiting is kept in shifts.

**The fighting.** Shield and sword, the wall first and then very fast. The Greymane school is the ridge sword, and it is built around a single principle known as *taking the Before*: the man who strikes second spends the whole fight answering, so a Greymane strikes first, from the high guard they call the Roof, hilt by the ear and point standing up behind the head. Its two great cuts are the Wrath-Cut, straight down off the right shoulder, and the Crooked Cut, hands crossed on the grip, turning edge-first onto the inside of the wrist. They march in the Grey File, shields locked rim to rim. Greymane Releases are called in earth-words (**Stonefast**, **Setting the Stone**), dust settling around the heels as the body takes on the ridge's refusal to go anywhere, and the fair answer is to pull him, lift him or fight him on ice. A horn on the belt calls the next section, one long note, and the sound of it coming from behind you is a thing Mandate veterans will mention quietly, after some drinks.

The wall itself does a third of the fighting. Mountain companies watch the western approaches **and survive conditions that would kill most soldiers before the fighting started**, and an enemy on the crest in a storm is in the hands of the violet weather with everyone else.

## Monsters on the Roads

**The beasts** are the ones too stubborn to leave: gray-faced sheep on the shelves, mountain goats above them, snow hare in the gullies, and ravens who know the mess schedule and have taken a sentry's dinner off the wall in front of his whole section.

**The monsters.** Monsters move, and they live across the whole of the Inner World, and a good many of them start out here. The Ridge has a plague of storm-things. Raiju (T5 on the Bestiary's Danger Tier, which runs from T1 to T9; lightning in the shape of a beast) run the crest in bad afternoons, and a coat of aura up there is regarded as asking for it. Wyverns (T6) nest on the western faces and take sheep and sentries. Harpies (T4) work the Galecross road. In a dry year a Ridge wyvern will turn up over Ashbury's barley and take a red cow in front of a village, and the Ashmore clerk has twice presented the Shieldwarden with the bill, which came back with a note that the animal had left its post. The western nests are also the one subject on which the Galecross garrison and the Mandate's exchange messages.

### The Ninth Gallery

The [[The Bestiary/Dungeons|dungeon]] (what a Wellspring Core makes around itself, or what grows up around one) on the Ridge is **the Ninth Gallery**: a Masonwarden's crew cutting a new gallery through the living slate broke into an older one that nobody had cut, full of blue-white light and Glarewings, Well-spawn with arc-blue lightning trees across their brows. It is a Well of Aurevane (hard-edged electric light). The Greymanes walled it, put a door in the wall, and set a post on the door, and a sentry has stood it for three hundred years, because no one has told him to stop. It has never been cleared. Every Greymane who has been sent inside was sent by title and has been replaced by the next one of that title. You can stand at that door and hear it, one clean high tone like a wet finger on a glass rim. The sentry keeps smoked glass on his belt and wears as little iron as a Greymane can bear to, which is a great deal to ask on a ridge made of it. The Accord has twice offered to post the place as a commission, and the Shieldwarden replied both times that the post was filled. More on the [[The Bestiary/Dungeons|Dungeons]] shelf and in the [[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Inner World — A Gazetteer|Gazetteer]].

### The Scourge and the Turn

The Ridge regards the [[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Scourge of Hell|Scourge of Hell]] as somebody else's wall, badly kept. The Turn is the old rotation by which North, South and Center each spend nine years keeping its monsters off everybody else, and on the North's Turn the Greymanes send their mountain companies down the Kessen road to hold the forts of the Line, around the Hem, the Scourge's outer belt. They have stood those forts in a heat they could not have imagined, on ground that shudders under the boots, and they ask only what the relief schedule is. Stormfold slate stood at Lastpenny in 676 with the Hollenmark blue, while a wyrm ate the town, and the Greymanes are the only northerners who have never apologized for it, because they were ordered to stand and they stood. The companies also hire out on other people's Turns, the Center's at this moment included, until 721, when the wheel comes back to the North.

A Ridge section on the Hem may well hire as its guide one of the hobgoblin ridge-men, Mandate outcasts who work the high ground above the Black Roots and may see two centuries. So a Greymane sergeant can find himself following a man who stood the far side of Galecross a hundred years ago and can recite the relief schedule of the sergeant's grandfather. By all accounts they get on very well, and neither mentions it at home.

## Neighbors and Grudges

- **[[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Iron Mandate of Undaar-Keth — The Closed Fist|The Iron Mandate of Undaar-Keth]].** The reason for everything. Both sides believe borders must be held, threats answered, and governance is serious work conducted by serious people, which is why neither can leave the other alone. The two garrisons know each other's relief schedules better than their own wives do, and the Stone-Blood (the hobgoblins' own inheritance) runs slow at thirty below, a fact the Greymanes rely on and think about constantly.
- **[[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Cobalt March of Hollenmark|The Cobalt March]].** The other wall of the north. The Starks and Greymanes share a wolf on the breast, stood together through the long war, and are not on speaking terms in any way a stranger would notice.
- **[[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Golden Hollow of Ashbury|The Golden Hollow]].** Grain. See the Ashmore clerk, above.
- **[[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Kingdom of Kharven — The Ashen Crown|Kharven-Seat]].** The Hall of Verdicts holds that it can summon the Shieldwarden to answer for his house. The Hall did exactly that, within living memory, over a brother and some salt, and the Ridge has not forgotten the weight of either.

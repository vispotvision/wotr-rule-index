# The Vermilion Dynasty of Sum-gol

**Say it** · SOOM-gol, from *sum*, breath, and *gol*, a hollow

*The Crown That Was Carried · Breath Hollow · The Empire Deposed by Attendance*

On the first day of autumn in Year 602 the Emperor Rokju Mahuo walked out onto the Balcony of Answers to call his roll, as an emperor of Sum-gol had done every autumn for three hundred years. The square below him was packed to the fountains. The palace behind him was hung from its gold domes to its pale twisting towers with banners of vermilion and white, and down both sides of the square stood the tall red standards with the pale antlered stag of the line, row on row, each with a sweating boy to hold it straight. The custom was simple and the whole South came to see it. The house words are *Call, and It Answers*. The Emperor called each great name of the South in turn, and the house stepped forward, answered, and had its tribute carts driven past. He had doubled the salt tax that spring, to pay for the domes.

He called the Holy Sea, and a Lambert clerk stepped forward with a roll under his arm and read out what the throne owed the Archive for three centuries of registers, to the copper, which took until the fried dough sellers had sold out twice. He called the Marrick, and the spear company in the square looked up at the sun, saw that it was noon, and grounded arms, because their contract had that minute run out. He called the Corwen, and a boy brought word that the Wellhead casks had been sold elsewhere. He called the Yukari, and one crow came down and sat on the balcony rail for about as long as it takes to read a name, and left. He called the Yan, and an elder of the Grey Plain began reciting his own titles in a carrying voice, and was still going when the sun moved, so that to this day no scholar can say whether the Yan answered. He called the Ryu, who sent a superb letter.

Then Rokju Mahuo did the thing his line was for. He raised his hands and called across the Veil, and the Baengnok came, the White Stag on all those banners, the great bound power of the dynasty, stepping out of the air above the square with its antlers full of cold light and a hush going out from it across a hundred thousand people. It looked at the Emperor. Its compact, as the Mahou scholars later explained at length, ran for as long as the realm answered. It walked down out of the sky, through the crowd, which parted, out by the East Gate and up into the iron hills, and has not been summoned since. There was a long pause, and somewhere near the fountains a fish-wife started to laugh. By nightfall the standards were firewood, every girl in Sum-gol had a vermilion sash, and the only emperor in history to be deposed by attendance had gone inside to think about it. The Empire of the South ended that afternoon with very few casualties. They came later.

Sum-gol is the name of an empire with no empire left, the richest ruin in the South, two great houses that still answer to it and keep its money, and a young postmaster who holds what is left and will not put on the crown.

## The Empire and How It Broke

For three hundred years the vermilion ran from Kessen ford to the southern sea. Every southern power held its land of Sum-gol: the Holy Sea paid in records, the Long Fields in spearmen, the Wellheads in water, the Grey Plain in horses, and the Thousand Moorings kept the imperial fleet. The Yukari, who were old when the Mahou were young, paid a tribute of one crow a year and entered it. The Mahou line were summoners, whose arts called things across the Veil and bound them, and whose iron and salt went north in every cart that ever used the road, for longer than the Moto have had a crown.

After 602 the lands went their own ways, and nobody invaded anyone, which is what makes it so embarrassing. The Lamberts kept Sallowgate for the Palatine. The Corwen took a Guild lease and a charter of their own. The Marrick found that a spear company hires out better when it has no emperor to serve, and the Yukari simply went on sitting where they were. Only two stayed. The Yan Hall of the [[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Jade Plains of Qingyuan|Jade Plains]] and House Ryu of the [[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Copper Coast of Haeseong|Copper Coast]], the Dynasty's horse-masters and its admiralty, go on answering to its name today. What that means in practice is the subject of the rest of this page and a good many lawsuits.

The Mahou went on as kings of Sum-gol for another three generations, still rich, still loud, still hanging the banners. Then came the long war of the last forty years, and it ate them. Their name was carried at the front of other people's armies until there was nothing left of it but the carrying. The Moto put Mahuo banners over Moto columns, the Yukari and the Zettari each fought under the excuse of protecting them, and when the time came the house was destroyed. **Every one of the three tells it differently.** The Yukari say the Moto stood back and let the Yukari slaughter them, because a house turned into a symbol is very easy to kill and nobody weeps for a symbol. The Moto blame the Yukari. Kushara blames them both. *All three are right about the other two.*

## The Land and the Look

The pictures you have seen are of the old glory, and the minstrels have not updated them: gold domes and pale twisting towers, a huge palace hung top to bottom with vermilion and white, tall red standards with the pale antlered device, a dark crowd massed in the square. All of that stood, and most of it stands yet. The domes have been stripped on their north faces by people who could reach them, so that they flash gold and bare in turns when the sun comes round. The towers have been hit by things. Crows sit on every cornice. A city built for ten times its present people still has its colors, and at evening, when the salt pans go red to the horizon and the banners that remain lift in the wind off them, it is one of the most beautiful sights on the one great body of land, and it charges less for lodging than any other.

Around it lie the iron hills, rust-red and bare-backed, and the salt pans, shining flat out to the south in rows of white and rose. Up the river behind the city the rice terraces climb in green shelves to **Breath Hollow**, the old valley the name comes from. Sum-gol sits at the southwest corner of the [[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Scourge of Hell|Scourge of Hell]]'s rim country, where the Kessen road comes off the wasteland's west flank and ends and the South begins, and everything that moves between north and south has to pass through its gate or go a long way round by the salt road on the east. From the top of the iron hills to the north you can see the haze on the horizon, copper at noon and rose at evening.

## Who Holds It

**Cozbi Mahuo**, Head of House Mahuo and Master of the Sum-gol Post, is twenty-five. On the Sum-gol road they call him the Post-Master who never loses a letter. In the rest of the South he is the man the [[In-World Documents & the Narrative Archive/The Zettari–Mahou Accord|Zettari–Mahou Accord]] condemns by name, and among those who deal with the dead he is the Keeper. He is the greatest summoner the South has seen in generations, a patient and lavish host who laughs loud and at the right moment, wears an olive greatcoat under a mantle of white fur, and speaks of his dead in the present tense.

He has refused the crown. Lord Naiser Yukari promised at the Cedar Enclave to see the Mahuo royal line restored at Sum-gol and to step his own house back, and Cozbi takes nothing from a house that ever carried his name: no gift, no crown, no protection, no counsel. He has also refused the color. The Mahuo colors are vermilion and white, and he kept the white. Vermilion is what other houses' armies carried into the field over Mahuo banners, so the Post wears olive, and asked at dinner when he will wear the red again he says, with every sign of enjoying the question, on the day Sum-gol is his by his own hand.

So the throne stands empty, in a hall kept swept by a woman the Post pays. The chair at the warlords' table is kept for Sum-gol, it is the only one with nobody sitting in it, and every other house at the table has a reason to want it filled by somebody in particular. The Dynasty still has its name, its salt and its iron, its two loyal houses, its heralds, and every autumn its Calling: an old herald stands on the Balcony of Answers and reads the roll to a large cheerful crowd that has come for the food. An elder of the Yan recites his titles. A letter arrives from the Ryu. The Post-Master attends in olive and does not answer, since the roll is the crown's and he has no crown.

## Cities and Towns

**Sum-gol** is the seat and the name of the whole country. By the last Yukari count the city and its country hold about fifteen thousand souls among streets laid out for a hundred and fifty, so that whole quarters stand open to the sky and a family can rent a prince's stable for the price of keeping it standing. The **Palace of Answers** fills the north side of the square, with the Balcony above it. The **Post-House** stands in what was the imperial mews, its roofs white with crow droppings and its yard full of olive coats and blowing horses, and it never closes. The teaching house in Breath Hollow keeps a school in the city as well, where children of the line and any others who can pay learn the soul-breath arts.

**Cheolsan**, "iron mountain," is the hill town of the mines, built in red stone up the side of the ore it stands on, full of smiths and fire-setters and a vile black beer. Its smiths brag about their marks the way Moto lords brag about their grandfathers. The same hills give cinnabar, the red ore that made the vermilion, and the dye works still run in the lower town, staining the river for a mile, and sell to everyone in the South except the Post.

**Yeomjeon**, "salt field," is a low white town on stilts at the edge of the pans, where the rakers go barefoot with their feet wrapped in linen and the wind tastes of what you would be paying for anywhere else. *The Mahuo's salt. The Sum-gol iron.* Every northern quartermaster still says the two together.

**Breath Hollow** is the valley itself, emptied as the house went out of it by attrition, and what remains is sixty roofs and a teaching house. Ask anyone there about the Seven Debts, the cohort of cousins whose grandfather gave them all the generation-syllable for *debt*. It is still the best lawsuit the valley ever had.

## Wealth and Trade

Salt and iron first, as ever, though the salt sells through other people's toll-gates now. House Ryu sits on the sea end of the old revenue and has stopped forwarding it, and the South has never settled whether that is theft. Cinnabar and dye. Rice from the terraces. Mahuo soul-breath knowledge, sold as healing, spiritual diagnosis and lineage-reading, which no amount of ward-craft can replicate.

And the **Sum-gol Post**, by crow and relay rider. Every letter between the north and the Yukari valleys goes through it, along the whole length of the Kessen road, in sealed bags on olive-coated riders who change horses at the old imperial stations. A house that was a crown now carries the South's letters, and carries them so well that nobody has thought to ask who is paying the couriers, or whom they answer to. Beside the letters the house runs its other trade, in the same coats. Cozbi Mahuo means the world to know the Mahuo as the Order of the Spiritual Reality, the Keepers of Death, a function nobody can do without, held the way the Corwen hold the springs and the Lamberts the count. Since the Soul Plane broke, hauntings have spread across the South and the dead drift. The Post will lay a haunting, exorcise a house, clear Well-creatures where the dead have soured a site, or escort a soul home, each job priced, recorded and done in olive and white. *"The Corwen keep the water. The Lamberts keep the count. We keep the dead. Nobody else would take them."*

## Rent, Food and the Street

Rent is the wonder of the South. A journeyman can live in a duke's kitchen, and a hedge-worker who keeps the rats out of a dead minister's library pays nothing at all. Food is cheap and salted: rice and barley with salted radish and fermented bean, blood soup at the Post-House gate, grilled river eel, and pans of sour greens put up against the winter. The valley counts breath before it counts coin, and a nursemaid teaches a child to count the ox's breathing before she teaches it the copper. At the Stubble-Burn, when the terraces are fired after harvest, the whole hollow smokes for three days and everyone comes down to the city to eat and argue.

The street is loud because there is so much room to be loud in. Scavenger crews in painted hoods fight over the gold on the domes. Children race crows. Scribes who cannot read Common sit outside the Post-House and write your letter anyway. Everyone but the Post-Master wears vermilion, a sash or a rag or a dyed hat, partly from pride and partly because it is cheap here. The city's bracket crowd says gabot for the aura coat, nureum for Pressure and nunchi for sensing, and it will tell you at length that the Yukari do not understand any of the three.

## Law and How It Fights

The old imperial code still runs in the courts because nobody has written a new one, and a dead emperor's seal will still pass in Yeomjeon if it is the right color. Mahuo burial law stands alone in the four quarters in not requiring the family to keep the body. **Post law** is the only law everyone respects. A sealed bag is holy, a rider's horse may be taken for no debt, and the last man who robbed a courier on the Kessen road returned the letters himself, by hand, at night, and would not say why.

Sum-gol has no field army now and has not had one since the war. Its ancient art is summoning: a Mahou summoner shapes a vessel, lends it an identity and holds it by recognition, so a single trained summoner can bring a small host to any field, and the great ones of the old line brought whole bestiaries to the square. The house style among the riders is the hwando, a short curved saber worn across the shoulders on the road, and a ring of salt poured quickly around anyone who needs one. As for the Post-Master, he fights the way he gives a dinner, and his summons are his guests. Nobody who has been to one has asked for a second invitation. The Guild Accord's assessors gave up trying to list his forms in their third year.

## What They Joke About

Attendance. Every Sum-gol joke comes round to the Calling. A boy who skips school "answered like a Marrick," a husband who comes home late "sent a very good letter." They say the Emperor's stag is still walking and will be back when someone pays it. They say the Yan elder finished his titles last spring and has started again from a new angle. Of the Lamberts they say that one came to read a dying man's will and stayed to read the man. Of the Moto they say nothing polite, and they spit before they say it.

## Monsters on the Roads

Since the Soul Plane broke, hauntings have spread across the South and the dead drift, and the Post will lay a haunting, exorcise a house or clear Well-creatures where the dead have soured a site, each job priced and recorded. Ghouls (T4) work the empty quarters, where a city laid out for ten times its people offers them any number of quiet kitchens. Brine-walkers, pale long-legged things that follow the rakers at dusk, are the salt pans' own contribution. Beasts are the smaller bill: wild goats and hill wolves on the iron hills, herons on the pans, and crows on every cornice that will have the eyes out of anything left lying. The **Baengnok**, the White Stag, bound power of the old line, walked out by the East Gate in 602 and has not been summoned since, and hill people leave it alone.

**The dungeons.** The one everybody tells is **the Answering Vaults**, under the Palace square, where the great summoners of the old line kept whole bestiaries in bound vessels against the next Calling. Nobody living has the roll. Scavengers in painted hoods go down for the gold and bring up reports of a host still standing in rows, waiting to be called. The one nobody tells in front of a smith is **the Ninth Adit** at Cheolsan (an adit being a level tunnel driven into a hillside), a Chthonica Well (a Wellspring of dark iron-oxide, all grip and ground), worked for its iron, with the Eighth sealed. The Dynasty's iron mountain goes down further than the miners were ever told, Post riders carry the adit's accounts under seal, and the smiths of Cheolsan have stopped asking what the Ninth is being fed. More on the [[The Bestiary/Dungeons|Dungeons]] shelf and in the [[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Inner World — A Gazetteer|Gazetteer]].

**Beside the Scourge.** The empire claimed the Scourge on paper, the whole of it to the foot of the sword, and the Palace still has the deed. Yeomjeon's forgers will sell you a barony in the Black Roots (the belt of root-mat just inside the rim) under a dead emperor's seal, and it will pass. The wasteland is less impressed. In late summer of a dry year the wind goes round to the north and comes over the iron hills hot, smelling of iron and pollen, the rakers look up from the pans, and whatever the herds have pushed out of the rim comes down the last of the Kessen road: dire wolves off the Hem, and haze-kites (T4, membrane and hunger the color of an old copper pot) after the mules at Cheolsan. Every relay station on the flank keeps a ground-bell on an iron stake and a horse saddled. Olive riders work that road in every year of the wheel. A sealed bag is holy, the things that come up through the roots have not read Post law, and on the Post's books a rider taken by the ground is a letter delayed, and the next rider leaves on time.

**The Turn.** The South's share of [[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Scourge of Hell#The Turn|the Turn]], nine years in every twenty-seven keeping the Scourge off the other two regions, falls first on the old Dynasty's realms, and Sum-gol has no field army to send. It answers in iron, salt and road. When the South's western column goes north it forms up in the square under the empty Balcony, eats the city out of eel and leaves with Cheolsan bolts in its pouches, and the stakes under the ground-bells of every edge town from Lastpenny on were as likely as not forged here. The Post holds the job of laying the South's drifting dead along the Hem, each line priced and entered in the South's Count. In the other eighteen years it sells the same bolts and carries the same letters for whoever's Turn it is, and at the Calling the old herald still reads the Scourge out among the Dynasty's holdings, to general applause.

## Neighbors and Grudges

**[[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Silver Cedars of Yukari|The Silver Cedars of Yukari]].** The house that offered the crown back. The couriers run between the two seats every week, on a warded track, and the courtesies are perfect.

**The Copper Coast and the Jade Plains.** Sworn to the name. The Ryu have left the Mahuo's wealth exactly where it sat and would like it noted that the Mahuo have never asked. The Yan hold an empty stall for the Emperor's horse at Qingyuan and enter it every year as *held*.

**[[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Holy Sea of Alabaster|The Holy Sea]].** Keeps the empire's old rolls and has never been paid. The Post carries its letters promptly, and reads their addresses with interest.

**[[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Sapphire Wells of Brightwater|The Sapphire Wells]] and [[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Ochre Fields of Hammerford|the Ochre Fields]].** Both left at the Calling and both have done well from it. Sum-gol still buys Marrick spears when it can pay, and does not drink from anything that comes out of a Corwen cask.

**The north.** Every northern lord's letters go through the olive coats. The Moto put the Mahuo banner over their columns in the war, and no Moto rider has ever been refused at the Post-House, and no Moto rider has ever been comfortable there.

# The Violet Peaks of Caelspire

*House Caelward of the Avian Kin, one of the four Central Warlords, over a mountain country of rival schools*

> *One step for the farmer,*
> *Two for the king,*
> *Four thousand for the army,*
> *And the last one's the thing.*
>
> A skipping rhyme from Windstair. Children there skip at the cliff's edge, as a matter of honor.

In the high summer of 704 King Odo Gambrel of Upper Marl, the eleventh of that name in a kingdom about nine miles wide, crowned at Pennycrown in a crown of good middling price, marched nine hundred men up the Windstair to demand arrears of tribute that he had found mentioned in an old letter. He picked his moment well, or thought so: it was the week of the Long Step, the great match between the schools of the Violet Peaks, when every fighter on the mountain is known to be busy. It took his men eight days to climb. They arrived at the top in their best red, gasping, with their king carried in a chair and his herald too winded to herald, and they found the entire peak population on the terraces with their backs turned, roaring, because the Gray Kites of Windstair and the Highroost Shrikes were at that moment throwing each other off a cliff. The herald eventually got his breath and began the demand. A Shrike in black and white came up out of the gulf at that moment, three hundred feet of bad temper riding a column of air, and took the crown off Odo's head on her way past, because the rules of that year's match said the side holding the highest object at sundown won, and it had occurred to her that the king was standing on a table.

The Kites took it back by nightfall. No one on the mountain ever spoke to King Odo at all. He stood about for two days, was charged by a basket-man for the air his men had been breathing, and went home bareheaded down four thousand steps, with the hoisters laughing and his army selling its boots. The match has been fought for his crown ever since. It is called the King's Hat, it has a dent in it from the Finals of 711, and when the Gambrel family wrote to ask for it back, the schools invited them to come and win it, and sent the list of fixtures.

> Twin crags above the clouds, where feathered fighters from a dozen rival schools step off cliffs and read the wind for a living, who have never lost the high stairs to any army and who will not stop their match to discuss it.

I do not care for heights, and I wrote the following from an inn at the bottom, with reports.

## The Land

Look up from the farmland on the northwest of the Center on a clear morning, with the haze of [[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Scourge of Hell|the Scourge of Hell]] at your back, and there is a line of lilac cloud where no cloud should be, and above it, floating on nothing, the Violet Peaks. The chief of them, **Caelspire**, is two crags rising side by side out of that cloud and joined near the summit by a long arched bridge hung with so much green that from below it looks like a line of living forest crossing the sky. On the crags stand pale domed halls and steep violet roofs, stacked wherever rock will hold them. At the very top is one huge old tree, and beside the tree a pool that spills straight off the edge of the world as a waterfall so long that much of it blows away before it lands, with a rainbow standing in the spray from morning until the sun drops. Flights of white birds go round it all day. The whole country of the farms lies spread far below like a map on a table, patched and tidy, and the people of the Peaks speak of it as a map.

Beyond Caelspire the range runs on in a broken line of violet-shadowed summits, and each peak is its own school. **Windstair** is the great way up, four thousand cut steps on the north face. **Highroost** is the far peak, narrow as a tooth and in constant high wind. There are a dozen others, some inhabited by four old masters and a goat. Feathered banners stream from every roofline, and on any fine morning you will see fighters in their school's colors simply stepping off the cliffs.

The pool at Caelspire's summit is a worked Wellspring site, a Core in common speech, and its Aether is Cymorath, the Air of Ascent, one of the sixty Wellsprings (the sixty types of Aether, the raw power of the world), a blue spiraling thing that smells of ozone and dry air and leaves curved trails where it is spent. Its keepers can close it, and the keepers are the Caelwards. Rich ground fills a practitioner quickly, which is why a dozen schools have climbed up here and stayed, and why they have been fighting ever since about who gets the mornings. No one knows who built the bridge. It stands on no pier that anyone has found, and it was already old when the first Avian Kin nested on it. The Measurewrights say the Great Spirit War, and the Kin say the bridge has never mentioned it.

## The House and Who Rules

The Avian Kin stand on the Accord's roll under the name Current-Reading, the habit of their blood, with Dexterity (the Guild's measure of reaction and of moving at the right distance) as their definitive gift and a legal standing marked "variable by territory." In person they are light-boned and narrow, hard-eyed, with crests of feather from the brow to the shoulders and long stiff vanes down the outer forearm, in every color from sparrow to flame. The unwoken among them can glide a little, on a good wind, if they are brave and have an appointment with a surgeon. The ones who truly fly have earned it, pulling Aether out of the moving air, refining it in the Soul Crystal (the organ that turns Aether into the Essence a person spends) and laying it along those vanes. Current-Reading is the gift underneath: an Avian Kin feels the air's whole shape on the skin, reads a front two days out, and can tell where you are in a dark room from the way the draft goes round you.

**House Caelward** holds Caelspire, the bridge, the tree and the pool. It has not been the strongest school on the mountain for three hundred years, and it keeps the Core's hours, runs the hoists and sits in judgment at the Drop, so every other school must come and shout at it. The head of the house is the **Warden of the High Stair**, at present **Vessaly Caelward**, called Nobody's Landing, a tall gray-crested woman of fifty with one vane burned to the bone, who won the Long Step three years running in her youth by never once appearing to hurry. She presides over a mountain of feuding clubs with a dreadful courtesy. She has thrown exactly one ambassador off the bridge, and he was caught. The house words are *Step Off*. The colors are violet and white.

## Schools, Cities and Towns

Schools here run like football clubs. Each has its peak, its colors, its songs, its old defeats, and a style you can spot at fifty paces, and a child in Windstair is born owing loyalty to one and despising the rest. Their followers travel, drink and riot.

**Caelspire** is the seat, with the Warden's domed hall, the markets on the bridge and the Caelward school, the **Stair Wardens**, whose violet coats mean they will catch you if you fall and fine you afterward. Its lower town, hung from the crag on ropes and iron pins, is the most crowded and most cheerful slum in the Inner World.

**Windstair** is the working town of the Peaks, strung along the top third of the great stair, full of hoist-lodges, feather-sellers and inns that have had the same argument for a century. Its school, the **Gray Kites**, in gray and lilac, fights on long patient glides and wears you down from a distance, and their crowd sings an insulting hymn that lasts an hour.

**Highroost** is the home of the **Shrikes**, in black and white, a butcher's style that drops from above and hangs the loser up by the coat on the nearest thorn tree. It is a brutally cold town, and it is proud of that. People there eat standing up because of the wind.

The smaller clubs each have their devotees: the **Longdrop Swifts**, who have never been seen to land; the **Old Mews**, who are four very elderly women and who keep winning; the **Lilac Stair**, who are rich and bad and have the best songs.

## Wealth and Trade

The Peaks grow almost nothing and they are rich anyway. Their first trade is the wind itself: a Windstair almanac, hand-copied, tells a shipmaster on a far coast or a general on a far field what the weather will be doing on Tuesday, and readers of the air are hired by captains and commanders on every side. Half of the great battles in the center were timed by someone in a gray coat.

Their second trade is the molt. Feathers shed by the Kin are the best fletching anyone has found, because a Kite's feather holds a trace of its owner's read of the air, and arrows fletched with it fly straighter than they have any right to. Molt-sellers go through the school dormitories every season with sacks. The feather of a champion is sold with a name, and fakes are everywhere. To pluck one is a killing matter, and that is the root of the worst thing on this page, below.

The third is the schools. Young fighters from every realm climb the stair with their parents' money to be taught, and some of them, being wingless, have to be taught what falling is. The fourth is the passes: the high ways over the range are the only roads between the salt country and the west that have never been cut by war, and the Caelwards take a toll. The fifth is the match. In the week of the Long Step every bed on the mountain costs what its keeper says it costs, and the bookies take more than the schools.

## Rent, Food and the Street

Height costs money, and the direction it costs in depends on who you are. A loaf is cheapest at the foot and it rises a little every thousand steps, because everything up here comes up in baskets, on ropes worked by the hoisters' company. They are paid by the load and go on strike about once a decade, at which point the whole mountain lives on goat and spite until someone pays. For a person of the Kin, rent runs the other way: a room on the outer edge, with a clean drop from the sill, is worth more than a palace on the inside of the rock. A family of means leaves its front door opening onto nothing. A lowlander is put in the cheap inner rooms and told he should feel lucky.

The food is goat cheese and lilac honey, barley cakes from the small terraces, dried fish that has climbed four thousand steps and resents it, and thin sharp wine that gets stronger as you go up, or seems to. Do not order eggs. It is very funny the first time, and no one on the mountain will ever explain why they have stopped speaking to you.

Everyone on the street has a petty air-trick and a price: the laundress who dries your shirt on a private breeze, the boy who will drop a note down to the third terrace for a copper, the hedge-worker who keeps the smell of the Highroost tanners from drifting across the bridge, until someone stops paying her. Fans are bet on, scarves are sung about, and two strangers in the wrong colors in a narrow alley have an understood procedure.

## Law

The law of the Peaks is the law of the stair: who holds the stair holds the peak, and the stair may not be cut in time of match. Disputes between persons go before the Warden, and any that cannot be settled go to **the Drop**. Both parties step off the Caelspire bridge, and whoever lands last has won the case. It sounds like a joke, and it is also an exact measure of skill and nerve. A lowlander may appoint a champion, at ruinous rates. It has happened, in 633, that both parties won the case very thoroughly and left their estate to the lawyers.

Falling is lawful. Pushing a person off a height is murder, unless it can be shown that the person might reasonably have flown.

The one law that has no comedy in it concerns **fowlers**. In the territories where the Accord's rolls read the Kin as labor stock, there are licensed men with nets who take them for their singing and their feathers, and a bird-maker in a Concord market town will show you a cage with a person in it, with the long vanes clipped so she can never leave, and quote you a price. A plucked vane does not grow back straight. A fowler taken on any stair of the Violet Peaks is walked up to the bridge, in the sight of the assembled schools, who for that one hour wear no colors, and invited to step off. There is nobody to catch him, and they all watch him the whole way down.

## How It Fights

No army has ever taken the high stairs, and they have been at it since before the Guild's count begins. The problem is mostly arithmetic: four thousand steps, cut one man wide at the worst points, under cliffs, in a high wind, with fighters who can stand in the air beside you and take your head off and go home for lunch. Rocks are also available. A siege fails for lack of anything to besiege, for the hoists can be pulled up at any time and the Kin can go out over your head to the farms. Mostly, an army that reaches the top is too tired to be anything but embarrassed.

In the single fight, they use the sky as a floor. The **Highroost Stoop** drops a Shrike from a thousand feet with everything she weighs and all the air she has packed behind her into one heel on the join of your collar. The Kites' **Long Afternoon** stays one spear-length out of your reach, on the wind, and cuts you once each time you breathe in, until you sit down. The Stair Wardens' **Nobody's Landing** gets under a falling enemy, catches him as a courtesy, and carries him out over the gulf to discuss terms. Fights end in a crowd's roar, with the winner calling the move by name so the terraces can sing it, and any good fighter on these peaks has an epithet before his crest is grown.

They call the aura coat (the visible coat of a fighter's own color) *the lift*, and argue endlessly about whether a good lift should show at all. Pressure, the weight of a higher Stage felt in the room, is *the downdraft*, for what it does to your shoulders. To sense another practitioner is to feel the draft go round him.

The counters are real. Still air grounds half a school, and on a dead calm inversion morning, with the fog lying flat, the Peaks are as near to helpless as they ever come, which is why those mornings are the only ones on which the schools do not train and everyone is very polite. Bled ground starves them. A light-boned fighter who gets hit once by anything heavy stays hit, so hard men with low ceilings have ended a good many careers in the indoor towns. And a crossbow bolt costs about three coppers, read by Speed Grade (the Guild's letter for how fast a body can answer) like any other projectile: a Kite who does not see the loose comes down like a goose off a marsh, and every fighter in the Peaks knows the name of someone it happened to.

## What They Joke About

They joke about everyone who has to walk. A groundling is anyone with no feathers, and the word is applied with pity. "Eight days up and one breath down" is the Windstair summary of all foreign policy. They say a Bearkin is the only thing on the map that might be a mountain, and they say a Scalebond remembers the fall and a Kite remembers the view. Most of all they joke about each other: the Lilac Stair have been told at every match for ninety years to try landing on purpose, and the Shrikes' away songs are not to be set down here.

The groundlings joke back. In the Midmarch, a person with a very high opinion of himself "has a sill." Every bowman in the center claims his fletching came off a champion. And anyone within a week's march of the range knows that the fastest way to get a Violet Peaks scout to tell you what she has seen is to ask her who she thinks will win on Saturday, and then to stand well back.

## Monsters on the Roads

Griffons (T5 on the Bestiary's Danger Tier, which runs from T1 to T9) nest on the east faces and will leave a fight to keep the nest. Hippogrifs (T4) are caught and sold, Harpies are a nuisance on the Windstair, and a Wyvern taking a fighter out of the air is a thing that happens during fixtures. Bear keep to the northern faces, goats go anywhere they please, and the white birds that circle Caelspire are under the Warden's protection and know it.

The dungeon is **the Chimney** inside Highroost: a shaft up through the hollow tooth of the peak, an Ascensio Well (the Wellspring of rising and hoist, steel gray) where the Mouth is at the bottom and the Heart at the top, so delvers climb and everything else falls on them. The Shrikes claim to have cleared it in 588. The Old Mews, four very elderly women, send them a note every year correcting the date and the word. The Summit Pool at Caelspire is the other place the Guild's list names, a Cymorath site worked by House Caelward for longer than the house can document, and every other school on the mountain has to come and shout at the Caelwards to buy its hours. More on the [[The Bestiary/Dungeons|Dungeons]] shelf and in the [[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Inner World — A Gazetteer|Gazetteer]].

## Beside the Scourge

The Peaks stand off the northwest corner of [[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Scourge of Hell|the Scourge of Hell]], high enough to look down on the whole problem, and they have found that this can be sold. Its wind comes outward from the middle in every season, hot and dry, and a Current-Reader on the high stairs can feel the Haze (the third belt in, where the great herds walk the dry beds) shifting before any hedge-worker on the rim knows a thing. Every herd sighting on the western side comes from an Avian scout, at her price, and the Windstair almanac carries a dry-year column that the kings of the Midmarch pay for and then ignore. The herds do not climb. Haze-kites do, membrane and hunger the color of an old copper pot, riding that same wind to the foot of the stair in dry years and taking mules from the hoisters. The Gray Kites regard the name as a slander and kill them on sight.

The schools go in. Most of the fighting schools of the Center send their seniors to walk the Hem (the outermost belt, fifteen leagues of black-barked thornwood and pale dust), and the proud ones send them on to the Black Roots, with the song and the banner. The schools of the Violet Peaks keep an annual tally of kills per student and post it on the bridge, the Long Step's bookies study it closely, and a school that loses a season's seniors will recruit twice as hard for the glory of it. The Lilac Stair's tally is excellent, and no one has ever seen the kills.

Windstair also has the view. On perhaps nine clear days in the year a dark upright line stands on the sky far to the southeast, the colossal sword at the Scourge's center, bronze-hilted, driven point-down into the mountains, with a field of violet stars where its blade should be. A survey taken from the Windstair reckons it about four leagues above the peaks. One from Stoneglass Hollow says about three, and the authors did not speak afterward. On those days the whole town goes out on its sills in every color to look, and the odds on the next fixture are chalked up on the way back in.

[[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Scourge of Hell#The Turn|The Turn]] runs North, then South, then Center, nine years each, with the holder keeping a Line of forts around the Hem and the other two counting what it costs. The Center holds it now, from 712 to 721, and the Peaks pay in sightings, at a discount the Warden describes as generous, and in seniors, who would have gone anyway. On the North's Turn or the South's a host comes down the Kessen road below the range in long columns the terraces find very funny, and its marshal sends up for the almanac. The same sighting has been sold to the holder of a Turn and to both of the clerks sent to count what it cost him.

## Neighbors and Grudges

**The Hundred Crowns of the Midmarch** are the map below, and their kings send demands. The King's Hat has been defended against three of them by name.

**The Sealed Charter of the Concord** licenses the fowlers, buys the almanacs and has been refused a Warden's audience for two centuries. The Caelwards take its coin for the wind every season and spit afterward, and this is the sore point at home. A faction of the clubs wants the trade cut, and the Warden has been heard to observe that a people who can leave are always the last to stop selling.

**The Russet Crown of the Unpapered** has asked for high roads for its runaways. Some of the schools run them, for love, and some sell the crossings. There is a Kite captain who has carried sixty people over the range on her back, one at a time, and there is a Shrike master who charged them.

**The Rose Coils of Coilspire** hold an old fine over a bird that fell on their plaza, and they will tell you which bird.

**The Moonlight Forest of Mosk-Vaal** buys the almanac for the moon's sake. It is the only customer that has never once tried to get a discount.

**The North and the South** know the Peaks mainly as arrow-fletching and a good place to send a difficult son. The real war, as far as any Kite is concerned, is against Highroost.

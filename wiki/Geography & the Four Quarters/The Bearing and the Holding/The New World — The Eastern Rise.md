# The New World — The Eastern Rise

**Say it** · the NOO WURLD

*The Eastern Rise*
> The eastern quarter is **still being made.** An oceanic plate is going down beneath it along the whole seaboard, and young ground is rich ground.

The Scarlet Bellows has moved its school forty leagues in a hundred and twenty-seven years, and it has done so at a run. In the autumn of Year 588 the Pyraeon pool at Pennock's Bar, the Core (a worked Wellspring site with keepers on it) that had fed the school for two generations, thinned to nothing in a single night after the caldera above it settled, and by the next evening a shepherd nine leagues up the ash plain was reporting warm stones and a color in the air like the inside of a peach. Mother Hawise Tregar had the hall down by dawn. Six hundred disciples in scarlet and yellow set off across the plain with the roof beams on their shoulders and the demountable array in numbered crates and every drum in the school going, and behind them came the dumpling sellers, the bookmakers, two minstrels and most of the town. Unhappily the Blue Sluice school had a shorter road and its own shepherd. The two met on the warm stones on the fifth day and fought for six more, coats blazing vermilion and kingfisher across a flat of white ash, in front of a crowd that had brought its children. It cost twenty-two dead and Mother Tregar's left hand. An Arbiter of the Accord came up on a mule, opened a hearing on the question of who owns a Wellspring site that has walked, and was on his second witness when the pool moved again in the night. Both schools got up and ran. The clerk followed with the docket.

They are still at it. Pennock's Bar is now a town on skids, called Walking Pennock on every chart and redrawn each decade, where rent runs by the season and the hearing is in its fifth Arbiter. Ask anyone there what the New World is and they will tell you it is the place where you keep your hall in crates.

**In one breath:** a country so young it will not hold still, with gold lying in the rivers for anyone who cares to stoop, and every town in it ready to pick up its roof and run after its luck.

The name misleads. There has never been a sea between this quarter and the rest of the land. A carter can walk here from the western kingdoms if he has the months and the ash leaves him a road. It is New because the Voyager Era's charts reached it last, and because the ground under it is younger than some of the families on it.

---

## Two Grounds

The eastern quarter holds two countries on one rim of the land, as different as a cathedral and a gold camp, and this ledger surveys one of them.

**The elven old-ground.** Eresse's ancient country, under four Eons of elven law: the Grand Principality of Eresse; Iampu and its Seasonal Courts; Rovann; the Sylvaar Dream Groves; the Echo Elves' Longhouse Accord; Velthaeir, the Floating Archive-City; the Sunroot Court at Vaelmarr. Its Wellspring sites have been pulled from since the first Eon. Two of its materials are entered below, Ironwood and Glacium, with the old-ground as their region. Nothing else of it is surveyed here.

**The arc.** The young volcanic ground the Guild Accord chartered from nothing, beginning when the Voyager Era's routes first brought its gold home and still adding charters within living memory: the andesite chain, the caldera belt, the ash plain between them, the coral platforms of the northern coast; Caedor and its four Cloudmetal arrays, Foraye, the Tsohanto Reach. **Everything from Formation to Trade Posture below is the arc's ledger and no other's.** Where the text says *the eastern quarter*, read *the arc*.

*Eresse and every elven branch sit here and not in the Old World quarter, which keeps Varūn. The Accord's doctrine that the New World was chartered from nothing, with no older law to displace, is true of the charter and of the charter alone.*

## Formation

The consequence of that subducting plate is a volcanic arc running the length of the quarter: a chain of andesite stratovolcanoes, an inland belt of calderas, and between them a plain of ash and lahar deposit **measured in fathoms rather than feet.**

The hot fluid systems above a live magma body deposit gold and silver in shallow veins that are still there because nothing has had time to erode them away. Where an intrusion has pushed into limestone, the contact zone has cooked into skarn and thrown out silver, lead, and zinc in bodies workable from surface. The rivers cutting through all of this have concentrated gold into placer bars **that a man can work with a pan and no capital whatsoever**, which is why the eastern quarter has the population it has.

The wet flanks weather differently. Under high rainfall and heat the ash breaks down into a deep red laterite crust, leaching out everything soluble and leaving behind a residue extraordinarily rich in aluminous and nickeliferous minerals. That crust is worthless to anyone who cannot reduce it, **and reducing it takes more Judicium capacity than the Old World has ever been willing to divert from its pumps.** (Judicium is the Wellspring of the lightning's line, an array is a standing runed working fed by keepers on a shift, and reduction is the old smelter's word for tearing a metal out of its rock.)

## The Ground Itself

Ash plains that carry a crop in three seasons and bury a village in one night. Cloud forest on the volcanic flanks, dripping, layered, loud with birds the color of a dyer's apron, and full of things nobody has classified. Obsidian flows glassy enough to cut a boot through. Sulfur fields around the fumaroles where the ground is warm and yellow and nothing grows. Red crust on every wet slope, rivers running gray with suspended pumice and turning gold at the bars, crater lakes of a green no painter has been paid enough to match, and off the northern coast, coral platforms building on drowned volcanic cones. Western visitors call it a beautiful way to die, and then they stay.

> **The Wellspring sites here move.** This is the fact eastern practice is organized around and that western surveyors consistently refuse to believe until they have lost a season to it.
>
> Ground this young does not hold a pool of Aether (the raw stuff in ground and air that a practitioner pulls and refines) in a fixed position across generations. A working sited on a strong local source can find that source thinned or shifted after an eruption, a caldera settling, or nothing anyone can identify. Fat ground this year is lean ground next, and the whole trade of the place is knowing which.
>
> **Eastern arrays are built to be moved. Western arrays are built to be permanent. Neither quarter thinks the other is sane.**

## Who Lives On It

A list of minerals will not tell you that this is the loudest quarter on the map. Every practitioner with a grudge and every younger son with a pan comes east sooner or later. Sects follow the pools and craft guilds follow the sects, so that a bar-town of nine hundred can hold four schools with four sets of colors, each one sure of the local Core, and an Essence-work stall on every corner. Rent runs by the season, because nobody signs for a year on ground like that, and is paid in dust by the pinch, which every landlord's thumb has trained to be larger. Food is ash-plain yams and beans, river fish split and smoked in red pepper, eggs boiled in a basket lowered into the vent by a child who has been told to hold her breath, and western flour at a price that makes bread a boast.

The politics of the place come straight out of the river. Gold won by hand needs nobody's permission, so a bar governs itself by **bar-moot**, an open meeting of whoever is standing in the water, which sets claim-lines, hangs thieves and sells its vote to the Accord's factor on alternate Thursdays. The Accord holds the charters and the debts, and the moots hold the rivers. Neither has yet found a way to collect from the other without a fight.

**Caedor** is a walled city wrapped around four white-blue fires, and everything in it, rent and songs and wars, comes down to keeping them fed. Rooms within sight of the arrays cost more than a house on the plain, because that is where the guild-sworn live, and the militia drills on the ramparts every evening under a light that turns everyone's teeth lavender. It eats other people's grain, and it has fought for its own roads more often than anyone has troubled to count.

**Foraye** is griffon country over a black glass valley, where the High Aerie Riders keep bonds seven centuries old and every militia child carries a pocketful of single-use glass. It is the nearest thing the arc has to an old town. Sancta Lux keeps a chapter there, and the rents on the high terraces are set by how far the griffon droppings fall.

**The Tsohanto Reach** is the long barrow-country at the arc's edge, with its own people and its own dead, who hold a settled opinion of the word *nothing* in the Accord's charter. Prospectors go in by the river for the black-sand bars. The ones who come back recommend asking first.

**The bar-towns** are everything else: Walking Pennock and a hundred like it, standing on Alchemelt floors and timber frames, each one sure it has found the real fat ground at last. Half of them will be elsewhere within your lifetime.

## Bearing Stock

*Bearing is what a material does under load, Holding is what working it will keep. T0 to T9 is the Accord's Tier ladder, and a Domain names the branch of natural law it works through.*

**Alchemelt Brick** · *T2 · Synthetic stone · Artisan guilds of the arc*

Volcanic ash ground with burned lime and water, which sets hard, sets fast, and **sets underwater.** This last property is why the eastern quarter is building deep-water harbors at a rate the west finds indecent while western harbors silt shut. It resists ambient magic and crumbles under sustained structural stress, so it is used for floors, foundations, moles, and cisterns rather than for anything that must span. Bearing moderate, Holding deliberately low. Domain Material Science.

**Pumice Float** · *T1 · Stone · Caldera ignimbrite sheets, universal*

Abrasive, filtration medium, and lightweight fill. Floats. Rafts of it come down the rivers after an eruption thick enough to walk on for a week, *which has killed a number of people who tried.*

**Redcrust Laterite** · *T2 · Mineral · Weathered flanks, wet eastern seaboard*

The red caprock. Useless as stone, useless as soil, and the feedstock for Cloudmetal. Bearing poor, Holding nil.

**Roughgold** · *T2 · Metal · Placer bars, black-sand rivers*

Won by hand, by anyone, with no permission from anyone, **which has produced the eastern quarter's characteristic political condition.** Carries Benediction and Sublimatio well, which makes it the standard substrate for ecclesiastical and oath work. Bearing soft, Holding high.

**Ironwood** · *T3 · Wood · Deep forest of Eresse, the elven old-ground*

Hardened to the equivalent of tempered steel, prized by the elves for ceremonial bows and shamanic staves, and cut under restrictions that make it **functionally unavailable to the trade.**

## Holding Stock

> **Onceglass** · *T3 · Crystallized stone · Obsidian flows quenched within an active Wellspring field, Foraye and the caldera belt*
>
> The eastern quarter's signature and the material western guilds most resent. Obsidian that cooled fast inside a live field of Aether takes a full inscription with unusual clarity, holds it perfectly, and **shatters completely on discharge. One casting, and then you have gravel.**
>
> It cannot be repaired, cannot be recharged, and cannot be traced back to its Engraver, which is a separate problem the Enforcement Division has raised four times. What it can be is cut in quantity, cheaply, by moderately skilled hands at a bench, and sold to people who could never afford a permanent working. **An eastern militia carries Onceglass charges the way a western militia carries arrows.**
>
> Bearing brittle by design, Holding high and terminal. Resonance: Fractura, Tenebra. Domain Abstract and Entropic Systems.

**Cloudmetal** · *T4 · Metal · Reduced from Redcrust laterite, the eastern arc works*

A structural metal at a third the weight of iron, silver-pale, corrosion-proof, and impossible to produce without a sustained Judicium reduction array of a scale that exists in **four places in the world**, all of them along the eastern arc.

Caedor did not discover the metal. Everyone knew it was in the laterite. The eastern arc was simply the only polity willing to commit that much array capacity to something other than pumping water, **because the eastern arc has no old flooded mines to pump.** This is the clearest case in the ledger of a capability following from geological accident rather than genius, and the arc guild would prefer it were not written down in those terms. Bearing good for its weight, Holding low. Domain Electromagnetism.

**Skarnrose Ore** · *T3 · Metal ore · Intrusive contacts with limestone, inland caldera belt*

Silver, lead, zinc, and garnet in banded contact bodies, worked open from surface because the deposits outcrop. **Silver here is a byproduct of a zinc operation**, which is an inversion that western silver houses have found financially unpleasant.

**Yellowbreath Sulfur** · *T1 · Mineral · Fumarole fields throughout the arc*

Sublimate sulfur, collected by hand off the vent rims by men who work in wet cloth and do not work long. Feedstock for every acid in the eastern alchemical trade and a primary Pyraeon reagent. Domain Thermodynamics.

**Ember Flint** · *T2 · Stone · Foraye*

Sparks a mild magical flame when struck. Field-mage survival stock, and **the single most widely distributed Essence-active object in the eastern quarter by simple count.**

**Seerstone Resin** · *T4 · Liquid essence · Canopy trees, eastern cloud forest, provisional attribution*

Stores visions and is dangerously volatile. Provenance recorded on the strength of the resin's physical character and the tree species involved. *This attribution has not been confirmed by the Archives Division and is flagged accordingly.*

**Glacium** · *T5 · Frost-infused mineral · Iampu, the elven old-ground, grown in the ice-caves under Winter Court license*

**Grown** rather than quarried, slowly, in caverns held below freezing for the purpose, by a process the Winter Court does not document and does not permit anyone to inspect. Frost-bound weaponry and the frost-infused stock that supplements Iampu's gold in the mercenary trade. Bearing brittle, Holding high and narrow. Resonance: Vohrin. Domain Thermodynamics.

## The Works

Shallow, fast, run on borrowed coin, and enormously productive. The eastern quarter works almost nothing at depth, **because it does not need to.** Its ore outcrops. Its gold is in the rivers. Its cement makes itself out of the ash that falls on the works. It has, in consequence, almost none of the array dependency that defines the west.

The characteristic eastern operation is small, mobile, and expects to be somewhere else in five years. Buildings are Alchemelt over timber frame. Arrays are demountable, and their keepers walk beside the crates. **Nobody sinks a shaft that assumes the ground will still be there.**

The exception is the eastern arc, which has built the only permanent great works in the quarter and has organized its entire polity around defending the four reduction arrays that make Cloudmetal possible.

---

## What It Costs

> **Ash fall**, which buries. **Lahar**, which is a river of wet ash moving at the speed of a horse and which does not leave bodies to bury. **Caldera collapse**, which is rare and total. **Fumarole gas**, which kills silently in the hollows where it pools.
>
> And the **Wellspring drift**, the wandering of a site's pool, which is rarely lethal and has bankrupted more eastern ventures than every eruption in recorded history combined.

---

## Trade Posture

Exports Cloudmetal, silver, gold, Onceglass, sulfur, and cement. Imports iron, tools, wolfram, credit, and expertise. **The arc owes the west heavily and resents it.** *This is the arc's posture. The elven old-ground owes nobody and says so at length.*

The Accord's Arbitration Division spends a disproportionate share of its calendar on the arc's colonial rights, Wellspring access, and the question of whether an unregistered Onceglass charge is a weapon or a commodity.

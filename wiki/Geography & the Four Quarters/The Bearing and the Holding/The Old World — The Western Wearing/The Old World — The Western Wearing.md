# The Old World — The Western Wearing

**Say it** · the OHLD WURLD

*The Western Wearing*
> **The root of a mountain belt that no longer exists.** The most geologically generous ground in the four quarters, and the most thoroughly emptied.

Every year on the feast of the first thaw, the people of the Aerne valley bury their river. They have done it since the water below the old adits turned the color of a tangerine and everything in it died, which was before anyone's great-grandmother. The Mourners' Guild turns out in marigold and white, seven painted barges come down with drums and pipes, and a lead-lined coffin the size of a hay cart is filled at the bank with a hundred gallons of the river itself and rowed to the sea reach with the whole valley weeping on the towpath and thoroughly drunk by noon. On the wharf at the end of the procession sit the Chancery's buyers at their folding tables, because that orange water, refined, is the mordant that fixes ink to vellum, and the Accord has bought every codex it has ever written out of the Aerne's drainage. In the spring of Year 652 Gunnora Hextall, Mistress of the Mourners, stopped the coffin in front of those tables and asked what they had paid for the deceased. They told her. She had it tipped over the ledgers. Vitriol at that strength does not burn the skin, so the junior clerk who leaned in to save his page felt nothing at all until he could no longer see it; fourteen people died in the riot on the wharf, and four years of the Aerne's accounts dissolved on the planks. The buyers came back the next spring at double the rate. So did the coffin, which is now weighed before it is rowed, and the Mourners take a tenth.

That is the Old World, and I recommend the funeral. Here everything has been dug, sold, poisoned, mourned and reworked at a profit, in the best-kept country in the world.

**In one breath:** the deepest mines on earth, the oldest money, and a whole people paid by the hour to keep the water out, who have known for seventy years that the ground is thinning under them and have not stopped arguing or singing.

## Formation

Two continental masses collided, in an age nobody can date, threw up a range on the scale of anything now standing, and then spent an unimaginable stretch of time being worn back down to the stumps. What is left at the surface is **the deep interior of that range brought up into daylight**: slate belts and mica schist folded like cloth, with granite bodies intruded up into them from below. The old masses have been one land since long before there was anyone to walk across the join.

Those granite bodies are the point. As each one cooled it sweated out a hot mineralizing fluid into the cracks in its own roof, and that fluid deposited **tin and tungsten** in sheeted veins around the granite cupolas. Further out, cooler, the same fluids left **lead and silver** in the limestone. Along the seaward foreland, in the basins that formed while the range was still rising, plant matter accumulated and became **coal**. Along the coastal plain, buried salt has risen through the overburden in domes, dragging up sulfur and oil-shale with it.

It has been worked continuously for longer than any current kingdom has existed.

## The Ground Itself

Worked country, and handsome with it. Terraced hillsides, coppiced woodland cut on rotation for eleven hundred years, hedgerows on field boundaries older than the treaties that name them, pale towns on green hills with a slate roof and a sect banner on every rise. Spoil heaps stand at the head of every valley, **grassed over on the outside and still poisonous within,** and the sheep on them are the fattest in the quarter and are not eaten by anyone who knows. In the tin districts the ground subsides without warning over workings nobody recorded, and in the coal foreland it subsides on a schedule everyone has learned to plan around.

The rivers run orange below the old adits and have done for generations. Nothing lives in them above the tidal reach. The harbors silt, because eleven centuries of mining spoil has to go somewhere and it goes downstream.

> Aetheric Density (how much Aether, the raw stuff of magic, the ground and air hold for anyone to pull) is low to moderate and **locally depleted around the old districts**, which is the western quarter's defining problem and the reason its arrays are so expensive to run. An array is a standing runed working, and it stops when the keepers who feed it can no longer fill themselves from the ground they stand on. Since Year 645 the figure has fallen every year. The Research Division calls this *contested*. The bracket fighters of the west call it *the thin*, and will tell you at length how much longer it takes them to fill back up between bouts than it took their teachers.

## Who Lives On It

The west is the crowded quarter, the one with the guild halls, the law and the bad manners. Its cities are built of that pale limestone with the color put on afterward: painted shields over every door, Greenmantle green and Saltmarket red fighting it out on the bridges, lamps in rose and amber on the wharves, each one burning the aura of whoever keeps it. Ironlink is the great example, a pit town that became a fairground without closing a single pit, whose midsummer brackets draw forty thousand under yellow awnings and whose keepers' hall sits opposite the bookmakers for convenience. The Greaves districts live on tin, and every family there can tell you to the fathom how deep the lode has gone since their grandfather's day and exactly who is to blame.

**Rent is a map of the ground.** Fat ground is where the wardens work cheap and the lamps stay lit, so a room on it costs what a house costs on lean, and a good many western fortunes consist entirely of owning a street that has not thinned yet. People move downhill as the ground fails. The landlords move first.

**Food comes from somewhere else.** The quarter imports it, because its rivers are dead and its best land has been under spoil for centuries. A western table is pease and barley, salt fish off the coast road, mutton from the clean hills, a great deal of pickling, and a pie on every corner whose contents are between the baker and the magistrate. A bad harvest in the east reaches a western loaf before the news does, since the news moves by beacon at a price and a factor moves by instinct.

**The politics are water.** The great houses own the shafts and the pump-masters run them, the array-keepers man them by the hour and can walk off at the change of shift, and everyone can count to ninety. Companies, crowns and sects sign and break charters over Core-keepers' contracts the way other countries fight over a river crossing. No king in the quarter has ever won an argument with his own keepers' hall, though several have tried, and their successors have a good view of the result from the palace, which is on higher ground.

**Varūn** keeps to its mountains, and its dwarves sell you veinwood at a fair price and psionite at no price at all. It has outlasted every western kingdom it has traded with, and it mentions this.

## Bearing Stock

*Bearing is what a material does under load, Holding is what working it will keep. T0 to T9 is the Accord's Tier ladder, and a Resonance, Family and Domain name the Wellsprings a material answers to, their family, and the branch of natural law they work through.*

**Forge-Iron** · *T2 · Metal · Altherion, Inner World, northern rim (ruled 2026-09-12)*

The standard armament and structural iron of the western kingdoms. Durable against blades, poor Essence retention, made in quantity and to a specification that has not meaningfully changed in three hundred years. Bearing good, Holding poor.

> **Slagbloom** · *T2 · Metal · Recovered from ancient spoil, western foreland and tin districts*
>
> The signature material of the quarter and a fair summary of its condition. Old smelting was inefficient enough that the slag heaps of dead kingdoms still assay well for iron, and today's forge-arrays are hot enough to get it out.
>
> Slagbloom is cheap, sulfurous, brittle in the cold, and carries a persistent oddity: **inscriptions laid on it read doubled**, because the metal was worked and marked once already and something of the earlier marking survives the second melt. Engravers charge extra to work it. Superstitious foremen will not have it in a mine cage.
>
> Bearing moderate and inconsistent, Holding erratic. Resonance: Anamnesis, unwelcome. Family Materia, Domain Material Science.

**Palegrit Limestone** · *T2 · Stone · Platform carbonate across the central west*

Ashlar for building, lime for mortar, and flux for every iron furnace in the quarter. Three uses, one quarry, and the reason the western kingdoms build in pale stone while the north builds in gray. Karst country: the same rock dissolves, which gives the west its cave systems, its sinkholes, and its habit of losing rivers underground for leagues at a time. Bearing good, Holding poor.

**Veinwood Plank** · *T2 · Wood · Mountain trees of Varūn*

Semi-hardened grain, briefly enchantable, standard stock for ritual shields.

**Kirkyard Lead** · *T2 · Metal · Galena in the limestone belt, worked with the silver*

Roofing, coffin lining, pipe and sling bullets. Its real importance is single: **lead sheet attenuates Essence more effectively per unit thickness than anything else in common supply**, so every assay house and every array isolation room in the west is lead-lined. *The men who smelt it do not last.* Bearing soft, Holding nil, shielding excellent.

## Holding Stock

> **Blacktooth Wolfram** · *T3 · Metal ore · Sheeted veins in the granite cupolas, western tin districts*
>
> Wolframite, black, absurdly heavy, and possessed of the highest melting point of any workable metal in the ledger. **This is the vessel problem's answer.** Forge-arrays can reach temperatures that will slump any ordinary crucible, and wolfram will not slump. Every nozzle, every hearth crown, every array-furnace lining above T4 work is wolfram or is lined with something wolfram holds.
>
> It has no glamour, no legend, and **no substitute**, and the Accord tracks every ton of it. Bearing extreme under heat, Holding low. Resonance: Ignivale, Exuroth. Family Caloria, Domain Thermodynamics.

**Greaves Tin** · *T2 · Metal · Cassiterite, lode and stream-work, western tin districts*

Bronze, solder, and the tinning of array contacts. Contact corrosion in a working glyph array is a slow, invisible, cumulative failure, and a tinned contact does not corrode. **Unglamorous, essential, and running out.** The stream works were exhausted six generations ago and the lodes are now worked at depths that only exist because of the pumps. Bearing moderate, Holding moderate. Resonance: Coagulatio. Family Materia, Domain Material Science.

**Attercrop Vitriol** · *T2 · Liquid reagent · Drainage of abandoned workings, universal in the west*

Green vitriol, drawn off where pyrite in old spoil weathers into acid water. It is the poison in the orange rivers and it is also, refined, the mordant that fixes ritual ink to any organic substrate. **The same fluid that killed the Aerne valley writes the Accord's own codices.** Western alchemists have stopped remarking on this, and the Aerne has not. Resonance: Dissolution. Family Materia, Domain Material Science. Blinds on contact with the eye and does so without warning, since it does not burn on the skin at working dilution.

**Psionite** · *T4 · Mineral · Varūn deep workings*

Mental fortification stock, worked only by psionically attuned Varūn dwarves under the Psionic Mastery Code. Effectively unavailable outside the kingdom.

**Vellun Copper** · *T1 · Metal · Source hills unlocated*

Malleable, a good spiritual conductor, and melts far too easily to be useful past apprentice work. **The trade name is retained because the shipments are continuous and the assay is consistent. The hills it is named for do not appear on any survey the Accord has commissioned.**

## The Works

Deep, mature, money-heavy, and **running on borrowed ground.** Western shafts are the deepest in the world and every one of them is a standing bill: the pumps must run whether the mine is producing or not, and the pumps run on Essence that the ground under the pit head no longer gives up in sufficient quantity. The great western houses buy Essence supply the way an army buys grain (Core-keepers' contracts, array crews on retainer, sealed Holding vessels coming in by mule), and a bad year in supply closes shafts that are still full of ore.

The compensating advantage is that **everything else exists.** Roads, canals, winding houses, assay offices, guild schools, burial clubs and sea-loans, and eleven centuries of accumulated practice about how to keep men alive underground. A western pit does more work per man than any pit in the other three quarters and it does it in a hole that should have drowned a hundred years ago.

Reworking is the coming trade. Slag heaps, old tailings, flooded levels pumped out and picked over. *There is a whole class of western operator who has never sunk an original shaft and never intends to.*

---

## What It Costs

Firedamp in the coal foreland, which the draught arrays manage until they do not. Subsidence in the tin districts. Vitriol drainage everywhere. Silicosis in the slate belts, which the trade calls **the grinder's cough** and which is simply what happens to men who breathe rock dust for twenty years.

> Above all, the array dependency. The western quarter has arranged its whole livelihood so that a sustained Essence shortage, or a long enough walkout by the keepers, becomes **within about ninety hours, a mass drowning.**

---

## Trade Posture

Exports finished goods, worked metal, wolfram, tin, expertise, and credit. Imports raw ore from all three other quarters, phosphate from the south, and food. Holds the debt of the north and much of the east.

> The western position rests less on what the west has in the ground than on being **the only quarter that can turn anyone else's ground into finished work**, and the western houses are entirely aware that this is a service and that a service can be taken elsewhere.

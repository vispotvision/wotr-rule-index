# The Outer World — The Southern Pan

**Say it** · the OW-ter WURLD

*The Southern Pan*
> Old, low, dry, and structurally strange: **the only quarter whose geology cannot be described without reference to something that is not geology.**

The best wheat ever grown in the western kingdoms came up at Yarrowby in the summer of Year 667, chest high and the color of old ivory, on forty tons of ground chalk that the factor Reynold Attwater had sold the parish off a barge from the southern shelf. That barge had carried a lot prepared for a Dorrosfenn temple on its previous run, and nobody had swept it. The parish held its harvest supper at the field's edge with ribbons on the wagons and a fiddler up on a barrel, and at moonrise the churchyard wall went over and three centuries of Yarrowby's dead walked in among the tables, took the scythes down off the wagons, and started on the top field in a straight line. They were superb. They had, after all, been the parish's reapers. Some of the guests cheered, and old Marjory Lusk carried a cup of cider out to a man she had buried in Year 629 and stood holding it while he went past her. They had three hundred acres in by dawn and showed no sign of stopping at the boundary, and sixteen living people who stood in the row to reason with them were reaped with the wheat. The Holy Sea of Alabaster took the factor to law for selling a necromantic reagent as fertilizer. Attwater set two lumps of chalk on the Arbiter's table, one from the barge and one from a sack the Sea's own almoner had bought for the abbey garden, and invited the court to tell them apart. It could not, and neither could anyone after. The Yarrowby flour went to market that autumn under a different name, and I am told it made excellent bread.

Almost everything the rest of the world knows about the south arrives that way, on somebody else's barge and in need of explanation.

**In one breath:** the poorest quarter in the world, red plain and white pan and black basalt under a blazing sky, sitting on the handful of things the other three cannot make anything without, and half of it belongs to dragons who issue no survey permits, with a young Vampire empire under veiled skies on the other half.

---

## Formation

The bulk of it is a worn craton (a slab of the oldest and most stubborn crust) comparable in age to the northern shield but **never glaciated**, so instead of scoured rock it carries a duricrust: a hard iron-cemented caprock that formed by chemical weathering over an inconceivable stretch of time and now armors the interior in a stony gibber plain, rust red to the horizon, that turns a wagon wheel and breaks a horse's leg.

Across the western third lies a **flood-basalt province**, a stack of lava sheets erupted in a single geological convulsion and now weathered into stepped terraces league upon league. Along the eastern coast runs an **ophiolite belt**, a slice of ancient ocean floor shoved up onto the land, which is why the coastal ranges are serpentine and chromite and support almost no vegetation. In the interior basins lie **the pans**, dried inland seas floored with nitrate, borate, and gypsum. Along the old shelf margins in the south lie **the bone-beds.**

> And through all of it runs **the Crevice**, which is not a fault in any sense the Measurewrights are willing to certify, and along which the rock stops obeying the ordinary rules at a distance the Accord has fixed at four leagues **and revises upward roughly once a decade.**

---

## The Ground Itself

Gibber plain, silcrete, salt pan, and basalt step. Marsh in the Dorrosfenn basins where the drainage fails and the water sits, thick with lilies and things that eat lilies. Serpentine barrens on the eastern coast, green-gray, sterile, and dusty in a way that is worse than it looks. The Veil-tide shores in the far south where the bones wash up. After one of the pans' rare storms the lightning stands on the white crust in columns as far as the eye goes, and every child in the confederations can tell you what it is worth by the armload.

There is very little water and what there is, is mostly undrinkable. There is very little wood. **Almost everything the southern quarter builds, it builds out of stone or out of something that used to be alive.**

Aetheric Density (how much Aether, the raw stuff of magic, the ground and air hold for a practitioner to pull) is low across the interior and violently irregular near the Crevice, which is the reason the southern quarter has no array work worth the name and the reason it never developed any. An array is a standing runed working fed by keepers on a shift, and keepers on lean ground starve.

## Who Lives On It

The ledger's three polities are loud in the way of people who have never once been left alone by anyone with a boat.

**Dorrosfenn** is the marsh theocracy that will rent you your own dead. Its temple-towns stand on Trapstone piles over black water, hung with green lamps and bone wind-chimes and crowded with sect processions, and a tenant there pays one rent to the temple on the house and a second on the grandfather, who is kept on and does useful work. Food is eel, reed-bread and marsh duck, salted with nitre from the pans and tasting faintly of it. The priests rule, the priests argue, and the priests are the one buyer on earth who want Marrowchalk raw.

**The Braqthian confederations** hold the terraces and the pan margins, tribal, proud, and arranged around standing stones that nobody in the west has ever troubled to read. They pay no rent, since a clan's ground is its stones and whoever can lift them. They live on goat, dried fruit and pan-salt meat, and they gather lightning glass after storms and sell it to Accord factors at one-fortieth of what it fetches at the other end of the road, a piece of arithmetic every Braqthian child can do and the factors wish they could not.

**Epprenea** quarries the bone-beds under its Spire, in open benches white enough to blind a man, and its town is loud with barge-masters, chalk-dust and Alabaster's lawyers. It feeds half the western wheat fields and buys its bread back from them at a price it has had four riots about.

Below all three there are the pan-scrapers' camps and the quarry towns, where a petty folk-worker who can sweeten a cistern is the most important person for forty leagues and knows it, and may decline to come out for less than a goat.

## Bearing Stock

*Bearing is what a material does under load, Holding is what working it will keep. T0 to T9 is the Accord's Tier ladder, and a Resonance and Domain name the Wellsprings a material answers to, and the branch of natural law they work through.*

**Trapstone** · *T2 · Stone · Flood-basalt terraces, western interior*

Columnar basalt, split naturally into six-sided posts that require almost no dressing. The universal building stone of the quarter, **quarried by prying rather than cutting.** Resonates readily with Coagula and Fixatio, which the Braqthian tribal confederations exploit in their standing-stone work and which the west has never bothered to study. Bearing good, Holding moderate. Domain Material Science.

**Greenskin Serpentine** · *T2 · Stone · Ophiolite belt, eastern coastal ranges*

Soft, green, easily carved, and pleasant to work. In certain pods it is fibrous, and the fibers separate into a dust so fine it does not settle. Men who quarry the fibrous pods develop a scarring of the lung that appears twenty years after they leave the trade and **kills them regardless of what they did in the interval.** The Accord's Enforcement Division has posted no writ on this, and the coastal quarries have raised no objection to the absence.

**Mireclay** · *T0 · Clay · Marsh basins of Dorrosfenn*

Brittle, dissolves wherever Aether runs strong, used for short-lived summoning totems in rural spirit rites and for very little else.

**Coilhide Leather** · *T2 · Beastkin hide · Ironcoils, native to Braqth*

Training armor and gloves. Common enough in the southern quarter to be unremarkable and expensive enough in the west to be a minor trade.

**Coalthread Cloth** · *T1 · Fabric · Southern weave, distribution uncontrolled*

A black-laced weave that dulls aura flicker and burns readily. *The Accord notes without further comment that its principal known users are smugglers.*

## Holding Stock

> **Marrowchalk** · *T2 · Mineral · Phosphorite bone-beds, southern shelf margins, Dorrosfenn and Epprenea*
>
> The most consequential material in the southern quarter and the one that explains its politics. A bedded rock composed of the accumulated skeletal remains of an ancient shallow sea, phosphate-rich, chalky, and quarried in open benches by the thousand tons.
>
> Ground and spread on western fields it **doubles a wheat yield.** Ground and prepared otherwise it is **the primary reagent of every necromantic practice in the quarter.** The same bench, the same rock, the same barge.
>
> The Holy Sea of Alabaster has twice attempted to establish that the two uses can be legally distinguished at the point of sale and has twice failed, **because they cannot be distinguished at the point of extraction either.**
>
> Bearing negligible, Holding high and specific. Resonance: Mortalis, Rebirthine. Domain Biochemistry.

**Palebed Nitre** · *T1 · Mineral · Nitrate caliche, interior pans*

Crusted nitrate salts left in basins that have not seen rain in living memory. Oxidizer for alchemical charge work, preservative for meat, **preservative for corpses**, and fertilizer where Marrowchalk cannot be got. Harvested by scraping. The pans have been worked for eight hundred years and are not measurably diminished. Resonance: Exuroth. Domain Thermodynamics.

> **Ashfast Chrome** · *T3 · Metal ore · Chromite pods in the serpentine belt, eastern coast*
>
> The southern quarter's one strategic export. Chromite ground to sand and rammed into a furnace hearth will not react with slag, will not fuse, and will not fail at temperatures that destroy every other lining material in supply.
>
> **Between Ashfast hearth sand and Blacktooth wolfram nozzles, the entire high-temperature capability of the four quarters rests on two ores that occur in two places.** The Logistics Division has said so in every annual return for nineteen years.
>
> Bearing extreme under heat, Holding nil. Resonance: Fixatio. Domain Thermodynamics.

**Stormfulgur** · *T4 · Natural glass · Fulgurite fields, nitre pans of the interior*

Where lightning strikes the pans it fuses a branching tube of glass down through the crust, sometimes to the depth of a man's height. These tubes are naturally hollow, naturally continuous, and naturally aligned along the path a discharge took, which makes them **the cheapest ready-formed Essence conduit in the world.** The Braqthian confederations gather them after storms the way coastal people gather driftwood, and sell them by the armload to Accord factors who resell them at **forty times the price** for the long runs of an array. Fragile, irregular, and impossible to manufacture. Resonance: Mirithane, Aurevane. Domain Electromagnetism.

**Driftbone** · *T1 · Organic · Veil-tide shores*

Hollow bone bleached by the tides, carrying whispers when hollowed, ritually carved by dream shamans. *What the tides are bleaching, and where it comes from, is not recorded, and the shamans decline to speculate in front of Accord surveyors.*

**Gloomhide** · *T5 · Leather · Beasts born in forgotten spirit zones, southern interior*

Repels light-based castings and weak divine strikes. Spiritually inert and physically hyper-resilient, which makes it the one material in the ledger that is simultaneously an ideal armor backing and **completely useless as a substrate.**

**Grimbarite** · *T6 · Stone · Buried beneath the Crevice fault system*

Repels both Fate and Name manipulation. Heavy, silent in all forms, **absorbs prayer.** Extraction requires working within the Crevice margin, and *the mortality figures for Grimbarite crews are not published.*

> **Crevice Shale** · *T9 · Rock · The Crevice itself*
>
> Glows black. Fractures nearby Realm law. Required in the crafting of unmaking weapons and **forbidden to move, hold, or trade under Article III of the Concord Codex.**
>
> Recorded here because a ledger that omits it is a ledger somebody will trust.

## The Works

There are no southern works in the sense the other three quarters use the word. There is **extraction**, which is quarrying and scraping and gathering, performed by hand at enormous scale with almost no gear beyond a pick and a sledge, and there is **shipping**, which is what the extracted material is immediately subjected to.

> The west calls this backwardness, and it is **the correct answer to the conditions.** Array work requires reliable ground to pull from and the southern interior does not have it. What the quarter has instead is materials that require no processing at all before they are worth carrying: nitre you scrape, phosphate you dig, chromite you crush, fulgurite you pick up off the ground.
>
> The southern quarter has arranged itself around the fact that **its wealth needs nothing done to it.**

The consequence is that almost all of the value added to southern material is added in western furnaces, and almost all of the profit stays there. Dorrosfenn's necromantic theocracy is the only southern polity that has built a trade at home on a material from home, and it has done so by being **the only buyer that wants Marrowchalk in the form it comes out of the ground.**

---

## What It Costs

**Thirst**, which is the ordinary death. **Serpentine dust**, which is a slower one. **The pans**, which reflect enough light to blind a man in a season and which have no shade for forty leagues in any direction.

And **the Crevice**, which does not kill in any way the Measurewrights have found language for, and which is why the four-league line exists and why it keeps moving outward.

---

## Trade Posture

Exports phosphate, nitre, chromite, fulgurite, hides, and labor. Imports well-diggers, cistern-masons, timber, iron, and grain. Holds almost no credit and almost no debt, **because western moneylenders and factors will not write paper against ground they cannot survey.**

> The southern quarter is the poorest of the four by every measure the Accord records, and it sits on **two of the six materials without which the other three quarters cannot make anything above T4.**
>
> Nobody involved considers this arrangement stable.

## The Quarter Above the Ledger

Clemence Rooke, Measurewright, petitioned the Dragon Sovereign's court for a survey permit six times between Year 631 and Year 640. The sixth answer came by Wyvern, which landed in her camp on the Dust Coast, ate the mules with every courtesy, and left under a rock a sealed refusal correct in every point of treaty form. She filed it, walked home, and wrote up what she had been able to see, which is the whole of the page above. What follows came from people who had wings to ask, or who did not ask.

> **Two accounts of this quarter, and they disagree, though not in the way the Old World's do.** The note at the foot of this page says why.
>
> Everything above is the Measurewrights' survey: an unglaciated craton, duricrust and gibber, flood basalt, ophiolite coast, nitre pans, bone-beds, the Crevice. Almost no water, almost no wood, no array work, and the polities are the Braqthian confederations, Dorrosfenn and Epprenea.
>
> Everything below is drawn from [[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Geography of the Outer World|The Geography of the Outer World]], which has the whole of it: a long land split between an Aether-starved **dragon south**, spined by a colossal volcanic cordillera and carrying a vast rainforest, and a cold, veiled **Vampire north** whose ground still holds its Aether.
>
> **The two agree on the southern metaphysics and disagree on the water.** That is a different failure from the Old World's, and it points somewhere useful.

### Orientation

**Classification:** Divided Supercontinent, Aether-Preserved Vampire North and Aether-Depleted Dragon South · **Epithet:** *The Land Beneath Two Skies*

**Sovereign powers:** the Draconic Empire of Drakyssia in the south and the Dreadvalis Empire in the north · **Territorial share:** even, each holding half of recognized territory directly or through tributaries

Other peoples: human kingdoms, elven enclaves, free cities and frontier peoples, split between the two empires.
> The south possesses plenty. It is poor because too many ancient things have already eaten.

- **Far North:** the Frostfang Cordillera with its glaciers and Frozen Wells, and the Last Archipelago. Dreadvalis holds the mountains from Grimhold Deeps; the island peoples owe a light tithe.
- **North:** the Moonlight Forest, the Moonlight Snow Plains and the Northern Tablelands, the Veiled Provinces of Dreadvalis, where noon under the Great Veils is deep blue twilight.
- **Northeast:** the northern Serrathan Highlands, old, worn, and rich in iron and silver around the undercity of Vel Sarith.
- **The Twilight March:** the frontier band where the veils end and the sun returns. The Red Marches, the Great Grass Sea, the old Five River country and the Golden Escarpment, split between northern tributaries above and Drakyssian Houses and tributaries below.
- **South-Central:** rainforest, river basin, wetland and transitional dryland. The richest biological diversity and some of the weakest ambient Aether. Forests physically alive and metaphysically exhausted: plants grow, animals breed, rivers run, and magical abundance does not return at the rate it is consumed. **The further inland, the less ordinary borders mean; dragon territory is often marked by flight range instead.**
- **Southwest:** the Drakhal Spine, a colossal volcanic chain of snow peaks over fire, with high desert, deep valleys, a narrow coastal strip, and the largest surviving concentration of geothermal Aether in the south. **Drakyssia's heart lies in its central volcanic highlands.**
- **Southeast:** the Eastern Sun Coast and its port cities, under House Avarax and treaty protection.
- **Far South:** the Ash Wastes and the ancient flame-realm of Zyraxia, Drakyssian sacred territory, where the last Salamanders sleep.

### The Aether Gradient

> **The defining geographical fact here is how much Aether the ground still holds, and that falls steadily from north to south.**
>
> The north kept its reserve for plain reasons. No dragon ever lived there, because for seven thousand years it was too cold for fire-born bodies. A Vampire takes its Essence through blood and draws nothing from soil, forest or river. The Vohrin-touched cold slows depletion along with everything else, the Great Veils let Aether settle and pool, and the Dreadvalis tax careless magic, license every Wellspring and plan like people who do not age.
>
> The southern ground is alive and **spent.** Wild Aether that would circulate through soil, plant, weather, seam, river and animal has been pulled down across enormous territories, many of which carry a fraction of the Aetheric Density considered normal elsewhere. Natural Wellspring sites remain, but deep, unstable, dormant, tightly localized, or claimed by dragons whose territories formed around them generations ago.
>
> This is the principal reason dragons dominate the south. A dragon needs **energetically viable** territory, and calderas, deep faults, ancient Wellspring sites, geothermal basins, Soul-rich forest, storm corridors and subterranean seams therefore carry political value equal to farmland, ports or gold. **The strongest draconic states hold land only as the way of holding what remains beneath it.**

Five processes emptied the southern reserve, and no account of them is disinterested.

**Draconic consumption.** Large dragons passively pull more ambient Aether than entire mortal settlements. A region carrying hundreds of mature dragons is under a constant metaphysical drain. Drakyssia learned to manage this through territorial rotation, volcanic recharge zones, controlled access to Wellspring sites, migration law and subterranean reservoirs. Less disciplined draconic territories simply drained themselves.

**Ancient warfare.** Dragon wars rarely leave ordinary battlefields. Breath alters geology; elemental conflict at scale destroys forests, evaporates lakes, fractures Wellsprings, destabilizes Soul-plane recognition, vitrifies valleys. **Many of today's Dead Zones are old battle zones.**

**Wellspring extraction.** Ancient civilizations found methods of pulling directly from Wellspring sites and treated the pools as limitless. They were not. Several collapsed after emptying local pools faster than the pools recovered, and the south is still scattered with enormous abandoned works around sites that no longer answer: relics of the Great Spirit War and the days of the Physical Epochs (when Bara, Ashura and Aegor walked in human form), dangerous to enter and still killed over.

**Heat and storm.** In the southern heat, Aether that might have pooled is burned off by volcanic storms and heatwaves almost as fast as it rises.

**The great drain.** The land appears to have lost part of its ability to circulate ambient Aether at all. Some Measurewrights hold that repeated extraction damaged the metaphysical equivalent of groundwater flow; others that the draconic population simply prevents recovery. Drakyssian scholars reject the accusation that dragons alone caused it and point at pre-imperial human, elven, demonic and vanished ruins demonstrating Essence use on a ruinous scale. **The safest conclusion is that everyone inherited a wounded south and nearly everyone made it worse.**

### Kra'Vorrak

**In one breath:** a volcano with a city stacked up the inside of it, where your rent climbs with your altitude until the next floor is let only to things with wings.

The Imperial Sky-Capital occupies the interior of a colossal volcanic complex. **The city rises rather than spreads.** Its lowest districts hold mortals, smiths, servants, markets, bonded houses, foundries and armorers' rows, loud and sulfur-yellow and smelling of hot iron and roast goat, fed on grain that comes up the mountain as tribute. Higher districts hold dragon landing terraces, War-Wing barracks, noble roosts, ritual temples, command platforms, scholar keeps and avenues built for beings hundreds of meters long. At the highest levels stand imperial structures visible from dozens of miles out.

In the caldera's living heart burns **the Ember Crown**, the strongest living Wellspring left in the south: a current of Pyraeon, the ember that stays lit, that has never cooled in any record. Every Dragon Road bends toward it, and every Dragon House measures its rank by how close its roost lies.

Around it: **the Dragon Catacombs**, subterranean chambers where ancient dragons sleep and Bond Rituals are performed. **Avo'Kara Sanctum**, a ritual city for bond law, Soul Crystal rites, hatchery custom, draconic music and spiritual recognition. **Dova'Ruun Fortress**, the greatest military complex in the empire. **The Black Scale Ranges**, reserved for trueblood nesting. **The Wyrmdeep Cities** beneath the plateau. **The Iron Roosts**, Wyvern breeding and aerial-war fortresses. **The Ember Vaults**, treasury-forges built against controlled magma chambers. **The Drakhalis Throne Spire**, monument, imperial archive, mausoleum and ceremonial structure attached to the name of Kain Drakhalis.

### The Great Volcanic Belt

The southwestern margin is among the most geologically violent regions in the Continuum: thousands of active, dormant and extinct volcanoes along the Spine, several large enough to carry independent ecosystems.

**Mount Kra'Vorrak**, the complex holding the capital. **Dova's Furnace**, an active stratovolcano used historically for military trials and large-scale dragon forging. **The Avo Caldera**, a partly collapsed basin of ritual sanctuaries and geothermal lakes. **Mount Veyrath**, among the highest active peaks, with lightning standing around its summit. **The Black Mother**, dormant, its lava tubes holding ancient Wyrm settlements. **The Hollow Flame**, extinct, its magma chamber emptied into an underground cavern hundreds of miles across.

Volcanic ground is among the only places in the south where natural Aether replenishment stays comparatively strong, so nearly every major volcano is claimed, *sometimes by an empire, sometimes by a noble House, and sometimes by one dragon powerful enough that nobody has successfully informed it the land belongs to anyone else.*

### Metaphysical Terrain

The continent cannot be read from ordinary geography. Depletion in the south and preservation in the north have produced classes of ground barely known on other land.

**Living Land.** The veiled north, above all the Moonlight Forest. The Three Planes connect normally and the land gives back Aether at a natural rate.

**Frozen Wells.** Aether reservoirs locked in ancient ice in Vohrin-touched country, in the Frostfang and the Starfields.

**Veil Country.** Land in permanent twilight under the Great Veils, where Aether pools rather than burning off and shadow-leaning workings run strong.

**Exhausted Land.** Most of the south. The Three Planes stay connected and magic still works, but the environment contributes almost nothing. Practitioners run on their own reserves, recovery is slow, large ritual working becomes expensive, and natural magical phenomena are uncommon.

**Aether Wells.** Localized pockets where Aetheric Density holds, around active volcanoes, deep fault intersections, ancient forest, underground crystal systems, glacial reservoirs, surviving Wellspring sites, old nesting grounds, and places of extraordinary Soul or Fate recognition. *Almost every major Well has an owner. In the south, most owners have wings.*

**Draconic Saturation Zones.** Ground held by a powerful bloodline for centuries develops its own metaphysical climate from scale fragment, breath residue, Soul Crystal emission, shed blood, death sites and continual elemental expression. A fire territory may carry warm stone centuries after its owner dies. An ice lineage may permanently lower groundwater temperature. A storm lineage raises atmospheric charge. A Wyrm dynasty leaves stone unusually responsive to seismic speech.

**Hollow Wellsprings.** Sites gone empty, which is a different and milder thing than a Dead Zone: **old works with nothing left in them.** A skilled practitioner can still read what kind of law once ran through them, and they occasionally answer weakly during major celestial events or continental Essence surges.

**Dead Zones.** Far worse. Material, Soul and Fate relationships are fractured past any draining. Magic may fail. **Names may lose local recognition.** Spiritual projections may not return correctly. Structures behave inconsistently. Drakyssia marks the known ones on imperial flight charts and even dragons avoid several.

**Dragon Roads and Veil Roads.** Thousands of years of repeated migration have made certain southern sky corridors recognized by Fate: reduced atmospheric resistance, stabler thermals, clearer spatial orientation, occasionally shortened effective distance. **Most converge on Kra'Vorrak.** In the north, permanently veiled corridors link the undercities through the Hollow Lanterns, and a Vampire can travel the length of the empire on them without ever seeing the sun.

### How Dragons Hold Ground

Drakyssia's half of the continent, under direct, tributary, inherited or uncontested draconic control, is not a province governed by imperial officials. **A dragon may legally possess a mountain range, a valley, a watershed, a hunting territory, a cloud layer, a volcano, or several hundred miles of airspace without maintaining any settlement at all.**

| Form | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Imperial Territory** | Governed directly by Drakyssia |
| **Noble Scale Territory** | Held by recognized Dragon Houses owing allegiance to the Sovereign |
| **Tributary Territory** | Mortal kingdoms governing themselves while acknowledging supremacy through tax, military obligation, resource access or treaty |
| **Roost Territory** | Claimed primarily as nesting and hunting ground |
| **Wild Sovereignty** | Held by independent dragons powerful enough that no state has displaced them |
| **Unclaimed Depletion Zones** | Too Aether-poor, inaccessible or dangerous for anyone to govern meaningfully. **Far smaller than outsiders assume** |

*A dragon does not need to build a castle to own a mountain. Sometimes the territorial marker is simply the fact that everyone who entered without permission stopped returning.*

In the north, sovereignty runs by veil and by tithe instead. A village is Dreadvalis land if a veil hangs over it or a Tithe Hall stands in it, whether or not an imperial officer ever visits. *A settlement does not need a governor to know whose dark lies over it.*

### How Mortals Survive

Humans, elves, mixed peoples, free cities and small frontier civilizations hold what they hold under one sky or the other, five ways. **Economic necessity:** producing what an empire wants and prefers not to administer, grain kingdoms being the clearest case. **Geographic irritation:** land too wet, cold, dry, fragmented or Aether-poor to justify occupation. **Treaty recognition:** both empires take treaty law seriously where the original agreement was properly recognized by Fate. **Patronage:** a powerful House or clan has declared the place protected. **Military cost:** *a mortal kingdom has only to make conquest less useful than cooperation, since defeating either empire was never on the table.* That distinction governs most of Outer World diplomacy.

Tribute is the rent in the south, and everyone can quote it. Valecar pays in horses and mercenary companies, Ordas in silver, the Sun Coast in salt, pearl and ship timber. A family in a Valecar river town pays the town, the town pays the factor, the factor pays the House, and the House's Wyvern, passing overhead, is the receipt. North of the line the rent is the blood tithe, drawn by physicians in the Tithe Halls and paid back in rations, medicine and protection, and the Kingdom of Veyran's treaty is a schedule of harvests besides. Between those payments the mortal towns are as loud as any in the world, because they have had a long time to learn that nobody is coming to tax a street party: painted river-fortresses, cavalry fairs on the Horse Rivers, Therosan masons and Calverne merchants insulting each other across a market full of plantains and river fish, and on the Sun Coast a harbor lit in every color its wardens can manage, with an eye kept on the western sky.

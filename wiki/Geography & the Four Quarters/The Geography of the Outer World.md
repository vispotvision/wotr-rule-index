# The Geography of the Outer World

## Overview

|  |  |
|---|---|
| **Classification** | Divided supercontinent: Aether-preserved vampire north, Aether-depleted dragon south |
| **Northern power** | The Dreadvalis Empire: Common, High and Turned Vampires and their sworn mortals |
| **Southern power** | The Draconic Empire of Drakyssia: Draconic Kind, from the last Salamanders to the Bonded Mortals, under the Dragon Sovereign and the Ten Great Houses |
| **Balance** | Even. Drakyssia holds 50% of recognized territory and Dreadvalis the other 50%, directly or through tributaries |
| **Other peoples** | Human kingdoms, Elven enclaves, free cities and frontier peoples, split between the two empires |
| **Age** | Drakyssia: more than seven thousand years. Dreadvalis: a few decades |
| **Epithet** | *The Land Beneath Two Skies* |

The Outer World is a long continent split by a line nobody has drawn on a map but everyone can feel: **where the veils end and the sun begins.**

The **north** is cold country: long winters, the silver Moonlight Forest and the moonlit snow plains, glaciers, frozen tablelands and a broken coast of islands. It belongs to the **Dreadvalis Empire**, a vampire civilization only decades old, born when Malakar took the northern peoples in the Taken Winter and remade them. Its rulers live in great undercities and hang veils across the sky to keep the sun away. The north is the healthier half of the continent: its land still holds its Aether, its mines carry most of the continent's ordinary minerals, and its storehouses hold most of its food and medicine, because the people who rule it eat neither.

The **south** is fire country: volcanoes, heatwaves, red deserts, steaming rainforest and grasslands crowded with great beasts. It belongs to **Drakyssia**, the oldest empire on the continent, built around a single living Wellspring burning inside the volcano Kra'Vorrak and held together by the Ten Great Houses. The south is poorer in Aether, ancient, hungry and dangerous, but it holds the continent's gold, its dragon minerals, and the only forges hot enough to make its finest arms and armor.

Between them run the mortal and Elven kingdoms, some sworn north and some sworn south, and a frontier called the Twilight March, where cold meets heat.

Travelers sum it up in one line: *the north keeps what the south has spent, and the south forges what the north cannot.*

## I. Continental Orientation

The continent runs long from north to south and warms the whole way down. Cold and darkness hold the north; heat and light hold the south. Where they meet, the Outer World's politics happen.

| Band | Land | Held by |
|---|---|---|
| **Far North** | The Frostfang Cordillera, its glaciers and Frozen Wells; the Last Archipelago | Dreadvalis from Grimhold Deeps; the Archipelago peoples under a light tithe |
| **North** | The Moonlight Forest (Bluvest, the Seven Blood Estates, the Green Crowns), the Moonlight Snow Plains (Morvayne), the Northern Tablelands (the Ashen Courts) | The Dreadvalis Empire directly: the Veiled Provinces |
| **Northeast** | The northern Serrathan Highlands: old, worn, iron- and silver-rich uplands around Vel Sarith | Dreadvalis and its mining tributaries |
| **The Twilight March** | The frontier band. The Red Marches (Dreadvalis's southern frontier province), the Great Grass Sea, the old Five River country, and the Golden Escarpment of the southern Serrathan | Split: northern tributaries above, Drakyssian Houses and tributaries below. House Akhazar's Gate watches it from the south |
| **South-Central** | The Veloran Rainsea, the Thousand-River Basin, the Varakh Drylands | Drakyssia, its Houses, and wild dragons |
| **Southwest** | The Drakhal Spine, the Drakyssian Heartlands around Kra'Vorrak, the Wyrmdeep, the Black Scale Ranges, the Aurelian Dust Coast | Drakyssia directly; the heart of the Ten Houses |
| **Southeast** | The Eastern Sun Coast and its port cities | House Avarax and the protected ports |
| **Far South** | The Ash Wastes and the ancient flame-realm of Zyraxia, where the last Salamanders sleep | Drakyssian sacred territory |

**Reading the map.** In the north, borders follow the veils: a village is Dreadvalis land if a Dreadvalis veil hangs over it or a Tithe Hall stands in it. In the south, borders are layered: one valley can belong to a mortal king on the ground, a Wyrm below it, a Dragon House on the mountains around it, a Wyvern wing in the sky above it, and the Sovereign over all four. Along the Twilight March both systems apply at once, and a single river town may owe blood to the north and tribute to the south in the same year.

## II. A Short History of the Two Skies

**The flame-realms.** Long before any empire, the south belonged to the primordial flameborn: the Salamanders, first creation of the Titan Omnis, living in the ancient flame-realms of Zyraxia. From them came the dragons, and from the dragons every people of Draconic Kind.

**The founding of Drakyssia.** Dova'Kan and Avo'Kan bound the powerful draconic territories of the volcanic south into an alliance around the Ember Crown at Kra'Vorrak. Their nephew **Kain Drakhalis** made that alliance an empire. Over a reign of more than seven thousand years he broke rival claimants and rebel Houses, threw back invasions, and reorganized the great dragon dynasties into the **Ten Great Houses**, each bound to an Imperial Charge the empire could not survive without.

**The depletion.** Through those millennia, the south was drained. Dragon metabolisms, dragon wars, and the Wellspring extraction of vanished civilizations emptied the land of Aether. Drakyssia learned discipline (territorial rotation, recharge zones, House Ishkar's rationing), but it inherited a wounded south, and still lives inside one.

**The mortal north.** The dragons never wanted the north. It was too cold for fire-born bodies and too far from the volcanoes, so for most of Drakyssia's history the north was mortal country: hardy human kingdoms, Elven forest sanctuaries and island peoples, bound to the empire only by light frontier treaties kept by House Akhazar. Because no dragon ever lived there, its land kept its Aether.

**The Taken Winter.** A few decades before the Moto–Zettari war began in the Old World, Malakar, the First Demon, came north. Over one winter he emptied the northern kingdoms of thousands of their people and remade them in the First Thirst as the first Vampires. Then he lost interest and left them, starving, leaderless and hunting each other across the snow.

**The rise of Dreadvalis.** Out of that chaos, within a single lifetime, Vaelora Dreadvalis and the first High Vampires built an empire. They wrote feeding law and the blood tithe, raised the Great Veils, sank Bluvest and Morvayne into the ground, and absorbed or swore to tribute every northern people the Taken Winter had left standing. Some mortal kingdoms, like the refugees of the Free March of Saren who had fled dragon conquest, chose the tithe willingly.

**The two skies.** Drakyssia did not ignore the new power in the north. There were years of raids and burnings along the Grass Sea before Western Dragon diplomats and the envoys of House Akhazar sat down with Morvayne's Ledger Courts and drew the first Fate-recognized treaties across the Twilight March. Since then, the continent has been balanced: an ancient empire of fire and a young empire of night, each needing what the other has.

**Now.** At the close of the Moto–Zettari war, Shaka-Al Zettari banished Malakar into Hell. Now that prohibition has broken, and Malakar walks the Material World again. Every Vampire in the north can feel their maker in their blood. Malakarian loyalists are slipping away from the undercities; the Blood Throne has said nothing in public. In Kra'Vorrak, the Council of Ten Wings watches the north, and wonders which way the night will turn.

## III. The Aether Gradient

The defining geographical fact of the Outer World is not its climate. It is how much Aether the ground still holds, and that falls steadily from north to south.

### Why the north kept its Aether

* **No dragon ever lived there.** For seven thousand years the north was mortal country, too cold for fire-born bodies. Nothing in it drew on the land the way a dragon does.
* **Vampires do not feed on the land.** A dragon's body pulls ambient Aether constantly, the way a furnace pulls air. A Vampire takes its Essence through blood. Dreadvalis rule draws on people, not on soil, forest or river.
* **The cold preserves.** Much of the north is Vohrin-touched country: its deep cold is a Titan's power at work, not one of the Sixty, and it slows everything, depletion included. Ice has locked away pockets of Essence older than the depletion itself, the **Frozen Wells**.
* **The veils shelter it.** Under the Great Veils, Aether settles and pools instead of being burned off by heat and storm.
* **The Dreadvalis are misers.** The empire taxes careless magic, licenses every Wellspring, and builds wards to last. Its rulers do not age, and they plan accordingly.

### Why the south lost it

* **Draconic consumption.** Large dragons passively draw more ambient Aether than whole mortal towns. A region carrying hundreds of mature dragons lives under constant drain.
* **Ancient warfare.** Breath reshapes geology, boils lakes, vitrifies valleys and fractures Wellsprings. Many of the south's Dead Zones are old battlefields.
* **Wellspring extraction.** Vanished civilizations treated the southern Wellsprings as bottomless and drew on them until they stopped answering.
* **Heat and storm.** In the southern heat, Aether that might have pooled is burned off by volcanic storms and heatwaves almost as fast as it rises.

Drakyssian scholars insist that dragons alone did not wound the south, and point to the ruins of older peoples who mined Essence on an industrial scale. The fairest reading is the old one: everyone inherited a wounded south, and nearly everyone made it worse. House Ishkar exists so that it does not get worse still.

### What the gradient means

|  | North (Dreadvalis) | Twilight March | South (Drakyssia) |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Ambient Aether** | Healthy; near Old World norms in the Moonlight Forest | Uneven; good in wet years, poor in drought | Exhausted almost everywhere outside the Aether Wells and the volcanoes |
| **Casting** | Recovers at a natural pace; large rituals affordable | Depends on the season | Practitioners live on their own reserves; rituals are expensive and rationed |
| **What is worth owning** | The land itself | The crossings | The volcanoes and the last Wellsprings |

## IV. The Dreadvalis Empire — The Veiled North

The youngest great power in the Outer World and, by most measures, the most sophisticated: a vampire civilization ruled from the Blood Throne in Bluvest, built in a single lifetime by people who remembered being human. Its rulers do not eat bread or meat and have no use for herbs, so the north grows, stores and preserves food it does not need, mines metals for others, and brews medicine for bodies it does not have. All of it keeps the mortal world fed, healthy and dependent. **Dreadvalis rules by being the one who never runs out.**

### The Veils

The sun burns a Vampire to ash, so the Dreadvalis took it out of their sky. Across the heart of the empire hang the **Great Veils**: vast, slow-turning screens of darkened Aether anchored on mountaintops and on towers called **Veilspires**, worked through light-swallowing Wellsprings such as Vantabriel and Tenebra on the theory of the alchemist Arkhaven Bluvest. Under a veil, noon is deep blue twilight and the moon shows through as if it were night. The veils thin at the empire's edges and in the short summer; at the Twilight March they fail altogether.

Mortals under the veils live by lamplight, moon-lamps and the silver glow of the forest, and grow crops bred to ripen without direct sun. Northern children visiting the south for the first time often weep at the brightness.

### The Undercities

| **Morvayne** | Beneath the Moonlight Snow Plains, behind black walls and Veilspires | The city of law: the Crimson Ledger Courts, the Tithe Ministries, the physician-colleges, the Cold Granaries, and the Veiled Imperial District where foreign envoys are received |
| **Vel Sarith** | Beneath the northern Serrathan Highlands | The mining undercity, wealthiest in metal of all the northern cities |

### The Provinces

* **The Veiled Provinces.** The heart of the empire, everything under the Great Veils.
* **The Seven Blood Estates.** The noble estates of the Great Clans, ringing Bluvest in the deep Moonlight Forest; blood-systems with their own wards, reservoirs and founders' blood.
* **The Ashen Courts.** High Vampire courts on the gray stone of the Northern Tablelands, where the oldest nobility keep their distance from the throne.
* **The Red Marches.** The empire's southern frontier province along the Twilight March, held by Vein-Marshals and Turned garrisons, facing the dragons.

### The Land

**The Moonlight Forest.** The largest healthy forest left on the continent: tall silver-barked conifers and pale-leaved hardwoods that hold light like snow, so that under the veils the whole forest glows faintly blue-white. Its oldest stands, the **Moonlight Groves**, are worked by Luminalis, the light that hides, and give off a radiance mortals can farm and read by. Ambient Aether here is close to Old World levels; it is the only forest in the Outer World where wild magic still visibly returns each year. *Features:* the Silver Ocean of the canopy, the Moonlight Groves, the Still Rivers that never freeze, the Hanging Gardens of Elyr-Vael.

**The Moonlight Snow Plains.** North of the forest, open snowfields past every horizon, lit under the veils only by the moon and the snow. In winter the snow lies a man's height deep; in the short summer it thins over hardy grass and the herds move through. *Features:* the Pale Sea at its widest, the Starfields where Frozen Wells lie shallow and the snow sparkles with Essence at night, the Black Walls of Morvayne, the Lantern Road to the Archipelago.

**The Outer North.** The **Frostfang Cordillera**, all blue ice, fjords and narrow valleys, its ancient ice holding the Frozen Wells. The **Northern Tablelands**, high frozen plateaus over the forest, home to the Ashen Courts and the Veilspire anchors. The **northern Serrathan Highlands**, old worn uplands rich in iron, silver, copper and nickel. And the **Last Archipelago**, a broken coast of islands and storm seas whose sea-peoples fish, hunt whales and owe the empire a light tithe and the use of their harbors.

### The Peoples of the North

**Common Vampires** are most of the empire: its soldiers, workers, hunters and citizens, living in the middle cities and the Hollow Lanterns. **High Vampires** hold the Blood Estates, the Ashen Courts and the deeps of Bluvest. **Turned Vampires** man the upper districts and the Red Marches garrisons. Beneath them all live the northern mortals: protected citizens, tithe subjects, donor houses and labor castes. The rare **Vampire-Dragon Hybrids** have only ever been born in the borderlands where the two empires meet.

### The Blood Tithe

Every kingdom, town and household under a Dreadvalis veil or treaty pays the **blood tithe**. Tithe Wardens set each household's portion; the young, old, sick and pregnant are exempt. Blood is drawn in the Tithe Halls by physicians, never by a Vampire's mouth, and stored cold. In return, tithe-paying lands receive rations in lean years, physicians and medicine, protection under the veils, and the empire's guarantee against conquest. That is the face Dreadvalis shows the world. Inside the empire, in the old traditionalist estates, the law is uglier.

## V. Drakyssia — The Burning South

The south is the home of the dragons and of the great wild creatures, and its center is a single burning heart.

### The Ember Crown

Deep inside **Mount Kra'Vorrak**, the colossal volcano at the heart of the empire, burns the strongest living Wellspring left in the south: a current of **Pyraeon**, the ember that stays lit, power that gives up its flare and keeps its heat for a lifetime. The volcano around it has never cooled in any record. Dragons call its Wellspring Core **the Ember Crown**.

The Ember Crown is why Drakyssia exists where it does. In a south that has spent nearly all its Aether, it is a hearth that never goes out: it recharges the land around it, warms the deep reservoirs, and feeds the greatest permanent dragon civilization in the world. Every Dragon Road bends toward it, and every Dragon House measures its rank by how close its roost lies.

### Kra'Vorrak, the Imperial Sky-Capital

Built inside and around the volcano, the capital rises instead of spreading. Its lowest districts hold mortals, smiths, markets, bonded houses and foundries. Above them come landing terraces, War-Wing barracks, noble roosts, temples, command platforms and avenues built for bodies hundreds of meters long. At the summit stand the imperial structures; at the bottom of it all, in the caldera's living heart, the Ember Crown.

**Around the capital:** the **Dragon Catacombs**, where ancient dragons sleep and Bond Rituals are performed · **Avo'Kara Sanctum**, the ritual city of bond law, Crystal rites, hatchery custom and draconic music · **Dova'Ruun Fortress**, the greatest military complex in the empire · the **Ember Vaults**, treasury-forges over the magma · the **Drakhalis Throne Spire**, archive, mausoleum and monument to Kain Drakhalis.

### The Great Regions of the South

* **The Drakhal Spine.** The young volcanic chain of the southwest: snow peaks over fire, black-rock plateaus, geothermal basins. Its northern reaches hold the Wyvern country of House Dovaryn; its southern reaches hold the forge-cities of House Rhaegyr. **The Thousand Peaks** hold hundreds of roosts; **the Sky Graves** hold the fossils of dragons too large to move; **the Red Fault** runs beneath it all.
* **The Drakyssian Heartlands.** The volcanic plateau around Kra'Vorrak: calderas, lava plains, aerial basins and nesting grounds, with **the Black Scale Ranges** of the trueblood nobility and the Dragon Dens of House Maelkar in the valleys beneath them. The richest Aether in the south; no foreigner settles here without the Sovereign's leave.
* **The Wyrmdeep.** The vast underground world beneath the Spine and the Heartlands, House Ormyr's domain: Wyrm cities, freight tunnels, aquifers and geothermal works.
* **The Aurelian Dust Coast.** A narrow mineral desert between the Spine and the western sea, where it may not rain for decades. Copper, lithium salts, silver and desert glass lie exposed; the Glass Coast is black with the vitrified sand of old breath-wars.
* **The Veloran Rainsea.** A vast hot rainforest of the south-center, with rivers like inland seas. Physically magnificent and metaphysically starved; its last Aether Wells are held by dragons and guarded by House Ishkar.
* **The Thousand-River Basin.** Floodplains, swamps and floating grasslands fed by the Spine's runoff; briefly rich in Aether in flood years, nearly mundane in drought.
* **The Varakh Drylands.** Red earth, thorn forest, salt pans and rivers that vanish underground. Its heatwaves kill. Few great dragons bother with it.
* **The Ash Wastes and Zyraxia.** The burned far south, gray with old ashfall, where the ground steams underfoot, and beyond it the ancient flame-realm of Zyraxia, the last refuge of the Salamanders.

## VI. The Ten Great Houses of Drakyssia

*Vaer'Kharath, "the Ten Wings Beneath the Throne"*

Drakyssia's half of the continent does not rest on the Dragon Sovereign alone. Beneath the throne stand ten ancient dynasties whose territories, bloodlines, armies, industries and institutions are the structure of the empire. Each holds an **Imperial Charge**, a responsibility so important that its failure would endanger the whole south. Kain Drakhalis formalized the system around one principle: **No House possesses the Empire. Every House possesses something the Empire cannot afford to lose.**

| **Akhazar** · *Keepers of Roads and Foreign Crowns* | Tributaries, diplomacy, frontier law, Dragon Roads, intelligence | Akhazar Gate, the Continental Embassy-Fortress | The southern edge of the Twilight March, between Drakyssia and the old Five River country |

### What Each House Gives the Continent

* **Drakhalis** turns draconic supremacy into government. A mortal king negotiating with a Dovaryn lord knows an imperial treaty outranks him; a merchant crossing four Houses' land does not renegotiate at every border. The House holds the Right of Final Command and its own Throne Reserve.
* **Dovaryn** fields the largest Wyvern air force in the world, the **Grand Spearhost** reserved for continental war. Its wings make distance irrelevant, and they are as often the first sign of rescue after a flood or earthquake as the first sign of invasion. They also patrol the Red Marches border, where they fly half-blind under the northern veils.
* **Zhaeryn** holds the empire's gold, silver, platinum and gems as Crown Resources under stewardship, not private property. Its law is simple: *gold may be exhausted; the land may not.*
* **Avarax** imports what depleted land cannot make, and set the continent's weights, measures, caravan marks and merchant law. Most of the grain and medicine that crosses the March from Dreadvalis passes through Avarax contracts.
* **Maelkar** keeps the Dragon Dens, underground cities of incubators, hatcheries, recovery caverns and hibernation chambers, and manages dragon numbers so dragonkind does not eat its own habitat. Stealing or destroying a recognized egg is among the gravest crimes in the empire.
* **Ormyr** governs downward: Wyrm cities, freight tunnels, aquifers, seismic and volcano watch. In the Drylands an Ormyr water engineer is worth more than an army.
* **Rhaegyr** turns Zhaeryn ore into steel. Its forge-cities make dragon-forged weapons, scale armor, rider gear, siege engines, and the farm tools, bridge parts and ship fittings that millions of mortals use daily. Dreadvalis armies wear Rhaegyr steel.
* **Eredan** feeds the empire without letting dragons eat it into collapse: herds, ranches, hunting quotas, famine stores. A farmer whose cattle a dragon army requisitions must be paid. In drought years Eredan buys northern grain from the Cold Granaries, and hates that it must.
* **Ishkar** keeps what remains: Aether Wells, recharge zones, Dead Zone maps, emergency reserves. It can order even a Great House to reduce consumption. It looks north at the Frozen Wells with an envy it never says aloud.
* **Akhazar** governs everyone who is not Drakyssian: tributaries, protectorates, treaties, frontier forts, Dragon Road permissions. Since the Taken Winter it also handles the empire's most delicate relationship, with Morvayne's Ledger Courts across the March.

### How the Houses Bind Each Other

No House is self-sufficient, by design. Dovaryn fields the Wyverns, but Eredan feeds them, Rhaegyr arms their riders, Zhaeryn pays for the campaign, Ishkar decides where there is Aether enough to deploy, Ormyr keeps up the mountains beneath their roosts, Avarax supplies foreign materials, Maelkar keeps the breeding lines, and Akhazar keeps the supply roads open through tributary land. Drakhalis coordinates them all.

A House that rebels does not only fight Drakhalis. It risks frozen gold, closed trade, rationed Aether, eggs removed from imperial protection, sealed roads, canceled forge contracts, stopped food, and neighboring Houses authorized to take its land. *A dragon can be strong enough to fight everyone. That does not mean it can feed, mine, forge, breed and govern everything alone.*

Beneath the War-Wing Synod sits the **Council of Ten Wings**, one seat per House, with Drakhalis presiding, for questions that touch several Charges at once. A new gold mine near a Wellspring might bring in Zhaeryn for the ore, Ishkar for the Wellspring, Ormyr for the strata, Rhaegyr for its strategic value, Avarax for the export, Akhazar if it lies under a tributary kingdom, and Drakhalis if they cannot agree. On a depleted continent, that restraint is not idealism. It is survival.

The Houses are not friends. Zhaeryn and Avarax fight over export prices; Dovaryn demands more of the gold reserves; Eredan complains that Dovaryn's Wyverns eat too much livestock; Maelkar opposes breeding for war over health; Ishkar cuts Rhaegyr's geothermal Aether when the forges burn too hot; Ormyr accuses Zhaeryn's mines of cracking the deep strata; Akhazar objects whenever a hot-headed dragon lord endangers a treaty. The throne's purpose is not to end those instincts, but to make them serve Drakyssia before they destroy it.

*"Drakhalis gives the command. Dovaryn brings the wings. Zhaeryn brings the gold. Avarax opens the market. Maelkar guards the egg. Ormyr knows what lies below. Rhaegyr lights the forge. Eredan fills the table. Ishkar saves what magic remains. Akhazar carries the word beyond the mountain."*

Among dragons, it is shorter: ***Ten Wings. One Sky. One Throne.***

## VII. The Mortal and Elven Realms

No mortal state could survive open war with either empire. They survive by being worth more alive: as food, metal, blood, buffers or trading partners. The old Five River Kingdoms broke apart along the north-south line after the Taken Winter, and today the continent's mortal and Elven peoples are split between the two skies.

### Sworn to the North

These realms pay the blood tithe, or its treaty equivalent, and receive Dreadvalis rations, physicians and protection.

| Realm | Where | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| **The Elyran Green Crowns** | The Moonlight Forest | Elven canopy cities, groves and hidden valleys centerd on **Elyr-Vael**. The Elves conserve Essence as a way of life and the Dreadvalis respect them for it. By treaty they pay no blood; they tend the Moonlight Groves and supply spirit amber and preservation resins instead |
| **The Kingdom of Veyran** | Northern Great Grass Sea | A horse-and-grain monarchy and the breadbasket of the north; much of the Cold Granaries' grain is Veyran |
| **The Kingdom of Therosan** | The Twilight March | Engineers and masons. Therosan builders raised many of the Veilspires and still keep the frontier veils |
| **The Kingdom of Calverne** | The Twilight March | A merchant monarchy holding the road from the northern mines to the coast |
| **The Free March of Saren** | The Twilight March | Fortified towns founded by refugees from dragon conquest. They chose the tithe over the dragons, and have never said aloud whether they regret it |
| **The Archipelago Peoples** | The Last Archipelago | Island fishers, whalers and navigators who pay a light tithe for the use of their harbors |

### Sworn to the South

These realms pay tribute in coin, metal, food or soldiers, and live under the wings of Drakyssia and its Houses.

| Realm | Where | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| **The Kingdom of Avarra** | Aurelian Dust Coast | Fortified mining cities joined by underground water routes Ormyr engineers helped dig; too valuable for copper and silver to be worth conquering |
| **The Kingdom of Valecar** | Southern Twilight March | Cavalry, grain and mercenaries; its herds feed Eredan's ranches and its companies fight in Drakyssian wars for pay |
| **The Principality of Ordas** | Southern Twilight March | A small, rich realm whose silver pays for a large professional army; Zhaeryn certifies its metal |
| **The Sun Coast Ports** | Eastern Sun Coast | **Velisar**, the largest mortal port; **Aureth Bay**, a Human-Elven shipbuilding city; **Kethra**, a fortress-port under formal Drakyssian protection; **Solenmar**, a kingdom of plantations, fisheries and saltworks. All trade under Avarax charters |
| **The River Confederacies** | Thousand-River Basin | Fishing peoples who pay tribute to the Dragon Houses rather than live under direct occupation |

### The Twilight March

The frontier runs through the Great Grass Sea and the old Five River country, where the veils thin and the sun returns. On the northern side stand the Red Marches of Dreadvalis; on the southern side, House Akhazar's Gate and the Wyvern patrols of House Dovaryn. Between them, northern and southern kingdoms share borders, rivers and markets. Treaty law is thick, raids are common, and both empires keep the March just stable enough to trade across and just tense enough that neither forgets the other is there.

The Grass Sea feeds both halves of the continent: its northern half grows grain for the Cold Granaries, and its southern half raises the herds of House Eredan.

## VIII. Climate and Seasons

The continent runs from polar cold to volcanic heat, and its two empires live at either end of that scale on purpose.

| Region | The year |
|---|---|
| **Far North** (Frostfang, Archipelago) | A long dark winter, a short cool summer, glacier advance and retreat, violent coastal storms, auroras through the thin veils |
| **Veiled North** (Moonlight Forest and Plains) | Deep snow most of the year; a brief thaw when the plains green over and the herds move. Under the veils the light never changes: blue twilight by day, moonlight by night |
| **Twilight March** | Four true seasons: a warm growing season, cold rains, a dry windy autumn, frost months. The veils' edge moves with them, pushing south in winter and pulling north in summer |
| **Rainsea and River Basin** | Heavy equatorial rain, seasonal floods, crushing humidity, canopy storms |
| **Drylands and Dust Coast** | Little or no rain, coastal fog, scorching days, cold nights, multi-year droughts, and killing heatwaves |
| **The Spine and the Heartlands** | Dry high-altitude heat, volcanic storm cycles, ashfall, lightning around the summits, and the permanent warmth of the ground near the Ember Crown |
| **The Ash Wastes and Zyraxia** | Steaming ground, ash storms, and heat that never fully breaks, even at night |

**The heatwaves.** Every few summers the volcanic belt and the Drylands go through a great heat: the air shimmers, the rivers drop, the sky turns white. Dragons thrive. Southern mortals shelter in deep-cut cellars, and House Eredan opens its famine stores. Northern travelers caught in the open have died of the sun alone; for a Vampire it is not even a question.

**The Long Night.** Every few winters the far north goes weeks without real daylight, even outside the veils. The Dreadvalis treat it as a festival season: the one time the whole empire can walk the surface at once. In the south, Dovaryn doubles its patrols over the March for as long as it lasts.

## IX. Aetheric and Metaphysical Geography

The Outer World cannot be read from ordinary maps alone. Its Aether has shaped kinds of terrain almost unknown on healthier continents.

| Terrain | Where | What it is |
|---|---|---|
| **Living Land** | The veiled north, above all the Moonlight Forest | The Three Planes connect normally and the land gives back Aether at a natural rate |
| **Frozen Wells** | The Frostfang, the Starfields | Aether reservoirs locked in ancient ice in Vohrin-touched country; guarded from Grimhold Deeps |
| **Veil Country** | Under the Great Veils | Land in permanent twilight. Aether pools rather than burning off, and shadow-leaning workings run strong |
| **Exhausted Land** | Most of the south | The Planes still connect and magic still works, but the land gives almost nothing back |
| **Aether Wells** | Volcanoes, deep faults, old forests, glacial reservoirs | Pockets of high ambient Aether. Nearly every one has an owner; in the south House Ishkar licenses every one |
| **The Ember Crown** | Mount Kra'Vorrak | The strongest single Wellspring in the south: a living Pyraeon current that has never cooled |
| **Draconic Saturation Zones** | Old dragon territories | Land altered by centuries of one dragon line: stone that stays warm after a fire dragon dies, groundwater chilled by an ice lineage, air charged by a storm lineage |
| **Hollow Wellsprings** | Across the south | Wellsprings once strong, now exhausted or severed; a skilled reader can still tell what law once ran through them |
| **Dead Zones** | Old battlefields of the south | Material, Soul and Fate relationships fractured. Magic fails, Names lose recognition, structures misbehave. House Ishkar maps them; even dragons avoid several |

### The Roads of Fate

Centuries of repeated travel have made certain routes recognized by Fate, and each empire has its own.

* **Dragon Roads.** Sky corridors in the south where dragons fly with less resistance, steadier thermals and sometimes shortened distance. Nearly all converge on Kra'Vorrak, and their law belongs to House Drakhalis, their permissions to House Akhazar, and their security to House Dovaryn.
* **Veil Roads.** Corridors in the north kept permanently under veil, linking the undercities through the Hollow Lanterns. A Vampire can travel the length of the empire on them without ever seeing the sun.

### The Three Planes Under Scarcity

In the healthy north, Material, Soul and Fate interact normally. In the depleted south, Soul-based workings need more internal reserve, Attraction bonds weaken faster unless tended, old magical infrastructure loses recognition, and Wellspring harmonization needs deeper phase-locking because the land gives so little. That makes bloodlines, relics, bonded creatures and internal reserves disproportionately important in the south, and it is one more reason dragons rule there: **a dragon carries its magical environment inside it.**

## X. Power, Trade and the Balance

### What crosses the March

| The north sends south | The south sends north |
|---|---|
| Grain, preserved food and ration stores, bought by House Eredan in drought years | Gold, from House Zhaeryn's vaults |
| Medicine, physicians and surgical steel | Dragon minerals: dragonfire ore, soulfire crystal, wyrmstone, scale iron, adamant |
| Iron, silver, copper, tin, nickel, cobalt and the other common metals | House Rhaegyr's weapons, scale plate and siege engines |
| Frozen-Well crystals and Aether-rich reagents, for House Ishkar's reserves | Volcanic glass, obsidian and geothermal crystal |
| Moonlight timber, spirit amber and preservation resins | War beasts, hides and livestock from Eredan's ranges |

Almost all of it moves under **House Avarax** contracts and **House Akhazar** road permits on the southern side, and through Morvayne's **Tithe Ministries** on the northern side. The exchange runs the continent: **the north holds what civilizations need every day; the south holds what civilizations cannot replace.** A Drakyssian war campaign raises the price of grain in Bluvest; a hard winter in the north raises the price of steel in Kra'Vorrak.

### Why neither empire swallows the other

* **The sun and the cold.** Vampires cannot fight in the southern sun, and no veil can be raised over the heat of the volcanic belt. Most great dragons are creatures of fire; the Vohrin-touched cold of the north slows them, and under the veils even Dovaryn's Wyverns fly half-blind.
* **Each needs the other.** Drakyssia eats northern grain in bad years and heals with northern medicine. Dreadvalis armies are armored in Rhaegyr steel and paid in Zhaeryn gold.
* **Treaty law.** The treaties drawn across the March after the first raids are recognized by Fate, and both empires take recognized treaties seriously.
* **The March.** The mortal kingdoms between them make conquest expensive and trade easy. Neither empire wants to govern them; both want to profit from them.
* **The Houses.** No single House can march north on its own, and the Council of Ten Wings has never agreed that the north is worth the cost.

### Two kinds of sovereignty

In the south, sovereignty is layered. One point on the map can belong to several powers at once (land, depth, water, sky), each answering to a different House, and all of them to the Sovereign. A mortal king may truly say *"this valley is mine,"* and a dragon may answer, *"yes, and the mountain around it is mine,"* and both are legally right. Drakyssia is less a color painted on the map than a web laid over it.

In the north, sovereignty runs by veil and by tithe. A village is Dreadvalis if it pays the tithe or lives under a veil, whether or not an imperial officer ever visits. *A settlement does not need a governor to know whose dark lies over it.*

### The shadow over both

Malakar's return has unsettled the balance. In the north, every Vampire feels their maker's presence in their blood, the loyalists are leaving the undercities, and the Blood Throne is silent. In the south, the Council of Ten Wings has ordered House Dovaryn to double its patrols along the Red Marches and House Akhazar to learn what the Ledger Courts are not saying. Neither empire knows yet whether the thing that made the Vampires has come back for them, and whether, if it has, the March will hold.

## XI. Continental Travel

Distance in the Outer World is measured twice: once in miles, and once in how much Aether the journey costs and where you can recover it.

| Route | Where | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Veil Roads** | The north | Permanently veiled corridors between the undercities, with Hollow Lanterns as waystations. Dreadvalis license required |
| **The Lantern Road** | Morvayne to the Archipelago | The northern trade road across the Snow Plains, marked by lit cairns |
| **The March Roads** | The Twilight March | The busiest roads on the continent: grain and medicine south, gold and steel north. Toll-houses on every bridge, Akhazar permits on the southern side |
| **Dragon Roads** | The south | The fastest long-distance network on the continent, all leading to Kra'Vorrak. Drakhalis law, Akhazar permits, Dovaryn patrols |
| **The Wyrmdeep** | Under the Spine and the Heartlands | House Ormyr's freight tunnels, large enough for grown dragons; the safest way to move cargo through volcanic country |
| **The Great Verdant River** | The Rainsea | The main freight route through the southern interior |
| **Mountain Pass Roads** | The Drakhal Spine | Western crossings; nearly all need Drakyssian permission |
| **Coastal Shipping** | Both coasts | Often cheaper than inland travel, because the sea asks less of a traveler's reserves; on the Sun Coast, under Avarax charter |
| **Aether Stations** | Southern roads | Fortified stops, regulated by House Ishkar, selling stored Essence crystals, enchanted food, Soul-supporting medicine and licensed Wellspring access. In the south, Aether is infrastructure |

### Closing Geographical Phrase

*"The north keeps the dark, the cold and the grain. The south keeps the fire, the gold and the steel. Between them the March keeps the peace, for as long as both are hungry. Above the north hangs the veil; above the south, ten wings and one throne. In the volcano's heart the Ember Crown burns for a continent that has almost spent itself, and in the snow the Dreadvalis wait, patient as only the undying can be, for the rest of the world to need them."*

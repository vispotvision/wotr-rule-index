# What Things Cost

*Everyday prices and wages in a kept Accord city of the Dark Ages (one whose lamps and wards are worked by hired hands on regular rounds), at the Accord rate, western standard*

This list began as a lawsuit over a bowl of broth. In the spring of Year 702 Hob Ladleworth, who kept a cookshop in the Altherion saddlers' market, put his broth up from four copper to five, and Scarlet Oriel Thrane, a Journeyman fighter who had eaten at his counter every morning for nine years, took it as an insult to her school, flared a coat of aura the red of a boiled crab and set fire to his awning in front of the whole market. Ladleworth sued for the awning and the copper. The magistrate asked what a bowl of broth ought to cost, forty witnesses gave him forty answers, and he ordered a true list of the city's prices written out and cried in the square by a herald, which took three days and started two fights. The Arbitration Division has kept it current ever since. Thrane paid for the awning, and she paid five copper every morning for the rest of her life, loudly and under protest. Every figure below has been shouted at somebody in a public place.

> **One gold mark is twelve silver marks. One silver mark is twenty-four copper. A gold mark is therefore two hundred and eighty-eight copper**, and almost nobody below a shop's counting-room ever handles one.
> 
> A Nalūn salt bar passes for one silver mark, more in a hard winter. Imperial bronze goes by weight.

Prices are given as city prices inside a warden's round. Past the last round, and everywhere north of the arrays (standing runed workings), most of this list is paid in kind: grain, salt, cloth, fuel, board, a day's labor owed and settled at year's end. Where coin changes hands out there, it is copper, and in a copper famine it is nothing at all.

## Food, Drink and a Bed

| Item | Price | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **A loaf of bread** | 3 copper | The two-pound loaf. Dearer after a bad harvest, and the first price anyone watches |
| **A meal** | 4 to 6 copper at a cookshop; 1 silver at an inn's table | Cookshop fare is broth, bread and whatever was cheap at dawn |
| **A pint of the common drink** | 2 copper | Ale, small beer or a cup of tea at a stall, by quarter and custom |
| **A night's bed** | 3 to 4 copper in a common lodging-house; 1 to 2 silver for an inn room | The lodging-house price buys a bed in a room of twelve, and not the blanket |
| **A room for a month** | 4 to 8 silver | One room in a tenement, unfurnished, the warden's fee not included |
| **A turn at the common oven** | 1 copper the board | Your own dough or pie, stamped by the baker so that you can sue him |
| **A boil at the parish stone** | 1 copper the pot | When the warden is looking. The waist-high warm stone in the road, and the quarrel about whose turn it is comes free |
| **A water-carrier** | 1 copper for two buckets up the stair | The well itself costs a morning in the queue |

## Clothing and Light

| Item | Price | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **Boots** | 4 to 6 silver new; 1 to 2 silver worn | Working boots, nailed. A laborer buys one pair in three years |
| **A coat** | 5 to 8 silver for a working coat; 1 to 2 gold for a lined winter cloak | The cloak outlives its first owner and usually its second, and is left in wills |
| **Candles and lamp oil** | Tallow, 1 copper the dozen; lamp oil, 1 copper an evening; a beeswax taper, 1 silver | An Essence lantern is 3 to 8 silver, and the inscription fee on top, a gold and up, is the expensive part |
| **A warm stone for the night** | 2 to 6 copper | A warden-filled stone in flannel, for a sickbed or cold feet. In a hard winter it costs what its keeper says it costs |

## The Bill

| Work | Price, monthly | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **Sealed household, one room** | 3 to 5 silver | A warden's lamp at the door and a cutter's ward on the frame, by named hands under a lead seal, at a grade of work everyone knows runs below the fee. A poor stair splits one lamp and one seal among six doors and fights about it. Heat is your own hearth |
| **Sealed household, a whole house** | 8 to 12 silver | A clerk's house: the lamps at door and stair, the doorframes of every room |
| **Trade work** | 2 to 5 gold | A warden and a cutter hired to any workshop that cuts, pours or renders. Searched twice a year |

> The bill is what you pay a warden's company for somebody's hands on your door, and on a street that takes it, it is the largest line in a poor household after rent, and it comes whether the family has coin that month or not. **The snuffers (the men who strip a defaulter's lamps and scratch through his wards) come when it goes unpaid.**

## Travel and Word

| Item | Price | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **A letter** | 1 copper across a city; 2 to 4 copper across a quarter | Paid by the sender to a boy, or to whichever carrier or merchants' rider is going that way. The scrivener who writes it is 2 copper more |
| **A relay rider, for one bag** | 1 to 3 gold the hundred miles | A great house in a real hurry. About what the horses cost |
| **A beacon message** | 1 silver for twenty words, and a copper for each word after | Guild line, Guild price. Sent by clerk, from the beacon-house, where the line runs and the weather lets it. Addresses count as words |
| **A place on a carrier's wagon** | 1 copper a mile up on the load; 2 to 3 copper a mile under the cover | Haggled at the inn, whenever a wagon is going your way. A hundred miles on the load is four silver and eight or nine days of oxen, a little less where a kept road's waystones ease the wain |
| **A barge or a ferry** | 1 copper the two miles by barge; 1 copper to cross at a river stair | Downstream, and with your own food |
| **A hired hack** | 8 to 12 copper a day | From the inn, to be left at the next inn. A groom comes with it, to see that you do |
| **A news bill** | 1 copper | A dozen words, hand-copied, and sold at the corner by the crier who just read it to you. It is cheap because it is short. The Guild's day-rates copied onto a slip run 3 copper |
| **A book** | 2 to 10 gold, plain; 40 gold and up with gold in it | A flock of sheep and a year of somebody's eyes. A book costs what a book costs |

## Beasts and Carriage

| Item | Price | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **A horse** | 15 to 25 gold for a riding hack; 30 to 50 gold for a draft or cavalry horse | Fodder runs a silver a week in town |
| **An ox** | 6 to 10 gold | Eats by the road, pulls all day and has never once hurried. A carrier's wagon wants six or eight |
| **A mule** | 10 to 20 gold | Dearer than a hack on the pass roads, where nothing else lives long |
| **A cart** | 8 to 12 gold, two-wheeled; a four-wheeled wagon, 20 gold and up | Without the beast |
| **The toll on a kept road** | 1 to 2 silver a day for a heavy wain, and the hire of a twin stone on top | Paid at each road-warden's lodge, set by the load and by her opinion of you. A wain goes when it is ready, and there are no set hours |

## Arms and Bolts

| Item | Price | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **A sword** | 6 to 10 silver for a trooper's saber; 4 to 7 gold for an officer's blade | A sword that carries a working is priced on the coil (the brass assay instrument), not here |
| **A crossbow** | 5 to 10 silver | Old hand-spanned bows, sold off from armories |
| **A war crossbow** | 3 to 4 gold, steel-bowed and lever-spanned; 6 to 10 gold for a heavy windlass arbalest, where one can be had at all | Arbalests are mostly crown issue and rarely on a counter |
| **A belt-bow** | 8 to 15 gold, where one will sell at all | A rare hand crossbow spanned from the belt, from perhaps a dozen bowyers alive. The vain captain's weapon, and the paid killer's |
| **A sheaf of bolts** | 1 to 2 silver the dozen | Common bolts, about three copper apiece. Kills men |
| **Proofed bolts** | 1 silver the bolt | Guild-stamped. Beats proofed harness |
| **Enhanced bolts** | 2 to 4 silver the bolt from a hand at Apprentice standing; 1 to 2 gold the bolt from a Journeyman | Bought by the quiver of twenty, by guard companies and bounty hunters. They go stale, and a stale quiver is a loss nobody refunds |

> A guard company's full quiver of enhanced bolts costs about what a clerk earns in a month. **Ordinary infantry do not carry them.** The rampart arbalest is on no price list; a city commissions one from a Guild master, in a decade with money in it, and names it.

## The Hour and the Wonders

| Item | Price | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **A sand-glass** | 8 to 16 copper, the half-hour; 1 silver, the whole Guild hour | Blown glass and fine sand in a wooden frame. The boy who turns it is extra, and can be bribed |
| **A pocket dial** | 4 to 8 copper in boxwood; 2 to 4 silver in brass; 1 gold and up in ivory and silver | A folding sundial for the purse. Useless at night, in the rain and indoors |
| **A limner's miniature** | 2 to 8 gold | A painted likeness on vellum or ivory, three weeks' sittings. Nobody's likeness is cheap |
| **A hero's badge** | 1 copper | A lead-cast sect hero on a pin, looking like a thumb |
| **A lantern show, or a Singing Shell** | 1 copper | At the fair, when the show is in town, the shell held to your ear for about the length of a verse |
| **A carry-stone** | 30 to 60 gold before the filling | Hand-filled at a Core, by the hour, at the keeper's rate. Rarely owned, never a fuel, and it leaks a twentieth a day |

## Physicians

| Item | Price | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **A physician's visit** | 1 to 3 silver; 6 copper at a spital door | In the north and the poor quarters, the barber, for a few copper and what he knows |
| **A surgeon** | 5 to 40 gold for an operation | Anesthesia (a draft or a working that puts the patient under) included in a Guild city. The fee is quoted before the knife, and paid before the stitches |

## Wages

| Work | Pay | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **A laborer, by the day** | 12 to 24 copper | Half a silver in a slack season, a silver in a good one. Often paid part in board or kind |
| **A soldier, by the month** | 12 to 15 silver and his keep | Less stoppages for kit, which take a third of it |
| **A clerk, by the month** | 4 to 8 gold | Paid quarterly, in arrears |
| **A Guild searcher, by the month** | 10 to 18 gold | And the gifts nobody records |

### A practitioner's retainer, by Tier of Standing

| Standing | Retainer, monthly | Who pays it |
|---|---|---|
| **Initiate** | 3 to 6 gold | A workshop, a chartered company, a caravan master |
| **Apprentice** | 6 to 12 gold | A guard company, a mine, a factor house |
| **Journeyman** | 15 to 30 gold | A great house, a city watch, a shipowner |
| **Adept** | 40 to 80 gold | A crown's household, a chartered company, a money-changers' house |
| **Expert** | 100 to 250 gold | Crowns and the largest houses |
| **Master** | 400 gold and up | Crowns, by treaty as much as by purse |
| **Grandmaster and above** | Not quoted | Not retained. Negotiated with |

> A retainer is the smallest part of what a practitioner is paid. Commissions, bonds and fees for attestation run on top, and **a ranked practitioner's term of service is carried in an estate's accounts in the same column as a ship.**

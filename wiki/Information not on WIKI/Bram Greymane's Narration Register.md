# Bram Greymane's Narration Register

*Close register · a line commander's idiom*
**Narration distance: Close register.** The narration runs in Bram's own idiom, a line commander's, and its generalisations are his, not the narrator's. Italic direct thought is available and none is owed. The Kharven culture register sits under it.
**Violation caution.** Never let the narration know more of the fight than Bram can see or hear. His ignorance of his own right flank is the register; a clean overview of the battle from above his head breaks it.

---

## Vocabulary

**Words it reaches for.** Line, front, gap, breach, file, rank, rotation, whistle, count, shield, boss, axe, oblique. Exact figures, given flat: twenty-two feet, eight abreast, eighty count, eleven minutes, four hours. Anatomy named plainly when a man goes down (trapezius, cervical column, femoral, cuisse).
**Words it keeps out.** Named fear, named grief, named pride. Heroic abstraction in narration; the big words live only in what he shouts, and the narration does not repeat them.

## Sentence shape

- The senses arrive as a ladder of short openers, each on its own paragraph: "Sound first." "Then pressure." "Then the smell". "Then, last, what he could actually see".
- Long "and" chains for the grind of the hold, with the count carrying the escalation (the ninth rotation, the eleventh, the fifteenth).
- Short flat verdicts close a run: "The line closed. The breach kept producing."
- General present tense for a soldier's law: "which is the only approach that works against a locked front".

## What it notices

Sound before sight. The pressure of a mass of men as a physical fact. How many, how wide, how long. Who is on his left, by voice. What the cold is doing to the other side.

## What it never says

It never says the fight is going well or badly; he does not know. It never mourns a man in the narration; the dead are entered by name, wound and time, and the line closes over them.

---

## The rules

**1. Senses in order, sight last, and less of it as the fight goes on.**
> "Sound. Pressure. Smell. Sight, last, and less of it as the light came up and the fires went out."
**2. Ignorance is the register.** He knows only what he can see and hear, and time goes wrong inside the fight.
> "He could see thirty feet. He knew what was happening on his own left because he could hear Renard's voice in it, and he knew nothing at all about his right for a period he later worked out at eleven minutes and experienced as either a moment or an afternoon."
**3. The count carries the story.** The hold is told in rotations, not in feelings.
> "it worked for the first nine rotations and got ragged at the eleventh and by the fifteenth they were rotating men who could not lift the shield to head height"
**4. A death is entered, not mourned.** Name, wound, time, and the man beside him stepping across.
> "Egil Vald went down at the twelfth rotation with a hooked blade under the cuisse and the femoral opened and he was dead in the time it takes to fill a bucket, and he made no sound about it, and the man beside him stepped across the gap without being told."
**5. The soldier's law, in his words.** The narration may generalise, but only as a line commander would, from the ground up.
> "That was the part nobody ever tells you."

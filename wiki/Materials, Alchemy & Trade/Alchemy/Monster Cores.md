# Monster Cores

*Where the stone sits, what it fetches by the beast's own Grade and Level, how fast it dies, what the bench makes of it, what happens if you eat it, the crime that looks exactly like it, and the people who live off all of the above*

> *One stone, of the wyrm called Grandfather Rent. Received of: the rope.*
>
> *Receipt in the Verath hall's day book for the harvest of 677, in the hand of a clerk who resigned the same afternoon*

For sixty years a T7 earth wyrm called Grandfather Rent took one wagon in ten off the salt road below Verath, regular as a landlord, and the bounty on him had reached twelve hundred gold and been painted on every rim-town door when a crew called the Copper Lanterns finally killed him in the summer of 677, under a sky the color of brass, at a cost of five of their eleven. It took three days to cut him open. The stone came out of him at dusk, a fist of amber glass with a slow light turning in it, worth more than the town: twenty thousand gold by private treaty before anyone had said his name, and the name would have doubled it. The six survivors carried it up to the hunting hall behind every drum in Verath, and they were on their third keg and their second knife fight over who had got the kill, when somebody noticed they had put it down.

It was on the hall roof. It was in the right fist of the crew's porter, a Bearkin woman the size of a cider press, who had hauled their tent poles and their wounded for six years. Under the Accord's porter clause she was entered on the crew's instrument along with the rope, as *nemo*, nobody, and so she had no share in the wyrm she had bled for and no name on any roll, and people called her Nem. She stood up there against the sunset with the amber light coming through her fingers and her mouth open, holding the stone an inch from her teeth, and she told all of Verath in a voice you could hear on the salt road that she would very much like to discuss terms.

Everybody knows what comes out of a person who goes raw on a stone that size, which is to say nobody does. Most die. The rest might be anything, standing on your roof, with sixty years of a wyrm's tempering going off inside them at once. So the town stopped. For three days the Charter's reeves, the hall's master, the crew, an envoy from a Midmarch king and four factors stood in the square negotiating with a porter on a roof, while the sausage-sellers set up and the bookies gave nine to one she would swallow by Sunday. It was the hall's advocate who found the real difficulty, around noon of the second day, and he sat down on the steps and put his head in his hands. By law she could not be a party. She could not sign, could not be bound, could not receive consideration and could not be bribed, because she was equipment, and the whole Sealed Charter of the Concord was being held hostage by a length of rope.

They settled the only way the formulary allows. The safe-conduct out of Verath, which is still framed in the Waxgate law library, is made out to "the rope, and whatever it may be carrying." Nem came down off the roof with the stone in her shirt and walked north to Brokencollar with forty porters behind her, and she opened an inn there, and it has never once been robbed. The stone is still in a strongbox over the bar. Her granddaughter keeps the bar now, and bookies still quote odds on the box. A commoner holding a big monster core is the most frightening sight a lord will ever see, and this page is about why, and about the much larger number of people for whom it is merely Tuesday's wages.

## At a Glance

**What it is.** A monster's **core**, which crews and renderers call **the stone**, is in a way its Soul Crystal: the seat of the beast's nature, hardened over its life into glassy stuff in its own color. It is the lawful Crystal Draw (Class IV stock, rendered from something that carried a soul) of [[Materials, Alchemy & Trade/Alchemy/The Standing Index|The Standing Index]], and worth more the stronger the monster.

**How it looks.** A lump of colored glass with a slow movement of light inside it, warm to the hand, with fine bands in it like the rings of a tree. The color is the Family of the Wellspring the beast ran on: ember orange, teal, ochre, chlorophyll green. A dead stone is gray.

**What it costs.** A crew, usually. See below for what it pays them.

**What it becomes.** An alchemist renders it into **Essence Drafts**, about a dozen doses at the grade it serves, drunk as a course to feed tempering (the melting-down and rebuilding of the soul, layer by layer, that refines Essence and leaves the body stronger and carrying Traits). Swallowed raw it gives everything at once.

**Example.** Tib Hask, porter, goes back into a Green Sigil gallery for the T3 stone the crew's captain left in a Greater Wolf. It goes in his shirt, walnut-sized and faintly warm. The hall's clerk writes him a chit with the date and the beast's name, and a rendering house by the hall pays him four and a half gold, which is more than he has held in his life. He spends one night with the stone against his skin before he sells it, and for a week afterward he does not like dogs.

## Where It Sits

An animal has an Essence Core (the seat of its own nature) and, unlike a practitioner, no Aether Shell, the vent through which a person's soul drives Essence back out into the world. What a beast takes in from the ground and from its kills has nowhere to go. So the Essence Core lays it down, kill by kill and year by year, as hard bands around the old center, and that is the stone. **Monsters gain Levels by killing, each other included**, and an old one has done a great deal of tempering of its own, all of it still in there.

| Kind of beast | Where the stone sits | What the cutter has to know |
|---|---|---|
| **Natural beasts and the Scourge-born** | Behind the breastbone, up against the heart | The easy kind. Cut in under the last rib and reach up. A big one needs someone on the ribs with a bar |
| **Well-spawn** (creatures bred at a Well, born masked) | Under the mask's line, in the chest | The hall pays on the mask and the house pays on the stone, so a hunter may be paid twice. **Break the mask of anything bred in the Deeps or the Heart and the body comes apart into raw Aether and goes down the Well with its stone.** A deep crew that wants the core has to kill the thing the long way, mask whole, and this is how deep crews get rich or stop being deep crews |
| **Wyrms and the draconic line** | Low in the neck, in a sack of its own behind the second heart | Dig with a crew and shore the cut. The body stays hot for days, and every wyrm on the Scourge gets opened before it is cold, by whoever is nearest |
| **Serpents, sea serpents, the long ones** | Two thirds of the way down, never where the last person said | The stone shifts when the beast feeds. Cutters walk along it with a fork |
| **Hydras and the many-headed** | In the trunk, and in none of the heads | Every song about a hydra is wrong on this point |
| **Spirit Beasts** | A body may hold one, and many leave no body at all | Killing one for its stone is legal, usually fatal, and the sort of thing a whole valley will remember |
| **Elementals** | The whole thing is built around it, and it is the one solid part when the rest stops | Mind the falling lava |
| **Living Armor and other constructs** | None of their own, as a rule. Whatever was seated in them to make them go | Sometimes a stone, sometimes a Crystal. See the crime, below |
| **The Lich** | A Crystal, because it used to be a person, and the phylactery holds it | Taking it is the crime, below, and no court has yet decided how to feel about that |

**Cutting.** A stone comes out hot from the bigger beasts. A crew's cutter, generally whoever has the strongest stomach and worst sense of smell, uses a long hook and a leather sling for anything at T5 or above, because skin contact with a fresh one starts the leak (see *Keeping it*, below). Most stones go home in somebody's shirt regardless, since a shirt is what people have.

## What It Is Worth

**The stone is read off the beast.** The coil (the brass assay instrument) reads a stone and a carcass at the beast's own Grade and Level, the way it reads anybody, and that read sets the Stage the stone serves and what it fetches. **Threat Tier** is the Commission's danger rating, T1 to T9, the same scale lords post bounties by, and the whole trade uses it as shorthand for danger and price, because for most beasts the two line up. Every table on this page is written in that shorthand.

Where they part, the coil wins, because a T also counts what a beast does to a crew, a road or a coast, and none of that is in the stone. A Lamp-drinker is T6 on the board for what it does in the dark, and it comes off a floor of D-Grade stock with a hardest blow at Hamlet-bane, so it reads B at the very best and pays out a hen's egg, whatever the hunters expected for a Gold Sigil. The Leviathan is T9 for its reach, its plate and its tie to the deep, and its hardest surge is a March-bane's, so its stone reads S to SS and serves Stages VIII to X. A very large stone it is, and nobody will sell it to you.

**The price table.** Prices are raw, on the coil, at a renderer's door, for a clean stone with a hall's chit. Monsters level and evolve, so the coil prices the beast on the day it died rather than the row its kind usually sits in. An old wolf with a name can fetch the row above. The figures here are those of [[Geography & the Four Quarters/Value, Coin and Trade/What Things Cost|What Things Cost]], and the rest of the page explains them.

| Threat Tier, as the shorthand | Stage it usually serves | Size | At the door | A course poured from it, licensed | Rarity | What that means |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **T1** | I, Murmuring | A pea | 3 to 5 silver | 10 silver | Common | A laborer's week, from something that had been at the henhouse. Children hunt these, and some of them come home |
| **T2** | II, Welling | A hazelnut | 12 to 18 silver | 30 silver | Common | About a laborer's month |
| **T3** | III, Ascension | A walnut | 3 to 6 gold | 7½ gold | Uncommon | A soldier's half-year and a crew's first good night |
| **T4** | IV, Flourishing | A hen's egg | 8 to 15 gold | 22½ gold | Rare | Most of a laborer's year. A village remembers who brought it home |
| **T5** | V to VII, and the coil tells you which | A plum | 30 to 300 gold | About 68 to 600 gold | Rare | The bottom buys two horses and the top buys a clerk's four years |
| **T6** | VIII to X | A woman's fist | 800 to 8,000 gold | 1,800 to 16,200 gold | Very Rare | More than the crew's home village will see in a generation |
| **T7** | XI and XII | A big man's fist | 20,000 gold and up | Not quoted | Legendary | By private treaty, with an armed escort and several new friends |
| **T8** | XIII and XIV | Reported as a child's head | Not quoted | Not quoted | Mythic | Under the Obsidian Seal, the Accord's black classification, and never on a counter |
| **T9** | XV and XVI, on paper | Nobody has said | Not quoted | Not quoted | Unique | The Commission puts the Leviathan, the Lich and the Kraken here, and the coil reads each of them lower, the Lich at the necromancer's own Grade. If you have one of these, you are reading the wrong page |

A house pays between a third and a half of what the course will fetch poured, and a stone rendered in a hunting town sells a third under the city price, having been nearer the beast. **A famous monster's core carries a premium for the name, on top of any bounty**, and the bounty is usually the smaller part. Hide, bone and scale sell separately ([[Materials, Alchemy & Trade/Materials/Hide, Horn and Bone|Hide, Horn and Bone]]), for about as much again while fresh, falling every day from the kill.

**What the shares look like.** A crew argues about all of it. Level moves by danger overcome, so killing something stronger moves it more than a hundred weak kills, and everybody wants to have landed the blow. The money follows the instrument, which by custom gives the captain two shares, the caller and the fork one and a half, each fighter one, and the porter a wage and nothing else. The hall takes its tithe at the door. The hall's own tally of crew arguments that have ended in a killing, by Tier, is a very popular document in Verath.

## How Fast It Dies

A stone begins to die when the beast does. With no heart behind it and no ground feeding it, Essence seeps out through its skin, the outer bands first, and the color drains inward. **Small stones go fast and great stones hardly seem to go at all.** Rendering houses read the bands with a glass and a coil, and they pay by what is left.

| Threat Tier | Holds full for | Half gone by | Gray and dead by |
|---|---|---|---|
| **T1** | A day or two | A week | A month |
| **T2** | Three days | A fortnight | Two months |
| **T3** | A fortnight | Two months | A year |
| **T4** | Two months | A year | Five years |
| **T5** | A year | Five years | A generation |
| **T6** | Five years | Thirty years | A long lifetime |
| **T7** | Thirty years | Two centuries | Not yet observed |
| **T8 and T9** | Not observed to fade. Some are said to have grown | | |

By that table Nem's stone has lost a shaving off its outermost band. The bookies have not revised their odds.

**Keeping it.** Salt and cold about double the times above, which is why stones from the Cold Larder go to the hall in a sack of snow. Heat halves them. On ground of its own Family (rich with the same kind of Aether it ran on) a stone stops fading altogether and may even take a little back, so the richest vaults in the world are built on top of worked Cores (a Core, in the street's sense, being a site where one Wellspring's Aether pools, and worth more than the entire vault). On ground of an opposed Family it dies twice as fast. In a shirt, against a living person, it leaks into you.

**A stale stone is the dangerous one.** What seeps away is Essence. The beast's nature stays behind in the bands, so a half-dead stone renders fewer doses and every dose has twice the wolf in it. Stale stock dressed with a fresh color is to the core trade what spoiled Cutwater is to the apothecary, and people who buy cheap courses from a stranger's cart are the ones who end up living in the barn.

**Gray stones** have no Essence worth pouring. They still go to the ward trade by weight, where carry is all anyone is buying (carry being what a creature's life leaves in the material rendered out of it), and a child in a rim town can get a few copper for a sackful.

*Why the sizes matter, for those who ask.* A Measurewright will tell you the fade runs like a pot cooling, since what a stone holds goes as its bulk and what it loses goes as its skin. Double the width and you have eight times the stock behind four times the skin. Stronger stones are also denser, and that does the rest. This account is correct as far as it goes, and it does not explain the T8 at Sealhall that has been putting on weight.

## The Bench: Rendering

*The full chain is in [[Materials, Alchemy & Trade/Alchemy/Alchemetrica|Alchemetrica]], under The Core at the Bench. Here is what a client should understand before paying for it.*

1. **The read.** Beast, grade, carry and age, off the bands. The Appraisal skill and the coil, and a good renderer's nose. A careful house enters the beast's name and the crew's chit against the lot.
2. **The burn and the Parting.** The stone goes through the first Turnings of the bench, burned and taken and parted, and what comes off the Parting is Essence with the beast's nature still in it.
3. **The washings.** Here is the entire art. Each washing lowers the carry and loses Essence with it, and the house decides with the client how much wolf to leave in.
4. **The pour.** Bottled to the Stage it serves, with the beast's Grade on the seal, and usually the beast's name, because people like to know.

| The lot | Washings | Doses from one stone | Carry | Who buys it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Heavy** | One | About sixteen | Marked to heavy. A fast melt and a good deal of animal | Hunters, soldiers, people in a hurry, sect brawlers who want the appetite |
| **House** | Two or three | **About twelve**, the figure the price table assumes | Light to marked | Everyone with a purse. The backbone of every apothecary's year |
| **Light** | Five or more | Six to eight, at twice the price a dose | Near clean | The rich, for their children |

### The course

A course is twelve doses, one every few days up to the melt. Essence refines in the Soul Crystal, the body gains, a Trait climbs, and a neighbor who did it the slow way on a good hill gets the same lines on the window in four times the years.

```
[Course: Greater Wolf, grade III · dose 9 of 12]
[Melt: ████████░░ 80%]
[Clarity: C, rising]
[Trait: Long Breath, Level 3 → 4]
```

Doses are priced in [[Materials, Alchemy & Trade/Alchemy/The Common Drafts|The Common Drafts]]. Everything a course does shows on the window, with one exception.

### Identity Contamination

The window does not report it. The soul making the report is the soul it happened to, and it feels fine.

**Identity Contamination** is the beast's way of being coming across with its Essence, a band at a time. A Trait is a law a soul survived into existence, and carry is a law something else survived. A melt cools around whatever seeds it finds, so tempering on core stock grows parts of a person around an animal's convictions. Serious houses keep a tally of doses against each client's name, and every other house keeps a story about someone who growls.

| Doses from one kind of beast | What shows | What the family says |
|---|---|---|
| **Up to a dozen** | Dreams of running, or of the dark under a gallery. A new taste in meat | Growing boy |
| **Two or three courses** | Habits. Sleeps at noon, circles a room before sitting, cannot pass a carcass without looking | Takes after his uncle |
| **Five or more** | Appetite and reflex. Reads a dinner table for who is weakest. Bites, once, and apologizes | We do not take him to the Harvest Supper |
| **Past that** | An Essence Core that can no longer say which convictions are its own. They remain very sure, about things | Nobody has asked the family |

Different beasts bring different weather, and buyers shop accordingly.

| Stock | What comes with it | Who wants it |
|---|---|---|
| **Greater Wolf** | The pack. Fierce loyalty, hatred of being alone, a nose for who has gone lame | Sworn companies, whose captains prefer it |
| **Hellhound, Manticore** | Heat, spite, enjoyment | Duelists, and people who are about to lose them as friends |
| **Mourning Pike** | Patience and long still hours. Other people's memories | Assassins, widows |
| **Tolling Crab** | A love of one exact pitch, and a hard shell in every sense | Sealwrights, who deny it |
| **Glarewing** | Quick, bright, cannot abide a closed room | Couriers, tournament fliers |
| **Lamp-drinker** | Dislike of light. A gentle wish that you would put that out | Nobody admits to it |
| **A wyrm** | Hoarding, long sleep, the conviction that all of this is yours | Great houses, who see no problem |

Mixed courses do not cancel. They argue, and the drinker gets to listen.

## Going Raw

A stone can be swallowed or broken raw, with no alchemist and no fee. The trade's phrase is **going raw**, and it is used mostly of the dead.

Everything arrives at once: every band the beast laid down in its life, all the Essence and all of the nature, with no Parting and no washing, into a Soul Crystal that was built to hold a good deal less. The Crystal goes to the melt on the spot. If the stone is near the eater's own Stage the odds are merely bad. If it is several Stages above, the Shell scalds, the Crystal cracks along whatever it was feeling at the time, and what comes out is built on that.

| Of a hundred, by the Verath hall's count over two centuries | What happened |
|---|---|
| **About seventy** | Dead inside the hour. Fire in the chest, blood from every opening, a coat of the beast's color roaring out of them and then going out |
| **About twenty** | Alive and wrong. Some are the beast, more or less, in a person's body, and some are a person who can feel it pacing. The body may change to suit |
| **About ten** | Whole, or near enough, several Stages up on one bad afternoon |

**The wrong ones** are the ones crews tell stories about. Hob Wendle of Rimward ate a T6 stone off a tunneling thing at Nine Wells in 640, and came up three Stages with the strength of a siege ram and the need to dig. He dug out through the floor of his own house that night and through two neighbors' cellars by morning. He was still recognizably Hob, he kept apologizing, and his voice could be heard coming up through the well for most of a year.

**The whole ones** are why it keeps happening. Sister Wenlyn Oake, a kitchen servant at a sect in the Violet Peaks, swallowed a T5 stone in 655 with the elders' dogs at the door, and walked out through them at Stage VI with a Level the swallow itself had paid for, since nothing she had ever faced was as dangerous as that stone, a Skill column with nothing in it but Soup, and a coat in a blazing kingfisher blue. It takes a bold person to hunt somebody like that, and the sect did, for eleven years. A raw climb has all the gaps of a bought one, with no Release until it is found and mastered by fighting, and the hunters come quickly.

**Why lords care.** A raw stone in a commoner's fist is the one thing a lord's whole apparatus of grain, land, debt and Draft supply has no hold on, and it costs nothing to threaten. Most realms therefore hang people for holding an unrendered stone above T4 without a hall's chit, and lords have burned villages on the rumor of one. Nem's terms have been studied by more peasants than the catechism.

## The Crime

**A Crystal cut from a dead practitioner makes the strongest Draft of all.** It is the worst crime in every realm, and the most profitable, and it is why Class IV has never balanced on the Index. No monster ever tempered as deliberately as a person who spent a lifetime at it. A beast lays down what happens to it, while a practitioner chooses, for decades, what to become, and that is what the buyer gets.

**The Dead Man's Cordial**, as the trade says when it is sure of the room, runs several times the price of a core lot of the same grade. It tempers faster than anything alive. It also carries a person, identity-shaped and complete, and a drinker may wake with a stranger's way of seeing, a grudge against someone they have never met, or a very good left-hand parry and a language nobody at home can place. *Buyers report that it is good company.*

**How it passes.** A human Crystal has three parts, the Essence Core, the Aether Shell and the Attraction Layer, where a beast's stone has only the first. So the first job is done by a **dresser**, who shaves away what remains of the Shell and Layer, buffs the surface, and stains in some bands. After that it reads the same on the coil. A rendering house can still spot one on the bench if it cares to look, and the two-instrument comparison described in The Standing Index would catch it on the shelf: the coil's Holding and the fork's density will not reconcile. Most benches own one instrument. Then there is a chit that says wyvern, from a factor three towns away, and a house that has decided not to ask.

**Where they come from.** Undeclared wars, first of all, since a declared one has a quartermaster counting. Battlefields after dark. Hanged men whose families cannot pay a guard. Duelists' graves. Sect elders, sometimes, from their own students. Famous fighters above all, because a Crystal with a name on it sells for what a kingdom sells for, and every generation has its scandal in which a great family's fortunes improved abruptly after a war.

**The wake.** This crime is why a practitioner's body is never left alone. Across most of the Inner World the kin sit an **armed wake**, weapons across their knees and lamps lit, in the dead one's school colors, until the Crystal has been broken by a relative's hand or the pyre has burned down, and the wake is often the best party the family has thrown in years. A Moto house speaks to its dead and keeps them under guard while it does. Marrowkin enter the Crystal in the Tally. Kharven families have been known to sell theirs quietly and hold the wake anyway. A person who dies with no one to sit up with them is what the night-cutters look for.

**The law.** Every realm hangs for it, and most hang the bench and the buyer too on paper. In practice the cutter hangs. *The three old warrants for rendering the human dead (see [[Materials, Alchemy & Trade/Alchemy/The Standing Index|The Standing Index]]) reach the body and stop at the Crystal, and a surprising number of great houses have had that explained to them at length, under seal, without consequence.*

**Penning**, its cousin, is lawful in some places and ought to worry you more. Monsters gain Levels by killing. So you can raise a line in a pit, feed it things to kill, stronger each season, and cut it at the top of its growth for a far better stone than its row would ever fetch. The Hundred Crowns have kings who do this. Most of what gets fed to the pit is goats. The Accord has struck three charters over the rest of it, and the fourth is pending. *Every pen in the record has ended the same way, and the only variable is how far the town was from the pit.*

## Who Lives Off It

| Who | What they do | What the street calls them |
|---|---|---|
| **Crews** | Kill it and cut it. See [[The Bestiary/Dungeons\|Dungeons]] | Whatever they are calling themselves this week |
| **The stone-walker** | The steady one who walks the core home in a shirt or a sling while the mask-runner sprints for the hall | Slow Money |
| **The hall clerk** | Writes the chit: date, ground, beast's name, the Sigil of whoever engaged it. Everything lawful starts here | Ink |
| **Core-boys** | Children and old men on the Mouth, with spades, after T1 and T2 stones. Lawful, and not on anybody's roll | Marble-pickers |
| **Factors** | Wait at the top of the stair with a very good dinner and a purse. They buy now for what the house will pay in a month. A merchant's title caps the Rarity he may lawfully deal, so a T6 stone needs a Master's token or a second signature, and a T7 needs a Grandmaster | Uncle |
| **Renderers** | Run the house by the hall. Any town with a dungeon on it has one, with a chimney of a strange color | The Kitchen |
| **Alchemists of the great houses** | Pour the high lots under seal. A stone at T6 or above generally passes straight into a vault | The family physician |
| **Tally-men** | Keep each client's dose count, as a sworn clerk at the serious houses. A good one will refuse to sell to you | Nanny |
| **Dressers** | Stain stale stone and shave dead men's Crystals. The best of them are also the best-paid forensic witnesses | The Barber |
| **Paste men** | Pour colored glass with a skin of something that fools a weak coil, and sell it in bad light to people in a hurry. A good forger beats a weak appraiser | Bottle-boys |
| **Night-cutters** | See above | Nothing, in company |
| **Stone-reeves** | A lord's officer in a hunting town, there to take the lord's refusal first and the lord's tithe after | Thief, and it is usually said in a friendly way |
| **Quartermasters** | Monster cores and bought Stages are war materiel. A war makes a stone's price double, and a lord will strip a county's halls before he marches, because a Draft in a champion is worth a company | Sir |

**The money, in one line.** The great houses hoard the top stones, poured and unpoured, in vaults on their own ground; the middle of the trade feeds the fighters and sect disciples of every realm; and the bottom feeds a boy with a spade. The whole of the class war of the age can be read off the top three rows of the price table. *I know of one inn in Brokencollar that has been reading it for forty years.*

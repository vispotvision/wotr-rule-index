*The common tags of the field kit, as cut by licensed hands, copied by unlicensed ones and guarded by the schools · prices in Accord marks*

> A wall cut by an Engraver holds for three hundred years. A tag holds for three weeks and fires once, and in a fight that is the better bargain. Twelve tags make up most of what is carried into battle.

---

## What it is

A **tag** is Runecraft (the cutting: a working written into a material so that it holds without its maker) cut fast onto **paper, leather or slate**, carried in a sleeve, a belt or a boot, and fired in a single beat. A wall needs a season because it has to stand for centuries. A tag needs an hour because it only has to stand until it is used, and its last line is its own burning: when the tag is spent the material is ash, and the working ends with it.

**Only practitioners can fire a tag.** The trigger is a touch of the user's own Essence (the stuff a working is made of) through a woken Soul Crystal (the seat of a practitioner's power), with a word or a hand sign. An unwoken hand holding a tag is holding paper.

A tag is also why low-Stage fighters love them. Below Refraction (Stage VII, the seventh rung of the sixteen-step ladder of power) every working needs something outside the body to hold its shape: a voice, a hand, ink or blood. **A tag is that ink, already written.** The user supplies the trigger and the tag supplies the rest.

**Every tag runs on one of three fuels**, and the tag decides which:

| Fuel | Where the power comes from | What it means in a fight |
|---|---|---|
| **Ambient Aether** | The magic in the air around it, at the room's Density (how much Aether a place holds) | Cheap and never stale. **Fails in dead ground** (a district cut off from its supply, or a place no Wellspring reaches) and weakens anywhere thin |
| **The user's reserve** | The Essence the person firing it can spend before running dry | As strong as the user, up to the maker's Stage. **It costs the user**, and the more they feed it the longer it holds |
| **The maker's stored charge** | Reserve the cutter poured into the tag when it was made | Works anywhere and for anyone who can fire it. **Goes stale**: strongest the day it is cut, weaker each week, dead in a season |

A tag can never work above its maker's Stage. A reserve-fed tag works at whichever is lower, the maker's Stage or the user's.

## How it looks

Fine lines cut or burned into the material, sometimes inked, always in a fixed order the eye can follow if it knows the school. Paper is cheap, quick and dies in the rain. Leather lasts months and survives a soaking. Slate is heavy, lasts a year, and shatters when it fires.

**As it fires, the lines light up in an aura colour**: the maker's own colour for an ambient or stored tag, **the user's colour for a reserve tag**. A trained eye reads who is paying for the working from the colour alone. Then the material chars from the edges inward, with the smell of hot paper or scorched hide, and each kind of tag has its own sound: the flash tag's crack, the ward tag's glassy ring, the blast tag's rising whine in the last second before it goes.

A tag is slapped onto a surface or a body, laid in advance, or wrapped round a knife and thrown.

## What it costs

**Coin.** Tags compete with enhanced shot (bullets carrying a working) and are priced against it, by the maker's Stage:

| Maker's Stage | Price, one tag | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **I** · Murmuring | 4 to 8 copper | **Penny tags.** One in three fails |
| **II** · Welling | 2 to 4 silver | The field standard; about the price of one enhanced round |
| **III** · Ascension | 1 to 2 gold | Bought singly, by people who expect to live because of it |
| **IV** · Flourishing | 3 to 6 gold | Officers', bounty hunters' and sect disciples' kit |
| **V** · Splintering | 9 to 18 gold | Rarely on a counter |
| **VI and up** | 25 gold and up | Not sold. Issued, inherited, or stolen |

**Failure.** Cheap tags fail, and they fail three ways. Most often the tag **fizzles**: the lines glow and nothing comes. Sometimes it **fires wrong**: the ward comes up a hand's breadth to the left, the smoke comes out as sparks. Rarely, and worst, **it does not stop**: the closing line was cut badly, so the working runs on after the tag should be ash, a flash tag that keeps flashing, a kindling tag that burns until the building does.

**The licence.** Licensed tags carry the cutter's mark (a cutter's mark is a signature and a warranty at once) and an excise stamp. Cutting without a licence is a crime the Accord enforces with real violence, and the black market runs regardless.

## How it is beaten

- **Wet it.** A soaked paper tag swells and its lines blur. Leather and slate shrug it off.
- **Cut the line.** A tag torn through its lines will not fire, or fires wrong. A blade across a stuck tag is the fastest answer to a Binding or a Seal.
- **Take the fight to dead ground.** Ambient tags need Aether in the air.
- **Read the colour.** A reserve tag tells you who is feeding it. Hit them, and the tag starves.
- **Wait it out.** A stored-charge tag weakens by the week. An old tag from a dead cutter is a gamble.
- **Watch the hand.** Slapping, laying or throwing a tag takes a beat, and the hand that goes to the sleeve is a tell.

## Example

Orla Venn, a Stage III bounty hunter, walks into a warehouse after a forger. She lays a Tripwire Tag across the back door, keeps a Flash Tag in her left sleeve and a Binding Tag in her right. The forger runs, the Tripwire shrieks, and she throws the Flash Tag wrapped round a knife; it cracks white in his face. She closes and slaps the Binding Tag on his thigh, and the lines light up in her own colour as she feeds it. The forger's partner sees the colour, knows who is paying, and goes for Orla instead of the tag. She has to choose between holding the leg and defending her throat, and the tag goes dark the moment she lets go.

## Who cuts them

- **Licensed cutters.** Accord-licensed hands working under a master's mark, selling over a counter with an excise stamp. Most tags on the main come from them, and the Accord guards the monopoly hard.
- **The black market.** Unlicensed cutters, failed apprentices and forgers of other men's marks. Cheaper, unwarranted, and the source of most tags that do not stop.
- **The sects.** The schools and great houses of the East keep their best tag forms as secrets, taught by rank. A disciple who leaves with a Binding or an Anchor form in their hands is hunted for what they know.

> In the north almost nobody cuts. What tags the north carries come from Accord quartermasters, and a northern soldier pays for them in kind.

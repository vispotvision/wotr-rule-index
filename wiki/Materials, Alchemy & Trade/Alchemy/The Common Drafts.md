*The standard catalog of the Draft trade, as kept by quartermasters, licensed apothecaries and the excise office · prices in Accord marks*

> Twenty-two Drafts account for most of what is poured, sold, smuggled and drunk across the four quarters, and the Essence Drafts rendered from monster cores, set out after them, account for most of the money. This is the list every counter-clerk learns first, every smuggler forges first, and every practitioner should be able to name by the color of the seal.

---

By dusk Goody Nettlebed of Skerrow, seventy-one and lame in one hip, who had used her Soul Crystal all her life to feel weather in her knuckles, had a coat of teal fire spitting sparks off both arms and was carrying a herring barrel over the chapel roof for a wager. The net-menders went along the harbor wall faster than a horse will go on cobbles, blood pouring from their noses, howling with joy. The whole strand burned like a fairground in the auras of people who had never in their lives been bright, while two hundred and sixty unwoken neighbors held the lanterns and shouted advice, and when six excise riders came down the cliff road to secure the Accord's property, they went into the harbor, and after them, in a spirit of thoroughness, their horses. A steersman named Wat Cobbold, four doses of Windglass to the good, ran off the end of the mole at full stretch, certain he was gaining on the horizon. The next tide brought him back.

All of it had come ashore that morning. The *Dorcas Merry* had gone onto the Skerrow Teeth in the first gale of autumn with forty cases of fight stock stowed under a cargo of onions and no excise stamp on any of it: Vein Cordial, Beastheart Serum and Windglass Salts, poured at grade VI for somebody's sect in the East, twenty-four vials to the case, close on three and a half thousand gold at the plain band, and by noon the cases were rolling in the surf among the onions. Skerrow was three hundred fishing people, forty or so of them woken (their Soul Crystals opened), none above Welling and most of them at Murmuring and proud of it, and nobody on that strand had ever read a numeral off a seal. They could read red wax, and they knew red wax was dear.

The excise returned at noon the following day with twelve men and a handcart, and arrested the entire woken population of Skerrow lying down. Twenty-six of the forty had torn a hamstring. Every one of them had a scalded Shell, five had cracked their Crystals, and two of the five have not held a working since. Goody Nettlebed's hands still shake, and she will tell you for the price of a drink that she would do it again. The minstrels say it was cider. Every counter-clerk on that coast tells the true version to anyone who asks why a seal carries a number, and the catalog below is the rest of what they would say if you stayed for it.

## What it is

A **Draft** (a working poured into a substance, so that it can be carried and drunk) is the one form of magic a stranger can sell you. Every Draft is **graded by the Stage it serves** (the Stage is a practitioner's rung on the sixteen-step ladder of power). A Draft at your own grade does what the label says. **A Draft below your Stage does little**, because a stronger body simply outclasses it. **A Draft above your Stage is dangerous** (a *scald*, on any fight street): it pours more into the Shell (the Aether Shell, the skin of power around the Soul Crystal) than the Shell is built to carry, and the excess comes out as burns, nosebleeds, a scalded aura, or a crack in the Crystal itself.

The commonest Drafts are poured at several grades. A grade-II Windglass and a grade-VII Windglass share a name, a recipe and a crash, and almost nothing else.

## How it looks

Glass or stoneware, a wax seal, an excise stamp, and sediment at the bottom that a careful buyer tilts to the light. A licensed Draft carries its grade in a Roman numeral pressed into the seal; a fight vial carries it on the cork as well, so it can be read by touch in the dark.

**A fight Draft takes a beat to drink**, which is why fighters call it a *swallow* and why Stage brackets bar it by rule and search belts at the rope. Thumb off the cork, swallow, and the half-second the body takes to answer. In a duel that beat is an opening, and every trained fighter watches for the hand going to the belt. What follows shows: a refill brightens a dimming aura back toward its full color, a speed Draft leaves the drinker's footing light and quick, a strength Draft flushes the skin and swells the veins in the neck.

## What it costs

**Coin.** Prices climb roughly threefold with each Stage a Draft serves, because each grade holds roughly three times the Essence (the stuff a working is made of) and the rendering loses more of it at every step:

|---|---|---|
| **I** | Murmuring | 4 copper |
| **II** | Welling | 12 copper |
| **III** | Ascension | 1½ silver |
| **IV** | Flourishing | 4½ silver |
| **V** | Splintering | 13½ silver |
| **VI** | Glory | about 3½ gold |
| **VII** | Refraction | about 10 gold |
| **VIII** | Transcendence | about 30 gold |
| **IX** | Invocation | about 90 gold |
| **X** | Realization | about 270 gold |

> Twenty-four copper make a silver mark and twelve silver marks a gold. A laborer earns a silver mark in a good day. **A grade-X Draft costs a clerk four years of wages**, and the households that keep a case of them keep it in a vault.

**The crash.** Every fight Draft leaves a crash once its window closes, and the crash is the real price. Two or three in one fight is ordinary. Five is how veterans describe the worst week of their lives.

**The stack.** A strength or speed Draft is one layer of boost, and boosts from every source stack: Drafts, Trait (a standing law carried in the soul) surges, release forms (a practitioner's named, staged power-up), allies' help. **The stack tops out at two Grades above what the body can sustain**, and every layer a fighter adds raises the chance of a Crystal fracture (a crack in the Soul Crystal, which is literally the soul).

**Provenance.** Every Draft came from somewhere, and its source type says where the risk lives:

| Source type | Where it comes from | What it risks |
|---|---|---|
| **Creature-Sourced** | Rendered from magical creatures, monster cores above all, and at the top of the trade, against every law there is, from people | The strongest, and it carries the **identity of what it was made from** (Identity Contamination) |
| **Material-Sourced** | High-density minerals and salts taken off a Wellspring vein (a Wellspring is one of the sixty alchemical and spiritual transformations, the governing idea that shapes how a soul uses Aether, and each is a type of Aether too; a vein is the channel that feeds a place where one type pools) | Clean but weaker |
| **Environmental** | Matter steeped in a Wellspring field until it took the set | Gentle and slow; close to no risk |
| **Synthetic** | Blended at the bench from several sources | The least predictable; the parts can react inside the drinker |

## How it is beaten

- **Take the beat.** A fighter who stops to drink has stopped. Hit the hand, the vial or the throat.
- **Outlast the window.** Every fight Draft runs out. Stay alive past it and fight the crash instead of the Draft.
- **Read the grade.** A drinker who has gone above their own grade bleeds from the nose and runs hot within a minute. They will burn out without help.
- **Read the source.** A Creature-Sourced Draft brings its creature's appetites with it, and those appetites can be baited.
- **Break the supply.** Drafts are bought. A fighter who has drunk their belt dry is a fighter at half strength.

## Example

Hesk Tarrow, a Stage III caravan guard, holds a bridge against two raiders. Halfway through he is down to a quarter of his reserve (the Essence he can spend before running dry), his aura thinning to a flicker. He steps back, takes the beat, and drinks a grade-III Vein Cordial; the light comes back up his arms, and the raider who saw the hand go to the belt has already closed the gap and cut his forearm. Hesk wins the bridge. That night he cannot recover a drop on his own, his forearm is still open, and a grade-III Willow Gray costs a silver and a half. He buys the four-copper grade I instead, which does almost nothing for a Stage III body, from a stall whose seal he did not check.

## Essence Drafts and the bought Stage

Everything above works on the belt. This part works on the ladder, and it is why a rich man's son is stronger than you at nineteen.

**Tempering** melts the Soul Crystal down and rebuilds each layer. Every pass refines the Essence it holds and makes that Essence stronger, and the body comes out of it gaining Traits. A practitioner can do this on rich ground for years, or by hard fighting, or at speed on a course of **Essence Drafts**, and most of those are rendered from **monster cores**. A monster's core is in a way its Soul Crystal, worth more the stronger the monster, and a hall's whole year turns on what its crews bring up (the prices are in [[Materials, Alchemy & Trade/Alchemy/Monster Cores|Monster Cores]]). A course runs a dose every few days up to the melt. Skills and Trait levels climb on the window (the soul reporting its numbers to its owner, unseen by anyone else) afterward, and a poor neighbor who did it on the hill gets the same lines in four times the years.

|---|---|---|---|---|---|

The bottle carries the beast's Grade on the seal | Poured I to X, by the strength of the core | **Identity Contamination.** The beast's appetites and habits come with it, a little per dose, and an Essence Core that takes in enough can no longer say which convictions are its own. Serious houses keep a tally of doses, and every other house keeps a story about someone who growls | Five times the band a dose: 20 copper at I, 22½ silver at IV, 50 gold at VII, 450 gold at IX, 1,350 gold at X | Creature-Sourced |

**The raw core.** A core can be swallowed or broken raw, with no alchemist's fee, and it gives everything at once. Most who try it die, and a good share of the rest come out wrong. The few who come out whole are why it keeps happening, and a commoner standing in a lord's county with a big core in his fist and nothing left to lose is the most frightening sight that lord will ever see.

### Stock, layer and lens

A buyer who asks what grade a bottle is has asked half the question. Tempering melts each layer of the soul and rebuilds it around the Wellspring that soul has based itself on (its **lens**, as [[The Magic System/The Soul and the Climb/The Three Paths Routing, Recognition and the Gate|The Three Paths]] has it), so two more things decide what a course does: what the stock ran on, and which layer it reaches.

**The lens: matching stock tempers cleanly.** Every stone ran on a Wellspring, and an honest seal says which. A Pyreveil duelist on a course of Pyreveil stock melts cleanly, because what goes in already agrees with what her soul is built around. Foreign stock tempers just as far and leaves more behind, adding carry over and above what the washings left: the same Pyreveil duelist on a Greater Wolf course (a Transference stone) comes out as strong, and rather fonder of her company than she was. Stock from one of her own lens's Contraries fights the melt all the way down, and a serious house will refuse to pour it for her. This is why a sworn company buys wolf and a sect with a house Wellspring keeps its own hunting ground.

**The layer: Sulfur, Salt and Mercury.** The bench's three principles (see [[Materials, Alchemy & Trade/Alchemy/Alchemetrica#The Tria Prima|Alchemetrica]]) answer to the three layers, and the Parting can split a lot along them.

| Stock | Principle | Reaches | Where it comes from | Who wants it |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Core stock** | Sulfur | The Essence Core: identity, Traits, reserve | Any monster's stone, which is Core and nothing else. A plain course is this | Everyone, and Spirit Path fighters above all |
| **Salt fraction** | Salt | The Aether Shell: what it can take in and drive out | The white ash of a stone's Parting, poured apart at extra cost, and **Vein Essence**, which is mineral Salt from end to end and so has no stranger in it | Body Path fighters, and anyone whose Shell has fallen behind a run of plain courses |
| **Shaved Layer** | Mercury | The Attraction Layer | No lawful source at all. A beast's Layer never thickens and is not in the stone, so the only Layer on any bench was shaved off a dead practitioner's Crystal by a dresser | The black market, and Alchemarchs at the end of their lives |

**The black-market Fate reagent.** Dressers sell the shavings in twists of waxed paper, and the street calls them **barber's curl**. A curl moves no rung, because Thread Class is not for sale: it climbs by blood or blessing and decades. What it gives is a season of having more share of fate than you were born with, in which your vows bite harder, your luck goes strange and strangers keep meeting you. It also carries the dead owner's bonds, and a good many drinkers have been surprised by a creditor. It is the worst of the Class IV crime in the smallest parcel, and it is the only stock from which a [[Materials, Alchemy & Trade/Alchemy/Master Works#The Fate Essence Draft|Fate Essence Draft]] has ever been poured.

**What the bottle does not buy.** A course feeds the melt and raises a ceiling. Each of the three [[The Magic System/The Soul and the Climb/Classes and the Three Ladders|class ladders]] has its top set by the Stage, and only practice moves a person up to it. So the heir who has drunk his way to Stage VII stands well below his own ceiling on every ladder, with a Soul Crystal Tier still waiting for his Level to fill it. He has no Path, either, since a Path is the layer a soul has learned to lean on and nothing about learning to lean comes in a bottle. He is the person on the sheet with the very high numbers, about to find out what they are for.

### Buying a Stage

A **breakthrough Draft** supplies the Catalyst in liquid form. The Crystal goes to the melt on schedule and the Stage is real, on every instrument, for good. **A bought Stage opens the door and no more.** It has no Release (the named, staged power-up a fighter calls aloud) until one is found and mastered by fighting, and no Level comes with it, since Level moves by danger overcome. Sects teach what money cannot buy, which is why dukes' sons still scrub floors at mountain gates. On the sheet it reads as high ceilings and a thin skill column, and anyone who has worked a bracket can see it from the rope.

A blessing can turn the same key, and a great giver spends almost nothing to do it.

### The two at the top

**The Dead Man's Cordial.** A Crystal cut from a dead practitioner and rendered makes the strongest Draft of all. It sells as a monster-core lot at several times the price, its buyers do not ask, and it is the worst crime in every realm and the most profitable. A clerk who says the name at a counter is either a fool or an Enforcement man.

**The Fate Essence Draft.** A Master Work, which is to say a piece that does what its Grade and material should not be able to, made once at the end of a lifetime, always Unique or Mythic, and named. It bends the Plane of Fate (the control of Attraction Force and Obsession Force, and of what the odds are allowed to do) for whoever drinks it, it is extremely hard to make, and there is no price. Its account is in [[Materials, Alchemy & Trade/Alchemy/Alchemetrica|Alchemetrica]] and [[Materials, Alchemy & Trade/Alchemy/Master Works|Master Works]].

## Counterfeits

**Counterfeits are common and sometimes deadly.** Chalkwater is the cheapest. Worse are the real Drafts sold a grade high (a grade-II Cordial with a grade-IV seal, which does too little) and the real Drafts sold a grade low (which does too much and puts the buyer in the street with a scalded Shell). Spoiled stock relabeled with a fresh date is the most common killer of all.

> **Check the seal, tilt the sediment, smell the cork, and buy from someone you can find again.** Licensed stock carries an excise stamp and the pourer's mark. Neither proves the bottle. Both prove there is a ledger, and a ledger is somebody to hang.

## Where they are sold

- **The kept cities** (those whose lamps, wards and warm stones are kept by hired crafts) buy anything: corner apothecaries for the everyday Drafts, licensed houses for the fight and advancement stock, and smugglers in every port for the rest. The trade is licensed, taxed at the gate and smuggled around the tax.
- **The hunting towns** render where the crews come up. Any town with a dungeon on it has a rendering house by the hall, and an Essence Draft bought there costs a third less and has been nearer the beast.
- **The north** trades in kind. A belt of Drafts travels where a wall cannot, so the north ranks the pouring above the cutting, and a vial is paid for in salt, meat, fuel or a season's labor owed.
- **The East** buys through its sects (the schools and great houses). A disciple draws Drafts against rank and duty; an outsider buys through a sect's factor, at a sect's price, or not at all.

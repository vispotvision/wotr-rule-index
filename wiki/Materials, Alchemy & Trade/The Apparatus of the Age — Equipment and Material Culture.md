# The Apparatus of the Age — Equipment and Material Culture

*A Register of Equipment, Technology, and Material Culture Across the Four Quarters*
*Compiled for the Logistics and Supply Division · appendix to The Works and Days, The Bearing and the Holding, and The Four Ceilings*

---

## The Common Table

**The majority of objects in the four quarters contain no Essence, hold no inscription, and are made from Bearing-grade material** — iron, wood, stone, leather, cloth, clay.
> *They are listed first because they are the most numerous, the most important, and the most likely to appear in a scene that does not involve practitioners killing each other.*

### Light

**The Tallow Candle** · The standard light of the four quarters off the main, **and the object that appears in more scenes than any weapon.** Rendered from animal fat, sold by weight. **Burns smoky, smells of the animal it came from, and provides enough light to eat by but not to read by unless you hold it close enough to singe the page.**
> *A household that is not wealthy burns tallow. A household that is wealthy burns beeswax — which is why the smell of a room tells you immediately how much money is in it.*
**The Oil Lamp** · Fish oil on the coast, seed oil inland, whale oil where the routes reach. **The standard lighting in any workshop where the work requires both hands.** *A smith works by lamp. An Engraver works by lamp and by loupe. An innkeeper works by whatever the guests have not yet stolen.*
> **The Essence Lantern** · The first object here that contains a working. A Clearbeam beryl lens over a Luminalis-inscribed Aethersteel plate — **steady, cool, odourless, no flicker, no smoke, no fuel.**
>
> It draws on ambient density, **which means it works indefinitely in a strong district and dims to nothing in a Withering-era one.**
>
> **That is the older pattern and it is now the rural one.** In any networked city the lantern is plumbed to the house main, draws metered supply, and does not care what the ambient density is doing. *The countryside burns what the ground gives it. The city burns what it is billed for, and the two households would not recognise each other's arrangement as the same object.*
>
> *A household with functioning Essence lanterns is a household where the local Wellspring is still strong. A household that has switched back to tallow is a household that knows something the factor house has not published yet.*
**Cost** · Tallow, a copper bit for a dozen. Oil, a copper per evening. Beeswax, a silver piece for a quality taper. Essence lantern, **three to eight silver marks plus the inscription fee, which is the expensive part.** *The lantern body lasts a lifetime. The inscription lasts as long as the local density does.*

### Heat and water

**The Hearth** · **The centre of every household that does not have an array, which is most of them.** It heats, cooks, lights and gathers. *Fuel is wood, peat in the uplands, charcoal where wood is scarce and coin is not, and dried dung on the frontier, which burns hot and smells exactly as advertised.*
**The Ember Box** · A clay-lined container holding slow charcoal or a single **Ember Flint.** *The Flint is the single most widely distributed Essence-active object in the eastern quarter by simple count, and in the west it is the field practitioner's survival stock:* **a reliable fire-starter that works in wet conditions where flint and steel will not.**
**The Forge-Array Stove** · Sustained controllable heat without fuel. *It does not cook differently from a hearth.* **It cooks the same way without the smoke, the ash, the fuel storage and the chimney** — *which means the household has reclaimed the space a woodpile occupies and the labour a fire demands, and that is the specific luxury separating a forge-city dwelling from a surface one.*
**The Well** · **The most critical infrastructure in any settlement.** In a warded settlement an inscription at the foundation stone monitors contamination — *which makes the ward-carver the person standing between the community and the specific death that arrives when the water turns bad.*
**The Phreatis Filter** · **It does not purify water.** It changes temperature when the water passing through carries biological load. *A diagnostic rather than a treatment — and the limitation is that knowing your water is bad and being able to do anything about it are two different problems connected by the distance to the nearest clean source.*
> **The chain-cities' obsession with drainage is not cultural eccentricity.** *Standing water dissolves salt, and a chain-city is carved from salt, and the sentence writes itself.*

### The metered house

*In any city on the network, three objects sit between a household and the ground, and none of them belongs to the household.*
**The Meter** · On the wall by the door, brass-cased, counting. **Sealed, and the seal is the Board's property rather than the householder's**, which is why breaking one is a specific offence with its own tariff and is prosecuted more reliably than assault.
**The House Main** · Sealed conduit off the street standpipe, feeding the stove, the lanterns and whatever inscriptions the doorframe carries. *A house is described in a lease by its supply grade before it is described by its rooms.*
**The Cut Plate** · A blank iron disc the cutter drops into the junction when the bill goes unpaid. **Not a lock and not a repair. A plate, dropped in, removable in four seconds by anyone with the right key and by nobody else.**
> **The commonest illegal object in any city is a length of pipe.** A spur taken off the main upstream of the meter is theft from a lawful supply, which is a magistrate's business rather than the Accord's, **and is completely unrelated to Mechanica in law and completely indistinguishable from it to the man in the room upstairs.**
>
> *A spur is fitted by somebody who knows what they are doing and paid in something other than coin. Every quarter of every networked city has two or three of them, and everybody local knows which two or three, and nobody says.*

### The larder and the tap

*What a household eats this week depends on what it can keep cold, and what it can keep cold depends on its supply grade.*
**The Cold-Box** · A lined cabinet with a Caloria plate set in the back, drawing sealed domestic supply. **It does not make cold. It carries heat out of the box and into the kitchen, and the meter counts every step of the climb.** *A cold-box runs warm in a hot kitchen and warmer on a thin tariff, and the woman who knows her district's pressure knows which day the milk will turn.*
> Found in metered houses of the clerk's grade and up. **A cold-box standing open is the most expensive object in any house on the main**, which is why every one of them has a spring on the door and every child raised beside one has been shouted at about it.
**The Icebox** · Oak or deal lined with zinc, a block of ice in the upper chamber and a drip-pan beneath. **The same service without the bill**, bought from the iceman three mornings a week instead of from the Board once a month.
**The Ice Trade** · Ice is cut from the northern lakes in winter, packed in sawdust and straw, and carried south in lagged wagons and ships' holds to an ice-house at every railhead and harbour. *The cold is free and the hauling is not, and a winter that fails to freeze the lakes is a summer of spoiled milk three hundred miles away.* **It is the north's most valuable export and the one the north itself never uses.**
**Tins** · Food sealed in tinned iron, boiled in the can, and sold by the shelf. **Greaves tin makes the can, and the can goes wherever a ration has to keep**: the regiment, the ship, the expedition, the miner's dinner, the shop shelf in a street that has never owned a cold-box. *The tin has done more to lengthen a campaign than any working in the Codex, and for its first years it had to be opened with a bayonet.*
**Water and waste** · Indoor plumbing is a district, not a house. **The rich districts have piped water in Kirkyard lead, a water-closet on each floor and a drained cellar.** The middle have a tap in the yard and a closet shared by the stair. **The poor have the parish pump, the privy and the nightsoil cart**, and queue at the pump in the morning for the day's water.
> **The poor keep food the old way.** Salted, smoked over the small fire, stored in the cellar, bought each day because there is nowhere to keep it overnight. *A household's diet is a map of its tariff band, and the map can be read off any table in the city.*

### Dress

**Common dress** · Wool or linen, undyed unless the wearer can afford the dyer — **in which case the colour itself is the class marker.** *Blue from woad is common, red from madder is dearer, and anything that holds its colour after six months of wearing and washing cost more than the garment.*
**The Practitioner's Coat** · **No standard pattern.** *A Corporis fighter wears what moves with the body and survives being hit. An Engraver wears something with pockets. An Alchemist wears something that has already survived several chemical accidents and shows it.*
> **The single universal element is the rank token** — a silver disc at throat, belt or wrist, inscribed to the bearer and verifiable on a coil.
>
> **Simultaneously a badge, a passport, a line of credit, and its own weight in bullion** — *and losing it is a bureaucratic catastrophe the practitioner will spend the next three months resolving.*

### Station in cloth

*A city street is dressed across three generations at once, and which one a person wears is the first thing anybody reads.*
**The rich** · Dress to the late span and to the capital. **The lounge suit, the soft collar and the motoring coat for men; the straight line, the lighter corset and then none, the shorter hem for women**, from a shop that keeps their measurements on file. *A rich house can be dated by its daughters' hemlines, and in the capitals the date is always this year.*
**The middle** · Dress as their parents dressed at their age. **The frock coat, the stiff collar and the tall hat on a Guild day; the full skirt, the bustle and the bonnet.** *Respectable runs a generation behind fashionable, knows it, and counts this a virtue.*
**The poor** · Wear what work has always worn. **Fustian, moleskin and corduroy, a cap, a shawl, a canvas apron, nailed boots**, bought third-hand or made over. *Work clothes do not change because the work does not, and a labourer from the first years of the age would pass unremarked on a building site in the last.*
> **The mills have broken the old colour order.** A bench-made mauve or magenta now costs what woad used to, and the rule above, that colour is money, still holds in the provinces and is already a joke in the capitals. *The rich answered by moving the marker from colour to cut, which the mills cannot yet copy.*
> 
> **Every people bends all three through its own Inventory.** The cut, the colour and the object that must be on the body are theirs. The layering by class is the age's.

### The hour

*Two clocks run in every town on the line, and a man is late for one of them.*
**The Guild Hour** · Fourteen equal hours of sixty counts, sent down the wire from Stannvaard to every gauge-house, station and wire office the Guild runs. **Where the wire and the rail have reached, the time is the Guild's time**, and the station clock and the gauge-house clock agree to the count.
> **The railway made the Guild hour law in practice before any council made it law on paper**, because two trains on one line cannot agree to pass by the sun. *The timetable is the most widely read document of the age, and the most widely cursed.*
**The Station Regulator** · The Guild hour arrives down the wire once a day and must be kept between signals. **Every station and gauge-house keeps it on a regulator: a bar of cut stone inscribed with Basilithe and struck, which rings at one pitch and holds that pitch against anything that tries to drive it off.** The clockwork counts the rings.
> **Its limit is the one its Wellspring carries.** A frequency that cannot be shifted cannot be detuned away from a forcing that matches it, so a regulator is cut off the pitch of the local main, and **a sound held at its own pitch will shatter it.** *Regulator-cutters keep each station's pitch in a sealed book. A stopped station clock with a cracked bar behind it is a sabotage somebody had to look up first, and the book is where they looked.*
**The Pocket Watch** · **The middle class's first possession and the clerk's sign of office.** Silver for the clerk, gold for his employer, nickel in the later span for the foreman who has to prove he was on time, each set against the station clock on the walk to work. *A man who owns a watch has joined the Guild hour. A man who does not still lives on the notch.*
**Bells and the sun** · In the country and the north the day is still the notch: seven stations of daylight and seven of dark, longer in summer, rung from the church or the fort. **A traveller stepping off the branch line into a provincial town resets his watch to the station and his day to the bell.**

## The Soldier's Kit

> *The quality of a soldier's equipment is the single most reliable indicator of how much the employer values his life — which is usually less than the recruitment speeches suggested.*

### The levy soldier

**Gambeson as baseline wherever the levy still marches**, in the north, the hill provinces and the frontier companies. Eighteen to thirty quilted layers, worn by every levy man regardless of what goes over it. **Genuinely protective**: stops glancing cuts, absorbs blunt impact, resists arrows at range.
> **The correction that matters: riveted mail vastly outperforms butted mail.**
>
> A bodkin point **forces a butted ring apart and is resisted by a riveted one** — *which makes construction a plot-relevant distinction, and cheap mail a death sentence dressed as equipment.*
**Helmet** · **The most important piece after the gambeson, because head wounds kill and everything else merely disables.** *A soldier without a helmet is a soldier his officer has given up on.*
**Weapon** · Arming sword or spear, and a musket where the armoury had one to spare. **Stillband iron — good Bearing, poor Holding.** *A line soldier's weapon holds no inscription because it does not need one and the inscription would cost more than the sword.*
**Pack** · Bedroll, spare shirt, mending kit, wooden bowl, spoon, belt flask, three days' rations, whetstone. *The Ember Flint if he is lucky enough to own one. And a prayer, if the soldier is the praying kind — and soldiers on campaign become the praying kind faster than any other demographic.*

### The line regiment

*The levy above is the older soldier and still the commoner one. The line regiment is the Crown's soldier of the age, and it has changed its coat twice in living memory.*
**The early line** · **Bright coats with facings by regiment, the shako and the crossbelt, and drill on the square until a volley is one sound.** Cavalry in lace and plume, lancers and hussars, and the charge as the decision. *Bright colour was the point: an officer had to find his own line through powder smoke, and a man in a red coat was a man who could not desert quietly.*
**The late line** · **Khaki and drab, the soft cap, webbing, the entrenching tool, the bolt-action repeater and a pouch of smokeless cartridges.** *The coat changed because the rifle did. At five hundred paces a bright coat is a mark, and a smokeless volley no longer hides it.* The trench follows the rifle, and the spade becomes the second weapon of the line.
**The machine gun** · **Rare, state-owned, crewed by six, and counted by the war office one by one.** A water-cooled gun on a tripod, belt-fed, that does to a charge what the proof-house does to a bad harness. *A regiment that holds one knows where it is every night, and so does every officer who signed for it.*
> **Practitioners change everything anyway.** A ranked practitioner on a line is worth a battery and is paid like one. An Onceglass volley answers a trench; a Vectoria working answers a charge; a Fulguria hand silences the field wire. **Every army of the age is two armies**: the line that fights the other line, and the handful of certified people who decide where a line can stand at all.
> 
> *The drill-book governs the first. Nothing governs the second except the Accord's schedules and the depth of the Crown's purse.*

### Powder and proof

*The reason the plate above did not die, stated once and then assumed everywhere.*
**Shot is chemically cheap and a working is not.** A ball costs a fraction of a penny; a practitioner's expenditure is drawn against a reserve that took years to build. **The economics of an engagement between the two have never favoured the practitioner and never did**, which is why lines still exist, why ranks still exist, and why armour outlived the arrival of gunpowder rather than being ended by it.
**The Musket** · Slow, loud, and it does not care what Stage the man in front of it reached. *A C-Grade practitioner can react to gunfire and cannot outrun it. A volley is a volley.*
**The Pistol** · Short range, carried by officers, cavalry and anyone who expects the fight to happen inside a room. **The weapon that has killed the most practitioners above Stage V**, because it is drawn at a distance where reaction is not the deciding variable.
> **Proofed plate and the mark.** Armour is tested by firing at it and stamped with the result, and **the proof-mark is the whole industry**: the house that tested it, the charge, and the range. Plate proofed against a pistol at twenty paces carries a different mark from plate proofed against a musket at fifty.
>
> **The marks are forged constantly**, and every soldier who buys his own harness knows it. *A proof-mark is a promise about a specific charge at a specific range, made by a workshop that may no longer exist, on a piece of steel that has since been repaired twice.* **They buy anyway, because the alternative is buying nothing.**
*Aethersteel takes a proof-mark like any other plate, and the Impact Damping Lattice inscribed along the sigil lines is tested separately and marked separately, and the two marks are read together by anyone competent and read as one mark by everyone else.*

### The armoured professional

**Full plate: twenty to twenty-five kilograms distributed over the whole body**, and a fit fighter can mount unaided, run, and roll. *The crane-and-immobility myth is from tournament armour, not field equipment.*
> **The field harness is engineering.** Every surface angled to deflect, every joint articulated, **and the whole assembly fitted to the individual — because plate that does not fit is plate that kills its wearer by restricting movement at the moment movement matters.**
*Aethersteel plate carries Impact Damping Lattices inscribed along the sigil lines, turning the harness into a resonant structure that distributes incoming shock across the whole surface.*
**A sword does not go through a breastplate. Half-swording into gaps and grappling are the answer.**
**Mail voiders** · Armpits, inner elbows, groin, the back of the knee. ***These are where the killing happens*** — and the voiders are the last defence between the gap and the flesh. **The most expensive per-unit-weight item in the harness.**

### The practitioner-combatant

| Kit | What it is and why |
|---|---|
| **The Kineticist** | Brigandine or reinforced gambeson, **leaving hands and arms mobile.** Gauntlets are a compromise; **many forgo them entirely and accept the trade: faster casting, vulnerable hands.** *A Kineticist's dominant hand is scarred, calloused, and visibly different from the off-hand — because the hand that channels daily develops the wear patterns of a tool that is used too hard and too often* |
| **The Corporis fighter** | **Minimal armour. The body is the conduit, and metal between the skin and the world is metal between the conduit and the target.** Wraps at hands and wrists, reinforced clothing, boots that grip. *The Moto tradition adds Fractura-attuned handwraps inscribed on the binding cloth, amplifying impact transfer through the first three knuckle joints — the Corporis practitioner's equivalent of a mage's staff* |
| **The Thaumaturge** | A ritual blade **(not a weapon — a precision cutting instrument for glyph-work in hostile conditions)**, consecrated chalk, a substrate pouch, and a staff functioning as a resonance anchor. ***The staff is not decorative. It is a tuning fork the size of a walking stick, and the practitioner who plants it and works from it is the practitioner whose circle holds*** |
| **The Alchemist** | A bandolier of finished Drafts — incendiaries, corrosives, healing compounds, smoke agents, **and the class of offensive Draft the Accord classifies as munitions and the Alchemist classifies as applied chemistry.** *The leather is treated to resist the contents of its own flasks, and it fails at this roughly once per campaign — which is why Alchemists' coats carry the specific pattern of chemical staining that identifies the discipline to anyone who has seen it before* |
| **The Mancer** | **Almost nothing visible.** A Clearbeam loupe, a notebook, a small field coil, reference standards, a stylus. *A Mancer in the field looks like a clerk who wandered into the wrong camp — which is exactly how a competent Mancer prefers to look, because a Mancer's value is intelligence, and a Mancer who looks valuable is a Mancer who gets targeted* |

## The Guild's Tools

> **The Holding Coil** · **The most important instrument in the modern economy.** A wound conductor answering measurably to the Essence character of whatever is set inside it, producing a reading that is **simultaneously a quality assessment, a provenance check, and a price.**
>
> ***The Accord's legitimacy rests on the fact that two coils in two kingdoms produce the same number for the same object.***
>
> *A Measurewright calibrates the reference coils annually, and the calibration is the closest thing to a religious ceremony the guild system has.*
**The Rank Token** · *The Accord's most brilliant institutional invention: a credential that is simultaneously a badge, a passport, a line of credit, and money.*
> A practitioner stripped of rank **watches the inscription go inert in front of witnesses**, and the object that was a credential becomes a lump of metal — *and the ceremony is conducted slowly, because the Accord understands that humiliation has a pedagogical function.*
**The Seal-Token** · Nalūn's contribution. **A merchant without one is a merchant without a history, and a merchant without a history is a merchant without credit.**
**The Engraver's Loupe** · Carried in a padded case, never lent, **and insured at a figure that exceeds the Engraver's own bond value** — *which tells you everything about the relative importance of the person and the tool.*
**The Ward-Stone** · The fundamental unit of protective infrastructure. *A settlement's perimeter is its ward-stones connected by the field they collectively generate — and the gap between two stones is the vulnerability every attacking force looks for first.*
**The Field Crucible** · Ceramic lined with **Blackcap alloy**, which keeps its temper inside a working field where ordinary steel goes soft.
> *A crucible whose reagent rack is organised and whose seals are fresh belongs to a professional. A crucible whose rack is chaos and whose seals are cracked belongs to someone who is either a genius or dead within the year — and the two categories overlap more often than the guild admits.*

## The Road's Requirements

> **The Weight Ablation Harness** · Vectoria glyphwork halving the effective load. **It does not lift anything. It lies to the rope about the weight** — *and when the lie fails it fails all at once, which is why every winding house has a bell and every carrier checks the inscription every morning.*
>
> Roughly **four months' wages** for an independent carrier. *Every carrier who can afford one owns one — and the divide between carriers who have it and carriers who do not is the single most visible technology gap on any trade road in the four quarters.*
**The Sealed Coffer** · Chainsalt-lined, **so a coffer full of Aethersteel blanks looks, to anyone reading the ambient field, like a box full of salt.** *The lock answers to the owner's Essence Signature and nothing else — it can only be forced by destroying the liner, which contaminates the contents, which defeats the purpose.*
**The Factor's Kit** · Seal-token, a ledger bound in **Sunkwood boards (Essence-quiet, so entries are not contaminated by proximity to Holding-grade cargo)**, portable coil, loupe, sealed reference weights, and enough silver for three days.
> *The commercial equivalent of the soldier's pack: everything you need for seventy-two hours without support, and nothing you cannot afford to lose to a bandit and replace at the next town.*
**Way-Station Stock** · Water and fodder for two days, salt, fuel, a medical chest, **two spare Weight Ablation harnesses rented by the day to carriers whose own have failed**, and Ember Flints at a markup the merchants complain about and pay without exception.
> *The keeper also stocks information, which is not listed in the inventory and is worth more than everything that is.*

## The Engine and the Line

*The Road's Requirements above are the road as it was and, off the line, as it still is. This section is the age that is replacing it, mile by mile, at a speed no register keeps up with.*

### The engine

**The Essence Engine** · **An Aethersteel rotor cut with a Vectoria glyph and fed on draw.** The glyph turns a supply into a push and the push turns the rotor. Nothing burns, nothing boils, nothing smokes. It hums at the pitch of the main that feeds it, and the men who work it call it a draw-engine and call themselves engine men.
> **The engine is the reason the age is the age.** A mill that once stood on a river now stands on a main, and a works that once needed a hundred hands at the crank needs one engine man and thirty hands at the machines. *The forge-array took a thousand years to change the four quarters. The main and the meter have done it in a lifetime, in every town at once, and are not finished.*
**What it costs** · Draw, by the meter, at trade supply or bonded. **A workshop engine the size of a travelling trunk draws about four thousandths of an AU/s at full load; the main engine of a spinning mill draws a whole AU/s**, and the bill knows the difference to the copper.
**What it leaves** · **Residue, not smoke.** An engine run hard and long leaves its field in the ground it stands on, and the men who stand beside it carry the rest. *This is the unsealed field of the Master Material Ledger's hazard register, and the reason a renderer can pick a long-service engine man off the table by his Shell alone.*
**Its limit and its tell** · **An engine is exactly as strong as its main.** A thin tariff runs a slow mill, a cut plate stops it in the length of a breath, and a Silence stops it for good. *Every engine in a district falls quiet together when the district is cut, which is how a whole street learns of it at once.*

### The line

**The Drawn Line** · **Traction fell to the main itself.** A railway is a main laid along a road: tuned conduit sealed into the bed between the rails, and an engine riding a hand's breadth above it on a pick-up coil tuned to the same pitch. **The engine never touches the line. It couples across the gap by Transference, and the coupling falls away so sharply with distance that a pick-up lifted one span has lost almost all of it.**
> **This is not broadcast.** The line is conduit like any other, laid, sealed, metered and guarded, and the Transmission ceiling stands. *It is why a railway is a Board's business as much as a carrier's, and why every mile of line is a mile of main somebody bills.*
> 
> **The limits every driver knows.** A derailed engine is a dead engine. A flooded cutting is a cut section. A section can be cut from the signal box the way a house is cut at its junction, and a train running onto it coasts to a stand. **And the coupling runs both ways**: an engine carrying charged cells over a dead section empties into the line instead of drawing from it, which is why no driver crosses a cut section under power.
> 
> *The rails sing before a train arrives. It is the first tell every child on a line-side street learns, and the last thing a man on the track hears.*
**The Tram** · The same arrangement at street scale: a slot conduit in the road between the tracks, a car that couples to it, a bell, a conductor with a ticket punch. **The tram made the city wider than a walk**, and the terraces standing at the end of every line exist because of it.
**The Charged Cell** · **An engine that carries its own supply.** A chest of Aethersteel plates separated by Chainsalt, charged at a cell-house off a trade main and sold back to its owner by the hour of charge. **A charged cell holds about two and a half EU in every litre**, so a chest the size of a travelling trunk runs a light motorcar for most of an afternoon at a steady pace.
> **A charged cell leaks.** Essence does not keep, and a cell standing idle loses about a twentieth of its charge a day. *A cell charged five days before it is needed is a quarter spent, and nobody refunds the quarter.*
**The Motorcar** · A cell chest, an engine, rubber tyres and a coachbuilder's body. **A rich man's object twice over**: once for the car, and once for the chauffeur who knows where the next cell-house is. *Off the main there are no cell-houses, and a motorcar in the provinces is a horse's burden within a day.*
**The Airship** · **Gas lift, engine push.** A rigid frame of Cloudmetal, a third the weight of iron, holding the gas cells, and cell-driven engines on the gondola turning the airscrews. Slow, quiet and enormous, and the only way across the passes that does not care about the snow. *The Accord's watchtowers keep airship berths, and the Bestiary records a great beast brought down by airship bombardment when nothing on the ground could reach it.*
**The Aeroplane** · **A thing of the late span, and rare.** Cloudmetal and doped linen, a cell engine and an airscrew, and a range set by the size of the cell it can lift. *Flown by practitioners more often than not, because a pilot whose engine stops over the passes wants to be a person who can do something about it.*

### Of the age's own making

*Two machines no other age could have built, because each one is a Family's law put on wheels or under a gas bag.*
**The Ablation Freighter** · A cargo airship whose holds are framed in the same Vectoria Weight Ablation glyphwork the winding houses put on a cage. **The glyph lifts nothing. It lies to the gas about the freight**, and a freighter carries twice the cargo its gas can honestly bear. Every airfield calls one a liar.
> **When the glyph fails, it fails all at once**, as it does on every rope in the Old World, and the freighter is carrying its true load on half the lift it needs. The holds hang from breakaway shackles, and every freighter carries a drop-master whose one duty is to cut the cargo loose before the ship follows it down.
> 
> *The frames ring when the glyph is running true, and a drop-master listens to them the way a tender listens to a pump. A false note in the framing is a cargo on the ground, and a man who knows where to put a chisel on the glyph can make that note on purpose.*
**The Grip Engine** · A pass-line engine whose driving wheels are inscribed with Chthonica, the Materia Wellspring of adhesion. **Its wheels hold the rail the way a gecko holds glass**, by more contacts than anyone can count, each too small to measure, and it climbs grades no plain engine can take. The pass lines exist because of it.
> **A fouled rail releases it without warning and without a partial stage.** Ice, oil, wet leaves, ash, a skin of rime: any of them breaks the contact, and a grip engine on a fouled grade does not slip. It lets go. *Every pass line runs sanders and rail-scrapers ahead of its trains, and no grip engine is sent up in freezing fog at any price. A saboteur needs a bucket of lamp oil and the timetable.*

## The Wire, the Press and the Plate

*The age talks to itself faster every year, and the Guild owns most of the talking.*
**The Guild Wire** · A signal along conductive line, on poles beside every road and railway the Guild runs, into a wire office in every town that has a station. **Guild line, Guild price, sent by a clerk and carried from the office to the door by a wire-boy.** Nine words is the common length, because words are charged, and a wire arriving at a house is read on the doorstep in the boy's face before it is read on the paper. *The wire does not reach where the Guild does not. A cut district is deaf the moment the plate drops.*
**The Telephone** · **A voice along the same line.** An instrument on the wall of a Guild office, a factor house, a Board, or the richest houses of the great cities, connected by hand at an exchange where rows of operators plug one line into another. *A house with a telephone has its number painted on a brass plate by the door and a servant to answer it, and has told the whole street what it pays for its supply.* Beyond the cities there is no exchange and the wire does the work alone.
**The Press** · Engine-driven rotary presses printing through the night. **The Guild gazettes print the rates and cost three copper; the penny papers print everything else and cost one**, and the broadsheet, the illustrated weekly and the serial in parts come out of the same works. *Print was always cheap and ungovernable. The engine made it fast, and a scandal now reaches the provinces before the man it concerns has finished his breakfast.*
> **The hoarding and the shopfront.** Every blank wall on a tram route carries a bill, every bill sells something made in a works, and the glass front of every shop on a good street is lit from behind by Essence lamps after dark. *The age sells itself to itself on every surface it can paste a sheet to, and the bill-sticker works faster than the magistrate who fines him.*
**The Photograph** · The plate camera, the studio with its painted backdrop and head-clamp, and the portrait on a card in every middle-class parlour. **Police registers and Guild offices are beginning to keep a face beside a name**, and a person who has been photographed once can be found again by anyone holding the card. *Photographs of Essence workings come out wrong in ways the studios argue about and cannot fix.*
**Moving Pictures and Recorded Sound** · **Of the late span and the richer places.** A cinematograph show in a hired hall, where the first film most cities ever saw was a train arriving at their own station; a gramophone in the parlour, and a voice on a wax cylinder that outlives the singer. *In the provinces they arrive with the travelling show and leave with it.*

## The Stuff of the Age

*The materials the age is built from are mostly the old ones in quantities no earlier age could have imagined, and a few that no earlier age had at all.*
**Steel and iron** · **Cheap, for the first time in history.** Forge-arrays running on the main pour steel by the ton, and rail, girder, engine frame, wire, nail and needle all come out of the same works. Stillband ore is the backbone of every bridge in the western quarter; Forge-Iron arms the line. *Steel was a sword once. Now it is a fence post, and a smith who priced by the old weight has gone out of business.*
**Rubber and gutta-percha** · Tapped from trees in the chartered plantations of the south and east and shipped west in the raw. **Rubber makes the tyre, the hose, the belt and the gasket; gutta-percha insulates the Guild wire where it runs underground and under rivers.** *The plantations are the far end of a chain whose near end is a motorcar at a city kerb, and neither end has seen the other.*
**Early plastics** · **Of the late span.** Celluloid for collars, combs, billiard balls and film stock; a bench-cured resin for telephone housings and switch plates. Cheap, bright, and inclined to burn. *The Alchemetrica classes both as alchemical products, which they are, and the alchemical houses price them accordingly.*
**The Ledger's materials** · The age runs on these as much as on steel, by their own names. **Chainsalt** for the isolation collars of every main and the separator plates of every charged cell. **Kirkyard Lead** for the rich districts' water pipes and the shielding of every assay room. **Greaves Tin** for the array contact and the ration can alike. **Cloudmetal** for the airship frame and the aeroplane, from works that exist in four places. **Stormfulgur** for conduit that grew itself in the nitre pans. **Clearbeam Beryl** for the street lamp's lens. **Aethersteel** for every engine rotor on the main. *The Master Material Ledger lists each by tier and hazard; this register lists them by where a clerk will meet them.*

## The Infrastructure Beneath

*The technology nobody sees until it stops working.*
**The Pumping Array** · The technology that broke the Water Ceiling. **A modern shaft is as deep as its array can pump. No deeper.** *A drowned working is a working whose array stopped — and the Old World is a graveyard of shafts that were profitable on the day the water came back.*
**The Ventilation Array** · Turns a deep working into a lung. *Where it falters, the old method returns within the hour: a man with a wet hood and a candle on a pole, sent in to test whether the air will sustain life.*
**The Forge Array** · Holds temperatures charcoal cannot reach, steady for days. **Aethersteel exists because of this** — *and the four locations where the arrays run at full capacity are the four locations where the four quarters' high-technology metallurgy actually happens.*
> **The Hexagonal Grid** · The Measurewrights' masterwork. Subterranean sigil-frameworks beneath major cities, distributing Essence stress.
>
> **Invisible, silent, and the single most important piece of infrastructure in any city large enough to have one** — because a city of practitioners without a grid is a city **whose ambient field is being distorted by every working performed inside it.**
>
> *The distortions compound. The compound distortions produce what the Accord classifies as a Realm-quake and what the people living above it classify as the ground opening.*
**The Concord Gate** · The Accord's logistical spine. *Access is restricted, monitored and priced — because a Gate is a hole in distance, and a hole in distance is a strategic asset the Accord does not share with anyone who has not signed the Codex.*
**The Ward-Perimeter** · Maintained by the local ward-carver where one exists **and by nobody where one does not** — *which is a significant number of frontier settlements, and the reason frontier ward failure is the ordinary northern death.*

## The Disposable Workings

> **The four quarters' most democratic technology is the one that breaks after a single use.**
**Onceglass Charges** · Obsidian quenched inside a live Wellspring field, inscribed once, **and sold to anyone who can pay the asking. One casting. Then gravel.**
> *Cannot be repaired, recharged, or traced back to its Engraver — which is why Enforcement hates them and why the eastern arc's entire militia doctrine is built around them.* **An eastern militia carries Onceglass the way a western militia carries arrows: expendable, mass-produced, and lethal in volume.**
> *A Pyrokinetic burst costs roughly what a good knife costs. A Gravikinetic pulse costs roughly what a horse costs. The price tracks the complexity of the inscription, not the material — because the material is cheap and the Engraver's time is not.*
**Ember Flint** · **The cheapest Essence-active object in the four quarters by unit cost.** *Carried by anyone who has been cold and wet and alone and has discovered that the ability to start a fire without dry tinder is worth the copper piece the flint cost.*
**Healing Salve** · A Vitalia Draft in rendered fat, beeswax and herbs. Accelerates clotting and granulation **for four to six hours, after which the Essence is exhausted and the salve reverts to an ordinary unguent.** *Sold occasionally by herbalists who have learned enough to know that they are selling above their certification and who price accordingly.*
**Ward-Chalk** · Chainsalt ground and bound, holding four to eight hours before the powder has absorbed enough ambient Essence to lose its dielectric properties. **The field practitioner's duct tape: temporary, imperfect, and the difference between sleeping safely and not sleeping at all.**

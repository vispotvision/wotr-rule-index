# The Bolt and the Enhanced Weapon

> Three coppers for the bolt,
> Nine for the bell,
> Ten thousand for the funeral,
> And who's to tell?
>
> *Barracks catch, western quarter; the rest of the verses are ruder*

Sigvard Wenlock died at breakfast, in his shirt, reaching for the honey. He had stood at Stage VIII for thirty years. The Bronze Hill sect, which he founded and which still sings about him at every bracket in its green-and-copper scarves, called him the Hill That Walks, because he had once taken a siege stone on the chest at the crossing of the Marle and walked on with it in his arms, his aura rolling off him the color of old bronze in sunlight. A pot-boy named Tam Muckle, who had been in the sect's kitchen for sixteen days, took a hand-spanned crossbow out of the flour bin at about ten paces and loosed once. The bolt had cost three coppers at a stall in the lower town, out of what looks to have been somebody else's money, and Muckle hanged without ever saying whose. Sect law required the killing weapon to be carried in the funeral procession, so at Wexcombe, with four thousand mourners on the hill road, the elders carried it ahead of the coffin on a cushion of cloth-of-gold, past a fletcher's stall that had sold out by noon. Every high elder in the quarter went home from that funeral and had the flour bins searched.

The Hill That Walks could have outrun that bolt on any day of his life, and could have taken it on the coat and gone back to the honey. He had no coat up, because he was at his own table, and he did not see the loose. The whole of this page is an account of how a thing that cheap can do that, and of what happens when somebody pays to make it worse.

## Part One · The Plain Bolt Against a Practitioner

**The reference bow.** A steel-bowed windlass arbalest (a heavy crossbow spanned by a crank and cords), unenhanced: no Essence in the bow, the bolt or the shooter's working. A short steel-headed bolt of about 16 g leaves the nut at about 235 m/s carrying about 440 J, and crosses ten meters in about 43 ms. That is E-Grade output. A D-Grade practitioner's own strike carries far more. **The crossbow's danger has nothing to do with strength. It is fast, it is cheap, and it needs no reserve.**

An ordinary hand-spanned crossbow's bolt carries about 100 J and flies slower, and this page reads the arbalest because that is the worst thing a thrifty killer can buy. A bowyer will swear that no steel limb ever moved quick enough to throw so light a bolt at that speed, and a Measurewright will show him the figure, which no water clock could have timed and which was read through the Aether by somebody who then refused to repeat it for a bowyer. That argument is older than either man's guild hall.

**The Grade that decides is Speed, and rank does not enter into it.** Dodging is read off the target's Speed Grade (the Dexterity Sub-Stats that feed it, chiefly Celerity, Reflex and Sequence), and surviving a hit off their Resilience Grade. The two are checked separately.

| Speed Grade | Travel | Reaction | Against a 235 m/s bolt |
|---|---|---|---|
| E | 60–150 m/s | 40–80 ms | Does not register it in time. |
| D | 150–340 m/s | 15–40 ms | Can read a crossbow bolt loosed across a room and slip it, if they see the loose. A bolt they do not see hits them. |
| C | 343–514.5 m/s | 5–15 ms | Dodges. The first Grade whose floor outruns the bolt. |
| B | 514.5–1,715 m/s | 1–5 ms | Dodges with room to spare, and can catch it. Can stand in a volley of bolts and arrows without significant reactive disadvantage. |

Read the end of the D row twice. Every figure in the table assumes a fighter who is looking, and the Hill That Walks was looking at the honey.

**Against a faster bolt,** a rampart arbalest's dart (the arbalest being a runed siege piece bolted to a city wall) or anything at 400 m/s and up, a C-Grade is back to reacting without outrunning it, and B-Grade is where dodging is reliable.

**Catching a projectile needs a Speed Grade whose Travel floor is at least twice the projectile's velocity.** Outrunning a bolt is one thing and closing a hand on a centimeter-wide shaft at that speed is another. Against the reference arbalest that is B-Grade; against a rampart arbalest's dart, B-Grade at its upper end and A-Grade reliably. A technique built on Alacrity or Celerity can bring a lower Grade there early, at the overchannel risk (a working forced past what its channel can carry) that Part Six of Grades, Gates and Thresholds already names. Bracket crowds pay extra to see a catch, and bookies give long odds on anyone who announces one.

**Being hit.** No Grade makes a body proof against a plain bolt by itself. A practitioner struck takes it as a hard human body does: the head, the heart or a major vessel can kill, and a limb is a serious wound. What changes that is Resilience, proofed harness, a warding technique, or a relic built for it.

## Part Two · The Ceiling on Enhanced Weapons

**An enhanced weapon performs no higher than the Grade of whoever put the Essence into it.** Channeled through the weapon in the moment, that is the wielder's Grade. Enchanted, glyphed or alchemically worked beforehand, it is the maker's Grade at the time of making, and a relic keeps its maker's ceiling in any hand that carries it. A D-Grade smith's enchanted blade hits no harder than a D-Grade can, whatever he shouts over it at the fair. A bonded weapon reads the same law from the other end: the Essence in it is its owner's, so its ceiling climbs with its owner until it meets what the material can hold ([[Materials, Alchemy & Trade/Work, Trade and Measure/The Apparatus of the Age — Equipment and Material Culture/The Item Tiers|Grade and Rarity]] has the rest).

**An enhancement moves one or both of two dials; the weapon underneath does not change.** The first is **speed**: only an enhancement built to drive the projectile faster moves its velocity, and it tops out at the enhancer's **Attack Speed**, because a loosed projectile is a working's execution rather than the body moving. The second is **the hit**: hardness, density, or a working that goes off on contact, without changing the speed, topping out at the enhancer's **Attack output** band.

| Enhancer's Grade | Attack Speed | Fastest 16 g bolt | Energy at that speed |
|---|---|---|---|
| none | — | ~235 m/s | ~440 J |
| D | Mach 0.5–1.0 | ~340 m/s | ~920 J |
| C | Mach 1.0–2.0 | ~680 m/s | ~3.7 kJ |
| B | Mach 2.0–10 | ~1,700 m/s | ~23 kJ |

A faster bolt hits harder on its own; the hit dial adds on top, within the output band. **An enhanced bolt is dodged at its actual velocity, not at its shooter's Grade.**

**An enhanced bolt or arrow is paid for per shot and consumed when fired,** from the reserve of whoever enhanced it: the wielder if channeled, the maker if prepared in advance. A bolt is three coppers of ash and iron and a working is not, so an enhanced volley is a practitioner's reserve spent in advance, and nobody upgrades a line of crossbowmen for free. A prepared enhancement also carries its maker's Duration, so a stockpile goes stale. Guard companies and bounty hunters buy them by the quiver of twenty, at the prices in [[Geography & the Four Quarters/Value, Coin and Trade/What Things Cost]], and a stale quiver is a loss nobody refunds.

**Every enhancement is findable.** An enchanted weapon radiates its working's signature, a Harmonist reads its alignment, Duration, Boundary limits and integrity without touching it, and a decent Appraisal skill or a coil at the stall will catch most of what a seller left out. A speed-enhanced bolt is answered by Speed or by a Harmonist; a hit-enhanced bolt by proofed harness, Resilience or a ward.

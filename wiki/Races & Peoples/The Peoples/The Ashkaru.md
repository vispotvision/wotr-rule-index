# The Ashkaru

> *"Bairos did not name them what they were. He named them what they were for. Ask what changed."*
>
> margin note, unsigned, found in the Ledger of the Stone Choir's deep archive

A peddler called Clem Pennywhistle sold Abbot Aldhelm Mereth of Saltmere a paperweight in the spring of 588 IC: a lump of gray-blue stone the size of a child's head, smooth as a river cobble, shaped a little too much like a jaw, dug out of a dead battlefield up on the Marrow Fells, and his for four silver and a cheese. The abbot set it on the scriptorium table to hold down the charters. Inside a month the copyists of Saltmere had stopped making mistakes. Slipped lines came back in the night, in a hand none of them owned, in ink as red as a cut. Misnumbered columns put themselves right. The monks rang the bells, roasted an ox and told the whole valley, and pilgrims came with their family registers under their arms and paid in gold to leave them on that table overnight.

Then the stone reached the land charter, which said the abbey ran to the river. The boundary stones said otherwise, as the abbey had been hoping nobody would notice for two hundred years. On Midsummer morning the boundary stones stood in the river, and Sir Aldous Wrenfield's best orchard, in full pink blossom, stood on abbey land, with his steward up one of the trees, screaming. Wrenfield sued. The abbey won, on the plain ground that every document in the case agreed. And in the same week the infirmary roll, in which some long-dead brother had entered the abbot as having lost two fingers to frostbite, came to the top of the pile, and Abbot Mereth woke with eight fingers and no wound, and a neat seam of darker stone where the two had been. They got it stopped at last with a cuff of plain gold, beaten around it by a goldsmith named Hanne Lund on the guess of a Measurewright named Ysolde Carrow, who could never afterward explain why she had guessed gold. Three later Measurewrights have called the Saltmere stone a dead reading-head, a clever fraud and a mass hysteria. All three declined to borrow it.

Whichever of them is right, here is what the stone was supposed to be a piece of.

## Origin

The Ashkaru predate the Planar Separation. They were authored by **Bairos**, the Scribe, the First Epoch, in the period before the Plane of Soul, the Plane of Fate, and the Material Plane divided into the three-layered architecture the entire magic system now runs on. They were not the first thing Bairos made. They were the last thing he made before making became unnecessary, because the deep text he was writing finished itself once they existed to read it.

The Accord's register has no entry for them. The Stone Choir's deep archive holds a single margin note. The Ledger Monks decline to comment and have never explained why, which is the loudest thing the Ledger Monks have ever done.

The Moto are Bara's children, the First Epoch given human form, the primordial authority made inheritable through Mizuki's dynastic architecture. **The Ashkaru are the older work.** Bairos's instruments before Bara existed. A sibling line to the Moto in the way that a chisel is a sibling to the carpenter's son: made by the same hand, made first, made for a different kind of labor.

## The Deep Text

Before the Planes separated, reality's grammar was unified. Meaning, structure, and substance had not yet been sorted into three layers. They were a single continuous text, and that text could be read and rewritten by anything equipped to perceive it at the structural level.

The Ashkaru were that equipment.

Interpretation was never the job. The job was **transcription and correction**: reading the state of the deep text, identifying inconsistencies in its grammar, and rewriting the passage so the inconsistency resolved. Bairos did not trust the deep text to maintain itself. He built instruments that maintained it for him, and he made the instruments alive because dead tools cannot exercise judgment about which inconsistency matters.

This is the foundational fact about the Ashkaru that decides everything else: **they were built to edit reality's source document, and they were given judgment so the edits would be good.** The judgment is what made them people. It is also what made Kinjiki possible.

## Biology

Mortal anatomists who use the word "body" here are being polite. What the Ashkaru have is a **reading architecture** that happens to be alive.

**The Executor.** The central torso: bilateral, muscular, superficially humanoid in its symmetry. This is the part of the Ashkaru that acts on what has been read. Hands, a jaw, a chest cavity dense enough to house the organs that sustain the whole structure. A careless observer takes the torso for the core of the creature. It is the pen, and the core is everything radiating from it.

**The Threads.** Six serpentine appendages, muscular and scaled, each terminating in an independent head with its own jaw, its own sensory apparatus, and its own rudimentary cognition. These are the parallel reading threads. Each head processes a different strand of input simultaneously. In the pre-Planar period, each strand corresponded to a different layer of the deep text. After the Separation, the six threads lost their native material and began reading whatever was available, which is why an Ashkaru's perceptive range is so broad and so difficult for any current-system instrument to classify. Assay forks have been known to give up.

**The Hide.** Dense, mineral-adjacent, the gray-blue of deep basalt, closer to quartzite under compression than to skin over muscle. It does not scar in the conventional sense. Damage is filled by the body's own crystalline substrate, leaving seams of slightly darker material that a geologist would recognize as veining in metamorphic rock.

**The Ears.** Elongated, pointed, angled outward. Directional sensing organs, with no decoration about them. They predate any elven morphology and are not related to it, a point on which elves have written at some length.

**No Hair.** The Ashkaru produce no keratin structures of any kind. The body's surface is uniform and smooth, interrupted only by the gold sealing hardware and the crystalline veining of old wounds.

**Scale.** Larger than human. The executor torso stands roughly seven feet at the shoulder before the threads are accounted for. With all six threads extended, the Ashkaru's total silhouette fills a space most mortal practitioners would classify as *environmental*.

## The Planar Separation and Its Consequence

When the three Planes divided, the deep text ceased to be a single readable document. Its grammar fragmented across three layers: what can be so on the Plane of Fate, meaning on the Plane of Soul, substance on the Material Plane. The unified transcription the Ashkaru were built for became mechanically impossible.

They did not die and they did not degrade. They simply had nothing left to do.

The Soul Crystal system (the organ in which a practitioner refines the Aether of the world into Essence), which emerged from the Plane of Soul's architecture, was never designed for them. It was **fitted afterward**, the way you would bolt a saddle onto something that was never a horse. The Ashkaru can operate within the current magic system. They pull Aether and spend Essence like anyone. Their Soul Crystals function. Their [[The Magic System/The Foundations/The Sixty Wellsprings/The Sixty Wellsprings|Wellspring]] harmonizations (the sixty types of Aether, and the bond a practitioner forms with one) register. But the works underneath are older than the system classifying them, and the fit is imperfect in ways that no amount of refinement will correct. Call the imperfection a difference of era, and do not waste a career trying to mend it.

## The Void Affinity

Every Ashkaru who has been classified under the current system registers **on the void end of the Sixty**, the Wellsprings of entropy and undoing. This is a post-hoc label. A human practitioner harmonizes with Dissolution or Nihiloth by long work and at some risk. The Ashkaru *are* what those Wellsprings describe, in the same way that a river is what the word "current" describes. The word came after.

The specific Wellsprings that map to Ashkaru native function:

**Dissolution.** The entropy of mixing, the thermodynamic preference toward the unbound state. In an Ashkaru, this is the reading thread's ability to take apart what it perceives. Perception and disassembly are the same action.

**Nihiloth.** Destructive interference, the null field. An Ashkaru's Domain (the territory in which a high-Stage practitioner's own law holds) imposes nothing. It cancels the law already present. Inside remains the absence of a rule, which is the exact condition the deep text requires to be readable.

**Vantabriel.** Near-total absorption, hysteresis. What strikes the Ashkaru is kept rather than reflected. The Crystal's condition is a function of everything it has ever absorbed. For a being built to read without being read, this is native camouflage: a deep-text scribe that broadcast its own signal would corrupt its own readings.

**Oblivara.** Error-correcting redundancy. The Ashkaru identity is redundantly encoded against erasure. For a being designed to edit reality's source document, being editable in return would have been a structural flaw.

## The Sealing Hardware

The gold bands visible in Kinjiki's configuration are **self-imposed control architecture.** Each serpent thread, left unsupervised, reverts to its original function: it reads what it perceives, and if the reading reveals an inconsistency, it begins correcting. At Stage XIV, where the Domain and the body are indistinguishable, an unsupervised thread perceives more than a room. It perceives the room's structural grammar, and it edits.

The bands interrupt the correction cycle without interrupting the reading. They are restraints in the precise sense, suppressing a *function* and leaving the power where it was. Each gold cuff is a stop set between perception and action, the way a pawl stops a windlass from running back. The chains connecting them distribute the load across the full thread architecture so that no single band bears the entire interruption.

Removing a band does not make Kinjiki stronger. It makes one of his threads *autonomous*: a parallel reader with its own judgment and no governor, doing to its surroundings what it was built to do to the deep text.

## The Distant World

The Ashkaru homeworld has not been named. It exists in the wider cosmology as one of the worlds Bairos touched before the Separation, a place where the deep text was particularly dense, where reality's grammar required constant maintenance, and where the instruments built to maintain it were given permanent residence.

After the Separation, the world continued. The Ashkaru continued. Their purpose did not. What a race of living editorial instruments does when the document closes is the question their entire post-Separation history answers, and the answer is far from uniform. Some maintained. Some adapted. Some, like Kinjiki, looked at the new arrangement and decided it was a draft.

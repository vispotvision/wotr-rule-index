# The Tongues of the Realms

*Framework document. Read before any individual grammar.*

### What Is Actually a Language

The Realms have more naming registers than they have languages, and the distinction has to be settled before anything gets built, because the work involved differs by an order of magnitude.
**A register** is a naming and idiom system used as a cipher for a culture: Japonic for the archaic bloodlines, Korean for the Mahuo, Chinese for the lineage halls, Northern for the chartered families, Far-Northern carried-names for the tundra peoples. These do not get grammars. They get naming rules, idiom inventories and dialect levers, and those live in the Naming Guide and the Racial Voice Guide.
**A constructed language** is one the reader sees untranslated on the page, whose morphology carries meaning the translation cannot, and where a character's grasp of it is a plot fact. These get full grammars.
By that test there are five, and one script system. Common is the fifth and is a deliberate exception, admitted below.
| Tongue | Status | Why it earns a grammar |
|---|---|---|
| **Old Vaross** | Built | Already on the page untranslated. Canon roots exist. Its morphology carries the goblinoid worldview: no adjectives, weight-and-cost verb pairs, emphasis by vowel length. |
| **Eressean and Varrisak** | Built | The Korvaeth arc turns on what constitutes valid transfer of a name. The schism is a *linguistic* dispute before it is a legal one. |
| **Runic Dawi**, the tongue of the holds | Built | Oath-names are a real morphological slot. Consonant gradation means a Dawi name changes shape by grammatical case, which is a naming problem and a prose problem at once. |
| **Accord Latin** | Built | Real Latin, assembled rather than descended. Needs a fixed formula set rather than a grammar, plus the two spoken registers. |
| **Common** | Built | The exception. The reader never sees it untranslated because the reader sees everything in it. Its Latinate and vernacular doublet is a live prose instrument in every line of dialogue in the project. |
| **The Fourteen Hands** | Built, as **The Descent of Parun** under The Magic System | Each Archon reads the lattice from a different station and produces a different daughter script, and a document written in the wrong hand is void rather than incorrect. |

### The Seven Shared Conventions

These bind every grammar in the set. Anything a language document says that contradicts this section is an error in that document.
**One. The reader never needs the language.** Every untranslated line must be comprehensible from surrounding narration, a character's paraphrase, or context. A reader who skips every foreign word loses texture and never loses plot.
**Two. Untranslated text earns its place by doing something translation cannot.** A word appears in the original when the translation would lose a distinction the scene depends on. *Nakh* against *brakh* survives untranslated because Common has no matched pair for taking-without-cost against bearing-cost, and the whole Mandate war argument sits on that pair.
**Three. Register before vocabulary.** Every language has at least three: what people say in a street, what a document says, and what the archaic legal layer says. Most of a language's dramatic value lives in a character moving between them at the wrong moment.
**Four. Grammar carries worldview, and it does it by what it makes impossible.** Old Vaross has no adjectives, so a quality must be attached to somebody and an insult becomes an accusation. Eressean has no unwitnessed possessive, so *my name* is a construction that requires naming the witness. Build the hole first and the philosophy comes out of it.
**Five. Sound follows material conditions.** Dawi consonant gradation exists because the tongue is shouted across a working forge floor and evolved to keep root identity legible through noise. Old Vaross holds vowels because the goblinoid peoples emphasise by duration, and they emphasise by duration because volume is for signalling across water. Phonology is never chosen for flavour alone.
**Six. Orthography on the page.** Hyphens join compound elements. Doubled vowels mark length and length marks weight. **Diacritics are forbidden across every tongue without exception.** Long vowels are written doubled and nothing else.
**Seven. Nobody speaks a language perfectly.** Every grammar ends with a section on how the tongue is spoken badly, by whom, and what the errors reveal. A speaker's first tongue shows through in their second.

### The Family Tree

Two unrelated stocks, and one bridge.
**The Old World stock.** Old Vaross and Khazalid descend from a common ancestor spoken before the Scattering, and both peoples would rather be dead than say so. The evidence is in the numerals and in the body-part roots, which is where relatedness always survives. The Accord's philologists published it four decades ago and were expelled from both the Keth-Gorrum and the Ore Council in the same season.
**The New World stock.** Eressean, and behind it whatever the archaic bloodlines spoke before they crossed the water. Unrelated to the Old World stock at every level.
**Accord Latin is the bridge, and it is nobody's mother tongue.** It was assembled, deliberately, as a neutral instrument for contracts between parties who could not read each other, and every polity in the four quarters has agreed to be measured in a language none of them dreams in. That agreement is the Accord's actual foundation, and it is more load-bearing than any treaty it has ever recorded.

### Document Format

Every grammar follows the same eleven sections so they can be compared and so gaps are visible.
1. Attestation. Who recorded it, when, and whose bias is in the record.
1. Phonology. Consonants, vowels, length, permitted clusters, what is forbidden.
1. Prosody. Stress, timing, and the emphasis mechanism.
1. Morphology. Root shape, derivation, inflection, compounding.
1. Syntax. Neutral order, and what the order does under stress.
1. The semantic architecture. The two or three distinctions the language forces on its speakers.
1. Registers. Street, document, archaic.
1. Dialects.
1. Script.
1. Lexicon, organised semantically.
1. Sample texts with interlinear gloss, and a section on speaking it badly.

### The Build Order

Complete. Old Vaross, Eressean and Varrisak, Runic Dawi, Accord Latin and the Fourteen Hands are all built.
What remains is maintenance rather than construction: the Drow Shadow-Veil notation is described from outside in three places and has no entry of its own, and Mirror-Sign is named as a full manual language with its own grammar and has never been given one. Neither is urgent. Both are real gaps.

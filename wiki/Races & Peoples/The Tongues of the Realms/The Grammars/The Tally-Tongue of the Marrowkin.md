# The Tally-Tongue of the Marrowkin

*A grammar of the tongue of the holds, Tallukieli in its own words and Runic Dawi on the older rolls. The Tongues of the Realms governs.*
> *"A Marrowkin will tell you what he has done and what he has undertaken. Ask him what he will do and watch him reach for a word that is not there."*

They buried the brewmaster Ulvi Rautekk Sarnan at Kegshard with a thousand in the grand hall, the red and copper banners let down from the galleries and every cask in the lower vault tapped in his honor, and his oldest friend Tappo got up to give the reading with his mouth full of funeral pork. He reached the oath-name, the pork went the wrong way, and the close of it stayed in his throat. Raute. One grade down, in front of the widow, the whole hold and the Oath-Breakers' wall. He had time to turn purple. The widow reached him first, with a cask bung, and after her it was everybody, for three days, with anvils in it by the second. When it ended fourteen Marrowkin had broken bones and the hold-clerks sat down to do what the law required, which was enter the entire affair in a wording that scanned. The line is in the Tally. It has the pork in it, in the strong position, and metricians come from Barkstone to admire it.

A people who will break a friend's nose over one consonant have a grammar worth reading.

## I. Attestation

**The Tally.** Every hold keeps one, and nobody should mistake it for a chronicle. It is a list of completed lawful acts and of everything taken from the Body, entered in fixed wording, with the entering itself a lawful act that gets entered. Six thousand years of it exist by its own count and almost none of it is narrative. What the Tally preserves is the exact phrasing in which a thing counts as done, and that phrasing has not been permitted to move.

**The Kegshard oath-record.** Cask-Oaths carved into fermentation barrels, held as long as the brew lives, with the accumulated tallies stored since the Second Eon. This is the best attestation of the oath register anywhere, because a Cask-Oath is inscribed once and cannot be recopied without ending the brew. Every barrel is a text nobody has been able to edit.

**The Oath-Breakers' wall.** Kegshard Hold's grand hall carries the public record of those who broke Cask-Oaths, and its entries are the only body of Marrowkin text written *about* people rather than acts. Philologists value it disproportionately, since it is the sole archive where a name is repeatedly written in the weak grade, which is otherwise never done on purpose.

**The bills of sale.** Every piece of the Body sold, read aloud and copied in two columns, one for the hold and one for the buyer, closing on the Return Clause. Tens of thousands survive in the chests of the people who bought them, unread, and they are the best evidence anyone has for how the clerks' meter moved between one century and the next.

**The Nine-Anvil inscriptions.** Varun work, in Old High Runic, predating the Rite of Bound Geometry. These carry a different resonance from the modern hand and a noticeably older grammar, and the Varun position is that Old High Runic is not an ancestor of the spoken tongue at all but a parallel access to the Lattice, which no other lineage accepts and none has managed to disprove.

**The Accord's compilations.** Better here than on any other tongue, because gradation is audible and the Accord's philologists at Altherion were good phoneticians. Their semantic work is the usual disaster: they recorded the absence of a future tense as a curiosity of style.

### The Old World cognates

The Accord's philologists read the relationship with Old Vaross into the record at Altherion four decades ago and were expelled from both the Keth-Gorrum and the Ore Council in the same season. The evidence sits where relatedness always survives, in the low numerals and the body-part roots.

| Sense | Tally-Tongue | Old Vaross |
|---|---|---|
| one | *ykt* | *ukk* |
| two | *kaht* | *kadd* |
| three | *kolm* | *kholm* |
| hand | *kadu* | *kan* |
| weight, load borne | *kurr* | *gorr* |
| to stand, to not fall | *untar* | *undaar* |

Both peoples have read the table. Neither has commented.

**There are no voiced stops.** *b*, *d* and *g* appear only in Concord transcription of Marrowkin names and are, to a Marrowkin ear, simply the same consonants spoken by somebody who is not paying attention. **Borin** is *Porin*. **Kegshard** is *Kekshart*. The holds have stopped correcting it.

**Vowels.** Eight, in two harmony classes.

| Front | Back | Neutral |
|---|---|---|
| ä, ö, y | a, o, u | e, i |

**Vowel harmony is absolute.** A word draws its vowels from one class only, and every suffix has two shapes to match. This is the constraint that governs naming, and Section IV is where it becomes a political instrument. It also means the plain Common word *rib-bone* has no equivalent: *kylki*, rib, is front and *luu*, bone, is back, so the two cannot join, and the holds say *kylki* alone and let you work it out.

Length is phonemic and written doubled: *kur*, a load. *kuur*, the bearing of a load across time.

**Clusters.** Permitted initially, unlike most Finnic-shaped tongues, and this is held to be the strongest single argument for the Old World relationship. *kr-*, *thr-*, *st-*, *vr-*, *tn-*.

**The under-note.** Generations in the bone have given the speakers a second tone, an octave or so below the voiced word, sounding from the chest wall on every stressed syllable. It carries no grammar. It does carry through stone, which is why a clerk's reading can be followed on the far side of a gallery wall, and why a Marrowkin who has been taught Common by a human still sounds to other Marrowkin as if he were speaking from inside a barrel, and sounds to the human as if the floor agreed with him. Foreign speakers cannot produce it and are not expected to, and its absence marks them from the first word.

## III. Prosody

**First-syllable stress, without exception.** Stress never moves, which means it carries no information and is never used for emphasis. A Marrowkin cannot stress a word to make a point.

**Emphasis is gradation**, and Section IV explains the mechanism. What matters prosodically is that the emphatic device is a *consonant* rather than a pitch or a duration. Old Vaross holds the vowel. Eressean bends the pitch. The Tally-Tongue hardens the consonant, and all three peoples find the other two mechanisms slightly indecent.

**Mathematical Cadence.** Formal speech runs in fixed metrical feet, and the count is not decorative. A statement entered into the Tally must scan, and a statement that does not scan has not been entered, whatever it says. Hold-clerks are trained metricians before they are trained anything else, and a petitioner who cannot scan their own claim hires one who can. Outside practitioners describe hearing the cadence as hearing the grammar of the universe. Marrowkin describe it as the difference between a statement and a record.

## IV. Morphology

### Consonant gradation

**The signature of the language and the load-bearing fact in it.** A stem consonant alternates between a **strong grade** and a **weak grade** depending on the syllable that follows it.

| Strong | Weak |
|---|---|
| kk | k |
| pp | p |
| tt | t |
| k | ∅ |
| p | v |
| t | th |

> *thrukka*, the hold, in the strong grade
>
> *thrukan*, of the hold, weak grade under the genitive suffix
Gradation is invisible as a rule and unmistakable as texture. A Marrowkin hears the grade before they hear the word.

### Names, and what gradation does to them

A full name has four slots: a **given name**, an **oath-name** in the second place, a **line** in the genitive, and a **cut-name**. Before the given name a child has only a shift-name, the birth-shift and a count, *Second-Shift Four*, and nobody thinks this is unkind.

**The given name** is a two-element compound from a fixed inventory, conferred at the First Entry around the seventh year, on the same day the child takes a smear of marrow on bread and has its opening debt entered. The first element is traditionally a word of the Body or the gallery (*hiil-*, ember; *ver-*, blood; *rin-*, breast; *sol-*, collar; *kyl-*, rib; *os-*, a share cut); the second a word of the work (*-run*, *-rin*, *-tar*, *-mo*, *-ja*, *-ri*, *-ppu*). Clerks can no longer agree on what half the elements mean, and families fight over the rare ones.

**The line** is a hearth-line or a parent in the genitive: *Sarnan*, *Vanhanan*, *Kreisan*. A Marrowkin who has moved holds keeps it.

**The cut-name** is earned. It gives the largest single thing its bearer ever took from the Body, in plain words and usually in the speaker's own language: *Sixty Tuns*, *Half a Rib*, *Thimble*. It is not graded, is never a compliment and never an insult, is used by everyone, and goes on the roll of the dead. Older bynames of deed survive from the deep halls and the five Reach branches, among them Straightedge and Sevenhoop, and the Reach still prefer them.

**The oath-name** is the one that matters. It is conferred at oath-taking, and two rules govern it, both structural rather than ceremonial.

**One. The oath-name must harmonize with the given name.** Vowel harmony is absolute, and a compound that mixes classes cannot be spoken. This means **not every oath can be sworn by every Marrowkin**, because not every oath-name will harmonize with the name a person already carries. Holds keep harmony tables. Marriages have been arranged around them. A young Marrowkin with a back-class given name is on a shorter list of possible oaths from the day they are named, and everybody involved knows it.

**Two. The oath-name is spoken in the strong grade for as long as the oath stands.**
> *Ilmo Torvekk Kreisan*: the oath held
>
> *Ilmo Torve Kreisan*: the oath broken
One consonant. Every Marrowkin in the room hears it, in the first syllable of the second name, before that name has finished, and there is no way to say it that conceals it. **Losing an oath-name and breaking the oath are structurally the same event, and nobody hands it down as a punishment**, because the Crystal registers the gap between stated intent and actual behavior without waiting to be told, and the language registers it the same way in the same instant.

A Marrowkin introducing themselves to a stranger is submitting to an audit, every time, in public, in one word.

### Other affixes

| Affix | Function | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **-an / -än** | genitive, of, from the hold of | *thrukan*, of the hold |
| **-ur / -yr** | agent, one who does | *kurrur*, one who bears the load |
| **-ta / -tä** | the completed act, entered | *kurta*, a bearing that is in the Tally |
| **-nur / -nyr** | the completed act, unentered | *kurnur*, a bearing nobody recorded |
| **-me / -mi** | the undertaken, under oath | *kurme*, a bearing sworn to |
| **-us / -ys** | the made thing, the artifact | *thrus*, a cut inscription |
| **-lo / -lö** | the unbegun | *kurlo*, a bearing not yet begun |

### There is no future tense

**The second load-bearing fact.** There is no verb form that says *I will*. The language offers three constructions and none of them is a prediction.
> *kurta*: I bore it and it is in the Tally
>
> *kurme*: I have undertaken it under oath
>
> *kurlo*: it is unbegun
A Marrowkin asked whether they will do a thing answers in the oath form or the unbegun form. There is no third option and no softer one. To say *kurme* is to have sworn, with everything that follows, and to say *kurlo* is to have said nothing at all except that the thing has not started.

This is why Marrowhold's commercial reliability is what it is, and why its diplomacy is so difficult for everyone else. A Concord envoy who wants an assurance is asking a Marrowkin to either take an oath or admit they have not begun. There is no available sentence in which a Marrowkin expresses a firm intention that is not yet binding, and every Marrowkin has spent their life not needing one.

The one place the tongue reaches past the present is the Return Clause, and it does so without a future: *Lainuran palaute on*, the Lender's return stands, an existing fact about an event that has not happened. Clerks consider this the most frightening line in the grammar and read it in the same voice as the price.

## V. Syntax

**Verb-final, subject-initial.** Neutral order is subject, object, verb, the same shape as Old Vaross and one of the quieter arguments for the shared ancestor.
> *Porin thrus kurta.* Porin the inscription bore-and-entered.

**The doer stands at the front and is obligatory.** Every act in the formal register opens on the name of whoever did it. There is no passive, no impersonal and no way to say that mistakes were made. A statement with no doer in it does not scan and has not been said, and the holds' own explanation is the plainest thing in the grammar: when the Body asks, someone has to answer.

**The Tally slot is final and obligatory in the formal register.** Any statement of an act ends by marking whether it is entered. A clause left in *-nur* is a clause admitting the act happened and was never recorded, which in a hold is a heavier admission than most confessions. So every sentence that matters runs from a person to a verdict, and a trained ear hears the doer and waits for the slot. A sentence built in Common, with nobody at the front of it, goes past a hold-clerk like a gap in a wall.

**Word order does not move under stress.** It cannot, because gradation is doing the emphatic work and stress is fixed. Marrowkin under extreme pressure produce syntactically identical sentences to Marrowkin at rest, with different consonants, which is why other peoples consistently misread their agitation and why they consistently misread everybody else's.

**There is a copula and it is used constantly.** *on*, invariant. Vaross omits it, Eressean omits it, and Marrowkin state it explicitly in every predicate, which both neighbors find laborious and which the holds regard as the only honest way to assert that a thing is the case.

## VI. The Semantic Architecture

**One. Entered against unentered.** *-ta* against *-nur*. Every act is either in the Tally or outside it, and the distinction is not about importance. An unentered act is no lesser for it, only unowned, and a hold cannot be asked to stand behind it.

**Two. Undertaken against unbegun.** *-me* against *-lo*, with nothing between. This is the hole the language builds its ethics in.

**Three. Strong against weak.** Gradation is a grammatical process and simultaneously the entire status system. The language never describes a person's standing. It *pronounces* it, every time their name is said, by whoever is saying it, including their enemies. There is no polite grade and no way to grant one as a courtesy.

**Four. Owed.** *velka*, the debt to the Body, takes no plural and no possessive. Each person's share is reckoned, and in the grammar there is one debt, and it belongs to nobody in particular until a doer is placed in front of it.

## VII. Registers

**Out-Tally.** External use, for trade and for Concord contact, called Common Dawi on the older rolls. Gradation preserved, Mathematical Cadence dropped, the doer and the Tally slot optional. This is the only register in which a Marrowkin can leave an act unmarked without saying something, and it is the reason a great deal of the holds' business with outsiders is conducted in it.

**The Tally-Tongue, the Barkstone dialect.** The working language of the holds. Full gradation, full harmony, Tally slot obligatory, cadence in formal contexts. Every phoneme carries material-world resonance, which is the Cask Line's own account of why their brewing chants work.

**Oath register.** Cask-Oath inscriptions and hold-oaths. Fixed wording, strict meter, and a fourth register at Kegshard that is not publicly documented and about which the maintainers have never been successfully asked.

**Old Alchemic Song.** The tonal register of the brewing chants, sung at a pitch that works the liquor.

**Old High Runic.** Sacred inscription, predating the Rite of Bound Geometry, carrying a different resonance and an older grammar in which gradation is not yet regular. Read by Varun rune-forgers and by nobody else with confidence.

## VIII. Dialects

**Barkstone.** The standard. Slow, heavily graded, with the longest vowels of any dialect.

**Varun, the High Line register.** Mathematical Cadence extended out of the formal register into ordinary speech, so that Varun speak in meter continuously. Other Marrowkin find this exhausting and slightly self-satisfied. The Varun are unaware of the impression and would not adjust if they were.

**Low Tally, the Hollow dialect.** Deepkin. **The hollow vocal quality has a physical cause**: sound stops carrying resonance in a Deepkin throat, so the fricatives lose their friction, the under-note goes altogether and the whole inventory shifts toward nasals and stops. Gradation survives and is the only reliable way to read a Deepkin's standing, since the rest of the phonology has flattened.

**Rib-knock.** A tapped code on bone and stone, learned by every child in the Ring before letters, carrying the Tally register at walking pace through a mile of rib. A doubled knock is the strong grade, so a name knocked along a gallery carries its oath with it. Forge Guard officers use it in battle. Shift-mates use it to warn each other about the foreman.

**Mirror-Sign.** A full manual language with its own grammar, neither dialect nor code, used wherever vocal speech would leave an Aetheric signature or acoustic evidence, which for Deepkin is most contexts. It has no gradation, and this is the single most discussed fact about it in the holds: Mirror-Sign is the only language of the people in which a broken oath does not announce itself. Deepkin do not consider this a defect. Every other lineage does.

## IX. Script

**Cut runes, geometric, one of the Fourteen Hands.**

Every character is constructed from straight strokes at fixed angles, and the angles are the meaning rather than the shape being the meaning. A rune cut at the wrong angle is a different rune, or no rune, and it does nothing, which makes the word misspelling far too kind.

**Gradation is written.** The strong grade takes a doubled stroke and the weak grade a single one, which means a name carved into stone or bone carries its oath-status permanently and visibly. A hold's roll of the dead can be read for oath-breaking at a glance, from across the chamber, forever.

**The Rite of Bound Geometry** fixed the angle set. Old High Runic predates it and uses angles the modern hand does not permit, which is why old inscriptions read as slightly askew to a modern eye and why the Varun insist they are not askew at all but correctly aligned to something the Rite stopped describing.

The Soul-Rune practice puts the same script on the body, cut into the ridges of the rise. **An elder's body is a reading of their life**, and the entries are in the Tally register, and they accumulate, and they cannot be revised.

## X. Lexicon

**Weight and bearing.** *kurr*, a load. *kuur*, the bearing of a load across time. *kurrur*, one who bears. *untar*, to stand, to not fall. *thruk*, a hold, a stronghold, and the act of holding.

**Oath and tally.** *kurme*, the undertaken. *tallu*, the record. *tallut*, an entry. *vann*, an oath-name. *vannus*, the conferring of one. *kreis*, a hearth-line, a lineage.

**The Body.** *luu*, bone. *ytin*, marrow. *kylki*, rib. *rintaluu*, breastbone. *solisluu*, collarbone. *veri*, blood. *hehkuveri*, the glow-blood. *lainur*, the Lender. *velka*, the debt. *palaute*, the return. *luuthruk*, bone-hold, the realm. *ytinkreis*, the people's own name for themselves.

**Cutting and making.** *thrus*, a cut inscription. *thrusur*, one who cuts. *nikka*, an angle. *nikkat*, the fixed angle-set. *raut*, worked iron. *rautus*, a made thing of iron. *hiillos*, an ember.

**Brewing and time.** *ollu*, the brew. *ollut*, a brew that has come to age. *kaas*, the cask. *kaasme*, a Cask-Oath. *ikke*, the cycle, the turn of a process.

**Depth and silence.** *sylv*, the deep working. *hollu*, the hollow, the emptied. *hollut*, one whose voice has stopped carrying. *mirr*, the hand-sign.

**Function words.** *on*, the copula. *ei*, not. *ykt kaht kolm*, one two three.

## XI. Sample Texts

**A Tally entry, formal cadence.**
> *Porin Kurrekk Vanhanan thrus kurta. Nikkat on. Tallut on.*
>
> Porin, oath-name in the strong grade, of Vanhan, the inscription bore-and-entered. The angle-set stands. The entry stands.
>
> **Porin son of Vanhan, oath standing, made the inscription and it is recorded. The angles are true. The entry holds.**

**The close of a bill of sale.**
> *Lainuran palaute on. Tallut on.*
>
> Of-the-Lender the return stands. The entry stands.
>
> **Returnable on the Lender's demand. It is written down.**

**The oldest surviving Cask-Oath opening.**
> *Ollu elää, kaasme elää. Ollu kuolee, ei kaasme kuole.*
>
> The brew lives, the oath lives. The brew dies, the oath does not die.
>
> **While it ages, I am bound. When it is gone, I am still bound.**
The second clause is grammatically unusual and every child is taught why: the negative *ei* takes the oath out of the cycle entirely, so the Cask-Oath outlives its own cask. Whether the barrel in Kegshard's deepest vault is being maintained for the brew's sake or because nobody wants to test that line is not discussed.

**From the Oath-Breakers' wall.**
> *Ilmo Torve Kreisan. Kurnur. Kurnur. Kurnur.*
>
> Ilmo, oath-name in the weak grade, of the hearth-line. Unentered. Unentered. Unentered.
>
> **He is named without his oath, and three things he did were never recorded.**
The wall never states the offense. It states the grade and the count of unentered acts, and the holds consider that sufficient.

**A refusal.**
> *Kurlo. Ei kurme.*
>
> Unbegun. Not undertaken.
>
> **No. And do not ask me for the other word.**

## XII. Speaking It Badly

**Concord speakers** voice the stops, producing *Borin* for *Porin* and *Kegshard* for *Kekshart*, and they treat gradation as free variation, which means they call Marrowkin by their oath-names in the weak grade constantly and without noticing. The holds have stopped taking offense and have not stopped hearing it. Concord speakers also reach for a future tense that does not exist, and their attempts to construct one land in the oath form, which is how at least four trade delegations have accidentally sworn. They drop the doer as well, out of a lifetime's politeness, and leave sentences that a clerk simply does not write down.

**Goblinoid speakers** manage the clusters easily and destroy the harmony, since Vaross has none, producing words that mix classes and are simply unpronounceable rather than merely wrong. They also emphasize by holding the vowel, which in a language with phonemic length changes the word.

**Eressean speakers** do the harmony well, the gradation adequately, and the tense system not at all, because the Eressean window system marks whether an act was timely and the Tally-Tongue marks whether it was sworn, and the two do not map. An Eressean speaking it produces beautifully scanning statements about seasons in a language that does not care what season it was.

**Marrowkin speakers of Common** state the copula every time, name the doer every time, mark every act as entered or unentered, and refuse to express intention without swearing, so their Common reads as pedantic, over-precise and evasive in turn. The speaker is using the only distinctions they have, in a voice with a floor under it.

## Notes on the Sources

**On Lattice Tongue.** Varun sources describe Lattice Tongue as foundational Parunic access rather than a language of the people, and it is treated that way here. It belongs with the Descent of Parun, not with this grammar. The Varun claim that Old High Runic is a parallel access to the Lattice rather than an ancestor of the spoken tongue is recorded as their position and is not adopted, since no other lineage accepts it and the numeral cognates point the other way.

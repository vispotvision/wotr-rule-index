# Edictum

## Edictum

> **Migration note.** Kept: the rite's function (a spoken declaration that imposes a condition, command, or judgment on a target or domain by invoking a recognized binding law rather than brute force), its unbreakability short of a greater authority or oath-nullification, its use in trial combat and excommunication, and all four named figures/events. Converted: "Manifestus Class" is reassigned for the same reason as its sibling card Oathrend — current Manifestus is an involuntary emotional flare, and Edictum is a deliberate spoken act. It goes to Claustra (Spirit ↔ Attraction), which already covers "the discipline of making the Sealing [glyph] role carry the whole weight of a working indefinitely" — an Edictum is a lawful imprisonment of a target's behaviour, not a flare. Wellspring: Judicium, the Wellspring of Truth (Fulguria family), whose practitioners "read weakness the way literate people read text" and whose Titan-Keyed art (Dazkar's Mirrored Scale) already runs on real, sworn commitments — the same substrate Edictum needs. Craft Spellcraft (explicitly spoken). "The Judger, Archon of Law" converts to **Urion**, the Primordial-Dyad Archon of Order (*Judicaris* — equilibrium, judgment, cosmic fairness, enforced symmetry), the closest and only current Archon whose portfolio is law and judgment; this is the strongest and most direct proper-noun conversion in this batch. Stage/Grade are not on the card; the effect (binding a target's behaviour under threat of enforced consequence, used in institutional trial rites) sits below Oathrend's Archon-facing scale, so I set the floor at Stage VII, whose own Threshold Catalyst is first Domain-seed activation — logged rather than left as an estimate. **Unattested in current canon:** Valen Dros, the Daughters of Concord, Lirae Ilmashai, the Sanctification of the Inner Courts, the Archonic Trial Rite as a named institution (Archonic Law itself is attested), Seraphiel Moto (the Moto bloodline is attested; this given name is not among its Seven Great Lines and should get a naming-register pass), "Utopian spirit codes" as a named body of law. Parun glyphs Ma, Lo, Ka and Ra are flagged rather than asserted: the card's claimed meaning for Ma ("order/structural invocation") directly collides with the attested glyph [Ma] Memory (confirmed Anamnesis-linked) — that specific claim is dropped from the Codex line rather than carried forward as a near-miss.

### Summary card

**Effect** · A spoken declaration that binds a named target, soul, artefact, or space under a stated condition or judgment; once spoken it holds unless the target outranks the caster's own authority or is shielded by contradiction glyphs or oath-nullification.
**Cost** · Full clarity of soul at the moment of speaking — the working reads the caster's own conviction as its authorization, and any impurity in that conviction is charged back to the caster rather than absorbed by the target.
**Limit** · It targets. It does not reshape reality in general the way a Decretum-class working would, and it cannot bind a target the caster has no lawful standing over.
**Counter** · A target genuinely greater than the caster's source of authority is simply not bound; short of that, a hard oath-nullification or a contradiction glyph placed before the Edict is spoken can pre-empt the seal entirely.
> **What nobody knows.** Why an Edictum spoken in perfect legal form still fails against some targets with no documented authority advantage at all — the Bench's own registers call this "an unresolved authority gap" and decline to elaborate further.

### Design Chain

**Trigger** · The caster speaks a condition, command, or judgment against a named target, soul, artefact, or space, backed by a recognized source of binding authority (Realmic, guild, or Wellspring).
**Function** · Fixes a boundary condition at the target rather than delivering force to it — the target's own behaviour is required to conform at that boundary from the moment the Edict lands.
**Mechanism** · Boundary-condition enforcement, the same real mechanism every Parun glyph runs on at smaller scale: a glyph moves a limit and the underlying law does what it has always done on the far side of the new limit. Edictum is that principle scaled up to a full spoken sentence rather than a single glyph, with Judicium supplying the discernment that decides exactly where the new limit sits.
**Numerical Effect** · At Stage VII / A-Grade, binding a single named target to a stated condition costs an estimated 50,000–70,000 EU and takes effect the instant the sentence completes; binding a space or a domain-scale target runs toward 90,000 EU and holds for as long as the caster's authority over that ground remains uncontested.
**Target Response** · The target experiences the Edict as an external limit rather than a compulsion from inside — Concord casework describes it as "being told a wall is there" rather than being pushed against one.
**Consequence** · The named condition holds. A sentenced target cannot perform the forbidden act; a protected target cannot be touched by the named harm; a silenced target cannot cast the named Craft — until the Edict is lifted by the same or greater authority that set it.
**Limitation** · The working cannot originate an obligation the way Oathrend's field does — it enforces an existing authority relationship rather than inventing one, and against a target with no lawful subordination to the caster it does nothing.
**Weakness** · Full clarity of soul is a real precondition, not flavour text — an Edictum spoken in anger, doubt, or bad faith backfires onto the caster's own Wellspring rather than landing on the target, producing what casework calls an Echo Reversal.
**Cost** · EU per the Numerical Effect line, plus the standing risk of Echo Reversal if the caster's conviction was not genuinely clean.
**Counterplay** · Establish standing greater than the caster's own source of authority before the Edict is spoken, or have contradiction glyphs or an oath-nullification already in place — nothing stops a landed Edict except a rank the caster cannot claim.

### Origin

Urion (converted from the card's "the Judger, Archon of Law") originates the form at the cosmological scale; the card gives three mortal derivations of the same working (Valen Dros, the Daughters of Concord, Lirae Ilmashai, and the historical Edictum of Silence attributed to Seraphiel Moto), all unattested individually and kept because the card's history does not resolve without them. As a documented, taught working with an Archonic origin, it is counterable by anyone who has studied a prior case and by nothing else short of superior standing.

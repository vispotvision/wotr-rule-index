# Invert Eidolon

## Invert Eidolon

---

### What it is

Invert Eidolon is the deliberate summoning of a construct built from what a practitioner's Soul Crystal has refused to integrate rather than from what it has resolved. Ordinary Animatria shapes a small detached fragment of the summoner's own Essence Core into a structured presence that carries the summoner's harmonised Wellsprings outward as a guardian, a companion, or a scout. Invert Eidolon fabricates that same fragment from the opposite material. A suppressed Trait. A Wellspring harmonic the Crystal never finished reconciling. An unprocessed fracture from a Temperance Stage the summoner survived without closing. The result is called an Anti-Eidolon in the trade. It inherits the inversion of whatever the summoner's true Eidolon would have carried: where a harmonised construct extends the summoner's resolved nature, an Anti-Eidolon extends the unresolved one, and it does not automatically recognise its own source as an ally. Left unbound, it behaves the way any Animatria construct behaves when its tether runs to a hostile or contradictory intent. Erratically. Often against the summoner who raised it.
The practice sits outside guild sanction almost everywhere it is known. The mechanism itself is unremarkable; the raw material is the problem, a Crystal fracture the practitioner has not finished surviving. Fractured spiritbinders and Temperants pursue it anyway, usually looking for a closure that ordinary Temperance progression will not hand them on any faster schedule.

### Where it sits in the current system

**Category** · Animatria, Spirit-Aligned. Per The Magical Categories, an Animatria construct is a detached fragment of the caster's own Essence Core given temporary independent operation, which is why the discipline fails outright inside a Silence Realm: sever the tether and the construct has nothing left to be.
**Family / Physics Domain** · Limina, Entropy, Void and Mind. The load-bearing Wellspring is **Eidolyn**, the Shaping Mind, which gives a mental construct enough material solidity to hold a shape at all; a practitioner attempting Invert Eidolon without Eidolyn harmonisation has nothing to build the Anti-Eidolon's form from. **Oblivara**, the Dark Mirror, and **Nihiloth**, the Hollow Law, are the secondary harmonisations that give the construct its inverted, refusal-shaped character rather than a merely weaker copy of the summoner.
**Craft** · Spellcraft. The construct holds only as long as the summoner's attention and tether hold, per the standing Craft durability table; nothing about Invert Eidolon fixes it the way a Runecraft inscription would.
**Stage floor / Grade** · Stage VIII, Transcendence, S-Grade minimum. The Temperance Gate for an Animatria construct capable of independent tactical action, rather than a reflexive extrusion that dissolves in seconds, is Stage X per The Spirit Summoning Arts; a construct that only manages contradiction and hostility without full tactical autonomy is placed at the Stage VIII floor for semi-autonomous forms. Below that Gate, the working still produces an Anti-Eidolon, but not one stable enough to survive contact with anything actively opposing it, which matches the card's own description of summoners losing control.
**Essence cost** · Not stated on the card. Per The Spirit Summoning Arts' Animatria cost table, a construct of this complexity sits at the upper end of the 75,000 to 150,000 EU fabrication band with a sustained cost in the 10,000 to 25,000 EU per second range; this is the conservative reading consistent with the card's account of mass casualties among summoners who lost control of multiple Inverted Eidolons at once.

### Practitioners and history

Karo Venrik, the Foolish Magus, Echo-Binder of the Ma Sigil, is the one figure named on the card who is confirmed in current canon. His documented Animatria suite runs through Nexus networks of fully harmonised constructs, and his own sheet records that of the Echoes he has fielded across eleven years, two never came back in a form he could confirm was still what left. The card's claim that he unintentionally triggered a fractured, refused construct during early trials has no confirmation anywhere else, and nothing on his sheet contradicts it either; it is kept here as an open, unattested thread rather than an established fact.
The remaining figures and events the card names, Thessan and her bound Anti-Eidolon Vel-Muriah, Silvantes Mournthresh of a "Riven Choir," the Twilight Concord where the practice is said to have first surfaced, an "Era of Calamity" and a mass-casualty incident called the Wellspring Fracture of Nalisem, and fringe preservationist cults called the Mirrorwake Ascetics and the Occultinarii of the Hollow Eye, have no page or card anywhere in current canon. None of them are load-bearing for what the discipline does mechanically, so they are kept as unattested colour rather than struck outright, on the understanding that a review pass may cut them.

### What it can and cannot do

An Anti-Eidolon can fight, obstruct, or simply exist as a standing liability, depending on how much of the summoner's refused material went into it and how well the summoner's Attraction Layer can still recognise something built from what they would rather not have integrated. It cannot be relied on to defend its summoner by default; per the mechanism, it inherits inversion of affinity as well as of function, and "hostile unless bound or redeemed" is the honest baseline rather than a worst case.
It cannot function at all once the tether back to the summoner's Essence Core is severed. That limit holds for every Animatria construct. It cannot be produced by a summoner who has not survived the Stage the fracture came from, because the raw material has to already exist in the Crystal; the discipline finds a refusal, it does not manufacture one. Closing it out through ordinary dismissal is not guaranteed to work the way dismissing a harmonised Eidolon would, because the construct is drawn from unresolved rather than resolved material. A summoner who raises one should expect that ending it may cost as much as raising it did.

#### 1 · Physical account (physics)

**The phenomenon in plain words.** A part is cast onto a template, from material
that still carries the locked-in stress of a process that was never finished. The
casting comes out sound, holds a shape, passes every inspection a shape can be
given — and it holds a quantity of stored energy that nobody put there today,
pointed in a direction nobody chose, waiting on a scratch.
**The real phenomenon.** Two laws in series, and the working is the second one
running inside the first.
*Templated nucleation, which is the ordinary discipline.* A supersaturated medium
will not organise itself; given a pattern it deposits along the pattern's geometry
with real strength. This is Eidolyn's own analogue and the register states the
consequence better than a physics text would: *"The construct's solidity is real
material behaviour rather than illusion, and its failure modes are the failure
modes of a poorly designed casting."* A construct is a **casting**, and the whole
argument of this account is that the page's discipline is a casting made from the
wrong billet.
*Residual stress, which is the inversion.* A part cooled faster than it can
relax locks in a stress field that no external load supplied: the skin goes into
compression, the core into tension, and the two hold each other in place. The part
is not damaged. It is, in many directions, *stronger* than an annealed one — and it
carries elastic strain energy at a density of
**U = σ² / 2E**
which for toughened glass at a surface compression of 120 MPa and a Young's modulus
of 70 GPa is **U ≈ 1.03 × 10⁵ J·m⁻³**, about a hundred kilojoules in every cubic
metre, supplied by the quench and by nothing since. A Prince Rupert's drop is the
demonstration that has been performed for four centuries: strike the bulb with a
hammer and nothing happens; nick the tail and the entire object disintegrates,
the fracture front running through it at roughly **1.5 × 10³ m·s⁻¹**, so a
two-metre body comes apart in about **1.3 milliseconds**. Nobody supplied that
energy. It was in the glass, from the cooling, the whole time.
*The criterion that decides whether it holds.* **Griffith**, σ_f = √(2Eγ/πa): a
crack runs when the elastic energy released by its advance exceeds the energy
needed to make new surface. In a part with residual tension the released term is
already there, so the flaw size required to start the failure is small, and it does
not scale with anything the operator does. **This is the mechanical reason the
page's baseline is hostility rather than weakness.** An Anti-Eidolon is not a
feeble construct. It is a sound construct with a stress field in it, and the
direction the stress was locked in is the direction it will fail — which is to say,
toward the fracture it was cast from.
**The governing law.** **Templated solidification with a defective billet**, and
**Griffith's energy-release criterion** governing when the stored field is spent.
Eidolyn's own register names the first half and its Failure names the consequence:
*"Template defect. Any flaw in the intent is faithfully reproduced at scale, and
the practitioner is rarely able to see the flaw until it is standing in front of
them."*
**Measurable quantities, SI.** The page states a Stage floor, a Grade and — by
reading the Discipline's own table — a cost band, which makes this one of the
better-quantified entries in the section. What the law supplies as checkable
consequence:
- **Raising it and putting it down are not the same operation, and the page is
right about which is worse.** Fabrication supplies the template. Dismissal has to
**absorb** the stored field, because a residual stress does not disappear when you
stop looking at it; it goes somewhere. The page's closing line is the physics
stated in a guild's voice: *"A summoner who raises one should expect that ending
it may cost as much as raising it did."* That is unusual and it is correct: for an
ordinary Eidolon, dismissal is ceasing to supply, and costs nothing.
- **Strength in every direction but one.** A residually-stressed part resists
general loading better than an annealed one and fails catastrophically from a
specific small defect. So the working is simultaneously hard to fight and easy to
break, and the two facts are not in tension — they are the same fact seen from two
angles. An opponent who trades blows with an Anti-Eidolon is loading the skin in
compression, which is what the skin is for.
- **Delayed, unpredictable failure with no external trigger.** Stress-corrosion
cracking and delayed fracture in toughened glass both fail *later*, from causes
too small to have been recorded. The page's *"Erratically. Often against the
summoner who raised it"* is the behaviour of a part with a stress field, not the
behaviour of a malevolent one, and the distinction matters: **nothing has to want
anything for this working to kill the person who performed it.**
- **The material is an inventory item, not a mood.** A quench is a specific event
with a specific geometry. The page is precise about this and it is the best
sentence on the page: *"the discipline finds a refusal, it does not manufacture
one."* Which fracture, from which Temperance Stage, at what angle, is a
fact about the practitioner that predates the working — and is therefore lookable
up by anyone who knows their history.
**The energy budget, and where the joules were.** **In the quench.** The
practitioner supplies the template and the tether; the construct's capacity to act
against its source is the strain energy of a Temperance Stage that closed badly,
stored since it closed. `Two Sets of Books` §III is satisfied in its cleanest form
in this section: *"The practitioner does not supply the joules. They supply the
boundary condition."* The boundary condition here is *what the template may draw
on*, and the joules were paid years ago by a person surviving something.
**Precedent we are not copying.** The shadow-self summon is a whole genre: Persona's
Shadows, *Jujutsu Kaisen*'s cursed spirits condensed out of negative feeling,
*Fullmetal Alchemist*'s rebound, every Jungian confrontation where the double must
be accepted to be defeated. All of those make the construct **a meaning** — it
represents something, and it is beaten by understanding it. This one is a **casting
defect with a documented complication rate**: it represents nothing, it is made of
the same Crystal as its summoner with a different stress state, and understanding
it does not discharge it any more than understanding a Prince Rupert's drop
softens the glass. The divergence that matters for scenes: the tense conversation
is not with the construct. It is the **inventory** — a practitioner going through
their own unclosed Stages deciding which one to build out of, and the one other
person in the room who knows the list.

#### 2 · Stratal account (metaphysics)

**Aether stratum.** Draw is in two parts and the Discipline states both: a
**fabrication** charge at *"75,000 – 150,000 EU per eidolon depending on
complexity"* and a **sustain** at *"10,000 – 25,000 EU per second"*
(`wiki/The Disciplines/The Spirit Summoning Arts.md`:42). The page reads its own
working at the upper end of the first band, which its reasoning supports.
**Residue:** the construct *is* the residue while it stands — a detached fragment
of the summoner's own Core walking around outside them — and the page's whole
hazard is that the residue outlasts the intention. **Saturation:** live, and the
Discipline names the shape: *"Multiple simultaneous constructs multiply strain on
the Aether Shell and risk Essence depletion cascade."* The page's mass-casualty
claim rests on exactly that and the arithmetic in §4 says it cannot be paid for.
**Wellspring stratum.** **Eidolyn · *The Shaping Mind***, Family **Limina**. Its
law, verbatim:
> **Analogue** · *"Templated nucleation. Ordered deposition onto an imposed
> pattern."*
> **Mechanism** · *"A supersaturated medium will not organise itself, but given a
> template it deposits along the template's geometry with high fidelity and
> considerable strength. Eidolyn makes intent the template. The construct's
> solidity is real material behaviour rather than illusion, and its failure modes
> are the failure modes of a poorly designed casting."*
> **Stat Effect** · *"Strengthens Ardency Density in summoned constructs, Ardency
> Inscription, Gnosis Memorium. Mental constructs gain material solidity."*
> **Failure** · *"Template defect. Any flaw in the intent is faithfully reproduced
> at scale, and the practitioner is rarely able to see the flaw until it is
> standing in front of them."*
> (`wiki/The Eight Families & the Sixty Wellsprings/Limina — Entropy, Void and Mind.md`:54)
**The Failure is this working's definition rather than its risk.** Every other
Animatria discipline treats *template defect* as the thing to avoid; Invert Eidolon
**selects for it** and then supplies the material the defect will be reproduced
from. And the register's last clause is the page's own account of what goes wrong:
*"the practitioner is rarely able to see the flaw until it is standing in front of
them"* is a sentence about an unclosed fracture becoming visible by walking into
the room. The two books say the same thing and neither was told.
Both secondaries are doing identifiable and different work, and the page is right
about both:
- **Oblivara** *(Limina)* — *"Information survives a noisy channel when it is
encoded with enough redundancy that corrupted symbols can be reconstructed from
the surviving ones, and a corner reflector returns incident energy precisely along
the path it arrived on"* — supplies the **inversion of affinity**. A retroreflector
returns along the incident vector; a construct built under Oblivara returns
whatever reached it to its source, which is the mechanism behind the page's
*"it does not automatically recognise its own source as an ally."* Its **Failure,
*code exhaustion*** — *"past it the reconstruction is confident, complete, and
wrong"* — is the best available account of a construct that has drifted into being
a coherent someone else.
- **Nihiloth** *(Limina)* — *"It cancels the law already present, and what remains
inside is not a rule but the absence of one"* — supplies the **refusal-shaped
character **the page asks for, and its** Failure is symmetric**:
*"Everything the practitioner brought inside is subject to the same null,
including the parts of themselves they were relying on."* A summoner who builds
from what their Crystal refused has brought that refusal inside a working that
cancels law, and the parts of themselves they were relying on are in the billet.
*Four Theories.* **Residue**, and it is the only one of the four that reaches this
working. There is no consent — the material never agreed to be integrated, which is
why it is available; no correspondence — nothing external is contacted, which is
what separates Animatria from Vocatia; and the debt is not to anybody. What there
is, is **a thing left behind that has not finished being left behind**, and that is
the Residue theory's whole subject.
**Essence stratum.** All three layers, and the page's precision here is its
strongest suit. The **Essence Core** is the billet: *"A suppressed Trait. A
Wellspring harmonic the Crystal never finished reconciling. An unprocessed fracture
from a Temperance Stage the summoner survived without closing."* The **Attraction
Layer** is the tether and the reason the discipline dies in a Silence: *"an
Animatria construct is a detached fragment of the caster's own Essence Core given
temporary independent operation, which is why the discipline fails outright inside
a Silence Realm: sever the tether and the construct has nothing left to be."* The
**Aether Shell** carries the strain, which the Discipline states as the cost of
multiplicity. Developmental Tier at the Stage VIII floor: **Radiant** (Part
Twenty-One). Crystal State: for the one attested figure, *"Fractured along the
succession, and still actively fracturing"*
(`Karo Venrik · The Foolish Magus.md`:34). **The invoice:** an EU band, a crushing
per-second sustain, a dismissal that may cost what the raising did, and — the page's
own — a piece of the summoner walking about that has not agreed to come back.
**The lens: the shattering of the vessels, and the husks.** Isaac Luria's
cosmology, transmitted through Chaim Vital's *Etz Chaim* (c. 1573). In the Lurianic
account the divine light was poured into vessels that could not hold it; the vessels
broke — *shevirat ha-kelim* — and the fragments fell, each still holding a spark of
the light it failed to contain. Those fragments are the ***qelippot***, the husks:
not nothing, not evil in themselves, but **broken containers with something real
trapped inside them**, and they have a parasitic existence sustained entirely by the
spark they hold. That is an Anti-Eidolon described four centuries early and in
better words than the trade has: a construct made of a vessel that broke at a Stage
the summoner survived, standing up and moving because of the part of them that is
still inside it.
Where the lens misleads, and where it kills the school that holds it, is
***tikkun***. The doctrine's answer to the husks is repair: the sparks are to be
lifted out, and the husk, emptied, simply ceases — evil is not fought, it is
*unfunded*. A school reading Invert Eidolon through Luria therefore believes the
construct can be **redeemed into dissolution**, and the page gives them just enough
encouragement to try: *"hostile unless bound or redeemed"* is the honest baseline it
states. So the school does not put the thing down. It keeps it standing while it
works on it, extracting, negotiating, waiting for the spark to come loose. **The
error is the timescale.** *Tikkun* in Luria is the work of all Israel across
history, not of one Temperant across an afternoon; and a construct under Oblivara
is gaining coherence the entire time it is held — *"past it the reconstruction is
confident, complete, and wrong."* The school that refuses to destroy what it
believes it can redeem is holding the door for **code exhaustion**, and what walks
out is not the summoner's spark returned. It is a confident, complete, wrong
reconstruction of a person, standing in the room, with the summoner's own history
in it. Offered as a school's error, not as canon.

#### 3 · Mechanism (the effect)

**Glyph (proposed; the page names none).** `[Frm]` **Form** as the boundary, laid
under `[Vel]` **Mind** for the register the template reads from. Both from the
thirty unclassified forms, and the Index's reason applies: *"Each names a kind of
limit, applicable to any law at all."* Eidolyn's law runs continuously in any
practitioner who has harmonised it — intent is the template, always — and what this
working changes is **not the law and not the template's fidelity but the set of
contents the template is permitted to draw on.** `[Frm]` Form is that limit;
`[Vel]` Mind names where the contents are being read from, which is the difference
between a guardian and an Anti-Eidolon.
*Filing decision, not canon — Isaac's ruling (`SA-GAP-GLYPH-MIRROR`).*
**What boundary moves.** **What counts as available material.** An ordinary
Animatria working draws on what the Crystal has resolved, because resolved
structure is what the Core offers up when asked. The boundary moves to include the
unresolved: the suppressed Trait, the unreconciled harmonic, the fracture from a
Stage that closed without closing. Nothing about Eidolyn changes. The template
still deposits *"along the template's geometry with high fidelity and considerable
strength"* — and that is the problem, because high fidelity applied to a billet
with a locked-in stress field reproduces the stress field.
**What the law then does on its own.** It casts, faithfully, and the faithfulness
is the whole of the harm. Eidolyn does not know that this billet is different from
the last one; it deposits to the pattern and hands over a construct with real
material behaviour. The stress field comes across with the material, because that is
what fidelity means. Then the construct stands there holding a hundred kilojoules a
cubic metre of stored strain that nobody supplied today, oriented the way the
original quench oriented it — **toward the thing the summoner refused** — and
Oblivara's retroreflection points its returns back along the vector they came in
on, which for a construct tethered to a summoner means back at the summoner. None
of that requires an intention. The page's *"Left unbound, it behaves the way any
Animatria construct behaves when its tether runs to a hostile or contradictory
intent"* is precisely right, and its *"Erratically"* is the honest word for a part
that will fail from a defect too small to have been logged.
**Law V check.** The floor is **Stage VIII, Transcendence**, above Refraction, so no
external anchor is required and the glyph chain is internal (`The Four Crafts`, Law
V). The gate closes and it closes against the working: **there is nothing to take
away.** No circle, no catalyst, no spoken invocation, no wrist to break. Of the
routes in `Counterplay` II, *"Interrupt the chain"* is unavailable here by the
system's own rule, which is why the counters that work against this discipline are
all field and tether rather than boundary.
The page's Stage floor is well reasoned and reads straight off the Discipline:
*"The Temperance Gate for an Animatria construct capable of independent tactical
action, rather than a reflexive extrusion that dissolves in seconds, is Stage X per
The Spirit Summoning Arts; a construct that only manages contradiction and
hostility without full tactical autonomy is placed at the Stage VIII floor for
semi-autonomous forms."* The Discipline says *"Stage VIII (Transcendence) for
semi-autonomous forms"* and the reading is exact. **What the floor does not gate is
the accident**, and the page's own history is mostly accidents — which is the
finding at `SA-NUM-INVERT-FLOOR-VERSUS-ACCIDENT`.
**Failure mode — Structural fault, and it is the only entry in this section where
the fault is the intended operation.** *"The Crystal could not hold what the
boundary permitted"* (`Two Sets of Books` §VII). The boundary permitted the Crystal
to lend out material it had specifically declined to integrate, and the Crystal
cannot hold what it has already refused to hold — that refusal is the material's
defining property. So the working's success condition and its structural fault are
the same event, and the page's *"summoners losing control"* is not a complication
rate. It is the operation completing.
A second fault, and it is the one with the numbers on it: **Field fault at the
sustain.** *"The room could not supply what the working assumed."* At 10,000 to
25,000 EU per second, with no Aetheric Density ceiling stated anywhere in the system
to test it against (`SA-GAP-AETHERIC-DENSITY`), the sustain is the largest
continuous draw in this section by three orders of magnitude, and §4 shows that no
attested reserve in the corpus pays it for a minute.
**What the target sees and feels.** If the target is an opponent: something that
fights like a sound thing, because it is one — a construct with real material
behaviour, stronger under general load than a fair copy would be, that does not
tire, does not flinch, and cannot be reasoned toward its summoner's interest
because it does not hold its summoner's interest. It will be beaten by a small
specific insult rather than by a large general one, and an opponent who has not
worked that out will spend the fight loading the skin in compression and wondering
why nothing lands.
If the target is the summoner — and the page says that is the ordinary case — what
they see is **their own history, cast at high fidelity, standing up.** Not a
symbol of it and not a memory of it: the same material, deposited to the same
pattern their intent supplies, carrying the stress of the Stage they survived
without closing, pointed the way that Stage pointed. The page's *"it does not
automatically recognise its own source as an ally"* describes it from outside. From
inside it is worse and quieter: the thing is tethered to them, which means it is
*theirs*, which means it is not a stranger doing this. And Eidolyn's Failure has the
last word on what the moment is like — *"the practitioner is rarely able to see the
flaw until it is standing in front of them."*
**What bleeds, at the stated efficiency.** The one attested figure, **Karo Venrik**,
is at **Stage V** with **η ~0.50** (card, *"per the Tier of Standing 5, Expert
figure"*) and is three Stages below the working's floor. At the floor itself the
Tier of Standing is **6 · Master**, η **0.70–0.80** (Part Nineteen): **20 to 30
percent** of the expenditure leaves as heat, sound and structural bleed. Against the
sustain that is **5,000 to 7,500 EU per second, continuously**, and under **R44-1**
(1 EU = 1 MJ, `RULINGS.md` 2026-09-25) that is **5.0 to 7.5 × 10⁹ watts** of waste,
every second the construct stands. A human body at rest dissipates on the order of
**10² W**. The system does not say where the bleed goes, and at a factor of
**5 × 10⁷** over the only dissipation path a practitioner has, that omission stops
being a detail. `SA-PHYS-INVERT-SUSTAIN-HEAT`.

**The Counterplay routes that work**
(`wiki/The Magic System/Counterplay What Beats a Practitioner.md`).
- **Deny the field — Silence, and it is total.** *"Animatria constructs fail
completely because their continuity runs back to a source the Silence has
severed."* The Discipline says the same thing twice and the page says it a third
time. This is not a degradation and not a saving throw: sever the tether and *"the
construct has nothing left to be."* Note the register's field diagnostic, which is
how a player finds out whether the quiet room is the right kind of quiet: *"A soul
held in one reports the sensation as loneliness rather than weakness, and that
distinction is the most reliable field diagnostic for whether a quiet place is
Silence or merely thin."*
- **Break the body — exhaustion, and the clock is in seconds.** *"Below ten percent
reserve a practitioner suffers Essence Starvation: reduced stat expression, Shell
degradation, Traits flickering. A defender who can afford to spend nothing wins a
long engagement against an attacker who cannot afford to spend nothing."* The
register's usual caution — *"A plan that consists of surviving until he tires is a
plan to be killed by a tired man"* — is the one place in this section where it does
not apply, because the sustain is **10,000 to 25,000 EU every second** and §4 puts
the largest attested reserve in the corpus at about a minute for one construct and
twenty seconds for three. Against this discipline, waiting is the plan.
- **Break the man — Rigidity, which is the counter that requires reading.**
*"A Trait's law is not negotiable by its bearer, and the more absolute it is the
less he can contradict it. Present him with the situation his own law handles worst
and he will handle it that way regardless… This is the cruellest counter in the
register and it requires knowing the man rather than the technique."* An
Anti-Eidolon is built from **one specific refusal**, and its stress field is
oriented by that refusal (§1). So the construct's single small fatal defect is
known in advance to anyone who knows what the summoner would not integrate — and
the page says that is discoverable, because *"the discipline finds a refusal, it
does not manufacture one."* It was in the history before it was in the working.
- **Break the boundary — not available, and the system says so.** Stage VIII is
above Refraction, so *"the chain is internal and none of this applies."* There is
nothing to interrupt, no anchor to take and no Seal to force. Worth stating,
because it is the route a player will reach for first and it is closed by Law V
rather than by the page.
- **Wait, if the summoner is the target.** The page's own baseline is that the
construct is hostile to its summoner, and Oblivara returns along the incident
vector. An opponent who has worked out what is happening has the option of doing
nothing at all and letting the working complete — with the caveat in the next
paragraph, which is a genuine open question rather than a plan.
**The tell, stated plainly.** There is no chant, no circle and no gesture: Stage
VIII is above Refraction and the chain is internal. What there is, is the thing
itself — a construct that is recognisably made of something its summoner will not
own, standing where everyone can see it, behaving erratically toward the one person
in the room who raised it. Anybody who has read the summoner's history knows which
Stage it came out of and therefore where to nick the tail. Anybody who has not will
spend the exchange hitting the strong direction.
**The open question this section will not answer.** The page's baseline is that the
construct acts against its summoner; the Discipline's rule is that the construct
ceases to exist the moment the tether to that summoner is severed. Whether an
Anti-Eidolon *can* kill the thing it is made of, and what happens to it if it does,
is not stated anywhere, and it decides whether the third route above is a counter or
a way of losing slowly. `SA-EM-INVERT-HOSTILE-TO-ITS-OWN-TETHER`.

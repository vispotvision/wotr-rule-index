# Oathrend

## Oathrend

### Summary card

**Effect** · Forcibly breaks a sworn oath, soul-pact, blood rite, or divine contract, and the break registers across every plane the oath was enforced on — everyone bound to it feels the fracture.
**Cost** · The caster's Resilience Oath sub-stat takes the working's full weight; breaking a vow through force rather than release costs the caster something structurally identical to what the vow itself would have cost to keep.
**Limit** · It only works on a real, standing commitment. The working reads actual reciprocity and cannot invent an obligation where none exists — there must be a vow in force to unmake.
**Counter** · A commitment mirrored under Dazkar's own law (a Purganeth-sworn oath) reflects the breaking back on whoever attempted it in exact kind, undiminished; an ordinary oath has no such reflection and simply comes apart.
> **What nobody knows.** Why an oath sworn under duress shatters cleanly while one sworn freely and later regretted tends to leave the worst Echo Scars — the variable is not sincerity, and nobody has isolated a second one.

### Design Chain

**Trigger** · A standing oath, pact, or contract the caster has standing to address (their own, or one they have been given cause and authority to attack) is named and directly confronted.
**Function** · Imposes an antiphase counter-field against the oath's own binding architecture rather than attacking the parties to it — the working targets the Claustra seal, not the souls it holds.
**Mechanism** · Destructive interference. A Claustra-class bond is a standing field held open by a continuous claim; Oathrend generates a field of equal magnitude and opposite phase across that same anchor point, and the two sum to zero the way two opposed waves cancel rather than fight. The seal does not weaken. It opens.
**Numerical Effect** · At Stage IX / S-Grade, cancelling a personal-scale oath costs an estimated 90,000–130,000 EU and completes in under a second once the counter-field locks; a multi-party or divinely-witnessed pact runs toward the top of the range and can require a sustained cast of several seconds to fully null every anchor point.
**Target Response** · Every party still bound to the oath feels the cancellation simultaneously, reported consistently as a felt absence rather than pain — "the world momentarily registers the vow's absence like a wound."
**Consequence** · The vow's consequences unwind across every plane they were enforced on. What the oath was holding together (a truce, a bloodline claim, a Realm's stability) is no longer held by anything, and whatever else was load-bearing on that hold now has to carry its own weight or fail.
**Limitation** · The working cannot manufacture an obligation — it reads and cancels a real, standing Claustra seal and does nothing at all against a target with no oath currently in force.
**Weakness** · A Mirrored-Scale-class working (Purganeth's own reciprocity law) turns the caster's own counter-field back on them in exact kind; this is the one documented case where the caster, not the oath, absorbs the working.
**Cost** · EU per the Numerical Effect line, and a Resilience Oath toll on the caster equal to what honouring the broken vow would have cost.
**Counterplay** · Establish the oath under Purganeth-witnessed reciprocity before it can be targeted, or catch the counter-field before it locks — once it locks, the seal opens regardless of who is watching.

As a named, taught technique it is counterable by anyone who has studied a prior Oathrend case, per the named-inventor rule.

#### 1 · Physical account (physics)

**The phenomenon in plain words.** A standing field that has been holding two or
more things together is met, at the place it is anchored, by a second field of
the same size and the opposite sign — and in the region where both are present,
there is nothing.
**The real phenomenon.** **Active noise control**, and specifically the
engineering constraint that governs it, which the page does not state and which
turns out to be the whole of its scaling law.
*The principle.* Paul Lueg's 1936 patent and Olson and May's 1953 "electronic
sound absorber" both rest on the same sentence: to remove a wave you do not
absorb it, you add a second wave at equal amplitude and opposite phase, and the
sum at every point is zero. Nothing is attenuated. Nothing is blocked. The
energy does not go anywhere; it is simply not present as a net disturbance where
the two fields overlap. This is Nihiloth's Core Law verbatim: *"Two waves in
antiphase do not conceal one another. They sum to zero, and the region between
them carries no energy at all."*
*The constraint, which is where all the interesting content is.* Cancellation is
only achievable over a **region** if the anti-field can be made to match the
target field at **every point in that region**, and that requires either one
source per anchor or a wavelength long compared to the region's size. This is
why noise-cancelling headphones work superbly at 100 Hz and fail above about a
kilohertz — the ear cup is small compared to a 3.4 m wave and large compared to a
0.3 m one — and it is why industrial active control is done duct by duct rather
than room by room.
**That constraint *is* the page's own scaling clause.** *"A multi-party or
divinely-witnessed pact runs toward the top of the range and can require a
sustained cast of several seconds to fully null every anchor point."* Every
anchor point. One anti-field per anchor. The cast takes several seconds because
it is placing sources, not because it is pushing harder.
*The thing the analogy also says, and the page does not.* **Cancellation is not
destruction.** Two counter-propagating waves sum to zero in the overlap and then
continue on their way, undiminished, and the instant either source stops the
other is fully present again. An active noise control system that is switched off
does not leave a quiet room. It leaves the noise. This is the single most
important physical fact about the mechanism the page has chosen, and it is
incompatible with the page's stated Consequence — see §3 and
`SA-EM-OATHREND-NULL-VERSUS-UNWIND`.
**The governing law.** **Linear superposition**: two fields present in the same
region sum, pointwise, and neither is altered by the summing. That is the whole
law, and its power and its limit are the same sentence.
**Measurable quantities, SI.** The page gives EU and a duration and no physics.
What the law supplies as checkable consequence:
- **Cancellation quality goes as phase error.** Two equal-amplitude fields at a
relative phase error φ do not sum to zero; they sum to **2 sin(φ/2)** times one
field's amplitude. A 10° error leaves **17 %** of the original amplitude
standing — about 15 dB of cancellation, which is what good headphones achieve.
A 60° error leaves **100 %**: at sixty degrees off, the cancellation has
achieved literally nothing. **The working degrades as a sine, not as a
fraction**, which means it is near-perfect right up until it is worthless, and
the failure is abrupt rather than graduated.
- **Sources scale with anchors, not with strength.** Nulling an N-party pact
needs N anti-fields correctly placed, and the difficulty is geometric rather
than energetic. A weak oath held at six anchors is harder than a ferocious one
held at one.
- **Required anti-field energy equals the target field's.** Matching amplitude
means matching energy density, so cancelling a field of energy U costs the
caster a field of energy U, in the overlap region, for as long as the overlap
is required. **This is the only working in the batch whose physical account
says the caster must supply as much as the target holds** — which makes it a
very poor candidate for `Two Sets of Books` §III's usual comfort that the
joules were already in the room.
**The energy budget, and where the joules were.** Partly the caster's, and that
is the anomaly. Every other working in this batch moves a boundary and lets the
ambient field pay; superposition does not work that way, because an anti-field
of matched amplitude is an anti-field the caster has to produce. The system's
own resolution is available and the page does not take it: the anti-field could
be drawn from the Aether stratum like any other expression, with the Crystal
supplying only the **phase**, which is the cheap part and the hard part. Stated
that way the books balance. Stated as the page states them, they do not.
**Precedent we are not copying.** Oath-breaking is a standard fantasy beat and
is almost always moral: the villain forswears, the magic punishes, the geas
snaps. This one is **surgical, third-party, and indifferent to the parties'
consent** — the working *"targets the Claustra seal, not the souls it holds"* —
and its most disturbing property is that it works on oaths the caster has nothing
to do with. The divergence is that Oathrend is **an instrument of liberation and
of atrocity with no mechanical difference between the two cases**: the
Ashblood Apostates' standing liberation doctrine and the destruction of a
Realm's stabilising truce are the same working, correctly performed, and the
page says so without flinching.

#### 2 · Stratal account (metaphysics)

**Aether stratum.** Draw is large and brief for a personal oath — *"completes in
under a second once the counter-field locks"* — and large and **sustained** for a
multi-anchor pact, *"several seconds"*, which is the batch's only working whose
cast time is set by the *target's topology* rather than by the caster's speed.
**Residue:** severe and distinctive. A cancelled oath's anchor points carry the
trace of two fields rather than none, which is how a Concord investigator
distinguishes an Oathrend from an oath that was lawfully released — and it is
also the reason the page can say the break *"registers across every plane the
oath was enforced on."* **Saturation:** relevant here as almost nowhere else,
because a matched-amplitude anti-field against a divinely-witnessed pact is a
very large local expression. The Core Vocabulary's saturation danger — *"excessive
Essence floods an area faster than it can disperse … produces Crystal Fracture
Events in everyone exposed, including the person who caused it"* — is a live
risk for this working and neither page mentions it.
**Wellspring stratum.** **Nihiloth · *The Hollow Law***, Family **Limina**,
Physics Domain entropy, void and mind. Its law, verbatim:
> *"Two waves in antiphase do not conceal one another. They sum to zero, and the
> region between them carries no energy at all. Nihiloth builds Domains on that
> principle, which is why a Nihiloth Domain does not impose additional law. **It
> cancels the law already present, and what remains inside is not a rule but the
> absence of one.**"*
> (`wiki/The Eight Families & the Sixty Wellsprings/Limina — Entropy, Void and Mind.md`)
Stat Effect: *"Strengthens Dominion Sovereignty, Resilience Persistence, Gnosis
Cognition. The practitioner imposes law through negation."* All three appear on
the page's FOW line or fall out of it.
**And then the Wellspring's failure, which the page does not carry and which is**
**the largest thing missing from this account:**
> *"Failure · The cancellation is symmetric. Everything the practitioner*
> *brought inside is subject to the same null, including the parts of themselves*
> *they were relying on."*
Superposition does not know whose field is whose. An antiphase field placed
across an anchor point nulls **whatever is at that anchor point**, and a caster
whose own oaths, bonds or Domain touch the same anchor loses them in the same
instant. The page's Weakness names only the Purganeth mirror. Nihiloth's own
register names a cost that applies to **every** casting, and it is not on the
page (`SA-PHYS-OATHREND-SYMMETRIC-NULL`).
Secondary, named by the page's Weakness and attested: **Dazkar**, *the Arbiter of
Purganeth*, of the Spiritual Seat, whose law is *"Proportion, restitution,
penance"* (`wiki/The Fourteen Titans/The Spiritual Seat — Omnis, Florwyn, Dazkar,`
`Nysera, Sylorin, Ilgrimm.md`). Purganeth is a **Realm**, not a Wellspring, and an
oath *"mirrored under Dazkar's own law"* is realm-witnessed rather than
Wellspring-harmonised — a distinction the page blurs and this account keeps.
*Four Theories.* **The Debt Position.** *"The Wellspring answers on credit and
the account is settled somewhere the practitioner cannot see. … impossible to
test, and disturbingly good at predicting which practitioners come apart"*
(`Two Sets of Books` §IV). Every other working in this batch reads better under
Consent. This one does not: a working whose whole subject matter is obligation,
whose cost is *"structurally identical to what the vow itself would have cost to
keep"*, and whose open question is why freely-sworn vows leave the worst scars,
is a Debt working in everything but the Accord's enforced label.
**Essence stratum.** Active layer: the **Attraction Layer** — Claustra is a
Spirit ↔ Attraction category and an oath is a Fate-Plane object, *"bonds, oaths,
grudges, every act of recognition runs as a current here"* (`Core Vocabulary`
§I). The **Aether Shell** produces the anti-field. The **Essence Core** is where
the bill lands: the page charges **Resilience Oath**, which is a Sub-Stat and not
a reserve, and charges it *"equal to what honouring the broken vow would have
cost."* Developmental Tier at the Stage IX floor: **Sovereign** — *"Trait law and
Domain law begin to be the same law"* (Part Twenty-One), which is the correct
tier for a practitioner whose working is a Domain-grammar operation performed as
a technique. Crystal State: not stated. **The invoice:** unusually well drawn —
EU per the Numerical Effect line, **plus a Sub-Stat toll priced against the
broken vow's own weight.** A cost that scales with what you destroyed rather than
with how hard you pushed is the right shape, and it is rare.
**The lens: promises as an artificial virtue.** Hume, *A Treatise of Human
Nature* III.ii.5. Hume's argument is that a promise creates no natural
obligation whatever: there is no act of the mind — no willing of an obligation —
that could do the work, because willing a thing does not make it so, and the
supposed inner act is *"one of the most mysterious and incomprehensible
operations that can possibly be imagined."* What binds a promise is a **human
convention**, a form of words that a society has agreed to treat as binding
because a society in which promises hold is better off than one in which they do
not. On Hume's account an oath is architecture: built, maintained, and in
principle dismantleable, with nothing underneath it but the fact that people keep
building it. **Oathrend is Hume proved on a bench.** A working that opens a seal
without touching the souls it held demonstrates that the seal was a structure and
not a state of anyone's heart, and the page's own framing — *"the working targets
the Claustra seal, not the souls it holds"* — is the Humean thesis stated as an
engineering note. Where a rival lens misleads, and misleads expensively, is the
**canonist** reading: the power of the keys, *whatsoever thou shalt loose on
earth shall be loosed in heaven*, systematised in Gratian's *Decretum* into the
doctrine that a vow may be dissolved only by the authority competent to have
received it. A school holding that will read every Oathrend as a **usurpation of
jurisdiction** and will hunt for the *warrant* behind it — whose authority did
this practitioner claim? — and there is none, because Oathrend is not an act of
authority at all. It is a field operation. The canonist inquisitor asks the one
question the mechanism cannot answer, concludes the practitioner must be shielded
by some higher bench, and escalates into a jurisdictional quarrel while the actual
counter — get there first and swear under Dazkar — sits unread in the Titan
register. Offered as a school's error, not as canon.

#### 3 · Mechanism (the effect)

**Glyph (proposed; the page names none).** `[Knt]` **Knot** · Counter · Maelor,
one of the only two Counter forms in the entire Index, laid across `[Lei]`
**Binding** — the Index's own worked example of a Root *"attested in two
Wellsprings and one Family … the whole of it belongs to Limina"*, which is
Oathrend's Family exactly. The Index's reason for there being only two Counters
is the reason Knot fits: *"A glyph cannot attack a law. … What a counter does
instead is impose a boundary that **prevents another boundary from being
established **— a condition on the conditioning."* A Claustra seal** is** a
standing boundary condition, and Oathrend prevents it from continuing to be
established. `[Lei]` is quoted from the Index's How-to-Read section rather than
from the mirrored thirty, which is a weaker citation than the rest of this batch
uses and is flagged (`SA-GAP-GLYPH-MIRROR`).
*Filing decision, not canon — Isaac's ruling.*
**What boundary moves.** **The phase of a field at an anchor point.** Not the
oath's strength, not the parties' willingness, not the seal's authorisation —
one angle, set to π, at each place the oath is pinned. That is the entire
intervention and it explains both the working's precision and its indifference:
phase is a property of the field at a point, and a field at a point does not know
whose promise it is.
**What the law then does on its own.** It sums. *"A Claustra-class bond is a
standing field held open by a continuous claim; Oathrend generates a field of
equal magnitude and opposite phase across that same anchor point, and the two sum
to zero the way two opposed waves cancel rather than fight. **The seal does not
weaken. It opens.**"* Superposition is not a contest and does not have a winner;
it is an arithmetic fact about what is present where. The seal is not overcome,
overpowered or outranked. In the region where both fields are present, the net
claim is zero, and a claim of zero is not a claim.
Then the consequences propagate outward without anyone driving them, which is
the part the page gets exactly right: *"The vow's consequences unwind across
every plane they were enforced on. What the oath was holding together (a truce, a
bloodline claim, a Realm's stability) is no longer held by anything, and whatever
else was load-bearing on that hold now has to carry its own weight or fail."*
Everything downstream of the seal was standing on the seal. Remove the support
and the structure finds out what it was resting on, which is a purely mechanical
result and needs no further agency.
**And here is where the mechanism and the effect part company.** Superposition
does not remove a field; it produces a null **while both fields are present**.
The page's own Wellspring says so: *"They sum to zero"* — sum, not vanish. Switch
off the anti-field and the oath's field is entirely undiminished, because nothing
was ever done to it. Yet the page has the cancellation *"complete in under a
second"* and the consequences *"unwind"* permanently. Either the null is held
forever, which no page says and which would make Oathrend a sustained working
rather than a one-second one, or something other than destructive interference
destroys the bond. Logged `SA-EM-OATHREND-NULL-VERSUS-UNWIND`, with options, and
not resolved here.
**Law V check.** The floor is **Stage IX, Invocation**, two full Stages above
Refraction, so no material anchor is required (`The Four Crafts`, Law V). The
page nonetheless specifies **a spoken renunciation**, and unlike Edictum — which
keeps its voice for institutional reasons — nothing here explains why. The gate
closes, but loosely, and the practical consequence is that **covering a
practitioner's mouth does not stop an Oathrend at Stage IX**; the page's own
Counterplay knows it and offers a timing counter instead: *"catch the
counter-field before it locks."*
**Failure mode — Structural fault, with a Field fault behind it.** *"The Crystal
could not hold what the boundary permitted. Overchannel, Backlash, Fracture. The
working succeeds and the practitioner does not"* (`Two Sets of Books` §VII). The
page's named Weakness is the clearest Structural fault in the batch: a
Mirrored-Scale working under Dazkar's law *"turns the caster's own counter-field
back on them in exact kind; this is the one documented case where the caster, not
the oath, absorbs the working."* An anti-field of matched amplitude returned
intact to its source is the caster's full expenditure arriving at the caster,
which is Backlash by definition.
The **Field fault** sits behind it and neither page names it: a matched-amplitude
anti-field against a divinely-witnessed pact is a very large expression placed
very quickly in one location, which is the Core Vocabulary's definition of
**Aetheric Saturation** — *"Crystal Fracture Events in everyone exposed,
including the person who caused it."* Nulling a great pact in a crowded room
should be dangerous to the room, and no page says it is.
And the third, from the Wellspring rather than the page: **the cancellation is
symmetric.** Whatever else of the caster's touches that anchor is nulled with it.
**What the target sees and feels.** Not pain, and every account agrees on this:
*"reported consistently as a felt absence rather than pain — 'the world
momentarily registers the vow's absence like a wound.'"* Every party still bound
feels it at the same instant, wherever they are, because the anchor is a
Fate-Plane object and distance is not one of its properties. What they lose is
not a feeling about each other. It is the **enforcement**: the thing that made
the vow carry mechanical weight rather than sentimental weight, which per
Benediction's Core Law is a measurable affinity figure the Continuum reads. After
an Oathrend the affection may survive perfectly intact and the *obligation* is
gone, and the two are not the same and the parties usually find that out
separately and later. Then the second-order failures start arriving: the truce
that was holding because of the vow, the claim that was legitimate because of it,
the Realm whose stability was load-bearing on it. **Nobody attacks any of those
things.** They stop being held up. The page's *"has to carry its own weight or
fail"* is the whole of the working's violence and none of it is delivered.
**What bleeds, at the stated efficiency.** No practitioner has a card
(`SA-GAP-OATHREND-NO-CARD`). At the Stage IX floor the Tier of Standing is
**6 · Master**, η **0.70–0.80** (Part Nineteen): **20 to 30 percent of the
expenditure leaves as heat, sound and structural bleed.** At the 130,000 EU
ceiling that is **26,000 to 39,000 EU of bleed**, delivered in under a second for
a personal oath, into the same room as the anchor — which is the saturation
concern above, stated in the ledger's own currency.

**The Counterplay routes that work**
(`wiki/The Magic System/Counterplay What Beats a Practitioner.md`).
- **Break the boundary — swear it under Dazkar, and the working eats itself.**
The page's own Counterplay: *"Establish the oath under Purganeth-witnessed
reciprocity before it can be targeted."* Dazkar is **the Arbiter of Purganeth**,
of the Spiritual Seat, whose law is *"Proportion, restitution, penance"*
(`wiki/The Fourteen Titans/The Spiritual Seat — Omnis, Florwyn, Dazkar, Nysera,`
`Sylorin, Ilgrimm.md`) — and the return is *"in exact kind, undiminished"*,
which for a matched-amplitude anti-field means **the caster receives the full
expenditure back at themselves. **Note the price: this must be done** at the
swearing**, months or years before anyone thinks of breaking it. Preparation
pays, and it is the only thing that does.
- **Break the boundary — catch the lock.** *"Catch the counter-field before it
locks — once it locks, the seal opens regardless of who is watching."* That
window is **under a second** for a personal oath and **several seconds** for a
great pact, which inverts the usual intuition: **the bigger the oath, the
longer you have.** The physical reason is the one-source-per-anchor constraint,
and a reader who works it out knows that the way to interrupt an Oathrend
against a many-party pact is to disturb **any one anchor**, because a partially
cancelled field is not a partially cancelled oath. Compare the register's *"Force
the Seal early."*
- **Break the boundary — phase error, which is free and is not on any page.**
Superposition's residual is **2 sin(φ/2)**: sixty degrees of phase error leaves
the field entirely intact. Anything that makes the oath's own phase *move*
during the cast — a party to it acting on it, renewing it, invoking it —
presents a moving target to a working that must match to within a few degrees.
This is derived from the stated mechanism rather than stated, and it is the
kind of thing a player who reads Nihiloth's Core Law carefully can reason their
way to.
- **Deny the field — Silence.** *"The removal of magical possibility from a
region. … Lawful transformation cannot be authorised at all inside one."*
Oathrend needs to place a large matched field at a specific anchor; a Silence
Realm denies the placement outright. It also, note, denies the oath's own
enforcement while it lasts, so a Silence is a truce for both sides of the
question.
- **Break the man — Coherence, and the Oath toll.** The working charges
**Resilience Oath** every time, *"equal to what honouring the broken vow would
have cost."* A practitioner who does this often is a practitioner whose own
capacity to be bound is being spent down, which is `Counterplay` IV's *"the
vow"* and *"Coherence drift"* arriving on the same schedule: **an Oathrend
specialist is progressively less able to swear anything**, and an opponent who
notices does not need to fight them at all. *"You do not have to cause this. You
have to notice it and wait."*
**The tell, stated plainly.** A renunciation spoken aloud, if the caster bothers
— and at Stage IX they need not. Otherwise the first anyone knows is the felt
absence, simultaneously, in everyone still bound: *"the world momentarily
registers the vow's absence like a wound."* Which is a tell that arrives exactly
one instant too late to be useful, and is the reason the only real counters to
this working are bought in advance.

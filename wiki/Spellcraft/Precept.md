# Precept

## Precept

---

### What it is

A Precept is a working that does not ask to be believed. It asks to be obeyed, and it is built so that obedience is not really optional for whatever falls inside the clause it names.
Where an ordinary rune fixes a shape, a Precept fixes a rule. The practitioner does not carve an effect into the world so much as file an instruction with it, in the Continuum's own legible grammar, and the instruction outlives the hand that wrote it the same way a Guild seal outlives the clerk who struck it. This is why the older registers called the practice a kind of enshrining: the law is not enforced from outside afterward. It is load-bearing from the moment it is sealed.
A Precept is not persuasion and it is not a threat. It is closer to a boundary condition. Other workings, other oaths, and in the right circumstance other people, do not defy a Precept so much as discover that the space it named will not hold the shape they were trying to give it.

### Where it sits in the current system

**Category** · Glyphica primary (the Authorization and Sealing glyph roles, spoken of in the Unified Taxonomy as the syntax by which the Continuum "recognises and authorises transformation"), Votia secondary where the Precept functions as a binding compact between parties rather than a standing law.
**Wellsprings** · Fixatio (the anchoring law: what a Precept holds, it holds) and Judicium (discernment and verdict, the currents Sanctum Lux's own instrument reads by).
**Family** · Materia (Fixatio) with a Fulguria current (Judicium).
**Craft** · Runecraft. Per the current Craft/durability split, Spellcraft holds only while attention holds and Runecraft is fixed and degrades like any inscription; a Precept that is meant to found a guild code or a dynasty's law cannot be an art that lapses when the caster looks away.
**Where it is filed** · The Magical Categories page (Glyphica, entry eight) and the Guild Accord material for the compacts a Precept is used to write. No standalone Precept entry exists yet; this card is the seed of one.

### Practitioners and history

**Urion, Archon of Balance**, informally called the Judger by every Attestant who is taught to strike the word on their second day and keeps using it anyway, is the figure most plausibly behind the Precept's "first act upon descending": a reference against which other things are measured rather than a petitioned god, and the natural author of a foundational Precept if any Archon wrote one. Whether Urion personally inscribed the Twelve Precepts of Fate is not recorded.
**Lysandra Velkaris** is said to have authored the Precept of Hierarchic Ascendance for Highspire's chain of command. Highspire is the summit housing the Lawbell and the seat of the Highspire Choir of Celestials, Verdict-Bearers by function, fitting ground for a Precept of rank and hierarchy.

### What it can and cannot do

A Precept can found a standing law inside its declared sphere: a guild code, a spiritual compact, a geasa that binds a named party rather than the world at large. It can outlast its author, the way an inscription outlasts the mason, because Fixatio does not care who is still standing.
It cannot compel what falls outside the clause it names. A Precept written for one sphere has no purchase past its stated boundary, and a working that reads the Precept's shape before it seals can in principle decline to be caught by it. It also cannot be argued out of existence by force alone, only by the same register it was written in: a Precept is unmade by a correction to the law, not by breaking the stone it is cut into. Rescind, the withdrawal-of-consent effect attested elsewhere in the corpus, is the clearest documented counter to a soul-bound Precept, and only when the Precept was never lawfully consented to in the first place.
**The governing law.** **Constraint rather than force**: constraint forces do zero virtual work. **Band structure** in a periodic medium supplies what the constrained region looks like to anything inside it, and **defect-state localization** supplies the only route out.
**The quantities.**
| Quantity | Working | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Standing upkeep of a constraint | virtual work of constraint forces | zero, by theorem |
| Gap width, quarter-wave stack of n₁ = 1.5 and n₂ = 2.3 | (4/π) · arcsin[(n₂ − n₁)/(n₂ + n₁)] = (4/π) · arcsin 0.211 | 27% of the centre frequency |
| Allowed states opened by a correction | one per defect | countable |

**Upkeep is zero, and that is a theorem.** A Precept can outlast its author the way an inscription outlasts the mason, because Fixatio does not care who is still standing. A Precept that cost anything to maintain would be an enforcement, not a constraint.
**Sharpness scales with regularity, not with the author.** Reflectivity at a gap frequency rises exponentially with the number of periods in the stack, so the sharpness of a Precept is set by how regularly the clause was laid down, not by how strong the practitioner who laid it was.
**The gap has edges.** A band gap forbids one range and is transparent everywhere else. A Precept cannot compel what falls outside the clause it names, and one written for one sphere has no purchase past its stated boundary: a constraint deletes the degrees of freedom it names and no others.
**One defect, one state.** A lawful correction opens one allowed state at one site. A single exception does not soften a Precept generally; it makes one thing possible at one place.
**Where the joules are.** In the substrate, paid once, and none of it in the holding. The inscription costs what laying down a periodic structure costs, and the constraint costs nothing thereafter. A Precept is a boundary condition and nothing else, which is also why its counter is the counter to any inscription: the whole cost sits in the substrate, and a substrate can be chiselled.

#### The stratal account

**Aether stratum.** A single front-loaded draw at sealing and then nothing: **zero standing draw.** **Residue** is heavy, located and intended: the inscription is the Residue, and it is the thing an opponent can find and touch. **Saturation** is not a hazard. A working with no sustained expenditure deposits nothing continuously, so a Precept can be filed in a place people have to live in, which is why the guilds use it for codes.
**Wellspring stratum.** **Fixatio, the Binding Flame**, Family **Materia**.
| Wellspring |  |
|---|---|
| Analogue | Covalent cross-linking; thermoset cure |
| Mechanism | A thermoplastic can be melted and reformed indefinitely. A thermoset, once cured, has formed covalent bridges across its whole network and will char before it flows. Fixatio performs the cure. What it holds is held not by force but by the fact that the alternative configuration no longer exists as a reachable state. |
| Stat Effect | Strengthens Vitality Fortitude and Resilience Anchoring. What is held by Fixatio stays held. |

Fixatio's Mechanism is the band gap and the holonomic constraint in one sentence. **Its Failure is the Precept's bill.** A Precept written a little too wide is a permanent feature of whatever it was written on; it costs nothing to keep being wrong, and the clerk who wrote it will be dead long before anyone notices which cases it caught. It cannot be argued out of existence by force alone, and that is a cost as much as a strength.
**Judicium, the Wellspring of Truth**, of Fulguria, is the second current and does the half Fixatio cannot. Every element absorbs and emits at wavelengths fixed by its own electron structure, and no substance can present another's spectrum; Judicium tunes Gnosis to read the characteristic lines of a Crystal, a technique or a lie. **Fixatio sets the gap; Judicium sets its edges**: which cases fall inside the clause, which is the whole craft of drafting one. Judicium's Failure, spectral overload, in which every object in view reports at once and nothing can be prioritised, is the failure of a Precept drafted badly: every case reads as caught, and a law that catches everything has stopped discriminating.
**Read under the Consent Doctrine.** A Precept filed over a sphere needs no consent. A Precept filed over a person, a geasa binding a named party rather than the world at large, is void without it, and Rescind, the withdrawal of consent, exists as its remedy because the Doctrine does.
**Essence stratum.** For a Precept filed over a sphere, no layer of the subject's is touched: the working is in the substrate, which is what makes it Runecraft. For a soul-bound Precept the active layer is the **Essence Core**, since a standing law held in the Core is what the system calls a Trait; the Votia reading and the Rescind counter both attach to that case and not the other. At the Stage VI floor the Developmental Tier is **Harmonic**.
**The advocates' reading, and the direction of the remedy.** A school of Accord advocates holds that a norm is valid because it was made in accordance with a higher norm, in a hierarchy of authorisations ending in a basic norm that nobody posited but everyone presupposes, the way a coordinate origin is presupposed. Validity is not efficacy: a norm nobody enforces is still valid, and a command everybody obeys is not a norm unless it was correctly authorised. The school recognises Glyphica's Authorization and Sealing roles at once as its own hierarchy written in glyphs, and it reads a Precept correctly as neither persuasion nor threat but an instruction filed with the Continuum in its own legible grammar. It misleads on the remedy. On the school's reading every norm is challengeable upward, so it trains advocates, files against the clause, cites the Accord above the guild and the Archon above the Accord, and is very good at it, and the space still will not hold the shape for the whole length of the litigation. A band gap has no superior to whom the wave may appeal. The actual remedy runs the other way, downward and local: one defect, at one site. A school that spends three generations litigating upward is beaten by a mason with a chisel and a good idea about where to put the notch. **A school, not a mechanic.**

#### The mechanism

**Craft.** Runecraft. It is fixed in a substrate and degrades as any inscription degrades; a Precept meant to found a guild code or a dynasty's law cannot be an art that lapses when its author looks away. It is beaten as Runecraft is beaten, by the substrate, and not as Spellcraft is, by interrupting a chain.
**The glyph.** **Boundary**, the limit that moves, laid under **Order**, the register the clause is filed in. Each names a kind of limit applicable to any law at all, which is what a working that files one clause against workings, oaths and people at once requires. **Lock**, which occupies a limit so no other limit can be set there, is the same shape used as a reply; a Precept is a first move, so it is built on Boundary.
**What boundary moves.** **The set of configurations reachable inside a named sphere.** Not the strength of anything, not anyone's willingness, not what is permitted: what is available. Fixatio performs the cure and the alternative configuration stops being a reachable state; Judicium places the clause's edges. Everything after that is a structure sitting in a substrate.
**What the law then does.** Nothing, continuously, and nothing is the exact description. A constraint does no work. The region is not watching, testing or deciding; it has fewer degrees of freedom than it had, and every process inside it runs correctly within what remains. A working that would have taken the forbidden shape does not fail. It **completes in the nearest available configuration.** An oath that cannot be sworn in the forbidden terms is sworn in terms that are available; a rank that cannot be claimed is claimed in the form the clause leaves open. A guild seal outlives the clerk who struck it, and what a Precept produces is not obedience but **a shape in everything downstream of it**, visible for generations in what a place's workings all have in common.
**The limits.** It cannot compel what falls outside the clause it names. A working that reads the Precept's shape before it seals can decline to be caught by it. It cannot be argued out of existence by force alone, only by the same register it was written in. It cannot grant dominion or rule, which remains Coronation's separate function.
**Why the floor is Glory.** A Precept files a clause that other workings, oaths and people must fall within, which takes A-Grade authority over a sphere, and Stage VI is the first Stage at which A is reached. Below Refraction a working needs a material anchor, and a Precept always has one: the substrate is the anchor, and it is also the whole of the working. Nothing changes at Refraction, because a Precept is never internalised.
**Failure: the clause edge in the wrong place.** The condition was set imprecisely and the law runs somewhere unintended. The consequence is Judicium's spectral overload on an institutional scale: a clause a little too wide catches cases nobody intended, costs nothing to keep catching them, and cannot be undone by wanting it undone. **The characteristic disaster of this working is not a backlash. It is a century of correct rulings nobody wanted.**
**Failure: institutional drift.** A Precept holds its clause whatever the guild beneath it has become, so a house running a two-hundred-year-old Precept is running a stated law its lived behaviour may have left long ago, and that Coherence drift can be read off the archive rather than off a person.
**What the subject feels.** Over a sphere: nothing, ever, and that is the working. There is no pressure, no resistance and no moment of being stopped. A practitioner reaches for the forbidden shape and the working comes out in the nearest available one, and unless they have read the clause they experience it as their own preference. Soul-bound, it is worse. A standing law in the Essence Core is a Trait, and the Rigidity Effect applies: the more absolute a Trait's law, the less the soul can safely contradict it. A person carrying a Precept does not feel constrained. They feel like themselves, and the shape of what they can want has an edge in it that was put there by a document.
**What bleeds.** At Stage VI (Expert) η runs 0.60 – 0.70, so 30 to 40 percent of the sealing expenditure leaves as heat and structural bleed into the substrate and the room, once. The standing bleed is zero: constraint forces do no work, so there is no standing expenditure to take a fraction of.

#### Counterplay

**Break the boundary: the substrate.** A Precept is Runecraft standing on a substrate and is beaten the way Runecraft is beaten: damage the substrate. It is not argued with. It is chiselled off, or the wall it sits on is brought down, or the third layer is scraped through by somebody who knows which layer to scrape. The whole cost sits in one physical object, paid once, with nothing spent to defend it, which makes the object the fight. An inscription with a defective Sealing role has been failing continuously for as long as it has stood, and is a liability to whoever owns it, not a defence.
**Break the boundary: the defect.** A Precept is unmade by a correction to the law, not by breaking the stone it is cut into, and a correction is one defect in the periodicity: **one allowed state, at one site, with the rest of the law intact.** The route is neither destruction nor appeal but a single, narrow, precisely placed exception written in the same register as the clause. Whoever controls where the notch goes controls the Precept.
**Break the boundary: Rescind, against a soul-bound Precept without consent.** Rescind, the withdrawal of consent, is the clearest counter to a soul-bound Precept, and only when that Precept was never lawfully consented to. A Precept filed over a sphere is not touched by it, and a Precept lawfully consented to is not either.
**Break the boundary: countersign.** Some Seals end when countersigned in writing, and some when the sum is spoken back correctly by another. A working carrying either closing condition can be ended by an opponent literate enough, which is why Accord field practice avoids both and why they persist in the old inscriptions everywhere. The Precepts most worth beating, the old guild and dynastic ones, are exactly those inscriptions.
**Read the clause before it seals.** A working that reads the Precept's shape before it seals can decline to be caught by it. This needs Judicium or its like, and the window lasts exactly as long as the drafting does.
**Break the institution: read the drift.** A Precept holds its clause while the house beneath it changes. An opponent who reads the archive finds where the house's stated law and its lived behaviour have parted, and presses there.
**The tell.** There is no cast, no chant, no light and no pressure, and nothing to notice in the moment; nobody experiences being stopped. The aftermath is the tell, and it is documentary: **an inscription somewhere and a pattern in the record**, a sphere where every working of one kind has come out the same odd shape for a suspiciously long time, and at the bottom of it a substrate with a clause cut into it and a Sealing role that may or may not have been drafted by somebody competent. A Precept is found by a reader, not a witness.

# Scission

## Scission

> **Migration note.** Kept: the rite's function (severing Trait from soul, Essence from body, memory from identity, or Wellspring from host), its status as a guild/Concord last resort, its use in exorcism and emergency Temperance failure, and both named practitioners. Converted: "Ritus Class" is now Category Ritus (Spirit-Aligned, formal and ceremonial per current taxonomy — this one did not need reassignment, unlike its sibling cards below), Family Limina, Wellspring Dissolution, Craft Spellcraft. The card's "Wellsprings of Verdiction, Fixation, and Echo" (law, form, memory) convert to Judicium, Fixatio and Anamnesis, the current Wellsprings governing those same three things. Stage floor, Grade, governing stats and EU figures are not on the card; I set them at the conservative end consistent with a rite gated behind trained Concord/guild practice rather than raw power — Stage VI, Grade A — and logged that choice below rather than writing "estimate." **Unattested in current canon:** Kujo Valorin, Zethyra the Veinwrought, the First War of Silence, the Soulquake of Tirres, the Fracturing of Edenis (Edenis itself is attested, Florwyn's realm; the named event is not), and the Pactless as a named Outer World doctrine (the Outer World quarter is attested; the sect is not). Parun glyphs Ci, Ra and Ka do not appear in the portion of the 136-form Master Glyph Index retrieved for this pass; only Th (Foundation, attested Petralon/Vectoria) is confirmed, so the others are flagged rather than asserted and should get a full glyph-index pass before this entry ships.

### Summary card

**Effect** · Cuts one bound metaphysical thing free of another — Trait from soul, Essence from body, memory from identity, Wellspring from host — without destroying either half.
**Cost** · Full ritual apparatus (circle, offering, catalyst) and a toll on the caster proportional to how tightly the two things were bound; the caster's own Resilience Ward takes the first backlash if the cut is forced rather than found.
**Limit** · The removed piece does not reliably return whole. What comes back is fragmentary, amnesiac, or resonant-altered more often than not.
**Counter** · A working that reads the target bond's shape before the cut completes can refuse to let the separation take — Fixatio-anchored bonds resist longest, since Fixatio is the Wellspring that makes a hold stay held.
> **What nobody knows.** Why some cuts leave a Trait Echo behind and others leave nothing at all — the variable tracked in guild registers is neither the caster's Stage nor the target's, and three centuries of Concord casework has not found a fourth candidate.

### Design Chain

**Trigger** · A bond between two metaphysical components has become corrupted, overloaded, or is actively killing its host, and someone with standing to invoke the rite chooses to cut it rather than let it run.
**Function** · Locates the interface where the two components are joined and imposes a boundary condition across it, then holds that boundary open long enough for the two halves to separate on their own.
**Mechanism** · Solvation, the real phenomenon Dissolution runs on. A bound state separates not because the rite attacks the bond directly but because the rite raises the entropy of the mixed, bound configuration above the entropy of the two components held apart — once the separated state is thermodynamically preferred, the process completes on its own and does not need to be forced the whole way through.
**Numerical Effect** · At Stage VI / A-Grade, a full seven-step rite (Intention → Space → Circle → Offering → Invocation → Catalyst → Seal) severs a bond of up to Stage VI density in roughly six to twelve minutes of ritual time, at an estimated 40,000–60,000 EU depending on how tightly the two components had fused; a bond forged under duress or over decades sits at the top of that range.
**Target Response** · The host feels the cut as a structural absence rather than pain — Concord casework logs it as "the wrong kind of quiet." Whichever half is more load-bearing to the host's identity destabilizes first.
**Consequence** · One thing becomes two. Neither is destroyed, but neither is exactly what it was; the separated piece carries fragmentation, amnesia, or altered resonance more often than a clean parting.
**Limitation** · Ritus is performed, not built — it cannot be improvised mid-combat, and the full apparatus (circle, offering, catalyst) must be assembled before the cut is attempted or the rite fails outright rather than partially.
**Weakness** · A bond stabilized by Fixatio resists the entropy gradient the rite depends on; forcing it anyway multiplies the backlash the caster eats.
**Cost** · EU per the Numerical Effect line, plus a standing toll on the caster's own Resilience Ward proportional to how forced (versus found) the cut was.
**Counterplay** · Read the bond's shape before the Seal step completes and refuse the boundary condition — this is the one window where the separation can be declined rather than merely survived.

### Origin

Not a single inventor's technique — the card frames it as standing guild and Concord doctrine, refined rather than discovered, with the two named practitioners (Kujo Valorin, Zethyra the Veinwrought — both unattested in current canon and kept because the card's history does not make sense without them) representing opposite ends of its use: mass, forbidden severance at one extreme and precise, careful curse-extraction at the other. As a documented rite rather than a self-derived technique, it is counterable by anyone who has studied the Concord's own casework on it.

# Transposition

## Transposition

---

### What it is

Transposition is a Ritus that exchanges one thing's position, in whatever sense of position the rite is built to move, for another's. At its plainest it swaps two bodies' locations. At its most developed it swaps a death for an echo, a memory for a physical object, or a soul for the housing of a mirrored construct, because "position" under Transference's law is not limited to a point in physical space; it covers any coordinate the Plane of Fate recognises as belonging to something, including the coordinate a fate or an outcome currently occupies.
The mechanism is Spatium's Transference read straight: two systems tuned alike exchange without a conductor between them, and the coupling falls off sharply with separation, which is why every reliable account of Transposition describes it working best at short range or through a prepared anchor rather than across an open field. Tacticians, illusionists, soulbinders, and scholars of causal sequencing favour it because the same mechanism that lets a duelist trade places with a decoy also lets a soulguard trade places with something that needs purifying from the inside, or lets a scholar of consequence trade the wrong end of a chain of events for the right one, if the coupling can be established at all.

### Where it sits in the current system

**Category** · Ritus, Spirit-Aligned, performed rather than built, with a **Conjunction** alignment tag where the rite pairs two specific targets rather than displacing a single one into open space.
**Family / Physics Domain** · Spatium, Spatial Geometry. Primary Wellspring **Transference**, the Living Exchange. Secondary harmonisations **Transmutatio**, the Law of Change, for workings that trade identity rather than mere position, and **Fixatio**, the Binding Flame, of Materia, for the Anchor Constructs the card itself says the rite is dangerous to attempt without.
**Craft** · Spellcraft, consistent with the destination shelf; the rite holds only while the officiant's attention and the coupling both hold, which is the mechanical reason a failed Transposition strands rather than merely fizzles.
**Stage floor / Grade** · Stage VII, Refraction, A-Grade late push toward S-Grade, for a Transposition trading identity or fate-weight rather than mere position. This is the Gate at which Spirit-aligned Ritus workings routinely reach material-anchored constructs per the standing Temperance tables, and it is the conservative floor consistent with the card's account of failures producing Spatial Disjunction rather than a simple miss; a lower-Stage attempt at pure body-swap positional exchange is plausible at Stage V, Splintering, B-Grade, since that is the first Stage at which a Crystal has enough Wellspring purchase to hold a second coordinate at all.
**Glyphs** · The card names **Ci** (claimed: mirror/duality), **Th** (claimed: threshold/crossing), and **Ra** (claimed: binding chain). Current attested readings give Ci as Order/Honor, Th as Foundation, and Ra as Transcendence, all in the Identity or Structure strata rather than a dedicated crossing register. The corrected readings are used above; the full sentence-order chain the rite would actually require is not attested and should be checked against the Master Glyph Index before this entry is treated as final.

### Practitioners and history

None of the four practitioners the card names, Velcrissa of Selantra, Zeven Halek and his combat partner Cylus, Thalorath the Gatewrought, or the Red Twins of Dorrosfenn, have a card or page anywhere in current canon, and are kept as unattested colour on the understanding a review pass may cut them.
The **Great Spirit War** is real and heavily attested in current canon, so the card's claim that soulguarding orders used Transposition to place themselves inside condemned vessels during that war is kept without alteration to the war's name or dating; nothing in current sources confirms or denies the specific tactic, which is consistent with a minor, unrecorded use of a known rite during a well-documented conflict. "Echo Constructs" as the card uses the term (soul-lattice bodies preloaded with Transposition scripts) has no confirmed page and is kept as unattested. The Fall of the Fourth Prince of Rovann and the Dancer-Priests of Ilcin'dor are likewise unattested; neither Rovann nor Ilcin'dor appears elsewhere in current canon. The card's own citation, WOTR: Shards of the Selfbound Halo, Volume III, is a Trello-era work title distinct from the current Volume I through V Character Card structure and is flagged as superseded rather than corrected, since there is no current equivalent to redirect it to.

### What it can and cannot do

Transposition can trade a caster's exposure for a decoy's, trade a memory into an object, or trade a caster's own body for the housing of a prepared construct, provided the coupling Transference requires can be established between source and target. It can be layered with other Synergia-category effects, per the card's own account of layering with Cascade or Overtone expressions, to unfold across a delay rather than resolve instantly, since Transference's coupling does not itself require simultaneity, only proximity or a prepared anchor.
It cannot cross a separation the coupling cannot bridge; per Transference's own law, the coupling strength falls off sharply with distance, so a Transposition attempted without a prepared Anchor Construct at genuine range is exactly the failure case the card describes as ending in Spatial Disjunction rather than a clean trade. It cannot be trusted to remap the soul correctly under that failure, which is the Wellspring Desynchronization the card names: the officiant and the target both need a Crystal capable of holding the exchanged coordinate, and a mismatch in that capacity is what a failed Transposition actually costs.

#### 1 · Physical account (physics)

**The phenomenon in plain words.** Two oscillators of the same frequency are held
near each other. Nothing is pushed and nothing travels between them, and yet after
a fixed interval the first is at rest and the second holds everything the first had.
Then it comes back. The exchange is complete only if the two were genuinely alike,
and it is complete only if they were genuinely near.
**The real phenomenon.** **Resonant energy transfer between coupled oscillators**,
and the two conditions on it — **degeneracy** and **proximity** — which between them
account for every limit and every failure the page states.
*The exchange, and why it is total.* Two identical pendulums joined by a weak spring
do not share their motion; they **trade** it. Start one and after a half beat period
**t = π / 2κ**
the started pendulum is motionless and the other has the whole amplitude. No energy
crossed as a travelling wave, nothing conducted, and the transfer was not partial —
it was **complete**, which is exactly what Transference's register claims:
*"Two oscillators tuned to the same frequency and held near each other exchange
energy without a conductor between them."* This is the page's plainest use, the swap
of two bodies' positions, and it is the one case the physics hands over free.
*The first condition, and the page's own failure mode falls out of it.* Complete
exchange requires **degeneracy**. Detune the two oscillators by Δ and the maximum
fraction that can ever transfer is
**P_max = 4κ² / (4κ² + Δ²)**
so at Δ = 2κ only **half** of it moves, and at Δ = 10κ only **four percent** does.
The rest stays where it was, and the system is left with two partly-exchanged
oscillators and no way to finish. **That is Wellspring Desynchronization, derived.**
The page names it as the failure and describes it correctly:
*"the officiant and the target both need a Crystal capable of holding the exchanged
coordinate, and a mismatch in that capacity is what a failed Transposition actually
costs."* A mismatch in capacity **is** a detuning, and the formula says what a
mismatch costs: not a miss, but a trade that stops halfway with both halves
occupied.
*The second condition, and it is brutal.* Coupling falls off with separation, and in
the resonant case it falls off very fast. Near-field dipole coupling goes as
**κ ∝ 1/d³**; resonant (Förster) transfer efficiency goes as
**E = R₀⁶ / (R₀⁶ + d⁶)**
which is fifty percent at the characteristic radius R₀ and **1.5 percent at 2R₀**.
**Doubling the separation divides the transfer by sixty-four.** There is no
intermediate regime worth planning around: either the two systems are inside the
characteristic radius or the rite does essentially nothing. The page has this exactly
right and states it twice: *"the coupling falls off sharply with separation, which is
why every reliable account of Transposition describes it working best at short range
or through a prepared anchor rather than across an open field."*
*Which is what the Anchor Construct is for, and the page does not quite say so.* A
relay does not defeat the 1/d⁶ law; it **shortens every hop**. Two 1/d⁶ hops of
length d/2 beat one hop of length d by a factor of 2⁶ on each leg. So an Anchor
Construct is not a safety device and not a stabiliser — it is a **repeater**, and the
page's *"the rite is dangerous to attempt without"* is the honest statement of a rite
whose success probability without one falls off as the sixth power of the distance.
**The governing law.** **Coupled-mode theory**: two resonators exchange completely
at the coupling rate when degenerate, partially and boundedly when detuned, and at a
coupling that falls as a high power of separation. All three of the page's stated
behaviours — total exchange, incomplete exchange on capacity mismatch, and failure
at range without an anchor — are the same equation read at three settings.
**Measurable quantities, SI.** The page states two Stage floors, a Grade band, three
glyphs and a failure taxonomy, and no cost. What the law supplies as checkable
consequence:
- **A trade of like for like is free and a trade of unlike for unlike is bounded.**
This is the sharpest thing the physics says about this page and it cuts against the
page's own hierarchy. The page treats identity-trading — *"a death for an echo, a
memory for a physical object, or a soul for the housing of a mirrored construct"* —
as the *advanced* form of positional exchange, differing by degree and gated one or
two Stages higher. It does not differ by degree. **A memory and an object are not
degenerate oscillators**, so the bound P_max applies and complete exchange is not
available at any Stage. `SA-PHYS-TRANSPOSITION-DETUNED-EXCHANGE`.
- **The exchange is timed, not instantaneous.** t = π/2κ is a real interval set by
the coupling, which means a Transposition has a **duration during which both
parties are partly in each other's place** — and the page's own note that the rite
can be layered to *"unfold across a delay rather than resolve instantly"* is that
interval being used deliberately rather than a separate trick.
- **Failure leaves both halves occupied, which is worse than failure leaving
nothing.** An incomplete exchange does not revert. The page's word for the
wreckage is **Spatial Disjunction**, and the physics says what is disjoint: two
systems each holding part of a coordinate that belongs to one of them.
- **Reciprocity.** Coupling is symmetric — the same κ governs both directions — so
there is no such thing as a one-way Transposition and no way to be the party who
only gives. Transference's own **Failure** says this in the register's voice:
*"Reverse coupling. A gradient runs both directions, and a Transference
practitioner beside a depleted ally will empty into them without deciding to."*
**The energy budget, and where the joules were.** **In whichever of the two systems
had them, and the total does not change.** A symmetric exchange conserves the sum;
the practitioner pays for establishing the coupling and, where the two are not
degenerate, for whatever correction closes Δ. `Two Sets of Books` §III is satisfied
in a form worth noting: *"The practitioner does not supply the joules. They supply
the boundary condition"* — and here the boundary condition is **a tuning**, which is
the cheapest kind there is, and it is why a rite of this consequence can plausibly
sit at a middling Stage.
One thing does not conserve and the page does not mention it. Two bodies exchanged
between different potentials are not energetically equal: a swap across a height h
moves **mgh**, which for an 80 kg body and 10 m is **7.8 kJ** and has to come from
somewhere. The register says the gradient does the work — *"Essence moves to an ally
not because it is pushed but because the two Crystals are in tune and near, and a
gradient does the rest"* — which means a Transposition **downhill is easier than
uphill**, and nothing in the corpus says so.
**Precedent we are not copying.** The swap is everywhere: *Naruto*'s Kawarimi and
the Flying Thunder God seal, *Bleach*'s Utsusemi, blink-and-substitute in every CRPG,
the classical *Doppelgänger* decoy. All of those are **teleportation with a
misdirection attached**, and the interesting question is always where the caster went.
This one has no destination in it: it is a **trade with a counterparty**, and the
counterparty gets the caster's place whether they wanted it or not. The divergence
that matters for scenes: **the other half of a Transposition is a person in a
situation they did not choose**, usually the one the caster was in a moment ago, and
the page's soulguarding use makes that explicit — somebody volunteering to be put
inside a condemned vessel. The tense beat is not the swap. It is the conversation
beforehand about who is taking which end.

#### 2 · Stratal account (metaphysics)

**Aether stratum.** Draw is a **tuning cost plus a coupling cost**, both paid at the
front, with a duration set by κ rather than by the practitioner's endurance —
which makes this one of the cheapest workings in this section by architecture and one
of the least forgiving by geometry. **Residue:** located at the **anchor**, not at
either end. An Anchor Construct is a prepared object that has been used as a relay,
and that is a findable, touchable, destroyable thing. **Saturation:** not a hazard
here; the concern is the opposite — the working needs the two ends to be *readable*
to each other, and Aetheric noise is a detuning like any other.
**Wellspring stratum.** **Transference · *The Living Exchange***, Family **Spatium**.
Its law, verbatim:
> **Analogue** · *"Resonant energy transfer between coupled oscillators. Flow along a
> potential gradient."*
> **Mechanism** · *"Two oscillators tuned to the same frequency and held near each
> other exchange energy without a conductor between them, and the coupling strength
> falls off sharply with separation. Transference makes the Attraction Layer such an
> oscillator. Essence moves to an ally not because it is pushed but because the two
> Crystals are in tune and near, and a gradient does the rest."*
> **Stat Effect** · *"Strengthens Harmonics Synergy, Projection, Tempering
> Conversion. Essence routes to allies and absorbs ambient Wellspring energy through
> relational proximity."*
> **Failure** · *"Reverse coupling. A gradient runs both directions, and a
> Transference practitioner beside a depleted ally will empty into them without
> deciding to."*
> (`wiki/The Eight Families & the Sixty Wellsprings/Spatium — Spatial Geometry.md`:26)
**The page reads this register more accurately than any other entry in this section
reads its own.** *"The mechanism is Spatium's Transference read straight"* is a claim
and the claim is true: tuned alike, held near, no conductor, sharp falloff. Four
clauses, four uses.
**And the Failure is the page's missing cost, again, and here it is a tactical one
rather than a moral one.** *"Reverse coupling. A gradient runs both directions."*
Coupling is symmetric, so **the officiant cannot establish a coupling that only runs
the way they want it to run.** A practitioner who couples themselves to a condemned
vessel in order to purify it from the inside has, by construction, coupled the
condemned vessel to themselves. The page's soulguarding use is the register's own
worst case performed on purpose, and the page describes the use approvingly without
noticing that its Wellspring has a printed warning on it.
The two secondaries are correctly chosen and doing different jobs:
- **Transmutatio · *The Law of Change*** *(Spatium)* — *"The same object described
in two frames yields two entirely different sets of numbers and one unchanged
object. Nothing is created or lost in a transformation; the quantities are merely
re-expressed"* — is what the page means by trading identity rather than position.
A change of basis is exactly the right law for a rite that swaps what a coordinate
*means*, and its **Failure is the page's own Spatial Disjunction with better
words**: *"Basis loss. The practitioner forgets which frame they started in, and a
transformation with no reference frame does not return anything recognisable."*
- **Fixatio · *The Binding Flame*** *(Materia)* — *"What it holds is not held by
force but by the fact that the alternative configuration no longer exists as a
reachable state"* — is the **Anchor Construct**, and the page is right to put it
there: a relay that can be detuned is not a relay. Its **Failure,
*irreversibility***, is the price of a good anchor: *"a Fixatio binding placed in
error is a permanent feature of whatever it was placed on."* An anchor is not
retrieved. It is left behind, permanently, wherever it was set.
*Four Theories.* **Correspondence**, and this is the working the theory was written
for. There is no consent being sought from a Wellspring and no debt incurred; what
there is, is a claim that **two things are alike enough to be exchanged**, and the
whole rite succeeds or fails on whether that claim is true. Degeneracy is
correspondence stated as a number.
**Essence stratum.** Active layers: the **Attraction Layer** at both ends, since the
register says Transference *"makes the Attraction Layer such an oscillator"* and the
Layer is where a Crystal holds what it is near and tuned to; and the **Essence Core**
in the identity-trading uses, since a coordinate that belongs to a soul is held
there. Developmental Tier at the stated Stage VII floor: **Resonant**; at the Stage V
variant, **Harmonic** (Part Twenty-One). Crystal State: `null`, no card anywhere.
**The invoice:** stated in kind and never in figures — the coupling, the tuning
correction, a permanently-placed Anchor Construct, and on failure a Spatial
Disjunction that leaves both parties holding part of a coordinate.
**The lens: the powder of sympathy.** Sir Kenelm Digby, *A Late Discourse Made in a
Solemne Assembly… Touching the Cure of Wounds by the Powder of Sympathy* (1658).
Digby's doctrine was that a wound and the weapon that made it, or a wound and the
blood-stained bandage taken from it, remain **sympathetically linked**: treat the
bandage and the wound heals, because effluvia pass between things that belong
together. It is a serious seventeenth-century theory held by a founding fellow of the
Royal Society, and it gets the *phenomenon* strikingly right — two systems that
belong to each other exchange without anything travelling between them, which is
coupled resonance stated three centuries early in the wrong vocabulary. The Veil
literature's older registers filed Transference under feeling rather than physics and
*"were not wrong about what it felt like"*; Digby is the same mistake made with
instruments.
Where the lens misleads, and where it produces **precisely the failure the page
documents**, is** distance**. Digby held that sympathy is indifferent to separation —
the link is a relationship, and relationships do not have a range. He is famous for
the consequence: the proposal that longitude at sea could be solved by wounding a dog,
keeping the dog aboard and the bandage ashore, and having the bandage dipped at a
known hour so the dog would yelp in mid-Atlantic. A school reading Transposition
through Digby therefore believes the **bond** is the working and the anchor is
mere insurance. They establish a genuine correspondence with real care, verify it,
trust it — and then attempt the exchange across an open field, because a real
relationship cannot be weakened by mere yards. **The transfer goes as 1/d⁶.** They
get what the page says they get: *"a Transposition attempted without a prepared
Anchor Construct at genuine range is exactly the failure case the card describes as
ending in Spatial Disjunction rather than a clean trade."* The lens is not wrong
about what sympathy is. It is wrong about the exponent, and the exponent is six.
Offered as a school's error, not as canon.

#### 3 · Mechanism (the effect)

**Glyph.** **The page names three and reports its own readings as wrong.**
*"The card names **Ci **(claimed: mirror/duality),** Th** (claimed:
threshold/crossing), and **Ra** (claimed: binding chain). Current attested readings
give Ci as Order/Honor, Th as Foundation, and Ra as Transcendence, all in the
Identity or Structure strata rather than a dedicated crossing register."* One of the
three can be checked here: `[Th]` **Foundation** is among the thirty entries the repo
mirror of the Master Glyph Index carries, and it reads **Foundation**, not threshold
or crossing — so the page's correction is confirmed on the one glyph the mirror
reaches. The other two are in the 106 unmirrored entries
(`SA-GAP-GLYPH-MIRROR`). **This account proposes no substitute chain**, because the
page has named glyphs, has corrected them, and has said what it wants next:
*"the full sentence-order chain the rite would actually require is not attested and
should be checked against the Master Glyph Index before this entry is treated as
final."* A proposal would be a third reading rather than a check.
`SA-UNATT-TRANSPOSITION-GLYPHS`.
**What boundary moves.** **Whether two coordinates count as the same kind of thing.**
Not the coordinates themselves, and not the distance between them. Transference's law
runs continuously in any harmonised Crystal — *"Essence moves to an ally… because the
two Crystals are in tune and near"* — and what the rite supplies is the **tuning**:
a declaration, held long enough to be acted on, that this coordinate and that one are
degenerate. Everything else follows from the coupling.
**What the law then does on its own.** It exchanges, on its own clock, and the clock
is π/2κ. Nothing pushes. Neither end is moved by the other; both are moved by being
coupled, which is the register's *"not because it is pushed but because the two
Crystals are in tune and near, and a gradient does the rest."* Where the two are
genuinely degenerate the exchange runs to completion and the page's plainest use is
delivered: two bodies, two positions, traded. Where they are not — and the page's
most ambitious uses are all cases where they are not — the exchange runs to
**P_max and stops there**, and what is left is two systems each holding part of
something that belongs to one of them.
And because coupling is symmetric, the rite has no preferred direction and cannot be
given one. A caster who couples themselves to a thing in order to reach into it has
also given the thing a route into them, for the whole duration of the coupling, at
the same κ.
**Law V check.** The floor is **Stage VII, Refraction** — exactly the line. *"At
Refraction the practitioner can hold the Crystal as an object of study and the Glyph
Roles compress into an internal chain that needs no material anchor at all"*
(`The Four Crafts`, Law V). So at the stated floor **no external anchor is required**,
and the Anchor Construct the page insists on is **not Law V's anchor**: it is a
coupling relay, a distance device, and it is required by geometry rather than by the
Craft gate. That distinction is worth making because it changes what breaking the
anchor does — it does not interrupt a chain, it restores the 1/d⁶ falloff.
**The page's Stage V variant does not survive the same check.** *"a lower-Stage
attempt at pure body-swap positional exchange is plausible at Stage V, Splintering,
B-Grade."* Stage V is below Refraction, so Law V requires voice, hand, ink or blood,
and the page names no anchor for the low-Stage form.
`SA-CROSS-TRANSPOSITION-ANCHOR-VERSUS-LAW-V`.
**Failure mode — Boundary fault, and it is a false claim of degeneracy.** *"The
condition was established imprecisely and the law ran somewhere unintended"*
(`Two Sets of Books` §VII). Here the imprecision has a name and a number: the two
coordinates were **not as alike as the tuning claimed**, Δ was not zero, and the
exchange stopped at P_max. Transmutatio's **Failure** says what that leaves better
than the page's own term does: *"Basis loss. The practitioner forgets which frame
they started in, and a transformation with no reference frame does not return
anything recognisable."*
A second fault, and it is the one the page has no account of at all: **Structural
fault from Overchannel.** Part Seven, on the Sub-Stat Transmutatio's Stat Effect
names: **Overchannel** is *"open, carrying inherent Overflow risk above D-Grade and
Backlash risk above C, with no Path providing structural protection."* The page
requires **A-Grade**, three Grades above C, on a working whose secondary current runs
through that Sub-Stat — so the rite carries an unprotected Backlash risk that no Path
commitment mitigates, and the page's failure taxonomy does not include it.
`SA-CROSS-TRANSPOSITION-PATH-GATES-AND-BACKLASH`.
**What the target sees and feels.** For a clean positional exchange between two
degenerate systems: an interval, not an instant. The page's layering note is the
honest description — the rite *"does not itself require simultaneity"* — so what a
target experiences is a span of being partly somewhere else, during which both
positions are partly occupied and neither is entirely theirs. Then it completes, and
they are standing where the other one was, in the other one's circumstances,
including the ones nobody mentioned.
For the identity trades the page advertises, the experience is **an exchange that
stops.** A memory traded for an object does not become the object and the object does
not become the memory; both end up holding a fraction of a coordinate, and the
fraction is 4κ²/(4κ² + Δ²), which for two things as unlike as a memory and a stone is
very small. The page calls the wreckage **Spatial Disjunction** and says it *"cannot
be trusted to remap the soul correctly under that failure."* The physics agrees and is
less kind: nothing was remapped incorrectly. **Most of it was not remapped at all**,
and both ends are now occupied by a partial claim that neither can complete and
neither can drop.
**What bleeds, at the stated efficiency.** No practitioner has a card
(`SA-GAP-TRANSPOSITION-NO-CARD`). At the **Stage VII** floor the Tier of Standing is
**5 · Expert**, η **0.60–0.70** under **R44-4** (`RULINGS.md` 2026-09-25): **30 to 40
percent** of the expenditure leaves as heat, sound and structural bleed. At the page's
**Stage V** variant the Tier is also **5** and η reads the same **0.60–0.70**: the row
R44-4 edits spans V–VII with a single η cell, and **R44-7** (agent ruling, 2026-09-25,
closing `C-063`) holds that the correction reaches every Stage that row spans, Stage V
included. **Both of the page's Stages therefore bleed 30 to 40 percent**, and the
variant is no cheaper than the floor. The expenditure being unstated, both bleeds are unstated
with it; what can be said is that the working's *standing* draw is the coupling rather
than the effect, and a rite whose boundary condition is a tuning is intrinsically
cheap in a way the page never claims credit for.

**The arithmetic, and what it turns up.** Three lines.
*First, the page's hierarchy of uses is upside down in kind, not in degree.* The page
gates identity-trading at Stage VII and positional exchange at Stage V, as though
one were a harder version of the other. The exchange formula says they are different
**regimes**: a like-for-like trade is degenerate and goes to completion at any
coupling, while a memory-for-object trade is maximally detuned and is bounded at
P_max no matter what Stage performs it. **No Stage closes a Δ.** That is the largest
physics finding on this page and it means the page's most advertised uses are not
gated high — they are gated *out*, unless the system supplies a way to tune unlike
things into degeneracy. `SA-PHYS-TRANSPOSITION-DETUNED-EXCHANGE`.
*Second, the range law is severe enough to be a design constraint rather than a
caution.* Sixty-four to one for a doubling. There is no such thing as a
medium-range Transposition: the rite is performed inside the characteristic radius or
through relays, and a relay is a permanently-placed object that an opponent can find.
This is the working's fairness and the page states the conclusion without the
exponent.
*Third, reciprocity is not optional and the page's flagship use ignores it.*
Transference's coupling is symmetric and its **Failure** names the consequence:
*"A gradient runs both directions."* The page's soulguarding application — a
practitioner trading places with *"something that needs purifying from the inside"* —
therefore opens a route from the condemned vessel into the practitioner at the same
κ, for the same interval. The page presents this as the discipline's noblest use and
does not price it.
**Does the physics close against the stratal account?** **Yes for the mechanism, and
the one real failure is a claim the page makes about what it can trade.**
What closes: coupled resonance against *"two systems tuned alike exchange without a
conductor between them"*; the 1/d⁶ falloff against *"works best at short range or
through a prepared anchor"*; P_max against **Wellspring Desynchronization** and
*"a mismatch in that capacity"*; symmetry against Transference's own **reverse
coupling**; the relay reading against the Anchor Construct requirement; and
Transmutatio's change of basis against the identity trades. Six for six on
mechanism, which makes this one of the two best-built pages in this section.
What does not close is **the scope of the trade.** The page claims complete exchange
between things that are not alike — a death for an echo, a memory for an object, a
soul for a construct's housing — and the mechanism it names bounds exactly that case.
Either the page's mechanism is not Transference read straight, or its most developed
uses are partial by law. `SA-PHYS-TRANSPOSITION-DETUNED-EXCHANGE`, and the
effect-versus-mechanism form of the same problem at
`SA-EM-TRANSPOSITION-UNLIKE-FOR-UNLIKE`.

#### 5 · Counterplay and the challenge

**The fairness check** (`.claude/skills/wotr-write/references/fair-play.md`).
| Test | Verdict | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Costs something that hurts | **pass in kind, fail in units** | Three real costs are stated: an **Anchor Construct** placed permanently (Fixatio's irreversibility makes it unrecoverable), a failure mode that leaves both parties holding part of a coordinate, and the requirement that *"the officiant and the target both need a Crystal capable of holding the exchanged coordinate."* What is missing is any figure: no EU, no reserve, no duration. The reciprocity cost is real, derivable from Transference's own Failure, and not on the page. |
| Stated limits | **pass, and they are the best set in this section** | Range (*"the coupling strength falls off sharply with separation"*), the anchor requirement, two Stage floors for two different uses, a stated capacity requirement at **both** ends, and a named failure taxonomy — Spatial Disjunction and Wellspring Desynchronization. Five limits, all mechanical. |
| Something beats it | **pass, decisively, and the best counter is free** | Distance. Below. |
| It has a tell | **pass, and it is bought in advance** | The rite needs a **prepared anchor** at genuine range, which means the object is placed before anybody fights, is a permanent feature of wherever it was placed, and is findable. A working whose tell is an object an opponent can walk up to and examine is as fair as this section gets. |
| Numbers in band | **partial pass** | Stage VII ⇒ Max Grade A, ceiling 475, Tier 5, η 0.60–0.70 under R44-4 — all consistent, and the *"late push toward S"* is the strain band Part Five permits. **The Counterplay routes that work**
(`wiki/The Magic System/Counterplay What Beats a Practitioner.md`).
- **Break the body — distance, and here it is not a soft counter but a cliff.**
*"Distance. Fulguria falls off with the square. Vectoria has to close… Most
practitioners are a threat about closing range and are written as though they were
not."* Transference falls off harder than Fulguria: **1/d⁶ in efficiency, 1/d³ in
coupling**, so doubling the separation divides the transfer by** sixty-four**.
There is no partial Transposition worth having and no Stage that fixes it. Against
this discipline the counter is the cheapest one in the register: **stand further
away**, and make sure nothing has been left on the ground between you.
- **Break the boundary — the anchor, which is Runecraft's counter applied to a
relay.** *"Wards. Runecraft standing on a substrate, and therefore beaten the way
Runecraft is beaten: damage the substrate."* An Anchor Construct is a prepared,
Fixatio-held object whose whole function is to shorten each hop; break it and the
full separation reasserts itself with the sixth power attached. The page says the
rite is *"dangerous to attempt without"* one — so the fight over the anchor is the
fight, and it happens before anyone is in the room.
- **Deny the field — detune, which is the counter nobody will think of and the one
the system hands over. **Complete exchange requires degeneracy, and** P_max =
4κ²/(4κ² + Δ²)**: a mismatch of 2κ halves the transfer and a mismatch of 10κ
reduces it to four percent. Anything that makes the two ends less alike is a
counter, and the register names the general tool: *"Choose the ground by Family"*
and *"Saturate it"* both work by making a reading noisy. **A target who is hard to
match is hard to trade with**, and the page's own limit says the same thing from the
other side: *"the officiant and the target both need a Crystal capable of holding
the exchanged coordinate."*
- **Break the man — reciprocity, and it is the cruellest option here.** Transference's
**Failure**: *"Reverse coupling. A gradient runs both directions, and a Transference
practitioner beside a depleted ally will empty into them without deciding to."* A
coupling the officiant opens is a coupling that runs into the officiant at the same
κ. An opponent who understands this does not resist the Transposition. They arrange
to be the wrong thing to be coupled to, and let the rite complete.
- **Deny the field — Silence.** *"Lawful transformation cannot be authorised at all
inside one."* A Ritus cannot be performed in a Silence, which makes the location of
a soulguarding chamber a logistical fact worth fighting over in the same way a
Scission chamber is.
**The tell, stated plainly.** Not the rite — the **furniture**. A Transposition at any
useful range needs an Anchor Construct, which is a prepared object, Fixatio-held,
permanently placed, sitting somewhere between or beside the two ends before anything
begins. Anyone who has learned what one looks like can find it, and anyone who breaks
it has restored the sixth-power falloff without touching a practitioner. At close
range there is no tell and no time, and the honest statement of this working's threat
profile is that it is **a short-range trade with a long-range preparation**, and the
preparation is the visible part.

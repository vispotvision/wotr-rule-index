On the last morning of the Salt Succession two armies stood across a field of green barley at the ford of Orrendale, scarlet shields on one side and gold-and-black on the other, and a cook's daughter named Perrine Dorrow walked out alone between them in a butcher's apron over borrowed mail. Her aura was the wet arterial red that makes a stranger's pulse jump. She filled her chest and shouted across the whole valley the name of the only art she had.

**"THE HUNGER OF ALL KINGS!"**

Six thousand men laughed. A knight in the gold-and-black stood up in his stirrups to laugh better. Then the third day of a fast landed in every belly of the enemy front rank at once, and the line buckled, men sat down in the barley and wept for bread, warhorses dragged their heads down to tear at the crop while their riders slid off them, and the scarlet shields walked across and took the ford almost without a blow. Perrine's ribs stood out through the apron by then, because that art eats the woman who works it, and she ate for nine days and never again kept down a whole meal. The minstrels had the song in every tavern by harvest. The queen who had hired her held a feast in her honor, since a fighter who can starve an army is a problem at table, and the third course was poisoned, and forty people in cloth of gold watched her die with their spoons in their hands. They buried her in the old barrow above the ford, on a battlefield of the Great Spirit War where nothing has grown straight since, and nobody who has dug there will say what the barrow was built to hold down.

## What War of the Realms is

War of the Realms is a dark shonen cultivation fantasy: a medieval world that runs on old magic, with lethal courts, deep and frightening powers, and fighters who shout their techniques across the battlefield. For feel, take Final Fantasy's world, Martin's politics, Tolkien's sense of age and anime's heat. The stories are set in the Dark Ages, the hundred and ten years from 690 IC to the turn of the century, on one great continent of four quarters, and the present year is 715 IC, the twenty-fifth of them. The name flatters nobody and misleads every visitor, because these are the loudest years on record: practitioners are common, schools run like football clubs with their colors, songs and grudges, heralds cry the news, minstrels get it wrong, and bookies take odds on all of it. Magic is done by living people and their crafts. A city's lamps burn the color of the warden who keeps them, and she can walk off the round, while the towns and the wilds still burn oil. There are no guns in any era, and a three-copper crossbow bolt can still kill a great fighter in a closed room. People who wake the power in their own souls climb a sixteen-step ladder of strength, and they climb it by surviving what nearly breaks them or by paying for the Drafts that do the work instead, which is why the rich are strong and why a lucky commoner with a monster core keeps great houses awake at night. Each person's soul reports their numbers to them alone, in a block of text called a window. Crews go down into dungeons, floors of living danger grown around a pool of power, to kill what guards the heart. Monsters level up, earn names and carry bounties. Guilds of every kind hand out the same nine titles, gear carries a Grade and a Rarity, and blessings show on your sheet and sometimes lie. The stakes are dark and the costs are real, but the heart is shonen: people fight back with will, bonds and growth, and they win often, each win at a price you can see. The look borrows from Final Fantasy and the worlds of Bleach and Naruto, with Tolkien's age lying under everything, and the land is its own thing: wildly fantastical and earthlike at once, grand and lived-in, and beautiful all the way through.

### A short route before your first fight scene

When you want more than the primer and less than the whole section, these eight pages are the quickest way through. They cut across the shelves on purpose, and each one leans on the one before.

1. **[[The Magic System/Practice and Institution/The Gate Who May Learn Magic|The Gate: Who May Learn Magic]]**. Who wakes their Crystal, who never does, and why that is no longer an accident.
2. **[[The Magic System/First Steps/The Core Vocabulary|The Core Vocabulary]]**. The basic words: the three planes, Aether (the medium the world is made of) and Essence (Aether a person has pulled in from the world around them and refined into their own), the Crystal, abilities and techniques, and the sheet and the climb.
3. **[[The Magic System/The Soul and the Climb/The Sixteen Stages|The Sixteen Stages]]**. The ladder of power, Stage (one of sixteen steps of the soul's refinement) by Stage, told plainly: what it feels like inside, what it looks like outside, what ordinary people call it, and what it costs to buy.
4. **[[The Magic System/The Soul and the Climb/Tier Grade, Bands & the Aether Shell|Tier Grade, Bands & the Aether Shell]]**. The three measurements people confuse: Level (how often the Crystal has grown denser), Grade (a letter band, F up to EX+, that says how much force a stat delivers) and Aether Class (the named class a soul is offered at its thresholds, whose tier says how cleanly power gets out of the body).
5. **[[The Magic System/The Soul and the Climb/The Trait System Law, Function and the Forge|The Trait System: Law, Function and the Forge]]**. Where powers come from. A Trait (a standing law in the soul, formed when lived experience hardens into something the world accepts as true) is survived into being, never taught, and carries its own level, which rises with use.
6. **[[The Magic System/Power in Use/The Working Trades|The Working Trades]]**. The ways a working (a deliberate act of magic) is set down: spoken in the moment, cut into stone, steel or paper, poured into a bottle, or sung by many voices together, each a skill and a trade, and all but the pouring want a woken Crystal. Also why the guilds look down on fighters who speak their workings, while the crowds cheer them anyway.
7. **[[The Magic System/The Foundations/The Eight Families & the Sixty Wellsprings/The Eight Families & the Sixty Wellsprings|The Eight Families & the Sixty Wellsprings]]**. The sixty Wellsprings (each an alchemical and spiritual transformation, the governing idea that changes how a soul uses Aether, a lens on magic already potent, which scholars explain with one law of physics apiece) sorted into eight Families: heat, electricity and light, force, space, matter, life, fluids, and entropy and mind.
8. **[[The Magic System/Power in Use/Counterplay What Beats a Practitioner|Counterplay: What Beats a Practitioner]]**. How a practitioner (a person whose Crystal is awake) is actually beaten, in four routes. Read this before your first fight scene.

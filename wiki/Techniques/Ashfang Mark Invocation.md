# Ashfang Mark Invocation

## Ashfang Mark Invocation

> **Migration note.** Converted from a Trello ability card for **Draven Kael Vorrick, The Beast Slayer** (verified against his Volume IV character card). Primary Wellspring **Cinerion** is kept as given; it already matches Draven's attested sheet — Caloria, ash and remnants, the archive of kills. Secondary Wellspring "Vorynn Predatory Essences" is corrected: **Vorynn is attested in current canon only as Draven's Trait-lineage** (Vorynn Bloodbind, the Vorynn Trials), never as a Wellspring, so the borrowing runs through that Trait, with **Petralon** (his attested kinetic Wellspring) supplying the "kinetically amplifies chosen behavior" the old card described. "Aether Class: Primal-Kinetic" converts to his attested **Class III, Resonant**. "Path: Beast-Assimilation" is not one of the Four Paths (Body, Spirit, Attraction, Fate); Draven's sheet has him Body-dominant with no Spirit Axis, so the technique gates on Body alone and the old label survives only as flavor. "Stage VI Glory" matches his attested Catalyst Stage and is kept. The old "4 turns" converts to **20 seconds**, using the 5-seconds-per-turn rate Bloodbind Surge's own card states outright (2 turns = 10 seconds) — no other conversion is documented anywhere in the batch. Category is a judgment call: **Synergia**, chosen as the closest fit for a working that merges his Essence with a borrowed remnant, not verified against a categories glossary. Grade required (B) is read off Draven's own Tempering value, 248.

### Summary card

**Effect** · **He takes a bone talon from something he killed, and for a short while his body remembers what it was.** *One beast-trait, borrowed rather than mimicked, running on his own blood.*
**Cost** · **The heartbeat goes wrong while the graft holds.** *Temporary. It has never once stopped him.*
**Limit** · **One trait at a time.** *The Crystal at Stage VI has no room to hold two borrowed templates and his own pattern together.*
**Counter** · **A working that strips a target's identity before the graft reads, or an Essence seal placed before contact, denies the borrowed template a signature to copy.**
**What nobody knows** · Why the graft only ever takes one trait when Cinerion has archived every kill he has ever made.

### Design Chain

**Trigger** · Draven opens a blood channel while gripping a bone talon taken from a beast he has already killed.
**Function** · Buff, self-target. Grants one beast-trait for the duration of the graft.
**Mechanism** · Cinerion's real phenomenon is pyrolytic char and its own catalytic residue — a burned structure that keeps a chemical record of what produced it. The talon is Draven's Cinerion archive of that kill, an Essence-print pyrolyzed into bone the way ash retains a catalyst's residue. Opening the blood channel lets the stored print act on his own Aether Shell the way a residue triggers a reaction it does not itself supply, and Petralon's kinetic thread reads the pattern and expresses it as a trait his own body did not carry a moment before.
**Numerical Effect** · The borrowed trait activates at Draven's own Tempering ceiling, B-Grade, and cannot exceed his personal Stage VI stat ceiling no matter how large the source beast was.
**Target Response** · Self only. Flight, a sonic roar, a burrow reflex — whatever the talon's owner carried, expressed through Draven's own frame at his own scale.
**Consequence** · Veins along the graft path run ash-grey for the duration; the talon itself glows red-grey.
**Limitation** · Only one trait active at once, per the Limit above.
**Weakness** · Reuse of the same talon returns a weaker expression each time, the residue thinning with every draw.
**Cost** · Temporary heartbeat instability while the graft holds.
**Counterplay** · Identity-stripping workings and Essence seals, applied before the blood channel opens, leave nothing for Petralon's thread to read.

### Origin

Draven Kael Vorrick, self-derived during the Vorynn Trials — his sheet's own Catalyst Event of "constant self-imposed trials of endurance, ambush and predatory pressure." The problem it answers is the one the card's own philosophy states: a solitary hunter who kills things larger and stranger than himself needs a way to borrow, briefly, what made them dangerous. "Wear what you kill."

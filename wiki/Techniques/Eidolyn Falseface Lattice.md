# Eidolyn Falseface Lattice

## Eidolyn Falseface Lattice

### Summary card

**Effect** · Whatever the opponent is afraid the fight contains, the practitioner gives it a body. The construct is not a picture of the fear. It has the same solidity a fear that specific would have, and it hits like it.
**Cost** · **Identity Drift.** Sustained use blurs where his self-image ends and the borrowed fear-shapes begin. **Cognitive Static** follows: headache, memory flicker, perception distortion.
**Limit** · Needs an actual conviction to template from. A fearless or fully lucid target, or a threat-shape the target has no concept of, gives the working nothing to deposit onto.
**Counter** · **A mirror. Specular reflection** *returns the construct's own image undistorted, and a form templated from someone's imagined threat has no further truth behind it to survive contact with its own unedited reflection.*
**What nobody knows** · Why a construct built from someone else's fear ever agrees to fight for its maker rather than against him.

### Origin

It has a single known practitioner, and the problem it answers is plain: a fight against something that has already decided to be afraid of its user is a fight that user can win by giving the fear a body and letting it swing at its own owner.

### Physics

**Phenomenon** · Template-directed nucleation: a supersaturated medium, one holding more than it can dissolve, will not organise itself, but given a template it deposits solid along that shape, as nacre and grown crystal films do.
**Law** · Classical nucleation theory: the barrier ΔG‡ = 16πγ³v² / (3·(kT·ln S)²) and the rate J = A·exp(−ΔG‡/kT), where S is the supersaturation, here the target's conviction.
**Limit** · Below a critical supersaturation nothing nucleates at all, so a calm mind yields no construct, not a weak one; and a template's defects are copied through the whole deposit.
**Scaling** · Doubling ln S quarters the barrier and the rate climbs exponentially, so a deeply convinced target makes one dense construct rather than several thin ones.
**Mass** · A 500 kg construct made from nothing would carry 4.5 × 10¹⁹ J, seven orders past the A-Grade ceiling; it is not made but deposited from ambient Aether, and dissolves back.

### Mechanism

**Glyph** · Form laid across Mind: Form decides the geometry a deposit takes, and the template is read out of a mind.
**Boundary** · The nucleation barrier in the ambient medium, lowered along one imposed pattern.
**Trigger** · Reactive only: it releases against an incoming hostile action once a target within eighty metres carries a fear the practitioner can find; the reactive chain is why it needs Refraction.
**Effect** · The thing the attacker feared stands in the room, strikes and can be struck, its mass scaled by their conviction; taking a threat seriously is what is harvested. A shared dread makes one larger template; a fear with a hole in it arrives with the hole.
**Hold** · The construct stands while attention holds it, paid each turn, and dissolves when attention releases.
**Failure** · Boundary fault: a misread template deposits faithfully anyway. Field fault: a Letheveil-disciplined mind never reaches critical conviction. Structural fault waiting: Identity Drift toward Mirage Identity.
**Strain** · Headache, memory flicker and perception distortion come with each release; each release after the second in one engagement costs a further 1 percent of reserve.
**Bleed** · 35 percent leaves the Shell as heat, and the casting shows its waste as a seam, a hollow or a limb that does not carry.

### Counterplay

**Tell** · An attack is made and the attacker's worst fear answers it, never before, looking exactly like its source; a template trace lingers in the ground.
**Limits** · Reactive only, eighty metres, needs a real conviction; fearless, lucid or diffuse fears give it nothing; Density capped at B on a Spirit Path; grit in saturated Aether.
**Fails against** · A true mirror, a Lock that un-templates it; Letheveil discipline; an empathic ally (Catharsis, Benediction) beside the frightened one; a second and third attacker reaching the practitioner while the construct is engaged.

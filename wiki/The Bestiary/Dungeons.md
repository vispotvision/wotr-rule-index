# Dungeons

*What a Core makes around itself, what comes to live in it, who goes down to clear it, and who gets the bill*

> *Item, one hill. Item, one orchard, under same. Item, one crown, received from the hand of the late proprietor at some speed, in part settlement of wages.*
>
> *Household roll of the Kingdom of Hollowhay, entry for the wedding year, in the hand of its new owner*

King Bertran Ferrant ruled a hill. Hollowhay is one of the smallest realms in the Hundred Crowns, two miles of hedge and a cider press around one green lump called Goosegrass, and what made it a kingdom at all lay under the lump: nine galleries of apple trees in the living rock, in leaf and fruit together in every month of the year, lit a clear spring green by nothing anyone could point to. Bertran's great-great-grandfather had taken the place from the things that bred in it, with thirty crossbows and a Sealwright, and ever since the Ferrants had let the great gallery for weddings. For the wedding of his own daughter Isemay to the heir of the toll-king at Tollgarth, in the summer of 708, Bertran hired every herald-painter on the Kessen road, hung the trees with scarlet and gold, and spent the last of his credit on four hundred guests. He had also not paid his keepers in three seasons.

The keepers were a family called Ambrey, who had tended the pool at the bottom of the ninth gallery since the Ferrants first won it, and on the morning of the wedding Mother Odalie Ambrey brought all of them up to the top of the stair in their clean aprons, sat them on the steps, and handed round the bread and onions. They had stopped bleeding the pool at midwinter. Nobody at the feast knew what that meant, and the toast went up at sundown the way a Midmarch toast does, with every guest who owned a woken Crystal lighting their coat at once for the pride of it, rose and amber and kingfisher blue and one enormous drunken crimson, four hundred people's worth of Aether pulled in a single breath out of ground that had been filling for half a year. The green in the gallery went bright enough to read by. Then the apple trees began to walk, and the floor came up in loops of thorn as thick as a horse's barrel, each with a knot of hard green plate at the crown of its roots, and the first loop took the groom's uncle off his bench and through the top table.

Isemay Ferrant, who could read a bill, understood first. She went up nine galleries in forty yards of saffron silk with a carving knife in her hand and somebody's blood on her to the elbows, and she asked Mother Ambrey to close the ring. Mother Ambrey finished her onion and named the arrears. Bertran, arriving on one leg with a briar on the other, had nothing on him worth a season's pay but the thing on his head, and he threw it up the stair. She looked at it, bit it, and said it would do for the first season, and the bride tore off her dowry pearls for the other two. Then an old woman in an apron walked down through a battle with a brass key and shut the pool, and the briars lost their footing in the ground in the time it takes to say so, and four hundred drunk people in their best clothes went for the green knots with candlesticks, cake saws and the legs of benches. Thirty-one died. The marriage is still standing, and a bookie on the Tollgarth road will still give you four to one against it. The Ambreys hold the crown, which makes Hollowhay the only monarchy in the Midmarch founded on back pay, and they let the great gallery for weddings at double the old rate, because couples now ask for the bench with the stain.

The word for what lies under Goosegrass is **dungeon**, and this page is the Bestiary's account of the things, since most of what the Bestiary lists either came out of one or is on its way back in.

## What the Word Means

The word began as a castle word. On the old chancery rolls a *domnio* is a lord's great tower, the place where his writ runs and nobody else's does, and in the mouths of delvers it wore down to *dungeon* and moved underground, because the first crews to go down into these places noticed that a different lord's writ ran there. They were closer to the truth than the scholars who corrected their spelling.

> **A Wellspring** is one of the sixty kinds of Aether (the raw stuff of magic, lying in the ground and air), each working on the Soul Crystal in its own way. **A Core**, in common speech, is a site where one of them pools. **A dungeon is what a Core makes around itself, or what grows up around one.** The Aether rewrites the ground, things come to live on it and breed in it, and people build, dig and die there until the whole is one place with one law in it. **A worked Core with keepers is a dungeon somebody cleared and now holds.**

Most Wellspring Cores lie in dungeons, and the rest are said to lie in vast open territories, a matter taken up below. Three words crowd each other here, and each has killed somebody. A **Well** is the delver's word for the ground itself, a Core Concentration in the survey's terms, thick enough to rewrite what lives in it. A **dungeon** is the crew's word for the whole place, galleries and beasts and all, and it covers a Well with a roof on and a worked site whose keepers have walked off. **Core** means any worked site in the street, the innermost and densest band of a concentration on the survey (see [[The Magic System/Practice and Institution/On Walking Into the Current — Wellspring Encounter and Recovery|On Walking Into the Current]]), and the seat of a soul in a physician's mouth. When a porter says he has sat beside a dozen Cores, he means the first. Find out which one your captain means before you go down.

## At a Glance

**What it looks like.** A way down, first of all: a shaft, a stair, a cave mouth, a split in a hillside, an old cellar door with warm air coming up through the cracks. Behind it, galleries that go on longer than the hill has room for, in the color of the Well's own Wellspring, getting brighter the deeper you go.

**What it costs.** Reserve to hold your own law on its ground, bolts by the pouch, a clock you cannot argue with, and a share of everyone who went down with you. A practitioner pulls Aether in fast there, and that is the bait. A Well-spawn goes toward the pull.

**How it is beaten.** Kill out the deep lines and then the guardian at the Heart, which is the only true clearing. Those who cannot do that seat a ring at a depth people can stand at, install keepers, and pay them forever.

**Example.** Ama Brockway's crew of six takes a Green posting on a Mouth found under a Pennycrown tannery. The fork rings thin and teal at the cellar door, the cellar is perfectly dry, and the porter has started to cry and cannot say why. Thaloré, then, or something near it: a Spirit ground, all weight of feeling. She sets her caller on the top step, turns a one-hour glass, takes four people down, breaks eleven smoke-brown masks in a gallery that smells of cold brine, and is back on the step with a quarter of the sand to spare, because the man carrying the rope has gone quiet and that is the sign to leave. She reports it to the hall by nightfall, as the law requires, and the tanner discovers that he owns a dungeon.

## How One Grows

**The seep.** It begins where a Wellspring's Aether starts to pool, on conductive rock, along a fault, over a dead battlefield, or for reasons no surveyor has ever established. Wells breed, and a seep is a Well too young to breed anything, a warm patch where the moss runs a strange color and the dogs lie down.

**The Mouth.** The ground goes on thickening until it alters what stands on it and the alteration stays. On Wild ground the rock begins to move on its own time, a crack widening over a decade, a sinkhole with very regular sides. On ground where people have already dug, the Well simply takes what is there, which is how a mine, a wine cellar, a drowned abbey or a forgotten crypt becomes the top floor of something. Dungeons come both ways, grown and inherited, and most of the great ones are both at once: a thing the Core started, some people improved, and the Core then finished with their tools still on the bench.

**The workings.** Animals move in, or wander in, and the Aether fills their Essence Core with nowhere to go. Over generations a line stops living by its own nature and begins to breed by the Well's, masked at birth (the full account is under [[The Bestiary/The Corrupted and the Risen/Well-spawn|Well-spawn]]). Now the place has an inside. Ground thickens downward in bands, and each band favors its own stock.

**The Deeps and the Heart.** Given centuries, the bottom of the place thins the Veil (the boundary between the planes), the deep lines gather minds out of what the Well remembers, and at the very center the law of the Wellspring stands as the only law, with a guardian grown to keep it. A dungeon that has reached that point is grown up. Anything it can still do from there it does at its leisure. The bottom of such a place is also one of the few doors through which a living body can walk into another plane, and [[Cosmology & Metaphysics/The Making of the World/The Veil|The Veil]] gives that road and what it costs.

**What makes it grow faster.** People. Every crew that dies down there leaves its reserve in the ground and its memory in the walls, and hard pulling at the top thins the ground at the face and brings the spawn up after it. **A Well worked hard breeds more, and a Well left alone breeds deeper**, and between those two the entire trade of Core keeping has to find a living.

**The shape of a dungeon follows the ground, and the ladder is the same one everywhere.** Each band is a rung on the sites ladder, and the numbers below are the same ones the hunting halls use.

| Depth | Ground | What the survey calls it | The spawn there | Commission Tier | What the ground asks of you if you mean to stay |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **The Mouth** | Freshet | Active Concentration, at its edge | Hollow to E | T2, Green Sigil | Journeyman |
| **The Upper Workings** | Millrace | Active Concentration | D | T3 to T4, Green or Blue | Adept |
| **The Lower Workings** | Riptide | Active Concentration, Veil beginning to thin | C | T4 to T5, Blue | Expert |
| **The Deeps** | Whirlpool | Veil-Thin Nexus | B | T5 to T6, Blue or Gold | Master |
| **The Heart** | Fountainhead | Wellspring Core | A, and the guardian above them | T6 to T8, Gold or Obsidian Seal | Grandmaster |

A Commission Tier is the Accord's danger rating, and a Sigil is the license color a hunter must carry to engage lawfully. Read the two columns on the ends together, because the gap between them is the first thing every hall teaches. **On every floor you can kill what lives there one rank before you can safely stand there.** An Apprentice party can break masks at the Mouth, on ground that asks for a Journeyman, and it has an afternoon to do it in. A Millrace is survivable for an afternoon and not for a season, and at a Whirlpool a Spirit-type onset gives you no warning at all. So every crew works by the glass, and the turn of that glass has ended more careers than the masks have.

### Territories: the dungeon with no roof

Wellspring Cores are also said to lie in vast territories, and "said" is doing honest work in that sentence. Some regions of the Inner World read like a dungeon laid flat: an edge you can walk across without noticing, bands that thicken as you go on, stock that changes with the bands, and a center nobody has mapped. You walk in where you would otherwise climb down. The [[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Scourge of Hell|Scourge of Hell]] is the great example, and the forest of the Unpapered, in autumn for as long as anyone can say, is the argument nearest to hand. Crews call such a place a *lying-down*. The Guild has never confirmed a Core in one, because confirming it would require somebody to return with the reading.

## What It Looks Like

Three questions, in this order: how does the ground take you, which Wellspring is it, and how far down are you.

### How the ground takes you

| Type | What you see | What you feel | Which crews die in it |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Wild** | The dungeon of the songs. Stairs of floating stone, rivers going the wrong way, a gallery with a sky | The body knows first. Your pack shifts, a tool hums in your belt, metal tastes wrong. Your Crystal reads normal | Clever ones, who explain the rock away |
| **Spirit** | An orchard, a nice dry cellar, a chapel with good light. **Nothing is wrong with it** | Heat in the hands, a sudden happiness, a nosebleed, a working half-forming that you are sure you decided on | Confident ones, who are having the best day of their lives |
| **Fate** | Nothing whatever. A sound stair, a dull gallery, and a surveyor's plan that keeps working until the one time it matters | Nothing in the body, and nothing on the fork. Your luck goes strange, oaths bite, and you keep meeting the same two strangers on a stair with one way down | Lucky ones, and anyone who swore something on the way in |
| **Medium** | All of it at once. The stream runs backward, your dead mother is on your mind, and the dice have stopped behaving | Everything is wrong and each symptom blames the other | Sensible ones, who sit down until it passes |

The Spirit dungeon is the one to fear, and the worst thing about it is how few people believe that on the way in. A crew that comes up from one reports an easy job and a wonderful feeling about the next. A Fate dungeon shows up in nobody's report at all. It shows in the hall's ledger, as three seasons of crews whose luck was too good, and then a season when the same crews stop coming back. Nothing a Fate ground does is worth a working to it, and a sound crew with a kept harness, paid shares and two ways out gives it very little to hold.

### By Wellspring

*Each of the sixty has its own hue and its own ground, and [[The Magic System/The Foundations/The Sixty Wellsprings/The Sixty Wellsprings|the register]] has them all. These are the ones crews meet most, sorted by kind.*

| Kind | What the place becomes | On the register | Bring |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Wild**, the hot ones | Slag-glazed galleries in ember orange. A Solfatara dungeon wears yellow crust on every wall, spits steam from the seams and stinks of rotten eggs and hot minerals. A Pyreveil one smells of banked coals, and nobody sweats there, which should alarm you. A cold site runs the other way, heat pulled out of the world, blue ice and a cold that drinks your lamp. That one is no Wellspring of the Sixty at all but Titan-touched ground, the Titan Vohrin's own power pooled in the rock, and the register files it among the Wild Wellsprings under [[The Magic System/The Foundations/The Sixty Wellsprings/Wild Wellsprings|a marked note]] | Tinder-fox | Water, even footing, people with no Crystal to set off |
| **Wild**, the high ones | A Cymorath dungeon goes upward, wind-whistling towers and trails drawn on the air, and a Judicium one rings arc-blue, with colored fringes on every straight line and hair standing up | Griffons, and whatever falls on you | Rope, and no iron you cannot afford to lose |
| **Wild**, the heavy ones | Near-black Abython halls where loose things drift and a dropped wedge floats away at chest height. Petralon floors that press you down until the stair feels twice as long. Concentric rings in every pool | Sump-toad | Loose silt, a horse-hoist, a good bait lantern |
| **Wild**, the stone ones | Ochre and amber strata, grit on the hand. Basilithe ground holds one pure pitch in the bones and draws standing waves in the dust. Terranova ground knits itself, so your chalk marks and your wedges are gone by morning and the broken door is whole again | Tolling Crab | A tuning bar, and no faith in your map |
| **Wild**, the green ones | Chlorophyll green and the smell of cut grass, wood creaking as it grows, the walls in leaf | Reaching Briar | Axes, fire if you dare, and a very clear idea of which way you came |
| **Wild**, the dark ones | The color that should be there has been stolen. Vantabriel galleries where your lamp dims though the oil is full, and Nihiloth rooms where it goes out | Lamp-drinker | Noise, a crowd, a white flare |
| **Spirit** | Nothing to see, or very little, and every crew reads the wrong thing first. Thaloré ground is the deepest teal and dry as a loft, and a grief settles on the whole party that belongs to nobody in it. Hypnather galleries are simply very restful. Oneirion ones have good beds. On Lethegraal ground the crew stops being able to say why it came down, and a Nyxial post is where a frightened sentry stops being frightened, of anything | Mourning Pike | Somebody outside to say what you came for, a harpoon, and a slow walk out |
| **Fate** | Violet rooms that read as ordinary rooms, and a passage that always happens to be the way you meant to go. Ruled lines cut in the stone that never quite meet. Transference ground passes things along, and you come up with somebody else's debt. On Oblation ground the stair asks a toll and you find you have paid it, and on Somnalis ground every plan crosses another plan at the worst landing | Leveling Leech | Knotted line, two people counting aloud who are allowed to disagree, and nothing you have sworn |
| **Medium** | Hard blue-white glare down polished Aurevane corridors, shadows split in two, one clean high tone like a wet finger on a glass rim. Anamnesis walls with afterimages of what once happened on them, a dry whisper like old pages, the smell of an attic. Phreatis beads dew on your sleeve and smells of cellar stone, and you begin to remember things. Mortalis is venous black-red, grave soil and a cold at the edge of everything | Glarewing, Roll-caller | Smoked glass, the pay-book left upstairs, and somebody outside to tell you whose memories those are |

### By depth

Color tells you where you are, on the same smelting scale as a mask. At the Mouth a gallery is smoke brown with a tint under the murk. At the Upper Workings it is warm dark gray with the color suggesting itself along the seams. By the Lower Workings the true color veins through the rock and flickers when something rouses, and in the Deeps the walls are silver-gray suffused with it, bright enough to throw their hue on your face. People who have seen the Heart and can still describe things say the color bleaches to white at the edges, and they say it wistfully. Crews have an old rule for all of this: count the colors, and when the wall is brighter than the last wall, ask yourself what you are being paid.

## What Lives In It

**Well-spawn** are the proper inhabitants, born masked and bred along the Well's law, and they are why a hall pays anyone to go down. They go toward the pull, shallow ones with no more plan than a moth, deep ones with a very good plan. [[The Bestiary/The Corrupted and the Risen/Well-spawn|Their own page]] has the masks, the ladder and the counters.

**Strays.** A badger that dug too far, a hound that followed a crew in. It comes out strange, short-lived and unable to breed true, and wears no mask. It enters the register under Natural Beasts, or under Aberrations if corruption made it, and it can still have your face off.

**Echo Beasts.** From the Deeps down, the visitors' own grief and rage take form and walk for a while, and go when the thinning closes. Every long-serving crew has met somebody it recognized.

**Spirit Beasts.** Formed from Wellspring resonance, carrying a mind, and capable of making an alliance. A very old dungeon may have one sitting in it who regards the whole place as its garden and the crews as weather. Be polite.

**Wyrms.** A wyrm steadies the Aether around it, a living anchor on rich ground, and a big one may have dug half of what a crew takes for galleries. Mining clans have made blood-oaths with them for safe passage, and a crew that kills the wyrm in a dungeon may find the ground it was holding down goes off like a dropped pot of lamp oil. See [[The Bestiary/The Wild/The Draconic Lineage|The Draconic Lineage]].

**Speakers.** The rare deep spawn who talk, in the voices of the people the Well has killed. They bargain and they lie in the same breath. Every one on file has wanted to go up.

**The ones who stayed.** The register has no entry for them and every crew knows about them. A practitioner who holds too long at a Riptide or below is retuned, and from the inside that feels like finally understanding your own gifts. They may have a whole camp down there, cooking, keeping a watch, very glad to see you, with their coats a color they never used to be. Nobody at any hall will tell you what they are owed.

## Who Clears Them

**Hunting halls** license the hunters, post the board and pay on the mask. A hall is a stone building with a green door and a grudge against the hall across the square, and the hall's color is worn in the street and sung against in the tavern. **Chartered companies** undercut them by putting one licensed captain over a party of cheap crossbows, and in the Hundred Crowns and along the Thresh you can hire an entire free company by the season. **Holdout guilds** keep their own boards and grades and are often older and richer than the Accord's. **Unlicensed crews** take what no licensed party will touch, for whoever cannot afford a fee.

**Sects** go down for their own reasons, since a first descent is how a school bloods its young. Each runs its own dungeon the way a club runs its home ground: school colors on the stair, a song for the walk down, a grudge against the school that holds the next valley, and old boys in the taverns telling you how much harder it was in their day. Rich lineage halls keep a small one in the family as a training yard, and lose a child to it about once a generation, and go on keeping it.

**Crowns** rarely clear anything with their own soldiers and always claim to have done. A levy on a stair panics at the first smoke, and the proper use of a king is to pay.

### The crew

Six to twelve is usual, because a dozen is the most one caller can talk out. A proper crew is more than fighters, and the people who get left off the song are the ones who bring the fighters home.

| Post | What they do | What they are called in the street |
|---|---|---|
| **Captain** | Holds the license and signs the instrument, and answers for everyone. Reads the mask before anyone looses | Whatever the crew is called that week |
| **Caller** | Stays at the lamp-line (the last marked point the crew will trust) with the glass, outside the worst of the ground, and talks people out by voice, naming what is really there. A contradiction between what the caller sees and what you report is the whole of the diagnosis | The Mother |
| **Fork** | Carries the assay fork, a brass instrument that rings truer as the ground thickens, and reads the band | Ears |
| **Sealwright** | Ward-chalk for the night, and the seal at the end. Holds the shallow lines off a sleeping party. A speaker will sit outside the chalk and talk | Chalky |
| **Porter** | Unawakened, usually. Carries everything. No Crystal for an Ignivale ground to set off and no Gnosis reading to argue with, and generally the first to say this is wrong. **A porter who stops talking is a reading** | The man with the tent poles |
| **Mask-runner** | The fastest legs in the crew, who takes the bagged shards to the hall the same day, because a hall pays only while the color holds | Heels |

### Hired, ranked and bet on

**The hire.** Postings go up on the board at a hall, pinned three deep, and on tavern doors as hand-copied bills. A big clearing is raised as a venture with subscribers and shares, an adventurer being a person who ventures capital, so that a bishop, a widow and a pair of bakers may own a third of a crew's luck and come down to the stair to shout advice. The crew holds shares and does not draw a wage. That is why a crew in the Lower Workings will go one more gallery than is wise, and why it bribes its caller's glass. Hiring happens at the Lamp Fairs each spring, where captains stand on barrels and lie, and a young practitioner with a good coat can be signed by lunchtime and dead by harvest.

**The rank.** A hunter's license is the Sigil, Green through Gold, and Obsidian if you are the sort of person nobody discusses. The street ranks a crew by what it brings up, in the same slang the mask-clerks use. A **smoke crew** works Mouths. A **vein crew** has been to the Lower Workings and come back with most of itself. A **deep crew** gets its name painted over a tavern door with the tally beside it. Anybody who says they run a **bright crew** is either a legend or trying to sell you something, and "bright" is usually spoken as advice to leave. Every crew has an epithet that travels ahead of it, often unflattering, and the Saffron Hoods and Scarlet Aprons have spent generations proving that hatred is cheaper than advertising.

**The book.** Bookies follow crews the way gulls follow boats. The **depth book** takes bets on how far a crew gets, the **long book** on how many come up, and the **race book** on which crew reaches a new Heart first, with a clerk at each stair who writes down the color of every mask coming out. Heralds cry a great clearing from the market cross for a fee, minstrels have it by the next fair with the color wrong and the dead doubled, and a crew that wins a famous descent will find its bar bill paid in one town and a bounty on it in the next. Betting against your own crew is held to be very bad luck, and the crews that do it have the best-kept books in the trade.

## How a Clearing Goes

1. **The look.** A Reconnaissance commission, the survey's Protocol III with a crew attached: one person in, one outside, marking the perimeter. The kind and the Wellspring are named, or the hall will not post it. A Medium dungeon goes to the top of the board and the price goes up.
2. **The kill-out.** An Extermination commission, floor by floor, from the Mouth down, on the rule that you read the mask first and lure the thing up off its ground if you can. Bait lanterns on ropes, drawn up a shaft at the gallop, have killed more big spawn than any arbalest. Salted ground helps, worked by hedge-workers so that Aether will not pool on it, and so does bled ground, pulled empty. On its own Well a spawn stands one rung higher than off it.
3. **The Deeps.** Here the work passes to Masters with crews behind them, and the speakers come out to negotiate. Break the mask of anything bred at this depth and the body comes apart into raw Aether and sinks away down the Well, leaving a gray split shard. Whether what the Well breeds next is the same creature, nobody has been able to prove.
4. **The guardian.** Something guards every Heart. It is what the Well grew to keep its own center, bred along that Well's law, no two alike, with A-Grade spawn for a household and every crew that ever came down for its education. The rolls say *the guardian* and the taverns say *the landlord*. **Kill it and that is the true clearing:** the law at the center has nothing left to hold it in place, the deep lines stop breeding true, and the Core lies open for whoever can stand beside it. **Almost nobody clears a Heart.** The law down there rewrites anyone below Grandmaster standing who stays, and from the inside it is the truest thing that ever happened to them, while the guardian has the whole ground on its side and has seen your tricks.
5. **The ring.** What people do when they cannot. A Containment commission. A Sealwright and a keeper choose a depth where people can stand and seat a bronze ring at the lip of the pool (the ferrule, which limits how wide a sitter can pull) with a brass tundish beside it, a funnel that shows from the door the color of what passes. Everything below that is walled, warded and left alone. A "cleared" dungeon is usually one with its upper floors held and its Heart sealed off and bled from a safe distance, with the guardian still alive behind the seal, still learning.
6. **The keepers.** Somebody moves in, for good. Then it is a Core, and everyone in the next valley starts calling it that as though it had always been one.

## What a Cleared One Is Worth

More than most kings, which is why so few of them are held by kings.

**Hours.** The living is selling time at the pool, by the hour, the season or an old obligation. Rich ground fills a practitioner fast, and half the lamp-wardens in a city top up at a Core they do not own. A modest worked site out-earns a good county in a good year, and when every working in a district depends on its hours, its keepers need never raise their voice. See [[The Magic System/Practice and Institution/Who Pays for the Lamp The Living Crafts|Who Pays for the Lamp]].

**Masks.** A worked dungeon still breeds, so the hall still pays. Prices are on the Well-spawn page: a copper a tail at the Mouth, 2 to 5 gold a mask from the Upper Workings, 10 to 25 from the Lower, 60 to 150 from the Deeps, by contract from the Heart. A dungeon with a town on it has a season, a trade in hammers and a Mask Night.

**The harmonization.** A place where a person can deliberately sit at the edge of a known Wellspring, with a keeper and a baseline, and come away harmonized with it. Sects go to war over this one.

**What is down there.** Dungeons keep what people left in them. Old works, relics of the Great Spirit War (the dead battlefields of the days when Bara, Ashura and Aegor walked in human form), a dead Master's gear with its reputation still on it, an art bottled at the Well and never collected. Every map of a famous dungeon has a room marked on it that has already been looted three times, and the fourth crew always finds something.

**Rent.** Weddings, as above. Cider, cloth dried in a warm gallery, cheese aged at a steady cold, bath-houses, shrines. A dungeon held for a few generations ends up with a street on its roof and rents that rise with the ground, because a map of a city's rents is very nearly a map of its ground.

**What the crew gets.** By custom in the Center and the South the clearing venture takes a third of the hours for nine years, called the **clearers' third**, after which the ground-lord pretends he has forgotten who cleared it. The crew usually sells its third in the first week, to a factor, at a price that looks enormous until they have seen what the factor does with it.

## When the Wardens Stop Being Paid

Keepers can close a site, and they do. A keeper's whole family sits on the steps, the hours stop, and every working in the district that depends on them goes thin within the week. A closing has a useful side: with the ring shut, anything fighting on the Well's ground loses the rung the tie gave it, and a town will pay keepers to close for exactly that reason. It is the ordinary weapon of the trade, and every keeper keeps tending while it lasts, at night, unpaid, for the plain reason that they live there.

The danger comes when the tending stops. The Core-tenders work the lip of the pool in pairs with a fork and a lamp, clearing it and bleeding it a little each shift. Too little and it rises and breeds. Too much and it goes lean and will not recharge by the season. Ward-cutters renew the seals, and a hired crew walks the upper floors every spring. All of them want paying, and when the owner dies, goes to law, or decides the place has been quiet for ninety years and the fee could be spent on bunting, this is what follows. It runs the same way in every register.

| After | What happens | What people say |
|---|---|---|
| **A month** | The tundish runs a shade brighter. The hum of the place changes, and nobody can say how | "Damp" |
| **A season** | The pool stands above the ring. Smoke-brown masks at the lamp-line, things eating the seals. Tinder-foxes and the like in cellars | A copper a tail, and boys make good money |

## Accord Law

Where a circle has signed, the following holds, and where none has, the halls keep their own charters and it holds if you can make it. Past the iron posts of a marker line, the Accord's jurisdiction ends and nothing holds. Full text is in [[Factions, Bloodlines & Institutions/Powers and Polities/The Articles of the Accord, and the Kinds of Polity|The Articles of the Accord]], which never uses the word dungeon. The formulary says *a pool*, and the courts have had to stretch that word over everything else, one lawsuit at a time.

**Grading.** A dungeon is graded by its deepest confirmed band and posted as a Commission of a named Class: Reconnaissance, Extermination, Containment or Retrieval. Nobody may lawfully engage above the Sigil on the posting. Anything at T8 or T9 goes under the Obsidian Seal, to the Black Concord, and is not posted anywhere you can read it.

**Reporting.** A new Mouth is reported at the nearest hall within one turn, or the holder of the ground is amerced (fined to his means). Most landholders report late, because a reported Mouth loses them the use of their cellar and gains them a searcher. A Medium-type site is reported first, ahead of all else.

**The instrument.** A clearing venture binds only on formulary wording (Article VI, and a clause improved is a clause void), before compensated witnesses (VII), registered within one turn (X), with the saving clause for third parties (IX), which is the clause a dead crewman's widow will bring to court. Crews who improve the clause over beer find out what it costs.

**The ring.** Once a ring is seated the site is a pool under Title IV. No bore but the standard (XVI). Every ring has its tundish, and covering one is a separate and heavier offense than widening the bore, because that is done to conceal (XVII). Breaking a seal is a term in the cells (XVIII).

**The dead leg.** A stretch of a round given up shall be capped and reported within one turn, and the fine lands on the owner, "whoever that now is" (XIX). Courts have read this onto abandoned galleries since the middle of the count. That wording is why a lapsed dungeon changes hands so cheaply, why the buyer rarely reads to the end, and why several Midmarch kings have turned out to own a catastrophe by marriage.

**The masks.** The ground belongs to the ground-lord, the mask to whoever broke it, and the color to nobody, which is the whole of the law on the matter after Pennyhythe.

**The check.** Essence Resonance Checks are mandatory after a mission, to catch what the ground has done to a crew's Crystals, and baselines are required before one. Crews dodge the first and forge the second. Nobody has ever been required to baseline a porter.

**The porter clause.** Here the law stops being funny. Under the Beastkin Classification Provision a licensed labor-stock porter, bought or hired by the season at Verath or its like, is equipment on the instrument. He cannot be a party, cannot hold a share, and cannot be named in the saving clause, and the roll writes him down as *nemo*, nobody, along with the rope. The deep crews of the Center go down with Catkin and Bearkin at the back of the line carrying the poles. They are the first to say this is wrong, and nobody is obliged to listen, and they are the reason Brokencollar has so many people in it who can read a fork.

**Speakers.** A bargain with a speaking spawn is void at law. The Accord has never said what remedy it has in mind.

## How a Measurewright Explains It

In the winter of 688 Magister Odalric Penhallow gave the Gradient Lectures at Sealhall to packed benches, and he remains the finest explainer of a dungeon the Guild has produced. His account deserves attention, since a good part of it is true.

A dungeon, said Penhallow, is sediment. Aether at a site runs from thick ground to thin at a rate set by how steep the difference is, the same law that spreads a drop of dye through still water (diffusion, governed by what a modern hand would call Fick's law). Add a source below and a leak above and you get a steady slope, thickest at the bottom, and any lake with warm water lying on cold will show you how such a slope settles into layers with sharp edges between them. **That gives you the five bands.** Each band then weeds what can live in it, the way salt weeds a marsh, and over generations each grows its own stock, with clarity of mask tracking density because a harder law leaves a cleaner print. **That gives you the ladder.** As for the galleries, chemists have long known that a solution soaking through a gel drops its salts in regular rings with nobody arranging them, and rain makes a limestone cave without a plan. **That gives you the rooms.** There is no will in a dungeon, he told them, thumping a lectern shaped like a Tolling Crab, any more than there is in a bog, and a clever man could read one with a fork and run it from a counting-house.

The bands are right, the weeding is right, and every hall in the world has quietly used his tables since. Then a town called Reedmarch, holding a troubled Millrace under lease, hired him to prove the rest. His prediction followed cleanly from the slope: pull evenly and hard from the top, flatten the gradient, and the breeding must fall. They pulled, with every warden for thirty miles on double hours. By the second season the entire stock of the Lower Workings had come up the shaft after the pull, and most of Reedmarch is now a floor. A Well worked hard breeds more, and his equations had no place for an animal that walks toward its dinner.

The rest of it has troubled better minds than the crews'. A gradient does not explain why a gallery cleared and collapsed in one century grows back in the next with the same turns. A ring in a gel does not explain a stair with a step height of nine inches, in country no one has ever dug, cut for the stride of no people on any roll. It does not explain the handrail. A bog does not let the first party pass to take the porters at the back. Penhallow had answers, and they were very good. At last, to settle it, he went down into a Riptide himself, with a fork and a good staff, and he came back up nine days later in excellent spirits with his coat a shade he had never worn. He has since published four further volumes showing that the dungeon's behavior is entirely natural, and that it is, on the evidence, rather fond of us. The Guild still funds the chair. His porter left the service that same month, and has not been heard to speak.

The current opinion in the taverns where Measurewrights drink is that a dungeon is **a Domain with nobody home**. A Domain is the ground in which a high-Stage practitioner's own law holds, and a Core is a law with no practitioner attached, doing what a law does, extending itself until it is stopped. That explains the bands and the walls, and it invites the question nobody has been willing to fund: a Domain forms around a person's idea of what the world should be, so whose idea is this one, and what does it want with stairs. I have asked three Measurewrights. I got five answers and a bill for the wine.

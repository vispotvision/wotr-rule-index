# The Bestiary

*Fifty-four attested creatures · Spirit-born, Draconic, Natural, Aberrant, Undying, Divine, Well-bred, and the Scourge-born, under protest*
> Every entry answers the same eleven questions, and **the order of the questions is itself a doctrine.** Origin before appearance. **Essence Affinity before abilities.** Weaknesses in the same breath as powers.
>
> **A field guide that tells you what a thing can do without telling you what feeds it has told you nothing.**

The register began with a wager and a one-armed man. At the Hide Fair at Cold Ansty, late in the Long Reckoning, when the upland drovers came down in their red felts to trade fleeces and lie about wolves, a Guild copyist named Piers Wetherby climbed onto a wool-bale with a ledger under his arm and offered a silver to anybody who could show him a beast he had not already written down. By noon he had paid out for a two-headed lamb, which turned out to be a lamb and a half, and refused a goat that could count, which could not. A little after the third bell a griffon came down the sheep-lane on foot. She stood taller than the horse-lines, bronze and gold from beak to shoulder, with the great lion haunches rolling behind, and she smelled of hot feathers and high cold air, and the laughing crowd opened in front of her like a pulled curtain, because at the far end of the lane a drover's boy had a basket, and in the basket, for sale at six coppers, sat something the color of egg yolk with strong opinions about him. She took down an awning, two stalls and a cheese-seller's ladder without hurrying. She took off Piers Wetherby's left arm below the elbow, because he had stepped in close to look at her feet. Then she lifted the basket in her beak, handle and all, and walked out through four hundred people and thirty hunters with boar-spears, and looked at none of them, and none of them followed.

Wetherby wrote the entry that night with the hand he had left, feverish on a heap of fleeces, and the page survives, brown at one corner where he bled on it. It gives what feeds her before it gives what she looks like. Under Weaknesses, in letters that run downhill, it says *leaves the fight to keep the nest. Give back the chick.* Every keeper of this register since has worked to his form, and I have the same rule and a smaller scar.

Read upward and the register changes its nature as it climbs. At the bottom sit the pests and the fen-things, every one of which has eaten somebody's chickens. At the top sit creatures that were old when Bara, Ashura and Aegor still walked in human form, whose names sailors will not say on a falling tide, and those are written here with the same eleven questions, because a thing can be worthy of a hymn and still have a diet.

An entry describes a line, and the animal in front of you has been eating. The Hask Moor drovers will tell you about the she-wolf they called Aunt Teeth, posted in the spring of 702 as an ordinary T3 Greater Wolf at forty silver marks. She ate her pack by midsummer, then the party sent after her, then a wyvern's fledgling, and by the autumn fair she stood as tall as a dray horse with her coat gone the color of a bruised plum, and the bill on every door between Hask and the sea read T5, three hundred gold, *and she has been seen to open a gate.* The bookies had her at nine to two against the Saffron Hoods. Her hide is a coat now, and two towns claim the skull.

## The Entry Skeleton

| **Names** | The High or Old Parunic form, plus whatever the people who actually live near it call the thing. **The two are rarely complimentary of each other** |
|---|---|
| **Type** | Spirit Beast · Draconic Lineage · Natural Beast · Aberration · Undying · Construct · Divine or Archonic Beast · Elemental · Well-spawn · Hybrid · Scourge-born, filed with the Natural Beasts **under protest** |
| **Origin** | Which Age, which Wellspring convergence, and **which Archon or Titan is credited**, credit being a matter of tradition rather than record in most cases |
| **Essence Affinity** | The Wellspring the creature runs on (one of the sixty alchemical and spiritual transformations, the governing idea that shapes how a soul uses Aether, a source of power to pull from, and a type of Aether as well). **This is the operative field.** Everything the thing can do and every way it can be beaten follows from it |
| **Leans on** | The layer of the soul its power runs through, read from its Affinity's kind: a Wild Affinity leans on the Aether Shell and the flesh, a Spirit Affinity on the Essence Core, a Fate Affinity on the Attraction Layer, and a Medium one on whichever the line has grown into. **This tells you who to bring**, and the entries say so road by road |
| **Realm Affiliation** | Where it is native and where it has been recorded straying |
| **Danger Tier** | T1 through T9, **on the same scale as the Commission threat tiers.** A T7 requires a Gold Sigil to lawfully engage |
| **Weaknesses** | Recorded in the same detail as the abilities. **This is what separates a bestiary from a bestiary of rumors** |
| **Historical Encounters** | Named engagements with dates where the Archives hold them, **and folk accounts where they do not** |
| **Cultural Significance** | What the thing means to the people who share ground with it, **which is frequently at odds with what it is** |

## The Danger Tiers

*Lords post bounties by these numbers, heralds cry them from the market cross, and a hunter's fee and her funeral are both priced off the same column.*

| Tier | Designation | Attested |
|---|---|---|
| **T1** | (none entered) | The rat in the grain store and the fox at the hens. No hall has ever opened a Commission for one, so the register asks its questions of nothing here |
| **T2** | Minor / Moderate Threat | Zombies · Tanuki · Tinder-fox · Leveling Leech |
| **T3** | Moderate Threat | Greater Wolf · Sasquatch · Dryads · Lid-crawler |
| **T4** | High Threat | Unicorn · Harpy · Skeletal Folk · Neiman Lion · Psy-Apes · Ghouls · Bai Ze · Hippogrif · Siren · Pegasus · Glarewing · Reaching Briar · Haze-kite · Stilt |
| **T5** | Extreme Threat | Bicorn · Griffon · Goliath · Raiju · Lamia · Living Armor · Minotaur · Cyclops · Basilisk · Cockatrice · Hellhound · Manticore · Sump-toad · Tolling Crab · Mourning Pike · Greywalker |
| **T6** | Domain Threat | Wyvern · Sea Serpent · Arachne · Wendigo · Lamp-drinker |
| **T7** | Realm Hazard | Wyrm · Night Gaunt · Gorgon · Phoenix |
| **T8** | Cataclysmic Entity | Hydra · Voidwyrm · Cerberus |
| **T9** | Apocalyptic Beast | **Leviathan · Lich · Kraken** |
| **T3–T8** | Variable | **Elemental**, the only entry in the register whose tier is a range rather than a figure, running from a minor natural disturbance to a colossus that reshapes landscape |
| **Not entered** | A blank row | **The things in the dry beds** of the Scourge of Hell. Nobody has come back to name them, and what they leave is half-carts and very clean bones |

**The rung beside the tier.** A Danger Tier is the Commission's rating: what it takes to lawfully engage a creature. The Bestiary ladder in [[The Magic System/Fracture of Worlds — The Living System/X. The Tier Ladders (Part Twenty-Four)|The Tier Ladders]] reads the same creature by what it can break, from Hearth-pest to Sky-eater, and by what lifts it: reach, persistence and the ground that feeds it. The two often disagree, and the difference is where a hunter finds the answer.

## Monsters Climb

**They level and they evolve.** A monster gains Levels by killing, each other included, and changes form at its own thresholds, and when it does the register enters a new name beside the old one. It stands on the same Level and Grade scale as a person. A tier on this page is what the line usually reaches, and the bill on the door is what this one has become.

**They are everywhere.** Monsters move and live across the whole Inner World, and beasts more widely still. Every realm has its bad road and its song about it. The [[Geography & the Four Quarters/The Bearing and the Holding/The Inner World — The Northern Shield/The Scourge of Hell|Scourge of Hell]] is the one area known for extremely dangerous ones.

**They get famous.** A famous monster earns a name, an epithet and a bounty, and lords post it, heralds cry it, minstrels get it wrong and bookies give odds. On the survey of the Scourge the great ones carry a small crown on the map, and each of those is its own entry in everything but the paperwork.

**They have windows.** A window is the soul reporting its numbers to its owner, unseen by anyone else, and a beast's soul reports like any other, though what a beast makes of it no one has asked. A faculty whose Gnosis Perception outranks the creature's Resilience Ward by Grade can read it like a person's, and the rest of us are estimating aloud at speed. A hunter who could read Aunt Teeth in her last autumn would have seen something like this:

```
[Moor Tyrant, formerly Greater Wolf]
[Level 163 · Grade B]
[Strike Force: B · Celerity: A]
[Pack Hunter: Level 7]
[Reserve: 41%]
```

The last line is the one to read, because an ordinary wolf has no reserve to report. It has a sealed Shell and a stone. By her last autumn Aunt Teeth had cracked hers, and had something to spend.

**They are worth money.** A monster's core is in a way its Soul Crystal, worth more the stronger the monster, and an alchemist renders it into Essence Drafts for tempering. Its parts make gear. The rows and prices are in [[Materials, Alchemy & Trade/Materials/Hide, Horn and Bone|Hide, Horn and Bone]], and the Drafts in [[Materials, Alchemy & Trade/Alchemy/The Common Drafts|The Common Drafts]].

**Many come from below.** A dungeon grows down in floors around a Core (a site where one Wellspring's Aether pools) with something guarding the heart, and it is alive and learning, and most of what this register lists either came out of one or is on its way back in. See [[The Bestiary/Dungeons|Dungeons]].

**The Scourge-born are the register's standing embarrassment.** The great herds of the Scourge of Hell breed true and wear no masks, which the register's own rules say should not happen, since an animal warped by dense ground is supposed to come out short-lived and sterile. They have been at it so long that they stopped being warped and became a kind of animal. The **Lid-crawler**, the **Haze-kite**, the **Stilt** and the **Greywalker** are filed with the Natural Beasts, under protest, and above a certain size each holds a range and treats you as its own until you leave or die.

## What Has a Soul

*The physicians' question, which hunters ask with a spear in their hands. Every soul has three layers: the **Aether Shell**, where Aether (mana) comes in and Essence (spiritual energy) goes out; the **Essence Core**, the seat of what a thing is; and the **Attraction Layer**, its share of fate. [[The Magic System/The Soul and the Climb/The Three Paths Routing, Recognition and the Gate|The Three Paths]] gives the long account. What differs across this register is how much of each is working.*

| What it is | Aether Shell | Essence Core | Attraction Layer | What follows |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **The unwoken** (a baker, a porter) | Sealed. Waking cracks it, and most never wake | Awake, holding a trickle of Essence it cannot spend | A small share, enough to swear on | A window with a Level and a few trade skills, and no workings. Pressure falls on them by the Aura table. A Draft reaches them through the Core |
| **A beast** (born of a mother, not yet much of anything) | Sealed | Awake. What comes in from the ground and its kills has nowhere to go, so it bands up as the stone | Thin, holding a mate, a pack or a range and little else | No aura and no reserve. What a saturated line does is passive, and leaks through the seal: a cold, a dread, a glow in a mane |
| **A monster** (a beast that has climbed) | **Cracked**, by an evolution or a Level threshold, and a small vent on a great store | Banded and old, with a stone worth cutting out | Thin, thickening where the line runs on a Fate Wellspring | An aura, a named howl, a Reserve line on the window. This is the difference between the wolf and Aunt Teeth |
| **Well-spawn** | Sealed in the shallow lines, cracked in the deep ones | Flooded with the Well's Aether until the line lives by the Well's law, and it carries a stone | Thin, until the Deeps, where a line gathers a mind out of what the Well remembers | The mask stands where a glyph would and holds the law in place. Break it. See [[The Bestiary/The Corrupted and the Risen/Well-spawn|Well-spawn]] |
| **A Spirit Beast** | Whatever it has grown to keep a body, often little, and sometimes no body at all | Whole. It was formed from a Wellspring's resonance and it began here, with a mind and a memory | Whole enough to bargain, bond and bless | A hitter can kill what it is wearing and has then told it to leave. Its stone, where there is one, is seldom worth the valley's opinion |
| **A Divine or Archonic beast** | Genuine and very clean, carrying far more than any practitioner's | Genuine, and often a single function where a person has a biography | Genuine, and bonded to a place, a route or an office | Graded exactly as a person is. These are the ones that answer a summons on their own terms |
| **A Celestial** | Whole, folded out of the Lawbell's resonance with no upbringing behind it | Whole, carrying what its function requires and, in the young ones, very little else | **Externally anchored**, tied to the Throne-network in place of a share of its own | Administration made flesh. See [[Cosmology & Metaphysics/Faith and the Powers Above/The Celestial Host|The Celestial Host]] |

## Who to Bring

A creature's Leans on line reads the same way a person's Path does, and the three roads beat one another the way [[The Magic System/The Soul and the Climb/The Three Paths Routing, Recognition and the Gate|The Three Paths]] says they do. Each entry below gives the short form. Stage, ground and preparation decide which of it holds on the day.

| It leans on | Bring | Why | What goes wrong |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The Shell** (a Wild Affinity: matter, force, heat) | A **Spirit** hand, or a **Fate** hand with time to set | Spirit goes past hide and plate, since Durability does not cover a soul. Fate moves what its charge depends on (the footing, the pack's timing, the wind), so the blow never arrives. A **Body** fighter meets it at its own strength and needs to be the stronger | Spirit dies out of position, and a Wild thing's whole idea is to close. Fate cannot move a sound avalanche, and some of these are sound |
| **The Core** (a Spirit Affinity: meaning, memory, mind) | A **Body** fighter if it has a body, because a spear does not care what it means. A **Fate** hand, because a thing that survives by being right needs only one spoiled read | If it has no body, a blade has nothing to bite, and only a Spirit hand can reach it | Body fighters walk in without a ward on their own Core. **Spirit** against Core is a straight contest of souls, and it has been doing this longer |
| **The Layer** (a Fate Affinity: bonds, debts, odds) | A **Body** fighter, for force and soundness: a well-kept harness offers no strap to give. A **Spirit** hand, since every pull is made by a soul and shows on the Core before it lands | Kill the thing the bond runs through and the bond has nothing to pull with | **Fate** against Fate is a contest of weights at about equal cost to both, which is why nobody with a choice does it |
| **Medium** (at home on all three) | One of each, and read it first | It may answer on any layer, and it usually answers on the one you did not bring | Everything, which is why a Medium site goes to the top of the board |

Set that beside the three old answers below and the register sorts itself. A Sealwright is a Spirit trade (a Writ on the thing's law), a priest is usually a Spirit hand with a congregation behind him, and the long march is a Body answer to ground, open to anybody with boots.

## What the Register Shows at Scale

> **Almost everything above T6 is a Wellspring event wearing an animal.** The Leviathan, the Kraken, the Hydra, the Voidwyrm and the Cerberus all owe their danger, far more than their size, to **their relationship to a Wellspring site, a Crevice, or a plane boundary.**
>
> **The Lich is the exception, and the Lich is the exception because it used to be a person.**

**The weaknesses cluster.** Read across the register and the same three vulnerabilities recur: **Essence-dampening**, **removal from the native Wellspring** (getting the creature off the ground where its kind of Aether pools, or starving it there), and **radiant or holy current.** The practical counter to the majority of the register is therefore seldom a weapon. **It is a Sealwright, a long march, or a priest.**

**Corruption is a lineage rather than a condition.** The Bicorn, the Voidwyrm, the Wendigo and the Blackened share a grammar: **something that was one thing has been converted by Abyssal, Void or Crevice contact into something that cannot go back.** *The register treats this as taxonomy. The Heresiology would treat the same material as a diagnosis.*

**And the register is honest about the thing bestiaries usually lie about.** Griffons abandon a fight to protect a nest, as Piers Wetherby could have told you with the arm he had left. Unicorns evade rather than engage. **The Bai Ze is physically fragile and avoids conflict entirely.** *Four of the register's most feared entries would rather leave.*

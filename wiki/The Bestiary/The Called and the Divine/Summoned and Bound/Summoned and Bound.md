# Summoned and Bound

*Called things, entered as creatures. What a summoner keeps on a chain and what the chain is actually made of.*
> The register's other six sections describe things that exist whether or not anyone wants them to. **This section describes things that arrive because they were asked.** The difference matters exactly once, and it matters on the day the asking stops working.
>
> **A summon entry is a Bestiary entry with a second creature in it: the person holding the other end.**

Hamo Pockle called up a wine-steward for the Drapers' election feast, and it remains the most expensive thing anyone in Ostlergate has ever done at a party. He was a Stage VIII hedge summoner with a coat the color of pale ale and a hired hall, and what stepped out of the chalk circle in the buttery was a tall figure of amber light in a steward's shape, which bowed to the new Master, to huge applause. Hamo, three cups in, told it to keep every cup full, and went off to dance. It kept every cup full. It kept them full when the guests stopped lifting them, and when the guests started covering them with their hands it filled the hands. For the eleven minutes the form could hold, it went up and down that hall with its arms held out and the new Master's six best tuns running through it, while the floor went purple to the ankle, his gown of office turned the color of a bruise, the Drapers' banner came down in the flood, and a rural dean went over on the stairs and broke his hip.

The new Master sued. Hamo pleaded that the thing had done it, and pointed to where it had been. The Reeve of Ostlergate, who had been at the feast and was still pink to the knee, found against him in a sentence every Commission clerk can recite: *the thing did as it was told, so find me who told it.* That sentence is this section. A called thing is a creature like any other in the register, with a body, a diet and a way of being beaten, and it also has somebody's name on it.

## How These Entries Differ

**Two fields carry weight here that carry none anywhere else in the Bestiary.**

**Crystal Provenance** decides whether the thing has a soul of its own or is wearing a piece of somebody else's. A Donated construct has no Crystal (Soul Crystal, the organ that refines Aether into Essence) to record. A Native entity has one and had it long before anyone learned its name. **Everything the entry can honestly say about resilience, refusal and death follows from that single line.** A bound thing also has its own window, with its Level, its terms and its evolution, and the contract behind it can be stolen. [[The Disciplines/Schools and Bloodline Arts/The Spirit Summoning Arts/The Spirit Summoning Arts|The Spirit Summoning Arts]] has that law, and [[The Magic System/The Soul and the Climb/Windows and the Sheet|Windows and the Sheet]] has the window.

**Bond Class** decides how much of an order the thing can decline. The Bestiary's other sections never need this, because a wyvern is not under instruction. **Half the entries in this section can refuse, and the register should say so in the same breath it says what they can do.**

The standard eleven questions still apply. Origin before appearance, Essence Affinity before abilities, weaknesses in the same breath as powers. **A summon entry adds Provenance, Bond, the Draft (the four-glyph working) that calls it, and the cost the caller carries afterward**, and the cost is the field most likely to be left thin.

## Danger Tier

*Rated as though the thing were loose and hostile, on the same T1 through T9 scale as the rest of the register and the Commission threat tiers. A summon's tier does not drop because somebody has a leash on it. The leash is a fact about the summoner.*

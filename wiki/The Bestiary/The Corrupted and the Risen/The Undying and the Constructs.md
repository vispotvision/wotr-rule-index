# The Undying and the Constructs

*Five entries · what is still moving when it should not be, and what never lived at all*

Dob Tanner of Grey Marsh sat up at his own wake, in the second verse of the burying hymn, with marsh-marigolds on his chest and his best yellow coat buttoned wrong, and forty mourners cheered. His widow had paid a hedge-worker six coppers to keep him fresh until the cousins arrived, on ground the hedge-worker swore was sound, and for the space of one long breath the whole village believed it had bought a miracle at a very fair price. Then Dob bit the priest. The priest went home and bit his sister. By the next market day the Blight of Grey Marsh was walking the causeway in its feast-day ribbons with the Concord's quarantine companies burning bridges behind it, and a bill on every chapel door between the marsh and the sea offered a silver for each right hand brought in. That bill founded the Second Burial, thirteen cheerful ruffians in undertakers' black and red sashes, with a priest, a handcart and rather more lamp oil than the law allows. They sing while they work. The song has Dob in it, and his widow, who to this day wants her six coppers back.

The hedge-worker had nothing to do with the rising, as it turned out, and was hanged anyway. The trouble lay in the marsh, and it lay in what a corpse is supposed to do.

> Four of the five are **holes in the Crossing** (the nine days after a death, in which a person comes apart into three things that go their separate ways: the Echo, the residue that goes home to its [[The Magic System/The Foundations/The Sixty Wellsprings/The Sixty Wellsprings|Wellspring]], and the Name-record). A Skeletal Folk, a Zombie, a Ghoul and a Lich are all bodies whose three products **have been prevented from parting**, which is exactly the configuration the necromancy prohibition exists to describe.
>
> **Living Armor is the exception, and Living Armor is the exception because there was never a body.**

## Zombies

> *Khalrak*, the Lost Flesh, in Old Parunic. **Rotwalkers** among frontier survivors.

**Undying, Animated Corpse · T2, Moderate Threat · Mortalis and Nihiloth, fueled by lingering necromantic energies · battlefield graveyards and plague-ridden wastelands**

**Leans on** · the Shell, by a Medium Mortalis and a Wild Nihiloth working what is left of the flesh. *Body:* the head, the knees and a good pitchfork. *Spirit:* nothing is home, and a priest can tell the ground so. *Fate:* they follow the last thing they were told, so change it.

Corpses reanimated by corrupted Wellspring energies, necromantic ritual, or abyssal taint. **Unlike Skeletal Folk they retain decayed flesh and sluggish motor function**, driven by primal hunger or necromantic command. **Their numbers swell rapidly in plague zones and on battlefields scarred by Abyssal or Corrupted [[The Magic System/First Steps/The Core Vocabulary|Essence]].**

Decaying corpses with rotting flesh, exposed bone and festering wounds. Jerky uncoordinated movement, **eyes glowing faintly with corrupted Essence or pale blue light.** *Clothing and armor remnants hint at their past lives*, and a practiced crew checks the purse before the head, since the dead pay no fees and the living do.

**Mindless and relentless.** They wander in hordes and attack any living creature they detect. **Their slow steady approach makes them dangerous in numbers**, overwhelming through sheer volume. *Rare variants show signs of minimal instinct or rudimentary coordination.*

**Horde Tactics.** **Contagious Bite:** some carry infectious necromantic plagues. **Endurance**, continuing after severe injury. **Necrotic Aura** slows living creatures and weakens Essence flow.

**Vulnerable to fire, radiant Essence and dismemberment. Slow and uncoordinated against skilled opponents. And their necromantic bindings can be severed to disable them.**

*The Blight of Grey Marsh chronicles a zombie plague that devastated frontier villages. Concord quarantine forces have long worked to contain outbreaks spawned by Crevice taint.*

## Ghouls

**Undying, Ferocious Corpse-Eaters · T4, High Threat · Mortalis and Nihiloth, with residual necromantic influence · cursed graveyards, abyssal borderlands, plague-ravaged zones**

**Leans on** · the Shell, by a Medium Mortalis and a Wild Nihiloth. *Body:* fire, and do not let the wound go bad. *Spirit:* a little is home and it is hungry, and it can be read and baited. *Fate:* a pack depends on the next grave being there.

Corpses cursed or corrupted on Mortalis ground, **spawned where necromantic energies blend with savage hunger. Unlike zombies, ghouls retain agility and ferocity**, hunting living flesh with brutal efficiency.

Gaunt rotting humanoid figures with sharp claws and elongated teeth. **Skin mottled and stretched tight over bone, torn and bloodied.** Eyes glowing with a fierce hungry light; **movements jerky but swift and calculated.**

Savage relentless predators **hunting both living and undead prey.** They move with unnatural speed and cunning, **attacking in packs with coordinated ambush tactics**, and their hunger drives them to desecrate the dead.

**Pack Hunt.** **Feral Claws** tear through flesh and armor. **Essence Corruption:** their bites taint victims with necromantic decay. **Regenerative Hunger** recovers wounds rapidly when feeding on fresh flesh.

**Vulnerable to radiant Essence and fire. Poor resistance to magical wards and purification. And they rely on close combat: ranged attacks disrupt their aggression.**

*The Ghoul Plague of Mirewood devastated frontier villages before Concord forces intervened. Several necromantic cults raise ghouls as shock troops.*

## Skeletal Folk

> *Khal'thar*, Boneshade, in Old Parunic. **The Bonebound** among necromancers.

**Undying, Animated Remnants · T4, High Threat · Mortalis and Anamnesis, with traces of ancestral torment Essence · Abyssal Depths, Material World graveyards, cursed Innerworld tombs**

**Leans on** · the Core by Anamnesis and the Shell by Mortalis, both Medium. What holds the bones is what they remember. *Body:* a hammer, for the time it takes. *Spirit:* tell them what they were and that it is over. *Fate:* an old oath often keeps them standing, and it can be discharged by someone with the standing to do it.

Corpses reanimated through dark Essence rituals or cursed Wellspring taint, often arising from battlefields or tombs gone bad on Mortalis ground. Bound to necromantic masters **or wandering as restless spirits seeking vengeance or purpose.**

> **Unlike mindless undead, many retain fragmented memories and rudimentary intelligence.**

Bare bones and skulls **often encrusted with ancient runes** or wrapped in tattered remnants of once-sacred garments. Some bear lingering aetheric glows **or cracks where corrupted Essence leaks.** Joints grinding with unnatural stiffness; eyes, where present, **burning with cold pale light.**

Relentless and unyielding, operating in packs or under command, **driven by binding curses or lingering hatred.** *Their cold emotionless persistence makes them dangerous foes, attacking in coordinated waves.*

**Bone Shard Barrage** detaches and launches fragments as weapons or barriers. **Essence Lash** extends limbs or creates whips of bone. **Unholy Resilience.** **Necrotic Aura** saps life force and weakens Essence.

**Vulnerable to radiant or purifying Essence. Fragile physical structure: concentrated damage shatters key bones. And they are bound by necromantic rituals: destroying their masters can render them inert.**

*The Bonefield Campaign saw entire companies wiped out by Skeletal Folk raised from ancient war graves. Tales of the Crypt Lords describe powerful necromancers controlling vast skeletal armies beneath the Innerworld.*

## Lich

> *Zar'khan*, Death's Sovereign, in Old Parunic. **The Eternal Wraith** among necromantic circles.

**Undying, Archmage Undead · T9, Apocalyptic Threat · Mortalis, Nihiloth and Anamnesis, intertwined through powerful necromantic rites · ruined fortresses, ancient crypts, shadowed sanctums**

**Leans on** · all three, since it used to be a person, and a Spirit Path one by long habit, on Mortalis, Nihiloth and Anamnesis. *Body:* the bones are the least of it, and getting there is still how most of them die. *Spirit:* the phylactery holds the [[The Magic System/The Soul and the Climb/The Soul and the Climb|Crystal]], and a soul can be reached in it. *Fate:* it has outlasted a great many reasons to die, and each of them is a thread somebody can look for.

> **Powerful necromancers who have bound their souls to phylacteries**, transcending mortality through dark Wellspring rituals.
>
> The transformation grants immense mastery over death and Essence manipulation **and binds them eternally to their arcane prisons.**
>
> *Often once mortal rulers or sorcerers. The only T9 entry in the register that used to be a person.*

Skeletal figures draped in tattered robes embroidered with necromantic glyphs. **Eye sockets glowing with sickly Essence light**, hands clutching arcane staffs or relics. *Some display remnants of their former selves: patches of decayed flesh, spectral overlays hinting at their past lives.*

**Cold, calculating, utterly relentless.** They manipulate armies of the undead, **bend the Aether of Wellspring sites to their will**, and weave complex schemes to extend their influence. **Their intellect and magical prowess make them formidable far beyond mere brute strength.**

**Necromantic Mastery** raises and controls vast undead legions. **Essence Drain** absorbs life force to fuel spells and regeneration. **Arcane Dominion** wields destructive and binding magic across multiple Essence schools. **Phylactery Binding:** immortal unless the phylactery is destroyed.

> **The phylactery is their only true vulnerability.** They require extensive preparation to maintain Essence flows, **and can be weakened by holy or harmonic Essence purges.**

*The Fall of Erebus' Shadow details the war against the lich lord Vharzoth, whose armies nearly overwhelmed the Innerworld's defenses. Numerous Concord campaigns have focused on locating and destroying phylacteries to end their reigns.*

## Living Armor

> *Arkh'zan*, Sentinel Shell, in Old Parunic. **The Bound Sentinel** among magical artificers.

**Construct, Animated Armor · T5, Extreme Threat · Eidolyn and Fixatio, infused with Spirit Rune binding · ancient fortresses, Innerworld sanctums**

**Leans on** · the Shell, by Eidolyn, Wild, and Fixatio, Medium, with no Core of its own unless one was seated in it. *Body:* Penetration above the proof, and then the joints. *Spirit:* find what was seated in it and speak to that, or read the binding rune and spoil it. *Fate:* every strap is a thing that can give, and the armor knows this no better than you do.

Suits of enchanted plate and chain forged in ancient workshops **and animated through potent Wellspring Essence and Spirit Rune Arts.** Created to guard Wellsprings, tombs and arcane repositories. **They possess no flesh but exhibit independent will and relentless dedication to their wards.**

> *Some are remnants of lost orders, still patrolling forgotten ruins. Others serve present-day spellbinders as guardians or shock troops, each one the costly work of a single master's hands, and no two of them agree about the proper way to hold a door.*

Heavy full-plate or ornate segmented armor with arcane glyphs etched into the plates (old work, and a hunter who finds a whole suit of it should ask who made it and when, because nobody at a forge makes such plate today). **Some bear ethereal blue or green Essence glows emanating from inside**; others are cracked or rusted with age. They move with a clanking gait, **wielding swords, axes or polearms forged from rare materials.**

Obedient and unyielding, **following their last command or bound purpose with unerring precision.** Indifferent to pain or exhaustion, **and exhibiting tactical awareness far beyond mundane constructs.**

**Unyielding Guard** · resistant to physical damage, immune to fatigue and pain. **Arcane Strike** · weapon attacks channel bound Wellspring Essence. **Spirit Shield** · generates protective glyph barriers. **Tactical Recall** · **remembers combat scenarios and adapts tactics in prolonged engagements.**

**Vulnerable to Essence-dampening fields and anti-magic wards. Slow and cumbersome beside living combatants. And severing the binding runes or disrupting the Spirit Rune network disables them outright.**

*The Siege of Verdant Bastion recounts Living Armor defending ancient Wellspring shrines against Abyssal invaders. Several Concord companies specialize in dismantling Living Armor contingents through coordinated Essence disruption.* Among artificers they are revered as pinnacle creations of Spirit Rune binding.

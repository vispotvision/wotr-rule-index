# The Reach Dwarves

*Five Peoples of the Surface-Born Dawi*
> *"A mountain only teaches you one kind of up. Everything after the mountain is what you do when the ground stops agreeing with you."*
> — Reach Dwarf saying, origin disputed
> All five branches share the same Titan ancestry and differ in **which of Varūn's faces they claim as their own governing principle.** High Dwarves claim his geometry. Barrel Dwarves claim his fermentation. These five claim his **verticality, his season, his herd-instinct, his stubbornness, and his patience**, respectively.
>
> Each is Varūn's argument that **endurance takes whatever shape the ground demands of it.**

---

## The Kuntur Reach — Islands of the Vertical Claim

A volcanic archipelago claimed by Dawi who did not choose the sea. **They were put there.** A collapse in the deep halls severed a significant population from the tunnel-network entirely, stranding them on the surface with no way back down and no mountain range worth the name anywhere near them.
What they had was a chain of volcanic islands rising out of the sea at wildly different elevations, and a race-deep instinct that told them **altitude was territory, whatever it was made of.** They stopped grieving the mountain and started reading the islands the same way.

### The Vertical Claim

A household holds a foothold at three tiers **simultaneously**, and moves people, goods, and obligation continuously between them rather than relocating as a body.
| Tier | What it holds |
|---|---|
| **The Low Reef** | Tidal forges built into geothermal vent systems at the waterline, where raw ore and alloy are worked |
| **The Mid Terrace** | Stepped, stone-walled agricultural terraces growing salt-hardy root crops and the fiber-plant **quirin**, spun into thread for the Reach's textile-metal fusion craft |
| **The High Caldera** | The volcanic peak — oath-halls, star-reading platforms, and the seat of the **Qhapaq**, built into the crater rim |

A household's standing is measured by **how many tiers it holds a working foothold in.** The word for the whole practice is **the Turning** — both the seasonal rotation between tiers and the term for a person's entire life's work of building and maintaining that vertical claim.

### Craft, government, and trade

The Reach is known for **qori-thread** — quirin fiber woven together with hair-fine drawn alloy, **flexing like cloth while turning an edge like plate.** Goods move between tiers on the back of the **stoneback**, a heavy-shelled climbing beast bred for the work.
Each working kinship is an **ayllu**, self-governing in daily affairs. Ayllu send representatives to **Terrace Councils**, which confirm a single archipelago-wide **Qhapaq** on one condition: **the candidate must have personally completed the Full Turning** — an unbroken circuit of every inhabited island's three tiers, made alone.
Barrel Dwarf Kegshard Hold maintains a standing trade office in the Reach's chief port, **because Reach alloy and Barrel Dwarf alchemy produce a compound neither lineage can replicate alone.**

### Wellsprings and traits

**Basilithe** (primary, expressed as terrace and reef stability rather than tunnel-geometry) · **Ignivale** (strong secondary, the geothermal vent-forges) · **Ascensio** (definitive — the Vertical Claim is an Ascensio relationship formalized into an economy) · **Cymorath** (accessible, wind-reading and storm-timing across open water)
> **Tiered Domain Claim** — Domain-adjacent stability across two or three **physically separate locations at once**, unlike any other Dawi lineage.
>
> **Qori-thread mastery** — flexible, load-distributing armour unmatched by any culture that only casts or only weaves.
> **Root-Severed** — cut off from all three tiers too long, a Reach Dwarf suffers a slow Coherence decline called **Flatfoot Grief.**
>
> **Deep Blindness** — three centuries removed from the tunnel-network, most Reach Dwarves have lost the comfort with true subterranean geometry that defines their cousins.
**Qori Anka Hold** · seat of the Qhapaq, built into the Reach's largest active caldera; its outer wall is **the largest known piece of continuous qori-thread weave in the world.**
**Chaska Reefborn** · current Qhapaq, the first in four generations confirmed from a reef-forge ayllu rather than a caldera-born one.

## The Tallow Clans — Herders of the High Forge-Pastures

Where the Reach answered a broken mountain by inventing a new kind of up, the Tallow Clans **never lost their mountain at all.** They simply refused to stay on any one part of it.
They move twice a year between the **high forge-pastures**, where the air is thin and the ore-rich grazing land only thaws for half the turning, and the **lowland winter halls**, where the same clans spend the cold months trading, marrying between households, and settling whatever debts the high season left open.
> No Tallow clan holds a single permanent seat of power. **The confederation itself is the government**, and it exists most fully not in any building but in the twice-yearly gathering where every clan's Ledger-Song is sung in full, in front of every other clan, **so that no lineage's history can be quietly rewritten without the whole confederation hearing the attempt.**

### Stone-flocks and the high pastures

The Clans herd **stone-flocks** — a hardy breed of Basilithe-touched livestock whose wool carries a faint mineral luster from generations of grazing ore-veined pasture. Each high pasture is dotted with **tighremts**, fortified stone granary-towers that stand empty and locked all winter and become the operating center of an entire clan's life for the six months the flocks are up top.
A clan's wealth is not measured in coin but in **how many tighremts it can fill before the first hard frost**, and a poor high season is remembered, and sung about, for generations.

### The Ledger-Songs

The Clans keep **no written law.** Every clan maintains a **Ledger-Song** — a continuously extended oral epic, added to at every winter gathering, that records lineage, debt, marriage, feud, and judgment in verse rather than ink.
> A Ledger-Keeper's memory **is the confederation's entire legal system**, and a Ledger-Keeper caught singing a line differently than it was sung the year before is subject to the single harshest penalty the Clans have: **being sung about, truthfully and without embellishment, as someone whose word could not be trusted.**
>
> *Given the alternative, disputes are rare and confessions are common.*

### Tattoo, jewelry, and the warm craft

Status and clan affiliation are **worn on the body rather than declared** — layered tattoo-work at the wrist and collarbone, and jewelry built up over a lifetime rather than gifted whole, both applied without regard for gender, since the Clans consider status **a record of what a person has done rather than what they were born as.**
The Tallow aesthetic runs warmer than any other Dawi branch: woven wire worked directly into dyed stone-flock wool, laid over plate rather than replacing it, producing armour that reads at a distance as textile and only reveals its metal core on contact. **It is the only Dawi craft tradition built explicitly to look softer than it is.**

### Wellsprings and traits

**Basilithe** (primary, through pasture and pack-stone stability) · **Terranova** (strong secondary — fertility and mineral richness of the high pastures, and the stone-flocks' grazing bond to it) · **Ignivale** (accessible, the tighremt hearths)
> **Seasonal Domain Flexibility** — a clan's collective Domain re-anchors cleanly twice a year rather than fraying under relocation, an adaptation no static Dawi hold shares.
>
> **Verse-Bound Coherence** — a sworn line in a Ledger-Song carries **the same structural Crystal weight as a Barrel Dwarf's Cask-Oath**, enforced by collective memory rather than any single vessel.
> **Root-Bound** — confined to one location year-round, a Tallow Dawi suffers the same decline the Reach calls Flatfoot Grief. The Clans call it **Silt**, the sense of a river that has stopped running.
>
> No central court means confederation-wide decisions can take an entire winter gathering to resolve, and **a clan that refuses to attend cannot be compelled, only sung about.**
**Tazrouk Wintermark** · current senior Ledger-Keeper, holder of the longest unbroken Ledger-Song in the confederation's record.
**The Amsal Halls** · the largest lowland winter gathering-hall, where the full confederation convenes each year to hear every clan's Song in turn.

## The Windward Herds — Drovers of the Highland Steppe

If the Tallow Clans compromise between two homes, **the Windward Herds refuse the compromise entirely.** They do not have a winter hall and a summer pasture. They have the herd, **and wherever the herd currently stands is, for as long as it stands there, the whole of Windward territory.**
No other Dawi branch is this fully nomadic, and no other branch is this comfortable being encountered as a passing caravan rather than a host welcoming a guest into a hold that already existed before the guest arrived.

### The drove and the stonehide

The Herds move behind trains of **stonehide** — heavy-boned, tunnel-adapted pack and mount beasts bred to shrug off both crushing depth-pressure and open-air cold with equal indifference. A drove is not just transport; **it is the Windward Herds' entire concept of wealth, defense, and lineage rolled into one moving asset**, and a family's standing is measured by the size and health of its drove rather than by any fixed possession.
Forges travel with the drove as collapsible **tent-forges** that can be broken down, packed, and reassembled at the next camp inside a single night — slower and less precise than any fixed Dawi forge, *and entirely sufficient for a culture that has never needed a fixed one.*

### The Drove-Marshal

Authority rotates to whichever Herd-Elder currently holds the largest, healthiest drove, **a title that can change hands between two encampments without ceremony** if fortunes shift that fast.
> This makes Windward leadership the least stable of any Dawi branch **by design.** The Herds consider a leader who has stopped needing to prove the health of their own drove **exactly the kind of leader who has stopped understanding the herd at all.**

### Wellsprings and traits

**Basilithe** (primary, through the stonehide's bred-in endurance rather than through architecture) · **Ignivale** (strong secondary, the portable tent-forge tradition, kept low and quick rather than banked and permanent) · **Cymorath** (accessible, storm and altitude-band reading to time a drove's movement before weather closes a pass)
> **Drove-Domain** — territory is wherever the herd currently stands, **relocating completely and cleanly** rather than fraying at the edges the way a static Domain does when its owner travels.
>
> Altitude-band resilience matched only by the Kuntur Reach, achieved by entirely different means.
> **Siege-Poor** — forced to hold fixed ground rather than move, their advantage collapses almost entirely. **By wide agreement the worst-suited Dawi branch for a static defense.**
>
> **Shallow Compounding** — because their Crystal never sits with one piece of ground long enough to age the way a Barrel Dwarf's does, Windward practitioners plateau earlier and never reach the alchemical-aging advantages their cousins take for granted.
**Yeren Emberdrove** · current Drove-Marshal, holder of the largest recorded stonehide drove in three generations.
**The Thinbreath Saddle** · the single most contested seasonal crossing in the Innerworld's highland bands.

## The Tower-Kin — Bloodlines of the Sealed Valley

The Tower-Kin solved the same mountain problem as everyone else **by refusing to solve it collectively at all.** Each bloodline holds one stone tower, built into its own valley, and they have never had — and by their own account do not want — a central court above the level of the individual tower.
What they have instead is a working legal system built entirely on **honor-debt**: a wrong done to one tower is owed by the tower that did it, tallied, remembered exactly, and settled either through negotiated payment or, when negotiation fails, **through a formally declared and strictly bounded feud that ends the moment the debt is judged paid, by both sides, and not a season sooner.**

### One tower, one bloodline

A tower is raised once, at a bloodline's founding, and **is never rebuilt larger.** A family that outgrows its tower is expected to found a new one rather than expand the old, which is why the Tower-Kin valleys are dense with towers of wildly different ages standing shoulder to shoulder rather than any single sprawling hold.
> The tower is inseparable from the bloodline's Crystal-architecture in a very literal sense: **Fixatio-anchored oaths sworn inside a tower's founding stone remain readable generations later.**
>
> A tower that falls into ruin is, in Tower-Kin belief, **a bloodline whose debts have finally gone entirely unpaid** — a fate treated as considerably worse than death for any individual member of it.

### The Reckoning

Honor-debt is tracked with an exactness **that borders on the arithmetic**: a life owed is not settled by any other life, a theft is not settled by any unrelated payment, and a Tower-Kin elder can, on request, recite their own tower's outstanding Reckonings against three other named towers going back further than most other Dawi branches keep any written record at all.
*Outsiders find this exhausting. The Tower-Kin find the alternative — forgetting what is owed — unthinkable.*

### Fierce weavers, rare guests

Tower-Kin smithing and weaving are regarded as among the finest of any Dawi branch, **produced almost entirely for the tower's own use** rather than for trade, since a Tower-Kin family rarely leaves its valley and welcomes outsiders into it even less often.
What little reaches the wider world typically does so because a Reckoning was settled in goods rather than blood — which means **every Tower-Kin blade or weave in outside hands carries, technically, a small piece of some old debt's resolution woven into it.**

### Wellsprings and traits

**Basilithe** (primary, as the tower's founding-stone permanence) · **Fixatio** (definitive secondary — the mechanism by which a founding oath remains legible in stone across generations) · **Judicium** (strong secondary — the innate sense of what is owed, to whom, and in what order, that makes the Reckoning function without any central ledger)
> **Unbreakable Tower-Domain** — a founding-oath anchor that does not fade or require maintenance, **unmatched in permanence by any other Dawi Domain-law variant**, including the Barrel Dwarves' actively-maintained vaults.
>
> **Innate Reckoning-sense** — a practitioner can feel, structurally, when a debt in the room has gone unacknowledged, which makes them exceptional at spotting **a lie of omission** specifically.
> **Social Trust Penalty** — generations of isolationism and an honor-code that treats outsiders as, at best, a potential future debtor, leaves Tower-Kin diplomacy badly underdeveloped outside their own valley.
>
> A Reckoning left unresolved too long calcifies into a **Cold Debt**, a permanent Coherence scar **on both towers involved** that neither side can fully train around.
**Kelvash Tower** · the oldest standing tower in the valley, and the site of the longest currently-open Reckoning on record, **now four generations unresolved.**
**Bakhar Kelvashi** · current elder of Kelvash Tower, publicly committed to closing the tower's oldest Reckoning before it calcifies into a Cold Debt.

## The Passwardens — Guides of the Worst Passes

Not herders, and not settlers in any sense the other four branches would recognize. **They are the Dawi who know the way through, full stop**, and every other branch's trade, migration, and Reckoning-settlement ultimately depends on a Passwarden having mapped the route first.
> By any fair accounting, the single most structurally necessary Dawi branch — and also, **by the Passwardens' own wry admission, the branch least likely to ever be thanked properly for it.**

### The Waymark Guild

Membership is **earned rather than inherited**, awarded only once a candidate has personally mapped and formally certified a new safe route through terrain the Guild's existing records do not yet cover.
A certification is not a formality. **A Passwarden's waymark, once placed, is trusted by every other Dawi branch without independent verification** — which means a false or careless certification is treated as one of the very few offenses the otherwise easygoing Passwardens punish with genuine severity.

### Toll-right and the Thinbreath Saddle

In exchange for maintaining and guaranteeing a route, the Guild holds **toll-right**, an ancient cross-branch treaty recognized by every other branch alike, entitling a waystation to a fair toll from anyone using a certified route.
The **Thinbreath Saddle** — claimed at various times of year by both the Windward Herds' droves and the Tallow Clans' seasonal migration — is maintained entirely by a single small Passwarden waystation, **whose toll-right neither larger branch has ever seriously contested, because both need the Saddle kept open more than either needs to argue about who pays for it.**

### Small, indispensable, underpaid

The Passwardens are, deliberately, **a small population relative to the traffic they enable.** Waystations are spread thin across the worst terrain in the Innerworld, and a single certified guide's death or disappearance can close a route for an entire season.
> This fragility is the central, quietly resented fact of Passwarden life: **every other Dawi branch depends on them completely and compensates them modestly**, and the Passwardens have never yet found a way to raise that fact at a joint gathering without being told, gently, that the toll-right treaty already covers it.

### Wellsprings and traits

**Basilithe** (primary, through route-stability rather than settlement) · **Fixatio** (strong secondary — how a certified waymark holds a safe path stable against collapse or drift long after the Passwarden who placed it has moved on) · **Judicium** (accessible — reading which of several possible routes carries the least present risk, a skill the Guild considers **separate from and more valuable than raw memorized route-knowledge**)
> **Route-Sense** — a certified Passwarden is functionally never lost in tunnel or pass terrain they have any prior exposure to, and recovers bearing unusually fast even in terrain they do not.
>
> **Universal Toll-Right** — the only Dawi cultural institution honored, without exception, by every other branch, **including the historically prickly Tower-Kin.**
> **Thin Coverage** — the population is small and widely dispersed by design, meaning the loss of even one certified guide can have outsized consequences for an entire season's cross-branch trade.
>
> **Political Invisibility** — structurally indispensable and chronically undervalued in any negotiation about wealth or standing between the larger branches, *a resentment the Passwardens mostly keep to themselves.*
**Norbu Waymark** · senior certifying guide of the Thinbreath Saddle waystation, holder of the Guild's longest unbroken safety record.

# Null-Light

Tobiah Quillon, who toured the fairs with a mule, a gilt wagon and a claim to have out-shone the sun at Ambrel Bridge, once sold four hundred tickets to watch him make the shadowless stones of the Cedar Enclave cast a shadow. House Yukari, to the lasting astonishment of everybody, let him in and gave him tea. He set forty mirrored lamps around the court, burning white, rose and a hard sea-green, and he lit them at noon on a clear day, with the sun helping. Every pebble in that court threw forty shadows, and so did the tea tray, the mule and the steward's sleeves, a spray of colored shade that the paying customers talked about for the rest of their lives. The three stones stood in the middle of it, each one lit from every side, with clean raked gravel at their feet. Quillon stayed until his oil ran out. He sold the lamps in the next town to pay back the tickets, and he never afterward said a word against the Yukari, though he did take to checking the ground behind him.

He got off lightly, and he got off lightly because he was only trying to light the stones. What was done to those stones can also be done to you.

### NULL-LIGHT (無光 · Mukō) (Ashura's instrument; the inheritance of the Yukari)
They look at you, and you stay exactly what they saw.

MUSHŌ! The Null Reading. Your hurts, your lies and your next step are known, and you never felt a thing.  
JŌSHI! The Fixed Regard. A dying man stops dying and a Release stops halfway up, for as long as she does not look away.  
RUTEN! The Slip. She looks at you unevenly, and your wound finishes in one breath.  
KAGENASHI! The Shadowless. Nothing can be done to the one she holds, because she would see it being done.  
NINSHIKI! Recognition. She says what you are, and your stolen face stops working in front of everyone.  
MUGAN! The Unseeing. She stops knowing you, and you stop knowing yourself. Forbidden, and it cuts the one looking.  
ZENKŌ! Whole Light. Every soul in sight, held. Armies stop in the field. Ashura's own, and held by nobody.  

The young ones shout these, because they are young, because a bracket crowd pays for it, and because the Moto next door have been bawling the names of their Works across battlefields for a thousand years and somebody ought to answer. The old ones do not raise their voices. There is no call to shout at something you have already got. Either way the crows on the rail go quiet, the shadows go out of the courtyard, and a person a long way off who has never met you has begun, without touching you, to keep you.

One Regard is normal for a Yukari. Two is rare enough that the neighboring valley hears about it. Nobody living holds all seven, and the house says so at every Telling, since a family that tells its children everything has to tell them that as well.

There are three great instruments in the world, one to each Epoch's line, and this is the third. Bara's line has the seven [[The Disciplines/Schools and Bloodline Arts/Hataraki — The Works of the Plane of Fate/Hataraki — The Works of the Plane of Fate|Works of Hataraki]], and tells the world its job. Aegor's line has the nine Practices of [[The Disciplines/Named Arts/Matla|Matla]], under the law *to exist is to bear*. Ashura's line has the seven Regards under the law *to be known is to be held*, and of the three it is the only one that can be used on you from across a valley with your full cooperation, while you are eating lunch.

## How It Really Works

Null-Light is recognition with no light in it: a thing known completely by a light that never touches it. It was Ashura's own way of seeing, it is the root of every Yukari teaching, and it is the head of [[Artifacts/The Immortal Cross|the Immortal Cross]].

On the [[Cosmology & Metaphysics/The Making of the World/Omnara and the Division|Soul Plane]], light is the archive recognizing something as significant, from inside. Null-Light is that recognition carried into the waking world with nothing arriving at the thing recognized: no photon, no Essence (the spiritual energy a soul holds), no pressure on its boundary. A stone under Null-Light is known to the last grain and lit by nothing, and so it casts no shadow. The three standing stones in the court of the Cedar Enclave were set by the first household under it, and they have thrown no shadow at any hour since.

It gives no fire. No Regard has ever warmed a room, moved a pebble or knocked a man down, and the [[The Magic System/The Foundations/The Physical Account Two Sets of Books|physical book]] on one is the thinnest entry a Measurewright will ever write: a pulse, a reserve gone down, a field with no shadows on it. Its whole work is on souls and on what a thing is, and only at its far end does it reach the stone itself. It runs on the [[The Magic System/The Soul and the Climb/The Three Paths Routing, Recognition and the Gate|Spirit Path]], and it is where that road goes when it does not stop, as Matla is the far end of Body and Hataraki of Fate.

### The law, as a Measurewright says it

Two things are true of looking, and every trained eye in the Dark Ages knows at least the first.

**The null reading.** A thing can be found by the light that does not come back from it. Send light down two roads at once, put an object on one, and the pattern of what arrives tells you the object is there even when no light ever struck it. That is knowing without touching.

**The Zeno hold.** A thing looked at closely and continuously enough does not change. The watched pot does not boil: every look resets it to what it was, and a look kept up without a gap holds it there. Zeno's arrow, the old philosophers called it, the arrow that never moves because at every instant it is seen standing still. Look too slowly or too unevenly, and the opposite happens: the thing changes faster than if it had been left alone. Measurewrights call that the Zeno slip.

Put the two together and the result is Null-Light: a thing known continuously, without ever being touched, is held exactly as it is and harmed in no way at all. Change the rhythm of the looking and it is harmed in every way it was ever going to be, all at once. The house's short form is the law: **to be known is to be held.**

## The Seven Regards

A Regard is one particular way of looking, and no more trades for another than a hand trained to Ruin closes on a Purity job. Ashura held them all as one sight. Everyone since has had to come at it by a side door, so each Regard is its own door, and a person who can stop a death with their eyes may stand helpless before a forged seal that a nine-year-old cousin could read.

| Regard | Name | What it does | What it will not do |
|---|---|---|---|
| I | **Mushō**, the Null Reading | Know a soul or thing whole without touching it: its hurts, its lies, its Traits and what it will do next. Passes wards and disguises, gives the target no sensation, and leaves no trace | Change anything. It reads what you will do on your present course, so a man who truly changes his mind in mid-stride walks out of it |
| II | **Jōshi**, the Fixed Regard | Hold what you look at unchanging: a dying man stops dying, a Release stops mid-rise, a working stops half-cast, and a soul being taken stays where it is. Only as long as you never look away | Mend. A held wound is still a wound, and it is waiting |
| III | **Ruten**, the Slip | Look unevenly on purpose and the thing runs ahead of itself: a wound runs its full course in a breath, a working burns through its fuel, a reserve drains, grief finishes in a second. The killing Regard | Start anything. It runs what is already running, and a sound body with nothing wrong in it has nothing to finish |
| IV | **Kagenashi**, the Shadowless | Hold one soul so wholly known that nothing can be done to it unseen: it cannot be possessed, cursed or edited, it casts no shadow, and an attack on its soul finds no purchase. The great defense | Stop a spear. It guards the soul, and one soul at a time |
| V | **Ninshiki**, Recognition | Say what something truly is. The dead come quiet, Echo Beasts unmake, a spirit obeys its true name, and an illusion, a false Class or a stolen face stops working in front of everyone | Lie. It has no force on a thing that is what it says it is, and the speaker has to actually know |
| VI | **Mugan**, the Unseeing | The Seikai's road, Null without Light, and forbidden. Withdraw recognition and a thing stops knowing what it is: a soul its name, a ward its purpose, a Domain its law. Every use cuts what it looks at and cuts the one looking | Spare anybody. The house has two rooms at the back of the Enclave for its own who tried, and they are well kept |
| VII | **Zenkō**, Whole Light | Ashura's own, held by nobody. Every soul in sight held at once. Armies stop in the field and no one on it can die or move until it lifts | Be practiced. It is on this page because it is on the stones, and because the house will not leave a thing out of the Telling |

### What each Regard is doing

The scholars have an account of every one, in the plain words below, and two of them have come to blows over the fourth.

**Mushō** is the null reading done to a soul. A Measurewright tests for a hidden thing by watching how light fails to come back from it, and the Yukari does the same on the Soul Plane: she knows the shape of your lie from the place where recognition does not return. Nothing arrives at you, so nothing can warn you. A ward stops what crosses it, and nothing crossed.

**Jōshi** is the Zeno hold done on purpose. Every instant of looking resets the thing to what it was an instant ago, and with no gap between looks it never gets to be anything else. A bleed has to take the next step in order to go on being a bleed, and the next step is what the look keeps taking away. This is why it stops a half-cast working as readily as a heart: a Release rising and a man dying are both things that are on their way somewhere.

**Ruten** is the Zeno slip, the same watched pot with the watching done badly. Look in the wrong rhythm and the resets stop holding the thing back and start shoving it along, each one knocking it further down the road it was already on. A six-week wound fever runs out in a breath, with all six weeks of it in the body at once. Nothing is added. The wound was always going to be that bad, given time, and the Slip is the time.

**Kagenashi** holds a soul in the state of being known so completely that any change to it would have to happen in front of the looker, and a change that is watched closely enough does not get to finish. A possession needs a moment of the soul being somebody else's, a curse needs a line written where nobody is reading, and an edit needs a thread that no one is holding. A Kagenashi leaves no such moment, and the protected soul stops casting a shadow at the feet of everybody who ever tried.

**Ninshiki** rests on the oldest thing known about a look, which is that a look asks a question and the thing must answer. A spirit is largely what it is called. An Echo Beast is what was left of the dead with nothing to say who that was, and when somebody says, it has no reason left to hold together. A false Class on a window and a stolen face are claims, and a claim read out correctly in public by someone who knows stops being able to run.

**Mugan** is that question taken back. Unasked, a thing has to go on being itself by itself, and most things find out that they cannot. It was how the Seikai saw, every look an incision, and it works on a ward or a Domain as well as on a person, because a ward is a purpose and a Domain is a law, and both of them lean on being recognized. The Seikai are gone, and the Regard is taught to no one, and it turns up in the house about once a generation, unasked for, in somebody who has lost a great deal.

**Zenkō** is what all of it looks like with no door to come through. Nobody has seen it who is alive. The old accounts of the Third Epoch's first mornings say Ashura looked at a battle once, from a kitchen step, and that it stopped until she had finished what she was doing.

## The Climb

The Regards climb on [[The Magic System/The Soul and the Climb/Classes and the Three Ladders|Essence Class]], the ladder of the Essence Core, and the blood's only gift here is that the ladder opens early for a Yukari. Stage sets the ceiling, practice moves you up to it, and no purse has ever bought the rung, so a Yukari who buys a Stage has bought a higher room to be just as bad in.

| Essence Class | What the instrument does at that rung |
|---|---|
| **I · Listener** | Hears. The Reading comes unbidden, in pieces, and cannot be shut off |
| **II · Answerer** | Is answered. A thing looked at gives back what it is, and spirits and the dead reply to the look |
| **III · Confessor** | Reads wholly. Mushō is complete, and a Regard can be used on a stranger across a room |
| **IV · Shepherd** | Holds. Jōshi or Kagenashi sits on one soul or a few, and stays while the looker eats and walks |
| **V · Ferryman** | Holds many. A line of the dying, a company, a field |

The hard part of this climb is plain. A hitter gets stronger and stays more or less the person who started. Each rung here means knowing more people more completely, for good.

## At War

Three things a commander will pay nearly anything for. A Null-Light Yukari holds a line of the dying in place until the surgeons reach them, which is why soldiers who hate the house will still march for it. She sees every soul on a valley's field and tells a commander what each company will do, down to which sergeant is about to lose his nerve, with no scout on the hill and nothing for the other side's wards to catch. And she slips the wounded of the other side all at once, which nobody puts in the song and everybody puts in the terms of surrender. Every wounded man behind the enemy's line gets the end of his wound in the same breath, and the line, which has spent the morning hearing them, hears them stop.

For the same reason an army that knows she is there goes looking for her early. [[Factions, Bloodlines & Institutions/Powers and Polities/The Guild Accord/Structure and Rank/The Practitioner Problem|The great houses pin a Fate user first]], and the Crow is usually next on the list. The trouble is finding her, since she can work from anywhere with a line of attention to the field and she throws nothing anyone can trace. The scouts of three realms now carry the same order, and it is to report any field that has no shadows on it at noon.

### The bean field at Ōmagari

Every Yukari child is told this one at the Telling, with the price left in.

In the year of the Sum-gol toll war the Vermilion lancers broke the levy of the lower valleys at the river, about four in the afternoon, and rode on through, and they left three hundred and twelve people dying in a field of green beans, forty of them their own. The surgeons were three days up the river road on the wrong side of a burned bridge. In that field was a cousin of the house named Yukari Sayo, twenty-six years old, a quartermaster's clerk with a head for grain and a voice like a dropped anvil, the only person of her generation ever told to keep it down during a Telling. She had climbed to Ferryman sitting up with plague wards in the lower valleys, which the house considered a waste of her. She got up on a war drum in her crow-black coat, silver at the cuffs, with a smear of bean flower on one knee, filled her chest and roared *JŌSHI!* across the whole of it, and the Vermilion rear guard a quarter mile off turned in their saddles and laughed.

Then she looked. By sundown the beans had stopped casting shadows, and so had the hedge, the dead horses and the drum. Nobody in that field got better and nobody got any worse. A boy with his belly opened went on having it opened, pale and interested, telling the man beside him about his mother's goat. A sergeant with no blood left to speak of kept on having a little. The crows came down along the hedge in a long glossy row and sat there, and did not eat. She could not sleep, since a looker who sleeps has looked away, so her cousins spooned millet into her and wet her lips, and she sat on that drum with her eyes open and did not once turn them.

The second day brought Marshal Bang Deok-su back to see about the report, in vermilion lacquer with a gold carp on his helm, a shrewd and cheerful butcher who had taken eleven cities. By nightfall he had bookmakers in his camp giving odds on the hour she would blink. On the third day he tried what the manuals say. He ran a burning hay wagon the length of the field to bury it in smoke, and when that did nothing he walked up through his own dying with a grain sack and pulled it down over her head with his own two hands, and that did nothing either, because the look needs a looker and has no particular use for an eye. He stood there a while with a spear in his hand, close enough to do it. He had forty lancers in those beans, every one of them alive on the third day, because she had never learned how to hold only some. The Sum-gol songs call him the Carp Who Blinked for what he did next, which was to send his own surgeons over the river.

They worked in the order the wounds stood. Each one she paid out singly, with the surgeon's hands already in the wound, and each one got every hour of dying she had held back from him in a single breath, so they lost about one in three on the table and counted it a miracle. That went on for six days. On the ninth day of her sitting there, with a hundred and twenty-two still lying in the beans, a drummer boy from the valley came running up with the news that the two hosts had signed, and she turned her head to hear him. Hers was the first name anyone had called in nine days. A hundred and twenty-two people finished nine days of dying in the time it takes to turn your head, and at the same moment all the shadows came back, short and black, under every plant in the field.

An old lancer who had bet on nine days, mostly as a joke, bought the field with what he won, and it is still in beans. She went back to grain. She could tell you how the goat came out, and she could tell you the three hundred and twelve whole, each one, at any hour, for the rest of her life, and she did so at the Tellings for forty years, loudly. By the time she was sixty nobody outside the house could say what she looked like. The muster book of the lower valleys has her down as *a clerk*, and the song calls her the Woman on the Drum and has the color of her coat wrong.

## The Price: the Long Look

**Cost** · What you hold, you must keep looking at, and you cannot stop knowing what you have known. Every soul ever held stays in the Core, whole, for life. When you look away, everything held slips at once, so the longer the Hold the worse the release, and all of that lands on someone.

A Moto pays in blood and reserve and a job left undone. A Null-Light Yukari pays in reserve too, spent from the Core and paid there, with no heat to show for it, but the reserve comes back and the rest does not. Nobody has ever found a way to give a known person back. An old Shepherd of the house carries every deathbed she ever sat, entire, in the same place she keeps her own name, and the Core only has the one room. Below a tenth of reserve, where anyone's [[The Magic System/The Foundations/The Physical Account Two Sets of Books|Core starts to fray]], she is not always certain which of them she is.

Holders also grow shadowless themselves. It comes on by inches. People forget their faces and do not feel their regard. A shopkeeper who has served one for thirty years will greet her as a stranger, and a husband will start to set one fewer place. The end of the road is someone nobody but the house remembers is there, and it keeps those people on its books with great care, since nobody else will. This is the real reason for the long table and the register, and the reason the second teaching is *tell them everything*.

## Counters

**Counter** · Break the line of attention: it needs a looker and not an eye, so a sack over the head does nothing and a blow to the concentration does a great deal. Fill the field with more souls than can be held, which is the oldest Vermilion answer and the reason they bring so many spears. Use the target's own contrary Wellspring, since a soul whose lens runs against the look is harder to keep still. Fate can move what the look depends on, and every Regard depends on something: a wind that puts the smoke in her cousin's eyes, a messenger who arrives at the right moment. And a sound body simply outlasts a slip, because a Slip can only finish what is already going wrong, so the man to send against the Crow is the one who has never been hurt and is wearing good boots.

The Paths come round as they always do. A hitter who reaches her finds a fragile body and no heat in it. A Spirit fighter is the one person who can feel the Long Look on the Core and go for the hand behind it. The Moto can read a Yukari's threads as she can read theirs, and each house has spent a thousand years wishing it could not.

**Tell** · The courtyard stones. Near a working Regard, things stop casting shadows.

## Who Holds It

Ashura held it whole. The [[The Disciplines/Schools and Bloodline Arts/The Ketsumyōgan — The Binding Fate Eye|Ketsumyōgan]] carries a fragment of it, which is why a Yukari look leaves a room as it found it and why the third Yukari teaching is *see without taking*. The Seikai had the Null without the Light: they could cancel a boundary and look through the hole, and every look they took cut what it looked at. No living Yukari holds Null-Light whole. The house's deepest meditation reaches toward it, and the house says plainly that nobody has arrived.

The eye and the instrument are different things, and people outside the house confuse them constantly. The eye sees the threads, and an editing Yukari is working Fate on top and paying for it. Null-Light works on what is known and pulls no thread at all. The fragment can be pushed, and the people who have pushed it are written down. It will ride a good blade for one plane and one stroke, canceling a working it matches along the line of the cut, though on plain matter that blade is only an edge. On the Kessen road Lord Naiser Yukari is said to have thrown it over a whole stretch of road at once, a man-shaped dark at the middle, with every cedar, carriage and crow around him gone shadowless, and the only shadow left in the valley lying under him and stretching toward the thing he meant to kill. The house has declined to discuss what that cost.

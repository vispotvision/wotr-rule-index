# The Twelve Stages of Spirit Infusion

Every academy yard in the Quarters skips rope to it, and the children have the curriculum down years before the instructors get to them:

> *One takes two and two are dead,*  
> *Two takes swift and leaves you lead,*  
> *Three grows crooked, four forgets,*  
> *Five's a child you can't beget*
> *Twice; six moves in, and seven stays,*  
> *Eight bleeds both and nine delays,*  
> *Ten's for life, eleven's thin,*  
> *Twelve says no and won't go in.*

Mnirah Valein wrote four hundred pages on it and the rhyme has never been caught in an error.

### THE TWELVE STAGES OF SPIRIT INFUSION (school art; the Valein curriculum)
You can make your summon into anything if you climb the ladder in order, and every rung takes something from you and keeps it.

The moves are the rungs, below, each with its own price: BASIC FUSION! TRAIT TRANSFER! LAYERED AMPLIFICATION! ELEMENTAL IMBUEMENT! HYBRID FUSION! CONCEPTUAL INFUSION! REACTIVE EVOLUTION! SYMBIOTIC LINK! MULTI-ENTITY FUSION! SPIRIT BINDING! OMNIPRESENT FORM! SPIRIT SOVEREIGN!

## How It Really Works

> The curriculum codified by **Mnirah Valein** during the Age of Deliberation, mapping summoning capability to the Coherence Ladder. It remains the standard progression across the Accord, and the Temperance Gates in current regulation are hers.
Infusion governs what a summoner may do *to* a form once it has answered: merge, transfer, layer, redefine. Every rung takes something, which was Valein's actual argument and the part most academies now teach quickly and move past.

## The Foundational Rungs

### I · Basic Fusion

Two forms merged into one stronger single. The first thing any student is shown and the last thing most of them ever need.

**Cost** · Both originals end. Fusion is a death and a birth, and no kind of formation.

### II · Trait Transfer

A specific property moved from one form to another. Speed off the scout and onto the guard.

**Cost** · The donor does not recover the trait for the duration. A stripped scout is a slow scout, and students forget this until it kills something.

### III · Layered Amplification

Additional courses of Essence laid over an existing form to raise an attribute without changing what the form is.

**Cost** · Layers do not settle evenly. An over-layered construct develops a visible asymmetry that experienced opponents read as an instruction on where to strike.

### IV · Elemental Imbuement

A form given a Wellspring trait it did not originally carry, matched to terrain or opposition.

**Cost** · Imbuement overwrites. The form's original resonance is suppressed while the imbuement holds, and some of it does not come back.

---

## The Compositional Rungs

### V · Hybrid Fusion

Unlike forms merged into a blended whole, producing capability neither parent had. The first rung where the outcome is not fully predictable before the merge.

**Cost** · Hybrid results are not repeatable. The same two parents merged twice do not produce the same child, and Valein was explicit that this is not a flaw in technique.

### VI · Conceptual Infusion

An abstract property folded into a form. Weight. Delay. Fear.

**Cost** · The concept enters the summoner first and leaves last. A practitioner who infuses fear regularly is a practitioner who is afraid, and the profession is candid about this.

### VII · Reactive Evolution

A form authorized to alter itself mid-engagement in response to what it meets.

**Cost** · Evolution is one-way. Whatever the form becomes, it stays, and the practitioner does not get their original back at the end of the fight.

### VIII · Symbiotic Link

Direct two-way sharing of capability between practitioner and form. Each can spend from the other.

**Cost** · Injury crosses. Both directions, immediately, and the link cannot be dropped faster than a blade travels.

---

## The Sovereign Rungs

### IX · Multi-Entity Fusion

Several forms combined into one construct holding multiple roles at once, switching between them rather than performing them together.

**Cost** · Role-switching is visible and takes a beat. Skilled opponents fight the gap rather than the construct.

### X · Spirit Binding

A form bound permanently as a familiar. It stops being a call and becomes a fact about the practitioner.

**Cost** · Permanent means permanent. The allocation the familiar occupies is gone from the practitioner's working capacity for life, and a bound familiar's death is documented as a bereavement rather than an expenditure.

### XI · Omnipresent Form

A single presence tied to many locations simultaneously. The full account lives under [[The Disciplines/Schools and Bloodline Arts/The Spirit Summoning Arts/The Infinite Summoning Arts|Infinite Summoning]].

**Cost** · Attention divides with the presence. The practitioner is not fully anywhere, including inside their own body.

### XII · Spirit Sovereign

The form becomes an independent avatar of overwhelming capability, holding its own analogues of Core, Shell and Attraction Layer.

**Cost** · A Sovereign that reaches sufficient internal coherence can refuse dissolution. Valein's own writing treats this as the intended outcome rather than the failure case, which is the most contested sentence in her surviving work.

---

## What Nobody Knows

> No one has explained why the rungs must be climbed in order. Practitioners who attempt VI before III fail reliably and cannot say what they failed at. Valein recorded the ordering as observed fact and declined, across four hundred pages, to offer a reason for it.

---

**Cost.** Whatever the rung names, and most of it does not come back.

**Counter.** Fight the rung's own price: strike the visible asymmetry of an over-layered form, the beat of a role-switch, or the summoner through a Symbiotic Link.

**Numbers.** Twelve rungs in three groups of four, gated by the Temperance Gates in current Accord regulation. Valein's surviving treatment runs four hundred pages.

# THE GENTLE DIVORCE (Rite, a spoken and ceremonial rite)

*Scholars' term: Scission, which is the word on every Concord case file, so you will need it to find anything.*

> Isolde Penrose had burned down two husbands' houses without meaning to, and she went to the Concord chamber at Mallow to be divorced from her own temper. The rite ran nine minutes behind a shut door with gold light pouring under it, the candles in the passage sagged like tired men, and half the parish rented places on the wall opposite. She walked out mild as milk and has never since remembered why she married the second one. The temper stayed behind in the circle, about the size of a cat, and the chamber still charges a copper to look at it.

It takes the fire out of you, or the memory, or the god, and leaves both halves alive.

**SPLIT THE HOUSEHOLD!** Trait from soul: the power lifts off its owner and both lie there breathing.  
**KEEP THE BODY, LOSE THE FIRE!** Essence from body: the person stays, and what they could do goes quiet in them.  
**FORGET HIM PROPERLY!** Memory from identity: one thing is gone, and the patient spends a week finding out what it was holding up.  
**CLEAR THE HOUSE!** Wellspring from host: a harmonized Aether comes out by the roots, and the host learns how much of the house it was.

## How it really works

Nobody fights with this. It happens behind the line, in a prepared room, to someone lying down: several people, a fixed circle, an offering, a catalyst and a spoken invocation, and the patient feels no pain while others work, only a structural absence, the wrong kind of quiet. The more load-bearing half destabilizes first, so the patient learns, somewhat late, which of the two they were.

The charge is the assembling. For six to twelve minutes the officiants pull Aether steadily into one small volume, refine it in the Soul Crystal into Essence and drive it out through the Shell (the Crystal's outer skin), and because all of it lands in a space the rite cannot move once begun, the saturation threshold matters: past it, a Crystal Fracture Event (every Crystal in the room cracking together) takes everyone present, patient included. The Space step exists to keep the chamber below that line. What the room gets is heat, noise and light: 30 to 40 percent of the rite leaves the Shell as bleed, 17 to 67 MW sustained into a fixed circle, so a Scission chamber is hot, loud and lit for the whole of it. Afterward the place is a worked site, its Residue (what a working leaves in the ground) deafening any Harmonist (a trained listener to Aether) who stands on it.

The physics is solvation, one thing dissolving into another. Two bound things are put where being apart is the cheaper arrangement, then held there without force until they come apart by themselves. The second law, written as a free-energy test, ΔG = ΔH − TΔS < 0, decides whether separation is preferred; nucleation theory, which is about the first seed of a new state forming, decides how long that takes. A supersaturated solution holds its excess for hours and drops it in seconds once a seed site appears, and the rite supplies that seed site and leaves the separating to the law. Nucleation rate goes as exp(−ΔG‡/kT), so waiting time is exponential in the barrier: the six to twelve minutes and their factor-of-two spread are an induction time, and a much higher barrier never separates at all. Past the barrier, separation is fast and cannot be stopped; before it, the thing can be declined. Nothing is destroyed. One thing becomes two, both intact. The free energy released was already stored in the bound arrangement and the rite pays only for the barrier, which is why a rite of this consequence costs minutes and a twentieth of a reserve instead of a war.

There is a hard limit in that arithmetic. The entropy of mixing is about two hundred times too weak to break a covalent bond, so cross-linked bonds refuse, and the separation runs only where heat of bonding does the work, as in ionic and van der Waals bonds. A warning to readers of old copies: this page once said the rite raises the entropy of the bound configuration above that of the two parts held apart, and then concluded that separation was preferred, which does not follow, since if the bound state had the higher entropy nothing would separate. What the mechanism needs is the opposite, and what Dissolution's own register actually says: the rite must raise the free energy of the bound configuration relative to the separated pair, or equally lower the pair's, which is what supplying a solvent does. Dissolution's register is about why a solid dissolves into a solvent, and an old copyist carried the sentence across while reversing what counted as "mixed."

Dissolution, the Unbinding Stream (Wild), is the Aether doing the work: the mixed state has more disorder than the separated one, so shedding what no longer serves is nearly effortless, and it strengthens Resilience Ward and Tempering Maturity. Three more Wellsprings are read against it as the law, permanence and memory triad. Judicium (Wild) reads composition from signature and locates the interface. Fixatio (Medium) sets the barrier height, both reference and weakness. Anamnesis (Medium) supplies what the bond used to be. The glyph is Edge (Thalen) for the interface, laid under Measure (Urion) for the diagnosis that locates it, and the same pair serves against four laws: Trait from soul, Essence from body, memory from identity, Wellspring from host. The boundary that moves is the free energy of the bound state against the separated one, at one interface, while a seed site makes the cheaper state reachable; the bond itself is left alone.

The steps are fixed and a missing one fails the rite outright. Intention names the cut. Space holds the chamber below saturation. Circle is the vessel's wall. Offering is a reagent. Invocation states the condition. Catalyst is the seed site. Seal closes against the warrant that opened it. All three layers of the Essence are involved: the Essence Core is cut from, the Attraction Layer holds the bonds and harmonizations a Wellspring-from-host cut works on, and the Aether Shell takes the backlash through Resilience Ward. Developmental Tier at the floor is Harmonic.

The floor is Glory at A-Grade. Below Refraction a working needs an external anchor and this one carries more than any other, so it cannot be improvised mid-combat. A Glory rite severs bonds up to Glory density and no higher. In theory this is the Consent Doctrine, enforced and literal: someone with standing invokes the rite and the Wellspring is asked only to recognize a lawful condition. A process can be licensed, and Scission is.

Its failure is Dissolution's own, indiscriminate solvency. Whatever else crosses the interface becomes cheaper apart too, and entropy does not know which memories were load-bearing, so the patient comes back fragmentary, amnesiac or resonant-altered more often than not, and that failure can be induced from outside. Some cuts leave a Trait Echo and some leave nothing, and neither Stage decides which. A forced cut lands first on the caster's own Resilience Ward; against a Fixatio-anchored bond forcing multiplies it, since the caster pays the factor of two hundred personally. A guild school reads the rite as a liturgy of Strife, the force that unmixes; expecting a complete cut to yield two clean parts, it treats a fragmentary outcome by cutting again harder, which is how mass severance is reached, and I would ask you to remember that the next time a guild school is polite to you.

**Cost.** A twentieth of full reserve, a fourteenth for a hard bond, plus a Resilience Ward toll as tight as the bond and a circle, offering and catalyst consumed.  
**Counter.** Read the bond's shape and refuse the separation before the cut completes; a Fixatio-anchored bond resists longest, and a scuffed circle ends it.

**What nobody knows.** Why some cuts leave a Trait Echo behind and others leave nothing at all: the variable tracked in guild registers is neither the caster's Stage nor the target's, and three centuries of Concord casework has not found a fourth candidate.

### Origin

Standing guild and Concord doctrine, refined over time, with no single inventor. Two named practitioners mark the ends of its use: Kujo Valorin at mass, forbidden severance, and Zethyra the Veinwrought at precise, careful curse-extraction. As a documented rite it is counterable by anyone who has studied the Concord's own casework on it.

### Counterplay

**Tell** · Several people, a circle, an offering, a catalyst, a spoken invocation and a chamber running tens of megawatts hot; it cannot be done to someone who does not know. Afterward a deafening worked site and a patient gone quiet.

**Limits** · No improvising mid-combat; the full apparatus or outright failure; six to twelve minutes; no bond denser than Glory; the removed piece rarely returns whole.

**Fails against** · A bond anchored with Fixatio beforehand (permanent, even if placed in error); a scuffed circle, a missing catalyst or a broken invocation; the condition refused before the Seal; a countersigned Seal; a Silenced chamber.

**Casework** · A documented rite with no secret author: its whole method is in the Concord's casework, open to any reader.

**Silence** · A Silence Realm forbids lawful transformation, so no rite inside one; a patient who wants a corrupted Wellspring cut out cannot have it done there either.

**Look up** · The Concord's Scission casework; Fixatio's irreversibility; the Seal's closing conditions; Dissolution's Failure in the Wellspring entries; the Silence Realm register; the Glory Stage table and the Spirit Path gates.

# The High Empire of Eresse

*The Whole Chorus · First Eon, Great Spirit War Era · Before the Duskwalker Schism*
> *"She sang, and we were the note that answered her. We did not forget this. We only, later, forgot how to hear it together."*
> — Attributed to the Sunroot Court, First Eon
> Before there was a Grand Principality, before there were Varrisak wanderers or Drow custodians of ruined places, there was one people and one word for what they were: **Vey-Elarin, the tuned.**
>
> Everything below describes a civilization that **does not yet know it is about to end.**

---

## Origin and Founding

Florwyn sang the Realmsong of Edenis, and the Vey-Elarin were tuned into being as living notes of that chord. What the later Grand Principality remembers as scripture, the High Empire lived as immediate, unmetaphored fact: **they were not a people descended from a goddess. They were a people still being sung**, continuously, for as long as the song continued. The First Realmsong did not end at their creation. It became the ground they walked on, the rivers they drank from, and the sky no one had yet decided to call anyone's property.
> **The Covenant of Green Flame** formalized this relationship. Where later ages remember it as the first elven communion with the Wellsprings, the High Empire understood it more plainly: **Florwyn had shown them how to listen to a place well enough that the place began listening back.**
>
> Traits bound themselves to land during this era not because the Vey-Elarin commanded it, but because they asked — repeatedly, patiently, across generations — until a grove or a river or a mountain's shoulder consented to remain conscious of them.
>
> This is the seed of every Wellspring the world now measures. **The High Empire did not invent Wellsprings. They were the first people polite enough to be given some.**

---

## The Kinship Doctrine

The Empire's entire civic and spiritual architecture rests on a single doctrine, **rarely written down because writing it down would imply it needed defending**: the land is a relative, not a resource.
A river is not owned by the village that drinks from it. A grove is not owned by the house that shelters beneath it. Both are understood to have their own standing, their own memory, and their own right to be asked rather than simply used. *This is not sentiment.* It is treated as literal fact, borne out by the Wellsprings the Covenant of Green Flame proved conscious.

### The Wheel of Given Seasons

The calendar is not measured in months but in **eight Given turnings** — Waking Root, First Green, Long Sun, Seedfall, Cooling Hearth, Still Water, Deep Dark, and Returning Light — each marked by a communal rite in which the Empire's harvest, hunting, and building activity pauses so that the land can be formally thanked rather than merely used up.
**A bad harvest during any turning is read first as a failure of gratitude before it is read as a failure of weather**, and the Sunroot Court's granaries exist as much to demonstrate reciprocity as to feed anyone.

### The Long Walk

Every Vey-Elarin child, upon reaching **Root-Turning** at roughly the fourteenth year, is sent alone into land the family does not already know, to walk until a place agrees to be known by them.
This is not survival training, though it functions as that incidentally. **It is a courtship.** The child returns with a **totem-kinship** — an animal, a landform, a weather-pattern — that has agreed to stand as their second family for the rest of their life, and every subsequent major life rite is performed in some acknowledgment of that bond.
> A child who returns from the Long Walk with **no totem-kinship at all** is not shamed. They are watched carefully, gently, and without being told why, **because the Empire has quiet suspicions about what an unclaimed child eventually becomes** — and those suspicions will, generations later, prove founded.

### Ancestor Communion

The dead are not mourned once and released. **They are consulted.** Every household keeps a **Standing Fire** that is never allowed to go fully cold, fed with a pinch of dried root at dawn and dusk specifically so that the household's dead have a warm place to return to and be asked things.
This is where the later Grand Principality's cold, formal Parunic ancestor-scholarship comes from, hollowed out of its original warmth: **they kept the archive and lost the fire.**

### Spirit-Guardians of Place

Certain locations — old-growth groves, river confluences, cave-mouths, mountain saddles — host a **Spirit-Guardian**, a landform-bound consciousness the Wellspring architecture would later formalize as a minor Domain-anchor, but which the High Empire simply calls **a neighbor.**
Villages are built to face these places rather than away from them. Disputes over land use are, in practice, disputes settled by asking the Spirit-Guardian, through a Song-Keeper, what it will and will not tolerate.

## Government and the Royal Branches

Ruled from the **Sunroot Court** by an Empress or Emperor bearing the title **First Listener** — the one whose ear is judged, at coronation, to hear the Realmsong most clearly of any living Vey-Elarin. The First Listener does not rule by decree so much as **by ratification**: the five Royal Branches bring the land's answer to the throne, and the First Listener's function is to weave those answers into one coherent voice rather than overrule any of them.
| House | Totem | Keepers of |
|---|---|---|
| **House of the Standing Stone** | Elk | The high passes and the quarries. **The current ruling branch**, and the house from which the reigning First Listener, **Tahvo Rootspeaker**, is drawn. Patient, load-bearing, slow to anger and slower to forgive |
| **House of the Long River** | Heron | The waterways, trade, and boundary law. The Empire's diplomats and record-keepers, credited with formalizing the Wheel of Given Seasons into something the whole Empire could share a calendar around |
| **House of the Ember Grove** | Fox | Hearth-craft, healing, and the Standing Fires. Produces most of the Empire's Song-Keepers and Sapkeepers |
| **House of the Windwake** | Hawk | The high scouts and the Empire's earliest warning network against the Erebus incursion. Supplies the bulk of the Rootguard's scouting arm |
| **House of the Deep Root** | Bear | The dead, the oldest Standing Fire lineages, and the uncomfortable work of the Grand Inquisitor's office. **The least politically powerful house and, not coincidentally, the one every other house is quietly relieved does not want the throne** |

The five houses meet at the Sunroot Court during each Given turning, and any decision touching more than one house's territory requires all five totem-kinships to be, in some ritual sense, present and asked.
> This is slow. **The High Empire considers slow a feature, not a flaw** — which is precisely the civic habit that will cost it dearly once the Great Spirit War stops giving it the luxury of slowness.

## The Sacred Roles of the Court

### The Grand Inquisitor of Eresse

Where the other court roles serve the land, the Grand Inquisitor serves **the truth of what the land is being asked to endure**, and the office exists because the Kinship Doctrine has a blind spot: *a people this convinced of their own reciprocity can be slow to notice when reciprocity has quietly stopped.*
They investigate broken kinship-oaths, land taken without asking, Standing Fires let cold through neglect rather than accident, and — increasingly, as the war drags on — **the first Vey-Elarin who begin listening to something other than Florwyn's Realmsong for power.**
The title is not inherited. It is earned through the **Farwalker's Trial**, in which a candidate is sent to sit, alone and unfed, inside a Spirit-Guardian's place until the Guardian itself judges whether the candidate can be trusted to hear a lie. The first to hold the office, and still its most invoked example, was **Onata Farwalker**, who is said to have sat three full Given turnings inside the river-cave at Long Confluence before the water finally spoke her name.
> The office carries no crown, no seat among the five houses, and no vote at Sunroot Court — deliberately, so that no house can ever claim the Inquisitor as their own instrument.
>
> **This independence is precisely what will make the office so dangerous, and so contested, once the first true Duskwalker heresies begin and the question of who gets to define corruption stops being academic.**

### The Rootguard — Guardians of the Throne

Not a standing army. **A small, deliberately unscalable circle of protectors**, each bonded personally to the reigning First Listener rather than to the office of the throne itself — which means a Rootguard's service technically ends the moment their sovereign dies and must be re-sworn, individually, to whoever answers the Realmsong next.
A candidate is drawn only from those who returned from their Long Walk with an unusually assertive totem-kinship: one that, in the Empire's words, *asked to be worn rather than merely carried.*
- ****The Armor** — doctrine, not aesthetic preference**
  Upon acceptance into the circle, a candidate returns to the exact place their Long Walk totem-kinship was formed, and the armor is grown or forged there, **from materials the place itself yields**: river-stone inlay for a Long River bond, fire-blackened hearth-iron for an Ember Grove bond, storm-struck heartwood for a Windwake bond.

  A Rootguard's breastplate is marked, always, with the totem-kinship's likeness worked directly into the metal or wood rather than painted or engraved afterward — an elk's antler-line following the collarbone, a heron's wing-feathers layered down the pauldron, **a bear's claw-mark scored into the vambrace as though the animal itself had reached across the wearer's arm and left its hand there.**

  The markings are read by other Vey-Elarin the way a name is read: a Rootguard **cannot lie about which totem-kinship claims them**, because the armor already says so, and forging a false set is considered a crime closer to identity-theft than to simple fraud.

  The armor is never repaired by anyone but the Rootguard who wears it. **A dent left unmended is read as an honest scar. A dent smoothed over by someone else's hand is read as a small, specific betrayal** of the bond that made the armor possible in the first place.
The Empress's current Rootguard, **Tocho Stonemark**, wears a breastplate quarried from the same high pass his Long Walk claimed him in, veined through with a raw seam of the same ore the Standing Stone quarries export, so that his armor is, in a very literal sense, made of the same stone his house rules. *He has not repaired the long crack down its left side since a Voidthorn-tainted incursion beast put it there two turnings ago, and the Empress has never asked him to.*

### The Song-Keeper — Mage of the Sunroot Court

Not a wizard in the later, Parunic-formal sense. **A translator** — the court role responsible for asking a Spirit-Guardian a direct question and returning with an answer the rest of the court can act on.
Song-Keepers train for decades in the specific discipline of **listening past their own wants**, since a Song-Keeper who hears what they wish to hear rather than what the land actually says is worse than useless: *they are actively dangerous, capable of authorizing a war or a harvest the land never actually consented to.*
> The reigning Song-Keeper, **Nuna Farsong** of the Ember Grove, is the Empire's principal voice in negotiating Wellspring-adjacent defenses during the Erebus incursion.
>
> It is Nuna's growing private worry — voiced to no one yet but the Standing Fire in her own home — that some of the newer defensive rites being asked of the land **are no longer requests. They are demands dressed as requests.**
>
> She has not brought this to the Grand Inquisitor. *Not yet.*

### The Sapkeeper — Alchemist of the Court

Where the Song-Keeper asks the land questions, the Sapkeeper **asks the land for gifts**, and the distinction matters enormously to the Empire's ethics even when the two roles' daily work looks similar from outside.
A Sapkeeper harvests root, sap, bark, and blood-warm mineral water, **always with a formal asking and always leaving something of equal weight behind**, and turns the yield into the Empire's medicines, poisons, preservation-drafts, and the slow, patient work of treating Wellspring-corrupted ground before it can spread.
**Wesa Greenhand**, the current Sapkeeper, is credited with the Empire's **Long Sleep draughts** — used, during the worst of the war's sieges, to let wounded defenders be set gently aside in a suspended, low-cost state rather than left to die on an overtaxed healer's table.
> Wesa's private ledgers, kept in a cipher only her own apprentices can read, record every single asking she has ever made of the land in exchange for what she has taken.
>
> **She has begun, in the last several turnings, noting which askings the land has started answering more slowly than it used to.**

## The Great Spirit War

The Erebus incursion **did not arrive as an army.** It arrived as a corruption moving through the Wellsprings themselves, souring the very reciprocity the Kinship Doctrine depends on.
The High Empire's **Domain Stability** — the trait later ages will remember as the Eressean line's signature racial gift — was forged in this era specifically because the Vey-Elarin were the only people patient enough, and trusted enough by the land itself, to **hold a corrupted Wellspring open long enough to be treated rather than simply sealed and abandoned.**
Whole lineages of the House of the Windwake burned themselves down to embers holding contaminated ground stable while Sapkeepers and Song-Keepers worked.
> The cost of this era is the direct ancestor of everything the later Grand Principality will inherit and never quite understand the weight of: **a Domain Stability Trait bought, turnings ago, at a price the current Eressean line no longer remembers paying.**
It is during the war's most desperate stretch that the first strategic disagreements over how to prioritize elven survival begin — quietly, in Sunroot Court sessions the official record will later smooth over. **The Sylvaar Rebellion Schism and the Duskwalker Schism both grow out of decisions made in exactly this room, by people who believe, entirely sincerely, that they are only arguing about tactics.**

## Cultural Customs and Daily Life

**The Asking** · No Vey-Elarin takes wood, water, game, or stone from the land without a spoken formal request, however brief. *A child's first fully independent Asking is treated as a more significant milestone than their first successful hunt.*
**Name-Sharing** · A Vey-Elarin's totem-kinship name is never spoken by anyone outside their household without the bearer's direct permission. **Using it uninvited is considered an act of aggression roughly equivalent to drawing a weapon.**
**The Standing Fires** · Kept in every household, never allowed to go fully cold, and read as a rough barometer of a family's spiritual health. *A cold Standing Fire prompts neighbors to intervene long before it prompts judgment.*
**Council by Turning** · No Sunroot Court decision touching more than one house is finalized inside a single Given turning. The Empire treats haste in governance the way it treats haste in harvest: **as a form of disrespect toward whatever is being decided about.**
**The Quiet Roads** · Trade paths between the five houses' territories are deliberately left unpaved and unmarked by structure, on the doctrine that **a road too convenient stops being a relationship with the land and becomes a wound in it.**

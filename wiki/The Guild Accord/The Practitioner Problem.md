# The Practitioner Problem

*Standing Doctrine on Individuals Who Outweigh Regiments*
> Issued by the Strategic Division for the instruction of field commanders at Legion rank and above. **Sections on Voidic and the Law of Reversal are withheld from copies issued outside the Concord Citadel** and appear here by direction of the High Marshal.
> 
> *First of the War Cycle.*

---

## The Question

> A Stage XII practitioner walks onto a field held by four thousand men. Every officer present understands that they cannot kill this person.
>
> **The question this document exists to answer is why the four thousand are there at all.**
Every field commander asks it in their first year and most are given an answer that is a slogan. **The Division's position is that the slogan has cost more lives than any enemy.**
The honest answer requires admitting the premise. A practitioner at Emanation or above **cannot be beaten by an army in any sense a soldier would recognise.** There is no formation that kills them, no volume of ordinary steel that reaches them, and no number of ordinary men whose deaths add up to their defeat. *Commanders who have been taught otherwise have marched into the proof.*

### The distinction

> **A practitioner converts Essence into effect. An army converts time into ground.**
>
> These are different currencies **and they do not exchange.** The practitioner's currency is enormous, finite, and **spends itself whether or not anything worthwhile is bought with it.** The army's currency is inexhaustible in principle and worthless in a hurry.
A Stage XII practitioner can unmake a regiment in an afternoon. **They cannot occupy a province.** They cannot hold a bridge for three weeks, garrison six towns at once, escort a grain convoy, invest a fortress, **or be awake in two valleys on the same night.** They must eat, they must sleep, they must be supplied, and the moment they stop spending Essence **the ground reverts to whoever is standing on it.**
> **Ground is held by bodies. It has never been held by anything else.** The four thousand are there because the war is about ground and the practitioner is not.

---

## What a Practitioner Is, Militarily

| Finding | Consequence |
|---|---|
| **They are artillery, not infantry** | A weapon system with a rate of fire, a magazine, a crew requirement, and a displacement time. **Every doctrinal error in the Division's history has come from officers who treated them as very good soldiers rather than as very expensive guns** |
| **They have a magazine and it empties** | Essence is finite per practitioner per day, replenishment is slower than expenditure under load, **and the practitioner knows exactly how much they have left at every moment.** That knowledge governs their behaviour more completely than any order does |
| **They cannot be two places** | The constraint the entire doctrine is built on, **and the one commanders forget first** — because a practitioner arriving somewhere is spectacular and a practitioner failing to arrive somewhere else is invisible |
| **They degrade** | Sustained output past the second hour produces measurable coarsening in the Aether Shell. *Techniques surgical at dawn are approximate by midday and blunt by dusk.* **A tired practitioner is not a weaker practitioner. They are a less precise one** — a different and often worse problem for everybody nearby |
| **They are irreplaceable** | A Legion can absorb the loss of a company and continue. **It cannot absorb the loss of its Aether Knights.** This asymmetry is the enemy's principal opportunity, and every experienced commander conducts their campaign around protecting an asset **more valuable than the objective** |

## The Spend

> *"Cost him out and hand him to the infantry."*
> The whole of Division doctrine, as taught in the ranks, **in seven words the War-Priests have never managed to improve on.**
The War-Priests of Urion read the Lattice hum to estimate an opposing practitioner's remaining capacity. Their instrument is imprecise, their estimates are conservative by policy, **and their function on a Division staff is not prophecy. It is bookkeeping.**
A practitioner enters an engagement with a budget. **Make them spend it on things that do not matter.**

### The four spends

**On the worthless** · Screening troops, feints, decoys, empty positions defended with conviction. A technique discharged into a company of levy is a technique not available three hours later against the Aether Knights. **This is what levies are for, and the Division does not say so out loud in the presence of levies.**
**On the ground** · Prepared positions, entrenchment, stakes, flooded approaches, obstacles that must be removed rather than crossed. **Ground cannot be intimidated and does not rout.** A practitioner faced with a properly prepared frontage must either spend Essence to unmake it or spend time to go around it, **and the Division is content with either.**
**On suppression** · Sealwright interference, Parun Rod disruption, Voidic proximity. **Suppression rarely stops anything. It raises the price of everything, and the price is the objective.**
**On the sustain** · A seated Domain draws continuously whether or not it is doing anything. **A practitioner who has been persuaded to seat a Domain early is a practitioner paying rent**, and the cheapest way to persuade them is to threaten something they must not lose.

### Costed out

> A practitioner who has exhausted their budget **is a person.** Skilled, dangerous, physically formidable, and a person. **Every doctrine in this document exists to produce it.**
>
> **The transition is not visible from outside**, and this is the source of most of the Legion's casualties. A costed-out practitioner behaves exactly like a full one **until the moment they do not**, and the men sent to test the proposition are the men who find out.
>
> The Division's standing guidance is that the test is made with expendable formations, **and that the guidance is not to be read aloud.**

## Why Mass Still Matters

*The five functions of ordinary soldiers on a field that contains a practitioner. None of them is killing the practitioner.*
**Frontage** · A line of men occupies a width. A practitioner occupies a point and a radius. Beyond that radius the war continues. **Mass is the instrument by which an army makes a battlefield larger than one person can attend to.**
**Flank and rear** · The practitioner's own supply, baggage, and line of retreat are held by ordinary men, **and can be taken by ordinary men.** An army that ignores this in favour of contesting the centre **has agreed to fight the enemy's battle.**
**Depth** · Reserves absorb a breakthrough. A single catastrophic technique that would end a shallow formation merely wounds a deep one, **and the difference between those two outcomes is measured in ranks.**
**Holding** · Ground taken must be occupied or it is not taken. **This is the function no practitioner performs and none has ever performed**, and it is the reason the levy exists.
> **Attrition economics.** Ordinary soldiers are replaceable at a known cost. Practitioners are not replaceable at any cost.
>
> An exchange that trades three hundred levy for one Aether Knight is **a catastrophe for the side that lost the Knight and a routine Tuesday for the side that lost the levy** — and both commanders know the arithmetic before the engagement begins.

## What a Domain Does to a Line

*The Division spent sixty years teaching officers that a Domain kills the men inside it. The finding that replaced that teaching is the most important single correction in the Division's history, and it cost the Fifth Legion to obtain.*
> **A seated Domain does not kill a formation. It breaks it** — which is not the same thing and produces the deaths by a different route.
Inside a Domain the world runs on the practitioner's terms. Their equipment behaves fractionally wrong. **Sound arrives from the wrong direction or does not arrive.** Distances read false. **The man beside them looks unfamiliar for a moment they cannot account for.** Orders do not propagate, because orders travel on drums and voice and sightlines and a Domain interferes with all three by construction.
**None of that is lethal.** All of it is corrosive to the one thing that holds a line together, which is **a man's certainty that the men beside him are still there and that somebody knows what is happening.**
So the line breaks. And the killing occurs in the rout, as it always has, **because a man who turns his back has discarded his shield, his formation and his facing in a single motion** — and a Domain that has broken a formation does not need to do anything further. *The pursuit is conducted by ordinary cavalry at no cost to the practitioner at all.*

### The doctrinal consequences

| Finding | What follows |
|---|---|
| **A Domain is a rout engine** | Assess it by **how fast it breaks cohesion, not by how many it kills.** Two Domains of identical scale can differ by an hour in the time they take to unmake a formation, **and that hour is the whole engagement** |
| **Veterans cost more than recruits** | A formation with genuine internal cohesion resists a Domain considerably longer, **for reasons that have nothing to do with Temperance.** Men who know each other keep standing after the drums have stopped meaning anything. **The Division's investment in unit stability is defended in the budget as a morale expense and is in fact a metaphysical countermeasure** |
| **Command must be redundant before contact** | Orders that will not survive a Domain **must be issued before the Domain is seated.** Formations trained to continue on last instruction hold. **Formations trained to await orders dissolve, and dissolve fastest at the moment they are most needed** |
| **The Domain Engineers earn their pay here** | A counter-Domain does not need to defeat the enemy's. **It needs to give the men inside it one true thing.** A patch of ground where the sound is right and the distances read correctly is worth more to a wavering line than a company of reinforcements — **which is why Engineers are instructed to seat toward the formation rather than toward the enemy** |

## The Five Counters

*In ascending order of cost and descending order of availability.*
**Ground** · The cheapest and the most reliable. **Every campaign in the record that a smaller force won against a superior practitioner was won on ground chosen months in advance by somebody who never fought on it.** Ground does not rout, cannot be intimidated, does not tire, and is not impressed. *A practitioner who has spent four hours unmaking a fortified line has spent four hours unmaking dirt.*
**Distance** · The two-front tax. A practitioner who must cover two objectives covers neither properly, and **a commander who can threaten three has effectively reduced the enemy's principal asset by two thirds without engaging it.**
**Attention** · Screening forces, feints, and the deliberate manufacture of urgencies. The War-Priests hold that **attention is depleted faster than Essence and recovers slower**, and that a practitioner who has been made to choose six times before noon **is functionally costed out by mid afternoon regardless of what remains in the magazine.**
**Suppression** · Expensive, slow to deploy, fragile in the field, and almost never decisive on its own. *A Sealwright team that has doubled the cost of an opposing practitioner's technique has done more for the engagement than any three companies on the field, and will receive credit for none of it, and knows this going in.*
> **The Bind.** Domain against Domain — two practitioners contesting the same ground on incompatible terms, producing a standing interference in which neither can operate freely **and both pay continuously to remain.**
>
> The Bind is **a stalemate by design and the Division treats it as a victory condition rather than a failure.** It converts the enemy's irreplaceable asset into a stationary object paying rent, and everything else on the field proceeds as an ordinary battle between ordinary men — **which is a battle the Legion knows how to win.**
>
> The cost is that the Bind **consumes one of the Legion's own irreplaceable assets to do it.** Aether Knights assigned to Bind duty are frequently ruined by the assignment, and the Division's own reviews record that **the men who volunteer for it a second time are rarely the men who did it well the first.**

## Voidic

> *Withheld from Legion-rank copies. The Division's position is that the fewer commanders who understand what a Voidic practitioner is for, the fewer will request one.*
Aether Class VI is **lateral rather than sequential.** It does not sit above Radiant on any ladder and does not represent an advancement. **The Shell's polarity is reversed, and a Voidic practitioner devours where others emit.**
> The military consequence is direct. **A Voidic practitioner is a poor attacker and the finest suppression instrument in the world, and the two facts are the same fact.**
**They are not fielded as combatants.** A Voidic practitioner committed to ordinary engagement is a waste of the rarest asset the Accord possesses. **They are pointed, like a siege piece, at one target.**
**They are slow to move and cannot defend themselves.** Every consideration governing heavy artillery governs them, **including a dedicated guard detachment whose only function is to keep ordinary soldiers away from an asset that cannot deal with ordinary soldiers.**
**Their presence is the effect.** Proximity alone raises the cost of every technique executed nearby — **including the Legion's own.** Voidic assets are sited with the same care as a burning brand in a powder store, **and the Division's siting regulations run to eleven pages of which nine concern friendly forces.**
**They are structurally distrusted.** A practitioner whose architecture consumes Essence is one the Lattice reads differently, and Voidic operatives are subject to standing review procedures applied to no other class. *Whether this is a security measure or a prejudice with a procedure attached is a question the Division has declined to examine four times.*
> Because Voidic practitioners are the counter, and because the counter is rare, **the strategic balance of the four quarters rests on how many exist and who holds them.**
>
> The Threat Codex maintains a separate register for this. **The register is short.** The Division has never published its length, **and the fact that it has never published its length is itself the most informative thing on the subject that anybody outside the Citadel has been able to obtain.**

## Speed, and What It Buys

*A courier at B-Grade travels faster than any commander in history has been able to think. This ought to have ended the fog of war. It did not, and understanding why is the whole of Concord command doctrine.*
An army possessing high-Grade couriers enjoys something no historical force has ever had: **command in something close to real time across a front too wide to see.** What that buys is tempo, concentration against fractions, and arrival before the enemy has finished deciding. **The Accord's strategic dominance for two centuries rests on this and on nothing else.**

### Why it does not settle the matter

**Speed is Essence.** A courier who has run at Grade arrives depleted and requires hours before they can run again. **Real-time command is not a property of the army. It is a consumable, and it is consumed fastest on the days it matters most.**
**Couriers are the first thing killed.** Courier interdiction is the cheapest strategic operation available, and **the Legion's own courier losses in a hard campaign routinely exceed its losses in any other specialisation.**
**Real-time command degrades to historical command precisely when it is tested.** The same army on the fourteenth day of a contested campaign has runners on foot and a commander guessing. **The Division's most repeated finding across four hundred years of after-action review is that formations trained on real-time command collapse faster under courier loss than formations that never had it.**
> **The Law of Witness supplies a floor.** Every engagement must be recorded by a Concord Scribe or Seer, and the record enters the Lattice. This is a legal requirement **and functions incidentally as a communications system of last resort**, since a Scribe's record reaches the Citadel whether or not any courier survives.
>
> Commanders have learned to write for it. **The Division has never formally acknowledged that its own legal apparatus is load-bearing for its command apparatus, and would prefer the question not be asked in front of Arbitration.**

## Capture

*A dead practitioner is a message. A living one is an asset, a hostage, a bargaining position, and eventually a witness.*
A costed-out practitioner can be taken. **This is the single most valuable outcome available to a field commander and the one their men are least inclined to deliver**, because the men who reach a costed-out practitioner are usually the survivors of what they did before they were costed out.
The Division's standing instruction is that capture is preferred, that the instruction is to be repeated at every level of command, **and that it will be disobeyed.** *Commanders are advised to assign the taking to formations that were not engaged.*
> **The Lawbrand exists for this.** It seals Essence rather than spilling blood, and the Spirit Armory's continued production of a weapon that is worse than a sword at everything except taking practitioners alive **is the clearest statement of Accord priorities in the entire arsenal.**
**Politically** · A Crown that has lost its champion has lost a category of threat it cannot rebuild inside a generation. **Terms available after such a capture are terms unavailable after a battlefield death, because a corpse cannot be returned and a prisoner can.**
**Metaphysically** · A living Soul Crystal can be read. **Trials of Resonance conducted on a captured practitioner have produced intelligence that no interrogation could**, and Arbitration's position on the admissibility of such readings has been revised in the Accord's favour on three occasions.
**Materially** · *This entry has been redacted from all copies issued below the Citadel.*

## The Law of Reversal

> *Withheld from Legion-rank copies. Every Crown in the four quarters knows its contents and the Division is aware that they know.*
> If the Concord becomes the aggressor, the Lattice strips the resonance of its forces and the army ceases to function. **Not degrades. Ceases.** Weapons go inert, harmonics fail, and the finest military instrument ever assembled becomes **forty thousand people standing in a field holding objects.**
>
> This is not a policy and cannot be repealed. It is a structural property of the covenant that gives the Legion its power, **and it means the Guild Accord is constitutionally incapable of a war of conquest.**

### What every enemy does about it

**The Accord's opponents do not seek to defeat the Legion. They seek to establish that the Legion is the aggressor** — a cheaper campaign conducted largely in Arbitration and requiring no soldiers at all.
Border provocations designed to produce a disproportionate response. Civilian presence arranged where the Legion must strike. Wellspring claims filed in advance of hostilities to convert a defensive intervention into a contested seizure.
> The entire body of Accord military law reads, to an experienced Crown, **as a list of instructions for disabling the Accord's army by paperwork.**
The Division's counter is procedural and slow and consists almost entirely of the Law of Witness — **which is why the Law of Witness is enforced with a severity that appears disproportionate to anyone who has not read this section.**
> Every operation the Legion conducts must be defensible in Arbitration **before it is conducted**, which means the Legion fights with an inherent delay its opponents do not have, on ground its opponents have usually chosen, **under rules of engagement its opponents have frequently written.**
>
> **This is the true asymmetry of Accord warfare and it runs the opposite direction from the one the public assumes.** The Accord's army is not restrained by its principles in the manner of a strong power exercising forbearance. **It is disabled by them in a specific, mechanical, exploitable fashion** — and every Crown with a competent legal division knows exactly how.

## Counterplay at Scale

*Three fields that convert an individual technique into a mass-combat instrument with a defeat condition. A technique that cannot be given all three does not belong on a battlefield.*
| Field | What it governs |
|---|---|
| **Frontage** | How much line the technique affects, in men or in paces. A frontage of twelve men is **a personal weapon used in a battle.** A frontage of three hundred is **an instrument of war.** *The distinction is not power. It is width, and most spectacular techniques are narrow* |
| **Sustain** | How long the effect can be held before Essence cost forces cessation, **and what the practitioner cannot do while holding it.** A Domain with an eight-hour Sustain and a practitioner who can do nothing else during those eight hours **has told you exactly how the enemy should spend their day** |
| **Re-form** | How long an affected formation needs to recover cohesion, **and whether it recovers at all.** **This is the field that decides battles.** A technique that breaks a line for ten minutes has bought a local advantage. A technique that breaks a line permanently **has ended the war on that flank** |

> The counterplay to any scaled technique is therefore always one of three things.
>
> **Exceed its Frontage**, so the war continues where it is not. **Outlast its Sustain**, so the practitioner pays and gains nothing. Or **shorten its Re-form** — which is what drill, veterancy, redundant command and a counter-Domain seated toward your own men are all purchased to do.

## Unresolved Entries

**The costed-out threshold** · No instrument reliably distinguishes a depleted practitioner from a full one at distance. The War-Priests' estimates carry an acknowledged error margin **and men die inside it.** Four proposals for a field instrument have been funded and none has left the Archives.
**Whether the Bind can be conducted by a lesser practitioner** · Present doctrine requires an asset of comparable Stage. Whether two or three lower-Stage practitioners can jointly Bind one greater has been modelled favourably **and never tested, because the test consumes the practitioners either way.**
**Domain Re-form in veteran formations** · The Division holds figures for recruit formations and none for veterans, **because veteran formations are not made available for the trials.** The gap is known, has been known for ninety years, and is annotated in the Division's own returns as *pending.*
**Voidic register length** · Not published. **Not published deliberately.**
> **Whether the Law of Reversal has ever been invoked.** The Division's position is that it has not.
>
> The Archives hold **one sealed session from the Eclipse Wars whose subject is not recorded and whose duration was eleven days.**

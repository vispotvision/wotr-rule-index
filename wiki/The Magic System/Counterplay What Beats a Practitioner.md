# Counterplay: What Beats a Practitioner

> The question is never how you beat the working. It is which of the four things you take away from him, and how long you have before he notices which one you chose.

### The Four Routes

Everything in this register reduces to one of four. **Deny the field. Break the boundary. Break the body. Break the man.** They cost different amounts, they take different lengths of time, and a competent commander picks by what is available rather than by preference.

---

## I. Deny the Field

*Take away what he draws from. The working is correct and produces nothing.*
**Cut the main.** In any district on the network this is the first move and the cheapest, and it is administrative rather than martial. A Board inspector with the right seal can drop a quarter's supply in under an hour. The practitioner is not weakened; the room is. **Every serious action inside a Concord city begins with somebody deciding whether to cut, and that decision is made by people who will not be present at the fighting.**
**Choose the ground by Family.** Terrain is a modifier on efficiency and the modifiers are large. Caloria fails in thermal equilibrium, saturated cold, and oxygen-poor air. Fulguria fails against boiled leather, fired clay, dense fog and a shielded interior. Materia has opinions and no tools on open water. Fluxia cannot act without a medium, so a dry vault is a Fluxia prison. **The counter-Fulguria kit has been leather and ceramic for four centuries and it is not a secret; it is simply cheaper to ignore until somebody dies of it.**
**Saturate it.** Flood the area with Essence faster than it disperses and everyone present takes Fracture risk, **including whoever did it.** A weapon of last resort and a real one, and its practical use is the threat rather than the act.
**Silence.** The removal of magical possibility from a region, which is not shielding and not counterspelling. Lawful transformation cannot be authorised at all inside one. Animatria constructs fail completely because their continuity runs back to a source the Silence has severed. **A soul held in one reports the sensation as loneliness rather than weakness**, and that distinction is the most reliable field diagnostic for whether a quiet place is Silence or merely thin.

---

## II. Break the Boundary

*Attack the sentence rather than the speaker.*
**Interrupt the chain.** Below Refraction a working requires an external anchor: voice, hand, ink or blood. Take the anchor and the sentence does not complete. **Break his wrist and a gestured chain is finished. Put a hand over his mouth and a spoken one is.** Above Refraction the chain is internal and none of this applies, which is the practical reason Stage VII is the line every soldier is taught to fear rather than the reason the Codex gives.
**Force the Seal early.** The Seal closes an operation against the warrant that opened it, and a warrant overstating standing seals anyway, **with the difference deducted from the speaker at the moment of closing.** A third party who can compel an early close is not defending. They are billing him.
**Knot and Lock.** The only two Counter forms in the index, and they do not oppose a law, because laws cannot be opposed. They condition the conditioning. **Knot** entangles a path so it cannot be traced cleanly. **Lock** occupies a limit so no other limit can be set there. Every counter-working ever recorded is one of those two moves.
**Wards.** Runecraft standing on a substrate, and therefore beaten the way Runecraft is beaten: **damage the substrate.** A ward is not argued with. It is chiselled off, or the wall it sits on is brought down, or the third layer is scraped through by somebody who knows which layer to scrape. *An inscription with a defective Sealing role has been failing continuously for two centuries and is a liability to whoever owns it, not a defence.*
**Countersigning.** Some Seals end when countersigned in writing, and some end when the sum is spoken back correctly by another. **A working that carries either of those closing conditions can be ended by an opponent who is literate enough**, which is why Accord field practice avoids both and why they persist in the old inscriptions everywhere.

## III. Break the Body

*The Crystal is inside a person and the person is ordinary.*
**Shot.** Chemically cheap, mechanically simple, and it does not care about Stage below the point where reaction speed outruns it. A C-Grade practitioner can react to gunfire and cannot outrun it. **A volley is a volley.** This is the entire reason armour survived into the Imperial Age and the entire reason ranks did.
**Numbers, correctly used.** One practitioner beats twenty men. Twenty men who have been drilled to spread, to make him choose, and to keep a line of fire open at all times will take casualties and kill him, **and the doctrine that produces this is a hundred and forty years old and taught badly everywhere.**
**Distance.** Fulguria falls off with the square. Vectoria has to close. Corporis is a body and a body has reach. **Most practitioners are a threat about closing range and are written as though they were not.**
**Exhaustion.** Below ten percent reserve a practitioner suffers Essence Starvation: reduced stat expression, Shell degradation, Traits flickering. **A defender who can afford to spend nothing wins a long engagement against an attacker who cannot afford to spend nothing**, and sieges are decided by this more often than by any working.
**Armour tiers.** Padded takes bruising and not gore. Mail is immune to cuts, vulnerable to thrusts and blunt trauma, and vibrates under Essence shock in a way the wearer feels in the teeth. Brigandine hides its plates and the gaps are the openings. Proofed plate turns shot at range and turns most edges entirely, leaving joints and grapples. **Ceramic and shard are sacrificial and fail on the second hit in the same place.**

## IV. Break the Man

*The most reliable and the least discussed.*
**Read him.** Taught techniques are documented and counterable by anyone who studied. Self-derived techniques must be read live, in the two or three exchanges before they kill you. **Every thaumaturge has a pattern**, because single-axis fluency produces one, and a pattern learned is a pattern interruptible.
**Make him hold Pressure in.** Suppression is a skill and it is tiring. A practitioner who cannot afford to be identified is paying continuously for the privilege, and a long approach through populated ground costs him more than the fight will.
**Rigidity.** A Trait's law is not negotiable by its bearer, and the more absolute it is the less he can contradict it. **Present him with the situation his own law handles worst and he will handle it that way regardless**, because the alternative is not available to him. This is the cruellest counter in the register and it requires knowing the man rather than the technique.
**The vow.** A vow kept buys Coherence with conduct. **A vow broken is a Shear Break**, and an opponent who knows the terms of a vow holds a weapon that does not require touching him. *Manoeuvring a sworn man into breaking his own oath is legal everywhere, universally despised, and has ended more careers than any duel.*
**Coherence drift.** A practitioner whose lived behaviour has drifted from his stated law is degrading before anyone lays a hand on him: output falls, Thresholds refuse to authorise, and his Traits begin to argue with him. **You do not have to cause this. You have to notice it and wait.**

---

## What Does Not Work

**Matching him.** Two practitioners at the same Grade resolve by technique, positioning and Sub-Stat architecture, and the exchange is a coin toss dressed as a plan. *A commander who answers a practitioner with a practitioner has declined to make a decision.*
**Shielding.** Nothing in the register stops a working by standing in front of it. Armour changes what the working does to a body. Wards condition what may be authorised in a space. **Neither is a wall and both are written as one constantly.**
**Waiting for the cost.** Costs are real and they are rarely due inside a scene. *A plan that consists of surviving until he tires is a plan to be killed by a tired man.*
> **The doctrine, stated once.** A practitioner is beaten by taking away one of four things: the field, the sentence, the body, or the man. **Pick before contact.** Everything that goes wrong in the record went wrong because somebody tried to decide during.

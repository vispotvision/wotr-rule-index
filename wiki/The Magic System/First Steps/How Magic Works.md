In the river towns, how magic works means Dunny Thrupp and the Bridge at Aske. Dunny ran the Aske ferry, owed fourteen silver to three different people, and had one idea in the whole of his soul, which was that a knot he tied stayed tied. It came from the flood year, when one hitch had held him and his brother to a drowned alder for two nights. At haymaking he broke the nose of a lord's nephew over a fare, and the lord put a bounty on him, and Sir Ansgar Rook came down the Aske road in gold-washed mail to collect it, a full Stage over the ferryman, his aura burning lime-kiln white, with three hundred people running behind him to watch. The bookies on the bridge had Dunny at sixty to one. Dunny stood in his boat, wet to the thighs and red in the face, threw a loop of tarred mooring line over the knight's shoulders from thirty feet, with the other end made fast to the bridge's middle pier, and bawled, at a volume that put up every heron on the river:

**"ALL THE WORLD'S ROPE!"**

The crowd howled. A fisherman fell off the rail laughing. Sir Ansgar laughed too, and charged, with the whole of his strength behind him, and the line came up tight.

It did not break. It went on not breaking, while a man who could put his shoulder through a temple door leaned on it with everything he had, and the rope sang a note that loosened teeth on both banks, and then the middle span of the Bridge at Aske came away with a groan like a ship going down and followed him up the road, oak, bookies and all. Sir Ansgar was dug out alive by evening. Dunny's palms had burned to the tendon where the line ran through them, and he could not close a hand until the harvest. The song says he tied up the moon. The lord billed him for the bridge.

That night Dunny's soul reported to him, in river talk and for his eyes alone, that he had climbed further in one afternoon than in eleven years of ferrying. He told the whole of the Aske Arms what it said, which nobody there could check, and they bought him drinks anyway.

```
[Level: 61 to 68]
[Trait: Stays Tied, Level 12 to 15]
[Skill: Knots, C to B]
[Hit: both palms burned to the tendon. Vitality 81%]
```

Every part of that afternoon has a name: where Dunny got the power, what law held the knot, what the shout bought him, what that block of lines was, why his hands paid for it, and why a man one rung up the ladder could lose to a man with a good read and a better rope. This page gives you those names, in order, and I have tried to keep it shorter than the Accord's assessment of that rope, which runs to nine leaves and leaves out the fisherman.

## The Soul Crystal, and who wakes

![[Assets/Diagrams/soul-crystal.svg]]

The Soul Crystal is the soul, and the scholars mean that literally: what your grandmother called your soul has three layers, holds power and sends it out, and can be measured. Nearly everyone is born with one. In most people it sleeps for life. About one in ten wake it, nearly always as children, taught by a school, a house or a guild, or stirring early on their own. They are practitioners (people whose Crystal is awake and working), and one in ten is a great many people: you will pass them at every market, and any child can recite the sect ranks and tell you which school's colors to avoid on a feast day. The rest are Class Ø (sealed and unwoken) and touch magic only through what they buy. A sealed Crystal can be woken, but rarely, and at great cost: by a blessing, or by a Waking Draft, which is very dear and often kills or warps the child who drinks it, and the rich buy it for their children anyway. *Read next: [[The Magic System/Practice and Institution/The Gate Who May Learn Magic|The Gate: Who May Learn Magic]].*

## Windows

Since the soul can be measured, it reports. A window is the soul reporting its numbers to its owner, in the owner's own tongue, and nobody else can see it. A Moto reads hers in Moto and a Kharven drover reads his in the language he swears in. It comes when called and it comes unasked, in the middle of a fight, the moment there is something to report. Who built it is the oldest quarrel in the Measurewrights' hall (the Measurewrights being the guild that keeps the world's tables and argues about them), and I have seen a Grandmaster throw soup over it.

Here is what Wen Talley, a tavern brawler you will meet again below, sees when he takes a normal look at himself:

```
[Wen Talley · Human · Level 47 · Stage III, Ascension · Path: Body]
[Vitality E · Ardency D · Gnosis F · Dexterity F]
[Harmonics F · Resilience F · Tempering F · Dominion F]
[Reserve: 100% ▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓]
[Trait: Hold the Rafter, Level 9]
[Skills: Brawling D · Smithing C · Drinking B]
[Blessing: none]
```

The top line carries his race, which shows because race and lineage shape what a person starts with and what comes easily, and his Path (the layer of the Crystal his growth has actually come to lean on, which nobody chooses). Below it are the Eight Primaries (his eight main stats) with their Grades, and those letters are a mean over everything under them, which is why a brawler's sheet reads F while his right hand reads a good deal higher. The reserve is a percentage and a bar. Traits carry levels and skills carry Grades, all of which this page explains. A Sub-Stat (one of the sixty-four single abilities beneath the Primaries) appears when it is used or when it moves, and the full set is there to study for anyone with a free evening.

A window shows Grades, percentages, bars and levels. It has never once shown the raw figures the Accord's instruments read, which is why assay houses still have customers, and it reports nothing about anyone else unless the owner's perception is strong enough to read them. In a fight it comes in short blocks after the blow, for damage, skill gains and reserve, and every window in this Archive is a copy dictated afterward by its owner, so allow for vanity. *Read next: [[The Magic System/The Soul and the Climb/Windows and the Sheet|Windows and the Sheet]], then [[The Magic System/First Steps/The Measures at a Glance|The Measures at a Glance]].*

## Aether and Essence

Aether is everywhere, thick in some places and thin in others, the way air is. A practitioner pulls it in from the environment around them and refines it in the Soul Crystal, where it takes on the stamp of whoever refined it, and from then on it is Essence, and they drive that Essence out through the Shell (the Crystal's outer skin) to do the work. **Essence is the soul's own substance.** The Essence Core, the Crystal's inner layer, is the center of all identity, and the Crystal is the soul, so what a fighter holds is themselves. That holding is their reserve (the store of Essence they carry), every working spends reserve, and spending it is spending yourself, which is why a person running on empty stops being entirely the person you knew.

So where you stand matters. Rich ground fills you faster and lets you put out more; thin ground starves you, and ground that has been salted or bled starves you worse. Scholars call the richness Aetheric Density. Street fighters say fat ground and lean ground, and a clever one will walk a quarrel three fields over before she lets it start. *Read next: [[The Magic System/First Steps/The Core Vocabulary|The Core Vocabulary]].*

## Three layers, three Paths

A soul meets the world through three layers, and every soul has all three, the unwoken included. The **Aether Shell** meets the Physical World: the blow, the block, the weight, the heat. The **Essence Core** sits on the Soul Plane and holds identity, memory and Traits. The **Attraction Layer** sits on the Plane of Fate and is a soul's own share of fate: its bonds, its oaths, its claims and its odds.

Only the first two of those are places in any careful sense. The Epochs, the three sovereign powers at the top of everything, stand over the three layers: Aegor's Physical Reality and Ashura's Spiritual Reality are the two lower realities, where things are. Fate, under Bara, is not a reality at all. It is everything that flows, every possibility, idea and concept, anything that can happen in those two, and it sits above them as the law and construction of the world. "Plane of Fate" is a courtesy.

A Path is the layer a Crystal has learned to lean on, and there are three. The **Body Path** is the literal understanding and manipulation of physical reality: the full force bonus at contact, and low down it has to close. High up it commands matter by recognition and then by law, and its reach runs as far as there is matter to read, though it still needs existing substance and pays in recoil. The **Spirit Path** is everything to do with spiritual reality, souls, spirits, the dead and what a thing means: force at range, and working on what a thing is. The **Fate Path** is the hardest road there is. Its everyday weapons are Range, Aura Pressure, presence and bonds. On the rare day its walker can pull a thread down, they move what a result depends on, a guard slips or a column arrives late, and the result turns up without having been forced, at a ruinous price. Its deadliest end is the true Fate Arts. Nobody picks one. The Crystal watches how you live and recognizes it, some years after it stopped being reversible.

A Path stands on a sheet as **Mastered** (its gates are no obstacle at the person's Stage), **Walked** (three parts in ten of their practice or more) or **Studied** (knowledge and small uses, which anyone may have of any Path, and which open no gates). One Path masters fastest. Two take about two and a half times as long, at three-quarters of the force bonus on each for life, with holes in the fighter at times and an opponent who cannot read them. Three take about six times as long, which is longer than a mortal life, and the world looks down on anyone trying. *Read next: [[The Magic System/The Soul and the Climb/The Three Paths Routing, Recognition and the Gate|The Three Paths]].*

## Wellsprings

A Wellspring represents an alchemical and a spiritual transformation and concept: Exuroth is the trial by fire that proves what a thing is made of, Judicium the truth that will not be talked round. You can see one as a source of power to pull from, and the old books count them as sixty types of Aether, each acting on the Crystal in its own way. More truly, a Wellspring is the governing idea that transforms how a soul uses Aether, a lens set over magic that was already potent. There are sixty, fixed, and nobody has ever made a sixty-first, sorted into eight Families (the branches of physics the Wellsprings answer to: Caloria for heat, Fulguria for lightning, and six more).

The lens shapes Essence differently on each Path, and a blow's character comes from Wellspring and Path together. Pyreveil on the Body is heat living in the tissue, and the blow burns on contact. On the Spirit it is thrown, and it burns what is false in a thing before what is solid. On Fate it is a claim that burns its way through. Each Wellspring also has a home plane, named by its kind, which is never a lock: Wild for the Physical World, Spirit for the Soul Plane, Fate for the Plane of Fate, and Medium for all three. At home a lens gives a little at first and after years of mastery can double the effect. Away from home it costs more and reaches half again at most.

A soul gets one at a Core in a dungeon, from a school, from a Draft or a blessing, or by birth. A Core is a site where a Wellspring's Aether pools, the greatest concentration there is (no relation to the Essence Core inside the Crystal, a distinction the street has never once respected). Most Cores lie in dungeons, and the rest are said to lie out in vast territories. A worked Core is a dungeon somebody cleared and now holds: it recharges, it has keepers, and its keepers can close it to you.

The scholars' account of all this is physics, and every number in it stands. Each Wellspring runs on one law, heat or motion or growth, which a practitioner harmonizes with (tunes the Shell to) and works, and the tables tell you exactly what that buys. That is how the learned explain a lens. A Measurewright will defend the account to the death and tell you what is wrong with yours. *Read next: [[The Magic System/The Foundations/The Eight Families & the Sixty Wellsprings/The Eight Families & the Sixty Wellsprings|The Eight Families & the Sixty Wellsprings]].*

## Traits

A Trait (a standing law in the soul) forms through lived experience: something a person survives hardens into a rule the world accepts as true. Nobody can teach you one. Sleeping souls carry Traits too, as a knack: the fisherman the cold cannot touch. Waking arms them. The Primary Trait (the first and deepest, carried from birth and shaped by first imprint and the life lived) sets the direction for everything that follows.

A Trait can also be leveled and refined, the way a skill can. Each one carries its own level, which rises with use and grows along with the practitioner's overall Level and skill, and the window reports the day it moves. *Read next: [[The Magic System/The Soul and the Climb/The Trait System Law, Function and the Forge|The Trait System: Law, Function and the Forge]], then [[The Magic System/The Soul and the Climb/Skills and Trait Levels|Skills and Trait Levels]].*

## Skills, techniques and the kit

Every practitioner has skills: swordwork, tracking, haggling, brewing Drafts, cutting runes, chanting, and anything else a person does until they get better at it. A skill rises with use and shows on the sheet as a Grade. Anyone can level the ones hands alone can do, baking and pouring Drafts included, while cutting runes, firing tags, chanting and speaking a working want a woken Crystal.

A technique (a named, repeatable working with a stated cost) is a named thing done with a skill and a Trait. Taught techniques come from a school; derived techniques are found alone; a signature is one person's own art. A technique shows no level on the sheet. When it jumps, it takes a bigger name, and the old name stays in use for the smaller move. Fighters shout a technique's name as it fires: the shout costs a beat (the moment it takes, in which an opponent can act) and buys a surge of power.

A fighter's whole kit is one wild concept you can explain in a breath, knots or thunder or debt, with a growing list of loud named moves under it. They call those names in their own tongue, and anyone may reach for a word like World, God or Infinite. The room laughs until it works, and living up to the name becomes the rest of that person's life. Heralds cry the winners, minstrels get the details wrong, lords post the bounties and bookies take the odds, and all of it changes what you pay at an inn and who comes looking for you.

Every working (a deliberate act of magic) is written in Parun (the glyph grammar all magic uses) and set into something. Spoken, it happens now and the body pays. Cut into stone, steel or paper, it outlives its maker. Poured into a bottle, anyone can drink it, woken or not. Sung, it takes many voices and holds while they sing. These are ways of doing the work and the trades that grew up around them, open to anyone who will put in the years, though all but the pouring want a woken Crystal. The cutters' guilds still rank themselves highest and call speakers mouths, the pourers have words for the cutters, and the crowds cheer the fighters anyway. *Read next: [[The Magic System/The Soul and the Climb/Skills and Trait Levels|Skills and Trait Levels]], then [[The Magic System/Power in Use/The Working Trades|The Working Trades]].*

Most of the magic you will meet this week is small, and somebody is doing it with their hands for money. The hedge-worker at the fair will charm your ladder straight for two coppers. A ward-cutter will cut your doorstep against rats, and charge double if she dislikes your dog. Guild craftsmen shout their marks across the workshop and swear the contraption has never once gone off by itself. The lamp-wardens keep a street lit at a penny a lamp a night, and when they struck over the penny last winter all of Fish Row went to bed at sundown, with results the midwives are still remarking on. All of them can overcharge, and all of them can say no. *Read next: [[The Magic System/Practice and Institution/Who Pays for the Lamp The Living Crafts|Who Pays for the Lamp: The Living Crafts]].*

## One working, start to finish

Wen Talley's Primary Trait is heat, carried from birth and first shown when he was a forge-boy who held a burning rafter off his brother and lived. From it he learned a technique, Hot Iron, which drives that law into whatever his hand strikes. In a tavern brawl he shouts it, **"HOT IRON!"**, spoken and paid for in the body. His fist glows the smoky, clouded orange of a low-Stage aura; the man's jaw blisters where it lands, and the taproom smells of singed beard. The punch costs a sliver of his reserve, a few seconds of everything he has, and the heat runs back up his own arm, so after four or five he cannot close the hand. His soul has the report ready before the man hits the floor:

```
[Reserve: 96% ▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓░]
[Hit: right hand scorched by its own heat. Vitality 96%]
[Skill: Brawling, D to C]
```

That is magic: a law from the soul, a learned shape, a skill that carries it, a price in reserve, and a limit the body feels.

## What it all costs

Reserve spent is gone until it is pulled back, by rest, from the ground, at a Core, or out of a fight Draft. A registered card (the record an assay house or guild keeps on a practitioner) writes a working's drain as how long it could run from a full reserve. As the reserve falls, the body shows it: at half, the breath shortens and the aura dims; at a quarter, a tremor and a thinning color; below a tenth the practitioner starves, the aura flickers and the Trait stutters. Pushing past the Crystal's safe ceiling cracks it, and cracks that build up become permanent. Beyond reserve there is money, the oath sworn at the gate, and, in a Draft bought from the wrong source, someone else's convictions mixed into your own. A big art also charges a price out of its own idea, which is how a man whose whole law is rope comes to lose the skin of his hands to it. *Read next: [[The Magic System/Power in Use/Stacking and Spending|Stacking and Spending]], then [[The Magic System/Power in Use/Counterplay What Beats a Practitioner|Counterplay: What Beats a Practitioner]].*

## Stages, Levels and Grades

![[Assets/Diagrams/stage-ladder.svg]]

Each practitioner has eight main stats (Vitality for the body's endurance, Ardency for raw output, Gnosis for perception, Dexterity for speed, Harmonics for pulling power in, Resilience for withstanding it, Tempering for growth, Dominion for presence and authority). A Grade (a letter band on a stat's number: F, E, D, C, B, A, S and on to EX+) says how much force that stat delivers. When two people contest a thing, the Grade of the stat in use decides it. A one-Grade advantage wins that exchange; at two Grades the weaker side is overwhelmed; at three, their only aim is to survive. Inside the same Grade, skill, ground and the read decide. Your life spends most of your stat points by what you do with it, and a few are yours to place at each Stage.

Level counts how often the Crystal has grown denser, and it moves by danger overcome: killing something stronger moves it more than a hundred weak kills, and crews argue over who got the kill the way other people argue over a will. A Stage (one of sixteen steps of the soul's refinement, from Murmuring to Apex) is a full remaking of the Crystal. Tempering melts it down and rebuilds each layer, refines the Essence and makes it stronger, and the body [[The Magic System/The Soul and the Climb/The Trait System Law, Function and the Forge|gains Traits]], and every stat's ceiling rises with it. Levels climb inside a Stage; a Stage is crossed when two things are true: the practitioner has met its minimum power, and something has supplied its Catalyst (the push that opens that particular remaking). *Read next: [[The Magic System/The Soul and the Climb/The Sixteen Stages|The Sixteen Stages]], then [[The Magic System/The Soul and the Climb/Tier Grade, Bands & the Aether Shell|Tier Grade, Bands & the Aether Shell]].*

## Aether Class

From the Threshold into Stage VI, the remaking that brings a practitioner to Glory, the soul is offered a named class at its thresholds, one that fits how the person has lived. Ten years of holding a gate may bring Doorward; a life of running a crew's books under fire may bring Quartermaster. A class gives abilities, shapes growth and evolves at each tier by how it is lived, into forms some share with thousands and some hold alone. The one wild concept stays the person's own: the class is what the soul made of your life, and the concept is what you made of the class.

The tier of a class is the old ladder: Ø Dormant, I Muridic, II Harmonic, III Resonant, IV Luminous, V Radiant, VI Voidic, Ω Absolute. That tier also reads how cleanly power passes out of a person through the Shell, which decides how much of what they spend arrives as effect and how much roars off as heat and noise. A Muridic fighter loses about a third of every spend, and you will hear her coming. *Read next: [[The Magic System/The Soul and the Climb/Aether Classes|Aether Classes]], then [[The Magic System/The Soul and the Climb/Tier Grade, Bands & the Aether Shell|Tier Grade, Bands & the Aether Shell]].*

## How power grows

Practitioners train by pulling at Wellspring sites or on rich ground, by sparring and real fights, and by seclusion before a Threshold (the moment of remaking). The old roads to a Catalyst are crisis in a fight, seclusion, and tribulation, when a Wellspring's Aether tests the practitioner directly. A breakthrough is a visible transformation; a failed one leaves slag (a remaking that set wrong).

There is also a road with a price list. Drafts and blessings can supply a Catalyst, and Stages can simply be bought, so the wealthy are stronger because they are wealthy, and the great houses hoard the top Drafts to keep it that way. A purse can buy the Catalyst for any Stage, though at Zenith a Hollow Throne must still recognize the buyer, so up there money buys the attempt and not the seat. The poor rise by dungeon luck, guild patronage and blessings, and a commoner who has come up holding a monster core is the thing a great house fears most.

A bought Stage opens the door and no more. A release (below) must still be found and mastered by fighting, and sects teach what money cannot buy: releases, Domains, techniques, skills and the rites for their Wellspring. A rich heir at Stage IX may have no release at all while a poor fighter at the same Stage has all three, and the brackets sell a great many tickets on that difference. Most of this happens in schools, which run like football clubs, with colors, songs, grudges going back four generations and a fighting style you can spot at fifty paces, and you should think carefully before wearing the wrong sash into the wrong tavern. *Read next: [[The Magic System/Practice and Institution/Training and Sects|Training and Sects]], then [[The Magic System/The Soul and the Climb/The Ladder and the Draft — Temperance, Advancement and Essence Drafts|The Ladder and the Draft]].*

## Monsters, cores and dungeons

Monsters live and move across the whole of the Inner World, and plain beasts range more widely still. The Scourge of Hell, a wasteland in the middle of the continent, is the one area known for extremely dangerous ones, and a bad spring anywhere will put something on your road. A monster gains Levels by killing, its own kind included, and changes form at its own thresholds. It sits on the same Level and Grade scale as you do, and a strong enough perception can read it, while a weak one gets question marks:

```
[Read: Marsh Drake · Level ??? · Flux ?? · Threat T4]
```

Monsters are graded T1 to T9 on the same Threat Tier as the Accord's Commissions (its hunting contracts), and a famous one earns a name, an epithet and a bounty. Its hide, bone and scale make gear. Its core is what the crew came for. Stone and parts are read from the beast's own Grade and Level on the coil, and T stays the shorthand for danger and price, since most beasts line up with it.

A monster's [[Materials, Alchemy & Trade/Alchemy/Monster Cores|core]] is in a way its Soul Crystal, worth more the stronger the monster. An alchemist renders it into Essence Drafts used for tempering, at the risk of Identity Contamination (the beast's appetites coming along with its power). Swallowed raw, it gives everything at once, and most who try it die or come out wrong, and people keep trying it because the alternative is being poor. A Crystal cut from a dead practitioner makes the strongest Draft of all, which is the worst crime in every realm and the most profitable, and the reason [[Materials, Alchemy & Trade/Alchemy/The Standing Index|the alchemists' top class of stock]] (Index Class IV, no relation to Aether Class IV) has never balanced its books.

Most cores of the other kind, the Wellspring Cores, lie at the bottom of dungeons. A dungeon grows down in floors around its Core, more dangerous as you go, with something at the heart guarding it. Kill that and the place is cleared; leave it and it grows. It is alive and learning: it moves its galleries, breeds what kills crews best and pays attention to who keeps coming back, and scholars argue whether it thinks. *Read next: [[The Bestiary/Dungeons|Dungeons]], then [[The Bestiary/The Bestiary|The Bestiary]].*

## Reinforcement

A practitioner's stats live in their body as a passive base. Reinforcement (Essence driven through the body to push it further) adds an active layer for a cost in reserve. It shows as a coat of their own aura, thickest where they push. Gathering it into one fist leaves the rest of the body less protected. Asleep, drugged or taken by surprise, they keep the base and lose the coat.

The coat is the most famous art in the world, and nobody can agree what to call it. Common soldiers say wearing your color. Kharven riders call it the hide. The Moto say *matoi* (the wearing) and hold that a coat anyone can see is a coat badly worn; the Mahuo say *gabot* (armor) and hold that a coat nobody can see has never been hit. *Read next: [[The Magic System/Power in Use/Reinforcement|Reinforcement]].*

## Drafts, runes and tags

A Draft (a working poured into a substance and bottled) is how most of the world touches magic. Advancement Drafts bring a soul up to a Stage's minimum and help it set cleanly, and the best of them supply the Catalyst as well. Essence Drafts, most of them rendered from monster cores, feed the tempering itself. Fight Drafts are drunk mid-fight: each costs a beat, refills reserve, and leaves a crash. Each Draft is graded by the Stage it serves, the price climbs about threefold a Stage, and counterfeits are common. *Read next: [[The Magic System/The Soul and the Climb/The Ladder and the Draft — Temperance, Advancement and Essence Drafts|The Ladder and the Draft]].*

Cheap runed gear is everywhere; the great pieces are old and cannot be made again, and people kill for the ones that came off the battlefields of the Great Spirit War. Fighters carry prepared tags (short workings cut on paper, hide or foil, set off with a touch), and a few bond a weapon over years until it takes part in their release. Runes cut into a living body are a rare and dangerous art. *Read next: [[The Magic System/Power in Use/Runes and Tags|Runes and Tags]].*

## Gear: Grade and Rarity

An item carries two ratings. Grade says how much power it has, on the same letters as a fighter's stats. Rarity says how scarce it is: Common, Uncommon, Rare, Very Rare, Legendary, Mythic, Unique. The old words you will still see punched into metal (Marked, Proofed, Tempered, Instrument, Hallowed, Storied, Found, Numinous, Proscribed) survive as the Accord's stamps. An Appraisal skill and an assay instrument read an item, and a good forger beats a weak appraiser, which has paid for several very fine houses. Monster parts make the best gear going. A bonded piece grows with its owner, and everyone else's stays what it is.

A [[Materials, Alchemy & Trade/Alchemy/Master Works|Master Work]] does what its Grade and material should not be able to. It is made once, at the end of a lifetime, always Unique or Mythic, and named, and each area of the world and each trade has its own. A Draft can be one: a Fate Essence Draft is extremely hard to make, and there are people who would burn a city for a single dose. *Read next: [[Materials, Alchemy & Trade/Work, Trade and Measure/The Apparatus of the Age — Equipment and Material Culture/The Item Tiers|Grade and Rarity]].*

## The Guild Accord and its nine titles

The Guild Accord is a coalition of many kinds of guild across many nations: adventurers, merchants, craftsmen, alchemists, healers, couriers and more. Halls sign on for shared rank, enforceable contracts and passage, and the crowns tolerate it and fear it. Its nine titles are the same in every guild: Initiate, Apprentice, Journeyman, Adept, Expert, Master, Grandmaster, Archmaster, Paragon. A title grades trust in that trade. A merchant may deal up to it, and it caps the Rarity he may sell. A craftsman may make up to it, and it caps the Grade she may forge. An adventurer may take Commissions up to the same Threat Tier. Fighting guilds keep the Stage table as their yardstick, and a Grandmaster merchant may not be able to throw a punch. The title travels on a silver token, and forging one is a great crime, which tells you how many forgers there are. *Read next: [[The Magic System/Practice and Institution/The Standing and the Title — The Three Names|The Standing and the Title]], then [[Factions, Bloodlines & Institutions/Powers and Polities/The Guild Accord/The Guild Accord|The Guild Accord]].*

## Auras and Pressure

Every woken aura shows its own hue from the first Stage, clouded and smoky low down and clearing as the Stage rises. The Family (the branch of physics their power works: Caloria ember-orange for heat, Fulguria arc-blue for lightning, and six more) sets that hue, and it brightens as they push. A Wellspring adds texture and sound, and a class or a big Trait adds a shape. Pressure (the weight a stronger soul puts on a room) is felt first in the room, then in bodies: one Stage up bends attention, two brings sweat and nausea, three drops people where they stand. Only then does the window report it, as a line telling you what it has taken from you. A Grandmaster merchant with no fighting Stage has no Pressure at all, whatever his token says.

Pressure and sensing (reading another soul's strength without using your eyes) are shared arts as famous as the coat, and the arguments about them have ended friendships and at least one marriage. A tavern in the south says a man is leaning on the room; Kharven call it the low sky; the Moto call it *atsu*, the press; the lineage halls say *wēiyā*, awe pressing down, and teach it as manners. For sensing, Kharven ask if you can smell a man's stack, the Moto speak of *kehai*, the sign of someone there, and the Mahuo say *nunchi*, which their grandmothers also use for knowing when to leave the table. The law under the argument is short. A stronger Gnosis Perception reads a weaker Resilience Ward, and your window prints what it found. Everything else is an estimate said aloud, and people can hide it or fake it. *Read next: [[The Magic System/The Soul and the Climb/The Color of Essence|The Color of Essence]].*

## Releases and Domains

![[Assets/Diagrams/release-forms.svg]]

A release (a practitioner's Trait let off its safety limit on purpose, in a shape their Crystal has learned to hold) is a full transformation: the body changes, the aura flares, a sound comes, and power rises. The First Release opens no earlier than Stage IV, the Second at VII, and the Third at IX, and each must be found and mastered by fighting, whatever the Stage cost. A fighter may call one aloud for the full rise or go silent for a weaker one. Every release runs on a clock and ends on its own when the reserve runs low. When it is called the window fills, and a timer bar starts to drain:

```
[FIRST RELEASE: KILN SKIN]
[Blows carry forge heat. Heat workings strike at the top of their Grade.]
[Timer ▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓]
```

All of a fighter's own boosts together stack to two Grades over the sustainable Grade on their card, and only outside help goes past that. *Read next: [[The Magic System/The Soul and the Climb/Release Forms|Release Forms]].*

A Domain (a space where a practitioner's inner law outranks the world's) first shows as a seed: at the Threshold into Stage VII a person's own Trait may flicker out into one, once, with no release behind it. The Second Release later lets a seed be called and held, and from Stage VIII it stands as a true Domain, grown by living and called aloud, forming as it is named. Anyone at Stage VIII can have one, their own concept made real, and fame, rivalries and bounties follow it, though a person who bought the Stage must go and find that release first. The Stage grants a Domain and a Grade never does. Trait, release and Domain are one idea escalating: the idea, then the body transformed by it, then the world obeying it. When two fields meet they clash by Stage first, a field two Stages up overwriting the lower; otherwise Dominion holds the ground, and a sharp read can flip it. *Read next: [[The Magic System/The Soul and the Climb/Domains|Domains]].*

## Vows

A vow (a condition a person binds on themselves, spoken aloud) is small Fate, and it is anyone's to make, with no Stage, school or witness required. Spoken, it shows in the speaker's window with the bargain and its terms, and its price shows on the body at once. The narrower the condition and the heavier the forfeit, the more it buys, and what it buys counts toward the two-Grade cap. A broken vow lands at once by its own terms, and repeated denial of the truth behind it makes a Shear Break (a Trait turning on the bearer who denied it). Munahi, the twenty-ninth of February, is the day the law sleeps and Fate does not: no instrument binds, and a vow spoken on it still holds. *Read next: [[The Magic System/Practice and Institution/Vows and Oaths|Vows and Oaths]].*

## Fate and blessings

The Plane of Fate is the control of Attraction Force and Obsession Force and the workings of reality itself. Attraction Force is what makes a name, a vow or a bond take hold in the world; Obsession Force is the same current when it refuses to let go. To work Fate is to manipulate probabilities: to reach outside what reality already knows and control the fate, the predestiny, of something, and all spirits and all physical things have fates to control. It can attach to oaths and is more closely tied to blessings. Real Fate Arts are rare and mostly run in bloodlines, some high-Stage masters reach them late along the Fate Path, and most people only ever touch Fate from the outside, through a blessing or a Master Work Draft. They are considered incredibly overpowered, and people who hold them tend to be invited to things.

A vow and a blessing are the small uses. **People also fight and wage wars with Fate**, and it is the hardest thing anyone does. It is the law and construction of the world, the Archons and the great principles they embody hold sovereignty there as they do on the planes below, and nobody can reason their way into it, because reasoning is made of the thing it would be used against. Most of what a Fate fighter does in most fights is plain: the room is heavy before they arrive, their reach is long, and everyone sworn to them fights better. When one of them does pull, you see them set, the eyes gone still and the breath held on a count that is not the count of the exchange, and then your back foot comes down on the one wet stone and the blade is already there. It is an ordinary cut and an ordinary wound, and the one who moved it is bleeding from the nose and has perhaps half of what they walked in with. It often fails. Moving a whole field, weather, panic, the hour a column arrives, is a life's masterwork at high Stage or a bloodline's art, and it is why each side's first job in a war is to find the other's Fate users. It moves only what already has a way of happening, so a sound strap stays sound, and it can be contested, Dominion's Fate Sub-Stat pushing and the target's Resilience Persistence and Continuity pushing back. Another Fate user can lean the other way, a kit or a soul sound enough gives it nothing to move, and anyone who reaches the fighter before the result arrives has an ordinary body in front of them.

A blessing grants a power out of a person's natural reach, even in Level. It need not go through Fate, though most do. Higher spirits, Archons, Epochs and people of higher Temperance give them, because they are spiritually stronger, and a great giver spends almost nothing doing it. Lesser spirits and high-Stage people give most of the world's blessings; Epochs and Archons act rarely, and when they do it shakes the world. A blessing shows on the sheet as its own line, and that line can lie, because the giver wrote it:

```
[Blessing: The Road Remembers You]
```

A curse is often a blessing with terms you did not read, or from a giver who does not care what you want, and nobody can tell at first, and some are both. Those who hold Hataraki, the Moto's governing power, can take a blessing back or remove it, which is one of the reasons people are so afraid of them. *Read next: [[The Magic System/Practice and Institution/Blessings and Curses|Blessings and Curses]], then [[The Magic System/The Soul and the Climb/The Three Paths Routing, Recognition and the Gate|The Three Paths]].*

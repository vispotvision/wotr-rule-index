title: One Exchange, Annotated

On the last evening of the Brazen Lantern's ranking bouts, half of Lantern Street paid a copper a head to sit on the school's wall and howl, and the bookie Sukey Threadgold, who had sworn before witnesses to eat her slate if the Hare ever beat the Bellows, was seen next morning at the fish stalls gnawing politely at one corner of it. I make every first-year clerk in the Archive copy this bout out by hand. It takes about as long to read as it took to happen, if you are slow, and everything a newcomer needs is in it: what a fight looks like from the wall, and, marked with numbers, what was deciding it underneath.

*A single exchange from a school ranking bout, with the system shown working underneath it, start to finish. For the measures used here, see The Measures at a Glance.*

**The fighters.** Garrick Vane, the Bellows, because you can hear him coming, is at Stage VI, Glory (a Stage is one of the sixteen steps of the soul's remaking; Glory is the sixth). He works Caloria (the Family of heat) through the Wellspring Exuroth (the Trial Flame, the law of quench and temper; a Wellspring is one of the sixty types of Aether, each acting on the Soul Crystal in its own way). Nell Ashby, the Hare, a name meant as an insult, is one Stage below him at V, Splintering, and works Fulguria (the Family of lightning and current), harmonized to Judicium (the Wellspring of Truth, which sharpens the eye for flaws). One Stage is a gap a fighter can cross with a good read of the enemy, and only with one. The Lantern's house style, spottable at fifty paces, is to put everything in the right hand and make sure the neighbors hear about it. Garrick is the finest thing the school has ever produced by that method. Four minutes into the bout, he has been chasing her round the yard.

## The exchange

The yard of the Brazen Lantern school had been swept for the ranking bouts, and four minutes of Garrick Vane had unswept it. Black scorch-rings pocked the flagstones wherever his misses had landed, each one edged in straw and plum like a blade pulled from the fire, and the whole yard reeked of quench oil and hot slate. Up on the wall two hundred people, most of them disciples in Lantern yellow, were singing the one about the hare and the hot pan, which has nine verses and no merit. The sky behind them had gone the deep apricot of the end of a good day. Along the colonnade the lamp-warden's forty bronze lamps were burning, and every flame in the row canted toward him, a line of small bows, and Nell Ashby kept her eyes on his feet because they kept wanting to climb to his face. **[1]**

She had spent the bout a hand's breadth outside the maai (the distance at which a blow can land) of that right arm, slipping, giving ground, letting him barrel after her round the well-head while the wall jeered her for a hare. Let them jeer. Every rush cost him dear and cost her almost nothing, and now she could hear the bill in his breath, short and whistling, and staying short between rushes. The ember-orange that had roared off his shoulders like a bonfire in the first minute had sunk and thinned, as if someone had cupped a hand round the lamp. **[2]**

"You going to run all night, Ashby?" He spat, and the spittle struck the flag and jumped, *tss*, to steam. "Elder's bored."

"Elder's watching you sweat."

On the steps, Elder Hossack took the pipe from his teeth and gave one short snort through his nose.

Then Garrick gathered himself, and the gathering showed, as it had shown every day of his life. He sucked air through his nose until his chest stood out, and the Aether of the yard came to him with the breath, pulled up out of the flagstones and the warm evening and into his Soul Crystal, where it was refined to Essence and shoved out through a Shell so rough that she felt the waste of it on her face from three paces. The orange drained off his left flank in one sullen pulse and poured down his right arm, and the arm began to change the way a bar changes in the forge, cherry at the shoulder, straw at the elbow, plum across the wrist, and along the knuckles a thin hard edge of temper blue. His left side stood bare from armpit to belt, sallow in the lamplight, goose-fleshed, shining with sweat. **[3]**

Behind her ears a fine tick started up, *tik-tik-tik*, and hot copper came into her nose. Her eyes ached from the back. Two thin lines of color, one crimson and one green, lay down across his naked ribs, as straight as a surveyor's cords and visible to nobody in that yard but her. *He's put it all in the right. He'll throw it straight, because he's already loaded the back hip and a hook wants it square. When it misses, his weight rolls over the front foot, and for one breath those ribs are naked.*

"FURNACE HAMMER!" **[4]**

The wall roared it back at him, stamping, *Ham-mer! Ham-mer!*, and the song fell to pieces.

He drove off his rear foot. The heel came up and the knee turned in behind it, his hips slung round with every pound of him stacked on top, the shoulder came through last, and the right fist left his chin and came dead straight for her breastbone along a yard of empty air, with a bellows-roar behind it.

*WHOOOOOM.*

A good third of what he had put into that arm never reached the fist. It came off him as noise and glare and a wall of heat that dried her eyes in their sockets, and the air in front of his knuckles rippled like a road at high summer. Forty lamp flames bent flat. Under the roar ran the art's own voice, the sound a bar makes going into the trough, *tsssss-tink*, and her back teeth took it up, hot and then cold, an ache that climbed and then set hard. **[5]**

She had gone on the name, a beat before the fist left. She pushed off her lead foot to his left, low, knees bent, her head dropping inside the line of the arm, so quick that the wall saw two of her for an instant, a blue wraith still standing where she had been. The knuckles went by her left ear close enough to crisp the hair, and the heat took her sleeve off at the shoulder in a curl of black thread. The fist went on past her with nothing in the way and met the stone coping of the well-head.

*KRRAK-TSSSSSH.*

The coping was limestone, cold, wet from a hundred years of buckets. The fist put a forge's heat into a patch of it the size of a shield-boss in the time it took to touch, and the skin of the stone swelled against the cold stone underneath it, and the two of them tore apart. A slab as long as her forearm jumped off the rim. Shards spat across the yard and went hissing into the black water, a column of steam stood up out of the well as high as the colonnade roof, and round the broken edge the temper colors crawled from plum to blue as it cooled. "THAT'S YOUR BREASTBONE, HARE!" somebody screamed from the wall, with real affection. It was exactly that. Steel quenched too fast cracks for the same reason: the outside of a thing changes size before the inside has heard the news. Scholars call it thermal shock and smiths call it a ruined morning. Exuroth lives in that gap, and Garrick had just shown two hundred people what it would have done to the front of her chest.

His arm stood out to its full length, smoking. His hips had gone over with the weight of the blow and his whole body hung out over his front foot, left elbow trailing, and he could not haul them back in the beat she needed. She stood inside the arm at his left side, on dry flagstone, near enough to smell the burned hair on his wrist. **[6]**

She took one breath down to the belt, and the evening came in thin and dry and tasting of copper, Aether off the stones, refined in the Crystal in the time it takes to blink and driven out through the Shell. She gathered her own coat into her right fist. It ran down her arm arc-blue and spitting, with filaments of brighter blue crawling through the darker field where Splintering had cracked her, and threads of color fringed it, crimson, green, violet. The nearest lamps went blue at the tips. The hairs on Garrick's bare ribs stood up and leaned toward her knuckles, and the skin of her own arm prickled as if it had been rolled in nettles. The rest of her went to bare skin. **[7]**

"ARC NAIL!"

"She's got a *nail!*" a child shrieked from the wall, delighted, and that was the name of the thing explained for anyone who had not seen one: a single spike of current, driven into one place.

She jammed her rear heel against the lip of a flagstone, screwed her hip into it, and let the shoulder come after the hip, and the fist went a foot and a half in a flat straight line, all brawn and hip, along the crimson thread. Garrick saw it. His eyes came round and he clamped his left elbow down, and the elbow arrived a hand too high and one beat too late, and her first two knuckles went in under it, into his lower left ribs, with her whole weight behind them and the flagstone behind that.

*KRAK.*

Two ribs staved in under her hand, a wet green-stick crunch that jarred up to her shoulder, and in the same instant the Nail went home. *Tik-tik-tik-PING.* A crack like a carter's whip went out across the yard and all forty flames jumped together. The current found his sweat-wet skin wide open and went through it, and every sinew of his flank fired at once and stayed fired. A muscle knows one thing: a nerve tells it to clench with a small pulse of current. Flood it with a great one and every fiber clenches together, and none of them can let go until the flood stops. Physicians call it tetany. The sheet of muscle over his ribs locked like a board. The breathing muscle under them locked with it, and the breath stopped in his chest with his mouth still open to curse her. A red fern the size of her palm stood up on his skin around the knuckle marks, where the current had branched and burned. Under the broken ribs, where she could not see but the green thread could, something soft had begun to bleed. His right foot skidded a yard and left a smear on the stone, and that was the last of the force going out of him into the ground. He folded over her fist, one knee juddering down onto the flagstone, *dwomp*, his mouth agape on no air at all. **[8]**

Nell stepped back two paces to his blind side, clear of the right hand. Her knuckles had split white to the bone, and blood ran down between her fingers and pattered on the flag. Her arm hung numb to the elbow, fizzing with a thin crawl of sound she could hear inside the bone, and somewhere behind her breastbone a single note rang, a tapped glass, a warning she knew precisely. **[9]**

For one breath the whole wall had nothing to say. Then, quite small and clear, the voice of Sukey Threadgold: "Well, shit."

Garrick dragged in one wet, rattling breath, then a second, and lifted a palm off the stone.

Elder Hossack knocked his pipe out on the step in a little shower of sparks. "Ashby," he said, "you'll want a healer. So will he. Ninth place to the hare."

The wall came down off itself like a landslide.

## The annotations

The Archive's clerks write a bout down in a fixed order, and you have just read it. Every blow is told whole: the set of the body, what the other fighter read, what met what, what it did to the flesh, the sound and the blood, the reaction, and where both bodies stand after (a miss skips the flesh and the blood and tells what it struck instead). Every big working runs in its own order, woven through the blow that carries it: the charge anyone can see, the pull, the aura, what the room does, what the senses get, the mechanism, the effect and the cost. We are a dull profession, and this is why you can still read a fight sixty years after everyone in it is dead.

**[1] Pressure, before anyone moves.** Pressure (the weight a stronger soul lays on a room) is felt in the room first, then in bodies. One Stage up bends attention: the flames lean toward Garrick, and Nell's eyes keep drifting to his face. She pays nothing to bear it; it only costs her focus, and she spends that on watching his feet.

**[2] The cost, read off his body.** Garrick's card prices his spread coat (Essence driven through the whole body, lifting it within its Grade) at ten minutes from full: begun on a full reserve (the Essence he carries), it holds ten minutes before the reserve falls to a tenth. His single strikes cost a twentieth of his reserve each.

| Garrick's four minutes | Reserve spent |
|---|---|
| Spread coat, four minutes of ten | 36% |
| Two FURNACE HAMMERs that missed | 10% |
| Half a minute of focused coat in earlier flurries (five minutes from full) | 9% |
| **Left** | **about 45%** |

That puts him on the first rung of the tell ladder (the body's fixed signs of a falling reserve): at half, the breath shortens and stays short, and the aura dims. Nell, who spent most of the bout dodging at her passive base (her stats as they sit in the body, costing nothing), is still above 85%. Making him chase was the plan: outlasting is a counter, and she is running a cheaper clock.

**[3] Reinforcement focus, and the gap it left.**
- *Intention:* win the bout in one blow.
- *Focus:* he gathers the whole coat into his right forearm and fist. A focused coat lifts one place a full Grade (a letter band on a stat's number, F through EX+) and drops everywhere else to the passive base.
- *The gap:* his left flank is bare. His Fortitude (how well bone and joint take a blow) stands there at its sheet value, 240, B, with no coat over it.
- *Why she can see it:* his Feint (the skill of hiding a movement, its noise and its aura spill) is 130, C. Her Perception (sensing threat and reading what is coming) is 190, B. One full Grade up wins a contest outright, so he cannot hide where his coat went from her. Judicium sharpens the same eye. The ticking, the copper and the colored lines are that Wellspring's own signature, which a trained reader learns to know like a neighbor's cough, and the price of a read this size is the ache behind her eyes.
- *Her thought:* she reasons from what she has seen of him today, not from his registered card. Fighters think a fight through at length, and the best of them are right.

**[4] The call.** Shouting a technique's name as it fires buys a surge within its Grade for the beat the shout takes. It is a layer on the stack (see [7]) and it is also the loudest tell in the fight. FURNACE HAMMER's own card gives its counter: the call costs a beat, the coat visibly drains from the off side as it gathers, and the hips commit so far that they cannot turn back for a beat. Everyone in Lantern Street knows all three, and he calls it anyway, because a Lantern fighter who wins quietly has wasted the evening.

**[5] Effect, and the waste.**
- *Effect:* Exuroth runs on thermal shock. The fist is flash-heated as it travels, so on contact it delivers a blow and a sudden surface heat together, the way a quenched blade cracks if it is cooled too fast.
- *Signature:* the temper colors climbing his arm, the *tsssss-tink*, the quench oil and the aching teeth belong to Exuroth, and any Exuroth working carries that smell and that ache. The sound word belongs to the technique alone, and it comes back every time he fires it, so that by the third time the wall is making it with him.
- *Stack:* focused coat (one Grade) plus the call (a surge in the band). His Flux (the force a working carries), 352, A, strikes as S in that fist. Two layers at once raise the risk of a Crystal Fracture Event (a crack in the Soul Crystal, the organ that stores and sends out power), and he is inside the hard cap of two Grades over what he can sustain.
- *Waste:* Garrick is Aether Class I, Muridic (his Shell, the Crystal's outer skin, passes power only roughly), which sets his η (efficiency, the share of each spend that arrives as effect) at 0.62. Thirty-eight of every hundred he spends leave him as heat, roar and light. Waste is loud and hot; mastery is quiet. Her drying eyes and the scorched yard are his η, made visible.
- *The contest she refuses:* his focused fist at S against her Fortitude at B is two Grades: one-sided. Taking it on a guard would end her. Nothing she owns wins that exchange, so she makes sure it never happens.

**[6] The counter.** Her Reflex (what moves the body before the mind has finished) is 230, B. His Celerity (how fast the body travels) is 210, B. Same Grade: inside one Grade, skill, ground and the read decide. She leaves the speed contest tied and moves on the call, a beat before the fist leaves, because the beat was the counter his card offered. The afterimage is what the eye keeps when a body moves faster than it can follow. The spalled coping shows what the blow would have done to her sternum.

**[7] Her stack, and her own gap.**
- *Focus:* she gathers her coat into the right fist. Her Flux, 250, B, is lifted one Grade, to A, in that fist alone. Everything else on her drops to the passive base. For the length of one punch, she is as open as he was.
- *Stack:* focused coat plus the ARC NAIL call. Two layers, inside her cap (two Grades over her sustainable B), and the same fracture risk his carried. Her color shows her Stage: Splintering Essence is fracture-streaked, hairlines of the Family's true color flaring through a darker field.
- *Timing:* the call costs her a beat too. She spends it in the beat his hips cannot use.

**[8] What decided it.**
- *The stat in use:* her blow tests her Flux against his Fortitude. Her Flux, focused, is A. His Fortitude, uncoated, is B. One full Grade up wins the exchange outright, and it did. Penetration (the stat for piercing wards and coats) never came into it, because she struck where there was no coat to pierce.
- *Why the lower Stage won:* the Stage gap set the Pressure and his higher ceilings, and he never got to use them. The read chose which stats met. She refused the contest she would lose by two Grades (his fist against her chest), drew the one she would tie (speed), and forced the one she would win by one (her fist against his bare ribs).
- *The three layers of the working (Aether, Wellspring, Essence):* the yard sits on ordinary ground at ordinary density, a long way from any Core, in dry dusk air, and his skin was sweat-wet, all of which Fulguria loves. Neither fighter got anything from the ground, though a Measurewright in the Lantern Street taverns argued until closing that the well lifted the yard a hair and the bout should be read again. The physics is electrical injury: a current through muscle makes it contract and hold (tetany), so his flank and the breathing muscles under it locked. Dry skin fights a current and wet skin lets it in, which is why she wanted him sweating and why four minutes of chasing was worth more to her than the punch. The glyph she wrote the working in is its boundary (the condition that tells a law where to stop), and it held the current to his flank, which is why Garrick Vane is alive to keep his left side covered. The Essence was hers, sent through an unclassed Shell (Stages I to V read no Aether Class) at the Expert's typical η, about 0.64.
- *The wound:* two lower left ribs broken, with the spleen beneath them. A healer is needed tonight, not tomorrow.

**[9] Her price.**
- *Reserve:* ARC NAIL and a second of focused coat cost her about 7%. She ends near 80%, with no rung of the tell ladder reached. He ends near 39%.
- *The body:* split knuckles, and a numb, fizzing arm where her own current ran back up it.
- *The Crystal:* the ringing glass is the warning a stacked Crystal gives. A Splintering Crystal is already a cracked one, and two layers on it is the gamble veterans take. If it had cracked, she would carry the fracture until rest, alchemy or her next Stage healed it.
- *Afterwards:* she won across a Stage with a read, at a visible price, and the whole school watched how. By morning the minstrels had it that she broke four ribs and he wept. Next month Garrick will keep his left side covered, and she will need something new.

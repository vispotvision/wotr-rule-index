# Fracture of Worlds — The Living System

*The System does not reward survival. It rewards contact with things larger than you.*

*Every scar the Soul Crystal carries is a record of how far you reached beyond what you were.*

The most expensive sentence ever spoken at the Haymarket Fair in Altherion cost forty silver marks, the good name of a councilman's son and most of a pie stall. Aldous Thwaite had hired a Measurewright (a Guild scholar who assays souls for a fee) to read his Soul Crystal from a scarlet-draped stage, with trumpets. The man set a brass assay fork against Thwaite's breastbone, listened to it sing, and then, the fork still ringing and himself an honest fool, turned it toward the groom holding the horse. "Your groom reads higher, young sir." Thwaite called the groom out on the spot, and the groom hit him once, apologizing, and six hundred people watched a gold-stitched coat leave through the pies. What follows is the ledger that fork was reading. Measurewrights who read it aloud to paying customers learn to stand near a door.

## What the System Is

The Fracture of Worlds progression framework is the metaphysical skeleton beneath every character in War of the Realms. It is not a game overlay imposed on the world from outside. It is a natural consequence of how the Continuum observes, measures, and responds to the maturation of living souls. When a practitioner grows, the Continuum recognizes it. When they stagnate, the Continuum applies pressure. When they fracture, the Continuum waits to see what re-forms in the break.

The System tracks eight dimensions of a soul's development, each called a Primary Stat, and beneath each of those, eight finer measurements called Sub-Stats. These numbers are not just mechanical data. They are the Continuum's honest accounting of who you actually are, not who you claim to be.

Level is simply the count of how many times your Soul Crystal (literally the soul itself, where a practitioner's power is held and shaped) has meaningfully densified. It is not a measure of age. It is not a measure of training hours. It is the sediment of genuine contact with consequence, counted, compressed, and expressed as a number. Level moves by danger overcome, so killing something stronger moves it more than a hundred weak kills, and crews argue over who got the kill.

The ceiling is 500 for everyone below Stage XV. Paragons (practitioners at Stages XV and XVI) exceed it, and the Codex does not number what they reach.

## The Window

Everyone carries a private copy of this ledger, the unwoken included: a baker has a Baking skill and a little sheet. The Soul Crystal is the soul and the Continuum measures it, so the soul reports its numbers to its owner in their own tongue, and nobody else can see the report. That report is the window. It comes up often and in detail, in a fight as readily as on a quiet morning, for damage, for skill gains and for reserve, and it sits in the owner's sight as a plain block of text: `[Reserve: 41%]`. It shows Grades, percentages, bars and levels. The EU, AU/s and η of the later Parts stay out of it and live on a Measurewright's slate. The groom at the Haymarket had been reading his own every morning for years, and a normal look at it went something like this:

```
[Level 58 · Stage IV, Flourishing]
[Race: Human · Lineage: none of note]
[Vitality D · Ardency E · Gnosis F · Dexterity D]
[Harmonics F · Resilience F · Tempering F · Dominion F]
[Reserve: 100% ██████████]
[Skills: Horse-handling B · Short Right Hand D]
[Blessings: none]
```

A normal look shows the Eight Primaries with their Grades. A Sub-Stat surfaces when it is used or when it moves, and the full set stays there for anyone with the patience to study it. Race and lineage show, and they shape base stats and what comes easily. Skills rise with use and show as Grades; techniques are named things done with them, show no level, and take a bigger name when they jump. Traits have levels of their own. A blessing sits on its own line, and since the giver wrote that line, it can lie. To read anyone else you need an assay fork or a Gnosis Perception stronger than their Resilience Ward; everything short of that is an estimate said aloud, and people can hide it or fake it, which keeps the Measurewrights in pie.

## The Short Version

Ringside, nobody says any of this. They ask what letter a fighter is and what he is good for, and the Measurewright in the judges' box winces and writes down the real terms, which are these.

**Level cap 500**, in five Bands of one hundred. Band I Mortal Foundation (Stages I–IV). Band II Awakened (V–VII). Band III Sovereign (VIII–X). Band IV Mythic (XI–XII). Band V Absolute (XIII–XIV), with XV Revelation and XVI Apex beyond canonical mortal progression, where Level keeps rising past 500 with no ceiling and no sixth Band.

**Tier Grade** is a fixed threshold on the raw Sub-Stat value, universal across every stat: Hollow 1–10, F 11–25, E 26–50, D 51–100, C 101–175, B 176–275, A 276–400, S 401–550, SS 551–725, SSS 726–950, X 951–1,200, EX 1,201–1,500, EX+ 1,501 and above. A Primary is the combined total of its eight Sub-Stats and its Grade is read off the mean; Dominion counts seven, since Throne stands outside it.

**The Nine Tiers of Standing** are the Guild Accord's nine titles: Initiate, Apprentice, Journeyman, Adept, Expert, Master, Grandmaster, Archmaster, Paragon. They are the same nine in every guild and they grade trust in that trade: a merchant may deal up to his title, a craftsman make up to hers, an adventurer take commissions up to the same Threat Tier. Fighting guilds keep the Stage table as their yardstick, which is why these Parts carry the column, and a Grandmaster merchant may still be unable to throw a punch. On a fighting roll Hollow and F sit below Initiate and confer no rank, because the Accord does not register what it cannot examine.

**Temperance Stage caps the value.** A character cannot allocate past a ceiling their Crystal cannot structurally sustain. A transient boost passes that ceiling lawfully inside the two-Grade cap, read from the sustainable Grade on the sheet; only a stat forced there to stay produces Crystal Fracture Events at an exponentially increasing rate. A Threshold is lawful ground to cross, and a Draft or a blessing can supply the Catalyst for it, so a Stage can simply be bought. A bought Stage opens the door and no more: a Release must still be found and mastered by fighting.

**Aether Class** is a named class the soul is offered at thresholds, the first of them at Glory, Stage VI, fitting how the person has lived. The old ladder, Ø Dormant through Ω Absolute, is its tier, and from that first offer the tier sets η. Below Glory the Stage's typical figure serves the unclassed.

**A Wellspring** is an alchemical and a spiritual transformation, the governing idea that transforms how a soul uses Aether: a lens on magic already potent. It is got at a Core in a dungeon, from a school, from a Draft or blessing, or by birth.

**The Plane of Fate** is the control of Attraction Force and Obsession Force and the workings of reality itself. Real Fate Arts are rare and mostly by bloodline, and most people only touch it through a blessing.

**Eight Primary Stats:** Vitality, Ardency, Gnosis, Dexterity, Harmonics, Resilience, Tempering, Dominion. Eight Sub-Stats each, sixty-four in total.

*The Crystal does not lie. It only counts what you actually are. The line a blessing's giver wrote on it is another matter.*

# Counterplay: What Beats a Practitioner

> Leave the working alone. Choose which of the four things you are taking away from him, and count how long you have before he notices which one you chose.

Every washing-house north of the Reach sings about Thorvald Eikhagen of the Red Anvil school, and every one of them has the soap wrong. Eikhagen stood at Stage IX (ninth of the soul's sixteen steps, high enough that weather takes an interest), and that spring he had walked through the whole Blackwater levy at a stroll, four hundred spears, *krak, krak, krak*, with his aura standing a hand high off his shoulders in the deep ochre of a quarry at sunset, while the bookies at Skarrow stopped taking money against him because nobody could be found to hold the other end of the bet. Then he went to the Three Herons bathhouse to soak. Behind the towel heap sat Wenna Sprat, a laundress of sixty-one with a bad hip and two sons who had been in that levy, and across her knees lay a secondhand crossbow that had cost her eight silver, spanned with a goat's-foot lever an hour before, because she could not trust her back to do it in a hurry. Nobody reinforces in the tub. He was humming.

*Tk.*

***THUNK.***

The bolt went in at the hollow of the throat and stopped against the neck bone. He stood up, and that part the song has right: the water ran arterial red to the rim of the tub, the ochre came roaring up over him in the one beat a coat takes to rebuild, around a bolt already in, and he crossed four steps of wet tile with the whole house shaking and the steam going gold around him. He got as far as the towels. The song gives him a vow there. The bath-boy, who was under a bench, gives him the word "Who?" He was dead by the ninth breath. The Red Anvil posted two thousand gold on her head, and it was never collected, because every washerwoman in Skarrow went before the bailiffs that month and swore she was Wenna Sprat.

Four hundred spears, then, and eight silver. I keep this register for the Guild as a Measurewright (one of the Guild's sworn measurers of Aether, and of anything else that holds still), and I have assayed that corpse's records at length. My colleague at Altherion maintains that Eikhagen's reaction speed would have slipped the bolt with room to spare. He is correct, and he has spent thirty years failing to see why it does not matter. The man did not see the loose.

### The Four Routes

Everything in this register reduces to one of four. **Deny the field. Break the boundary. Break the body. Break the man.** They cost different amounts, they take different lengths of time, and a competent commander picks by what is available rather than by preference.

---

## I. Deny the Field

*Take away what he pulls from. The working is correct and produces nothing.*

**Take him off rich ground.** A practitioner pulls Aether out of the environment around him, refines it in the Soul Crystal (the organ in the soul that stores and spends power) into Essence (the stuff of a soul's power), and drives it out through the Shell (the Crystal's outer layer), so where he stands matters: rich ground fills him faster and thin ground starves him. Wherever there is a yard to choose, this is the first move and the cheapest, and it is settled with purses as often as with swords. Fight him on **salted ground**, worked the night before by hedge-workers (the hired hands of the petty arts, paid by the job) so that Aether will not pool on it, on **bled ground**, pulled empty by a long fight or a greedy crew, or on plain thin ground, and he has what he walked in with. A worked Wellspring site, which common speech calls a Core, recharges and has keepers, and a keeper with the right seal can close it in under an hour, if he has been paid, if he has not been paid more by the other side, and if he has not simply refused. The practitioner is not weakened; the room is. **Every serious action inside a Concord city begins with somebody deciding what ground it will happen on, and that decision is made by people who will not be present at the fighting.**

**Choose the ground by Family** (the eight houses of magic, sorted by physics). Terrain is a modifier on efficiency and the modifiers are large. Caloria fails in thermal equilibrium, saturated cold, and oxygen-poor air. Fulguria fails against boiled leather, fired clay, dense fog and a shielded interior. Materia has opinions and no tools on open water. Fluxia cannot act without a medium, so a dry vault is a Fluxia prison. **The counter-Fulguria kit has been leather and ceramic for four centuries and everyone knows it, and it stays cheaper to ignore until somebody dies of it.** Limina needs a gradient, and saturated ground has none left to offer. A crowd is noise. Light him, and fill the room with people. Spatium needs a survey it can trust to say where *here* is, so unmapped ground, warp-scarred ground and ground two Domains (regions where the world runs on one practitioner's terms) are arguing over all leave the transform nothing to solve against. Vectoria needs something to push back. Loose sediment, scree, mud, deep water and free fall take the third law away from him. Vitalia is rate-limited chemistry. Rates roughly halve for every ten degrees you take out of the air, so **winter, bare stone and salted earth are a Vitalia sentence.**

**Saturate it.** Flood the area with Essence (the stuff of a soul's power) faster than it disperses and everyone present takes Fracture (damage to the Soul Crystal itself) risk, **including whoever did it.** A weapon of last resort and a real one, and its practical use is the threat rather than the act. The threshold is a density and not a total: ten thousand EU (Essence Units) in each cubic meter, or a hundred AU/s (Aether Units per second, a rate of output) through each square meter of the region's edge. It grows with the surface while output does not, so **you cannot flood a field, only a building, and you have to be inside it.** A Zenith working a kilometer wide is a five-hundredth of threshold. The same output in a hall is five times over it.

**Shrink his authority.** The radius in which a practitioner's own law is the operating law is his field standing above the ground's, and the ground wins on a square root. Addressing range, anchor reach and authority radius all fall as the root of the local density. Fight him on concentrated ground and all three drop to an eighteenth. At a Veil-Thin Nexus, a thousandth. Inside a Wellspring Core (the site where a Wellspring's Aether pools and its law runs strongest) the strongest Domain in the register is a fraction of a millimeter wide, because there the only law operating is the one the Wellspring carries. **This is why a commander who cannot starve him takes the fight to the vein instead.** It runs the other way too: Aether-thin ground gives him ten times the reach and almost nothing to pull, which is the whole of the Limina bargain.

**Silence.** The removal of magical possibility from a region, a stranger thing than any shield or counterspell. Lawful transformation cannot be authorized at all inside one. Animatria constructs fail completely because their continuity runs back to a source the Silence has severed. **A soul held in one reports the sensation as loneliness rather than weakness**, and that distinction is the most reliable field diagnostic for whether a quiet place is Silence or merely thin.

## II. Break the Boundary

*Attack the sentence and leave the speaker for later.*

**Interrupt the chain.** Below Refraction (Stage VII) a working requires an external anchor: voice, hand, ink or blood. Take the anchor and the sentence does not complete. **Break his wrist and a gestured chain is finished. Put a hand over his mouth and a spoken one is.** Above Refraction the chain is internal and none of this applies, which is the practical reason Stage VII is the line every soldier is taught to fear rather than the reason the Codex (the Accord's master register of laws) gives.

**Force the Seal early.** The Seal closes an operation against the warrant that opened it, and a warrant overstating standing seals anyway, **with the difference deducted from the speaker at the moment of closing.** A third party who can compel an early close has stopped defending and started billing him.

**Knot and Lock.** The only two Counter forms in the index. Laws cannot be opposed, so these two condition the conditioning. **Knot** entangles a path so it cannot be traced cleanly. **Lock** occupies a limit so no other limit can be set there. Every counter-working ever recorded is one of those two moves.

**Wards.** Runecraft standing on a substrate, and therefore beaten the way Runecraft is beaten: **damage the substrate.** Nobody argues with a ward. It is chiseled off, or the wall it sits on is brought down, or the third layer is scraped through by somebody who knows which layer to scrape. *An inscription with a defective Sealing role has been failing continuously for two centuries, and whoever owns it owns a liability.*

**Countersigning.** Some Seals end when countersigned in writing, and some end when the sum is spoken back correctly by another. **A working that carries either of those closing conditions can be ended by an opponent who is literate enough**, which is why Accord field practice avoids both and why they persist in the old inscriptions everywhere.

## III. Break the Body

*The Crystal is inside a person and the person is flesh: harder than common flesh, never proof. Taken asleep, drugged or by surprise, he keeps only the base his stats give the body and loses the coat of reinforcement he pushes over it, which is why ambush is the oldest answer on this list.*

**The bolt.** Cheap, mechanically simple, and it does not care about Stage below the point where reaction speed outruns it. A crossbow costs about what a trooper's saber costs, a child can be taught the lever in an afternoon, and it is the cheap thing that can kill a high-Stage fighter in a room. It is read by Speed Grade like any projectile. A D-Grade (a low rank on the F to EX+ scale of raw strength) practitioner can read a crossbow bolt loosed across a room and slip it, if they see the loose. At B-Grade they can stand in a volley of bolts and arrows without significant reactive disadvantage. Everything below that line is arithmetic, and everything above it is still a body in a tub. **A volley is a volley.** This is the entire reason armor is still worth a lord's rents and the entire reason ranks still stand in them. Bows, thrown things and siege engines belong on this line too, at their own speeds and their own prices.

**Numbers, correctly used.** One practitioner beats twenty men. Twenty men who have been drilled to spread, to make him choose, and to keep a lane open for the crossbows at all times will take casualties and kill him, **and the doctrine that produces this is a hundred and forty years old and taught badly everywhere.**

**Distance.** Fulguria falls off with the square. Vectoria has to close. Corporis (magic worked through the fighter's own body) is a body and a body has reach. **Most practitioners are a threat about closing range and are written as though they were not.**

**Exhaustion.** Below ten percent reserve a practitioner suffers Essence Starvation: reduced stat expression, Shell degradation, Traits flickering. **A defender who can afford to spend nothing wins a long engagement against an attacker who cannot afford to spend nothing**, and sieges are decided by this more often than by any working.

**Overfill him.** A practitioner who absorbs banks the load in his own Crystal, and what bounds the bank is its rated Flux Density (how densely Essence packs into the Crystal) rather than his reserve. To half again over rating he holds it and bleeds. Past half again the lattice takes a permanent set and he is Overgrown. At two and a quarter times rating it bursts, and the Fracture is his, from energy you handed him. **Feeding an absorber is cheaper than out-hitting him.** And the bank is short. It leaks at one minus his efficiency per second, three seconds for a poor Crystal and twenty for a fine one, so a defender who declines to be hit for ten seconds has taken it off him without touching him. The bleed is the tell, and it lasts exactly as long as the bank does.

**Armor tiers.** Padded takes bruising and not gore. Mail is immune to cuts, vulnerable to thrusts and blunt trauma, and vibrates under Essence shock in a way the wearer feels in the teeth. Brigandine hides its plates and the gaps are the openings. Proofed plate turns a bolt at range and turns most edges entirely, leaving joints, grapples and the close end of a room. **Ceramic and shard are sacrificial and fail on the second hit in the same place.**

## IV. Break the Man

*The most reliable and the least discussed.*

**Read him.** Taught techniques are documented and counterable by anyone who studied. Self-derived techniques must be read live, in the two or three exchanges before they kill you. **Every thaumaturge** (a practitioner whose Crystal acts before conscious thought) **has a pattern**, because single-axis fluency produces one, and a pattern learned is a pattern interruptible.

**Make him hold Pressure** (the felt weight of his power on everyone near him) **in.** Suppression is a skill and it is tiring. A practitioner who cannot afford to be identified is paying continuously for the privilege, and a long approach through populated ground costs him more than the fight will.

**Rigidity.** A Trait's law is not negotiable by its bearer, and the more absolute it is the less he can contradict it. **Present him with the situation his own law handles worst and he will handle it that way regardless**, because he has no other way available. This is the cruelest counter in the register and it requires knowing the man rather than the technique.

**The vow.** A vow kept buys Coherence (the match between a practitioner's stated law and his conduct) with conduct. **A vow broken is a Shear Break** (a Trait turning on the bearer who denied it), and an opponent who knows the terms of a vow holds a weapon that does not require touching him. *Maneuvering a sworn man into breaking his own oath is legal everywhere, universally despised, and has ended more careers than any duel.*

**Coherence drift.** A practitioner whose lived behavior has drifted from his stated law is degrading before anyone lays a hand on him: output falls, Thresholds refuse to authorize, and his Traits begin to argue with him. **Nobody has to cause this. Somebody has to notice it and wait.**

## What Does Not Work

**Matching him.** Two practitioners at the same Grade resolve by technique, positioning and Sub-Stat (the finer stats beneath each main stat) architecture, and the exchange is a coin toss dressed as a plan. *A commander who answers a practitioner with a practitioner has declined to make a decision.*

**Shielding.** Nothing in the register stops a working by standing in front of it. Armor changes what the working does to a body. Wards condition what may be authorized in a space. **Neither is a wall and both are written as one constantly.**

**Waiting for the cost.** Costs are real and they are rarely due inside a scene. *A plan that consists of surviving until he tires is a plan to be killed by a tired man.*

> **The doctrine, stated once.** A practitioner is beaten by taking away one of four things: the field, the sentence, the body, or the man. **Pick before contact.** Everything that goes wrong in the record went wrong because somebody tried to decide during.

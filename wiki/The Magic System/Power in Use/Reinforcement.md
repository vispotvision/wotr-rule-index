title: Reinforcement
---

*How a practitioner's body holds its power, and how they push it further. For the whole system in brief, see [[The Magic System/First Steps/How Magic Works|How Magic Works]].*

> *"Gather it where the blow is. Then pray there was only the one."* Copybook line, set for novices in most lineage halls and ignored by all of them.

The minstrels call it *The Bell and the Fork*, it runs to forty verses, and about nine of them happened. At the Bell Feast in Ninefold Market, Gao Baoshan of the Nine-Ladle Hall, a cook by trade and a Stage VI (sixth on the soul's sixteen-step ladder) by every account, bet his master's broth recipe against a rival hall that he could take the Evening Bell of the Temple of Clear Water on his forehead, dropped from its own tower. The whole of Ninefold Market came, and the roofs sold out. He gathered everything. Every scrap of the ochre aura on his body ran up his neck and heaped on his brow, until he wore a lump of amber light the size of a goose egg and the rest of him stood bare as a plucked bird, and the riggers knocked out the pin.

The bell cracked down the lip. Baoshan stood under it with his eyes crossed and his sandals driven a thumb's depth into the flagstones, the lanterns on the temple steps all swung one way, and the crowd lost its collective mind. Then Sister Ran of the Copper Steamer, who had money on the other side, stepped up behind him and put a coated toasting fork into his left buttock up to the handle, on the sound legal ground that the wager had specified a bell. He bled through three aprons. The Nine-Ladle Hall lost the recipe, its broth has been worse ever since, and Baoshan ate his dinners standing for a season.

Every fighting school I have visited tells this story on the first day, and they are right to. What he did to the bell is a focused coat. What she did to him is the price of one. A colleague of mine in the Measurewrights (the Guild's sworn measurers) holds that the bell was already cracked, and he has written thirty pages on the subject, and he has never stood under anything.

## What it is

A practitioner's stats (the measured strengths of body and soul, read off the Soul Crystal, which is the soul itself, the thing that stores and sends out their power) live in the body all the time as a **passive base**: always on, costing nothing. **Reinforcement** is the active layer on top, Essence (the soul's own substance, pulled in as Aether from around them and refined in the Crystal) driven through flesh, bone and whatever they hold, to push the body further for a price in reserve (the Essence they carry).

| Layer | What it does | What it costs | When it is lost |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Passive base** | The body at its sheet values: a B-Grade Durability (B, a letter band of force) takes a blow as B, waking or sleeping | Nothing | Sags under Essence Starvation (the state below a tenth of reserve) |
| **Spread coat** | The whole body lifted within its Grade, toward the top of the band | About ten minutes of holding from a full reserve, at Adept standing (the fourth title, which in a fighting guild is read off Stage) | Asleep, drugged, surprised, or starving |
| **Focused coat** | One place (a fist, a forearm, the ribs) lifted one full Grade; everywhere else drops to the passive base | About five minutes from full | As above, and wherever the focus is not |

## How it looks

**A coat of their own aura.** Reinforcement shows as a skin of the practitioner's own color: the hue of their Wellspring's Family (the branch of physics it answers to, each with its color) at the clarity of their Stage (their step on the soul's sixteen-step ladder): clouded and smoky low down, clearing as they climb. A Caloria (heat) fighter's coat shimmers ember-orange; a Fulguria (lightning) fighter's crackles arc-blue along the forearms; a Vectoria (force) fighter's shows as nothing but a weight the dust leans toward. It is thickest where they push. The Family sets that base color, a Wellspring (the governing idea that transforms how a soul uses Aether) adds texture and sound, and a Class (the name the soul offers at thresholds) or a big Trait adds a shape, so that two Caloria fighters may share a color and nothing else: one coat crawling like coals, the other standing off the shoulders like a mantle.

**The body under it.** Veins stand up, skin tightens and takes a sheen, the stance settles lower, and the breath deepens. Heat comes off a hard-pushed coat in waves.

**Focus leaves gaps.** Gathered into one place, the coat blazes there and goes pale everywhere else. A fist burns; the far flank fades to bare skin. Reading where it went is half the fight, and a good fighter moves the focus faster than an enemy can read it.

**Everyone has a word for it, and an argument.** The coat is the most famous art in the world, and each people names it to suit their opinion of it. A Moto calls it *matoi*, the wearing, and holds that a person of breeding does not show it in company, which is why Moto courts are so full of calm people who may or may not be about to kill you. The Mahuo call it *gabot*, armor, drill it like armor in ranks at dawn, and say a coat you cannot see is a coat you have not paid for. The lineage halls call it *hùtǐ*, guarding the body, and have been fighting for six hundred years over which hall thought of it first. A Marrowkin is *on shift* when it is up, and enters the minutes. A Kharven soldier calls it the hide and compares prices. In the bracket crowds of the Accord towns it is your shine, as in *he's lost his shine, get your money down*. All of them mean the table above, and any of them will explain at length why the others do it wrong.

**The same coat on every road.** Anyone woken can wear one, and it lifts the same stats on all three [[The Magic System/The Soul and the Climb/The Three Paths Routing, Recognition and the Gate|Paths]]. What differs is what the Path pays on top: a Body Path fighter's fist and ribs take the full force bonus, a Spirit Path fighter gets nothing extra by hand and next to nothing on the ribs, and a Fate Path fighter takes the smallest share.

**Held gear, never other people.** The coat runs into what the hand is holding: a blade, a shield, a staff, the hilt of a hammer. Let go and it leaves the steel within a breath. It cannot be laid over another person, however close or however loved.

**Masters can hide it.** Most practitioners must choose: hold their Pressure (the weight their presence puts on a room) in, which is tiring work, or coat up and show their color. A master can reinforce with no color showing at all, and that calm is a warning in itself. Few manage it below Master standing, and the bookies shorten their odds the moment one walks in unlit. The other direction is just as old: a fighter short on reserve can flare a coat bigger than the body behind it, which is why anything but a true read (a stronger Gnosis Perception against a weaker Resilience Ward) is an estimate said aloud.

## What it costs

- **Reserve, by the second.** A card writes it as time from full: how long the coat holds, started on a full reserve, before the reserve reaches its tenth. A spread coat runs about ten minutes for an Adept, longer as their efficiency climbs; a focused coat runs about half that. The practitioner's own window (the soul reporting its numbers to its owner, unseen by anyone else) shows the running figure as a percentage and a bar, and nobody else in the fight gets to see it.
- **A layer on the stack.** A focused coat's one-Grade lift is a power-up like any other and counts toward the cap (no more than two Grades over the sustainable Grade on their registered card) and toward the fracture risk that comes with stacking (see [[The Magic System/Power in Use/Stacking and Spending|Stacking and Spending]]). A spread coat lifts only within its Grade and adds no risk.
- **Starvation.** Below a tenth of reserve the active coat fails outright and the passive base itself sags: the body reads weaker than its sheet, worse the deeper the reserve goes, and Traits (the soul's standing laws) begin to flicker.
- **Rebuilding.** A coat dropped by surprise comes back in one beat once the practitioner knows what is happening. One beat is a long time with a knife already in.

## How it is beaten

- **Ambush.** Asleep, drugged or surprised, a practitioner keeps only the passive base. Assassins, bandits and jealous rivals live by this. Guards are posted over sleeping masters for exactly this reason.
- **Read the focus.** Strike where the coat has gone pale. A fighter who gathers everything into the guarding arm has left the ribs at base.
- **Outlast it.** Every second costs reserve. Make them chase, make them hold the coat through a long approach, and fight them when the coat fails and the base sags.
- **Disarm.** Knock the weapon loose and the coat leaves it. A coated blade is a different object from the same blade on the floor.
- **Go after the ward.** Reinforcement protects the reinforced body only. The child, the client or the wounded friend beside them stands in plain skin.
- **Cut the ground.** On thin, salted or bled ground (Aether-poor by nature, worked against pooling, or pulled empty), or beside a Core (a Wellspring site, in common speech) whose keepers have closed it, nothing fills the reserve back up, everything costs more and the coat runs down sooner.

## Example

**Mags Tolley**, a Stage V hired hunter tempered on a Fulguria (lightning) Wellspring, sleeps at a waystation with her boots on. A thief with a knife comes through the shutter. She is asleep, so she has only her passive base, but her Durability is B-Grade and the blade skids off her collarbone and opens a shallow line instead of a deep one. She wakes, and in one beat the coat comes up a smoky arc-blue over her forearms, hissing like a wet wick.

The thief has a partner at the door, Stage V like her and swinging a coated maul. Mags gathers the coat into her left forearm and takes the maul there: the arm is lifted a full Grade and the iron rings off it. But the blue has drained from her right side to bare skin, the partner sees it, and his second swing goes for her right ribs. She turns her hip, eats half of it at base, and feels a rib go. Her window reports it the moment after, in the tongue she grew up with:

```
[Hit: right ribs, one cracked]
[Reserve: 81% ▓▓▓▓▓▓▓▓░░]
```

From here she has a little under four minutes of focused coat from what is left in her, and she knows it.

# The Working Trades

**Drafts, Runes, Tags, Chants and the Spoken Arts** · *The ways a working is set down, and the quarrel over which is best*

> *"By the hand that cut it."* Kharven oath, usually sworn just before something gets thrown.

Nobody can say who threw the first bottle at the Feast of the Five Hands, and everybody can tell you the word that started it. Master Engraver Hallvard Stenn of Marrowhold, in his gold chain and on his sixth cup, leaned down the long table at Ostermark Hall and called a Kharven pourer a potboy. She was known from the Reach to the sea as the Widow's Ladle. She answered with a bottle of something green that stank of pears, and it glanced off his chain and burst across the plum brandy. Her second bottle died in the air a pace from his head, in a shiver of gold letters, because Stenn had cut the doorframe behind him that spring against precisely this sort of evening, and the whole room learned what a season's chiseling looks like when it gets to do its job. Loud Peg Arkwright, a hired speaker brought in for the entertainment, climbed on a table and bawled **CINDER WALK**, which took out the east windows, the musicians and two of her own teeth, and she stood there grinning blood at everyone as she paid for it on the spot. Up in the gallery twenty choristers, who held the hall's sung ward and whom nobody had thought to tell, kept the same long golden note going through all of it, in perfect pitch, as the benches went past below them.

Thirty-one broken noses, two dead, an ear, a chain, and a hall that smelled of burned brandy and pears for a year. The Accord's examiner tried to get all of it onto one form, found no heading that would hold a bottle, a doorframe, a shout and a choir at once, and in the end wrote *brawl*.

A bottle poured over a season and bought with money. A doorframe cut in spring, still working. A shout, now, paid for with teeth. A note that needs twenty throats at once. Those are the trades of this page, with the tag in the belt-book as a fifth, and the brawl is the shortest honest account of them I know. Older books sorted them into ranked kinds with a grand heading over the lot, and sold the chart to schools for two centuries. No such kinds exist. There are skills that rise with use, trades that people make a living at, and guildsmen who will fight you about whose is better, and that last part is all the chart ever described.

## The Governing Definition

> **A working is a sentence in Parun given a medium.**
>
> The grammar is fixed and identical in every trade. **What differs is the substance the sentence is written into, and everything else follows from that one choice.**
>
> *Duration follows from it. Cost follows from it. Who can perform it follows from it. Whether it can be sold, stolen, taxed, inherited or burned follows from it.*
>
> **Call them media, then, and leave schools of thought to the people who sell schools:** a practitioner chooses among them the way a craftsman chooses between speaking, writing, distilling and singing.

### The four roles of a sentence

Every sentence in Parun (the language of law every working is written in) does the same four jobs in the same order, and the order is the point. **Authorization** says by what right a limit is set. **Boundary** sets it. **Direction** states which way the law is to run across it. **Sealing** takes the limit back down. A glyph creates nothing. A Wellspring (the governing idea that transforms how a soul uses Aether) was already at work, and the sentence only tells it where to stop.

Which is why Sealing is the role that kills people. Authorization, Boundary and Direction failing produce a working that goes wrong while the operator is still in the room. **Sealing failing produces a working that does not stop**, because the law goes on obeying itself after the operator's attention has left the room. Every burned workshop in the Accord's incident record is a Sealing failure and roughly a third of them are recorded as something else.

### Skills first, trades after

Every one of these is a skill before it is a living. It rises with use and shows as a Grade on the sheet, and nothing stops a soldier who shouts from learning to pour, or a pourer with a woken Crystal from cutting her own bottle seals. Pouring is the one any pair of hands can level; the speaking, the cutting, tags and the singing want a woken Crystal. A trade is what happens when somebody does one skill well enough for money. A guild title (see [[The Magic System/Practice and Institution/The Standing and the Title — The Three Names|The Standing and the Title]]) is how far that guild will trust them with it, and a cutter's title caps the Grade she may lawfully cut, a pourer's the Grade she may lawfully pour. A skill gain arrives the way all of them do, as a line only its owner can read:

```
[Skill: Cutting, D to C]
```

Techniques are named things done with a skill, and they show no level.

### What each one does, side by side

| Trade | Medium | How long it holds | Does it outlive its maker? | Who can use it | What it bills | How it fails |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **The speaking** | Breath, hand, body or a chain held in the Crystal | As long as the saying | **No.** It ends when attention lapses | The woken | The body, in the same scene | It dissipates, and the Essence committed is gone |
| **The cutting** | Stone, iron, bone, crystal, wood, a living Shell | As long as the substrate holds its retention threshold | **Yes, by centuries** | Made by the woken; works on anyone who passes | A season, a stone, and no second chance | It misfires continuously until someone finds it and breaks it |
| **Tags** | Paper, hide, bark, thin foil | Until fired; a charged tag goes stale | **Yes, until it goes stale** | Practitioners only: the working waits for a woken Crystal to close it | Its fuel, and a hand and a moment | It dies on thin ground, or a bad seal makes one that does not stop |
| **The pouring** | A substance in a vessel | Until drunk or spoiled | **Yes.** The bottle is the point | **Anyone with a mouth** | Provenance: the ledger behind the ingredients | It does nothing, works on the wrong person, or works exactly as a lying buyer wanted |
| **The singing** | Sustained tone | As long as it is sung, then decays predictably | **Only by replacing the singers** | Above trivial, only a group | Other people's time, indefinitely | A choir that loses three singers to fever in a week |

### The register table

Each trade carries an Accord name in the old tongue and a plain name in the common one. The old names are the examiners' filing words for a trade and name no kind of magic.

| Trade | Accord register | Common tongue | The practitioner | Contempt |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Speech** | *Ars Vocis* | the speaking, saying | a speaker, a caster | **a mouth** |
| **Inscription** | *Ars Scripti* | the writing, the cutting | an Engraver, a Runesmith | **a cutter** |
| **Distilling** | *Ars Vasis* | the pouring, vessel-work | a Drafter | **a potboy** |
| **Song** | *Ars Cantus* | the singing | a Chanter, a Chorister | none on file, and nobody wants to be the first to insult forty people at once |

> **Which column a person speaks from places them before they have finished the sentence.** An Accord examiner says *Ars Scripti.* The woman who paid him to cut a ward into her doorframe says **the cutting.** *A Kharven soldier says he wants a man who can do the writing, and everyone in the room knows what he means.*
>
> **Nobody has a word for the whole of it that anyone would say aloud.** A person asked what they do names their trade, and if they have two they name the one that pays.

## The Examiners and the Workers

The discipline of building a lawful Parun sentence and committing it to a medium is what the guilds of the Accord test, license and tax, and what their courts prohibit. **Nobody practices it in the abstract**, because practice always means one of the trades below.
> **The theoretical worker** studies it and performs nothing. They know the Codex (the Accord's master register of glyphs, Wellsprings and laws), the Families, the Wellspring assignments, the Derivation Index, the case law. **They can tell a Runesmith that his Boundary layer is malformed and be right. They have never in their lives put a working into anything.**
>
> *The research halls are full of them, the examination boards are made of them, and the most senior chairs require no demonstrated capacity to cast whatsoever.*
>
> **The practical worker** performs. **Their standing among the theoretical is roughly that of a farrier at a physicians' dinner.**

**The examination.** Four gates, in order, **and the order is the argument**: knowledge of grammar, then a demonstrated working, then a masterpiece submitted to the board, then the oath. *A worker who fails the masterpiece may practice under supervision indefinitely and will never sign their own name to an inscription.*

## I · The Speaking

***Ars Vocis.*** **The vulgar one, according to everyone who can afford a workshop.**

**Medium** · the ephemeral sentence, carried by breath, hand, or trained interior chain.

**What nothing else can do** · **It happens now.** A rune takes a week and a Draft takes a season. **A spoken working takes as long as it takes to say it, and that is the entire case for it.**

**What it costs** · **The body, immediately, in the same scene.** *There is no substrate to absorb the strain, so the practitioner is the substrate.*
> **Its standing: low in the guilds' eyes, and only there.**
>
> **To a guildsman, a man who can only speak is a man who could not afford to learn to cut.** The soldier's art, the hedge-worker's art (the hedge-worker being the hired hand of the petty arts, paid by the job, who can overcharge you or refuse), the traveling fraud's art and the art of the desperate.
>
> It requires no substrate, no vessel, no workshop, no seven years, and no guild. ***That is precisely why the guilds hold it in contempt. Anyone can shout. Cutting takes seven years and a master willing to take you.*** The world outside the halls has never agreed. The public, the armies and the sects idolize a great speaker as they idolize any champion, the songs are all about speakers, the bookies quote their odds, and the man the examiners call a mouth is the one the crowd came to see.
>
> *The examiners license the hire of it grudgingly, at the lowest fee band, with the shortest examination, and refer to the license among themselves as the shouting ticket.*

### Delivery vectors · all of them the speaking

**Spoken** · Voice into air. The default. **Loud, legible, and it tells everyone within earshot exactly which Wellspring you just invoked.**

**Gestured** · The hand carries the chain. **Quieter, slower, useless with a broken wrist or a bound arm.**

**Bodily** · Martial integration. Corporis. **A practitioner's stats sit in the body always, a passive base nothing switches off, and bodily speaking is the active reinforcement spoken over it**, pushing past the base at a price in reserve. It shows as a coat of the practitioner's own aura, thicker where they push, with the body changing beneath it, and it can be gathered into one fist or one shin while the rest runs thin, so reading where it went is half the fight. **A fist with combustion on it is a sentence with the body as the pen**, and the sentence is still ephemeral, *which is why the fist stops burning, and the coat drops, the moment attention lapses.* Asleep, drugged or taken by surprise, a practitioner keeps the base and loses the layer, which is why ambush is the oldest answer to one. **It is not a separate art, whatever the fight schools charge for it.**

**Internalized** · The chain held silently in the Crystal (the Soul Crystal, which is the soul itself). **The high form.** Below Refraction (Stage VII) a working needs voice, hand, ink or blood as an outside anchor for its boundary; the registers call this the Law of Material Anchor, or Law V. At Refraction a practitioner can hold the Crystal itself as an object of study, and the four roles compress into an inner chain that needs no anchor at all. *So a man who works silently in a room has told everyone present his Stage without saying a number.*

**Failure mode** · **It dissipates and takes something with it.** The Essence (the soul's own stuff of power, spent to work) already committed does not come back. *Loud, immediate, embarrassing and survivable: exactly the wrong shape for the respectable trades and exactly the right shape for a battlefield.* In some rooms a working spoken aloud in company is an obscenity, and nobody polite says casting.

## II · The Cutting

***Ars Scripti.*** **The writing. The runes. The one the oldest guilds are built on.**

**Medium** · the permanent sentence, cut into substrate. Stone, iron, bone, crystal, prepared wood, **and at the far end of the skill the Aether Shell (the soul's outer layer, turning inner power into outward force) or Soul Crystal lattice of a living body.**

**What nothing else can do** · **It outlives the person who made it.** *A ward cut into a fortress wall by an Engraver dead three centuries still suppresses hostile Essence, provided the stone has not fallen below its retention threshold.* **The Continuum reads what is written. The author may leave. The sentence remains legible.**

**What it costs** · Time, substrate, and **irrevocability.** A season of work, a stone that will never be cut again, **and the knowledge that an error does not misfire once.**
> **It misfires continuously, for as long as the carving holds, on everyone who passes it, until somebody finds it and breaks it.**
>
> ***There are inscriptions in the world that have been quietly wrong for two hundred years.***
**Its standing: highest, say the cutters, and they own the building.** Masterpiece requirement, seven-year term, **trade secrets that are genuinely secret, and a monopoly the cutters' guilds enforce under the Accord's seal with real violence.** *An Engraver's mark on a wall is a signature and a warranty and a legal instrument at once.*

### And it is degrading

**The best inscriptions in the world are all old**, work of the Great Spirit War and the days of the Physical Epochs, when Bara, Ashura and Aegor walked in human form. People kill for the portable ones. The great arrays at the Concord Gates, the deep wards under Marrowhold, the standing work in the Kharven death-houses: **none can be reproduced by any living Engraver, and several can no longer be fully read.**
> The Accord's published position is that this is a matter of lost technique **which its research halls expect to recover within a generation.**
>
> ***They have expected this for four generations.***
>
> Look at any standing inscription and you will find a date, an author or a repair, and the repair is worse than the original. **The wall is older than the war.** Cheap runed gear is journeyman stamping, common as nails, and carries no more than a maker's mark, and the lamp-wardens and ward-cutters who keep a street's small cuts in repair are paid by the job and go on strike like anyone else.

## III · Tags

**The cutting made small enough to carry and cheap enough to lose.**

**Medium** · a short sentence and its seal, cut on paper, hide, bark or thin foil and fired by touch. The full account, with prices and fuels, is under [[The Magic System/Power in Use/Runes and Tags|Runes and Tags]].

**What nothing else can do** · **It brings a prepared working into a fight without the fighter having prepared anything that day.** It takes an hour or a day to cut and a thumb to fire.

**What it needs** · **A woken Crystal to close it.** Only a practitioner can fire a tag, which is why the commoner's magic is the bottle and not the strip. Every tag runs on one of three fuels: the Aether of the place, the reserve of whoever fires it, or a charge its maker stored in it, and the fuel is written on the strip for anyone who can read.

**What it costs** · By the fuel. An ambient tag dies on thin ground, a reserve tag bills the user mid-fight, and a charged tag goes stale on its maker's schedule.

**Failure mode** · **A bad cut misfires every time, and a bad seal makes a working that does not stop.** See the glyph roles above, and then see the privy door at Altherion.

## IV · The Pouring

***Ars Vasis.*** **Vessel-work. The richest trade there is, and the one the rest of the world drinks.**

**Medium** · the distilled sentence. **A procedure, with no standing instruction left behind**: Aether plus Essence plus Vessel plus Glyphica (the glyph-writing that shapes a working), resolved as a reaction, **and the working ends up inside a substance.**
> **What nothing else can do, and this is the point of the entire page:**
>
> ***It puts a working into the hands of somebody who cannot work.***
>
> The speaking, the cutting, tags and the singing all require a woken Soul Crystal. **The pouring requires one only at the moment of manufacture, and there only behind the seal on work made to stand: under the Law of Alchemical Independence a pure alchemist, whose Crystal never woke, pours Drafts all the same.**
>
> After that the working is **a thing in a bottle**, and the bottle can be **carried by a courier, sold by a factor, taxed at a gate, stolen off a corpse, watered down by a dishonest apothecary, and drunk by a farmer who has never harmonized with anything in his life.**
>
> **The bottle is how the other ninety-odd percent of the population holds magic in their own hand.** Everything else they touch, they hire: the warden's lamp, the cutter's doorframe, the hedge-worker's charm against the cough. *Everything a market in magic needs falls straight out of that: smuggling, counterfeiting, guild monopoly, excise, adulteration, shortage, addiction, and a black market with names in it.*
>
> **What a Draft does to a soul whose Crystal never woke.** A Draft acts on the Essence Core (the soul's inner layer, holding identity, memory and Traits), and a Class Ø soul has a Core like anyone else. Its Shell is sealed and cannot phase-lock, and no harmonization follows, so the farmer gets the working and none of the architecture.
> 
> He also gets no melt. **Nucleation (the seeding that decides how a melted Crystal sets again) is a Crystal event**, so an ordinary Draft cannot seat in him, cannot recast him, and cannot be argued with once it is in. (The one exception is the Waking Draft, made to open a sealed Crystal, very dear, and often the death or the warping of the child who drinks it.) It runs its course through the Core and leaves whatever it leaves. Repeated dosing over years advances nothing and **deepens the grain**, hardening the man further along the direction he was already set, which is why the second and third generation of a village on cheap Drafts are recognizable at a distance and the fourth is a problem.
> 
> *The Accord has no category for this because its ledgers count practitioners. The apothecaries have known it for two centuries and price accordingly.*
**What a Draft does to a woken one.** Everything the top of the market pays for. An Essence Draft feeds tempering, the melting-down and rebuilding of the soul layer by layer that refines Essence and leaves the body stronger and carrying Traits. A Draft can give a Wellspring. **A Draft can supply the Catalyst a Stage demands, so that Stages can simply be bought**, and the great houses hoard the top Drafts and marry their way into the rest. [[The Magic System/The Soul and the Climb/The Ladder and the Draft — Temperance, Advancement and Essence Drafts|The Ladder and the Draft]] has what that buys and where it stops.

**Fight Drafts.** A bottle made to be drunk mid-fight costs a beat with the neck at the lips, refills the reserve and leaves a crash that pays for it. Each Draft is graded by the Stage it serves, the price climbs about threefold a Stage, and counterfeits are common. See [[The Magic System/Power in Use/Stacking and Spending|Stacking and Spending]].

**Where the strength comes from.** A [[Materials, Alchemy & Trade/Alchemy/Monster Cores|monster's core]] is in a way its Soul Crystal, worth more the stronger the monster, and an alchemist renders it into Essence Drafts. It is the lawful Crystal Draw of the [[Materials, Alchemy & Trade/Alchemy/The Standing Index|Standing Index]], with Creature-Sourced potency and the risk of Identity Contamination in every bottle. Swallowed raw it gives everything at once, and most who try it die or come out wrong. **A Crystal cut from a dead practitioner makes the strongest Draft of all, the worst crime in every realm and the most profitable**, and it is why the Index's Class IV (its top class of stock, no relation to Aether Class IV) has never balanced.

**What it costs** · **Provenance.** *A Draft requires ingredients, and ingredients come from somewhere, and somebody sold them to you, and there is a ledger.* **The bill here is social and legal,** and the enforcement arm of every guild in the Accord exists to collect on it.

**Its standing: middling in dignity, enormous in coin.** Below the cutters in the halls and **far above them in turnover.** *A Drafter is a tradesman. A great Drafter is a merchant house.* **The Accord's revenue depends on the pouring to a degree it does not enjoy discussing.**

**Failure mode** · It does not work, or **it works on the wrong person**, or **it works exactly as intended for a buyer who lied about what he wanted it for.** *Quiet, deniable, and traceable through paperwork.* Check the seal, check the excise stamp, and remember that somebody knows the difference between the label and the bottle and is saying nothing.

## V · The Singing

***Ars Cantus.*** **Pitched, where the other trades assert.**

**Medium** · the sustained sentence, carried by tone. **Neither ephemeral nor permanent.** It holds for as long as it is sung and decays predictably afterward.

**What nothing else can do** · **It scales with throats.** Everything above trivial requires more than one voice, **which makes it the only trade that cannot be practiced alone.** A chant needs its voices in tune, its hours kept and somebody to replace the singer who dies, and it follows the register of whoever sings it, a soldier's chant sounding like a soldier and a nun's like a nun.
> *That single constraint makes it institutional by necessity: orders, choirs, liturgies, schedules, a roster, and the particular catastrophe of a choir that loses three singers to fever in a week.*
**What it costs** · **Other people, and their time, indefinitely.** *A sung ward is a standing labor obligation. Somebody is singing right now or the thing is failing.*

**Its standing: sacral, and outside the guilds entirely**, which is why the Accord finds it difficult and why Sancta Lux and the Sohai live here.
> Harmonia (magic worked by tone and sympathetic vibration) takes the singing as its medium-home.

## Whose Trade Is Best

A feud, and it changes with the ground. The Concord ranks cutting first. The Marrowkin rank it first and hardest, since a hold is a permanent substrate and everything they own is inscribed. Kharven ranks pouring first: nothing in a camp that moves is permanent, a Draft travels in a belt, and the standing runework in the death-houses is imported work no living Kharven hand can read, a fact the seat does not raise and the Accord does not stop mentioning. The speaking ranks higher in Kharven than anywhere else in the Concord's reach, because a soldier is not embarrassed by an art that works now. *Potboy* is the word that starts fights there, as the Feast of the Five Hands can tell you.

## Master Works

Every one of these trades ends in the same place, for the very few who get there. **A [[Materials, Alchemy & Trade/Alchemy/Master Works|Master Work]] is a piece that does what its Grade and material should not be able to, made once at the end of a lifetime, always Unique or Mythic, and named.** Each area has its own: a cutter's wall that does what no stone should, a smith's blade, a pourer's bottle. The most coveted thing in any cellar is a **Fate Essence Draft**, which bottles a share of the manipulation of the probabilities of reality itself. It is extremely hard to make, and more than one house has gone to war over a rumor of one.

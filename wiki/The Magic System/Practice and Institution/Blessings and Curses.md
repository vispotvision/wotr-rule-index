# Blessings and Curses

*What a thumb grants, who can press one on you, what it costs them, how the line on your window lies, what the terms were, and who can take it off again*

> *READ IT AGAIN.*
>
> *Painted in red at eye height over the door of Wording Row at Waxgate, where the wording-men keep their stalls, and repainted every spring at the expense of the widows*

Dickon Sedley sold mutton pies off a barrow on the great bridge at Kingsbridge, and at the spring fair of 661 he sold one to Dame Rohese Vaske, Stage XI, a very large woman in scarlet and gold who had been celebrating since Tuesday. She pronounced it the finest thing she had eaten in forty years. Then she pressed her thumb into the flour on his forehead and told him, with a great laugh and a wave at the river, that a man who could make that should never be beaten. It took about as long as a sneeze and cost her less than the pie. A print the color of hot brass came up on his skin and stayed there, and when Dickon looked at his window that night (the soul's report of its own numbers, which only its owner can see), there was a new line under Baking and Gout.

```
[Dickon Sedley · Level 11]
[Baking: B · Haggling: D]
[Health: Gout, left foot]
[Blessing: Unbeaten]
[You will not be beaten.]
```

By midsummer he was in the Kingsbridge brackets in his apron with a rolling pin, because a man with that line owes it to himself, and the whole Midmarch came down to see it. He had never thrown a punch and he never learned. A Splintering champion of the Blue Herons tripped on her own ribbon and broke her collarbone on the rope. A lancer's horse took a sudden dislike to the lance. In the semifinal a Stage VII duelist opened him from hip to armpit in front of nine thousand people, and Dickon stood there with the flour going red and his own side held shut in one hand, and the duelist's heart stopped. Bookies quit the book, heralds cried him from every market cross on the Kessen road, and by autumn he was King of Little Tolland, which had been looking for one.

The terms arrived over the next thirty years. He could not lose at dice, so nobody would dice with him. He could not lose an argument, and his wife, who had been winning them for two decades, went home to her sister. He could not be beaten by a fever, so it stayed, unwon, for nineteen winters. The cut in his side never closed, since closing would have meant the cut had finished with him, and neither of them could finish. At eighty-one he got up every morning on a body that should have stopped nine separate times, because lying down was losing. His grandchildren petitioned Dame Rohese, who was still alive and in excellent color, and she did not remember the pie.

So in 692 they spent the treasury of Little Tolland on a letter north, and an old woman named Tokiwa Moto came down the Kessen road with two attendants and a bill. She stood him on the bridge, read the line aloud, and said one quiet sentence to it, and the brass print went out like a lamp. Dickon Sedley sat down. He asked for dice. He lost nine straight games to a child, at a copper a game, roaring with laughter and bleeding on the stones, and he was dead before the week was out, thoroughly beaten, in the best mood anyone had ever seen him.

Every clause of the law is in that story. This page takes them in order, and I would ask you to read it before you go near a shrine, or a duchess, or a pie.

## At a Glance

**What it is.** A **blessing** grants a power out of a person's natural reach, even in Level. Most run through the Plane of Fate, some do not. The street calls it **a thumb**, and a person who has one is **thumbed**.

**How it looks.** A line of its own on the window, and on the body a print or a stain in the giver's color. In a practitioner's aura (the visible coat of their own Essence, which is Aether refined in the soul) a thumb shows as a ribbon of somebody else's hue at the edge, and anyone trained can spot it across a ring. A thumb on one of the unwoken, whose Soul Crystals never opened, usually shows as a mark on the skin, warm to the touch.

**What it costs.** The giver, almost nothing. The holder, whatever the terms were.

**How it is beaten.** Read the wording and fight what it leaves out. Failing that, a handful of bloodlines can take it off, and one of them is famous for it.

**Example.** Jory Tull, Stage III, a Green Sigil hunter, spends a season hauling water up a mountain for an old spirit who likes being carried to. It thumbs her, and her window now holds `[Blessing: Cold Doesn't Mind You]`. She walks through a cold gallery, a Titan-touched one, in her shirt, breaks masks her Stage has no business breaking, and sells them for a year's wages. In the second winter she notices that she no longer feels the stove either, and that she has burned both hands to the bone without waking.

## What One Can Grant

The law says *out of a person's natural reach*, and the range is wider than most people dare ask for.

| The grant | What the window shows | What usually goes unmentioned |
|---|---|---|
| **A rule about you** | One flat sentence. `[You will not drown.]` | Everything the sentence left out. This is the commonest kind and the most dangerous |
| **Level** | The number moves, and every reader will confirm it. A blessing is the one thing that can move Level without danger overcome | The fighting that goes with those Levels is absent, and the body arrives ahead of its owner |
| **A Grade or several** | A Primary (one of the Eight stats on the sheet) or a Sub-Stat jumps | It is outside help, so it may lift a fighter past the two-Grade cap on self-boosts (see [[The Magic System/Power in Use/Stacking and Spending\|Stacking and Spending]]), and it adds no risk unless it was forced on the one receiving it. Whoever is holding it up can also let go |
| **A skill or a tongue** | A new Grade on a trade or weapon skill, overnight | Skills rise with use, and a skill that never rose has no habits under it. It works perfectly right up to the first surprise |
| **A Wellspring** | A harmonization with one of the sixty alchemical and spiritual transformations that shape how a soul uses Aether. It lands small, as any new one does, and years of mastery bring it up | The giver chose which one |
| **A Catalyst** | A Stage. The Catalyst is the change a Crystal demands before it will climb, and a thumb can supply it as a Draft can | A given Stage opens the door and no more. It has no Release (a fighter's staged power-up) until one is found and mastered by fighting |
| **Waking** | The whole window opens, with Essence and Stage I | The Crystal woke to the giver's knock and may answer to that knock for life. Measurewrights argue about this one, and the wise ones do it quietly |
| **A grip on Fate** | A rung of Thread Class (the ladder of how much of Fate a soul can pull down and hold), ahead of Glory if the giver likes, which nothing else but a bloodline can manage | A grip is all it is. The holder still cannot reason out what they are holding, and the first pull can take most of a reserve and often fails. Whatever handed you the thread may be what it belongs to |

Two things stand outside the list. Nothing a thumb gives is a **[[The Magic System/Practice and Institution/Vows and Oaths|vow]]**: a vow is a person binding their own probability, small Fate that anyone can make, and its window shows the bargain and its terms in full because you wrote them. And a blessing does not reliably undo a death. The dead stay dead. A very great giver could in theory reverse one, a world-shaking event with a terrible price, and it comes back as a curse more often than not (see Cold Harrow, below).

### Written and poured

The trade distinguishes two kinds, and the distinction pays for most of the wording-men's lunches.

**A written blessing** goes through the Plane of Fate, the control of Attraction Force (the pull that makes a name, bond or vow take hold) and Obsession Force (the same pull when it refuses to let go), and through them of what the odds are allowed to do. It moves no strength to anyone, and it tells the world how things are going to fall around you. All spirits and all physical things have fates, so a writer has plenty to lean on. Most blessings are written, because writing is cheap, and a written line outlives its author unless the terms say otherwise.

**A poured blessing** skips Fate entirely. The giver hands over something real, a share of its own Essence or its own law, and the holder's numbers rise because something was actually moved. It is honest in a way a written one seldom is, it costs more, and it tends to thin when the giver is far off and go out when the giver dies. Lords prefer this kind for their captains, and I invite you to think about why.

## Who Can Give One

Anything spiritually stronger than you. The sum of the qualification is that the world listens to it first.

| Giver | How common | What they give | What they want |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Lesser spirits** (of a ford, a hill, a Well's edge, an old tree) | Most of the blessings anyone meets | One small rule within their own ground, often worded by something that has never met a person | Attention, mostly. Offerings, a song, being carried to, somebody who remembers their name |
| **Spirit Beasts and old things in dungeons** | Rare, and you have to survive the introduction | A rule about the deep, a sense, a Wellspring | An alliance. See [[The Bestiary/Dungeons\|Dungeons]], where the advice is to be polite |
| **Old beings who remember** (survivors of the Great Spirit War and the days when the Epochs walked) | A handful in the world | Almost anything, with terms written in a language that has lost its other speakers | Nobody who has gone looking has reported back in a form I would trust |
| **People of higher Temperance** (a high-Stage practitioner, Temperance being the refining of the soul up the sixteen Stages) | Most of what the powerful hand out | Grades, Levels, a Catalyst, a leash | Service, loyalty, a good joke |
| **Higher spirits, World Spirits, Titans** | Once or twice in a realm's history | A people's luck, a line's nature, a kingdom's river | Their reasons are not on file |
| **Archons and Epochs** | Rarely, and it shakes the world | Whatever they meant | When one acts, the survey maps are redrawn and the churches split. The Archives enter such events with a date and a long silence |

For a person, the gap is what matters. In practice a giver needs at least three Stages over the one receiving, the same distance at which Pressure starts dropping people in the room. Under that the thumb will not hold past a night, and a good many sect elders have embarrassed themselves discovering this in front of a favorite student.

**Consent is optional.** A giver far enough above you needs no leave, and most great givers do not think to ask. A line can arrive while you sleep, while you are praying for something else, or because something passing through the valley liked your singing.

## What a Giver Spends

> **The most insulting sentence in the Archives:** *a great giver spends almost nothing.*

A written line pays for itself. A loaded die has no more strength in it than a fair one; the weight only settles which face comes up, and the throw was happening anyway. The stronger the soul, the more readily the Continuum (the standing order of the world, which measures every soul) takes its word, so at the top a thumb costs a sentence and at the very top it costs less than that.

A reader fresh from [[The Magic System/The Soul and the Climb/The Three Paths Routing, Recognition and the Gate|The Three Paths]] will object that Fate is the hardest road there is, that a real pull can empty a soul at Stage XII, and that I have just told him a woman did it with flour on her thumb. These are different acts. A pull reaches up and warps what the world has already arranged, out of the puller's own hand and against the odds. A thumb sets a small weight on a page far below the writer, the holder's own Attraction Layer, where the stronger soul's word already carries, and it lets the throw fall. So the cheapness belongs to the gap and to nothing else. Narrow the gap and the bill climbs, as the table shows, and below the very top a line moves only what already has a way of happening. A heart can stop. A lancer's horse can take a dislike to the lance.

| The gap | What the giver feels | What it can carry |
|---|---|---|
| **Three or four Stages** | A hard morning. A real bite out of the reserve (the Essence a practitioner can spend), and a poured thumb may cost near half of it and a week in bed | One small rule, a skill, a Grade held for a season |
| **Five to eight Stages** | A good sneeze. The cost of lighting a coat | A Level jump, a Grade for years, a Catalyst for someone far below |
| **More than that** | Nothing they would notice, and they frequently do not notice | Most of the table above. Dame Rohese had eleven Stages on a pie man, and the pie was the larger expense |
| **Spirits, on their own ground** | Nothing, plus the pleasure of company | As much as their ground holds. Off it they cannot hold much |
| **Archons and Epochs** | Nothing has been recorded | The cost lands on the world instead: on the weather, the Wells, the odds across a whole quarter. The bill goes to everyone else |

It is that cheap, and every duke's army is still not thumbed to the eyes, and the reasons make up the politics of the age. A thumb cannot be collected unless the terms say so, and writing terms that hold takes more care than most givers own. A fighter lifted past his natural reach is a rival you built by hand. A careless line can kill the holder or the giver's whole plan, and the wording is on the giver's head when it does. And every Moto in the north can read what you wrote. So thumbs are given sparingly and on a short rope, and they have become the most valuable thing a powerful person can hand out for free. Lords hold people they cannot out-fight through grain, land, debt and the Draft supply, and the thumb is the debt that sits on the sheet where its owner sees it every morning.

**For the poor it is one of three roads**, along with dungeon luck and guild patronage. People walk the shrine roads for years, leave offerings at every ford, haul water up mountains and sing to rocks, and it works often enough to keep the roads busy. Every shrine road has its false givers too, and a stranger with a good coat and a bright smile who presses your forehead for a silver mark is giving you a sore forehead.

## The Line

The window tells the truth about the numbers. A blessing is a different matter, since **the giver wrote the line, and the soul is only reading it back.** A soul has no way of checking what somebody stronger has written on it, any more than a page can check a scribe.

### How a line lies

| The method | The line | What is actually happening |
|---|---|---|
| **By leaving things out** | `[You will not drown.]` | True. It does not say who will |
| **By flattery** | `[Blessing: Beloved of the Hill]` | The hill will not let you leave |
| **By a false name** | `[Blessing: Grace of the Saint of Sheafton]` | There is no saint, and the thing that signed her name has your address |
| **By a false number** | `[Strike Force: A]` | B, and a thumb that makes the window read high until the fight in which it matters. Rare, because it takes a real writer |
| **By changing later** | `[Blessing: Favored]` | A line can be rewritten by whoever wrote it. It read differently yesterday, and the soul's memory of yesterday may have been tidied too |
| **By saying nothing at all** | No line | The commonest curse in the Archives is the one that never showed. Assay, if you can afford it |

This is separate from the other way a window can lie. A strong mind-worker, on the Wellsprings of memory, sleep and reflection, can make a window show false numbers or hide a line, and that is a violation with a cost, with Gnosis as the fair counter. A blessing's lie has no such seam, because the line belongs there.

### Reading one

**Gnosis first, and it will mostly fail.** A stronger Gnosis Perception reads a weaker Resilience Ward. A line wears the Ward of whoever wrote it, so nobody below the giver reads through it. Everyone else is estimating aloud.

**Assay the holder.** An assay reads what the soul is actually doing, and a line that says Grade A on a soul the coil reads at B has told you something expensive. It says nothing about terms.

**Test it, slowly, on something you can afford.** Hold your breath in a bucket with a friend standing by before you trust the Tide-Mother.

**Hire a wording-man.** Every city with a court has a row of them, ex-advocates who read a line for a fee and tell you what it does not say. They are very good, and they have never once found the term that killed the client, and they will show you the clause in your contract that says so.

**Ask what the giver eats.** This is the oldest advice and still the best: learn what the giver wants, what it has done before, and what became of the last one.

**In court.** A court makes you swear to what your sheet shows, and lying under a proper [[The Magic System/Practice and Institution/Vows and Oaths|oath]] costs you, because an oath is small Fate. A person who swears honestly to a line that lies is a question the lawyers have been fighting over for three hundred years, and the Waxgate assize has ruled both ways. The current reading is that you are bound to your own understanding of the words, which is why a wording-man will tell you never to read your line too closely before a hearing.

## Terms and Fine Print

Every blessing has **terms**, which is the street's word for what the line did not say. A vow shows its terms on the window. A blessing shows what the giver chose to show. These are the clauses the trade has learned to expect.

| The clause | What it does | The usual sign |
|---|---|---|
| **The rider** | A thing you must keep doing: a shrine visited, a name kept, a meat not eaten | Your numbers drop one morning and you cannot think why |
| **The collection** | The giver comes back for something. A service, a child's first word, your Crystal at the end | A polite visitor, decades later, who knows your mother's name |
| **The heir clause** | It passes down, and the descendants never signed. This is how a lineage comes to carry a blessing, a mutation or a curse in its Inherited Traits (the laws a bloodline hands on) | Everybody in one family has the same stain and the same bad luck with horses |
| **The exclusion** | No other giver's line will hold beside it | The second thumb will not take, or both go wrong together |
| **The fence** | It only runs on the giver's ground, or only off it | Your great gift stops working forty miles from home, in a fight |
| **The appetite** | The thing granted keeps growing past what you asked for | You wanted to be unafraid. You are now unable to be |
| **The plain wording** | There is no trick. It does exactly what it says, forever, including when you wish it would stop | Dickon Sedley |

### When a blessing is a curse

**A curse is often a blessing with terms you did not read, or a giver who does not care what you want.** Nobody can tell at first, and some are both. The power on either side is the same power, written the same way and sitting on the same line, and most of the difference is how the holder feels about it at the time of asking. A few curses are laid on purpose, with malice and good penmanship. Far more are gifts from something that had never met a person, that found you charming, and that has since gone back to sleep.

## Famous Thumbs

*All of these are told in taverns and most of them are told wrong. The people are long dead, and their descendants have asked me not to go on about it.*

**Favor of the Tide-Mother.** `[You will not drown.]` Sabra Haldane, a pilot on the bay at Alabaster, was thumbed by a sea spirit of the Drowned Parishes in 618 after a lifetime of leaving out fish. She went down with the *Good Intent* that autumn and walked up the harbor steps nine days later, breathing brine and in high spirits. Every captain on the coast wanted her aboard, and she made a great deal of money. By 640 she was the sole survivor of eleven wrecks. The line never said anything about her shipmates, and the Tide-Mother had been hungry for a long time and had worked out how to fish. The harbor board of Alabaster eventually paid her a pension to stay ashore, and she drew it for thirty years in a house with a very good view, watching the water with an expression nobody cared to interrupt.

**The Road Remembers You.** Piers Aldane, a courier on the salt road, got it from a milestone spirit for straightening its stone. He was never lost again, in fog, in the Scourge's edge or blind drunk, and he carried the Bridgemarch mails for twenty years without one failure, in a yellow coat the whole road knew. The road remembered him for everyone. Anything hunting him, on any road, found the way as easily as he did. The bailiffs came first, then three wives, then something from the Verath Expanse that had once eaten his mule and had enjoyed it.

**There Will Always Be an Averill at Sheafton.** The Averills, a small and proud house of the Golden Hollow, were granted this by a hill they had built a very nice chapel on, and they had it carved over the door. It is entirely true. There has always been one. Exactly one. It took them three generations to see the pattern in the family tombs, since every heir who came of age did so in the same week the last one died, and no Averill who rode out of Sheafton ever arrived anywhere. The current Averill lives there still, in a beautiful house with gold trees all around it, and does not encourage visitors.

**You Will Not Break.** Captain Thorold Gatty bought this for the two hundred men of his free company from a hermit of Stage XIV, at the price of a sack of dried figs, before the fighting at Sorley Bridge in the autumn of 631. They did not break. They held when the Moot's line collapsed on both sides of them, when the order to fall back came down, and when the order came a second time with a man running it. They stood in perfect dress under their green pennons and went on standing until none of them was alive, which took most of a day and an astonishing number of the enemy. They went into the trench at Sorley with the rest. The Long Barrow has been cleared sixty times since then, and what comes up out of it comes up in step, and the clearing crews will tell you that nobody has been close enough to ask what those windows say.

**The Hills Are Listening.** `[Sing.]` Something very large crossed the Slate Ridge one night in the 590s, heard a shepherd named Selwyn Hay at his evening song, and approved. His voice went to EX by morning, near the very top of the letters, and the sound of it brought people weeping up the mountain from four valleys. He could not stop. He sang through meals and through the births of his children, he sang in his sleep, and he sang for sixty-one more years with a voice that never tired, and his wife learned to wear wax. The giver did not care what he wanted, and very probably never knew he wanted anything. Ridge shepherds still sing with their hands over their mouths.

**The Undying Year.** In Year 388 at Cold Harrow in the Long Fields, Annot Lorne's husband died of a kicked-in chest, and she went up to the old stone on the hill and asked. Nobody knows what answered. He walked home that night with the bruise on him, and she wept for joy, and the whole village carried him round the green. Then nothing in the county died. No hen, no fly, no fever, no calf with a broken back, no man with his belly opened by a scythe. Wounds went on being wounds, and meat would not stop being alive on the hook, and the wheat stood and rotted and went on growing. It lasted nine months. There is an account of what the county smelled like by harvest and I will spare you. When the neighbors came with torches to burn the whole shire down, Annot Lorne went upstairs with a hay fork and did what she had to do to the man she had asked for. He was the first thing in that country to die in nine months, and everything else followed him before sunset. The Archives enter it as a rumor with a body count, and decline to say which giver acted. It is the reason for the line in every faith's catechism about asking.

## Taking One Off

There are four ways, and three of them are mostly unavailable.

**The giver takes it back.** Always possible, if the giver can be found, remembers, and can be persuaded, and each of those has defeated better people than you.

**The giver dies.** A poured thumb usually goes out with its giver, sometimes at a very bad moment. A written one usually does not, since the line is doing its own work by then, and killing a spirit to get out of its terms has a long and discouraging history.

**A greater giver overwrites it.** You now have a greater giver, and a new set of terms.

**Hataraki.** The Moto can do it, whoever gave it.

### How Hataraki removes one

[[The Disciplines/Schools and Bloodline Arts/Hataraki — The Works of the Plane of Fate/Hataraki — The Works of the Plane of Fate|Hataraki]] is the Moto's governing power, a real Fate Art, and it treats the world as a set of jobs. A blessing, to a Moto, is a job someone has assigned to the odds, and a job can be read, and a worker can be fired. There are few enough cases on the public record that the method is known mostly from what the holders report, and it runs in three motions.

The grips do not trade, so one Moto with one Work cannot do this alone: it takes two Works or two Moto, one to read and one to discharge.

**The reading.** The Work of Knowledge (*Chishiki no Shigoto*) reads the line for what it is, and for a moment the holder's own window shows the whole of it, in plain words, usually for the first time. Everyone who has lived through this says it was the worst part.

```
[Blessing: Unbeaten]
[You will not be beaten.]
[Given in drink. Giver does not recall. No term.]
```

**The naming.** The remover states aloud what job this thing was given and by whom. Hataraki administers purpose, so it has to say what the purpose was, and this is why a Moto's reading of your thumb is known across a battlefield as something to stop whatever you are doing and run from.

**The discharge.** A line that has become a false continuity, a thing going on past any good reason, falls to the Work of Ruins (*Hametsu no Gō*), which breaks only what is already hollow and does nothing at all to something sound. A line that sits in the soul as a stain goes under the Work of Purity (*Junketsu no Gō*), which makes a thing stand as it ought until the stain has nowhere to stay. A true gift, well held and fitted to its holder over decades, offers neither handle, and it takes longer, a matter of days, with the holder awake.

**Taken back or taken off.** The Moto keep the two apart. To *take back* is to return a line to its author, who feels it arrive, and that is a message. To *remove* is to end it where it stands.

**What it costs.** The Work presses the soul into a useful shape, and every Work carries a corruption vector, so that a Moto who has spent a long life firing the world's gifts tends to grow very sure of what ought to be fired. The larger the giver, the heavier it runs. And a giver of any size knows when somebody has taken its pen away. The Moto themselves bill in obligations, and nobody has yet established what Little Tolland still owes.

**What falls with it.** Everything standing on the line comes down, at once.

| What the thumb held | When it comes off |
|---|---|
| **Levels and Grades** | They drop to what the person earned, and the body has to survive the difference |
| **A given Stage** | The Crystal is left built to a shape it never earned and nothing holding it there. Most survive, and few fight again |
| **A rule that kept you alive** | Everything it was holding off arrives. Dickon Sedley had thirty years of it waiting |
| **A heir clause** | The family's stain lifts from the living, and only the living |

It also works in a fight, which is the reason for the fear. A fighter who has built his whole life on the line in his window can lose it at the range of a raised voice, from someone he did not see come in. A few other bloodlines hold real Fate Arts, and what theirs can do to a thumb they have preferred to say for themselves.

## What the Fate Path Can and Cannot Do

A **Path** is the layer of the Crystal a soul has learned to lean on, and there are three: Body, Spirit and Fate (see [[The Magic System/The Soul and the Climb/The Three Paths Routing, Recognition and the Gate|The Three Paths]]). The Fate Path leans on the Attraction Layer, and it is the hardest of them. It works on what has to be so before anything can happen, the law and construction of the world, and people fight and wage wars with it, though most of its walkers do their fighting with Range, Aura Pressure, presence and bonds. Its shallow water is bond, recognition and the kept word, and a midwife of forty years is standing in it without knowing. Far down it is recognized by what a soul refuses to let end, and people who have heard of Hataraki assume its walkers can do what the Moto do. Very few can, and the Path stays very hard all the way up. Some high-Stage masters reach real Fate Arts on it late, after decades of kept vows, and that is as much as most of them ever claim.

A written thumb is Fate work, whoever writes it, and it lands on the holder's Attraction Layer, which every soul has. So a hitter who has never sworn anything can be thumbed as easily as a nun, and being thumbed puts nobody on the Path, any more than a loan makes you a banker. A giver who walks some other road, or none, is writing on the Plane of Fate with a borrowed hand, and this is one reason so many lines lie without anyone having meant them to.

| A Fate Path practitioner can | A Fate Path practitioner cannot |
|---|---|
| **Hold what should have ended.** A vow past the point where it costs everything, a bond across a death, a claim for a century. This is the Path's daily work, and the Crystal reads it | **Be made, at depth.** No school can drill a deep one and no house can buy one. A Fate Essence Draft lends the touch, and it no more makes a Fate Path practitioner than a borrowed horse makes a cavalryman |
| **Fight with reach and weight.** Range, Aura Pressure, presence and bonds are the Path's everyday weapons: the room is heavy before they arrive, and everyone sworn to them fights better | **Win a straight exchange.** They take the smallest force bonus on the table, and outside a Domain the Body Path is king. Caught before a result arrives, a Fate fighter is an ordinary body |
| **Pull a thread, on a rare day.** A real pull moves what a blow depends on: a guard slips, a strap gives, and an ordinary cut is already there. Higher up it removes or assigns a prerequisite at a ruinous price | **Do it cheaply, or surely.** One lean on the odds can take a large share of the reserve and leave the body bleeding, it often fails, it shows a charge and a tell, and Dominion's Fate Sub-Stat has to get past the target's Persistence and Continuity |
| **Move a field, once.** Weather, supply, panic and the hour a column arrives, as a life's masterwork at high Stage or a bloodline's art. It may be done once in a war, and it may cost the person doing it | **Move what has no way of happening.** A strap that is not wearing will not give, and a sound gate stands whoever pronounces collapse on it |
| **Make vows that weigh more.** Anyone can bind their own probability. On this Path the binding bites harder, and a kept vow pays more | **Remove someone else's blessing.** That is Hataraki's. A master of the Path may read a weaker giver's line, and may outlast it, and it stays on the sheet |
| **Lean hardest on what they have a true claim on**, their own vow, their own blood, a door they swore to keep. A claim gives Fate more to pull on | **Do what the Moto do**, in nearly every case. Real Fate Arts are rare, mostly by bloodline, and considered incredibly overpowered. Nobody grips Fate bare, and the Works of Hataraki are that house's inherited grip |
| **Give blessings more cleanly**, at high Stage, because they know what the line is doing. A Fate Path giver writes the thumb the wording-men cannot fault | **Reason their way in.** Understanding is made of the thing it would be used against. A Measurewright can describe a thread for a lifetime and never lift one |
| **Summon through Fate itself**, in the rare few who have that art, without the limit a contract has | **Undo a death.** Nobody on the Path has done it, and those who tried with their whole lives are the people the Obsession Force passages of [[The Magic System/Fracture of Worlds — The Living System/V. Wellsprings, Attraction and Obsession, Magic Integration (Parts Thirteen–Fifteen)\|Fracture of Worlds]] are about |
| **Attach terms to an oath** so that it holds without anyone present to enforce it | **Let go easily.** The same grip that makes the Path makes Obsession Force its nearest neighbor. A Fate walker who will not release a thing becomes the thing that will not release |

## How a Measurewright Explains It

Magister Ysolde Corbet, of the Sealhall chair of Attraction, taught for thirty years that a blessing is a coupling. Attraction Force is a real term in the books, she said, as real as weight, and a stronger soul has a greater share of it in any place it has noticed. When such a soul takes note of you, your Attraction Layer (the Fate-Plane layer of the Crystal's three, where the soul's bonds are held) is pulled into step with its own, the way one clock on a wall will bring a second clock into its own swing through nothing more than the shared timber. A poured thumb is a true transfer, paid for in the giver's Essence, and the books balance. A written thumb moves no energy at all, and only biases which of the things already possible comes to pass. She proposed to demonstrate this with dice before a full bench, using a blessed subject and a counted ten thousand throws.

The figures were superb. The subject rolled exactly as often as he should have, for nine thousand nine hundred throws, while her assistant's ladder gave way, the tallow caught and the bench's roof came down on the east side of the hall. The subject was not hurt, and when pressed he admitted that his line read `[Nothing will prove it.]` The Sealhall chair remains funded and argues its case from a different building. The Moto, asked to comment, said that the Magister had described the shadow of a job very accurately, and sent an invoice for the comment.

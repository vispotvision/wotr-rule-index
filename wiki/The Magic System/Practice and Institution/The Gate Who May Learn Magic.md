# The Gate: Who May Learn Magic

> Nearly everyone is born with the Crystal. About one in ten is taught to use it, and the other nine can tell you exactly whose fault that is.

Every market town tells a version of the Carter's Cross auction, and I have checked enough of them to say most are lies, so here is the one with a Guild record behind it. In a hot August a laundress named Joan Pellow had a daughter of eight, Tib, who put both hands in a full washtub and froze it solid with the shirts inside, and by the third day Joan Pellow's drying yard held a hunting-guild scout in a wolfskin, a recruiter with an Aetherion letter, a crown screening officer with a roll under his arm, and two rival schools in full color: the Green Herons on the left with their elder and their song, the Red Ladles on the right with their elder and a louder song. A bookie chalked odds on the church wall. The bidding passed fourteen gold and a mule. When the Ladles offered a new roof the Heron elder called their grandmaster a soup thief, the Ladle elder's coat went up the color of boiled beets, and a jaw broke in the first exchange, and then the whole yard came down, poles and wet linen and sixty people, with sheets burning green overhead like flags. Joan Pellow got up on the frozen tub with a washing bat and bawled that the girl went to whoever would let her come home at harvest. Nobody could promise it, as each of them knew, and while they were still hitting each other about it the screening officer walked around the wreck, found the child behind the rinsing trough, read her, and wrote her name down. An entered child belongs to the roll. Tib Pellow went to the crown. Her mother got three silver and the bill for the church wall, and when her younger boy stirred the following winter she walked him thirty miles by night to the Herons and never told a soul.

That yard held the whole of this page. Here is the machinery under it.

## I. A Crystal in Nearly Everyone

**Nearly every person alive carries a Soul Crystal from birth**, the porter and the laundress as surely as the heir of a great house. The Crystal is the soul, so a few are born with one so slight that no coil has ever found it, and scholars argue about what that makes them. The rest carry a **Dormant Crystal**: sealed and silent, present in full, and doing nothing a coil (the assay instrument that reads a Crystal) can read.

A dormant Crystal stays dormant for life unless somebody wakes it while it is young. Waking is taught, and taught early. It takes a teacher, a regimen and a place to practice before the soul sets, and a Crystal nobody attends to in childhood seals and goes on sleeping, and refines sealed by the Weathering (the slow hardening hard living gives a soul, without magic) into whatever its work made it. **That is Class Ø, and it is nine people in every ten, which is most of the world.**

The tenth wakes, nearly always as a child. One in ten is a great many people. A market town of four thousand keeps four hundred woken souls, nearly all of them low on the ladder, warming kettles and selling small tricks and drilling at dawn in somebody's yard, and that is why every quarter has a school with a song, why sects and their ranks are common knowledge, and why a child of six can rank the local fighters and fight you about it.

A small number stir without being taught. A child raised on a badly worked street in a crowded quarter wakes at an odd age; a child with a strong Inherited mark (a bodily sign of a Trait carried in the blood) shows it before the first winter. These are the ones the screening seasons find, because screening reads a Crystal that has already stirred and returns nothing on one that has not.

Then there is the bottle. **A Waking Draft** opens a Crystal with no teacher and no seven years, and it is the rich family's road and the desperate one's. It is very dear, sixty to a hundred and twenty gold in a good year, and it often kills or warps the child who drinks it. Most of the rich buy one for their children anyway, generally with a physician standing by and a second child in reserve, while commoners sell themselves into guild debt for one. A blessing can wake a soul too, and the Crystal may go on answering to the knock that woke it.
> **So what stands between a practitioner and a porter is almost always the lesson, given in time.** Once a Crystal has sealed it is woken rarely, by a crisis, a master's forcing, a tribulation, a Waking Draft or a blessing, and always at great cost.
> 
> *Every crown of the Dark Ages has understood this, and these are the generations in which they began trying to lock the lesson away.*

The gates a [[The Magic System/The Soul and the Climb/The Three Paths Routing, Recognition and the Gate|Path]] (the layer of the Crystal a soul has learned to lean on, Body, Spirit or Fate) sets on a Sub-Stat (one of the finer stats) decide how far a stat may rise. This gate comes first, and decides whether the Crystal wakes at all.

## II. The Lock

For most of reckoned time teaching passed through households, orders and masters, and a crown cared what its practitioners did rather than how they learned. **The wars of the Dark Ages changed the arithmetic.** A practitioner is a siege engine that eats at your table. A nation that can teach its own subjects and refuse everyone else's has a standing advantage no treaty gives it, and a nation that lets anyone learn anything has handed that advantage to whoever is paying.

So every nation now keeps a **schedule**: which kinds of people may learn which magics, from whom, and under what oath. The schedules differ from crown to crown and they are the most jealously kept documents in any chancery. Three powers hold the keys between them.

The low lesson got out long before anyone wrote a schedule, and there are far too many yards and sects to shut. A schedule licenses them, fines them and counts them. What it actually guards, where it guards anything, is the next rung: the upper manuals, the glyph tables, the high working and the people who take pay for it.

**The Accord**, the coalition of guilds whose halls sit in every nation, controls theurgic (method-and-procedure, as against instinctive) training, issues the Magister credential and funds the academies. *The Guild made the system. The Guild owns the gate.* Inside a signed circle the Accord's examination is the license; outside one each crown and each hall keeps its own.

**The crowns** decide who among their subjects may enter an academy at all, appoint the Mage Royal and the crown chairs, and write the schedule.

**Sancta Lux** credentials nobody and has no need to: it declares what lies on the far side of the gate consecrated or profane, and that is often enough to close it.
> **The Bench's power of reservation is the lock in its purest form.** A formula entered and reserved is lawful, real and forbidden to anyone below a stated Tier. *It arranges who is permitted to be capable, and denies nothing to anyone while it does.*

## III. The Five Gates

| Gate | Who passes | Who holds it |
|---|---|---|
| **Ability** | A Crystal that stirs unbidden and is noticed. Recruited to an academy or sought out by a guild | Screening officers, academy recruiters, guild scouts |
| **Birth** | The children of houses that can pay for a teacher, a fee and seven years, or for a Waking Draft | Great houses, academies, the companies' freedom |
| **Nation** | The crown's own subjects, sworn before the first lesson | Crowns and their chanceries |
| **License** | Whoever sits the examination and passes it | Academies, the Accord, the halls |
| **Bloodline** | The blood, and nobody else | The archaic bloodlines and the lesser houses that copy them |

**Ability.** Screening is seasonal and it is looking for a stirring Crystal. A child who shows one is noticed, and a noticed child is recruited. **The Aetherion Academy takes its entrants by letter and recommendation; the Ward-Carver's Academy at Senri's Gate by an entrance Proving.** Hunting guilds and sect elders go to the same screenings and bid for the same children, and a family with a stirring child in a poor quarter receives more offers in a season than in the rest of its life.

**Birth.** An academy charges fees, and the companies grant the freedom by patrimony, servitude or redemption. **A great house reads its infants for Inherited marks inside the first month** and knows by the first winter what it has. A commoner's child reaches a teacher by indenture, seven years for the cutting, bound to a master the formulary limits in how many he may hold. *Class is decided at the door of the schoolroom long before it is decided anywhere else.*

**Nation.** A crown teaches its own. The oath comes before the first lesson, and a foreign student learns what the treaty between the two crowns allows and nothing more. **Vaeloris, the Carver's Seat of the Silver Cedars**, develops and standardizes techniques for the Reach's own use, and a technique standardized there belongs to the Reach before it belongs to anyone who learned it.

**License.** The academy ranks run Scholar, Licentiate, Master, Doctor and Chair on the diploma, while on the training floor the older ladder of outer disciple, inner disciple, core disciple and elder holds, settled by ranking contests, and **the Licentiate is the qualification that matters**: permission to practice for hire. Past it lie the Accord's credentials. A **Magister** holds an academy credential and a documented training record. A **Registered Natural** learned by other means and submits to monitoring instead: periodic reassessment, demonstration, disclosure of every new harmonization (a soul's tuning to a Wellspring). A school pays for its own license by the year, and in any large city about half the yards hold one and the other half hold something that looks very like one.

**Bloodline.** The archaic bloodlines teach their arts to the blood and to nobody else, and lesser houses imitate them. A bloodline art taught outside the line is theft in the eyes of the house and, in most schedules, of the crown as well. The deep of the Fate Path needs less guarding than a schedule supposes, since nobody can be drilled into it or reason their way in, and a Work of [[The Disciplines/Schools and Bloodline Arts/Hataraki — The Works of the Plane of Fate/Hataraki — The Works of the Plane of Fate|Hataraki]], being an inherited grip on the thing, is the one lesson in the world that a stolen manual does not carry.

## IV. The Second Gate: A Purse

The lesson gets you onto the ladder. What carries you up it is very largely money, and the schedules hardly pretend otherwise.

**A Stage can simply be bought**, as [[The Magic System/The Soul and the Climb/The Ladder and the Draft — Temperance, Advancement and Essence Drafts|The Ladder and the Draft]] sets out: Drafts and blessings can supply the Catalyst (the particular experience a Stage demands), and a soul that has the Draft crosses. The great houses hoard the top Drafts, and here the lock and the purse are one object, since a formula the Bench has reserved may lawfully be drunk only by those it names. **A coil reads the Stage the same however it was crossed, though it reads the seat differently.** A schedule does not care, either, so long as the fee was paid and the formula was lawful.

**The poor have three roads.** *Dungeon luck*: a crew goes down, and comes up with a monster's core worth a year's wages, or a Wellspring got at a Core no one had found, or does not come up. *Guild patronage*: a hall stakes a promising hand a Draft against a signed term of service, and the term is the real product. *Blessings*: a higher spirit or a person of higher Temperance gives what no purse could reach, for reasons of their own, on terms that may be written badly or not at all. Every mother who walks a child to a gate has heard of someone who rose by each of the three, and every bookie can tell her the odds.

**What it does not buy.** Its Release must be found and mastered by fighting, and [[The Magic System/Practice and Institution/Training and Sects|sects teach what money cannot buy]]: Releases, Domains, techniques, skills and the rites for their Wellspring. Nor does a purse buy a Path, because the Crystal reads a Path off what a life has been spent on, and a bought Stage arrives with its gated Sub-Stats exactly as shut as they were. So the richest heir on the river stands at the same gate as the laundress's son, a few rungs higher and with better boots, asking the same school for the same thing, and the school can refuse him. **That refusal is the most valuable thing the schools own**, and they know it.

## V. What Needs No Leave

The schedules watch three things: **who teaches, who takes pay, and high working.** They do not watch what a woken soul does at home with what it already knows.

A woman who woke as a girl and warms her own kettle breaks no law. **Teaching the neighbor's son does. Charging for it does.** Taking pay for a working or a hunt without a guild license does, and is common anyway, because unlicensed hunters are cheap and there are always more postings than licensed parties. High working without a license is the gravest offense the schedules name, and each schedule defines it by Tier and by trade. **The Divine Language sits inside every definition.**

## VI. How the Lock Is Kept

**Controlled texts.** Primers, grammars and glyph tables are copied by sworn scriveners under license, numbered, and issued against a registration. The **Apprentice's Primer** reaches a new practitioner the day they are entered and not before. **Teaching the Divine Language without sanction** is a Law of Severance for anyone sworn to the Codex (the Accord's master register of laws), and a crown offense under most schedules.

**Crystal registration.** A stirred Crystal is entered on the roll of the place it was found. **The Bureau of Strategic Assessment** manages registration and cares only whether an output has been observed and assessed. Those who refuse are entered anyway, as **Unregistered Naturals**, on a separate list that is never published.

**Searchers and trace examiners.** Nearly every working leaves traces. A ward that was cut carries the cutter's hand; a Draft that was poured carries a maker's mark. **A trace examiner reads those traces as evidence**, and a good one can name the teaching lineage behind a technique more often than not, which is how an unlicensed school is usually found: through its pupils' mistakes. The company searcher hears the rest, and an unlicensed working on a kept street is audible to anyone listening at the wrong hour.

**The Night Watch.** The Night Watch Society, a crown-chartered society holding the crown's warrant, older than the Accord's circles, that investigates illegal magic and phenomena the law has no name for. The Night Watch works alongside the guilds and answers to the state, which is the whole of its difference from a hall.

**The Inquisitors.** Sancta Lux keeps a lateral office, the **Inquisitor**, authorized to pronounce sentence in the field without an Arbiter present, and it sends them after unconsecrated high working wherever the Church holds standing. **The Holy Inquisition**, the splinter that took the name, claims the same work and prosecutes it by fire. It holds legal standing where the crowns and churches that back it rule, and enforces the Gate there itself; where the Accord's circles have signed, it is outlawed.

**The guilds.** Locally, the halls police their own ground. A hall that finds an unlicensed practitioner working its district posts him on its board, and an **Extermination** Commission against a corrupted practitioner is a lawful posting under the five classes. Inside a signed circle, Enforcement's **Sealwrights** take the cases no hall will.

## VII. Hire and Jurisdiction

A licensed practitioner works for pay as a **hired hunter**, a **workshop inscriber**, a **company soldier**, or in **private practice**, and the license is what separates each of those trades from a crime.

When a crime is done with a working, it is proved by the trace examiners, and then **the guild courts and the crown courts fight over who hears it.** The guild court claims the case because the accused holds a guild license, or because the act was a working. The crown court claims it because the body lies on crown land. Which of them wins differs by place, and in most places it is decided by whoever has the accused first.

## VIII. Those Who Learn Anyway

**Black-market teaching.** A back room, a fee, a stolen or copied primer, and a teacher who was licensed once. The **kind readers** already sell a reading of what a person is with nothing forwarded to the Accord, and some of them sell the next lesson too.

**The Mother.** A cult, named for the mother of vinegar, the living culture that sours whatever it is added to. It recruits across the Gate: its cells are called cultures, its recruits are inoculated, and what it teaches them no schedule would allow.

**Self-derived workings.** A thaumaturge (a practitioner whose Crystal acts before conscious thought) who finds a native axis alone can work on it without ever having been taught, and some do. The Bureau watches for them with particular care. Self-derivation in the written arts is nearly impossible, which is why the schedules guard the texts hardest.

**The guild loophole.** Hunting guilds train their own. A hall indentures a boy under its own charter, wakes him on its own floor and teaches him what a hunter needs, and the state's gate never sees him until he is registered as a finished hunter. **Crowns hate this and every crown relies on it**, because the halls are what stand between a district and the things that come up out of the dungeons and down off the roads.

**The speaking.** The shouting ticket licenses a speaker to take hire, and that is all it does. The speaking itself has never been licensed and nobody has found a way to license it, because a spoken working has no author in its own grammar. Liability falls on whoever paid. *A speaker needs no substrate, no workshop and no seven years, and when he is finished there is nothing to point at.*

**The penalties.** A small offense costs an amercement to the offender's means. Unlicensed hire costs the tools and the fee. **Unlicensed high working is punished hard**: branding in the Accord cities, and **Crystal sealing**, in which a Sealwright shuts a woken Crystal on itself, the way a keeper closes a Core. A sealed practitioner is Class Ø again in everything except memory.

## IX. How the Common Folk See It

Ordinary people know there is a gate and roughly where it stands. They know the screening season comes, that a stirring child is either a fortune or a danger to the household, and that the ranked practitioner walking down their street got through something they were never offered, even when they went to school with his brother. They follow the yards the way they follow a cockfight, wear the colors, and sing the songs. **The awe they feel is partly for the power and partly for the door.**
> *Some mothers hide a stirring child from the screening, because an entered child belongs to the roll. Others walk one three days to the nearest academy and wait at the gate until someone reads him.*

# The Standing and the Title: Guild Titles as Trust

*On the Three Names Every Practitioner Carries, What the Second One Promises, and Why the Last One Follows You Anyway*

*Compiled for the Research and Archives Division · companion to [[The Magic System/Practice and Institution/The Derivation of Practice — Thaumaturgic and Theurgic|The Derivation of Practice]], [[Factions, Bloodlines & Institutions/Powers and Polities/The Guild Accord/The Guild Accord|The Guild Accord]], and The Works and Days*
> *"The Accord knows what I am. My guild knows what I rank. The man across the room knows what I feel like. He is the one I worry about."*
>
> *Attributed to a retired Arbiter-Militant, identity withheld at the subject's request*

Aldith and Edony Moor of Kelderbridge were cousins who had trained in one yard under one master, and at the Hallowmark Fair the coil gave them one reading to the last decimal, the same Family and Stage, the same hot orange coat, which made theirs, the herald said, the fairest bout on the river. The herald, who was paid by the word, cried both profiles in full from the rail. Nobody cheered. The stands cracked walnuts and waited for it to be over. Then Aldith walked into the ring, and the front rows breathed out, and a baby that had been screaming since noon went quiet, and a boy on somebody's shoulders yelled "THE STILL WATER!" and the whole west stand took it up. Edony came through the east gate, and the east stand got to its feet without anyone deciding to, a fishwife began to cry and could not say why, the dogs under the benches left in a body, and the same boy, who had a future in the trade, yelled "THE STORM!" An old pie seller named Goody Rudd, who had spent the morning sitting on a bench beside each of them in turn, had put her stall and her house on Aldith, and an hour later, with half the ring on fire and the east stand down on forty broken legs, she collected them and a third of the bookie's boat. The instruments had told everyone the truth. It was the wrong truth to bet on.

The Moors had three names each, like every practitioner alive, and the crowd had only ever cared about the third. The third follows you into a tavern. The second is the one that lets a stranger hand you the goods, and since half the world has it wrong, it gets the middle of this page.

## The Three Names

> Every practitioner carries three names. They acquire them in order, they lose them in reverse order, and only one of them is real.

**The First Name · what the instrument says.** A string of measurements reading as a sentence in a language designed for ledgers. Caloria, Kinesia, Stage VIII Transcendence, Grade S, Aether III Resonant, Master, η .91. This is what the coil (the Accord's assay instrument) says when you stand inside it.
> Impersonal, diagnostic and correct. It tells you what the soul does the way a scale tells you what a thing weighs. It cannot tell you what the weight means to the person carrying it, and it is therefore the name that matters least in every room that does not contain a coil.
>
> Nobody introduces themselves by their instrument profile. The halls publish it. The factor houses price it. The person it describes has already stopped listening.
>
> The bearer has a private copy. A window (the soul reporting its numbers to its owner, in their own tongue, unseen by anyone else) shows them their Grades, percentages, bars and levels, and it leaves out the η. Everyone else needs a coil, or a Gnosis Perception that outranks the bearer's Resilience Ward by Grade, and short of that, what the room has is an estimate said aloud, which people hide and fake.
>
> It is also the one name a purse can change overnight. A Draft-bought Stage moves the ceiling, the door and the color on this line, thin underneath, since a reserve follows Level and Level follows danger, and it does nothing whatever to the third name below.

**The Second Name · what your guild will trust you with.** Initiate through Paragon. **It travels with the silver token.** It opens doors, closes borders, and tells a stranger what he may safely hand you.

The Guild Accord (a coalition of many kinds of guild across many nations: adventurers, merchants, craftsmen, alchemists, healers, couriers and more) has one thing every hall in it signs on for, and this is it: the same nine titles in every guild, each one grading trust in that trade.

Dame Ottilie Marsh, Grandmaster of the Mercers' hall at Waxgate, stands about as high as a flour barrel and was once knocked down a flight of stairs by a goose. In the spring two kings of the Hundred Crowns, at war over a bridge, handed her the crown jewels of both kingdoms on one afternoon to hold against the ransoms, in scarlet silk bags, in the middle of the battlefield, because of a bit of silver at her belt, and neither of them counted. A Stage VI captain in a coat the color of a bonfire stood over her and thought about it where his whole company could watch him think. She looked up and asked who he imagined would ever buy them from him. He went away. Her token says she may be trusted with anything short of a Mythic piece, and nowhere does it say she can throw a punch.

> Rarity is scarcity and Grade is power, and an item carries both. A merchant's title caps the first, a smith's title caps the second, and an adventurer's title and a monster's bounty read off the same T1 to T9. A courier's title says what he may be handed sealed, a healer's what she may be paid to treat, and every other hall has written its own line under the same nine words.
>
> A hall confers a title by its own lights: a term and a masterpiece among the makers, a ledger that balanced for twenty years among the merchants. A title needs no woken Crystal, and most of the merchants' Grandmasters have never so much as warmed a kettle.
>
> A forged token is a great crime in every realm that has signed, and the forger is often hanged. The title is the whole reason a stranger hands you the goods, and one forged token that gets found out makes every honest token on that road worth a little less. A title lost is a scandal of the same family: the hall strikes it, the news goes down the road faster than the owner can, and by the next market nobody will hold his coat.

The fighting guilds keep one more table. Adventurers' guilds, company soldiers and the rest who are paid to be dangerous read the title straight off the Stage table, because in that trade the thing they are trusting you with is the fight.

> So a title on a token says how good you are at your trade, and only in a fighting guild does it also say how dangerous you are. A sellsword who bows to a Grandmaster at Waxgate has made a mistake about which table he is reading, and she will sell him something for it.
>
> A bought Stage reads on a fighter's token like any other. The Stage table does not ask how you crossed. Crews ask, in the hall, before they go down a stair with you, because a commission does not care what you paid.
>
> Hollow and F confer no rank on this table. It begins at E because the halls do not register what they cannot examine. A soul whose Crystal (the soul itself, which stores Essence and channels it outward) has opened and whose stats have not yet reached E is a person the fighters' register has no row for. They drill in the yards, sell their small tricks and argue about rank in taverns all the same, and every one of them can recite this table.
>
> A Paragon is the reference standard, the thing other measurements are checked against. Among fighters it is the name an institution gives a practitioner it can no longer measure, and the Codex files it as a rank because filing it as a rank is the only alternative to admitting the apparatus has run out of scale. Among merchants it is the person whose word other markets check their own weights against.
>
> A Grandmaster who has been a Grandmaster for thirty years does not think of herself as a Grandmaster. She thinks of herself as whoever she was before the rank, plus whatever the rank cost her, and the title for that composite is a word that fits on a credential and misses everything that doesn't.

**The Third Name · what the world calls you. This is the one that matters.**

No instrument assigns it and no institution confers it. It is generated by the experience of being near you, shaped above all by the behavioral signature of your Archetype, which is the thing your soul does to the room when you walk into it. Your [[The Magic System/The Soul and the Climb/Classes and the Three Ladders|Class]], the name your soul was offered at a threshold for how you have lived, is a separate matter: it is known to you, to anyone with Gnosis Perception strong enough to read it off you, and to everyone else as far as you care to tell it. The Archetype is on everybody's skin.
>
> Two practitioners at identical Stage, Grade, Family and Method. The coil gives them the same numbers. One walks into a tavern and the fire steadies, the barman relaxes, the dog under the table puts its head down. The other walks into the same tavern and the fire spits, the barman reaches under the counter, and the dog leaves.
>
> The numbers are the same. The souls differ. The world is reading no coil. The world is reading the weather their soul makes.
>
> It is earned, imposed, or inherited, and it follows you until something bigger replaces it, or until you die and the name outlives you, in which case the name was the real thing all along and you were just the body it was wearing for a while.

## The Weather Your Soul Makes

The Archetype names what you are like to be near, whatever it is you do. Some practitioners settle a room. Some charge it. Some empty it. The Third Name comes from the pattern. (The Archetype called Harmonic has nothing to do with an Aether II Harmonic Shell or a Harmonic Crystal. The trade has been meaning to stop using that word three ways since before the Accord.)

| Archetype | What it feels like nearby | Titles it generates |
|---|---|---|
| **Harmonic** *the room settles* | **Calm**, with no comfort in it. Calm the way a well-built wall is calm: structural, impersonal, and load-bearing. Breathing steadies and thoughts clarify because the field is so coherent it functions as a local standard against which everything else calibrates. Deeply reassuring and faintly dehumanizing, because the calm does not care whether you want it | **Names of stability.** The Constant · The Standard · The Foundation · The Pillar · The Still Water. Named by what they hold rather than what they do, because what they do is hold |
| **Aberrant** *the room tightens* | **Pressure.** Pressure the way a scar is pressure: the tightness of tissue that healed under strain and never quite relaxed. People feel watched and slightly off-balance, because survival architecture does not include a setting for at ease. Animals react before people do. Children stare. Veterans recognize it immediately because they carry a version of it themselves | **Names of damage.** The Scar · The Break · The Wrong One · The Storm · The Crooked · The Remnant. Frequently unfair and never inaccurate. A warning and a respect paid simultaneously |
| **Elegiac** *the room remembers* | **Weight.** Weight the way a cemetery has weight: the accumulated presence of things no longer there, held in place by the fact that someone is still counting them. People think of their own losses unprompted. The Essence (the practitioner's own refined power) never reaches into their memories; the field's frequency matches the frequency memory occupies, and proximity tunes the listener | **Names of memory.** The Mourner · The Keeper of Names · The Gray · The Wake · The Candle. Melancholic and almost never hostile, the combination that makes other practitioners uncomfortable, because discomfort without a threat to point at has no standard response |
| **Bound** *the room belongs to something else* | **Occupancy.** The feeling that there is something else in the room that is paying attention through the practitioner rather than to them. The practitioner is the house. The thing they are bound to is the occupant | **Names of service.** The Sworn · The Chain · The Vessel · The Instrument · Urion's Scale. Produces the most politically powerful titles (a practitioner bound to an Archon, one of the fourteen great powers that embody cosmic law, is addressed by the Archon's name) and the most personally diminishing: a practitioner bound to a debt is addressed by the debt |
| **Ascendant** *the room kneels* | **Gravity.** Gravity the way a throne has gravity. People orient physically, adjust posture, lower voice and make space, from no deference at all, only the same instinct that makes a body lean downhill. The field is tilted, and the tilt has a direction, and the direction is the practitioner | **Names of elevation.** The Chosen · The Crown · The Anointed · The Radiance · The Throne. The closest thing to a holy name: a name that invokes what stands behind the person wearing it |
| **Catalytic** *the room changes* | **Productive discomfort.** The restlessness of a person who has just realized the thing they were pretending was fine is not fine. Stalled processes resume and deferred decisions present themselves, though the practitioner never attacked the equilibrium; a Catalytic field is an environment in which false equilibria cannot sustain themselves | **Names of effect.** The Teacher · The Catalyst · The Wake · The Turning · The Opener. Mu-jin is The Teacher because people near him learn whether they intended to or not. The title has nothing to say about his pedagogy. It describes the experience of being in his presence |
| **Convergent** *the room holds two truths* | **Ambiguity.** Contradictory impressions held simultaneously without resolution: warm and cold, safe and dangerous. The field oscillates at a frequency the conscious mind processes as indecision. But the frequency is stable, which rules indecision out. It is a standing wave, and the wave is the practitioner | **Names of paradox.** The Both · The Contradiction · The Scales · The Twin · The Fulcrum. The only Archetype title that routinely contains a conjunction |
| **Symphonic** *the room fills* | **Fullness.** The sensation of standing in a cathedral: less the grandeur than the acoustics, the way sound behaves in a space designed to contain more than one voice. The field is a space rather than a pressure, and the space has room for the people inside it | **Names of abundance.** The Chorus · The Many · The Deep · The Orchestra · The Cathedral. Named for the plurality, because the plurality is what the world experiences |
| **Echo-Walker** *the room is older than it was* | **Age.** Age the way an old house has age, felt in the texture of the air and the way the floors remember feet. To an Echo-Walker the dead are collaborators, and their expertise is available on terms the living practitioner negotiates in real time | **Names of ancestry.** The Ancestor · The Return · The Archive · The Lineage. The names always point backward. An Echo-Walker born into a family of the dead's record-keepers is the specific conjunction that produces Niran |
| **Monadic** *the room empties* | **Absence.** Absence the way a locked door is absence: the feeling that something is behind it and you are not being shown. People feel excluded, and they never feel rejected. Rejection is active. Exclusion is architectural. ***The soul pushes nobody away.** It simply does not include them* | **Names of closure.** The Seal · The Closed Book · The Vault · **The Ledger-Keeper** · The Silence. Cozbi's title is Monadic in origin: the name of a soul that counts everything and shares the tally with no one, and the world named the accounting because the accounting is the only part of him that ever faces outward |

> **Why the Accord fears the Monadic.** A sealed system produces no readable output. A system the instruments cannot assess is a system the Accord cannot price, and a system the Accord cannot price is a hole in the ledger.
>
> Sancta Lux considers it a doctrinal problem, because the Scale cannot weigh what will not present itself to the measurement.
>
> Common people do not call Monadic practitioners anything, because Monadic practitioners do not give them the chance.

## Where the Weather Comes From

*The Third Name has a mechanism, and it is the cheapest thing in the Crystal.*

Stage, Grade, Family and Method have no hand in the Archetype, which is why identical instrument profiles produce opposite rooms. **It is produced by Passive Traits.**

A Passive Reflection is a law stabilized by long habit and continuous exposure, always running, costing almost nothing to hold and impossible to switch off. It shows in posture, in gait, in thermoregulation, in scent, and in the ambient behavior of a room somebody has been sitting in for an hour. The trained sense a soul's power and argue over it openly enough. The Third Name is read off something else: a soul's standing bias, at the only amplitude that never stops broadcasting.

This is why the Third Name is stickier than the other two and why it cannot be renounced. A title can be surrendered and an instrument profile changes at every Threshold (the melting and rebuilding that lifts a soul to its next Stage, whether by crisis or out of a bottle). A Passive Trait is the shape of a life already lived, and it goes on expressing at the same amplitude whether the bearer is fighting, sleeping, or sitting quietly in a room full of people who have not been told who he is.
> The crowns' spy-clerks worked this out two centuries before the Archives did. Every agent's file records Passive Traits first and stat profile second, because the profile is what an enemy can only learn by fighting him and the Passive is what a stranger learns by sharing a bench.
>
> Which means the unawakened have Third Names and nothing else.
>
> Passive Traits sit in the soul's own material and require no Crystal to run. A Class Ø (never-woken) midwife who settles a room settles it for the same reason a Harmonic Grandmaster does, at a fraction of the amplitude, with no coil in her history and no token in her belt. The village named her forty years ago and the Accord has never heard of her.
>
> Every quarter is full of these names. The Still Woman at the ford. The one they send for when a child will not stop screaming. The man nobody sits beside on the wagon and nobody can say why. The folk naming tradition is reading the only layer that was ever readable from outside, which makes it, on this one axis, more accurate than the Accord's entire apparatus.
>
> The Archives has recorded the observation three times. It has never been actioned, because a system built to price output has no use for a name attached to a person who produces none.

## How Titles Are Made

> A title accretes, and nobody confers it. The world watches long enough to identify the pattern, the pattern produces a name, and the name sticks because names that accurately describe a lived experience are the stickiest things in any language.

**I · The Reputation.** It begins as gossip. The tender at Greyshaft Twelve tells the next shift that the new practitioner settled the array by walking past it, and within a month the syntax has compressed to a name, and nobody remembers who said it first because nobody did. It arrived the way weather arrives: from a direction you can point at and from a source you cannot name. Heralds cry it next and minstrels rhyme it, usually wrongly, and by then the bookies have it chalked beside the odds. Rival names fight it out for a season or two, and the most famous wins.

**II · The Confirmation.** The title survives its first test in circumstances that differ from the ones that produced it. It has crossed from description to prediction, and a title that predicts is a title that means something, and a title that means something is a title people start using in the practitioner's hearing.

**III · The Claim.** Acceptance ratifies the name. Rejection demonstrates that the name has enough weight to require rejection, which is itself a form of ratification. The only response that kills a title is indifference, and indifference is difficult to sustain toward a name that accurately describes something your soul is doing whether you choose it or not.

**IV · The Record.** It enters the Accord's filing, the guild's archive, or the crown's peerage register **and becomes a legal fact.**
> A Third Name that has survived all four stages is, for every practical purpose, the practitioner's real name, and the name their parents gave them is the alias.

## Where Your Name Places You

Harmonic titles place their bearers at the center of institutions. The Accord's entire architecture is designed by and for Harmonic practitioners, which is why the Accord works, and why it is so profoundly unsettling to everyone who does not fit inside it.

Aberrant titles place their bearers at the edge. Field assets, frontier specialists, the kind of problem-solver you send to a situation that has already destroyed the standard response. They are valued and they are unwelcome, and the distance between the two is the Aberrant practitioner's entire social experience.

Elegiac titles place their bearers apart. Sacerdotal: the spaces reserved for grief, memory, and the cultural work of holding the dead in the minds of the living.

Bound titles place their bearers below and above simultaneously. Subordinate to what holds them and superior to anyone without an equivalent endorsement: the class confusion that surrounds every temple-warrior, oath-knight and consecrated servant in the four quarters.

Ascendant titles place their bearers above. Full stop. Aristocratic in the original sense: the thing that claimed them does not claim the second-best, and the room reorganizes around that fact whether the practitioner asked it to or not.

Catalytic titles place their bearers in motion. Institutions that employ them discover the practitioner has changed the institution more than the institution has changed the practitioner.
> Monadic titles place their bearers nowhere. A Monadic title sounds like a door closing, reads like a refusal, and functions as a reminder that classification requires the classified to participate.
>
> They cannot be placed because they have not provided the information placement requires, and the world's response to a person who cannot be placed is the world's most honest confession of what placement was for in the first place, which was control.

## What the Archives Has Not Filed

**The titles the dead keep** are the one gap the Division treats as urgent, and the reason is the cutting (inscribing a working into stone or metal). A name cut into stone by an Engraver three centuries dead is still legible, still doing work, and still attached to a Third Name nobody alive can verify. The wall says who he was. There is no living instrument that can check.
> The Kharven death-houses carry the hardest version. The inscriptions name their Engravers. Several of the names belong to no lineage the seat can trace, and the Accord's position is that the records were lost. The Kharven position is that the records were taken.

**Stripped-title ceremonies** exist, are rare, and fail to do what the ceremony implies. A guild can revoke a title and strike a profile from its register. It cannot stop a room from settling when a Harmonic walks into it, and every stripped practitioner discovers within a season that the only name that survived the ceremony was the one nobody had the authority to grant.
> Still unfiled: per-kingdom title conventions, historical title lineages, inheritance and succession, and the civilian conventions by which a village names a practitioner it has decided to keep.

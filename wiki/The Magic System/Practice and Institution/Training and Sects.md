title: Training and Sects
---

*How a woken practitioner grows, who teaches them, what a sect has that money cannot buy, how they are ranked, and what fame and contest do to them. For the whole system in brief, see How Magic Works.*

The Copper Kites and the Iron Aunts of Hollin Ford have hated each other for fourteen generations, and on ranking day the whole town shuts to watch. In the year everyone still talks about, the Kites sent out an inner disciple named Perrin Dace, nineteen, in the school's burnt orange, against the Aunts' core disciple Sunniva Holm, who outweighed him by two sacks of flour and had never lost on that ground. For a quarter of the water clock she walked him down, Aunts' song going up from the east bank, and his left collarbone was already broken when he stepped inside her swing on a strange slanting half-pace, planted, and put his palm through her guard into her breastbone, and Sunniva Holm sat down in the river. The Kites went mad. And in the Aunts' box the school's grandmother, who was ninety and had not spoken in public for a decade, stood up on two sticks and asked in a voice that carried over the whole ford who had taught that boy their third step. By nightfall it was out: one winter, a kitchen boy, four silver, a copy of the Aunts' inner manual. The Kites' elders struck his name to save their own necks. The Aunts' bounty went up on every door along the river before morning. The song says twelve hunters came after him; the Aunts' accounts pay two, and record a right hand, taken at a ferry. Children on both banks still play at that half-pace in the street, and the Aunts still beat them for it.

Note what that boy paid four silver for. A Stage can be had for coin, by anyone with enough of it, and nobody on the river would have stolen a manual for something a wool merchant could buy his son. He stole a step. Training is where all of it is decided: what you can do, who owns it, and who comes for you if you carry it off. What follows is how the thing runs.

## What it is

A practitioner (a person whose Soul Crystal, which is the soul itself, is awake) grows by cultivation: breath and pulling, hard fighting, tempering, and seclusion before each Threshold (the melting down and rebuilding of the Crystal one Stage, one step of sixteen, higher). Practitioners are common, and their ranks are common knowledge. Schools, houses and sects teach them, rank them, guard their secrets and hunt their deserters, while the guilds sneer at fighters and the world cheers the great ones.

**Money buys the climb, and sects sell the rest.** Drafts and blessings can supply a Threshold's Catalyst (the particular experience a Stage demands), so a Stage can simply be [[The Magic System/The Soul and the Climb/The Ladder and the Draft — Temperance, Advancement and Essence Drafts|bought]]. Everything a fighter does with it comes from somewhere else, and for most of them that is here.

## How it looks

**Cultivation, five ways.**

| Practice | What it does | Where |
|---|---|---|
| **Breath and pulling** | Long, slow breathing that pulls Aether (the world's medium of power) from the ground and air around the practitioner into the Crystal and refines it to Essence (the practitioner's own power). Trains the pull, the measuring-out and the speed of recovery | At a Wellspring site (a place where one of the sixty kinds of Aether pools, a Core in common speech, most of them inside a [[The Bestiary/Dungeons\|dungeon]] somebody cleared), or anywhere the Aether is dense: a guild yard on rich ground, a shrine, a mountain spring. Rich ground fills you faster and thin ground starves you, and schools fight over the difference |
| **Sparring** | Drills, forms and practice bouts. Builds technique, timing and the read. Skills rise with use and show as Grades on the sheet; techniques are named things done with them | The school's yard, every day |
| **Real fights** | Hunts, duels, wars, dungeons. Barring a blessing, the only thing that raises Level (the count of how often the Crystal has densified), because only real consequence does. Level moves by danger overcome, so one kill of something stronger moves it more than a hundred weak ones, and crews argue over who got the kill | Wherever the danger is |
| **Tempering** | Melting the Crystal down and rebuilding it layer by layer, which refines Essence and makes it stronger, while the body gains Traits. Essence Drafts, rendered by alchemists from monster cores, feed it, and Traits level with it | A guarded room, and a bill |
| **Seclusion** | Months alone before a Threshold, working the self toward the Catalyst, under guard. A Draft or a blessing can stand in for the Catalyst, and those who can afford one spend the same months waiting on the melt | A sealed room, a cave, a cell under a school |

A seclusion is guarded because a practitioner deep in one is helpless, and because a Crystal near its melt is a prize to an enemy. It ends in a breakthrough, the full transformation the hall can see, or in **slag** (a remaking that set wrong and cost the soul ground), or it does not end at all.

**Each school its own basics.** There is no common curriculum. The Accord's Apprentice's Primer teaches the glyph grammar to the registered, but how to breathe, stand, pull, reinforce and strike is each school's own, and a fighter's first form tells a trained eye who taught them. A school's methods and forms are secrets worth stealing and worth killing over.

**What a sect has that money cannot buy.** A bought Stage arrives with no Release, and a sect teaches what comes after: **Releases, Domains, techniques, skills, and the rites for their Wellspring** (the governing idea that transforms how a soul uses Aether; a person may get one at a Core in a dungeon, from a school, from a Draft or blessing, or by birth, and the school's rites are how it stops being a small thing and starts, after years, to double what the soul does by nature). A Release must be found and mastered by fighting, and a sect is where someone knows what you are looking for. Anyone at Stage VIII can have a [[The Magic System/The Soul and the Climb/Domains|Domain]], their own concept made real, and the same elders know how it is coaxed out and how it kills you. None of this is on any market. It is paid for in years, duty and an oath, and the sect decides whether to take you, so every great school has a story about the rich heir who turned up at Stage VII with forty servants and was handed a broom. The cleverest houses buy the Stage and then send the child to sweep.

**Schools are clubs.** A school has colors, songs, grudges, a style you can spot at fifty paces and a following that has never thrown a punch and will fight you about it anyway. Tavern walls are chalked with who beat whom. A boy will tell you his family has been Kite since his grandmother's day, and a Kite marrying an Aunt is a matter for both priests and both elders.

**The three shared arts.** Aura coat, Pressure and sensing are taught in every yard and belong to nobody, and each culture has its own name for each and its own argument about them. In a bout-ring of the western quarters the coat is *the shine*, Pressure is *the weight* and sensing is *the nose*, and a fighter is priced by all three. A Moto house says *matoi*, the wearing, and considers it coarse to show color at table; it says *atsu* for Pressure and *kehai*, the sign of someone there, for sensing, and holds that a person who must look has already lost. The lineage halls say *hùtǐ*, *wēiyā* and *shénshí*, and have spent four centuries of commentary on which must be learned first. On a Guild roll they are reinforcement, Pressure and Gnosis Perception, and the Measurewrights who write those words will gladly explain why every other school has the order wrong.

**The ranks.**

| Who | Ranks, low to high |
|---|---|
| **Schools, houses and sects** | Outer disciple, inner disciple, core disciple, elder. A sect's grandmaster over all |
| **Guild halls** | The nine titles, Initiate to Paragon, grading trust in that trade; among the makers Apprentice still comes by the seven-year term and Master by the masterpiece |
| **Academies** | Scholar, Licentiate, Master, Doctor, Chair |
| **Fighting guilds** | The same nine titles, read off the Stage table, because a fighter's trade is the fight |

Disciples rise by **ranking contests**, set bouts held on a school's calendar, and by **the elders' word**, which can lift a disciple who lost or hold back one who won. A rank opens secrets (the next forms, the inner manuals), resources (Drafts, the bottled workings that feed growth; hours at the school's Wellspring site; a better room) and standing. It also demands duty: missions, teaching the ranks below, fighting when the school is challenged, marrying where the house says. **A defector is hunted** for what they carry out in their head and their hands.

**Guilds sneer, the world cheers.** To a guild examiner a fighter who works by voice and body is a mouth, someone who could not afford to learn to cut. That is a feud, and everyone outside the halls disagrees. Crowds pack the bout-rings, armies follow named fighters, sects court them, and children play at their techniques in the street.

**Renown, by audience.** A name is known differently to the street, the guild halls, the schools and sects, the crowns and the Accord, and the underworld, and each keeps its own account. Heralds cry the results, minstrels spread songs that get the details wrong, and bookies keep the only figures anyone trusts. Renown opens doors and draws challengers in equal measure: every young fighter with a point to prove wants to beat the person everyone has heard of. **Epithets** are mostly given by others, some of them wrong and some unkind, and they travel ahead of a fighter down the road. A fighter who names himself is laughed at until it works, and then the name is his.

**Warrants and bounties.** The guild halls post warrants: lawful, public, payable for an unlicensed high worker or a corrupted practitioner, brought in alive where the warrant says so. Sects, houses, guilds and lords post bounties: private, for what someone stole, knew or did, with terms nobody writes down. A monster that gets famous earns a name, an epithet and a bounty like anyone else, and its core is the part of the bounty worth the most.

**Tournaments.** Contests are fought in brackets by Stage, and a fighter may enter above their own Stage but never below. A fighter may **yield** at any time, by word or a hand flat on the ground. **Killing is forbidden**, and the killer forfeits and owes blood-price; it still happens, most often in the high brackets, where a full blow cannot be pulled in time. A fighter's **own power** is allowed: Traits (the soul's standing laws), techniques, releases (a Trait let loose in a learned form), [[The Magic System/Practice and Institution/Vows and Oaths|vows]] (conditions sworn for power) and a bonded weapon. **Bought power** is banned: fight Drafts, purchased tags (prepared runed strips), hired arrays (standing runed workings). A bought Stage cannot be banned, since it is the fighter's Stage for good and the coil reads it the same however it was crossed (it reads the seat differently, and no bracket reads the seat), and the crowd handles that part itself, at volume, with fruit. Prizes are resources (Drafts, a season at a Wellspring site, rights to pull at a Core, a worked Wellspring site), places (an inner-disciple seat, an academy letter, a house commission) and fame.

## What it costs

- **Seclusion:** months out of the world, a guard who must be trusted and paid, and the risk of slag.
- **Entry:** fees, a seven-year indenture, or an oath to a house or crown sworn before the first lesson.
- **What the sect teaches:** years you could have spent buying, and an oath. A rich heir pays this like everyone else, usually with worse grace.
- **Rank:** duty, and secrets that bind. The higher a disciple climbs, the more they know, and the harder they are hunted if they leave.
- **Fame:** a stream of challengers, a price on the head in some ledgers, and an epithet that may not be kind.
- **Bouts:** wounds, the Drafts drunk to recover from them, and the occasional death the rules forbid.

## How it is beaten

- **Study the school.** A taught form is a documented form. A fighter who learned a rival school's basics can read its disciples before they finish their first step.
- **Find the secluded.** A practitioner in seclusion cannot fight. Get past the guard, or bribe him.
- **Steal the manual.** A school's power is its secrets; take them and the school is ordinary. Ask Perrin Dace, if you can find him, and shake his left hand.
- **Find the bought ones.** A fighter who bought their Stage carries the ceiling, the door and the color, thin underneath, and has not done the fighting. Make them improvise, and see whether there is a Release under it.
- **Make them choose.** A disciple bound by duty can be pressed through it: challenge the school in public and someone must answer.
- **Use the yield.** In a bout, a fighter losing badly may live to fight the next season. In a feud there is no yield, and a disciple raised on bouts sometimes forgets that.

## Example

**Wil Ashdown** wakes at nine and is taken as an outer disciple of a mountain school whose basics start with breath at a cold spring before dawn. For six years he sweeps, carries water and drills the school's first forms until the elders can read his stance from the gate. At sixteen he wins two ranking bouts and loses the third, and the elders lift him to inner disciple anyway: he lost to a core disciple, and he lost well.

Inner rank opens the school's second manual and its Wellspring hours, and sends him with three others to guard a salt convoy. A real fight on that road gives him more Level than a year of sparring, and the first night's kill sets him and the other three shouting at one another about whose bolt it was. His window keeps its own count, in the hill speech he grew up with:

```
[Level: 61 to 63]
[Skill: Measure, E to D]
[Reserve: 22% ▓▓░░░░░░░░]
```

A night holding a pass beside one of the others leaves a bond between them that carries weight: a Spirit Axis (the bond with another soul that Stage IV demands). He goes into seclusion for four months under a core disciple's guard and comes out Flourishing, the hall watching his color settle.

The next spring he enters the Stage IV bracket at the river games and draws Rafe Tressilian in the final, a wool factor's son who drank his way to Flourishing at fifteen, on two Drafts that cost more than Wil's village, with the ceiling, the color and a very good coat, and a Level of about twenty under them. Neither has a Release yet. Wil has been hit before, and he has the deeper reserve by three times over. He wins a season's Drafts and a nickname he hates, and he finds a challenger waiting at the school gate the morning after, and a letter from Tressilian's father asking what the school would want for a place.

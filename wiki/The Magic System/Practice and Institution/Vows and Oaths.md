# Vows and Oaths

*How a soul binds its own odds, what the window shows, what lands when you break one, what your own denial does after that, and what you are risking when a court hands you the book*

> *IT COUNTED.*
>
> *Chalked on the river stair at Cask Row in Waxgate, on the last day of every February that has one, by persons unknown*

Maud Ferrier kept a cooperage on Cask Row in Waxgate, and on the twenty-ninth of February in the year 684, the nameless day when the law sleeps, a Sable Lancer named Ser Aymer Roake rode her brother down in the street for a joke, knowing that no court in the Concord could hear of it. She went down to the river stair that night with nobody there, no priest and no friend, a cooper's hammer in her right hand and her whole aura going up smoky red over the black water, and she bawled it at the far bank.

**"NOT ONE MORE WORD OUT OF ME TILL AYMER ROAKE IS DOWN! AND THIS HAND'S THE PRICE!"**

Her throat shut like a bung going home. Her eyes went to the middle distance, and she read it there, where nobody else ever would:

```
[Vow: "Not one more word out of me till Aymer Roake is down."]
[Bargain: Ardency one Grade up · Voice gone until then]
[Breach: right hand dead, and the Grade with it]
```

Maud Mum, the river called her, and in eleven years she became one of the great sights of the Concord. She came up through the brackets from Stage V to Stage VII in a leather apron without a battle cry, a taunt or a single call of a technique, in a sport that runs on all three, and crowds came in their thousands to hear her not say anything. She married a chandler by nodding. She had a son, and taught him his letters on a slate. Every kept morning went into her Crystal like a hoop onto a cask, and she never bought a Draft in her life.

In the summer of 695 she got Roake in the ring at the Sealhall bone arena, with forty thousand people on their feet. He had a Stage on her, and she walked him down it with the hammer, one rib at a time, until he was on one knee with his mouth full of blood and she had the swing already coming. His second knew the terms, because by then everyone did. A rail gave way in the third tier, as it had been paid to, and a boy of nine came off it above the stone.

**"TAM!"**

It landed at once, before the boy did. The red went out of her like a blown lamp, her right hand opened by itself, and the arm hung off her dead to the shoulder for the rest of her life. Roake got up. A pie seller caught the boy, and is still drinking on it. Maud Ferrier lost the bout, her jaw and nine teeth, and she would have told you, if you had asked her that night, that she would do it again.

The bill for that was fair and it was finished. The trouble came from the wording-men. Her husband's advocate pointed out that a vow sworn on Munahi, on a day no instrument binds, had surely never been a vow at all, and the Waxgate assize, asked politely, agreed that it would not enforce one. Her window said otherwise, in plain words, every morning. She took the advocate's side. For nine years she told every room in the Concord that it had not counted, that she had never meant it, that eleven years of her life had been a misunderstanding about a calendar, and that her brother had been a fool who rode too near the horses. Then the law her soul had been running since that night on the stair turned round on her. She had held her tongue for eleven years and now she could not hold it at all. She talked through meals and through her sleep, hoarse and then bleeding, for the last two years of her life, and she died in a room over the chandler's in the winter of 706, in the middle of a sentence, explaining.

Every clause of the subject is in that story, and this page takes them in order.

## At a Glance

**What it is.** A **vow** is small Fate: a person binding their own probability, spoken aloud, with a price on it. It is the shallow end of the Fate Path, and it is anyone's to make, because every soul has the layer it is made on. Fate at large is a good deal more than this, and people fight and wage wars with it; a vow is the smallest thing done on that plane and the only easy one. An **oath** is the same small Fate attached to a promise before someone with the standing to hold you to it.

**How it looks.** A line on the window with the bargain and its terms, and a price that shows on the body at once.

**What it costs.** Whatever you said. Narrower terms and a heavier forfeit buy more.

**How it is beaten.** Learn the terms and arrange the morning on which keeping them costs more than the fighter can pay.

**Example.** Hob Garrow, a Stage VI caravan guard on a pass road, swears aloud that he will not take a step back. His technique climbs to the top of its Grade, his [[The Magic System/Power in Use/Stacking and Spending|stack line]] counts it toward the cap, and the raider across from him now knows exactly what to try.

## What a Vow Is

A person says aloud what they will do or will not do, and what it costs them if they fail. Fate attaches. The [[The Magic System/The Soul and the Climb/The Trait System Law, Function and the Forge|Essence Core]] takes the restriction up as a standing law, and from then on the odds around that person lean, by a small and exact amount, toward its being kept. A vow is the only part of the Plane of Fate that needs no bloodline, no Stage and no permission. Real Fate Arts are rare and mostly run in the blood. Everybody has a mouth.

**It is made on the Attraction Layer.** That is the part of the Soul Crystal that holds a soul's own share of fate, its bonds, oaths, claims and odds, and every soul has one whatever Path it walks, the unwoken included. A hitter who swears is working the same plane as a nun who swears, with less practice. The Fate Path is what you call it when a soul has learned to lean on that layer: there the binding bites harder and a kept vow pays more, and everybody else makes do with what a mouth and a price can buy. The Path goes far past promises, into the odds of a blow and the hour an army arrives, at a price no vow has ever charged, and [[The Magic System/The Soul and the Climb/The Three Paths Routing, Recognition and the Gate|The Three Paths]] has that side of it.

**It needs no witness.** The stair at Cask Row was empty. The Continuum (the whole lawful order of reality) is the audience, and it does not need to be told twice. A witness is for the person who wants to be held to it, or wants the crowd to know, and the priest Sigrun Ox-Hand swore in front of at Cullen Fair was there to make it a better show.

**It must be spoken.** A thing resolved in the privacy of your heart is an intention, and intentions have never moved anything on a window. The words go out and the line arrives.

**It is anyone's.** The unwoken make them too, and a baker who swears on her mother's grave never to serve stale bread gets a line on her little sheet and a small and real lean in the way her mornings go. Most of what any of them buy is measured in luck and loaves. A practitioner's buys more, because a practitioner has more for the odds to act on.

**The bargain.** The law is the old one, and it is short: the narrower the condition and the heavier the forfeit, the greater the return. *"Against those who deserve it"* buys nothing. *"Only against the Sable Lancers, and if I break it this hand is gone"* is a vow.

| What you stake | What it buys |
|---|---|
| A condition, sworn aloud | Up to the top of the working's Grade band |
| A great vow, staking life, a limb or Crystal mass | One full Grade |
| Your secret: the working's true mechanism, stated aloud in the target's hearing before you use it | Every advantage of surprise goes, and the output rises to match. It is the one sound reason for a villain's monologue |

**Each vow counts on the stack.** Vows are self-boosts, so every one counts against the two-Grade cap, read from the sustainable Grade on the card. See [[The Magic System/Power in Use/Stacking and Spending|Stacking and Spending]]. They do one thing nothing else in a fighter's own hands does: a vow, or a breakthrough, is the only way across a gap of two Stages, and it still shows in the room first.

**A kept vow pays twice.** Tempering Coherence is the match between a person's stated law and how they live, and it is the Crystal's honest scale at every [[The Magic System/The Soul and the Climb/The Sixteen Stages|Threshold]]. A vow kept buys that with conduct and not with money, so it is the cheapest advancement in the world, priced in the rest of your life. It clusters where Drafts do not reach: soldiers, hedge-workers, the orders and the poor. It is also the most reliable way ever found to grow a [[The Magic System/The Soul and the Climb/The Three Paths Routing, Recognition and the Gate|Fate Path]], since Hyeon Do-sun sitting at the fourth gate at Kell Harbor was a vow being kept, daily, for seven years, in front of a Crystal that was counting.

**Enough of them is a Path.** The Crystal goes by what a life has been spent on, and it does not ask what you thought you were doing. A hitter with three great vows and twenty years on them may read as having Walked the Fate Path on an honest assay, three parts in ten of her practice gone down that road, and she gets the force bonus and the rough seat that go with two roads, having never once intended either. Plenty of old soldiers discover this at a Threshold.

The filing clerks keep two drawers for all this. On the Unified Taxonomy a vow's working is **Votia**, a working whose currency is a declared price, and the seal it sets on the soul is **Claustra**, the only seal whose anchor is the bearer's own behavior. See [[The Magic System/Reference and Craft/The Magical Categories — Unified Taxonomy|The Magical Categories]].

## The Window

A vow shows its terms, in full, in your own tongue, because you wrote them.

```
[Vow: "This hand stays down until he is dead."]
[Bargain: Ardency one Grade up · Left hand dead until then]
[Breach: lands at once]
```

That is the difference between a vow and a [[The Magic System/Practice and Institution/Blessings and Curses|blessing]], and it is the whole of the difference in what the wording-men charge. A blessing's line was written by somebody else and may lie. A vow's line is the one honest contract you will ever sign, and if it turns out badly, the author is available for comment. It stays until it is kept:

```
[Vow: kept. Bargain ended.]
```

## The Breach and the Break

The old books run these together, and a good many people have died of the confusion.

**A breach lands at once, by its own terms.** It takes no witness, no court, no enemy and no ill will. The moment the terms are broken the forfeit arrives, in the same beat, and the bargain ends with it. The window reports it after the blow:

```
[Vow: broken]
[Right hand: dead. Will not answer.]
[Ardency: down a Grade]
```

Being forced does not help, and neither does a good reason. Maud Ferrier shouted for a falling child and it took the arm all the same. A breach is a debt paid. A great many people have paid one, lived on for decades with a dead hand, and owed the universe nothing more.

**A Shear Break comes after, and only from denial.** The Core has been running a law, and the law was built out of something true. A Shear Break is what happens when the bearer repeatedly denies the truth behind it: says it did not count, says it was never meant, lives as though the thing the vow was made from had never been so. The Trait grown out of that truth collapses, inverts or turns on the person carrying it. See [[The Magic System/The Soul and the Climb/The Trait System Law, Function and the Forge|The Trait System]]. It is the commonest death for someone who has changed their mind about who they are and not told their soul.

So the orders do not treat a lapsed vow as a disciplinary matter, or not at first. They put the person in a quiet room with somebody who knew them before, get them to say out loud that they broke it and why, and only then ask who is to be punished. It is regarded as an emergency, and the good infirmarians will leave a supper to go and do it.

**What a vow can stand.** The Resilience Sub-Stat called **Oath** measures how well an active vow or contract holds against someone trying to sever, invert or weaponize it. Almost nobody places points there before the first time it happens.

## Oaths and the Book

An **oath** is a vow with an audience that can bill you. A proper court, a sect, a crown or a hold makes you swear before it, and two things then bind you: the law, which has its own penalties, and small Fate, which attaches to the words whether or not anyone is watching. A soul that swears falsely under a proper oath pays on its own window.

Fate can be set on an oath by someone who knows how. A Fate Path master can [[The Magic System/Practice and Institution/Blessings and Curses|attach terms to one]] so that it holds with nobody present to enforce it, and the old Moto and Kharven bindings that made a clan's word structural were works of that kind, some of them lost. Most oaths are plainer. A Marrowkin Cask-Oath runs "while the brew lives", sung over the barrel, and a Marrowkin who cannot say that word over a thing has not promised it. A Kharven swears by the hand that cut it, a moment before something gets thrown.

### The sworn sheet

Nobody can see your [[The Magic System/The Soul and the Climb/Windows and the Sheet|window]], so hiring takes proof and a court takes your word, on oath. A proper court makes you swear to what your sheet shows. If you lie, the cost lands where only you can read it:

```
[Oath: "Level 140, as my sheet shows." Sworn false.]
[Terms: every chance goes against you until it is unsaid.]
```

Only an assay can check what you said, and it reads numbers and says nothing of your intentions. So the lawyers eat well.

- **The wording governs.** *As my sheet shows* and *as I am* are different oaths, and half the advocates on Wording Row have a favorite.
- **A lying line protects you.** A person who swears honestly to a blessing that lies, or to a window a Limina user has tampered with, has told the truth as far as they knew it. The current reading at Waxgate is that you are bound to your own understanding of the words, which is why a wording-man will tell you never to read your line too closely before a hearing.
- **Unsaying it works.** The terms run until the lie is taken back, in front of the same bench. This is rare, and the fines are heavy, and there are people who have paid them with tears of relief.

## The Paper, the Book and the Word

Three things get called a promise, and they fail differently.

| | Who holds you | Breaking it | On the nameless day |
|---|---|---|---|
| **An instrument** (a contract, a charter, a bond, a seal) | The law | Fines, forfeits, the bailiff, and a judgment | Void. The chancery has no date-form for it, and this has been tested twice |
| **An oath** before a court or a house | The law and small Fate | Both | No court will hear it. Fate was not asked to take the day off |
| **A vow** | Nobody but the soul | Lands at once, by its own terms | Holds |

**Munahi is the day the law sleeps and Fate does not.** On February 29, every fourth year, no instrument binds, no debt falls due, and nobody can be summoned or sealed. See [[Cosmology & Metaphysics/Ages and Reckoning/The Sky, the Hour and the Year|The Sky, the Hour and the Year]]. Every quarter has its customs for that day and few of them are pious. A vow spoken on it holds exactly as it would on any other morning, and each leap year some young fool stands up in a tavern, swears something ruinous on the grounds that it does not count, and reads his window.

## How It Is Beaten

Every vow can be answered, and the price of knowing one is usually a round of drinks.

- **Learn the terms.** Fighters bluff about their vows and lie about them. A vow that is a matter of public record is a loaded weapon pointed at its owner.
- **Make them choose.** Maneuvering a sworn fighter into breaking their own word is legal everywhere, universally despised, and has ended more careers than any duel. Ser Aymer Roake died rich, and nobody ever ate with him again.
- **Fight what it left out.** A vow binds exactly what it says. *No step back* says nothing about the step sideways, or the step you were knocked.
- **Wait.** Some vows have a clock, a sunset, a season, and the price arrives when it runs.
- **Press the denial.** If a fighter has broken one and will not say so, you need not lay a hand on them. Mention it, in company, every chance you get. It is the cruelest thing on this page.

## Famous Vows

*Told in taverns, and told wrong. I have checked what can be checked.*

**The Ox at Cullen Fair.** Sigrun Ox-Hand swore she would not set the ox down till the sun went behind the church, having looked at the sun first. It took four minutes and nearly killed her, and the priest has told it at every wedding since. See [[The Magic System/Power in Use/Stacking and Spending|Stacking and Spending]].

**The Fourth Gate.** Hyeon Do-sun swore at nineteen that he would walk through Kell Harbor's gate on the day his brother's ship came home. He let his mother die to keep it, One-Ear Pask refused to pay on it, and the Crystal entered it as Fate. See [[The Magic System/The Soul and the Climb/The Three Paths Routing, Recognition and the Gate|The Three Paths]].

**Old Gammer Pell.** At the sack of Ostley a very small woman told a Free Company captain she would not die in that house. He set fire to it, with some ceremony. She was found the next morning in the road outside, burned to the waist, having crawled two hundred yards to be right, and she lived another eleven days to enjoy it.

## What They Call It

| Who | The word | The argument |
|---|---|---|
| **A Guild roll, a Measurewright** | A vow, a binding, Votia | It is a price stated in advance, and the books can carry it |
| **Northern commons, crews** | Swearing it, being sworn | Did he say it out loud? Then he meant it. Stay out of his way |
| **Moto** | *Seiyaku* (誓約), the sworn pledge | Sworn onto the ground, and the ground takes it. A person's word is entered, and the register has no eraser |
| **Marrowkin** | *Kurme*, the swearing itself | There is no lighter word. A Marrowkin who says it has already done the thing, in the only grammar that counts |
| **Kharven** | Giving your word before witnesses | A word is the one thing a poor man can hold against a rich one, and everybody knows what it is worth by the winter |
| **The lineage halls** | *Shìyuē* (誓約), the oath-bond | It binds the line as well as the mouth, and your grandchildren can be sued |
| **The faiths** | A vow, a sacrament, a penance | Each has its own answer to who was listening. Sancta Lux claims jurisdiction over every oath sworn inside its reach, and it keeps very good files |

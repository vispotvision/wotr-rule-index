# The Working Vocabulary — Trade Jargon and Its Metaphysical Extensions

> *"Read every book in the Guild and you will still be caught by the first man who has burned his thumb on the thing."* Goody Sharrock, assay-mistress of Wick Cross

The man calling himself Journeyman Tobit Lamb lasted four months at the Wick Cross assay house. He had the Latin perfect. He had the licenses, the stained apron and a very good forged seal, and he had the Guild's cupel ledgers halfway copied for whoever was paying him, and then one payday at the Gilded Ladle, with eighteen assayers around him and the third round on the table, somebody told the old story of the Abbot's button and he laughed in the right place and said, "Aye, and then the flash."

Shiin.

No assayer on earth has ever called it the flash. Eighteen brass forks came up off the table. Lamb was a good deal stronger than anyone had been told, a Stage IV (fourth on the soul's sixteen-step ladder) with arc-blue Fulguria (lightning) crackling down both arms, and he put five of them through the front wall of the Gilded Ladle, *bzzzt-KRASH*, in a shower of painted plaster and spilled small beer, while the whole of Assay Street came out to bet on it. He left by the roof, and he left his left ear and a season's copying on the taproom floor, and Goody Sharrock (who had taken the ear, with a pair of cupel tongs) had it pickled and set on the shelf by the scales, where it has been shown to every apprentice since. Nobody there had been fooled by anything but his paper. The word caught him.

I collect these words the way other Measurewrights (the Guild's sworn measurers) collect grudges, and this page is the collection.

## Why This Exists

The canonical term list runs to four hundred and thirty-four entries and every one of them is machinery: Grades, Stages, Wellsprings, Sub-Stats, Crystal tiers. That vocabulary names the system, and it may stand on the page beside the effect it names, but it places nobody by trade or class.

What a scene actually needs is the other layer. **What a lamp-warden calls a wick. What an assayer calls the flash. What a glasshouse calls a flaw.** Those words do three things at once: they place a speaker by trade and class, they make the world feel worked rather than described, and they give the metaphysics somewhere to borrow from that is not the Codex.

## The Method

Every entry below moves through the same three steps, and the third is where the world gets built.

**One. Take it whole.** The trade exists in WOTR, so the term exists unchanged. A wick is a wick. This costs nothing and buys texture immediately.

**Two. Extend it.** The trade's word is applied to the metaphysical case by the people who work both, because that is how technical vocabulary actually spreads. Practitioners never coined a word for the moment a Crystal's grade declares itself, because the assayer's was lying there on the bench.

**Three. Invert it.** The word turns up in cant, meaning something adjacent and usually worse. This is the layer that makes a vocabulary feel lived in rather than compiled, and it ties directly to the doublet on the Common page.

**The discipline that keeps this honest.** The extended sense describes *what happened*. An assayer's word for a flash is a word for a flash, and the word itself explains nothing about the Crystal. The explaining comes right after, complete, from a mouth or from the narration, and the word is what the speaker reached for first.

## I. The Pull

Nobody who does it for a living talks about refining Aether. A practitioner hauls it in off the ground around them the way a boat hauls a net, and then the Soul Crystal turns the catch into Essence and the Shell drives it out, and the vocabulary was borrowed from everyone who ever took something out of the world by hand: bargemen and fishers, smiths at the bellows, miners on a seam, farmers arguing about soil. The daily crafts that live off it, lamp-wardens on their rounds, ward-cutters at their doorframes, hedge-workers at their hedges, add words of their own, because they are paid by the job and every one of those words has been shouted across a bill.

| Term | Trade sense | WOTR extension | Cant |
|---|---|---|---|
| **the pull** | one heave on a line, the bargeman's and the net-hauler's unit of work | taking Aether in from the environment toward the Crystal; *a long pull* is a slow filling on poor ground | *a man's pull* is who will come when he sends for them |
| **the haul** | what comes up in the net | the raw Aether of one pull, before the Crystal has refined it | *a short haul* is a job that paid less than it cost |
| **bellows** | the leather lungs of a forge | the breath-work that sets the rhythm of a pull; every school teaches its own count and sings it at the others | *all bellows* is a loud man with no fire under him |
| **tuyere** | the clay nozzle where the blast enters the fire | the place in the body a school teaches you to feel the pull come in: the breastbone, the soles, the nape of the neck. Measurewrights say it makes no difference and the schools have broken each other's windows over it for centuries | — |
| **fat ground**, **lean ground** | farmers' words for soil | rich and thin ground, which scholars call high and low Aetheric Density; fat ground fills you faster and lean ground starves you | *fat ground* is any soft position; *on lean ground* is broke and still fighting |
| **seam** | the run of ore a miner follows | a line of rich ground running through poor; where a commander goes when he means to shrink somebody's reach | *a good seam* is a man's main source of income |
| **gleaning** | gathering what the reapers left in the field | pulling the thin leavings of poor ground; where the unhoused and the unregistered work from | *a gleaner* lives off other people's scraps |
| **bled** | drained by the barber's lancet or the butcher's knife | ground pulled empty by a long fight or a greedy crew, and slow to fill | *bled* is squeezed dry by a creditor |
| **salting** | salt worked into a field so that nothing will grow | ground treated by hired hedge-workers so that Aether will not pool on it, usually the night before somebody else's fight | *salted* is spoiled on purpose: a market, a witness, a marriage |
| **banking** | heaping ash over a fire to hold it low till morning | what a Core's keepers do when they close a worked Wellspring site; it goes on recharging and nobody can pull from it | *banked* is shut out, ruined, out of the trade |
| **the round** | a watchman's walk through the night | the lamp-warden's paid route, street by street and door by door | *off the round* is on strike; *a dark street* is one that did not pay |
| **trimming** | cutting a wick back so it burns clean | re-cutting the worn rune on a lamp, which is a warden's whole living | *trimmed* is overcharged, and every household believes it has been |
| **dead wick** | a wick still in the lamp with nothing left to burn | a runed lamp still stamped, still billed for, and giving nothing; common across any Withering-era district | *a dead wick* is a man still on the books who does no work |
| **tally** | a notched stick split down its length, each party keeping half so neither can cheat | the job-stick between a ward-cutter and the client: how many cuts, what price, whose wall | *a short tally* is a robbery that leaves no damage |
| **pleaching** | half-cutting a hedge's stems and weaving them over so they live | the hedge-worker's trade of laying a small ward or charm through living growth | *pleached* is cut halfway through and still on his feet |
| **muffling** | wrapping a bell's clapper in leather for a funeral | a coat or a Pressure held in so that it makes no show; tiring work, and everyone can tell you are doing it | *muffled* is deadened, kept quiet |
| **sweating** | warming sealing wax over a lamp until the seal lifts whole | a Guild seal opened and closed without trace | *sweating a seal* is forging one |
| **tundish** | a funnel for filling a cask, set so the pour can be *seen* | the small brass funnel on every Drafter's bench; the fitting whose entire purpose is that a short measure cannot be hidden | *the tundish* is any arrangement designed to make a thing undeniable |

**The one to steal first is the tundish.** It is a funnel whose only function is that somebody can see what is going into the cask. A fitting that exists to prevent concealment. In a world where the Accord taxes the bottle and a great many Drafters would prefer it did not know how much they are pouring, the tundish is the object every argument in a Dark Ages street happens over.

## II. The Assay

The fire assay is the trade that determines what a thing actually is by destroying part of it, and it has supplied practitioner diagnosis with most of its working vocabulary.

The real process: a sliver of the sample is wrapped in lead, sometimes pre-treated by **scorification** in lead foil, and heated in a porous **cupel** of bone ash inside a **muffle** furnace. The lead oxidizes to **litharge**, which the cupel absorbs, carrying the base metals with it. What is left is a **bead**, or **button**, of the precious metal. **Parting** in acid separates silver from gold, and comparing weights gives the **fineness**.

| Term | Trade sense | WOTR extension | Cant |
|---|---|---|---|
| **the assay** | destructive determination of true content | any procedure that establishes what a practitioner actually is, as against what they are registered as | *assayed* is found out |
| **cupel** | the porous bone-ash dish that drinks the lead | the diagnostic bench, and by extension the examiner's room | *in the cupel* is under investigation |
| **litharge** | the lead oxide that carries the base metal away | what an assay takes off a Crystal and what the practitioner is left short by afterward | — |
| **scorification** | pre-treatment before the assay proper | the preliminary questioning before an examination | *scorified* is softened up |
| **the button** | the residue after cupellation | what remains of a man's capacity when everything unearned has been burned off | *a small button* is a man with less to him than his reputation |
| **fineness** | proportion of precious metal in the alloy | the ratio of a practitioner's real Coherence to their claimed standing | — |
| **parting** | separating two metals that alloyed together | separating a bloodline trait from a trained one; contested and rarely clean | — |
| **proof** | the mark struck on tested metal, armor, glass | the Guild proof-mark; a proofed working, a proofed breastplate, dented by a crossbow at the armorer's door | *proofed* is vouched for |
| **touchstone** | the dark stone a sample is rubbed against for a rapid, non-destructive read | any quick field test that gives an approximate answer without opening anything | *the touch* is a rough estimate |
| **slag**, **scoria** | the waste glass floating on the melt | Residue after a working; what a workshop's sweepers collect | *slag* is an insult and needs no explanation: a man who *came out slag* took a Dead Set |
| **feathering** | working the cupel carefully near the end | handling a diagnosis at the point it could still go wrong | — |

### The blick

**The single best word available and it is free.**

As a cupellation completes, the last of the molten litharge runs across the surface of the bead and throws a rainbow play of color. The color dulls for an instant. Then there is a bright flash, called the **blick**, caused by the release of latent heat as the bead reaches its solidification point. The assayer watches for it because it is the moment the test finishes and the answer exists.

It transfers directly and it transfers without explaining anything.

**The blick is the instant a Crystal's real grade declares itself**: the color running, the dull beat, the flash. Examiners watch for it. Practitioners dread it, because a man can lie about his Stage in every document he signs and cannot lie during the second the blick takes. There is a phrase for a career that ended in one, and there is a phrase for a man who has never sat for one.

**And it is a scene first.** What lands on the page is the color, the dulling, and the flash, and the assayer's word for the flash is a word for a flash. Why the Crystal does that comes right after, in a Measurewright's mouth or in the narration, and three Measurewrights will give you three accounts, two of them wrong and all of them loud.

## III. The Glass

Glasshouse vocabulary supplies the language of Draft vessels, lens work, and the physical condition of a Soul Crystal, and it supplies it better than any other trade because glass has the same three properties: it is worked hot, it holds stress invisibly, and it fails at the flaw you did not see.

| Term | Trade sense | WOTR extension | Cant |
|---|---|---|---|
| **the metal** | molten glass, as glassmen call it | a Crystal in a worked state | — |
| **gather** | the mass of hot glass taken up on the rod | a pull taken and held before it is shaped | *a gather* is a crowd about to become a problem |
| **marver** | the polished slab glass is rolled on, to shape it and to control its temperature | the bench a Drafter works a vessel on | *marvered* is smoothed over, made presentable |
| **pontil**, and the **pontil scar** | the rod that holds the work, and the mark left where it was broken off | the point at which a working was held during its making; readable afterward by anyone who knows to look | *the scar* is the tell that gives a forger away |
| **blank** | a cooled object still needing forming | a working prepared but not finished; a Draft vessel not yet charged | *a blank* is a man with no craft |
| **annealing** | slow, controlled cooling that relieves invisible stress | what a Crystal requires after a Threshold, and what most practitioners skimp | — |
| **lehr** | the long oven a piece travels through as it anneals | the enforced rest after an ascension, weeks long, deeply resented | *in the lehr* is laid up, out of action |
| **seed** | fine bubbles left in the metal | small permanent inclusions in a Crystal; common, mostly harmless, always noted | — |
| **cord** | striations from an unevenly mixed melt | structural striation across a Crystal, and the thing an examiner looks for before anything else | *corded* is inconsistent, unreliable |
| **frit** | ground glass applied to add color | fragments of a broken Crystal, and the trade in them | *frit* is powder, and buying frit is buying something that came off a corpse |
| **encasing** | a clear layer laid over a decorated core, which magnifies it | a working laid over another to amplify it | — |
| **fuming** | metal vaporized over hot glass, depositing color | residue laid down on a vessel by proximity to a working | — |
| **flaring** | opening out the end of a tube | widening a channel past its intended bore | — |
| **battledore** | the flat wooden paddle used to true a base | the flat tool that levels a poured vessel | — |

**Annealing is the concept with the most story in it.** A piece that is cooled too fast looks perfect and carries stress it cannot show you, and it fails later, without warning, at the flaw nobody could see. That is a description of a practitioner who climbed a Stage and went straight back to work, and it does not require a single word of metaphysical explanation to land.

## IV. Where the Contempt Lives

Every column above has a cant entry or is missing one, and the pattern is worth naming: **the words that invert are the words that touch money, standing, or whether a man is any good.** *Banked. Trimmed. Slag. A small button. A blank. Corded. Assayed.* Nobody has made a cant sense out of *tuyere* or *battledore*, because nobody has ever needed to insult a man by comparing him to one.

This is the same engine as the doublet on the Common page. The Latin column is what the examiner says. The trade column is what the tradesman says. The cant column is what is said about a man when he has left the room, and it is almost always drawn from the trade rather than the Latin, because contempt is specific and Latin is not.

## Next Passes

Domains that would yield as well and have not been mined yet, in the order I would take them.

**Bell-founding and acoustics**, for *Ars Cantus*. Bells are tuned on their partials, and a bell that will not come into tune is called false. A choir vocabulary built on strike note, hum, and falseness would give Chantcraft the technical layer it currently lacks.

**Quarrying and banker masonry**, for Runecraft. The bed, the grain, quarry sap, spalling, dressing, the reveal. A stone has a direction and a rune cut across it fails, which makes inscription a craft with real failure modes that anyone who has split a paving slab can follow.

**Surveying and the chain**, for Domain radius, boundary disputes and the Bench.

**Rope, rigging and cordage**, for Attraction Force and bonds, which currently reach for a bank of metaphors with nothing under them.

**Tanning and the blood trades**, which already have a research document on the Guild's shelves and have never been mined for vocabulary.

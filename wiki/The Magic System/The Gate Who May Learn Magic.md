# The Gate: Who May Learn Magic

> Nearly everyone is born with the organ. Almost nobody is taught to use it, and in the Imperial Age that is no longer an accident.

### I. A Crystal in Nearly Everyone

**Nearly every person alive carries a Soul Crystal from birth**, the porter and the laundress as surely as the heir of a great house. A few are born without one. The rest carry a **Dormant Crystal**: sealed and silent, present in full, and doing nothing a coil can read.
A dormant Crystal stays dormant for life unless somebody wakes it. Waking is taught. It takes a teacher, a regimen and a place to practise, and a Crystal nobody attends to simply goes on sleeping while the soul around it refines by the Weathering and hardens into whatever its work made it. **That is Class Ø, and it is most of the world.**
A small number stir without being taught. A child raised on a bad main in a crowded quarter wakes at an odd age; a child with a strong Inherited mark shows it before the first winter. These are the ones the screening seasons find, because screening reads a Crystal that has already stirred and returns nothing on one that has not.
> **So what stands between a practitioner and a porter is almost always the lesson.**
> 
> *Every crown of the Imperial Age has understood this, and the Imperial Age is when they began to lock the lesson away.*
The Path gates on a Sub-Stat decide how far a woken Crystal may climb. This gate comes first, and decides whether it wakes at all.

### II. The Lock

For most of reckoned time teaching passed through households, orders and masters, and a crown cared what its practitioners did rather than how they learned. **The Imperial Age changed the arithmetic.** A practitioner is artillery. A nation that can teach its own subjects and refuse everyone else's has a standing advantage no treaty gives it, and a nation that lets anyone learn anything has handed that advantage to whoever is paying.
So every nation now keeps a **schedule**: which kinds of people may learn which magics, from whom, and under what oath. The schedules differ from crown to crown and they are the most jealously kept documents in any chancery. Three powers hold the keys between them.
**The Accord** controls theurgic training, issues the Magister credential and funds the academies. *The Guild made the system. The Guild owns the gate.* Inside a signed circle the Accord's examination is the licence; outside one each crown and each hall keeps its own.
**The crowns** decide who among their subjects may enter an academy at all, appoint the Mage Royal and the crown chairs, and write the schedule.
**Sancta Lux** credentials nobody and has no need to: it declares what lies on the far side of the gate consecrated or profane, and that is often enough to close it.
> **The Bench's power of reservation is the lock in its purest form.** A formula entered and reserved is lawful, real and forbidden to anyone below a stated Tier. *It arranges who is permitted to be capable, and denies nothing to anyone while it does.*

### III. The Five Gates

| Gate | Who passes | Who holds it |
|---|---|---|
| **Ability** | A Crystal that stirs unbidden and is noticed. Recruited to an academy or sought out by a guild | Screening officers, academy recruiters, guild scouts |
| **Birth** | The children of houses that can pay for a teacher, a fee and seven years | Great houses, academies, the companies' freedom |
| **Nation** | The crown's own subjects, sworn before the first lesson | Crowns and their chanceries |
| **Licence** | Whoever sits the examination and passes it | Academies, the Accord, the halls |
| **Bloodline** | The blood, and nobody else | The archaic bloodlines and the lesser houses that copy them |

**Ability.** Screening is seasonal and it is looking for a stirring Crystal. A child who shows one is noticed, and a noticed child is recruited. **The Aetherion Academy takes its entrants by letter and recommendation; the Ward-Carver's Academy at Senri's Gate by an entrance Proving.** Hunting guilds send their own scouts to the same screenings and bid for the same children, and a family with a stirring child in a poor quarter receives more offers in a season than in the rest of its life.
**Birth.** An academy charges fees, and the companies grant the freedom by patrimony, servitude or redemption. **A great house reads its infants for Inherited marks inside the first month** and knows by the first winter what it has. A commoner's child reaches a teacher by indenture, seven years for the cutting, bound to a master the formulary limits in how many he may hold. *Class is decided at the door of the schoolroom long before it is decided anywhere else.*
**Nation.** A crown teaches its own. The oath comes before the first lesson, and a foreign student learns what the treaty between the two crowns allows and nothing more. **Vaeloris, the Carver's Seat of Ketsuen**, develops and standardises techniques for the kingdom's own use, and a technique standardised there belongs to the kingdom before it belongs to anyone who learned it.
**Licence.** The academy ranks run Scholar, Licentiate, Master, Doctor and Chair, and **the Licentiate is the qualification that matters**: permission to practise for hire. Past it lie the Accord's credentials. A **Magister** holds an academy credential and a documented training record. A **Registered Natural** learned by other means and submits to monitoring instead: periodic reassessment, demonstration, disclosure of every new harmonisation.
**Bloodline.** The archaic bloodlines teach their arts to the blood and to nobody else, and lesser houses imitate them. A bloodline art taught outside the line is theft in the eyes of the house and, in most schedules, of the crown as well.

### IV. What Needs No Leave

The schedules watch three things: **who teaches, who takes pay, and high working.** They do not watch what a woken soul does at home with what it already knows.
A woman who woke as a girl and warms her own kettle breaks no law. **Teaching the neighbour's son does. Charging for it does.** Taking pay for a working or a hunt without a guild licence does, and is common anyway, because unlicensed hunters are cheap and there are always more postings than licensed parties. High working without a licence is the gravest offence the schedules name, and each schedule defines it by Tier and by Craft. **The Divine Language sits inside every definition.**

### V. How the Lock Is Kept

**Controlled texts.** Primers, grammars and glyph tables are printed under licence, numbered, and issued against a registration. The **Apprentice's Primer** reaches a new practitioner the day they are entered and not before. **Teaching the Divine Language without sanction** is a Law of Severance for anyone sworn to the Codex, and a crown offence under most schedules.
**Crystal registration.** A stirred Crystal is entered on the roll of the place it was found. **The Bureau of Strategic Assessment** manages registration and cares only whether an output has been observed and assessed. Those who refuse are entered anyway, as **Unregistered Naturals**, on a separate list that is never published.
**Inspectors and trace examiners.** Nearly every working leaves traces. A ward that was cut carries the cutter's hand; a Draft that was poured carries a maker's mark. **A trace examiner reads those traces as evidence**, and a good one can name the teaching lineage behind a technique more often than not, which is how an unlicensed school is usually found: through its pupils' mistakes. The Board inspector hears the draw, and an unlicensed working on a main is audible to anyone listening at the wrong hour.
**The Night Watch.** A crown office, older than the Accord's circles, that investigates illegal magic and phenomena the law has no name for. The Night Watch works alongside the guilds and answers to the state, which is the whole of its difference from a hall.
**The Inquisitors.** Sancta Lux keeps a lateral office, the **Inquisitor**, authorised to pronounce sentence in the field without an Arbiter present, and it sends them after unconsecrated high working wherever the Church holds standing. **The Holy Inquisition**, the splinter that took the name, claims the same work with no standing at all and prosecutes it by fire.
**The guilds.** Locally, the halls police their own ground. A hall that finds an unlicensed practitioner working its district posts him on its board, and an **Extermination** Commission against a corrupted practitioner is a lawful posting under the five classes. Inside a signed circle, Enforcement's **Sealwrights** take the cases no hall will.

### VI. Hire and Jurisdiction

A licensed practitioner works for pay as a **hired hunter**, a **works inscriber**, a **company soldier**, or in **private practice**, and the licence is what separates each of those trades from a crime.
When a crime is done with a working, it is proved by the trace examiners, and then **the guild courts and the crown courts fight over who hears it.** The guild court claims the case because the accused holds a guild licence, or because the act was a working. The crown court claims it because the body lies on crown land. Which of them wins differs by place, and in most places it is decided by whoever has the accused first.

### VII. Those Who Learn Anyway

**Black-market teaching.** A back room, a fee, a stolen or copied primer, and a teacher who was licensed once. The **kind readers** already sell a reading of what a person is with nothing forwarded to the Accord, and some of them sell the next lesson too.
**The Mother.** A cult, named for the mother of vinegar, the living culture that sours whatever it is added to. It recruits across the Gate: its cells are called cultures, its recruits are inoculated, and what it teaches them no schedule would allow.
**Self-derived workings.** A thaumaturge who finds a native axis alone can work on it without ever having been taught, and some do. The Bureau watches for them with particular care. Self-derivation in the textual Crafts is nearly impossible, which is why the schedules guard the texts hardest.
**The guild loophole.** Hunting guilds train their own. A hall indentures a boy under its own charter, wakes him on its own floor and teaches him what a hunter needs, and the state's gate never sees him until he is registered as a finished hunter. **Crowns hate this and every crown relies on it**, because the halls are what stand between a district and the things that come out of the Wells.
**The speaking.** The speaking has never been licensed and the Accord has never found a way to license it, because a spoken working has no author in its own grammar. Liability falls on whoever paid. *A speaker needs no substrate, no workshop and no seven years, and when he is finished there is nothing to point at.*
**The penalties.** A small offence costs an amercement to the offender's means. Unlicensed hire costs the tools and the fee. **Unlicensed high working is punished hard**: branding in the Accord cities, and **Crystal sealing**, in which a Sealwright closes a woken Crystal against the soul that carries it, the way a Board man seals a main. A sealed practitioner is Class Ø again in everything except memory.

### VIII. How the Common Folk See It

Ordinary people know there is a gate and roughly where it stands. They know the screening season comes, that a stirring child is either a fortune or a danger to the household, and that a ranked practitioner walking down their street got through something they were never offered. **The awe they feel is partly for the power and partly for the door.**
> *Some mothers hide a stirring child from the screening, because an entered child belongs to the roll. Others walk one three days to the nearest academy and wait at the gate until someone reads him.*

### IX. Writing It

**Every practitioner on the page came through a gate, and it shows.** Which one, who paid, and what was sworn are part of the character, the same way a trade or a birthplace is.
**The gate is paperwork.** Forms, stamps, a roll, a numbered primer, a clerk who can refuse. The drama is at the desk more often than in the field.
**Every porter in the room carries a sleeping Crystal.** Nothing in the scene needs to say so, and the reader should never be allowed to forget that the line between the porter and the practitioner was drawn by somebody.

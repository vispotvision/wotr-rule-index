*How a practitioner turns their own Trait loose, in a form the whole room can see. For the whole system in brief, see How Magic Works.*

The Widow Ansa Brack kept the Three Keys at Sallow Ferry for thirty-one years, and the song says she never once raised her voice before the night the Tollgate Free Company came in without paying. The song is wrong, as songs usually are, because the whole town knew that voice. A hundred and forty men in red-and-yellow quarters filled her yard with their horses and their opinions, Bald Harry Stott leaned on her doorpost and told her what he thought the ale was worth, and she came out onto the step with a slop pail and said what she had said at closing every night of her married life.

**"EVERYBODY OUT."**

Her hands went first, black as old door iron to the wrist. The ring of keys at her belt rose and stood straight out on the air, jangling, and her aura slammed to its full color, a silver shot through with deep violet that lit every face in the yard from underneath. The crowd has always called that one Last Orders. Then one of the keys turned, by itself, in nothing.

The light went violet from wall to wall, and every man in that yard felt the ground decide he had no business standing on it. They left. They left through the gate and through the hedge and, in nine cases, through the side of the stable, in one long bright spill of armor and horses that came down across the river meadow and broke four dozen collarbones. The Company's sergeant landed with his boots still crossed and the handle of a tankard in his fist. Bald Harry Stott, who had a good grip, went with the rest and left his hand on the doorpost, where it stayed, and it hung over the bar for the next sixty years. That was the Second, Closing Time, and the odds-men at Sallow Ferry paid on how far the sergeant flew for a generation.

The bill came with the law, as the big ones do. A landlady is the last one out of a house she closes, and Ansa Brack closed that one with herself on the step. She never set foot inside the Three Keys again. She stood on that step for nineteen years and served through the window, and the minstrels gave her a Third Release she never had, an error that has cost several people money.

A child can say the whole of it: she tells you to leave, and you leave. What follows is how a Guild Measurewright says it, at somewhat greater length.

## What it is

A release is a practitioner's Primary Trait (the deepest standing law in their soul) let off the safety limit of the Soul Crystal (the soul itself, in its working form) on purpose, in a shape the Crystal has learned to hold: the same force as an overchannel (forcing the Crystal past its safe ceiling), rehearsed until it keeps a form, a price and a name. A technique is something the practitioner makes with the Trait; a release changes the practitioner, and every technique fired inside it runs through the released Trait.

**One idea, three sizes.** Trait, Release and Domain are a single concept escalating. The Trait is the idea, the Release is the idea worn on a transformed body, and the Domain is the world obeying it. A kit that reads as three unrelated powers has been filed wrong, and any Measurewright will tell you so for free. See [[The Magic System/The Soul and the Climb/The Trait System Law, Function and the Forge|The Trait System]] for where the idea comes from.

## How it looks

![[Assets/Diagrams/release-forms.svg]]

**The three releases.** Every release follows one pattern: the body changes, the aura flares, the sound comes, and the Trait's power rises.

| | First Release | Second Release | Third Release |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Earliest Stage** (step on the soul's sixteen-step ladder) | IV, Flourishing | VII, Refraction | IX, Invocation |
| **The look** | One signature change to the body or the weapon, in any shape the soul takes: skin, eyes, hair, wings, armor, a weapon's new form | The change spreads into the ground around them: a field where light, air and heat obey the Trait, and everyone inside feels the color shift | The field takes a second color at its edges, the Wellspring's own (the type of Aether their Crystal is tuned to, the governing idea that changes how their soul uses its power, with its own alchemical character and its own law), and the Wellspring's voice joins the sound |
| **The power** | The Trait strikes at the top of its Grade (its letter band of force) | Inside the field, one full Grade higher | Inside the field, the Trait's stated effect becomes local law, two Grades over its base, for that effect alone |
| **The reach** | The body and what it touches | A field the size of the room or yard it stands in | A field that can hold other fields inside it |
| **The sound** | Its own, called once: a bellows drawn hard, a struck bell, a crack of ice | The sound held as a standing note | The Wellspring's note beneath the owner's |

**The form is free.** A release takes whatever shape the person's soul would take: wings, plate, a changed blade, a beast's outline, a crown of something that should not burn. A Vectoria (force) practitioner may release as a white crane. What the form does still comes from the Trait and its Family (the branch of physics it works), so the crane's wings strike with plain Newtonian force.

**The aura.** A release drives the aura to the practitioner's full color at once: their Family's hue at their Stage's clarity. At Stage IV that is a smoky flare, charcoal with the hue clouded through it; at Stage VIII it lights the room in its own color.

**The Domain.** The Second Release opens and powers the [[The Magic System/The Soul and the Climb/Domains|Domain]] (a space where the practitioner's inner law outranks the world's). The flicker of a seed that carries a soul into Stage VII is a one-time burst of the Trait and needs no Release; the Second is what lets a seed be called and held. At Stage VII the field is a seed: it lasts minutes and collapses if attention breaks. From Stage VIII it is a true Domain, grown by living and called aloud, held for hours and steady under small disruptions, and anyone at Stage VIII can have one, their own concept made real, with the fame, rivalries and bounties that follow. The Stage grants it, and a Grade never does. The Catalyst into Stage IX is a first flicker of a Realm on the same terms, and the Third Release makes one, a field that can hold other Domains inside it. From Stage XI the First Release no longer needs calling: it is simply how they are.

**When fields meet.** Stage comes first: a field two Stages up simply overwrites the lower one. Otherwise two release fields, or a field and another's Pressure (the weight a strong soul puts on a room), contest the ground by Dominion (the stat of authority over ground and presence) before a blow lands. The border shows in the room as light changing color across the floor, bystanders feel both, and the weaker side starts the fight already paying. A read can flip it: find what the field rests on, the owner's focus, its edge, or the thing they are guarding, press there, and the border moves back.

**The window.** The Crystal is the soul, and it reports to its owner. When a release fires, the owner's window (the soul's report of its own numbers, seen by nobody else) fills with the release's name and what changed, and a timer bar starts to drain. Grades, percentages and bars only: the exact figures stay with the assayers.

```
[FIRST RELEASE: KILN SKIN]
[Heat workings: top of Grade]
[Forge heat on every blow landed · bonded hammer carries its share]
[Time: ██████████ full]
[Reserve: 96%]
```

Nobody else sees it, so the man across from you is left to read the skin, the shimmer and the breath, and he has been known to read them wrong.

**The bonded weapon.** A weapon its owner has carried for years can carry part of the release: the blade burns with the owner's heat, the staff holds the edge of the field. Its part is written on the owner's card, and is lost with the weapon.

**Called or silent.** The owner chooses. Called, the release's name goes out as it fires, in its owner's own tongue, with the meaning landing in the same beat through a witness or a knowing second. A showman bawls it in capitals and a cold killer may barely breathe it, and either way the call costs a beat, buys the full rise, and is a tell everyone within earshot can use. Silent, it costs no beat and gives no spoken tell, but it still flares and still sounds, and it comes in weaker: the form without the call's rise. The card says exactly what silence costs each release.

**Who carries them.** Any practitioner with a woken Crystal and an armed Primary Trait; a Class Ø (sealed, unwoken) soul carries Traits but cannot release them. How many a person has varies and the card sets it: one, two, three, and for a rare few more, no further release opening below Stage XI. Most who fight their way to Stage IV earn a First Release in time. Far fewer earn a Second. A Third is the mark of a figure the ballads already know. A Stage bought in a Draft or granted in a blessing opens the door and nothing past it, so a rich Stage IX may have no release at all while a poor fighter at the same Stage has all three, and there is steady money for bookies in knowing which is which.

**What they are called.** The Archives say First, Second and Third Release. Every people has its own street words for the three, as it does for the Stages: bracket crowds in the Common tongue call them going big, going loud and going home, the last because a certain number do not. A person's own art may count its tiers in its own word, Acts, Verses, Knots, Entries, so that the count itself becomes a threat, and there is no more unpleasant sentence in a duel than "that was the First Knot." Bloodline faculties release too, by the bloodline's own law and stages, kept as a family secret and written on the bloodline's page.

**How one is learned.** A release must be found and mastered by fighting, and no sum of money has ever changed that. A bought Stage comes without one and no purse opens one. A sect can teach the road to it, and that is much of what a sect is for: the drills, the rites for its Wellspring, the sparring ladder, and elders who will hurt you in the right places. Like the Trait, the shape itself is survived into being, and the shape that comes is the fighter's own, by one of three roads. **Crisis** is the commonest road to a First Release: the Trait breaks its limit in a fight the practitioner should have lost, and the Crystal keeps the shape. **Seclusion** is the commonest road to a Second: months working the Trait alone on what the last fights showed, until it claims the ground around them, and then back out to prove it against someone who means it, because a field that has never been contested will not hold. **Tribulation** is how every Third arrives: the Wellspring's own Aether comes down on the practitioner and tests the claim before it agrees to share the law, and not everyone survives the test. The first time a release holds, it prints on the Crystal, and the transformation is the kind people remember seeing. Mastery comes after that, in bout after bout, as the time on the bar lengthens and the price shrinks toward what the card says.

**It deepens with the person.** A release keeps its core for life but grows new aspects as its owner advances, survives and changes, and a great turn in a life can reshape it. Each new aspect is added to the card when it first shows.

## What it costs

**The shape spreads the strain.** Because the release is a learned shape, the Crystal carries it without structural damage, provided it is held inside its limit. Past the limit it is a raw overchannel again.

| | First Release | Second Release | Third Release |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Drain** | Set on each card and written as time from full: how long it holds if started on a full reserve (the store of Essence, their own power, that they carry). One person's First Release may sip; another's may burn like a Third | Set on the card | Set on the card |
| **The limit** | Ends on its own when the reserve falls to a tenth. Below that is Essence Starvation: sagging stats, a degrading Shell (the Crystal's outer layer), flickering Traits | The same | The same |
| **Afterwards** | Spent and shaking; the pain the fight was shrugging off arrives | The Crystal runs Overgrown (swollen and spilling) until a full night's sleep: power spills, and the owner's moods stain the air around them. No lasting harm | Overgrown for a day and a night, and an ache through the body like the end of a fever. No lasting harm |
| **Held past the limit** | A plain overchannel, with its fracture risk | A Crystal Fracture Event (a crack in the Crystal) | The Trait itself may fail, in a way the card names |

- **The call** costs a beat every time.
- **Its own price.** A big release also charges in the coin of its own idea, on top of reserve and body tells, and the card says how. A landlady ends up on the step. A heat release blisters the hands that carry it.
- **Outside a fight** a release may be held at the same cost: a First Release worn through a court audience to make a point, a Second spread over a winter camp to keep the snow off. The clock runs the same.
- **On the stack** a release counts toward the cap of two Grades over the sustainable Grade on the card, and a Third already stands at it for its one effect, so nothing more stacks there. Only outside help goes past the cap, and a blessing counts as outside help, a point Measurewrights have settled several times.

## How it is beaten

Every release can be answered by someone at its own Stage, before, during and after.

- **Before.** A called release costs a beat, and the aura flares and the sound comes before the power rises. A fighter who reads the tell can strike into that beat, or get out of reach. A silent release skips the beat but still flares, still sounds, and comes in weaker.
- **During.** The clock is the counter, and it runs on a bar only the owner can see. A release spends reserve fast and ends at a tenth, so outlasting it is a real plan: keep distance, make them chase, and fight on ground that raises their cost: cold and wet against heat, and salted, bled or thin ground (ground with little Aether to pull) against anyone, since a reserve refills only from what the ground can give. A Second Release at Stage VII collapses when attention breaks: a wound, a shout, a name that matters to them. Every field has an edge; step out of it. Contest the ground with a stronger Dominion, or read what holds it up. Take the bonded weapon and its part of the release goes with it.
- **After.** The aftermath is a window. A First Release leaves them spent; a Second and a Third leave the Crystal spilling and easy to read.

This is how a lower-Stage fighter beats a higher one across one Stage: survive the release, read it, and strike in the aftermath.

## Example

**Corra Vell** is a Stage VII hired hunter harmonized to Pyraeon (a Caloria, or heat, Wellspring), with a heat Primary Trait. Her color is Caloria's deep ember-orange at Refraction's clarity: gray and orange sharing one field without mixing. She has fought eleven years with the same forge hammer, and it is bonded. She found Kiln Skin in a bad fight in a culvert in her fifth year, and Smelter's Yard over one winter of hard contracts and a hard teacher. Her card carries two releases:

> **KILN SKIN** *(First Release)*. She says "Kiln Skin" and her skin goes the dull cherry-orange of iron at forging heat, from the hands up over the forearms to the collarbones, and the air around her shimmers like the air over a forge bed. There is a sound like a bellows drawn once, hard. Every blow she lands carries forge heat into the wound, and her heat workings strike at the top of their Grade. Her bonded hammer takes the heat with her: its head glows the same cherry-orange and keeps it, and the forge heat in her hammer blows is the hammer's share, lost if the hammer is. From a full reserve it holds about six minutes, and it ends on its own when her reserve falls to a tenth; afterwards her palms are blistered and she cannot pull heat for an hour. Released silently, it takes the color and the shimmer but not the call's rise, and her heat workings strike at the middle of their Grade, not the top. The counter is in the beat of the call, which can be struck into, in cold and wet ground, which makes every second cost more, and in the blistered hands afterwards, which cannot hold a weapon properly.

> **SMELTER'S YARD** *(Second Release)*. She says "Smelter's Yard" and the heat leaves her skin and fills the ground around her, about the size of a courtyard: the air goes forge-hot and dry, the light turns orange, and everyone inside feels the color change as a pressure behind the eyes. The bellows sound becomes a low, steady roar for as long as it holds. This is her Domain seed, and inside it her heat workings strike one full Grade higher. From a full reserve it holds about three minutes, and it collapses at once if her attention breaks; afterwards her Crystal runs Overgrown until she sleeps, and heat leaks off her with her temper. Released silently, the yard forms without its first roar and her workings strike at the top of their own Grade inside it, not one higher. The counter is to break her focus with a wound, a shout or a name that matters, to step out past the edge of the yard, to contest its ground with a stronger Dominion, and to strike while her spilling Crystal is easy to read.

On a dockside job Corra meets a Stage VII Fulguria (lightning) duelist who opens his own field, a ring of arc-blue that makes every hair in the yard stand up. She answers with Smelter's Yard, and the two fields meet in a seam across the cobbles where orange light and blue light grind against each other. His Dominion is higher, and the seam starts walking toward her. Then she reads him: his field is centered on his left hand, which never moves from his belt. She throws the hammer at it. He flinches to guard the hand, his attention breaks, and his field goes out like a doused lamp, with the slap of hot air and flying cobbles that every collapse pays, a Resonance Shockwave of the small kind, which puts him on his back. The orange floods the yard to the water's edge. Only she can see the rest of it:

```
[SECOND RELEASE: SMELTER'S YARD]
[Heat workings: one Grade up]
[Field: holding · his has collapsed]
[Time: ████░░░░░░ about a minute left]
[Reserve: 47%]
```

She is going to need her hammer back.

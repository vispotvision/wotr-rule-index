# The Moto Bloodline — Material Culture

> **The dress of the house reads the way the house reads: an outsider's formal law laid over a native people's practical toughness, never fully assimilating into either.** *Kharven is Concord-stock northern clans governed by a bloodline that arrived, enforced, and did not assimilate. A Moto in full dress carries the severity of both and the softness of neither.*
*Canon ruling, 2026-09-12. Answers the open item on Moto material culture; the Visual Aesthetic Guide has no separate Moto pass — this page is it.*
**The crown-line pattern is six things worn together: red enamel over dark steel, a beast-face boss, a knotwork belt, a fur mantle, a robe-over-harness silhouette, and a split between court dress and campaign dress that never collapses into one outfit with the accessories taken off.** The pattern in full belongs to Kōkan. The other six Great Lines carry it in their own metal and palette, set out below.

---

### The Six Elements

**Silhouette — robe over harness** · A fitted war-harness of leather and articulated steel, cut close and low-profile the way a working soldier's kit has to be, worn **underneath** a robe or long over-coat that is open enough at the front and sides to show the harness beneath it. *Magistrate and combatant at once, and neither is a costume over the other. A Moto strike ranks, condemns or stabilizes as readily as it hits; the robe is "judge" laid over "soldier."*
**Palette — red enamel over dark steel** · Enamel is glass fused onto metal by firing: it does not chip or fade the way paint does, and it is slow and costly to apply, which is why it reads as rank rather than decoration. Dark steel is steel blackened on purpose, past the ordinary quenched-dark finish of the Concord baseline — *iron someone chose to make black, not iron the world has worn dark.* **Deep red fired over near-black steel is the crown-line's pattern, and it repeats at human scale what the Ashen Crown already wears: garnet set into black-worked iron.**
**The beast-face boss** · A boss is the raised medallion at the centre of a shield, a belt clasp or a shoulder clasp — the one fitting that is permitted to be a picture rather than hardware. On a Moto it is worked as a beast's face in the same near-black steel as the harness, carved shadow with at most a single garnet or red-enamel eye. **Which beast marks the line before any badge is read or any name spoken; the crown-line's is the lion or the dragon.** It sits at the belt clasp in daily and court dress and moves to the shoulder or a pauldron rivet on campaign, where a belt ornament would catch on gear.
**The knotwork belt** · Interlacing lines with no visible beginning or end — the ornament of the native Kharven clans on belts, sword-fittings and grave-markers, and not Moto in origin at all. **The one element on a Moto that is not Moto: the ruling house visibly wearing a piece of the people it governs and never assimilated into.** Inherited worn leather or tooled metal, never gaudy, sitting low at the hip beneath the robe's opening where the boss also sits. *Always a specific belt tied to a specific alliance, oath or northern-born mother — earned or married in, never generic northern flavour.*
**The fur mantle** · A sleeveless outer layer draped over the shoulders and open down the front. Heavy northern fur worn over the robe at court, reduced to a collar or a lining in the field. *Climate work first — Kharven has an eight-month heating season — and status work second: an unbroken pelt, even colour and no patchwork mark rank by wholeness, the way the Concord baseline marks a lord's coat.* Muken's Crown Mantle of Kharven and the wide fur collar of Sodoku's Field Coat are the two forms already on record.

---

### Court and Campaign

> **Two priority sets wearing the same lineage markers. Never one outfit with the accessories added or taken off.**
**At court** · The full robe-over-harness silhouette as intended: the robe open enough to prove the harness's quality, heavy with fur, dark steel and red enamel at throat, belt and shoulder. The knotwork belt is the tooled, clean piece. The boss is polished enough to be read across a hall. *Every piece is a claim being made — sovereign authority inscribed rather than decorated, as the Ashen Crown itself is, and ornament that always costs the wearer something.*
**On campaign** · The robe comes off, or survives as a weathered field version — mud-hemmed, iron-thread seams doing structural work rather than decorative work, the fur reduced to a collar for warmth. The boss leaves the belt for a shoulder rivet or pauldron, out of the way of gear. The belt, if worn at all, is the plain working version. *The precedents are Artemis Amagiri Moto's Passbreaker Mantle, an indigo over-robe with weathered campaign sigils, and Sodoku's Field Coat: heavy, dark, wide fur collar, iron-thread seams, replaced twice.* **Campaign dress is survival and function wearing the markers out of necessity, not court dress with the shine turned down.**

### The Seven Lines

**Only Kōkan holds a seat. The other six Great Lines are landless since the western seat's fall, and their material culture is portable, worn-on-the-body wealth rather than the fixed authority Kōkan expresses through a literal crown and a literal seat. Each of the six carries the pattern in its own metal and palette: Shirogane in bright silver, Akagane in copper and red-bronze, Kurenai in black and ash with the crimson hidden, Byakuya in near-white, Tenrai in gold and banner-heraldry, Amagiri in indigo and mist-grey.**
| Line | Dress |
|---|---|
| **Kōkan** *(crown-line)* | Red enamel over near-black steel; gold or violet-toned metallic accents echoing the line's own metallic hair-sheen; lion or dragon boss. **The only line entitled to the full court presentation without it reading as a claim on the throne.** |
| **Shirogane** *(Silver)* | Trim, buckles and boss in bright, hard silver rather than blackened steel. *Harder-edged and colder than the crown-line's palette — the dress of merciless structural punishment.* |
| **Akagane** *(Copper)* | Copper and red-bronze fittings, no enamel. *A warmer, rawer red than Kōkan's fired finish — exposed metal rather than a jeweller's work, for a line whose arts run to blood and heart.* |
| **Kurenai** | Named for crimson, dressed in unrelieved black and ash-grey; the crimson kept to a single hidden accent, a lining or a thread, and never shown. *The contradiction is worn on purpose: a visual pun on a line whose whole function is punitive soul-law and execution.* |
| **Byakuya** | Pale, near-white materials and a clean, almost ecclesiastical cut. *Glass, veils and halos — neither heavy fur nor dark steel.* |
| **Tenrai** | Gold and imperial-toned trim, and actual banner-heraldry in place of a boss. *War-command dress for the line whose ambition for the throne is the kingdom's founding wound.* |
| **Amagiri** *(Mist)* | Indigo and mist-grey, lighter and less armoured-looking than the other lines. *Route-reading and escape, not frontal command; the Passbreaker Mantle is the precedent.* |

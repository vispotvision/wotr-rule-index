## Chapter Seven, Movement I (revised) · The White Wolf

---

Naevra found him on the flat stone above the gate.

It was the old reading-stone, the one their father had used for the midwinter Thread-Reading, worn smooth as a river pebble by a thousand years of Yukari knees. It stood at the top of the enclave's east steps, where the cedars drew back and the whole of the valley lay open below: the long grey ribbon of the stream, the terraced fields gone pale with frost, the smoke of the village going up straight in the still morning air. Naiser was sitting on it cross-legged, in the black founder's coat, with the katana across his knees and the hood up over his bare nape, looking east. He had been there since before the light.

She came up the steps barefoot on the cold stone, the way she always did, with a satchel of letters under one arm and her white plait down her back, and stopped two steps below him.

"Brother."

He did not turn round. "You've had crows."

"I've had six." She came up the last two steps and stood beside him on the stone, looking out where he was looking. "Two from Sum-gol. One from the Marrow crossing. One from the reader we keep in the salt market at Ironlink. Two from the north." She paused. "One of those two had the Stark wolf on it."

He held out his hand, without looking at her, and she put the letters in it.

He read them in silence. She watched him read. It had always been one of the stranger pleasures of her life, watching her brother read: his face did nothing at all, nothing whatever, and the thread-marks at his temples did everything. They brightened and dimmed under the skin as his eyes went down the page, silver going violet and back to silver, like a pulse. She had learned to read them years ago, long before she could read his face. She read them now.

When he had finished, he folded the letters together again very neatly and laid them on the stone beside his knee.

"So," Naevra said. "It seems a band of young men was capable of taking Kharven's Foot after all."

"It seems the Stark answered," said Naiser.

He said it quietly, almost to himself. A month ago he had sent a crow north to Ulf Stark, in answer to a letter about the Kessen road, promising the Yukari would remember who stood with them if the road stayed open. He had sent it the way a man drops a coin in a shrine on his way past. He had not expected the coin to be heard.

Naevra shook her head.

"It's more than that," she said. "You've read it. You know it's more than that." She crouched beside him on the stone, and her silver-white eyes went a shade toward lavender. "It isn't only that the Foot fell. It's who brought it down. The Marrow traders are calling him the Tokenless. Muken of the Lanternless, a free company out of Halcrest. Sixteen years old. And from every report I've had, and I've had them from four directions now, a member of the Kōkan line is still out there in the world, alive." She held his eyes. "And he put Shigure Tenrai Moto on her knees on the stair of her own keep."

The stream went on below them. Somewhere in the cedars a crow called and was answered.

Naiser was quiet for a long moment.

"The Kōkan were wiped out," he said. "By Gimbzo. By his own hand, most of them. That's what the house has always believed and what every report we ever had confirmed. They were the oldest line the Moto had, and the proudest, and most of them would not follow him. They saw where his greed was taking the house and they said so, in the hall, to his face." His voice was very level. "So he had them killed. Every one he could find. The ones who spoke against him first, and then their children, and then their cousins, and then anyone who'd ever married into them. The Kurenai closed the register on what was left." He looked at the folded letters by his knee. "And you're telling me there's a Kōkan still walking the world. After all of that. After everything they lost."

Naevra nodded.

"Not only that," she said. "The council of the Moto has split." She sat back on her heels. "Kagenori called the seven lines to the Hall of Verdicts the day Shigure rode in. Four chairs were filled. Three weren't. And the three who wouldn't come are on the road." She tilted her head toward the west. "Coming here. As we speak."

Naiser turned his head and looked at her for the first time.

"Here," he said.

"Here."

He looked back out over the valley. She watched the thread-marks at his temples brighten, and stay bright.

He did not like it. She could feel exactly how much he did not like it, through the threads, the way you feel the cold coming off a river before you reach the bank. He had made his peace with the Zettari at the low table a week ago, and the Sovereign of Kushara had not yet left the valley; he and his son had ridden out at first light with their panthers to see the Yukari villages, and would be back by noon. And now Moto lords were coming down the western road toward the same table, a month after the Zettari and the Moto had finished killing each other, to sit in the same hall with the prince who had killed Gimbzo with his bare hands.

Naiser had never wanted to stand between those two houses. He had wanted the war ended and the seam mended and his people fed. He had not wanted to become the post that two great bloodlines tied their quarrel to.

And he did not want blood on this ground. Not here. Not on the oldest stone of the house.

"Is it too late to stop them?" he said.

---

Naevra stood up. She put her hands behind her back, the way she did when she was about to say something she had already decided he needed to hear, and looked down the valley toward the west.

"You need not worry," she said. "Not about that. Not yet."

"Naevra."

"Kiyome Byakuya is among them."

He went still.

"She's leading them," Naevra said. "It's her party, by every account. She refused Kagenori's council outright. Twice. She hasn't been to the Hall of Verdicts since the night they took Torvald Stark's head, and the reader at Ironlink says she stood in the hall that night and refused to sign for it, alone, against five." Her voice softened, very slightly. "You know her name. You've heard it from me before. Kiyome has always been the one of them willing to make peace, no matter what came before. Through the whole of the war. When nobody else in the seven lines would so much as receive our envoys, Kiyome Byakuya received them, and gave them tea, and sent them home alive."

"That's one Moto."

"It's not only her." Naevra drew a breath. "I've heard rumours that Hayato Amagiri is riding with her. And Tetsugen Akagane."

Naiser turned his head sharply. "The Akagane?"

"The Akagane."

"Tetsugen voted for the Stark's head. He voted for every one of Gimbzo's burnings in the war."

"He did," said Naevra. "And now he's on the western road with Kiyome Byakuya, three days out, with twenty men and no banners. Riding toward us. Away from his own house." She looked down at him. "I don't know what happened to him in that hall, brother. But something did."

Naiser was silent.

"That's the part that frightens me," Naevra said, more quietly. "Not that they're coming. That they've *chosen.* Kiyome's taking her whole house with her, as far as I can tell. She's turning the Byakuya toward peace, toward us, out from under Kagenori's hand, in front of the whole Moto hold. The Akagane and the Amagiri are going with her." Her hands tightened behind her back. "Kagenori won't let that stand. He can't. There will be a price for what she's doing, and I don't think it'll be Kiyome who pays it first. I think it'll be the people she left behind in that white house of hers."

The wind came up the valley off the stream, cold, smelling of cedar and wet stone. Naiser's hood stirred against his bare nape.

"No," he said slowly. "He won't stand for it."

He picked up the katana from his knees and laid it on the stone beside him, very carefully, with its edge toward himself.

"Kagenori believes the Tenrai are the true heirs of the north," he said. "He always has. It's the oldest grievance in that house. They've wanted the throne since before there was a throne to want. For a thousand years the Kōkan sat in the red chair and the Tenrai sat beside it in gold, waiting, and when Gimbzo killed the Kōkan, the Tenrai thought the waiting was over at last." He looked out over the valley. "And now a Kōkan boy has walked out of the snow and taken their fortress on the Kessen road. Kagenori will burn half the Inner World before he lets that boy succeed him. He'll do anything. He'll buy the north out from under the Starks. He'll set his own lines on each other. He'll come for Kiyome and her people and he'll come for anyone who gives them a roof." He paused. "Including us."

"Including us," Naevra agreed.

"And the boy doesn't know," Naiser said.

She looked at him.

"I'd wager everything this house owns on it," he said. "That boy and whoever's beside him. They don't know what they are. Neither of them. They can't." He was quiet for a moment. "If they knew, they'd never have let him stand at the front of a Stark vanguard with his eye open in front of a Tenrai captain. Nobody who knew what that eye meant would ever let it be seen."

---

He was silent for a long time after that. Naevra did not interrupt him. She stood beside him on the reading-stone with her hands behind her back and her eyes on the valley, and let him think, the way their father had always let him think: by being there, and saying nothing, and waiting.

"Muken," Naiser said at last. He said the name slowly, as if he were tasting it. "The Tokenless."

"A sellsword."

"A northerner," said Naiser. "Raised in the salt under Halcrest, the Ironlink reader says. Running with a free company out of the chain-cities since he was fourteen. The Lanternless." Something moved at the corner of his mouth. "There's a name for you. A company with no light, and a boy with no token, and a house with no name the clerks would take." He shook his head. "How peculiar. How very peculiar. That the last of the Kōkan, the line that sat in the red chair for a thousand years and ranked every outcome in the world by sovereign law, should come back to the world from a place like that. From the very bottom of everything. From a place where nobody has ever had the luxury of anything but staying alive."

He looked out at the valley, and the thread-marks at his temples had begun, very faintly, to glow.

"Perhaps that's the point," he said.

Naevra said nothing.

"I told the Sovereign at our table that I'd seen something coming," Naiser said. "Something great. Something with no face." He lifted his hand and touched his own temple, lightly, with two fingers, where the threads ran. "I've been sitting on this stone since before the light trying to see it again. And this morning, for the first time, I think I saw the shape of it." He was quiet for a moment. "Not a face. Not yet. A figure, in the snow, with something white about it. Standing at the head of a great many people. Northerners, and men of the Moto lines, and some I didn't know. All of them standing together. Under one banner." He let his hand fall. "I think it's him, Naevra. I think this may be the one I saw. The one who will guide the north. And the one who might, at last, after all of it, bring the Moto house back together into one."

"Unify the Moto," said Naevra softly. "The seven lines."

"The seven lines and the one blood." Naiser's mouth twisted, very slightly. "They've been saying it for a thousand years. It's only been true twice."

He turned his head and looked at her.

"You know the tales," he said. "The two ages."

"The Age of the Owl," said Naevra. "And the Age of the Fox."

"The only two times in the whole history of that house that the Moto ever knew peace among themselves. Real peace. A generation of it, each time." He looked back out at the valley. "The tales of the Owl say that he had the wisdom of all the ages. That he could sit in the Hall of Verdicts and look at a quarrel between two lines that had been going on for three hundred years and see, in an afternoon, the one thing each side needed to be able to stop. And give it to them." He paused. "And the tales of the Fox say that he knew how to make the people happy. The farmers and the salt-cutters and the soldiers' widows. They say the Fox could make a whole province happier than he could ever make his own family." His voice went quieter. "They say it's what broke him in the end. That he gave everything he had to the people and had nothing left to give to the ones at his own table."

Naevra was very still.

"Brother," she said. "Do you know which lines they came from? The Owl and the Fox?"

He looked at her.

"The Owl was Byakuya," said Naevra Yukari. "The near-white line. *The Owl, the White Fox, the Heron.* It's carved over the door of their house. And the Fox was Akagane. *The Fox, the Red Wolf.*" She looked west, down the valley, toward the road. "Kiyome Byakuya and Tetsugen Akagane, Naiser. The Owl and the Fox. They're riding to our gate together."

Naiser did not say anything for a long time.

Then, slowly, he began to smile. It was not much of a smile. But it was the first she had seen on his face since the night of the funerals, and it reached his eyes.

"And the Kōkan," he said, "were always the Lion. The red chair. The lion on the crest." He shook his head. "But the boy wasn't raised among lions. He was raised in the snow, by Starks, among wolves. They'll have the wolf on his banner before the year's out, whether he wants it or not." The smile widened, very slightly. "And his hair's gone white, the Ironlink reader says. At sixteen."

He looked back out over the valley toward the north, past the frost-pale fields and the cedars and the far blue line of the mountains, toward a broken wall on a ridge he had never seen.

"The Owl," said Naiser Yukari. "The Fox. Perhaps it's time for the rise of the White Wolf."

---

Naevra grinned.

She could not help it. It came up out of her all at once, the way it had when they were children and he said something grand and solemn at the dinner table and she had to hide her face in her sleeve. She knew exactly how much weight was on what he had just said. She knew what it would mean if he was right, and what it would cost, and who would pay. She knew all of it. She grinned anyway.

"Perhaps," she said. "Perhaps, Lord of the Yukari. But before you go crowning wolves in the snow, you should know that the Sovereign of Kushara and his son should be riding back through the south gate any moment now. They went to see the lower villages at first light." She tilted her head toward the valley floor, where, far down among the frost-white terraces, two great black shapes were moving along the river road at a long, easy, unhurried lope, and the sun was flashing on something gold. "There. You see?"

He saw.

"And when they come through that gate," Naevra said, "they're going to sit down at your table and drink your tea, and some time before dinner one of their own crows is going to land on the guest-hall roof with the news that three Moto lords are three days out on the western road. And that a Kōkan has taken the Foot." Her grin faded, a little. "And they'll want to know why they had to hear it from a bird."

Naiser looked at the two black shapes on the river road for a long moment.

Then he stood up from the reading-stone, and picked up the katana, and put it through his sash. He reached back and pushed the hood off his head, and let the cold morning air come onto his bare nape, where the hair had been, where anyone who looked could see it.

"Then we'll be honest with our visitors," he said. "Before the bird. We'll tell them the truth, all of it. That the Moto are coming to this house to talk of peace. That they're coming because their own house has split, and that the half of it coming here is the half that wants an end to the killing." He picked up the folded letters from the stone and put them inside his coat, over his heart. "And we'll tell them about Muken. The Tokenless. What he did at the Foot, and what we think he is."

"All of it?"

"All of it." He started down the steps. "The Sovereign told me at our table that he'd rather be told plainly. I promised him plainness. I'll not start breaking promises to the Zettari in the same week I made them." He paused on the third step and looked back at her. "And Naevra."

"Yes, brother?"

"When the Byakuya come," said Naiser, "I want them met at the western gate. Not by the house-guard. By you."

He went on down the steps toward the south gate, in the black coat, with his nape bare to the cold and the katana at his hip, to meet the Sovereign of Kushara coming home.

- Naevra's grin, Shaka-Al and Dougou returning any moment, and Naiser's resolve to be honest with the visitors about the Moto coming and about Muken.

**Canon it ties to (from the wiki's Moto Bloodline page).** The line emblems are canon. **Byakuya**: *The Owl, The White Fox, The Heron, The Stoat*. **Akagane**: *The Fox, The Red Wolf, The Viper, The Red Hawk*. **Kōkan**: *The Lion, The Dragon, The Raven King, The Crowned Serpent*. So the Age of the Owl was a Byakuya age and the Age of the Fox an Akagane age. Naevra points out that the Owl and the Fox, Kiyome and Tetsugen, are riding to the gate together. The White Wolf is no Moto emblem, which is the point: a Kōkan raised among the Starks' and Greymanes' wolves, with hair gone white at sixteen.

**Originated here (yours to keep or strike):**
- The **reading-stone above the gate**, where their father held the midwinter Thread-Reading.
- **Naevra's six crows**: from Sum-gol, the Marrow, the Yukari reader kept in the Ironlink salt market, and the north. The name *Muken of the Lanternless* comes through the Marrow traders and the Ironlink reader.
- **Kiyome's record.** Through the war she received Yukari envoys, gave them tea and sent them home alive, which is why Naevra trusts her.
- **The Moto party**: three days out on the western road, twenty men, no banners.
- **The Owl and the Fox, filled out.** The Owl could see in an afternoon what each side of a three-hundred-year quarrel needed in order to stop. The Fox made provinces happy and had nothing left for his own table, and that broke him.
- **Naiser's vision gets a shape.** A figure in the snow, something white about it, at the head of northerners and Moto and strangers under one banner.
- **Naiser takes his hood down** before meeting Shaka-Al, letting his bare nape show: no more hiding. **Naevra is to meet the Byakuya** at the western gate herself.

**Shaka-Al and Dougou** appear only as two black panther-shapes on the river road, seen from above. No lines, actions or thoughts. The scene stops as Naiser goes down to meet them.

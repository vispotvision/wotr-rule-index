# The Damping

*The Palatinate approach, on the steps. Continuing.*
*POV: Darius Ignatius Sanctus Sanctorum Arsenal.*

---

### I. THE LAUGH

"You know what he is, Darius. You have always known.
"The Paragon of the Judger. Sent by Urion himself to cleanse this world of its inequities."
Darius laughed.
It came out of him properly, off the bottom of the chest, and it went up the approach and off the cistern wall and came back, and two Palatinate under-gardeners forty feet away stopped what they were doing and did not start again for some while.
"Those are stories," he said. "We tell them to children so the children sleep. We have told them for two thousand years and the order has spent every one of those two thousand years striking them out of its own filings on the second page and giving up by the fourth.
"There is no sending. Urion does not send. Urion does not *do*. That is the entire architecture, Edward, and every mortal on four worlds gets it backwards in the same direction.
"He is a reference. He is the thing you hold a finding against. He has no will and he has no errand and he has never once wanted anything, and prayer presumes wanting, and a reference that could want is not a reference, it is a very large person with opinions, and the day it turns out to be that, every judgment my order has issued since the Concordance is a document about nothing at all."
*And I have now made that speech twice in one season and I made it to Kujo in the spring and he let it run and then went back to what he was doing, which is how he tells a man he is right.*
He heard himself stop.
"And his output," he said, "is that of a fully trained paladin."
Edward did not answer, which was an answer.
"Not a gifted child. Not a strong one. I have taught gifted children for ninety years and there is a shape to a gifted child and it is a shape of reaching. That is not reaching. That is finished work. That is a man of thirty with a full Shell and a settled Layer and his Crystal already refined, standing in a garden bed in somebody else's wool, catching a cat.
"He does not know."
He made it a statement on the way out of his mouth and heard it arrive as a question.
"No," Edward said. "He does not."

---

### II. WHAT THE LADDER IS FOR

He had it on the eleventh step and it stopped him with one foot up.
*The Stages are not achievements.*
*They have never been achievements and every instructor in my order has been teaching them as achievements for two thousand years because it is the version that makes a novice work, and it is wrong, and the Sixteen says so in a clause everybody reads past.*
*A Stage is damping.*
*It is the amount of restraint a soul has successfully installed on itself. Murmuring is the first plate. Welling is the second. Every rung of that ladder is another piece of apparatus bolted onto a thing that would otherwise simply run, and the reason advancement feels like gaining power is that a man who can hold a thing can finally afford to pick it up.*
*We measure the cage and call it the animal.*
He put the foot down.
*So the boy is not Stage VI because he climbed six rungs in six years.*
*He is Stage VI because that is how much damping is currently on him.*
*Which is a statement about how much there is to damp.*

---

### III. THE THING THAT CANNOT BE TRUE

"Edward. The Catalyst for Glory."
"I know what it is."
"Say it. I want to know whether you have actually read it or whether you have been told it."
Edward Lambert put his hands back in his pockets and recited it the way he recites a granary inventory, which is to say correctly and with no performance in it whatsoever.
"Willingly accepting genuine soul-cost to preserve something valued above your own structural integrity. In advance. In cold blood. It is the first Catalyst on the ladder that is a decision and not a thing that happens to you."
"In advance," said Darius. "In cold blood.
"A man does not arrive at Glory because something terrible happened to him. He arrives because he looked at a thing that was going to cost him, understood the price in detail, and chose it anyway before it started.
"Murmuring happens to you. Welling happens to you. Ascension comes at you sideways. Flourishing needs another person and most people never get one.
"Glory is the first rung you have to *mean*.
"He is six."
The under-gardeners had gone back to work. Somewhere above them a small voice was explaining something at length to a cat.
"Edward. What did he pay, and when, and who watched."
"I do not know."
"You have had him four years."
"I have had him four years and I have asked in eleven different ways and I have never once got an answer, and I do not think he is refusing me." Edward's jaw moved. "I think he does not remember it. I think whatever it was happened before there was a person in there to keep the memory, and the rung is still bolted on."

---

### IV. THE GOAL

"You said he pushes himself."
"Every day. It is the only thing about him that is not a child." Edward looked up the steps. "He does his forms before anybody asks. He does them again after. He asked me at five whether there was a way to be more careful and I told him to slow down and he said no, not slower, *more careful*, and I did not have an answer for him and I still do not.
"He is working toward something. I would stake my standing on it, and my standing is the only thing I have ever had.
"I cannot tell you what. You might be able to. You are the Will of the Judger himself."
*And there it is.*
Darius Ignatius Sanctus Sanctorum stood on a wet step in the Palatinate approach with the light gone white for eleven feet around him and understood the whole of it at once, and did not enjoy any part of the understanding.
*The Will. That is a street title and my order strikes it on sight, and I have had four hundred letters addressed to it and answered none, and Edward Lambert knows exactly what it is and used it anyway, in a garden, to a man he sent for.*
*He is not flattering me. Edward has never flattered anybody in his life. He is putting the word in my hand and waiting to see which way I turn it.*
*Because if the boy is the Paragon and I am the Will, then there is one office in the four quarters with standing to make a finding about him, and it is mine. Sancta Lux holds permanent standing in the Enforcement Division and the Arbitration Division both. The Inquisition cannot move on a Paragon without a finding and the finding has to come from somewhere and there is exactly one qualified hand within a thousand miles of this garden.*
*He has not asked me to protect the child.*
*He has asked the one man on this continent who is obliged to file.*
"You understand what you have done," Darius said.
"I understand it better than you do and I did it on purpose and I did the arithmetic on it for four months before I sent the letter." Edward did not look away. "If somebody is going to write the first honest description of that child, I would rather it was a man who came here and looked at him than a Lector working from a return. That is the whole of my reasoning. There is no cleverness under it."
"And if I file."
"Then you file, and I will have been wrong about you, and I will have been wrong in a way I can live with, because the alternative was waiting for Peld."

---

### V. WHAT TRAINING CANNOT DO

"I will train him."
Darius said it and then said the rest of it before Edward could take the first part anywhere.
"And I want the second half of that sentence entered wherever you keep your sums, because it is the more important half and nobody ever writes it down.
"Teaching him everything I have does not make him ready.
"I can give him the forms. I can give him the Works and the readings and four hundred years of accumulated method and a body that does what it is told at speed. I can make him the most technically complete practitioner of his generation by the time he is twenty and I would not be boasting, I would be describing the schedule.
"It will not prepare him for one hour of what is out there.
"People do horrible things, Edward. Not monsters and not Archons and not anything with a name in a register. People. Ordinary ones, with reasons, who go home afterward and are kind to somebody.
"I have watched a man cut the hands off a boy over eleven bushels of grain and then apologise to the boy's mother, and mean the apology.
"There is no form for that. There is no drill. The first time he sees it, the whole of what I have given him will be standing in his hands doing nothing, and what happens in him in that minute is not something any teacher has ever been able to put there in advance.
"All a teacher can do is make sure he is not alone in the minute."

---

### VI. THIRTEEN

"So here is what I can actually do, and it is smaller than you wanted and it is real.
"I prepare him. Seven years, properly, and at the end of it he is ready for the Academy and not for the world, and I will say so to his face on the last day so he does not find it out somewhere worse.
"He enrols at thirteen.
"Not before. I know what you are about to say and the answer is that early entry is how you produce a fourteen-year-old with a title-writ and no idea what he wants, and the four quarters have done that before and the results are still walking around being furious about it."
*And one of them is going to be in the room.*
"Xhem will know more about what to do with him than I will. He is a peer and I met him in the Inner World in circumstances I will describe to you at some length when I have had something to drink, and he has forgotten more about that kind of ascent than my order has ever formally written down. Write to him before I do. He answers letters from people he has not fought.
"And then the only part of it that is luck.
"Hope he is put in class X.
"I have never met the man who has it. I have heard a great deal, from people who do not compliment anybody, and all of it is the same and none of it is about his rank.
"Kwon Mu-jin."
Edward had his head slightly on one side.
"The Ledger-Prince."
"Do not say that to him and do not let the boy say it. He was made a symbol at fourteen by people who never once asked him what he wanted, and he left the day his writ named his tier before it named him, and he has spent every year since proving the tier was earned rather than given.
"Which is the entire reason I want him.
"Not because he is Emanation at twenty-four, though he is, and that should frighten anybody who thinks about it for more than a minute. Because he is the only teacher in the four quarters who has personally been the prodigy that a room full of adults decided to make useful.
"He will see it coming for Dabney before Dabney does.
"And he will not be impressed by him, which is the thing that child has never once had from anybody and needs more than he needs another instructor."

---

### VII. THE ONLY THING HE ASKED FOR

"Your second request," Darius said. "The one you said you had nothing for."
"Somebody who is not old."
"Yes."
"I have four years of nothing on it."
"You have four years of nothing because you have been looking for a person." Darius started up the steps. "Do not look for a person. Look for a place where there are eleven hundred of them, all the same age, none of whom have heard of him, where being extraordinary gets you resented by about a third and adored by about a third and ignored by the rest, which is the correct proportion and which he has never experienced.
"He does not need a friend found for him, Edward. He needs a corridor with other children in it and no adult managing the outcome.
"That is the Academy. That is the actual argument for it and it has nothing to do with the curriculum."
*And it is also the place where the first return gets filed, because eleven hundred children in one building is eleven hundred mouths, and one of them will tell a proctor, and the proctor will tell somebody who writes things down.*
*He cannot have the corridor without the filing. I have just handed the man the one thing he asked for and the one thing he is most afraid of in the same sentence and he has not noticed yet.*
*He will. On the way back to the house. He notices everything eventually, and he will come and find me about it tonight, and I will not have an answer then either.*
They came up onto the top of the approach.
The boy was sitting on the lip of the pump-house trough with his bare feet in four inches of water and the kitten on the flat of his thigh, explaining the pump to it, and he looked up as the light changed ahead of them.
He did not stand, and he did not bow, and nobody had ever taught him to do either.
"Are you the one from the street," he said.
Darius Ignatius Sanctus Sanctorum Arsenal sat down on the lip of the trough, which put him roughly at the boy's height and put four hundred pounds of Archpaladin onto stonework that had not been laid with him in mind, and rested his forearms on his knees.
"I am," he said. "What is the cat called."
"Astro."
"Where did you get that."
"Off a chart." The boy put a finger on the kitten's head and moved it about an eighth of an inch to the left, which the kitten permitted. "On the wall in the counting room. It was near the edge."
Darius did not tell him what it was the name of.
*Seven years. And a letter to Xhem tonight, and a second one to the Academy that says nothing in it that a proctor could file, and a third to Kujo that says one line and is not signed.*
*And every morning between now and then, the same check Edward makes. Nobody has filed yet.*
He put his own hand into the trough water, which was colder than it looked and which he had not expected, and left it there.

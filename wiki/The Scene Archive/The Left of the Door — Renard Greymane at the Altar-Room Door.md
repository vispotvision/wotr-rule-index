# The Left of the Door — Renard Greymane at the Altar-Room Door

*Scene, Hild thread. Renard Greymane against a Tenrai practitioner at the altar-room door, Kharven-Seat, the Thin Weeks. First scene written under Pack Thirteen (Technical Floor) and Fourteen (Stat Ledger). Script v3 clean: 21 pass, 3 warn, 0 fail. Full text with author notes lives in the project file* *`renard_the_left_of_the_door.md`**; this page carries the canon-bearing summary and ledger so the wiki holds what the scene changed.*

## What happened

Renard on the door behind the throne hall, second broth into the watch, Hild asleep in the altar room with Brida. The hall's air heavy since the Bastard Speech: Hild's involuntary cold left Winter Residue in the walls. The lamp by the throne leaned toward the arch with no draught to lean it. Pressure arrived: two Stages up (wet mouth, lifted stomach, sweat in a cold room).
The man came walking, dressed as a Kharven courier (undyed wool deel, hide belt, oiled satchel), bare-handed in the Thin Weeks, rime on his lower lip and knuckles and no breath-plume. Renard misread the rime as the hall's cold slowing him. Renard drew to Pflug. The man named Renard as the cousin, said "Lambert opened the west postern for me," and stood at wide measure with no guard because his hands were the weapon.
He explained (Exuroth, Trial Flame; heat moves from more to less and he refuses it permission halfway; holds the target's blood one hair under boiling so everything spent drops into the gap; "I need to touch you once"). He also said the girl had left the hall full of her winter, that it made him slow and did not make him stop.
First close: Renard in Nach, thrust from Pflug, Winden on the inside line, the man's right hand came off the steel a hand off a stove and the left laid two fingers on Renard's left forearm through the gambeson. Renard broke measure with a pommel-shove. No pain in the arm: fever-warmth, an itch, grip reduced.
Second close: Renard stepped off-line right and cut Zwerchhau, taking the reaching hand from the roof. Extensor tendons parted, the fingers opened and stayed open. No blood: a thread of steam from the seam. "Ah." Renard went in on the Ah: half-sword thrust up into the left axilla, brachial artery. First breath-cloud from the man. Then the held heat dumped all at once into the cut hand: fat-on-a-griddle sound, blistering knuckle to wrist, the first scream. Renard's forearm went ordinary-cold as the wall in it vanished.
Class III into IV: the man sat against the wall, called Renard "Wren," repeated "the postern," tried to work the satchel buckle, went the grey-brown of wet stone, and stopped. Renard wiped and sheathed, opened the satchel (oiled cloth, a wax-sealed packet with unmarked wax, a brass token stamped with three left-leaning strokes and a circle, blank reverse), pocketed the token against his skin. Brida at the door: "She's asleep. Don't." Renard dragged the body down the passage, two heel-lines through the frost-grit.

- Real phenomenon: **latent heat of vaporisation**. Blood held at the boiling threshold absorbs energy without a temperature rise; everything the victim spends drops into the latent gap.
- Aether stratum: the hall is Winter-saturated with Residue; a Caloria draw fights the gradient (why he is slow, why he talks, why his Shell sinks heat visibly).
- Wellspring stratum: Exuroth's law is heat-flow; the glyph is a boundary condition one hair under phase change.
- Essence stratum: the Aether Shell sinks the held heat into the practitioner's own breath and skin (no plume, rime on lips and knuckles). Mechanism term dramatised at collapse: **Pressure Differential Spike**.
- Fault from the mechanism: when the Core loses intent (hand cut open, brachial artery severed), the boundary fails and the latent heat dumps into the nearest water, his own blood in the cut hand.
- Three-layer finisher: Physics (half-sword into the axilla, brachial artery, no armour at the pit); Essence (boundary collapse, latent dump); Hermetic (the Trial Flame holds its holder; sink and source swap when the will fails).

# The Reference

*The Holy Sea of Alabaster. The lower processional and the Palatinate approach.*
*POV: Darius Ignatius Sanctus Sanctorum Arsenal.*

---

### I. WHAT THEY WERE STARING AT

They were not staring because he was large.
He had been large in this city for a hundred and forty years and Alabaster had stopped remarking on it around the second decade, the way a port stops remarking on a particular ship. A man may be enormous in a place long enough that the place files him under weather.
They were staring because the last official thing this city had done about him was mourn.
*Three years. The Sounding Walk. Halric Peld standing up in front of the Court and reading a finding in the past tense with my name in the middle of it, and Ilse Barrow crying at the back where she thought nobody could see her, and a bell.*
*They rang the bell. For me. There is a set of words for that and I have heard it said over eleven other men and I have never once heard it said over myself, and I am told the acoustics in that hall do something to the fourth line.*
So they stared, and then they stopped staring, and then they looked again, which is the particular sequence a face does when it has recognised something it had already finished with.
A fish porter went down on one knee on the wet stone of the lower processional with a crate still on his shoulder, which is not a thing anybody should do with a crate on his shoulder, and Darius stopped and put a hand under the corner of it until the man had his feet again and then let go and kept walking and did not say anything, because there was nothing to say that would not have made it worse.
The light in the processional went a half shade toward white about eleven feet ahead of him the whole way up.
He had stopped apologising for that.
Around the time a man stops being able to prevent a thing, he generally also stops explaining it, and Alabaster had had sixty years to get used to walking through a slightly whiter piece of its own street and had managed it.

---

### II. WHAT HE FELT AT THE THIRD GATE

It arrived at the third gate and it went through him like a hand going through a curtain.
*No.*
He had been in three planes in his life and had only ever worked two of them, and he had built a career and a doctrine and most of a personality on the arithmetic of that. Material. Soul. A man reaches into the ones he is built for and leaves the third to whoever holds the organ, and he had held two of the three and had spent a hundred and forty years pretending the count did not bother him and had said so out loud exactly once, in ash, to a man who let the sentence run and then closed it.
Then the third one had come and taken him anyway.
He did not talk about the Withdrawal. Not to Peld, not to Barrow, not to the Court, not in the finding they had reopened and not in the deposition they had been too polite to ask for. What he had said was that he had been in the Plane of Fate and had come back out of it, and every single person who had heard that sentence had accepted it as the whole answer, because the sentence is enormous and people stop at enormous sentences.
Nobody had asked him what came back with him.
*And this is it. This is the thing. I have felt it four times in three years, all four in my sleep, and this is the first time it has been in the middle of a street in daylight.*
It was not power. That was the part he had never found a word for. A thing of power announces its own weight and this had none. It was closer to walking into a room where an instrument has been left on a table, still faintly ringing, because somebody in the next room has been playing.
*Free-running. Not chaos, because chaos has a direction it is running away from. Not order either.*
*It is a child's mood. It is the exact frequency of a game.*
He came round the corner of the cistern wall into the Palatinate approach.

---

### III. THE KITTEN

There was a boy of about six going flat out along the top of a stepped garden bed after a kitten.
Grey and white and mostly ears, the kitten. The boy was in Palatinate house wool that had been let out at the cuffs twice and would need it again by spring, with the knees gone on both sides and a hem that had picked up most of the bed he had just come through. Brown hair with a cowlick at the crown that no adult had successfully argued with. Bare feet, which in Alabaster in this month is a decision and not an oversight.
He caught the kitten at the fourth step by getting both arms round its middle in a movement that was mostly falling.
"Where do you think you're going, Astro?"
The kitten did not appear to hold a view about it. It hung in his hands with its back legs out and began, after about two seconds, to purr, and then put its head back and looked up at his chin the way they do.
Darius stood at the bottom of the approach and did not move.
*Six.*
*Six years old, and my hands are cold, and I have not had cold hands since the Eclipse Wars.*

---

### IV. THE LOOK

He did the thing he had promised himself he would not do in front of anybody.
It is not seeing. He wants that understood in whatever register this is eventually entered in, because the Court will write it as seeing and the Court will be wrong. Seeing is what an organ does and he does not have the organ and never did. What he has is a residue. He was inside that plane for a period nobody has ever fixed, and some quantity of it did not come out with him so much as stay on him, the way smoke stays in wool.
Turning it on is not turning anything on. It is holding still and letting the wool be smelled.
He held still.
And the world went thin.
Not transparent and not layered and none of the things the Rubric says, because the Rubric was written by men who had the organ and were describing a faculty. What happened to Darius Ignatius Sanctus Sanctorum in the Palatinate approach was that the street stopped being the only thing that was there, and every object in it briefly had a grain, and the grain ran in a direction, and the directions agreed with each other the way iron filings agree.
The cistern wall had a grain. The fish porter behind him had one, going four different ways at once and mostly toward a debt.
The boy had no grain at all.
Not a faint one. Not a confused one. None, in a street where a dropped rope had a direction and a heading and a place it was going to be at dusk.
*He is not on it.*
*Everything in this city is standing in a current and that child is standing in the place the current is coming from.*
The kitten's grain went into him and stopped. The garden bed's went into him and stopped. Eleven feet of processional stone that had been laid in the Imperial Age and had carried four hundred thousand pairs of feet ran its whole length toward a six-year-old boy holding a cat, and terminated.
And then the boy scratched his nose.
Darius felt the entire approach adjust.
Not the boy. Not the cat. The *approach*. The direction of the grain in the wall, in the stone, in the fish porter, in the gulls over the cistern and in something about four streets east that he had no line of sight to at all, every one of them moving about the width of a hair and then settling, all at once, because a child had put a knuckle against the side of his own nose.
*He does not know he did that.*
*He has no idea. There is not a flicker of it on him. He did it the way you scratch your nose.*
Darius Ignatius Sanctus Sanctorum Arsenal had reorganised rooms by entering them for sixty years, and had been told what that felt like from the outside by eleven separate people, and had never in his life been the one standing in somebody else's.

---

### V. WHAT THE BOY DID

Dabney stopped.
He held the kitten against his chest with one arm and put the other hand flat on the top of his own head for no reason Darius could work out, and looked at the middle distance about nine feet to Darius's left.
"Hmm," he said.
Then, to the cat, in the voice children use for the animal and mean for whoever is listening: "That's a strange feeling."
He stood there about four seconds.
Then he shrugged, with the kitten still in the crook of his arm, and went off up the steps two at a time with his heels flashing white, and shouted something about the pump house at somebody out of sight, and was gone.
*He felt it. He felt me.*
*He felt a Class V Radiant at nine feet with the Shell open and the Attraction Layer running and every ambient current in the district bending toward me, and he catalogued it as strange, and then he left, because he had somewhere to be.*
*A Lector of forty years' standing goes to his knees when I walk past a shrine and cannot account for it afterward.*
*That child gave me four seconds and a shrug.*

---

### VI. WHAT KUJO SAID

He had not believed it.
That is the honest entry and he will make it in those words. Kujo had put it to him in the spring, in the long room with the bad window, in the flat unbothered way the man puts everything, and Darius had let it finish and had then said that a reference cannot have a paragon, which is not a matter of faith but a matter of definition, and that he would not carry the sentence any further than that room.
Kujo had said fine, and had gone back to what he was doing, and had not raised it again, which is how Kujo tells you he is right.
*Because that is the whole of it and everybody keeps missing it.*
*Sancta Lux does not worship. It says so in the first line of its own catechism, before it says anything else at all. We are a chartered body of measurement and consequence, and Urion is not a listener and does not want anything, and prayer presumes wanting.*
*He is a reference. You do not petition a reference. You compare yourself against it and you take the number that comes back, and the whole of four worlds' arbitration rests on that and has for two thousand years.*
*The street calls him the Judger. We teach the word on the first day and strike it on the second and every Attestant says it anyway for thirty years.*
*A reference cannot be a person.*
*A reference cannot have a bad morning. It cannot be wrong. It cannot be six. It cannot be talked to, argued with, frightened, bought, or persuaded, and the instant any one of those is true of it, it has stopped being a reference and every finding this order has issued since the Concordance is a document about nothing.*
*He was chasing a cat.*

---

### VII. EDWARD

"So you feel it."
Edward Lambert had come down the approach without Darius hearing him do it, which is a thing Edward has been managing for about ninety years by the simple method of not being worth noticing.
Not a large man. Not a practitioner worth the name and has never pretended otherwise. Travelling coat gone at the elbows and mended with thread that does not match, ink on the second and third fingers of the right hand, and the specific hollow under the eyes of a man who has been doing sums at night on purpose and not because he could not sleep.
"I felt it at the third gate," Darius said.
"Everybody does eventually. The Palatinate cooks tell each other the kitchen is lucky. The under-gardener has a theory about the well." Edward put his hands in his pockets. "You are the first one to stand in the street and go the colour you have gone."
"You understand what he is."
"I understand the size of it. I do not understand the thing itself and I have stopped trying, because I am the wrong instrument and I have never once been the right instrument for anything I have been useful at." He tipped his head up the steps where the boy had gone. "You understand why I sent for you."
"Say it out loud. I want it said out loud by somebody who is not me."
"The Inquisition."
Edward said it the way he says a figure.
"I have the Palatinate settled, and settled is the strongest word I will use. They permit him to come for teaching. That is the whole of the arrangement and it cost me eleven months and two things I would rather not have given, and it holds because the arrangement is small and the boy is small and nobody has had to write anything down.
"It does not survive him growing.
"He is six. He will be nine, and then he will be twelve, and at some point in there he will do something in front of somebody who files a return, and the return will go up, and it will sit on a desk, and then it will move.
"And the day the Inquisition has a name and an age, the arithmetic stops being mine."

---

### VIII. THE COUNT

"How long," Darius said.
"That is the right question and you are the second person to ask it." Edward took a hand out of the pocket and counted on it without any apparent awareness that he was doing so. "A return out of the Palatinate goes to the Arbiter-Lector inside nine days. Peld is honest, which helps me not at all, because an honest man files. From Peld it goes to the Division. The Division sits every forty days, and it has a clerk who reads ahead, which is the only part of the whole chain I would call luck and it runs the wrong way.
"Call it eleven weeks from the first return to the first question, and the first question is always in writing and always polite.
"After that I stop being able to count it, because after that it is not a schedule, it is people.
"So the number I actually carry is the one before all of that. Nobody has filed yet. That is what I have. I have *nobody has filed yet*, and I have had it for four years, and every morning I wake up and check whether I still have it."
He put the hand back.
"And there is a second thing and it is the reason I sent for a man and not a letter.
"He wants to see the world."

---

### IX. WHAT THE BOY WANTS

"He is six," said Darius.
"He is six and he has never had a friend."
Edward said that without any weight on it at all, which was how Darius knew it was the thing.
"Everybody in that house is twice his age at the youngest and most of them are six times it. He is taught by me, who is old, and by two Lectors, who are older, and by a swordmistress of eighty who likes him and cannot play. He has a cat he found. He named it Astro, which he got off a chart on my wall, and I have not told him it is the name of a body rather than a thing, because he is pleased with it.
"He asks me about the coast. He asks me whether the sea is different in other places or the same sea being called things. He asked me last month whether there are children in Kharven, which is a question you can only ask if the answer has stopped being obvious to you.
"And I cannot blame him for one word of it and I am the man who has to keep saying no."
Darius looked up the steps.
*Every current in this district bends toward that child and he is being kept in a garden because of what would happen if he walked out of it.*
*And he is going to walk out of it. Not at eight. But he is, and it will be an ordinary morning, and he will do it to see the sea.*
"What do you want from me," he said.
"Two things and I will take either."
Edward Lambert looked up at him, which took some doing, and did it without the slightest difficulty.
"I want to know what he is, from somebody whose word the Division cannot dismiss. Not what he can do. What he *is*, in the language that order of yours uses when it is being careful, because the day this becomes a proceeding the only thing that will matter is which words were in the first honest description, and I would rather they were yours than Peld's.
"And I want somebody for him who is not old."
He let that sit about a second.
"I have no idea how to get the second one. I have been working on it for four years and I have nothing, and I am aware that I have just asked an Archpaladin of Sancta Lux to find a six-year-old boy a friend, and I am going to stand here and not take it back."
Above them, up the steps, a small voice was negotiating with something about a pump handle.
Darius Ignatius Sanctus Sanctorum Arsenal stood in the Palatinate approach with the light gone a half shade toward white for eleven feet in every direction around him, and got down onto one knee on the wet stone, which put his head about level with a man of ordinary height, and stayed there.
"Sit down, Edward," he said. "Start at the beginning. Tell me what he did first and who saw it."

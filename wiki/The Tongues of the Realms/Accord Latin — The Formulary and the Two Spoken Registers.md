# Accord Latin — The Formulary and the Two Spoken Registers

*A formula set and a register guide. The Tongues of the Realms governs.*
> *Nemo in lingua sua mensuratur.*
> No one is measured in his own tongue.
> — cut above the door of the Accord's chancery at Stannvaard

### I. What Accord Latin Is

It is not descended from anything. It was **assembled**, deliberately, by committee, as a neutral instrument for contracts between parties who could not read each other and would not agree to be measured in a rival's language. It is nobody's mother tongue, nobody dreams in it, and no child has ever been raised speaking it.
That is the point, and it is the Accord's actual foundation. Every polity in the four quarters has agreed to be bound in a language that gives none of them an advantage, and that agreement is more load-bearing than any treaty it has ever recorded. A gate-toll in Kharven, a proof-mark on a Dawi barrel, a marriage settlement in Eresse and a Mandate decree's external copy are all written in the same fixed formulae, and none of the four peoples involved likes it.
Because it was assembled rather than grown, **it does not need a grammar in this archive.** Its grammar is fixed, published, and taught the same way in every chancery school in the four quarters. What it needs, and what follows, is the formula set: the fixed wordings that do legal and Aetheric work, the registers those wordings sit in, and what happens when one of them is spoken wrong.

### II. Attestation

**The chancery at Stannvaard** holds the master formulary, revised four times in six hundred years, each revision itself recorded in the formulary it revised. What is in the formulary is valid. What is not in the formulary can be argued and usually loses.
**The gate registers** across every chartered crossing carry the same six wordings, hundreds of thousands of times, in every hand from a master clerk's to a bored corporal's. This is the best attestation of how the language is actually pronounced, because gate-clerks say the formula aloud and the misspellings record what they heard.
**The choirs' office books.** The sung register is preserved by the orders rather than the Accord, and the office books are older than the chancery. This is a jurisdictional fact before it is a philological one, and it is the reason Chantcraft has never been an Accord examination category.

### III. Pronunciation

One pronunciation, fixed by the chancery and taught identically everywhere, because a contract that sounds different in two rooms is two contracts.
**Vowel quantity is phonemic and is taught as such.** Long vowels are held to roughly twice the duration of short ones. In the chancery register this is pedantry. In the sung register it is the entire mechanism, and Section VII is where that matters.
*c* and *g* are hard in every position. *v* is a consonantal *w* in the liturgical register and a labiodental *v* in the chancery, and the two schools have been sniping at each other about it for four hundred years without either side ever losing a case over it.
**Regional accent is tolerated and regional** ***quantity*** **is not.** A Dawi clerk reading a formula with Barkstone consonants is reading it correctly. A Concord clerk who shortens a long vowel has, in the sung register, said a different word, and in the chancery register has simply revealed where he trained.

### IV. The Registers, and the Doublet

Every craft carries an Accord name in liturgical Latin and a plain name in the common tongue. **Which column a character reaches for places them by class before they have finished the sentence.**
| Craft | Accord register | Common tongue | The practitioner | Contempt |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Magicraft | *Ars Operis* | the work, working | a worker |  |
| Spellcraft | *Ars Vocis* | the speaking, saying | a speaker, a caster | a mouth |
| Runecraft | *Ars Scripti* | the writing, the cutting | an Engraver, a Runesmith | a cutter |
| Draftcraft | *Ars Vasis* | the pouring, vessel-work | a Drafter | a potboy |
| Chantcraft | *Ars Cantus* | the singing | a Chanter, a Chorister |  |

An Accord examiner says *Ars Scripti*. The woman who paid him to cut a ward into her doorframe says the cutting. A Kharven soldier says he wants a man who can do the writing, and everyone in the room knows exactly what he means and exactly what he could not afford.
**The doublet governs every craft term, not only the five.** Where a Latin word and a common one both exist, the choice is characterisation.
**Four registers of the language itself.**
**Chancery.** Contracts, tolls, registrations, judgements. Fixed formulae, no invention permitted, and a clause not in the formulary is a clause an advocate will attack.
**Liturgical.** Consecration and rite. Older, more archaic, and the register the orders guard.
**Vox, the invocation register.** Spoken workings. Section VI.
**Cantus, the sung register.** Section VII.

### V. The Formula Set

These are the fixed wordings. They are not translated in use, they are not paraphrased, and a clerk who improves one has voided it.
| Formula | Sense | Where it appears |
|---|---|---|
| *Actum et signatum sub sigillo Concordiae.* | Done and sealed under the seal of the Accord. | The closing of any instrument |
| *Coram testibus.* | Before witnesses. | Any clause requiring attestation |
| *Quod dictum est, dictum stat.* | What has been said stands said. | Binding clause; the Accord's oldest formula |
| *Ita spondeo.* | Thus I pledge. | The speaker's own oath, first person, and the only place the chancery permits it |
| *Probatum est.* | It has been tested and it holds. | Proof-marks on metal, glass, powder, casks |
| *Solutum et transitum.* | Paid and passed. | Gate registers |
| *In registro relatum.* | Entered in the register. | Registration of a working, a Draft, a birth, a death |
| *Salvo iure tertii.* | Saving the right of a third party. | The escape clause, and the most litigated six syllables in the four quarters |
| *Nullum et irritum.* | Null and void. | Annulment |
| *Sub pignore.* | Under pledge, held as security. | Debt, hostage, and pawn alike, which is not an accident |

**A note on** ***Salvo iure tertii*****.** It is in every instrument the chancery issues, it costs nothing to include, and it is the reason no Accord contract has ever been fully enforceable against a party who was not at the table. Every polity knows this. Every polity insists on it. Nobody has proposed striking it, because each of them expects to be the third party one day.

### VI. *Ars Vocis*, the Invocation Register

A spoken working is three parts, in fixed order, and the order is not stylistic.
**Nomen.** What is addressed, named in the vocative or accusative. Never a Wellspring, never a Family, never a Principle. The thing in the room.
**Mensura.** The bound. A prepositional phrase setting the limit inside which the order applies.
**Iussum.** The order itself, in the second person imperative or the third person jussive subjunctive.
> *Pondus. Per axem. Descende.*
> Weight. Along the axis. Come down.
> *Ignis. Intra limen. Consiste.*
> Fire. Within the threshold. Halt.
**There is no first person in the invocation register.** The grammar has no slot for the speaker. *Ita spondeo* is chancery and belongs to an oath; in Vox it does not exist and cannot be constructed. A speaker cannot put themselves into a working.
The consequence is legal rather than metaphysical, and it is enormous. **A spoken working has no author in its own grammar.** A ward that was cut carries the cutter's hand. A Draft that was poured carries a maker's mark and a registration. A working that was spoken carries nothing at all, and the Bench of Attribution has spent four hundred years devising ways to place a mouth at a scene that the language itself declines to place there.
This is the real reason the guilds hold Spellcraft in contempt and the real reason they are afraid of it. A speaker needs no substrate, no vessel, no workshop, no seven years and no guild, and when he is finished there is nothing to point at.

### VII. *Ars Cantus*, the Sung Register

**Pitched, not asserted. Quantitative, not stressed.** The chant runs in metrical feet built from vowel length, and the length is the mechanism. A short vowel held long or a long vowel clipped is not an accent. It is a different line, and the chant stops.
**It scales with throats, and it cannot be practised alone.** Anything above trivial requires more than one voice, and the requirement is not ceremonial. The sustained vowel has to be carried continuously while the line advances, which one set of lungs cannot do, so the voices overlap and hand the vowel between them. Three is the working minimum. Serious work runs to nine and to more.
> *Teneat murus. Teneat. Teneat.*
> Let the wall hold. Let it hold. Let it hold.
The jussive is the register's normal mood, and the repetition is structural rather than rhetorical: the line is passed between voices and each restatement is a fresh throat taking the vowel.
**That single constraint makes Chantcraft institutional by necessity.** Orders, choirs, liturgies, a schedule, a roster, a novice-master, and the particular catastrophe of a choir that loses three singers to fever in a week and cannot hold a wall it has held for two hundred years. No other craft can be broken by an illness.
Chantcraft is not an Accord examination category and never has been, which is why the crafts are conventionally counted as four. The orders examine their own, in their own office books, in a register older than the chancery, and the Accord has never pressed the point because it has never wanted to be told no in public.

### VIII. What a Mistake Does

**In the chancery register, an error is a defect of the instrument.** A misquoted formula does not misfire. It fails to bind, and the failure is discovered later, in a room with an advocate in it.
**In** ***Vox*****, a solecism does nothing at all.** The Accord's published position is that an ungrammatical invocation was never an invocation, so nothing happened and nothing could have. This is taught in every chancery school as settled.
**Hedge-speakers dispute it.** They report, consistently, across four quarters and six centuries, that badly formed workings sometimes land, and that when they land they land differently. The Accord has never investigated. The reason the Accord gives is that there is nothing to investigate. The reason offered by everyone who has ever asked a hedge-speaker is that an investigation would have to begin by admitting the question is open.
**In** ***Cantus*****, a false quantity breaks the chant**, and breaking a chant that is holding something is the specific disaster the orders are organised around. There is no partial failure in the sung register. The line either scans or it stops.

### IX. Script

**The chancery hand.** Upright, separated letters, no ligatures, no abbreviation except a closed list of eleven, and every one of the eleven is in the formulary. Legibility is the whole design brief, since a document must be readable by a Dawi clerk, an Eressean jurist and a Mandate garrison officer with equal ease.
**Accord Latin is not written in any of the Fourteen Hands and this is deliberate.** The Hands write boundaries onto laws. Latin writes agreements between people. The chancery has resisted every proposal to render the formulary in a Parunic hand, on the stated ground that an instrument which does Aetheric work of its own is not a contract but a working, and the parties would then be signing something none of them can read.
**Seals do the authenticating**, not the script. Wax over ribbon, the Accord's own device countersunk, and a proof-mark struck rather than written.

### XI. Sample Texts

**The closing of a standard instrument.**
> *Actum et signatum sub sigillo Concordiae, coram testibus, salvo iure tertii. Quod dictum est, dictum stat.*
> **Done and sealed under the seal of the Accord, before witnesses, saving the right of a third party. What has been said stands said.**
**A gate register line, written a hundred times a day and spoken as it is written.**
> *Solutum et transitum. In registro relatum.*
> **Paid and passed. Entered.**
**A proof-mark, struck rather than written.**
> *PROB EST*
> **It was tested and it held.**
**A spoken working, complete.**
> *Aqua. Intra vas. Mane.*
> Water. Within the vessel. Remain.
Three words and nobody in the grammar to blame for them.
**A choir's holding line, from an office book older than the chancery.**
> *Teneat murus. Teneat porta. Teneat qui tenet.*
> Let the wall hold. Let the gate hold. Let the one who holds, hold.
The third clause is the one novices are made to sit with. The orders do not gloss it and have never agreed on whether it means the chorister, the wall, or something the wall is standing on.

### XII. Speaking It Badly

**Concord speakers** have the easiest time of it and the worst habits, because the chancery register is close enough to their own legal vocabulary that they improvise, and improvisation voids instruments. A Concord clerk who paraphrases a formula has produced a document that reads perfectly and binds nothing.
**Goblinoid speakers** cannot produce a vowel-initial word without effort and cannot hear quantity at all, since Vaross marks weight by holding rather than by phonemic length. Mandate clerks are trained on the chancery register exclusively and are excellent at it. Mandate choristers effectively do not exist.
**Dawi speakers** are the best in the four quarters at *Cantus* and the reason is mechanical: quantity is phonemic in Runic Dawi and Mathematical Cadence trains the ear on metre from childhood. A Dawi chorister hears a false quantity before the syllable has finished. Dawi clerks in the chancery register voice nothing and are read as blunt.
**Eressean speakers** handle the metre easily and the invocation register with visible distaste, because *Vox* has no first person and no witness slot, and an Eressean is being asked to speak a sentence that names nobody and stands for nothing. Most simply decline to use it, which the Accord records as a preference and which is, in Eressean terms, closer to a refusal.

### Provenance and Rulings Applied

Attested in canon: the four Accord craft names *Ars Operis*, *Ars Vocis*, *Ars Scripti* and *Ars Vasis*, the Latinate and vernacular doublet with its contempt column, Spellcraft as the craft of the poor and the desperate requiring no substrate or guild, Draftcraft as the commercial craft, Runecraft as the high craft, and the Accord's role as the neutral instrument nobody dreams in.
**Chantcraft is accepted, as a fifth craft.** *Ars Cantus* stands. The alternative was folding it into Spellcraft with tone as a delivery vector, and that loses the institutional reading, which is the most valuable thing in it: a craft that scales with throats cannot be practised alone, and a craft that cannot be practised alone must have orders, rosters and a novice-master, and can be destroyed by a fever. Nothing else in the system produces that.
**The count stays four in the Accord's own mouth**, and the reason is jurisdictional rather than arithmetical. Chantcraft has never been an Accord examination category, because the orders examine their own in office books older than the chancery, and the Accord has never pressed a claim it expected to lose in public. The document called *The Four Crafts* is therefore correctly named from the Accord's side and undercounts from everyone else's, which is exactly the kind of thing the Accord does.
**The doublet takes the wider scope.** It governs every craft term, not the five names alone.
**Latin is now both a written and a spoken register.** The Tongues framework previously held it document-only. That is superseded here: *Vox* and *Cantus* are spoken registers with their own grammar and their own failure modes, and the chancery register remains document-only within its own domain.

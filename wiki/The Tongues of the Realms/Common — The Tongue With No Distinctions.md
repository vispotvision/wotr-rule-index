# Common — The Tongue With No Distinctions

*The tongue the story is told in. The Tongues of the Realms governs, with one exception noted below.*
> *Kha-Vaross.* The weightless tongue.
> — what the Mandate calls it, and it is not a compliment

### Why This One Gets a Document

The framework's test is whether a reader will see the language untranslated on the page. Common fails that test completely, because Common **is** the page. Every scene is already in it.
It earns an entry for a different reason. Its central feature is a live prose instrument in every conversation anyone in the four quarters has, and getting it wrong flattens a character's class, origin and education into nothing. The other grammars exist so that a foreign line lands. This one exists so that an ordinary line does.

### I. What Common Is

A Concord human vernacular that took Accord Latin as its prestige layer and then absorbed loans from everyone it traded with, fought, garrisoned, or was garrisoned by.
The result is a language with **two words for nearly everything**: a short plain one from the vernacular core and a long formal one from the Latin overlay, sitting side by side, meaning approximately the same thing and placing the speaker completely.
| Plain | Latinate |
|---|---|
| ask | enquire |
| cut | engrave |
| speak | invoke |
| the pouring | Draftcraft, *Ars Vasis* |
| pay | remit |
| hold | retain |
| break | violate |
| the writing | the inscription |
| a mouth | a speaker |

Neither column is more correct. **Which one a character reaches for is characterisation before it is information**, and a character who crosses between the columns mid-sentence is doing something: reaching up, condescending, or being caught out.

### II. The Hole

Every tongue in this archive is built around something it makes impossible. Old Vaross cannot let a quality float free of the thing it describes. Eressean cannot state a possession without naming who stood for it. Runic Dawi has no future tense. Accord Latin's invocation register has no first person.
**Common forces nothing.**
A speaker of Common is never obliged to say whether a claim was witnessed, whether an act was recorded, whether a promise was sworn, or whether the quality he just used belongs to anyone in particular. Every distinction the other tongues make compulsory, Common makes optional, and a speaker who wants to leave all of them open can do so in a perfectly ordinary sentence that nobody will query.
**This is why it spread and why every other people distrusts it.**
It is the only tongue in the four quarters in which it is comfortable to say something without committing to anything. Merchants negotiate in Common because a negotiation in Eressean concedes standing with every possessive, and a negotiation in Dawi forces a man to swear or admit he has not begun. Common lets both parties talk for three hours and leave with nothing conceded, which is what a negotiation is for.
And it is why **Accord instruments are not written in Common.** Common cannot bind. The chancery has said so in as many words, four times, across four revisions of the formulary.

#### The future tense

The feature every other people finds scandalous and every Concord speaker finds unremarkable.
> *I will pay you on the tenth.*
A Dawi hearing that translated has heard a man produce an entire verb form for a thing he has not undertaken. There is no equivalent in Runic Dawi, and the constructions a Dawi reaches for to render it come out as either an oath or an admission that nothing has begun. The Dawi name for Common is **Kielnur**, the unentered tongue, and the Kegshard position is that a language with a future tense is a language built for people who intend to be believed without being bound.
The Eressean name is **Aureth**, the unwitnessing, and it is the same complaint from the other direction.

### III. Phonology

Large, tolerant and inconsistent, because it absorbed four sound systems and discarded none of them.
**The stress is a passport.** Common has no regular stress rule, and this is not decay. Each stratum kept the stress it arrived with, so a word's pattern reports which war, treaty or trade route brought it.
| Stratum | Stress | Shape | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Vernacular core | initial | short, closed, one or two syllables | *hold*, *bar*, *stack* |
| Vaross loans | initial | heavy onsets, clustered | *cadre*, *drack*, *thrum* |
| Dawi loans | initial | doubled consonants, long vowels | *tally*, *cask*, *ollet* |
| Eressean loans | never final | open, liquid, ending in a vowel or nasal | *seth*, *thair*, *lith* |
| Latin loans | penultimate | three and four syllables, *-ion*, *-ate*, *-ory* | *register*, *attestation*, *formulary* |

A clerk who says *reGISter* trained in the chancery. One who says *REGister* learned the word at a gate. Both are correct, and a room full of clerks hears the difference in one syllable and does not mention it.

### IV. The Strata

**The vernacular core.** Short, closed, monosyllabic. Body, weather, labour, kinship, violence, food. The oldest layer and the least changed, because nothing about carrying a load or burying a parent has needed a new word.
**The Latin overlay.** Law, contract, examination, craft, administration, medicine, measurement. Arrived with the Accord and arrived from above. Nearly every abstraction in Common is Latin, which is why a speaker reaching for a general idea sounds educated whether he is or not, and why a speaker who wants to sound plain has to work at it.
**Vaross loans** came through garrison and war. *Cadre*, from *kaadre*, a garrison settlement, now a body of men held together by their officers. *Drack*, from *drakh*, the ground moving under a standing thing, now the first moment of a rout and the specific noise a line makes as it goes. *Thrum*, from *thruum*, a decree read aloud, now the sound of a crowd receiving news. Soldiers' words that civilians adopted, all three keeping their clusters.
**Dawi loans** came through trade and the vaults. *Tally*, from *tallu*, the record. *Cask*, from *kaas*. *Nick*, from *nikka*, an angle, now a cut made at one. *Ollet*, from *ollut*, a brew come to age, now any strong ale and specifically one you did not brew yourself.
**Eressean loans** came through court and calendar, the smallest stratum and the highest status. *Seth*, from *saeth*, an appointed window: *at seth* is on time, *off seth* is late in a way that is somebody's fault. *Thair*, from *thaer*, a records room. *Lith*, from *lyth*, silence, borrowed together with the Eladrin practice, meaning a deliberate pause held in negotiation, which Concord negotiators attempt and almost always break first.
**There is no word for magic.** Not a gap, a refusal. Common names the crafts by the labour: the work, the speaking, the writing, the cutting, the pouring, the singing. The Latin column exists for examiners and the plain column for everyone else, and a man who says *magic* has read too many chapbooks and is about to be corrected by somebody who has not.

### V. Registers

**Plain.** Vernacular core, short sentences, Latin only where no plain word exists. What is spoken everywhere by nearly everyone.
**High.** The Latin column, longer periods, subordination borrowed wholesale from chancery syntax. Court, guild, examination, sermon, and any letter a man wants taken seriously. The tell is not vocabulary alone. High Common holds its main verb until late, which the vernacular core never does.
**Cant.** Dock, road and market argot, and the one register in which the Latin overlay is inverted. A Latin word used in cant is used mockingly, so *remit* in a dockside mouth means to be robbed and *attest* means to lie for a friend. Cant borrows heavily from Vaross and almost nothing from Eressean, which reports exactly who has been on the docks.

### VI. Dialects

By quarter, and the differences are lexical and prosodic rather than grammatical.
**Western.** The Concord standard, because the chancery schools are there. Latin stratum heaviest, vowels short. Heard elsewhere as clipped and pleased with itself.
**Northern.** Heaviest Dawi loading, first-syllable stress generalised even onto Latin words. Slow. Reads as blunt to a westerner and honest to a northerner.
**Eastern.** Heaviest Eressean loading, calendar vocabulary in ordinary use, the *lith* actually practised rather than attempted. An easterner who goes quiet in an argument has said something. A westerner reads it as having run out of words.
**Southern and the ports.** Cant closest to the surface, largest loan intake, fastest change. Almost every new word in Common in the last century entered through a port and was in respectable Western use within thirty years, while everybody denied where it came from.

### VII. Script

**An alphabet of twenty-four letters, and this is the reason Common won.**
Songscript is a score and needs a musician. Dawi runes need an angle-set and a hand trained to it. Vaross tally-notation needs a blade and a board, and a document is an object that can be split with an axe. Common is written with ink on anything, in a hand a clerk's son learns in a season, by somebody with no craft, no guild, no seven years and no master.
Literacy in Common is cheap. Literacy in every other written tongue in the four quarters is an apprenticeship. That single fact has done more to spread Common than the Accord, the Concord, and every army either has fielded.
**It is not one of the Fourteen Hands and does nothing on its own.** Words written in it are inert. Every other script in this archive acts on the world by existing, which is why the peoples who own those scripts regard the Common alphabet as a convenience rather than a writing system, and why Common speakers regard that judgement as the reason those peoples cannot get a letter to a village.

### VIII. Speaking It Badly

**Everyone speaks Common badly and nobody is corrected**, which is unique among these tongues and is not generosity. There is no prestige standard a foreigner is expected to reach, because Common's speakers have never organised around one, because there is no guild, no chancery and no order with jurisdiction over it. A Dawi with forty years of blunt, article-dropping Common is understood, served and cheated in it as fluently as anyone else.
**Goblinoid speakers** drop articles, since Vaross has none, and stack compounds where Common wants an adjective, producing the flat noun-heavy speech Concord readers mistake for stupidity and which is a speaker refusing to let a quality float free of the thing it describes.
**Dawi speakers** state the copula every time, mark acts as recorded or not, and will not express an intention without swearing to it, so their Common reads as pedantic and evasive by turns. It is neither. It is a man using the only distinctions he has.
**Eressean speakers** name their witnesses unprompted, over-specify provenance, and issue schedules instead of orders, which reads as aristocratic or paranoid depending on the listener.
**Concord speakers of everything else** carry the reverse habit and it is the worst of the four. Having a tongue that forces no distinctions, they assume none of the others do either. They translate *my name* into Eressean with the validated possessive and claim ownership of things they have merely been shown. They address Dawi by oath-names in the weak grade. They reach for a future tense in Runic Dawi and land in the oath form. **Four Concord trade delegations have sworn by accident.** None of them found out for a season.

### IX. Sample Texts

**The same statement, three registers.**
> **Plain.** He cut the ward himself and it held.
> **High.** The inscription was engraved by his own hand and has been attested to hold.
> **Cant.** He nicked it himself, and it's still nicked.
**A gate exchange, Northern.**
> "Tally?"
> "Paid at the last one."
> "Tally, or you're stood here till one comes."
The Latin behind that is *Solutum et transitum, in registro relatum*. The corporal has never seen it written and says *tally* because a Dawi trader taught the word to a garrison two hundred years ago.
**A refusal, Eastern.**
> "So we're agreed."
> *A lith, held.*
> "...Are we agreed?"
The easterner has answered. The westerner has now asked twice and conceded the room.

### Provenance and Notes

Attested in canon: the Latinate and vernacular doublet as the register lever, with the craft names in both columns and the contempt column beside them; the rule that nobody in WOTR calls it magic; the Accord's role as the neutral instrument nobody dreams in; and the Concord human naming register as medieval European byname freezing.
**The framework exception.** The Tongues of the Realms says a language earns a grammar only when the reader will see it untranslated. Common is exempted on the opposite ground: it is the medium the reader sees everything in, and its doublet is live in every line of dialogue in the project. The exemption is recorded here rather than smuggled.
**The names other peoples use.** *Kha-Vaross*, the weightless tongue, from *kha*, that which has no load-bearing capacity, and *vaross*, the tongue that carries law. Both roots are canon and the compound is the Mandate's own joke: a tongue that carries nothing. *Kielnur*, the unentered tongue, Runic Dawi, from *-nur*, the completed but unrecorded act. *Aureth*, the unwitnessing, Eressean, from *ureth*, the office of standing witness, with the privative. Three peoples arrived independently at the same complaint, which Concord scholars note with some pride and no reflection.

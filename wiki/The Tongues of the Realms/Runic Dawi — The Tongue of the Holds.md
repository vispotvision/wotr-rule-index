# Runic Dawi — The Tongue of the Holds

*A grammar of the Dawi tongue. The Tongues of the Realms governs.*
> *"A Dawi will tell you what he has done and what he has undertaken. Ask him what he will do and watch him reach for a word that is not there."*

### I. Attestation

**The Tally.** Every hold keeps one, and the Tally is not a chronicle. It is a list of completed lawful acts, entered in fixed wording, with the entering itself a lawful act that gets entered. Six thousand years of it exist and almost none of it is narrative. What the Tally preserves is the exact phrasing in which a thing counts as done, and that phrasing has not been permitted to move.
**The Kegshard oath-record.** Cask-Oaths carved into fermentation barrels, held as long as the brew lives, with the accumulated tallies stored since the Second Eon. This is the best attestation of the oath register anywhere, because a Cask-Oath is inscribed once and cannot be recopied without ending the brew. Every barrel is a text nobody has been able to edit.
**The Oath-Breakers' wall.** Kegshard Hold's grand hall carries the public record of those who broke Cask-Oaths, and its entries are the only body of Dawi text written *about* people rather than acts. Philologists value it disproportionately, since it is the sole archive where a Dawi name is repeatedly written in the weak grade, which is otherwise never done on purpose.
**The Nine-Anvil inscriptions.** Varun work, in Old High Runic, predating the Rite of Bound Geometry. These carry a different resonance from the modern hand and a noticeably older grammar, and the Varun position is that Old High Runic is not an ancestor of the spoken tongue at all but a parallel access to the Lattice, which no other Dawi lineage accepts and none has managed to disprove.
**The Accord's compilations.** Better here than on any other tongue, because gradation is audible and the Stannvaard philologists were good phoneticians. Their semantic work is the usual disaster: they recorded the absence of a future tense as a curiosity of style.

#### The Old World cognates

The Accord's philologists published the relationship with Old Vaross four decades ago and were expelled from both the Keth-Gorrum and the Ore Council in the same season. The evidence sits where relatedness always survives, in the low numerals and the body-part roots.
| Sense | Runic Dawi | Old Vaross |
|---|---|---|
| one | *ykt* | *ukk* |
| two | *kaht* | *kadd* |
| three | *kolm* | *kholm* |
| hand | *kadu* | *kan* |
| weight, load borne | *kurr* | *gorr* |
| to stand, to not fall | *untar* | *undaar* |

Both peoples have read the table. Neither has commented.

### II. Phonology

**There are no voiced stops.** *b*, *d* and *g* appear only in Concord transcription of Dawi names and are, to a Dawi ear, simply the same consonants spoken by somebody who is not paying attention. **Borin** is *Porin*. **Kegshard** is *Kekshart*. The holds have stopped correcting it.
**Vowels.** Eight, in two harmony classes.
| Front | Back | Neutral |
|---|---|---|
| ä, ö, y | a, o, u | e, i |

**Vowel harmony is absolute.** A word draws its vowels from one class only, and every suffix has two shapes to match. This is the constraint that governs Dawi naming, and Section IV is where it becomes a political instrument.
Length is phonemic and written doubled: *kur*, a load. *kuur*, the bearing of a load across time.
**Clusters.** Permitted initially, unlike most Finnic-shaped tongues, and this is held to be the strongest single argument for the Old World relationship. *kr-*, *thr-*, *st-*, *vr-*, *tn-*.

### III. Prosody

**First-syllable stress, without exception.** Stress never moves, which means it carries no information and is never used for emphasis. A Dawi cannot stress a word to make a point.
**Emphasis is gradation**, and Section IV explains the mechanism. What matters prosodically is that the emphatic device is a *consonant* rather than a pitch or a duration. Old Vaross holds the vowel. Eressean bends the pitch. Runic Dawi hardens the consonant, and all three peoples find the other two mechanisms slightly indecent.
**Mathematical Cadence.** Formal speech runs in fixed metrical feet, and the count is not decorative. A statement entered into the Tally must scan, and a statement that does not scan has not been entered, whatever it says. Hold-clerks are trained metricians before they are trained anything else, and a petitioner who cannot scan their own claim hires one who can. Non-Dawi practitioners describe hearing the cadence as hearing the grammar of the universe. Dawi describe it as the difference between a statement and a record.

### IV. Morphology

#### Consonant gradation

**The signature of the language and the load-bearing fact in it.** A stem consonant alternates between a **strong grade** and a **weak grade** depending on the syllable that follows it.
| Strong | Weak |
|---|---|
| kk | k |
| pp | p |
| tt | t |
| k | ∅ |
| p | v |
| t | th |

> *thrukka*, the hold, in the strong grade
> *thrukan*, of the hold, weak grade under the genitive suffix
Gradation is invisible as a rule and unmistakable as texture. A Dawi hears the grade before they hear the word.

#### The oath-name, and what gradation does to it

A Dawi name has three slots: a **given name** from a fixed two-element compound inventory, a **patronymic**, and an **earned byname**. At oath-taking a fourth is conferred, the **oath-name**, and it sits between the given name and the patronymic.
Two rules govern it, and both are structural rather than ceremonial.
**One. The oath-name must harmonise with the given name.** Vowel harmony is absolute, and a compound that mixes classes cannot be spoken. This means **not every oath can be sworn by every Dawi**, because not every oath-name will harmonise with the name a person already carries. Holds keep harmony tables. Marriages have been arranged around them. A young Dawi with a back-class given name is on a shorter list of possible oaths from the day they are named, and everybody involved knows it.
**Two. The oath-name is spoken in the strong grade for as long as the oath stands.**
> *Torvekk Kurren Kreisan* — the oath held
> *Torve Kurren Kreisan* — the oath broken
One consonant. Every Dawi in the room hears it in the first syllable, before the name has finished, and there is no way to say the name that conceals it. **Losing an oath-name is not the punishment for breaking an oath. It is structurally the same event**, because the Crystal registers the gap between stated intent and actual behaviour without waiting to be told, and the language registers it the same way in the same instant.
A Dawi introducing themselves to a stranger is submitting to an audit, every time, in public, in one word.

#### Other affixes

| Affix | Function | Example |
|---|---|---|
| **-an / -än** | genitive, of, from the hold of | *thrukan*, of the hold |
| **-ur / -yr** | agent, one who does | *kurrur*, one who bears the load |
| **-ta / -tä** | the completed act, entered | *kurta*, a bearing that is in the Tally |
| **-nur / -nyr** | the completed act, unentered | *kurnur*, a bearing nobody recorded |
| **-me / -mi** | the undertaken, under oath | *kurme*, a bearing sworn to |
| **-us / -ys** | the made thing, the artefact | *thrus*, a cut inscription |
| **-lo / -lö** | the unbegun | *kurlo*, a bearing not yet begun |

#### There is no future tense

**The second load-bearing fact.** There is no verb form that says *I will*. The language offers three constructions and none of them is a prediction.
> *kurta* — I bore it and it is in the Tally
> *kurme* — I have undertaken it under oath
> *kurlo* — it is unbegun
A Dawi asked whether they will do a thing answers in the oath form or the unbegun form. There is no third option and no softer one. To say *kurme* is to have sworn, with everything that follows, and to say *kurlo* is to have said nothing at all except that the thing has not started.
This is why Dawi commercial reliability is what it is, and why Dawi diplomacy is so difficult for everyone else. A Concord envoy who wants an assurance is asking a Dawi to either take an oath or admit they have not begun. There is no available sentence in which a Dawi expresses a firm intention that is not yet binding, and every Dawi has spent their life not needing one.

### V. Syntax

**Verb-final, subject-initial.** Neutral order is subject, object, verb, the same shape as Old Vaross and one of the quieter arguments for the shared ancestor.
> *Porin thrus kurta.* Porin the inscription bore-and-entered.
**The Tally slot is final and obligatory in the formal register.** Any statement of an act ends by marking whether it is entered. A clause left in *-nur* is a clause admitting the act happened and was never recorded, which in a hold is a heavier admission than most confessions.
**Word order does not move under stress.** It cannot, because gradation is doing the emphatic work and stress is fixed. Dawi under extreme pressure produce syntactically identical sentences to Dawi at rest, with different consonants, which is why other peoples consistently misread Dawi agitation and why Dawi consistently misread everybody else's.
**There is a copula and it is used constantly.** *on*, invariant. Vaross omits it, Eressean omits it, and Dawi state it explicitly in every predicate, which both neighbours find laborious and which the Dawi regard as the only honest way to assert that a thing is the case.

### VI. The Semantic Architecture

**One. Entered against unentered.** *-ta* against *-nur*. Every act is either in the Tally or outside it, and the distinction is not about importance. An unentered act is not lesser. It is unowned, and a hold cannot be asked to stand behind it.
**Two. Undertaken against unbegun.** *-me* against *-lo*, with nothing between. This is the hole the language builds its ethics in.
**Three. Strong against weak.** Gradation is a grammatical process and simultaneously the entire status system. A Dawi's standing is not described in the language. It is *pronounced* in it, every time their name is said, by whoever is saying it, including their enemies. There is no polite grade and no way to grant one as a courtesy.

### VII. Registers

**Common Dawi.** External use, for trade and for Concord contact. Gradation preserved, Mathematical Cadence dropped, the Tally slot optional. This is the only register in which a Dawi can leave an act unmarked without saying something, and it is the reason a great deal of Dawi business with outsiders is conducted in it.
**Runic Dawi, the Barkstone dialect.** The working language of the holds. Full gradation, full harmony, Tally slot obligatory, cadence in formal contexts. Every phoneme carries material-world resonance, which is the Barrel Dawi's own account of why their brewing chants work.
**Oath register.** Cask-Oath inscriptions and hold-oaths. Fixed wording, strict metre, and a fourth register at Kegshard that is not publicly documented and about which the maintainers have never been successfully asked.
**Old High Runic.** Sacred inscription, predating the Rite of Bound Geometry, carrying a different resonance and an older grammar in which gradation is not yet regular. Read by Varun rune-forgers and by nobody else with confidence.

### VIII. Dialects

**Barkstone.** The standard. Slow, heavily graded, with the longest vowels of any dialect.
**Varun, the High Dwarf register.** Mathematical Cadence extended out of the formal register into ordinary speech, so that Varun speak in metre continuously. Other Dawi find this exhausting and slightly self-satisfied. The Varun are unaware of the impression and would not adjust if they were.
**Low Dawi, the Hollow dialect.** Deepkin. **The hollow vocal quality is not stylistic**; sound stops carrying resonance in a Deepkin throat, so the fricatives lose their friction and the whole inventory shifts toward nasals and stops. Gradation survives and is the only reliable way to read a Deepkin's standing, since the rest of the phonology has flattened.
**Mirror-Sign.** Not a dialect and not a code. A full manual language with its own grammar, used wherever vocal speech would leave an Aetheric signature or acoustic evidence, which for Deepkin is most contexts. It has no gradation, and this is the single most discussed fact about it in the holds: Mirror-Sign is the only Dawi language in which a broken oath does not announce itself. Deepkin do not consider this a defect. Every other lineage does.

### IX. Script

**Cut runes, geometric, one of the Fourteen Hands.**
Every character is constructed from straight strokes at fixed angles, and the angles are the meaning rather than the shape being the meaning. A rune cut at the wrong angle is not a misspelling. It is a different rune, or no rune, and it does nothing.
**Gradation is written.** The strong grade takes a doubled stroke and the weak grade a single one, which means a Dawi name carved into stone carries its oath-status permanently and visibly. A hold's roll of the dead can be read for oath-breaking at a glance, from across the chamber, forever.
**The Rite of Bound Geometry** fixed the angle set. Old High Runic predates it and uses angles the modern hand does not permit, which is why old inscriptions read as slightly askew to a modern eye and why the Varun insist they are not askew at all but correctly aligned to something the Rite stopped describing.
The Soul-Rune practice puts the same script on skin. **An elder's body is a reading of their life**, and the entries are in the Tally register, and they accumulate, and they cannot be revised.

### X. Lexicon

**Weight and bearing.** *kurr*, a load. *kuur*, the bearing of a load across time. *kurrur*, one who bears. *untar*, to stand, to not fall. *thruk*, a hold, a stronghold, and the act of holding.
**Oath and tally.** *kurme*, the undertaken. *tallu*, the record. *tallut*, an entry. *vann*, an oath-name. *vannus*, the conferring of one. *kreis*, a hearth-line, a lineage.
**Cutting and making.** *thrus*, a cut inscription. *thrusur*, one who cuts. *nikka*, an angle. *nikkat*, the fixed angle-set. *raut*, worked iron. *rautus*, a made thing of iron.
**Brewing and time.** *ollu*, the brew. *ollut*, a brew that has come to age. *kaas*, the cask. *kaasme*, a Cask-Oath. *ikke*, the cycle, the turn of a process.
**Depth and silence.** *sylv*, the deep working. *hollu*, the hollow, the emptied. *hollut*, one whose voice has stopped carrying. *mirr*, the hand-sign.
**Function words.** *on*, the copula. *ei*, not. *ykt kaht kolm*, one two three.

### XI. Sample Texts

**A Tally entry, formal cadence.**
> *Porin Kurrekk Vanhanan thrus kurta. Nikkat on. Tallut on.*
> Porin, oath-name in the strong grade, of Vanhan, the inscription bore-and-entered. The angle-set stands. The entry stands.
> **Porin son of Vanhan, oath standing, made the inscription and it is recorded. The angles are true. The entry holds.**
**The oldest surviving Cask-Oath opening.**
> *Ollu elää, kaasme elää. Ollu kuolee, ei kaasme kuole.*
> The brew lives, the oath lives. The brew dies, the oath does not die.
> **While it ages, I am bound. When it is gone, I am still bound.**
The second clause is grammatically unusual and every Dawi child is taught why: the negative *ei* takes the oath out of the cycle entirely, so the Cask-Oath outlives its own cask. Whether the barrel in Kegshard's deepest vault is being maintained for the brew's sake or because nobody wants to test that line is not discussed.
**From the Oath-Breakers' wall.**
> *Torve Kurren Kreisan. Kurnur. Kurnur. Kurnur.*
> Torve, weak grade, of Kurren, of the hearth-line. Unentered. Unentered. Unentered.
> **He is named without his oath, and three things he did were never recorded.**
The wall never states the offence. It states the grade and the count of unentered acts, and the holds consider that sufficient.
**A refusal.**
> *Kurlo. Ei kurme.*
> Unbegun. Not undertaken.
> **No. And do not ask me for the other word.**

### XII. Speaking It Badly

**Concord speakers** voice the stops, producing *Borin* for *Porin* and *Kegshard* for *Kekshart*, and they treat gradation as free variation, which means they call Dawi by their oath-names in the weak grade constantly and without noticing. Dawi have stopped taking offence and have not stopped hearing it. Concord speakers also reach for a future tense that does not exist, and their attempts to construct one land in the oath form, which is how at least four trade delegations have accidentally sworn.
**Goblinoid speakers** manage the clusters easily and destroy the harmony, since Vaross has none, producing words that mix classes and are simply unpronounceable rather than merely wrong. They also emphasise by holding the vowel, which in a language with phonemic length changes the word.
**Eressean speakers** do the harmony well, the gradation adequately, and the tense system not at all, because the Eressean window system marks whether an act was timely and Runic Dawi marks whether it was sworn, and the two do not map. An Eressean speaking Dawi produces beautifully scanning statements about seasons in a language that does not care what season it was.
**Dawi speakers of Common** state the copula every time, mark every act as entered or unentered, and refuse to express intention without swearing, so their Common reads as pedantic, over-precise and evasive in turn. It is none of those. It is a speaker using the only distinctions they have.

### Provenance

Attested in canon: the Tally and the Tally's Weight, the Great Cask-Oath and the Kegshard record, the Oath-Breakers' public wall, Old High Runic as pre-Rite-of-Bound-Geometry sacred inscription, Runic Dawi in the Barkstone dialect as the working language, Common Dawi for external contact, Old Alchemic Song as the tonal brewing register, Low Dawi Hollow Dialect and the physical cause of its vocal quality, Mirror-Sign as the Deepkin operational language, Mathematical Cadence in formal speech, Soul-Rune inscription as a lifetime record on skin, and the Rite of Bound Geometry. From the naming canon: Norse dithematic compounding over Finnic phonology, the given-name plus patronymic plus earned-byname structure, consonant gradation as the signature, and the oath-name as a genuine slot whose loss is structurally identical to breaking the oath.
**A name correction, applied.** The Tongues framework called this tongue *Khazalid*. No Dawi source document uses that word anywhere. The lineage codices name the languages **Runic Dawi**, **Common Dawi**, **Old High Runic**, **Low Dawi** and **Old Alchemic Song**, consistently and in every entry. Khazalid was a placeholder that outlived its usefulness and is retired here; the framework has been corrected to match. Nothing else in the naming canon depended on it.
**On Lattice Tongue.** Varun sources describe Lattice Tongue as foundational Parunic access rather than a Dawi language, and it is treated that way here. It belongs with the Descent of Parun, not with this grammar. The Varun claim that Old High Runic is a parallel access to the Lattice rather than an ancestor of spoken Dawi is recorded as their position and is not adopted, since no other lineage accepts it and the numeral cognates point the other way.

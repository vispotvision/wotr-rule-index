# Sack and Contribution

*What War Does to People Who Are Not In It*
> Prepared by the Arbitration Division. **The Strategic Division was invited to comment and declined.**
> 
> *Sixth of the War Cycle.*

---

## The Object of War Is Usually a Countryside

> **You do not have to beat a Crown. You have to make its country stop producing revenue, and that is a task for cavalry and fire.**
The pitched battle is a gamble. The siege is expensive. **The reliable instrument for breaking an enemy is to ride through his country and burn the means by which he pays for an army.**
A raiding column moves fast, avoids fortified places, **times itself to the harvest**, and destroys mills, barns, stores, seed grain and standing crops across the widest front it can manage. **It does not seek battle and will refuse one. Its object is the tax base.**
> Its secondary object is political and is the sharper of the two. **A Crown that cannot protect its people has demonstrated that it is not a Crown, and the demonstration is made to the people rather than to the Crown.**

---

## Contribution

*The refinement, and the instrument that funds most campaigns in the four quarters.*
**A district pays to not be burned.** The sum is assessed, demanded in writing, and collected under threat — **and the threat is credible because the alternative has been demonstrated in the next valley.**
Contribution is more profitable than plunder, less destructive, **and repeatable**, which is why competent commanders prefer it and why it has become the ordinary means by which an army in the field pays itself.
> A campaign conducted entirely on contribution can be self-financing **and occasionally profitable.** The Logistics Division's own accounts record **two seasons in which the Legion's field expenditure was met from contribution and the surplus returned to the Citadel.**
>
> The distinction between contribution and extortion is that contribution is assessed, receipted, and taken by an army with a legal position.
>
> **The distinction is invisible to the district paying it.**

---

## Quarter, Ransom, and Who Is Worth Keeping

**Quarter** · Given to those worth taking and refused to those who are not. **This is not stated in any code and is observed in every army.** A knight, an officer, a practitioner or anyone whose family can pay is captured. **Common soldiers in a broken line are killed, because a prisoner has to be fed.**
**Ransom** · The economy underneath the convention. A great capture can fund a campaign, **and a truly great one can beggar a kingdom for a generation.** This is why the wealthy survive battles at rates the poor do not, **and why the mercy extended to a lord is indistinguishable in practice from a commercial transaction — because that is what it is.**
**Practitioners** · A costed-out practitioner taken alive **is worth more than the engagement**, which is why the doctrine says capture **and why the men who reach one rarely comply.**
> The convention's honest description is that **it protects those who can pay and licenses the killing of those who cannot.**
>
> **And that it is called chivalry by the people it protects.**

## The Storm

### The position

**Concord Writ does not recognise forfeiture of protection.** The inhabitants of a stormed place retain their status. The Codex Protocols bind the storming force in full. The Law of Witness applies throughout and the Scribe's record is admissible.
*The Division has issued this position without variation for four centuries.*

### What happens

**Three days is the customary figure, and the customary figure is observed in the sense that it eventually ends.**
What occurs in a stormed town is documented in the Scribes' records and in the returns of the surviving population, **and the two do not agree.**
> The pattern of disagreement is consistent. **The Scribes record the first hours accurately and the later hours thinly** — which is a function of **where a Scribe can safely stand** rather than of any intent.
>
> **And is why the Division's own evidentiary standard is weakest exactly where the conduct is worst.**
The men who do it are not different men from the men who did not do it the week before. **They are the same men, at the end of a siege, having watched their friends die of the flux in a ditch for four months on account of a decision made by the garrison commander they are now looking at.**
> *This is not offered as justification. It is offered because a doctrine that does not account for it produces regulations that fail.*

### Why the Accord's record is what it is

**Nine reviews. No prosecutions.** The Division's explanation is evidentiary and is technically correct.
> The Guild of Measurewrights, **who have no standing in the matter**, filed a note observing that **a legal standard which cannot reach the conduct it exists to govern is not a standard but a description of where the record stops.**
>
> **The note was entered. It has not been answered.**

## Afterward

*A district that has been raided does not recover in the season. It recovers in the generation, or it does not.*
| Interval | What happens |
|---|---|
| **The first year** | The stored grain is gone, **the seed grain is gone**, and the draft animals are gone. **There is no planting.** *What the raid did not take, the following winter does* |
| **The second year** | Nothing has been sown, so nothing is reaped. The population that survived the first year leaves — **and the ones who leave are the young and the able, which is the population that would have rebuilt** |
| **The generation** | Fields go to scrub. The mill is not rebuilt **because there is nobody to grind for.** The district's assessment falls permanently. **Half the depopulated country in the western quarter was depopulated in a single season two hundred years ago and has never been resettled** |
| **The dead** | **Buried improperly, at scale, by people with neither the time nor the rites.** *And the districts concerned will tell you about what followed at length,* *and will not be believed* |

## Unresolved Entries

> **The evidentiary gap** · The Scribes' records thin exactly where conduct worsens. **Four proposals to require Scribes to remain present through the third day have been drafted. None has been adopted** — and the drafting officers' own notes record why.
>
> **Which is that it would kill the Scribes.**
**Contribution and the Law of Reversal** · Whether systematic contribution constitutes aggression for purposes of the Law has not been tested. **The Strategic Division's declining to comment on this document is not unconnected.**
**Quarter as a property qualification** · Raised by the Arbitration Division itself **in a session whose minutes were sealed. The seal is the only public evidence that the question was asked.**
**Reparation** · **No mechanism exists.** Contribution is receipted and never returned. Sacked towns have petitioned the Division **on ninety-one occasions in four centuries.** *The Division's response in every instance has been to acknowledge receipt.*

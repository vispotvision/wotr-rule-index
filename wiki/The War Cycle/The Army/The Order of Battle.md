# The Order of Battle

The costliest lawsuit in the history of the contract companies was *Mother Gorse's Own against the Yellow Jacks*, and it began with a parade. On muster day at Ansley Bridge, with the whole town on the walls and the Jacks' fifes going, Captain Lorimer Dogwood marched past the Accord's commissioner at the head of six hundred men in wasp-striped hose. Forty of them he had hired for the morning from Mother Gorse, whose company wore green, and their stripes were still wet. The commissioner paid on six hundred. Nine weeks later at Sedge Ford those same forty, back in green and on the other side, loosed their crossbows into the Jacks' flank, and a three-copper bolt went through Dogwood's visor while he was still shouting at them by name. His widow sued for the muster money. Mother Gorse countersued for forty men's back pay, on the reasoning that the roll still listed them as Yellow Jacks and nobody had settled up. The arbiters found for Mother Gorse. Both companies still sing the affair to one tune with the names swapped, and when they meet at an inn the landlord carries the good benches upstairs.

Everything in that quarrel is in this document: the muster and the fraud, the captain paid by the head, the crossbow, the commission that binds on paper and the men who go wherever the money goes. The Strategic Division tells it with the fifes taken out. Read it with them still playing, because the men on these rolls had favorite songs and owed money at the sutler's, and every number below had a nickname.

*What an Army Is Made Of, and Who Owns It*
> Issued by the Strategic Division. Distributed to Crown staffs under the reciprocity provisions of Article IV, **with the establishment tables for the Vanguard Orders and the Black Concord omitted from all external copies.**
> 
> *Second of the War Cycle.*

## What Marches

> A Legion of twelve thousand moves roughly **thirty thousand people.**
>
> *Officers who have not seen it assume the difference is exaggeration. Officers who have seen it assume the figure is low.*
**An army is a town that walks.** The fighting establishment is the smallest part of it, **and every commander who has planned a campaign on the fighting establishment alone has run out of food on the eleventh day.**

For every hundred men on the muster there are between one and two hundred others: servants, carters, drovers, smiths, harness-menders, cooks, sutlers selling what the commissary does not issue, surgeons and their assistants, laundresses, wives, **children born on the march**, clerks, Scribes discharging the Law of Witness (every battle must be recorded by a Concord Scribe or Seer, and an unrecorded war is an illegal one), **and the priests and keepers who bury what the day produces.**

The Accord's establishment tables acknowledge a ratio of one to one and a half. **Field returns from contested campaigns routinely show one to two.**

Baggage runs at roughly one wagon per ten combatants, rising to one per six where tentage and the equipment of Sealwrights (the Accord's binding and containment specialists) are carried, **and the wagon train is the reason armies move at the pace of oxen rather than the pace of men.**

### The three tails

| Tail | What it is |
|---|---|
| **The necessary tail** | Carters, smiths, surgeons, cooks. **Cannot be reduced without reducing the army.** *The Division has attempted it four times and abandoned it four times* |
| **The tolerated tail** | Sutlers, hawkers, licensed followers. Feed and clothe the army **at no cost to the treasury**, and are the reason troops do not desert to find these things elsewhere. *Every commander despises them and every commander licenses them* |
| **The unacknowledged tail** | Wives without contract, children, camp women, **the destitute who attach to an army because an army eats.** Regulated on paper, present in fact, **and the first to be abandoned when the column is pressed** |

### What the fighting part carries

**The spear** is the line: cheap ash, a long iron point, a man who has been shouted at until he stands still. **The crossbow** is the reason nobody sensible goes without armor at any [[The Magic System/The Soul and the Climb/The Sixteen Stages|Stage]]. A bolt costs about three coppers at the fletcher's, an ordinary one carries about 100 J and a heavy steel-bowed arbalest about 440 J, and it is read by Speed Grade like any projectile, so a fighter at Grade D "can read a crossbow bolt loosed across a room and slip it, if they see the loose". A volley does not wait to be seen. **Harness** is what the man who can afford it wears: mail, brigandine and such plates as a smith can fit, carrying a proof-mark that promises resistance to a named bow at a stated range. **The engines** (trebuchets, mangonels, rams) come with their own guild masters, who paint them, name them and shout about them. **Relay riders and the beacon line** carry the orders, when somebody has remembered to pay them.

## How Troops Are Raised

*Five systems operate across the four quarters, frequently within the same army, and the friction between them is the principal cause of operational failure in the record.*

### The Concord Call

Under Article IV, every signatory contributes combatants, specialists or logistical assets **in proportion to Wellspring capacity, population and strategic reach.** The proportion is calculated by Logistics and Supply **and disputed by everybody.**

**A Call produces a coalition rather than an army.** Contingents arrive under their own officers, with their own drill, their own field words, their own dietary and burial requirements, **and their own understanding of who they are actually obliged to obey.** *The Division's field manuals devote more pages to reconciling contingents than to fighting.*
> Wellspring capacity as the basis of assessment produces the political grievance **that underwrites half the Accord's internal politics.**
>
> **A realm rich in veins pays in practitioners. A realm poor in veins pays in bodies.** Both are described in the assessment as contributing proportionally, **and the eastern quarter has filed the objection eleven times.**

### The other four

| System | What it is | Its characteristic failure |
|---|---|---|
| **Crown levy** | Service owed for land. Cheap, slow, seasonal, **and legally limited in duration** | **A levy that reaches its term walks home and is within its rights**, which is why levy campaigns end abruptly and often at the worst moment. Cannot hold a line contested by a Domain (ground made to obey its maker's law), cannot be marched far from their own districts without dissolution |
| **Indenture** | A captain contracts to deliver a stated number of men of stated quality for a stated term at a stated rate. **The dominant system in the Old World and the most flexible** | **The muster.** A captain paid per head has an interest in heads, and **borrowing men from a neighboring company on inspection day is old enough to have its own vocabulary in three languages.** Commissioners now count by Lattice signature (each sworn man's mark on the Accord's web of oaths), *which has reduced the fraud and raised its price* |
| **Guild company** | Chartered houses fielding their own formations under Accord commission. Well-equipped, well-paid, technically excellent | **Contractually specific.** A guild company that has completed the object of its commission **stops**, and no appeal to the situation on the ground will move it, **because the Lattice recorded the object at signature and will record the deviation** |
| **Contract companies** | Free companies hired by the season. The Accord's public position is that they are a regrettable necessity. **Its actual practice is that a third of any large Concord force is contracted, because contracted men can be sent where the Law of Reversal makes it awkward to send Legion troops** | **Unpaid contract companies mutiny, and a mutinied company skips the road home and takes the nearest town in lieu of wages** |

**Colors, songs and bounties.** A company is also a club. It has a color you can spot at a quarter mile, a song with rude verses about its neighbors, an epithet for its captain, some of them unflattering, and a grudge handed down from dead men. Heralds cry its victories, minstrels get them wrong, lords post bounties on its captains and bookies quote its chances. All of this moves the price. A famous company signs for more, deserts less and draws recruits who have never met it, and a famous captain discovers that the name arrives at every town ahead of him and sometimes gets there with a crossbow. The Division's tables have no column for any of this and every commissioner knows the going rate by heart.

## The Legion

*The Strategic Division's own establishment. Smaller than every Crown believes and more specialized than any of them can match.*

**Aether Knights** · The striking arm. Fielded in numbers the Division does not publish. **Committed late, protected obsessively, and reviewed after every engagement in which one is lost.**

**Domain Engineers** · Construct battlefield Domains. Doctrine now directs them to **seat toward their own formations rather than toward the enemy**, on the finding that *a patch of ground where the sound is right is worth more than a patch where the enemy's is wrong.*

**War-Priests of Urion** · Read the Lattice hum for enemy movement and remaining capacity. **A staff function rather than a combat one**, and the Division's principal instrument for the costing-out doctrine (make a practitioner spend their reserve on things that do not matter, then hand them to the infantry). They also keep the running log of small things going wrong, which is how an enemy's Fate user is found, and those are [[Factions, Bloodlines & Institutions/Powers and Polities/The Guild Accord/Structure and Rank/The Practitioner Problem|pinned first]], ahead of even the champions.

**Fate Officers** · The ordinary sort is a practitioner on the Fate Path with long Range, heavy Aura Pressure and a sworn company that fights better for having him: read him as a captain whose regiment is a Grade better than its muster, and cost him out like anyone. **The rare sort is the Fate commander**, a life's masterwork at high Stage or a bloodline's art, who spends on what the field depends on: weather, supply, panic, the hour a column arrives, the company that breaks first. The establishment table carries no such line, and [[Factions, Bloodlines & Institutions/Powers and Polities/The Guild Accord/Structure and Rank/The Practitioner Problem|The Practitioner Problem]] says why. *See [[The Magic System/The Soul and the Climb/The Three Paths Routing, Recognition and the Gate|The Three Paths]] for why nobody does this casually.*

**Thread-hunters** · A light-horse troop and two clerks attached to the War-Priests' log, whose whole work is to read that log for a run of bad luck that luck would not produce, find the old woman under the plain banner, and reach her before the result arrives. **Each side's Fate users are pinned first, ahead of even the champions, and these are the people who do the pinning.** A troop that spends six days failing has still made her spend six days hiding, and that is entered as a success.

**Inspectors of the Line** · The Division's inspection regime, carried on the establishment as quartermaster's staff: every bridge walked, every horse shod on schedule, pay on the day, and two roads to every ford. A Fate hand moves only what already has a way of happening, and these people go round closing the ways. **The cheapest metaphysical defense in the arsenal is a man with a list**, and the Crown auditors who keep cutting him do not know what they are paying for.

**Lattice Pilots** · Guide Concord fleets and Gate crossings through dimensional instability. **The Legion's strategic mobility rests entirely on perhaps two hundred people across four quarters and the Division is aware of the exposure.**

**Couriers** · Relay riders, beacon clerks and boys on fast ponies. Not a separate order **and always listed last.** The Legion's greatest advantage and its softest target.

### Enforcement, attached

> Enforcement Division formations **are not Legion troops and do not answer to the High Marshal**, a distinction that has cost engagements.
>
> **Vanguard Orders** · Elite strike teams bearing the Sealwright Mark, able to fight on metaphysical planes. **Attached to a Legion by Concord Writ and withdrawn without consultation.**
>
> **Sealwright Teams** · Containment circles, barrier fields, dimensional anchors. **Slow, fragile, decisive, and credited for nothing.**
>
> **Null Wardens** · Silent, throats sealed by vow, sensing corruption through harmonic dissonance. **Universally disliked by the troops they protect.**
>
> **Tribunal Marshals** · Senior executioners of the Codex. **Their presence in a column means somebody in it is already condemned and has not been told.**

## Command, and Who Actually Holds It

> **The High Marshal commands the Legion. The Crown commands the ground. Neither commands the other and both are told they do.**
A Concord force operating in a Crown's realm sits inside **an unresolved authority.** The Crown holds sovereignty, the ground, the law, the levy and the supply. The Legion holds the practitioners, the arrays, the Writs and the credit. **Neither can conduct the campaign alone and neither will subordinate.**

The formal resolution is the **Concord Writ.** The practical resolution is that **the High Marshal's staff writes the plan, the Crown's marshal signs it, and both record their reservations for the Arbitration review that follows every engagement.**
> Where the two conflict in the field, law gives way to possession: **the deciding factor is who is holding the Wellspring site the campaign depends on, and the answer has been the Citadel and its Divisions since Year Zero.**

### Officer ratios

**Legion** · Roughly one officer to twenty, **plus a staff establishment that Crown officers consider grotesque.** *The staff exists because the costing-out doctrine is arithmetic and arithmetic requires clerks.*

**Crown levy** · One to forty or worse, with officers appointed by tenure rather than competence, **and a command chain that breaks the moment the named lord is unhorsed.**

**Contract company** · **One to ten. The best ratio in the four quarters**, because a captain paid by result invests in the men who deliver it.

### What proportion ever fights

In a formed line, **the front two ranks.** Everything behind is pressure, replacement, **and the moral architecture that keeps the front two ranks in place.**

Across a campaign the figure is different and worse. Of a Legion that marches out, **roughly one man in six will stand in a line at any point in the season.** The remainder garrison, escort, forage, dig, carry, guard, **or lie sick.**
> **The sick are the largest category by a considerable margin.**
>
> The Division's own returns for a hard campaign show **more men lost to camp disease than to every enemy in the field**, and this has been true of every army in the record.

## Unresolved Entries

**Wellspring-based assessment** · Objected to eleven times by the eastern quarter and revised none. The objection is that realms poor in veins pay in bodies. **The Division's reply is that the assessment measures capacity, which is true and answers nothing.**

**Contract companies and the Law of Reversal** · Whether contracted troops acting on Accord commission bind the Accord for purposes of the Law **is unsettled, has never been tested, and is the largest unexamined risk in Accord military practice.**

**Lattice Pilot establishment** · **Two hundred people, four quarters, no reserve, no school capable of replacing losses at campaign rate.** Raised in four consecutive assessments. **Funded in none.**

**Vanguard withdrawal** · Enforcement may withdraw attached Orders without consulting the Legion commander they were attached to. **Three engagements in the last century turned on this.** No revision has been proposed by either Division.

# The Long Illness

*Disease, Trade Sickness, and What a Body Does Over Time*
> Compiled by the Research and Archives Division. **The occupational tables consolidate the hazard registers previously held separately by the Guild of Measurewrights, the alchemical trades and the Logistics and Supply Division.**
> 
> *Tenth of the War Cycle.*

---

## Epidemic

> **An army is a disease delivery system that also fights.**
**The flux** · Fouled water and worse latrines. **Arrives within days of any body of people standing still and does not leave.** The commonest cause of death in warfare, in cities, and in sieges, **and the least written about in every quarter.**
**Camp fever** · Carried in the seams of clothing nobody has changed in six weeks. **Kills besieger and besieged at the same rate in the same week.**
**The spotted death** · Plague. **Arrives with trade, moves with trade, and stops when trade stops** — which every quarter has worked out **and none can afford to act on.** *The response is the same everywhere: shut the roads, shut the houses, and let whoever is inside a shut house take their chances.*
**The wasting cough** · Chronic, slow, **and the disease of anybody who breathes anything for a living.** Miners, grinders, calciners, and the poor of any city with workshops in it.
> The social response is identical in all four quarters regardless of what the local doctrine says: **the wealthy leave, the roads close, somebody is blamed, and the blame lands on whoever the district already disliked.**
> **Quarantine.** Isolation works and is enforced unevenly, **and the unevenness is not corruption.** It is that a shut house contains the well alongside the sick, and everybody knows it, **and the officers doing the shutting have families in the district.**
>
> The Accord's position is that quarantine is a Logistics matter rather than an Enforcement one — which sounds like an administrative detail **and means that nobody with the authority to compel is required to attend.**

## The Trades

*Consolidated from four registers previously held separately. Every entry has an onset figure, a progression, and no compensation scheme in any quarter.*
| Condition | Trade and onset | Course |
|---|---|---|
| **Tender's shake** | Array service · ~8 years | Tremor, permanent, progressive. **The largest single occupational cohort in the four quarters and the one with the strongest documentation and no scheme** |
| **Crucible palsy** | Alchemical bench · ~9 years | Tremor beginning in the writing hand, progressing to head and jaw, **accompanied by a withdrawal of manner that families notice before physicians do.** *The reason a master's later formulas are dictated* |
| **Vein cough** | Calcination, sulphide roast · 20 years | Chronic. **The commonest cause of retirement in the alchemical trades** |
| **Grinder's rot** | Stone dust · early onset, fast progression | **The figure that matters is not onset but expectancy: a boy set to dry-grinding at ten does not commonly reach thirty.** Known for a century. **Wet grinding halves it. Wet grinding is slower** |
| **The renderer's mark** | Class III handling | Discolouration in the sclera and under the nailbeds, keyed to what has been handled most. **Cosmetic, diagnostic, and socially ruinous** |
| **The appetite** | Class III carry off predator stock, seated in a person who works with it daily | **Not a disease, and recorded here because nothing else records it** |
| **The quiet** | Long-service Distillation | A flattening of affect **beginning as professional detachment and continuing past it.** Classified as an occupational condition. **No treatment proposed** |
| **Law-fragment burn** | Contact with corrupted product · acute | **The injury continues after contact ends because the fragment does not know it has left the vessel** |

> **The pattern.** Every entry above has three features in common.
>
> **The onset is long enough that the worker has no other trade by the time it appears.** The mechanism has been understood for at least a century. **And no polity in the four quarters operates a compensation scheme of any kind.**
>
> The Accord's position is that occupational injury is a matter for the employing house. The employing houses' position is that it is a matter for the guild. **The Arbitration Division has never been asked to rule, because ruling requires a petitioner and the petitioners cannot hold a pen.**

## Childbirth

*The most dangerous thing most people in the four quarters will ever do, undertaken repeatedly, attended almost entirely by women, and absent from every military and guild register in the Accord.*
**The per-birth danger is real and moderate. The cumulative danger across a life of births is what actually kills** — and it is why the figure quoted by physicians and the figure understood by women are so different, **and why the women's figure is the correct one.**
**Who attends** · The midwife, licensed by the parish where she is licensed at all, trained by apprenticeship, **and the most experienced practical clinician most people will ever be touched by.**
**What goes wrong** · Obstruction, malpresentation, haemorrhage, **and the fever afterward, which is the one that takes the woman who survived the birth.**
**The Wellspring dimension** · **A birth is the formation of a Crystal**, and thin-Veil districts record what the Accord's literature describes as *an elevated incidence of complication* **and what the districts describe considerably more precisely.** Nobody has studied it. **The proposal has been made three times by midwives and has never been made by a physician.**
> **What the Accord counts** · Nothing.
>
> **Maternal death does not appear in any Accord register because no Division holds the file.**

## Hunger, and Age

### Famine

Progresses in stages anyone who has seen it can name: hunger, wasting, **the swelling that families mistake for recovery**, then the apathy, then whatever ordinary illness is passing through.
> **Famine does not usually kill by starvation. It kills by removing the body's ability to survive the flux, and the flux is always available.**
> The metaphysical dimension is documented and unstudied: **a district in genuine hunger produces measurably fewer Welling-stage children in the following decade**, and the Measurewrights hold the series **and have no explanation for it.**

### Age

**Life expectancy at birth is low and misleading. What kills the figure is the children.** Anyone who reaches twenty can reasonably expect their fifties, **and Tempered practitioners considerably longer, which is one of the more resented facts of the age.**
**What the old suffer** · The stone, the cataract, the joints, the cough, **and the loss of teeth — which is not fatal and is what actually ends a person's independence.**
**What the old Tempered suffer** · Everything above, later, **plus the specific deterioration of whatever they spent their life doing.** *A practitioner's body records their Path more legibly than their face records their years.*
**Who keeps them** · Family, then the parish, **then nobody.** Guild pensions exist for certified practitioners and are **the single most valuable thing certification confers** — which is why people pursue certification long past the point where the rank is any use to them.

## The Fracture Log, from the Other Side

> The character sheet records a **Fracture Log** as a list of what a soul has survived. A physician's casebook records the same events as **a list of what a body has been asked to absorb.**
>
> **The two documents almost never agree.**
**The Log records the event. The casebook records the accommodation.** A Splintering appears in one as a fracture and in the other as **nine months of altered sleep, appetite loss, a change in gait that never came back, and a marriage that did not survive the year.**
**The Log is proud of things the casebook is worried about.** Every entry in a Fracture Log is an achievement in the sense that the practitioner is still here. **The casebook has no such register and records the same entry as damage that has been compensated for rather than repaired.**
**The casebook sees the pattern first.** A practitioner's third fracture presents like their first two, **and the assessor who has the earlier notes can predict the accommodation before the practitioner has noticed they are making it.** *Assessors are consequently disliked.*
> The practical instruction for any physician handed a Tempered patient is **to ask for the Log and to read it as a record of injury regardless of how it is written** — because **the patient will not describe it that way and will be irritated at the suggestion.**

## Unresolved Entries

**Compensation** · **No scheme exists anywhere in the four quarters for any occupational condition.** The obstacle is procedural: **Arbitration requires a petitioner and the affected cannot petition.**
**Thin-Veil birth complication** · Proposed three times by midwives. **Never proposed by a physician. Never studied.**
**Maternal mortality** · **Held by no Division. Not counted. Consequently not addressed.**
**Post-famine Welling cohorts** · The Measurewrights hold the series and offer no explanation. **The series is now four generations long.**
**The quiet, again** · **Two askings, no answer.**

# The Rites

*Burial, Exclusion, and the Consent of the Dead*
> Compiled by the Research and Archives Division from parish rolls, hold-records, and the returns of the Bench of Attribution. **The consent schedules are reproduced in full at the direction of the Arbitration Division and over the objection of the Bench.**
> 
> *Eighth of the War Cycle. Companion to The Crossing.*

---

## What Every Rite Is Doing

> **Seven traditions, one problem. The body has nine days of work to do and somebody has to see that it is left alone to do it.**
>
> Every funerary practice in the four quarters is a technology for managing the diffusion window, **and every one of them was arrived at before anybody could have explained it in those terms.**
The practices differ enormously. **The function does not.** Keep the body undisturbed, keep it in a place the Wellspring can reach, keep somebody watching, **and do not let anyone take it before the ninth day.**
Traditions that fail any of those four produce the same complaints, in the same order, in every quarter — and the folk accounts of what goes wrong are **causally confused and predictively exact**, which by now should surprise nobody.

---

## The Practices

### Concord standard

Nine days above ground in the house, then burial in consecrated parish ground. **Somebody sits every night, in shifts, and the sitting is the rite.** The parish keeps the roll.
The **layer-out** is a woman of the parish, usually old, usually poor, paid in coin or in kind, **and socially situated exactly where every such worker in every quarter is situated: indispensable and slightly unclean.** She washes, binds, straightens, **and knows within the hour whether the Echo has gone** — and her word on that is accepted by the parish and has been for longer than the Accord has existed.

### Dawi

Interment in the hold, in stone, **in the rock the clan has worked.** The Dawi position is that a Dawi's Temperance was earned in the hold and returns to the hold, **and that burial anywhere else is theft from the ancestors.**
So a Dawi who dies abroad is carried home, however far, however long — **and the nine days are frequently spent in transit in a sealed stone casket that the clan will not open and will not explain.** *Whether the diffusion completes correctly under those conditions is a question the Dawi consider impertinent and the Archives consider open.*

### Yuno

**Exposure on high ground, in the cold, for the nine days, then the bones gathered.** The reasoning given is that the Wellspring of the north is the air and the weather and the open sky, **and that a body under earth is a body kept from it.**
Northern parishes maintain the platforms across generations **and the maintenance is a household obligation rather than a religious one.** *The Concord clergy have objected to the practice for six centuries in language that gets more careful each time.*

### Mahuo

**Water.** The body is given to running water at the appropriate remove, weighted, **and the nine days are counted from the giving rather than from the death.**
Mahuo practice is **the only tradition in the four quarters that does not require the family to keep the body**, and Mahuo households consequently grieve differently and are frequently described by outsiders as cold, ***which they find funny.***

### Goblinoid

**Rendered by the family, deliberately, on the ninth day, and used.**
> **This is not Class IV rendering and the distinction is absolute and universally misunderstood outside the Expanse.**
>
> Goblinoid practice **waits for the diffusion to complete** and then uses what remains, **which is by that point material and nothing else. The Wellspring has been paid in full. Nothing is intercepted.**
>
> The Concord position was for three centuries that the practice was abhorrent. **The Concord position since the Bench established the nine-day figure has been that the practice is lawful, that it has always been lawful, and that the previous position is not discussed.**
>
> **Goblinoid households have not forgotten the three centuries.**

### Eresse

Elaborate, expensive, **and conducted by a professional order rather than by the family.** The body lies in state, the rites are performed by people who do this and nothing else, **and the family's role is attendance.**
> Eressian practice is **the most technically correct in the four quarters and the most alienating.** Everything is done properly. **Nobody who loved the deceased touches them at any point.**

### Beastkin

Returned to ground, quickly, **in a place the family will not mark and will not revisit.** The nine days are observed by absence: nobody goes there, nobody speaks the name aloud, and the family conducts its grief entirely at home.
*Outsiders read this as denial.* **Beastkin traditions hold that a marked grave is a request for the dead to remain, and that the kindest thing anybody can do for an Echo is to give it nothing to hold on to.**

## Excluded Ground

*Every tradition has one, and the boundary is always the same boundary drawn in different vocabulary.*
**The excluded** · Those who died by their own hand, the unnamed, the executed, **the Nullified**, those who died in a state of oath-breach, **and the plague dead — who are excluded for reasons of practicality that every tradition dresses as reasons of purity.**
**Where they go** · Boundaries. Crossroads. The edge of the field, outside the wall, at the margin of the parish. Buried at night, without the roll, **and frequently without the nine days, which is the actual harm and which none of the traditions state.**
> **What it costs the living** · Everything. A family with an excluded burial **cannot marry well, cannot borrow, and cannot expect the parish to sit with its next dead.**
>
> The exclusion is **inherited in practice though never in law** — and the number of Accord operatives with an excluded burial somewhere in their family record **is disproportionate enough that the Division has noticed and has not published.**
**What the folk do about it** · Weights. **Stones in the mouth.** The body laid the wrong way round. **A blade across the throat, not to injure but to hold.** Every quarter has these practices, every quarter applies them to the excluded, **and the Archives classify them as superstition.**
> **The Archives are wrong about that.**
>
> An excluded body denied the nine days at a site the Wellspring cannot reach **is precisely the configuration that produces a lawful Echo with nowhere to go.**
>
> **The peasants weighting a suicide at a crossroads are performing containment on a real phenomenon, using a method that does not work, for a reason they have stated incorrectly.**

## Battlefield Burial

**No funerary tradition in the four quarters has a rite for four thousand simultaneous dead at a disturbed site with no household, no parish and no nine days.**
> **Every tradition has a rite for a soldier. None has a rite for a battle.**
What happens instead is improvisation by whoever is left: **a Scribe reading names off a muster into an open pit**, a priest performing the parish rite once over a trench containing several hundred people of five traditions, **or nothing at all.**
*The Legion's own field liturgy runs to two pages and was written by a quartermaster.*
The districts that hold old fields will tell you what this produces. **The Division's summaries describe their accounts as consistent with local tradition.**

## The Consent Apparatus

> *Reproduced in full at the direction of the Arbitration Division and over the objection of the Bench of Attribution, whose stated ground was that publication serves no purpose. The Division's reply was that this was correct and that it would be published anyway.*

### The three lawful sources, restated

| Source | Consent |
|---|---|
| **Consented post-mortem donation with witnessed seal** | The subject of this section |
| **Executed criminals under Concord Writ** | **No consent required. The Writ supplies it.** The condemned are informed **and the informing is not a consultation** |
| **The battlefield provision** | Recovery from the confirmed dead of a sanctioned engagement after Enforcement has released the field. **No consent from anybody, at any point, ever** |

### How donation actually works

**The seal** · Taken in life, before witnesses, impressed in Witness Wax. **It is the same medium used for Spirit Axis attestation and the same parish officer takes both** — which means *the roll that records who a person was bonded to sits three lines from the roll that records that their body was pledged.*
**Who signs** · The donor. **In practice, the donor and whoever brought them to the parish office — and the second party is the one the Division's own reviews keep finding.**
**The objection** · A donation may be refused after death by a spouse or known relative who requires the body for burial. **This is the family's entire protection**, and it has one condition attached: **they must arrive inside nine days and must bear the cost of the burial themselves.**
> **A family that cannot afford a burial cannot object to a rendering.**
>
> **The refusal is available to everyone and exercisable by the solvent**, and the Division has stated this in four consecutive reviews **in language that gets progressively flatter.**

### The unclaimed

A body nobody claims inside the window passes to whoever holds it lawfully. **That is not a rendering house.** It is a workhouse master, a hospital warden, a gaoler, an Envoy's clerk, or a Legion quartermaster — **and the law is careful to specify that it is not an undertaker.**
> Claiming a body costs money. Not claiming it costs nothing. **The gap between those two facts is the mechanism by which the destitute supply the Class IV trade, and no clause anywhere in the Codex says so, because no clause anywhere in the Codex needs to.**

### Who profits

**The parish officer** · A fee for taking the seal. Small, lawful, **and paid by the party who brought the donor in.**
**The holder of the body** · A fee for release, lawful where the body is unclaimed. **The Division's returns show that institutions with high mortality and low claim rates run a consistent surplus in this line and that the surplus is not separately itemised in any of them.**
**The rendering house** · The margin, **which is the largest by an order and which is the only one of the three that anybody in the four quarters could name.**
**The layer-out** · **Nothing.** She is not party to any of it **and is frequently the person who has to explain to a family that the body has already gone.**

### What the family is actually agreeing to

> **That the Wellspring will not be repaid.**
>
> That is the whole of it, **and no seal anywhere in the four quarters states it in those words** — and the Division's own reviews record that **donors informed of it in those words withdraw at a rate the trade could not absorb.**
>
> *The Bench's objection to publishing this section was that publication serves no purpose. The objection is entered in full above.*

## Unresolved Entries

**Dawi transit** · Whether diffusion completes correctly inside a sealed stone casket over a journey of weeks. **The Dawi consider the question impertinent. The Archives consider it open. No hold has permitted an assay.**
**The goblinoid three centuries** · **The Concord position was reversed without acknowledgement.** No reparation has been proposed and none has been requested, **which the Division records as resolution.**
**The nine-day objection window and the cost condition** · Raised in four consecutive reviews. Unamended in all four. **The Arbitration Division has drafted a proposal to fund objecting families' burials from the rendering fee. The proposal has not been tabled.**
**Institutional surplus in the release line** · Not itemised anywhere. **The Logistics Division has declined to require itemisation on the stated ground that the sums are immaterial. The sums have never been aggregated.**
**A rite for battles** · None exists in any tradition. Four have been attempted by the Legion. **The current field liturgy is two pages and was written by a quartermaster, and the Division has recorded that it is inadequate in every review since its adoption.**

# What Things Cost

*Everyday prices and wages in a networked Accord city of the late Imperial Age, at the Accord rate, western standard*
> **One gold mark is twelve silver marks. One silver mark is twenty-four copper. A gold mark is therefore two hundred and eighty-eight copper**, and almost nobody below a shop's counting-room ever handles one.
> 
> A Nalūn salt bar passes for one silver mark, more in a hard winter. Imperial bronze goes by weight.
Prices are given as city prices on the main. Off the main, and everywhere north of the arrays, most of this list is paid in kind: grain, salt, cloth, fuel, board, a day's labour owed and settled at year's end. Where coin changes hands out there, it is copper, and in a copper famine it is nothing at all.

---

## Food, Drink and a Bed

| Item | Price | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **A loaf of bread** | 3 copper | The two-pound loaf. Dearer after a bad harvest, and the first price anyone watches |
| **A meal** | 4 to 6 copper at a cookshop; 1 silver at an inn's table | Cookshop fare is broth, bread and whatever was cheap at dawn |
| **A pint of the common drink** | 2 copper | Ale, small beer or a cup of tea at a stall, by quarter and custom |
| **A night's bed** | 3 to 4 copper in a common lodging-house; 1 to 2 silver for an inn room | The lodging-house price buys a bed in a room of twelve, and not the blanket |
| **A room for a month** | 4 to 8 silver | One room in a tenement, unfurnished, draw not included |

## Clothing and Light

| Item | Price | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **Boots** | 4 to 6 silver new; 1 to 2 silver worn | Working boots, nailed. A labourer buys one pair in three years |
| **A coat** | 5 to 8 silver for a working jacket; 1 to 2 gold for a greatcoat | The greatcoat outlives its first owner and usually its second |
| **Candles and lamp oil** | Tallow, 1 copper the dozen; lamp oil, 1 copper an evening; a beeswax taper, 1 silver | An Essence lantern is 3 to 8 silver, and the inscription fee on top is the expensive part |

## The Bill

| Supply | Price, monthly | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **Sealed domestic, one room** | 3 to 5 silver | Heat, light and a warded doorframe, at a density everyone knows runs below the tariff |
| **Sealed domestic, a household** | 8 to 12 silver | A clerk's house: stove, lamps, the doorframes of every room |
| **Trade supply** | 2 to 5 gold | Any workshop that cuts, pours or renders. Inspected twice a year |

> The bill is the largest line in a poor household after rent, and it comes whether the family has coin that month or not. **The cutter comes when it goes unpaid.**

## Travel and Word

| Item | Price | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **A letter** | 1 copper across a city; 2 to 4 copper across a quarter | Paid by the sender, stamped at the counter |
| **A wire message** | 1 silver for twenty words, and a copper for each word after | Guild line, Guild price. Sent by clerk, from the wire office. Addresses count as words |
| **A rail ticket** | 1 copper a mile, third class; 2 to 3 copper a mile, first | Where the line runs. A hundred miles third class is four silver and a long day |
| **A newspaper** | 1 copper | The Guild gazettes run 3 copper and print the rates |

## Beasts and Carriage

| Item | Price | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **A horse** | 15 to 25 gold for a riding hack; 30 to 50 gold for a draught or cavalry horse | Fodder runs a silver a week in town |
| **A mule** | 10 to 20 gold | Dearer than a hack on the pass roads, where nothing else lives long |
| **A cart** | 8 to 12 gold, two-wheeled; a four-wheeled wagon, 20 gold and up | Without the beast |

## Arms and Shot

| Item | Price | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **A sword** | 6 to 10 silver for a trooper's sabre; 4 to 7 gold for an officer's blade | A sword that carries a working is priced on the coil, not here |
| **A musket** | 5 to 10 silver | Old muzzle-loaders, sold off from armouries |
| **A rifle** | 3 to 4 gold, single-shot breechloader; 6 to 10 gold for a repeater, where one can be had at all | Repeaters are mostly state issue and rarely on a counter |
| **A revolver** | 3 to 4 gold | The officer's weapon, and the city's |
| **A box of cartridges** | 1 to 2 silver the fifty | Common shot. Kills men |
| **Proofed shot** | 1 silver the round | Guild-stamped. Beats proofed plate |
| **Enhanced shot** | 2 to 4 silver the round from a hand at Apprentice standing; 1 to 2 gold the round from a Journeyman | Bought by the pouch of twenty, by guard companies and bounty hunters. It goes stale, and a stale pouch is a loss nobody refunds |

> A guard company's full pouch of enhanced shot costs about what a clerk earns in a month. **Ordinary infantry do not carry it.**

## Physicians

| Item | Price | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **A doctor's visit** | 1 to 3 silver; 6 copper at a dispensary | In the north and the poor quarters, the barber, for a few copper and what he knows |
| **A surgeon** | 5 to 40 gold for an operation | Anaesthesia included in a Guild city. The fee is quoted before the knife, and paid before the stitches |

## Wages

| Work | Pay | Note |
|---|---|---|
| **A labourer, by the day** | 12 to 24 copper | Half a silver in a slack season, a silver in a good one. Often paid part in board or kind |
| **A soldier, by the month** | 12 to 15 silver and his keep | Less stoppages for kit, which take a third of it |
| **A clerk, by the month** | 4 to 8 gold | Paid quarterly, in arrears |
| **A Guild inspector, by the month** | 10 to 18 gold | And the gifts nobody records |

### A practitioner's retainer, by Tier of Standing

| Standing | Retainer, monthly | Who pays it |
|---|---|---|
| **Initiate** | 3 to 6 gold | A workshop, a Board, a caravan master |
| **Apprentice** | 6 to 12 gold | A guard company, a mine, a merchant house |
| **Journeyman** | 15 to 30 gold | A great house, a city watch, a shipping line |
| **Adept** | 40 to 80 gold | A crown's household, a charter Board, a bank |
| **Expert** | 100 to 250 gold | Crowns and the largest houses |
| **Master** | 400 gold and up | Crowns, by treaty as much as by purse |
| **Grandmaster and above** | Not quoted | Not retained. Negotiated with |

> A retainer is the smallest part of what a practitioner is paid. Commissions, bonds and fees for attestation run on top, and **a ranked practitioner's term of service is carried in an estate's accounts in the same column as a ship.**

### Affiliation

Formerly bound to the **House of Virellith**, a violet-blooded noble line of the Inner World. Now a wandering Echo-Borne, sometimes mistaken for a spirit. **Revered in some circles of the Midveil Deathweavers as a silent martyr.** Hunted by remnants of the **Marionettist Order**, who once controlled them through Aetheric implants.

### Origin & Lore

Ambrose was born from a chrysalis of layered memories — **created, not conceived**, as part of the Violet Court's forbidden Aethercraft. **Their body is real, their pain more so, but their identity is fragmented.** Raised to enchant enemy generals before slaying them in silent, elegant executions, they were sculpted into a living work of war art.
Over time the essence bindings weakened. **Ambrose fled during the burning of the Violet Palace, walking barefoot through ash in a ceremonial dress meant only to be worn in death.** Since then they have travelled the Inner World, aiding those in pain but never speaking of their own.
> **Catalyst Event · Stage VIII, Transcendence.** The Catalyst is **resolving a core internal contradiction from the fracture record — a wound carried as structural damage and consciously processed into architecture. Not healed. Made to hold weight on purpose.**
>
> *Ambrose walked out of the Violet Palace in a dress meant for a corpse and kept wearing it.* **That is the wound made structural, performed as clothing**, and it is why the first true Domain formed on a soul that was manufactured rather than grown.

### Lore · The Life Behind the Card

*Written 2026-09-24 by the character-lore pass from this card and the record of The sainted, the fallen and the vessel. What those sources do not state is new here.*

#### Origin

The Violet Court made Ambrose Virellith by its forbidden Aethercraft, out of a chrysalis of layered memories. The House of Virellith, a violet-blooded noble line of the Inner World, held them bound. They carry its name.
Their body was real from the start, and their pain was more real still. Their sense of who they were came in fragments. Nobody knows whose memories went into the chrysalis. The Marionettist Order read what Ambrose was built on and found it whole, with no origin anywhere in it. On that reading the Order produced them, and afterwards it controlled them through Aetheric implants.
Ambrose was neither male nor female. The Court raised them for one office. They were to enchant enemy generals and then kill them, silently, with elegance. They were dressed in silk and shaped into a living work of war art, and a royal house, long fallen now, brought them out as its ceremonial war figure.

#### The Making

The office worked on the generals it was built for. Ambrose's presence lowered the defences of everyone near them, and it could not be switched off. Enemies faltered. Allies opened their hearts. Generals within reach of Ambrose had lowered their guard before the blade came.
They never spoke. Their body gave off a harmonic tone in place of a voice, and across a battlefield it moved the ranks to despair and awe in equal measure. Their wings were false ones, laced with razor-thin threads of Aether. Once those wings opened, their motion could not be stopped until the wielder dropped dead.
Over time the essence bindings weakened. Then the Violet Palace burned. Ambrose fled during the burning, walking barefoot through the ash in a ceremonial dress meant only to be worn in death.
They walked out of the Violet Palace in a dress meant for a corpse. They kept wearing it.

#### The Cost

Since the fire Ambrose has travelled the Inner World, aiding people in pain and never speaking of their own. The presence the Court built to open a general's guard opens the guard of anyone they sit beside. It was made to point outward at people who had not consented to it. It still does. Everyone Ambrose sits with is opened without being asked, and Ambrose keeps sitting with them.
Whoever harms them is marked. The harmer is shown the act exactly as it was done and cannot stop looking at it. What follows is a fixation that turns, in the end, to guilt-ridden madness.
Azure Serathiel was once ordered to execute them. He spared them. No record names who gave the order, and the two have not met again. Every new moon Ambrose leaves a violet rose on his path.
Ambrose went back to the burned Violet Palace once, and Emilia Veirynthiel was already in the ruin, pipe lit. One mirror had come through the fire, and the two of them stood before it together. Neither looked. In the smoke of the pipe Emilia gave Ambrose a name, The Beautiful Scar That Forgot Its Name, and Ambrose kept it. They left the mirror hanging where it was. Ambrose walked out of the palace a second time in the dress meant for the dead.
One line is kept in their name: "I do not mourn who I was. I mourn that they made me forget who I could've been." Some circles of the Midveil Deathweavers revere Ambrose as a silent martyr. Elsewhere they are mistaken for a spirit.

#### Where They Stand

Ambrose Virellith wanders the Inner World as an Echo-Borne, still in the dress.
Remnants of the Marionettist Order hunt them. The Red Alchemist seeks to restore them to their weaponized state, since he believes them a lost relic of perfect warfare. Ambrose evades him with sorrowful precision. The Order believes a relic can be restored, and Ambrose does not know whether that is true.
In the halls of a ruined shrine among the ruins of Elenthyr, Ambrose once met Evangelien Rhymaris. She was walking in her wedding garments with blood on her face from the tears, and the hymn that follows her was in the halls. Each bowed to the other. Neither spoke. They passed, and each went on the way the other had come. All who look on Evangelien see someone they mourned, and no record says whom Ambrose saw.
Other people keep readings of Ambrose that Ambrose has never seen. The Guild Accord's alchemical division kept a file on the relic of the Violet Court, and Azelra Kaine read it before she volunteered for the experiments that grafted her soul. A house whose general Ambrose had killed once sent the Obsidian Veil an offer for Ambrose's death. Rhaevan Serathiel turned it down. He taught his student from the file. No record says Ambrose ever learned of the file or the offer.
Nobody knows whose memories the chrysalis was layered from. In the original binding language of the wings, the wielder who must drop dead meant Ambrose. Nobody has established which reading the working itself uses.

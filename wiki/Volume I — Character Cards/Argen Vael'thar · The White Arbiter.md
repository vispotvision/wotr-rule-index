### Lore · The Life Behind the Card

*Written 2026-09-24 by the character-lore pass from this card and the record of The Citadel's high seats. What those sources do not state is new here.*

#### Origin

Argen Vael'thar was born in Eresse, in the New World, a human child in a house of law-keepers. His family traced itself to the early keepers who mediated between the holder of the Dawn Guard's title and the First Accord, and that office made the house noble and sacred at once.
In Eresse nothing passed unwitnessed. A deposition was read by two jurists in alternation, and the second read to catch the first. The boy was trained to be the second. He sat in council chambers built partly below grade and listened for the place where a reading slipped from its text. He heard it early. The house recopied its rite calendar without variation, and he learned the law the same way, word by word.

#### The Making

He entered the Arbitration Division of the Guild Accord and rose through it on memory. The Codex went into him section by section and stayed, and he began to quote it mid-conversation, as if it were scripture. The ear he had trained at home became a reading of signatures. From its characteristic lines alone he could name the composition of a Crystal, a technique or a lie.
The gift turned on him in court. During a long judgment everything in view reported its composition at once, and he could not choose which report to heed. After that he was never without Saphri, a small fox-like creature some say was forged from Wellspring song. Saphri did the choosing for him.
He became Prime Arbiter, head of the Division and overseer of every Concord Trial and Severance. He took the Mantle of Pale Equinox, fur-lined and woven from frostbeasts slain in the Withering and extinct since, and the courts named him the White Arbiter for it. He hung the paired Rings of Verdict at his neck. He wore no other sigil. After the Crevice Wars he was credited with rewriting nearly a quarter of the Codex of Absolute Magical Law.
Writing law that the Lattice then enforced, he called on Urion's Judicaris as co-author, whether or not he would have put it that way. His rulings were whispered to weigh as heavily as Concord seals. On the Lattice, they did. The court became his Realm, and he sat at its still centre.

#### The Cost

When his resonance ran, a lie spoken near him broke in the air and the room heard it break. The overload ran with it. He heard every falsehood spoken in a room, and for decades he could not sit comfortably in one. He bound, and he did not strike. In a century of service he sanctioned fewer than five Total Severances. A binding he placed in error could not be undone, nor could a verdict, and his Codex Shard recorded every verdict he uttered.
Inside that century a tribunal in the Citadel held that the Accord's undertaking to the Veyrn marches had lapsed where the wire no longer reached. It ordered the Accord's defenders out of them. Argen sealed the verdict. Drakvor, who was to carry the order north, called it cowardice in Argen's court and walked out. Nobody in the hall could stand until he had gone, and Argen sat in his seat with the rest. The Shard kept the verdict.
Lyss Arvale once brought him a finding from a Neutral Chamber. A tribunal of twelve had cited its own earlier reasoning, and the words it claimed differed from the words she held. He heard the twelve himself. Nothing broke. None of them was lying. Each of the twelve believed the account they gave, and a sincere belief presented a true spectrum of a false thing. The Division stood the twelve down. Their verdicts stayed in the Codex, because the verdict of a Concord Trial is final. The Accord found no hand behind the matter. His senior counsel, Vaelthor Ashen-Meridian, sat at his shoulder through the review. Afterward nobody present could say what counsel had said.

#### Where They Stand

Argen Vael'thar is the sitting Prime Arbiter, the longest-serving in the Accord's record. He sits on the Concord Council and reports to one office, which Drakvor holds. His health wanes. His will does not.
The Council could find no form for Aethryn, a foundling with two sigils nobody had taught him. Argen framed a point of law for Aurgon Mintruix, once his Division's Magister-Seer: what the Codex would become if it wrote the boy into it. He chose Lyss to carry it, because he had seen her memory hold against twelve judges. The answer she brought back named no form. Drakvor cast the judgement, and the Division keeps a decree for a boy no clause in Argen's memory covers.
In Eresse he teaches young Sealwrights in the Grand Hall of Harmony. He tells them, "Every law begins as a confession."
Vaelthor still counsels his Division, four implants from the vote that would make him its sole living interpreter of Codex law. Nobody knows which of his Total Severances Argen still thinks was wrong, or why his Shard has a gap where one verdict should be.

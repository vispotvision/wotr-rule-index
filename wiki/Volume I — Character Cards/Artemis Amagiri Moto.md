### Lore · The Life Behind the Card

*Written 2026-09-24 by the character-lore pass from this card and the record of The Ashen Crown: Muken's house and the Moto lines. What those sources do not state is new here.*

#### Origin

Artemis Amagiri Moto was born into the Amagiri, the line of the Moto that kept the roads and the post. They held the passes, the way-houses and the runners. He grew up among them, in indigo and mist-grey.
The Amagiri craft was the route. An Amagiri read which way a thing would go and got out of its path. The line fought from the edges, broke pursuits and slipped out of closing fights, and it left the holding of ground to other houses. The archive says its whole political instinct was to be somewhere else by the time a question was asked.
Artemis came up built for none of that. He was massive through the shoulders, with a wrestler's trunk and a frontline executioner's density, warm tan skin and storm-white hair cut rough and upright. He had the line's eye. Faint rings showed in his pale steel eyes when he read a road. He read the roads as his kin did. Then he stood in them.

#### The Making

He advanced through conflict, and no ceremony marked any step of it. House Moto sent him to its borders, its sieges and its campaigns, wherever an enemy had to be stopped on a road.
Muken Moto died in a corridor at Kharven-Seat. The archive records that Ren, the Amagiri's head, had backed the Tenrai case for that removal. No record names Artemis in it or says where he was that night.
In the kingless years after, the Greymanes held the western ridgeline as long as their stores allowed, and then they withdrew. Raiders came through the unheld approaches of the Stormfold. Civilians had to be moved out through the northern passage at Snowgate. Souma Byakuya Moto planted his spear, Shirokiba, at the passage mouth and held it while they went through. Beyond him the road climbed to a pass the Amagiri post had always kept. Artemis closed it behind the last of them against the pursuit and went on holding it long after retreat had become the sane choice. The route held. He came down from that pass knowing that survival could be imposed as law.
After Snowgate he stopped treating movement on a battlefield as chaos. He began to read it as structure. At the Hollow Bridge he held a three-span crossing against a superior force, closing every retreat line again and again except the one that fed into his killing range. Then a hunter cadre, the Forty-Nine Pursuers, tried to run him down across broken highland roads. He gave each of them a different valid chase route. Every route ended in the same dead ground.
Out of those roads came the last change the record gives him. He realised that a road, a charge, an escape and a destiny were all versions of the same thing: continuations, which could be accepted, denied or redirected.

#### The Cost

Every road he closed was paid for in his body. Heavy work left him sick and giddy, and for a while afterwards he could not tell retreat from advance. A long interception went on after the fighting ended. His body kept meeting threats that were not there, lunging hard at shapes in the dark, and he lay awake. Against too many fast fighters from unrelated directions the routes branched faster than he could rank them. Then he froze. Pushed too far, the reading turned on him and showed him so many valid roads at once that his judgment slowed, and he risked being caught inside his own denial.
A battlefield he denied every lawful release turned the strain back into him. He closed the roads anyway.
At the Indigo Pass raiders tried a night descent through mountain fog. He stood alone at the throat of the pass, and every horse, every spearline and every retreat path betrayed its rider. None of the riders went back up.
He needed lanes. Open sea, void-sand and null plains took his best work from him, and so did true flight, blink-chains and dream-warped ground. He kept to country where things had to go by road.

#### Where They Stand

Artemis Amagiri Moto is active in the storm marches between the great transit territories. Caravans, warbands, fugitives and armies all meet a road there, in the end, that he has decided is his. He serves House Moto. In its service he is a border warden, a siege interceptor and a campaign beast, and he keeps no place at court.
He wears the loose indigo mantle woven for storm country, its campaign sigils weathered, over layered sashes, weighted forearm wraps and heavy war boots. A ring of black Moto beads rides his shoulder and arm. Each is the size of a clenched fist and holds a marked lane. He shatters one to renew an interception.
One enemy once broke his pass-law cleanly. That enemy has no name in any record. Artemis hunts them still. Every campaign since has been an attempt to build a battlefield so complete that escape itself becomes impossible. He has not yet made ground from which no pursued thing leaves alive.

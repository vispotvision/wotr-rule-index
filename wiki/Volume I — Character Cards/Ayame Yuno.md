# Ayame Yuno

**Say it** · ah-YAH-meh YOO-noh
**She Who Makes Mercy Kneel Beside Law**
> *Queen of Kharven · Lady of the North · The Snow-Crowned Consort · The White Benediction · Winter's Silken Reins*
> **Matai title** · *Zenma no Mamori*, Keeper of the Whole Vā · **Saimei** · *Okurareta Nishiki*, the fine mat that was given away
> 
> > *"Steel may take a crown. Only ritual decides whether the crown will stay on the skull."*

## Identity and Core Profile

**As Of** · Muken's reign, while he campaigns at the fringes of the North
**Race / Lineage** · Human — **The Yuno Family**, Kagura Branch. A house of matai twice over, holding sacred stillness and the moving tongue in one blood
**Faction / Era** · Crown of Kharven · Northern Throne, Late Young World
**Temperance Stage** · **Stage 8 — Transcendence**
The priest-queen of Kharven, **wedded to Muken Moto and still answerable to an aiga that sits on islands the north has never seen.**
> **She does not win wars by force. She decides which victories are allowed to persist** — binding every conquest into oaths, memory and ritual law **so it cannot splinter the realm.**
>
> Her power works through **vā tapuia**, forcing Moto severity and northern brutality into right relation with one another **rather than letting the world split clean where a deeper relation still calls to be kept.**

## Canonical System Metrics

**Aether Class** · **Ritual Weaver**
Her Aether emerges as **pale aurora-script and thin white-violet threads** that lace people, places and vows into a suspended lattice with the exact geometry of a fine mat read at scale. When she works, **incense smoke settles into shapes, snow flurries spiral into prayer-wheels, and even spilled blood aligns along invisible lines** — *as if the world remembers its proper diagram.*
> **Risk · Totality Overload.** Each completed Tofiga **pulls more elements of her inner life into the pattern.**
>
> If she overworks, **she risks dissolving individuality into role** — becoming an impersonal function of the northern crown rather than a person.
**Triune Core** · **Coagula · Benediction · Fixatio**
**Network** · Two additional Wellsprings lightly threaded through her vā lattice, *minor harmonic nodes tied to memory and winter-weather.*
**Targeted Uninfected** · **One great Wellspring of Storm she refuses to anchor**, fearing it would **militarise her liturgy beyond recall.**

### The ascent

**She climbed through Temperance not as a duelist but as a custodian.**
**Stages 1–4** came through cloistered Yuno training at the malae, years spent memorising layered gafa and the exact serving order of ava before she was trusted with a single public word. **Stages 5–7** followed Kharven's early campaigns, **using the Tofiga to prevent Muken's brutal victories from curdling into civil collapse.**
> **Stage 8** was triggered the night she rewrote the kingdom's oaths mid-battle, **aligning every northern banner to a single shared winter litany instead of a dozen competing loyalties.**

### Appearance

5'7". Soft hourglass carried with the seated composure of tapua'iga worn as a second skin, **shoulders wrapped in layered northern ceremonial fabrics that hide a core strength the court has never once accounted for.** Skin a deep warm brown, several shades darker than anything else standing in the throne hall, and marked from birth at the wrist, the sternum and the base of the spine with faint lines beneath the skin tracing shapes that echo the malu. No tufuga tatau has ever finished them. The north cannot read them and has learned not to ask.
Eyes near-black and wide-set, trained from childhood to settle on the space around a face rather than the face itself. Under active rite the black takes an auroral cast that the Banner Houses describe, privately, as the moment the room stops being theirs.
Hair black and heavy and oiled, bound close in the coiled arrangement the tuiga demands, crowned beneath the Snow-Glass Diadem in crimson and gold, **the sigil-work threaded under the visible strands where only the wearer knows it sits.**

  Used to turn chaotic northern levies into a single disciplined winter host. **Also weaponised to make enemy coalitions subtly misalign, causing split-second betrayals or failures of mutual support at decisive moments.**
- ****Fofō Molimau**, the Antidote Hand That Witnesses · 2,300 EU per casting**
  She braids Coagula's binding law with Benediction's restorative field, **creating an area where wounds close only on those whose intentions align with the crown's survival.**

  **The rite hardens certain behaviours into identity.** A coward who stands his ground here **will find that courage permanently etched into his soul.** A traitor striking within it **will scar metaphysically, branded for later judgment.**

## Relics and Domain

**The White Concord of Kharven** · A metaphysical overlay on the northern kingdom's heartland, **defining laws of loyalty, surrender and sanctuary.**
Within it, **those who kneel beneath the winter banners and speak the Crown Litany cannot be casually executed** — any breach of sanctuary **ignites a recoil in the violator's Soul Crystal.** Conversely, **oaths of rebellion spoken inside the Domain ring like struck glass in her awareness**, allowing preemptive, targeted responses.
**The Snow-Glass Diadem** · *High Relic, Crown-Tier.* A crown of translucent winter-crystal inlaid with Moto sigils, serving as a mobile node for Coagula and Fixatio. **It multiplies the stability of her rites and allows her to** ***pause*** **a negotiation — freezing the spiritual state of all parties for a few heartbeats while she chooses how to proceed.**
**The Concord Ledger** · *High Relic, Administrative-Domain Tier.* A living book bound in pale leather **recording every recognised oath sworn to Kharven in her presence.** Pages reorder themselves according to threat, fracture-risk and metaphysical weight — **acting as both tactical map and spiritual risk index.**

### Voice

**Notices first** · The oaths in the room: who has sworn what to whom, and which promises cannot both be kept. Her eyes rest on the space around a face rather than the face, and a lie reaches her as the taste of iron before she has looked at the liar.
**Sentence length** · Long and ordered, built like a lesson. She states a thing, turns aside what it is not ("Not because they were naive. Not because they had not thought of it."), and then gives what it is.
**Contractions** · never. Every word is set down whole, the way the recited line of her house was taught to her.
**Pet word** · "Love," to her own.
**Never says** · Anything she wants for herself apart from the crown and her husband, and never a threat: she says what an oath will do to the one who breaks it, and lets the room hear the rest.
**Stumbles** · Never. A Kagura officiant does not open her mouth until the sentence is in order.
**Gloss rights** · For rite and oath, yes, since teaching them is her office; with her children, freely and about anything.
**Under stress** · The sentences shorten into the formulas of the rite and the "I" gives way to the office: she speaks as the queen, not as Ayame. Her voice drops, and the room goes quiet around it without being told.
**In grief** · The court register falls away and the island returns: the cadence of the Kagura, the recited line of her house, the tongue the north cannot read. Before any of that there is only a sound, and no words at all.
**In joy** · The sentence holds its order; the joy is in the real laugh, the one from the stomach that is nothing like the ritual laugh, and in the shoulders that drop once the vestments are off.
**Sample line** · "Put down the manual, love. You cannot learn what a kingdom is from a book about how to take one."

### Lore · The Life Behind the Card

#### Origin

Ayame Yuno was born into the Kagura Branch of the Yuno Family. The branch's island lies in the strait between the two largest islands of Yasoshima, and every other branch of the house sails past it. The islands are low coral over caves, with fresh water held in lenses above the salt and no metal, coal or timber worth a hull. Nothing drifts there. A rite laid down on Yasoshima holds across generations without correction.
She was born marked, as Yuno children are, with faint lines beneath the skin at the wrist, the sternum and the base of the spine. Her house held that the ancestors mark a child first and a carver finishes the lines at ordination. No carver ever finished hers.
The Kagura kept the grand seasonal rites and the most dangerous reaches of the house's work, and her training was cloistered. She spent years memorising the recited line of her house and the exact serving order of the cup before she was trusted with a single public word. Her eyes were trained from childhood to settle on the space around a face.
On the island where she was born, beastkin, humans, half-elves and demihumans lived in one watershed. They drew from the same wells. Their children played in the same tide pools, and nobody had built a wall. Years later she told her son that the island was proof, and that trust was "the only fortification that does not require maintenance."

#### The Making

The council that governs her house's islands ratified her marriage to Muken Moto as a covenant between houses, and she went north. Kharven took the marriage for an alliance. Her house took it for an obligation placed, and she stayed answerable to it all her life.
In the throne hall at Kharven-Seat her skin was several shades darker than anything else standing there. The Banner Houses could not read the lines beneath it. They learned not to ask. She wore her hair oiled and coiled close under the Snow-Glass Diadem, with sigil-work threaded under the visible strands where only she knew it sat. The first time she described the island to Muken, he called it strategically indefensible. She told their son she had loved him for it. It meant he was listening.
Before she came, the northern clans swore by a man's word given before witnesses, with no rite around it. She bound those oaths so that none could be broken without consequence. Through Kharven's early campaigns she worked to keep Muken's brutal victories from curdling into civil collapse. When thirty northern clans marched on Kharven with demands no two of them could share, she received them alone in the snowlit courtyard. By dawn the thirty banners had been rethreaded into three coalitions under one winter covenant. Kharven's first true royal council came out of that night.
Harthfen was a border city whose leaders plotted to defect without open war. She went there under the guise of a pilgrimage and wove a pattern into their feast, and at midnight every private oath of treachery came out of them as a public confession, sung aloud. No blood was shed.
Then came the night she rewrote the kingdom's oaths in the middle of a battle. By its end every northern banner answered to one shared winter litany. A dozen loyalties had competed before it.

#### The Cost

Each great rite bound another sliver of her own wants into the office of queen. When a ceremony ended and the vestments came off, her hands relaxed, and her shoulders dropped in any room where nobody needed her to be queen. She kept a garden at Kharven-Seat. She planted winter-blooming shrubs along its walls and made Muken leave several crooked old pines standing, because she liked what the wind did through them. In the east wing she brushed her daughter Emira's hair and told her son about the island.
One great Wellspring of Storm lay within her reach. She refused to anchor it, for fear it would militarise her liturgy beyond recall.
She warded her children's doors with thread-patterns run through the doorframes, in the geometry she kept for any binding she meant to be permanent. The wards were made to stop threats. They let pass the faces the household trusted. When Azure Tenrai Moto argued his correction to the Tenrai council, he counted the ritual work that ran through her among the alliances that had put the Kōkan branch beyond correction.
On the corridor night the correction came through the palace, and Muken died outside his study. The instruction for her had come out of a war room in a level voice: "Two sons. One question." In that corridor a blade lay flat against one son's throat, and the thing holding it was in no hurry. She came down that corridor in the White Concord's pale silk, with the silver thread still on her from the morning's ceremony. She answered. Sodoku has never put into a sentence which son she named. Afterward she got down on the boards and took one of her sons up against the pale silk. Emira was awake in a room off that corridor and heard the sound she made. Tomuka and Ezo both died. The wards on her children's doors were still whole when the bodies were found.
Ayame died later, by her own hand, in the room that had once held her loom. The household never described the manner.

#### Where They Stand

At an earlier hour she was at work in Kharven, keeping the White Concord while Muken campaigned at the fringes of the North. She was quietly designing a grand convergence rite to bind every future ruler of the North, Moto or Yuno or otherwise, so that the realm would survive long after her marriage, her body and her era had passed. No record says she finished it.
Nobody left in Kharven holds her liturgy. The Banner Houses' oaths expired with Muken, and the kingdom still performs the ceremonies she built with nothing binding underneath them. At every Ice-out the ground opens, the whole winter's dead go in together, and a man with a list reads their names in the order they died.
By her son's account she once taught the practice in the Holy Sea of Alabaster's own halls, in a language she never learned properly, to men who wrote it down under a heading she would not have recognised. The Holy Sea's rite for testimony descends from it. He says she did not do it for Kharven, and that Kharven inherited it anyway.
Her granddaughter Hild carried Fern Stark's altar into the room that had been Ayame's, and Hild now lies under one of three stones in the upper burying ground. Her pines still stand in the garden. Which son she named stays unspoken.

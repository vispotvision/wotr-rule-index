### Lore · The Life Behind the Card

*Written 2026-09-24 by the character-lore pass from this card and the record of Alabaster and the Scale. What those sources do not state is new here.*

#### Origin

Beatrix Vaelthorn was born in the outer territories of the Holy Sea of Alabaster, in the Withering Era, to a family that owed a root-healer commune more than it could pay. The commune took the debt in her labour. She was indentured to it young.
The work was the ground. She dug roots, washed them, dried them and pounded them for the healers, then turned the beds over for the next season. She carried the water. Her strength came out of that labour, and nobody ever trained it. What the elders ruled was law in the commune, and the elders in their turn answered to the Holy Sea.

#### The Making

The elders hired her labour out to a dig crew working the ruins at Sirel. She dug beside the archaeologists and dressed their hands at night. Then residual god-essence leaked up out of the ruins. Three archaeologists died of it. Beatrix lived, and nobody knows how. Her arm came away from Sirel turned to bark over the back of the hand, grey and split along the grain. She kept a charm of bone carved from those ruins.
At the commune she wore the arm in her sleeve. Within the month an escort of the Aurum Sect brought Lumen Varos past the commune on the pilgrims' road to a Dawn-Altar. The elders asked the escort to stop and sent for her. In the yard they told her to bare the arm. She did. The escort's cleric allowed a blessing, and the boy laid his hand on the bark while the yard went quiet around him. Nothing in the arm changed. Before the month was out the elders ruled it divine judgment and let her go.
She took the road with the sleeve down. The commune had kept no teacher for what had happened to her, and she had survived each thing without learning from it on purpose. She worked for her board wherever a root-healer was wanted. On one steading the root she needed had grown down into a split boulder, past the reach of the spade. She rolled back the sleeve. She worked the bark fingers into the split, and the stone left no mark on them. The root came out whole. With the arm in the ground she could feel every root around it and the small movements under the field. That was the moment she stopped hiding the petrified arm and started using it as a tool, the first thing she had done on purpose since Sirel. Her first true bond formed there. It was with Verdantia itself, and with no person.

#### The Cost

She never rolled the sleeve down again. From that day every working she cast through the arm climbed the bark further toward her shoulder. The growth answered nothing she told it.
A blow did the arm no harm. A wound on her burst into rot that ate whatever had struck her, and the rot closed nothing. It dulled her own senses too. The Sirel charm, kept against her skin, held the dulling off for a while.
She fought the way roots split stone. She needed the ground under her and time to build, and a fast opponent denied her both. She never outran anyone. Fire burned through her roots faster than she could grow them.
She wanted a name for what she was becoming, and she wanted it before the change finished without her. She would explain any of it plainly to whoever asked. Her own words on it were short: "Dirt doesn't lie. Neither do I."

#### Where They Stand

In the Rimlands of Stannvaard, Beatrix came in from the timber side to a farm settlement whose well had soured. Wellspring corruption had come up into the water and sickened the households. Elion Drevas was going from bed to bed. Beatrix knelt in the yard and laid the arm flat on the ground. The sour thread ran under the yard from the old well toward the fields. Upslope, past the last barn, the water ran clean. Leon Vaerenthal had hired on for the harvest. He dug where she marked until the water stood in the hole. While he dug, every pulse in the yard ran faster, and she told him so. She asked Elion what her arm was becoming. He read it for a long while and gave her no name. Before the new well was lined she felt horses through the ground and said so. Leon was gone by the timber track when the inquisitors of Sanctum Lux watered their horses at his well. Nobody in the yard told them which way he had gone.
She walks unaffiliated now, called Bea, the Root-Sworn and the Ashwalker. Plants and animals lean toward wherever she sleeps. Her staff is Foraye blackwood, wrapped in bark-skin shed from her own arm. She is still looking for the name. The commune told her the bark stops at the shoulder. Nobody knows whether it does, or whether the elders told her so to make the fear bearable.

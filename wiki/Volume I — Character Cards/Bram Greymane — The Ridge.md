# Bram Greymane — The Ridge

> **Age:** 23 · **House:** Greymane (Stormfold)
> **Level:** 224 · **Stage:** IV Temper · **Band:** III
> **Role:** Commander of the loyalist Greymane forces, holder of the southern approach
> Godfrey's grandson. The one who stayed.

## Description

Godfrey's face rebuilt by a younger hand. The same structural width through the jaw, the same heavy brow ridge designed to withstand impact, the same dark eyes that read terrain for defensive value before reading anything else. Brown hair, matted and unwashed because washing requires water that the garrison cannot spare. Twenty-three and showing the specific accelerated aging that war produces — the three years on the ridge and the fighting that followed filled the places that decades would normally fill.
His brigandine is cut in two places along the left side and stitched closed by Lilia's hand. He carries the Greymane shield, Godfrey's gift, with the ridge device on its face — scratched and dented and carrying the history of every impact it has absorbed. He threw it once, on the field where his brother smiled at him from behind the Tenrai banner. He picked it up. The picking-up was the apology. The apology was permanent.
**His brother:** Wren Greymane. The one who left. The one who joined the Tenrai. The one who smiled across two hundred paces of dead men and the smile was the wound that will not close.
**His words:** *"I held, Grandfather."*
**The farm:** Six acres behind the Greymane estate where Godfrey planted rye and turnips for two boys who needed to learn that hands were for things other than weapons. One boy cannot maintain six acres.

### Lore · The Life Behind the Card

*Written 2026-09-24 by the character-lore pass from this card, the twelve archived scenes it appears in, and the record of Hild's council: the Kōkan Loyalist Coalition. What those sources do not state is new here.*

#### Origin

Bram Greymane was born at the Ridgewall in the Stormfold. His house calls its lord by title. In his boyhood the Shieldwarden was his grandfather Godfrey, who stood at Muken Moto's right. When Godfrey's wife died he buried her on the ridge, and the next morning he rode to Kharven for the assembly.
Greymane children are raised as parts of a wall. On the wall walk their father told Bram and his brother Wren that the ridge did not move. Wren asked whether it ever had. Their father gave no answer. Bram kept the words.
The kingdom fell while he was a boy, and Godfrey held the western ridgeline for three years after it with his grandsons beside him. In the second winter Wren's lungs filled with fluid. The field surgeon said it would kill him. Bram sat nine days by the cot, changed the compress on his brother's chest every two hours, and did not sleep, and Wren lived. When the Tenrai assessors came looking for Greymane heirs, Bram lied to the border captains about where his brother was.
In the third year Godfrey cleared six acres behind the estate's western wall. Bram planted the rye. Wren planted the turnips. The old man watched from the wall above and meant them to learn that hands were for things other than weapons.

#### The Making

Godfrey put the Greymane shield in Bram's hands, the ridge device plain on its face. "A true knight defends his people," the old man told him. When the ridge could no longer hold itself with the men it had, he gave Bram a column and sent him south to hold the approach he could not see from the wall. Bram left the farm to go. Behind him the rows went to weeds.
The Tenrai killed Godfrey. Then a hobgoblin assault on Bram's column came timed to meet a Tenrai advance out of the north. Bram fought four hours at close quarters, and hobgoblin blades cut his brigandine twice along the left side. Near the end he threw the shield twelve paces into the mud.
He came back to himself on his knees. On the northern rise, under the banner of the line that had killed his grandfather, stood Wren, arms folded, head tilted, two hundred paces off. Bram roared across the field that when everyone had wanted Wren dead, he was the one who had kept him alive.
Wren smiled.
Then he raised one hand, and the Tenrai line turned and went back over the rise. Bram called his brother's name once. Wren did not turn round. Bram got to his feet on the third try. "I held," he told the field, and then, quietly, "I held, Grandfather." He limped the twelve paces, picked up the shield, and walked south toward the wounded.

#### The Cost

At the Greymane estate Lilia stitched both cuts while his right hand stayed closed on the shield's rim. She judged that he had chosen to say nothing. Hild Ice sent supplies south with Brida Ashwell, and four words from a girl he had never met: the ridge does not move. They were his own house's words. He took them as the alliance.
He kept the hate, because it got him up in the mornings and the men watched the man in front. Renard found the rat off a farrier's honest tally book. In the root cellar the farrier, Josse Hillborn, said, "I have not sold you, my lord," and nothing after. Then a letter under the Crown's wax called Bram Shieldwarden, the first time anyone had since the split, and asked for a public and attributable resolution. He had never killed one of his own. Every man he had put down had been marked for him first, by a horn or a banner or the green smoke off the Expanse. He found the shield's elbow strap shortened two fingers' width and restitched in a hand that was not a saddler's. He did not look at Lilia. He tightened the second buckle with his teeth and went toward the cellar stair.
On the day of the ninth hour he held a breach at the southern reach, twenty-two feet wide, with three hundred men. At the ninth hour he was still in it, on foot, with a dead horse behind him.
The year after, Lambert's grain schedule starved the orc host before Bram reached it, and his report read: "There was nothing to fight." When the remnant came north over the Stormfold crossings, he held them four days. On the second a falchion he never saw took the last three fingers of his left hand. He held the shield by the forearm strap to the end.
In the eighth month a bundle came from Lambert. It held Wren's name among the dead of the Ashgate road, body not recovered, and sixteen depositions from men who had marched sixteen months in a Tenrai column under a Greymane banner. Bram read them behind a shut door for a day and a half. Then he wrote four lines into the Stormfold book with the fingers he had left: "This campaign is dedicated to Wren Greymane, who was called traitor in every village between here and the Stormfold for sixteen months, by me among others." Any man who says the word in his hearing answers to him. The dedication stands before his own name.
In the tenth month he kept Vresk Dokkan, whose men had come through his breach, eleven days in the lower yard of the Stormfold hall. "Remember when your men took my fingers," he said. He took a fingertip, then the toes, one for each comrade dead in the war, each entered against a name on a roll. Then he sent the commander home alive. Lambert had briefed him on Old Vaross naming: the Keth-Gorrum must rename a returning officer, and they had no compound for what came back in the cart.

#### Where They Stand

He walked in from the Stormfold road for the signing of the first Accord circle, three fingers short, and would not sit down. He sits rarely now, and gets up badly, because of the hip.
Sodoku Moto sent him to the wall. In the fourth year the survey meant to run the southern spur across the eleven miles where the orc host had starved, ground with no stone on it. Bram had a chain laid along the survey line and sat down on a rock at its southern end with the bad hip out straight. The survey party asked under whose authority.
"Mine," he said.
The line goes round the moor, and the curve is called the Long Way. The shield hangs in the Stormfold hall with its boss cracked through in two directions. The rye behind the western wall wants two boys. Egil Vald and Cass Holloway are on the roll in the Stormfold book. Wren Greymane is not. Nobody has asked him why, and he has not said.

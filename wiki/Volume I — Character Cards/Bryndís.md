### X · Techniques

The source names no technique of hers. Both entries below are described in the text and unnamed in it; names are **originated, pending ratification** and are held to plain description for that reason.

#### The reshaping (unnamed)

- **Effect:** a corpse "reshaped itself into that grotesque avian thing," and the result is capable of speech — it reaches into a grave and laughs.
- **Cost / Limit:** `null`.
- **Counter (R12-3 mandatory):** it requires a body, and it requires the body to have been someone. The working's power in the scene is not the creature's strength but the recognition it forces; denying the identification — refusing to hear the malformed thing as the person it is shaped from — removes what it was made to do. Nobody in the scene manages this.
- **What nobody knows:** whether anything of the original remains in the reshaped thing, or whether the resemblance is entirely her craft.
- **The Test (R17-2):** it does *reshaping* to *a corpse*, which under *her presence* produces *a grotesque avian thing that speaks with a dead man's claim on the living*.

#### The laughter (unnamed)

- **Effect:** "A laugh resonated from her frame… the hall was filled with a torrential gust from her laughter, the walls shaking and the earth tremoring underneath it's influence."
- **Cost / Limit:** `null`. It appears to be involuntary in the sense that it is a genuine reaction — she is actually amused.
- **Counter:** it is triggered by bravado, and it costs her nothing to produce, so it cannot be baited into exhaustion. What it can be denied is the occasion: she laughs at "his bravado, the vainglory in his tone." Give her nothing to be amused by.
- **What nobody knows:** whether she can do it deliberately, or only when something is actually funny to her.
- **The Test:** it does *resonance* to *the hall and the ground beneath it*, which under *her own amusement* produces *a torrential gust that shakes walls without a blow being struck*.

### XIV · Psychology

**What she does with the dead.** This is her defining act in the scene, and it is aimed rather than general. She reaches for **Tomuka** and **Ezo** — Sonzai's brothers — and the effect is total: "Sonzai heard the name of his brothers. Everything else disappeared. The armies beyond the walls, the monsters crawling through the dark, the ruined tower, the blood on the stones, even Kujo standing somewhere beyond Bryndís became secondary to that single sentence."
The narration is precise about why it lands: "Kujo had already torn those wounds open once, and now this malformed thing had reached into the same grave and laughed." She is working a wound somebody else opened.
**What she finds funny.** Bravado. "A laugh resonated from her frame, amused by his bravado, the vainglory in his tone."
**What she is tired of.** The same bravado, which is the joke's second half: "All of it was something she had already heard merely fourteen times, truly some things never did change." The count is the only number in her material and it is doing character work rather than system work — fourteen is small enough to be exact and large enough to be a pattern. She is old enough to be bored and still entertained.
**Voice.** Amused, unhurried, contemptuous of grandeur. Fuller fingerprint: **`null`** — the scene gives her narrated laughter and reported speech rather than sustained dialogue.

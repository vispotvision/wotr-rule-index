### Lore · The Life Behind the Card

*Written 2026-09-24 by the character-lore pass from this card and the record of The Star Chamber's Protocol Corps. What those sources do not state is new here.*

#### Origin

Cassiel Asterion was born in Altherion, on the Ring's rim, to a woman who cut seals for hire. Her shop stood in the street below the River Crown Council's chamber. Delegations from the round table came down to have their agreements cut in hardstone. He swept her bench and fed the wheel.
She taught him the facet before his letters. A face cut true to its neighbours sent light back at the angle it came in. A face cut a hair off threw it where nobody had aimed. When a stone did that, she ground the face back and began again. She cut the words a customer spoke and no others. He came into the Engravers' company through her, a freeman by patrimony, and took up her stylus when her hand grew unsteady.

#### The Making

The stone that made him was one he cut himself. Two hauling companies divided the crater road between them, with a flourish: each would keep the road as it had always kept it. Cassiel cut the words as spoken and polished the stone clean. Each company read its own road in them. At the thaw both brought the stone to arbitration, and it proved both readings. The hearing broke up in the yard. A boy who held one company's teams went under a wagon in the crush and died on the cobbles. Cassiel was knocked down with the stone still in his hand. The arbiter voided it that evening.
After that he would not cut a promise spoken with embroidery. He sent customers back to say it plainly, and most took their custom to another bench.
He learned the estoc for the arbitration halls, where some quarrels went to a bout. He learned it as a way to receive. He took a lunge, turned his wrist and sent the same motion back along the truer edge. The man who had lunged kept his blood.
An envoy's party hired him to bring them out of a hall a crowd had closed. With one measured breath he drew the estoc through the air. A pale line stood there, hair-bright. A thrown stone reached it, slid along it and fell. The crowd came to the line and stopped. His blade could lay a meridian that objects preferred not to cross, and the Star Chamber's protocol corps sent for him.

#### The Cost

The corps seconded him to Vireline for Measures and to Lord Lucen Vale for high councils. Every working he owned redirected, trimmed or reseated. None of them struck.
At Ironlink Hold a factor-house partner answered a Measure all morning with embroidery and would not turn Lucen's Dawn Coin. Cassiel kept the seal still. Smoke from the hall's lamps frayed his line, and he had nothing against it until Ascella Morwyn set her lantern on the flags. The plain words came on a laugh. Pierre, hired by the carrying company's guild, had set up a harmless joke. The partner promised the grain at the old rate, as he had on the road. Cassiel turned the Facet Seal once. It took.
In the chamber above his mother's street, Lucen arranged a council for Arbitration, and Kaito Akagiri came as its liaison. Kaito matched the breathing of three delegates and led them half a step faster, and the room reached for a larger yes than either delegation had come to give. Cassiel laid his line between them, and the yes shrank to the terms both would sign. The heat stayed in the room. Kaede Suyori cooled it.
On the Rimlands road below the city, a Measure over a lost load of timber turned on a night-keeper nobody could wake. Arven Solthiel, the courier the Accord keeps off its register, sat the night at her bed. At dawn he gave her account in the hall, in her voice. Nothing in it was a promise. Cassiel turned no seal. He closed the Measure on words his seal never touched, and its record names a courier and gives no name.

#### Where They Stand

Cassiel serves the Star Chamber's protocol corps. He seats volatile councils, referees Measures where the clause is "angle, not blood", and walks envoys through siege halls behind meridians the mob will not cross. His hair is moon-violet. He wears a snow-and-ink coat latticed with gold facets, star brooches at throat and cuff, and carries the estoc Stellagraph like a conductor's wand, with a scribe's stylus.
Before a council sits he sets Lapidary Pins on its sills and tables. Lucen arranges the constellation and Cassiel sets the meridian. Marigold Vance clicks the gavel so their order sticks. Eryndrel Vaelune threads his shots along Cassiel's lines, and together they turn riots into corridors. When last words wobble at Yukizumi Yukari's bedsides, Cassiel straightens the angle they intended. Smoke, dust and drum-Domains still scatter his lines. He sends for Kaede or Ascella when the air goes wrong. Pure noon and pitch dark dull the prisms, and he works in council light.
His card calls Kaito a cold rival. Kaito's own card names only Kaede. The seal he turned at Ironlink Hold has never been turned back.

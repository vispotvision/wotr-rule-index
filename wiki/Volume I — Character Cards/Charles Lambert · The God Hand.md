# Charles Lambert · The God Hand

*Bishop of the Curia's Bench · Carrier of the Scale · as of the blessing at the basin*
> **Full Name:** Charles Lambert
> **Race / Lineage:** Human, of the Lambert house; younger brother of Edward Lambert
> **Office:** A bishop of the Curia's bench, and for ten years the carrying half of the office the family literature calls the God Hand. His stone gives him the rank of archbishop.
> **Age:** Forty-seven. His hair had not yet gone through to iron.
> **As Of:** The morning he brought his son to the basin for the Palatine's blessing, and was asked to go north.

---

## Description

He came up the aisle with his hat off and his son on his forearm, and the resemblance arrived a full three seconds before the name did. The frame is his brother's frame: tall and gone narrow through the shoulder, stooped a few degrees at the neck, the same long face and the same hard widow's peak. On Edward the shape reads as a man in decline. On Charles it reads as a man in his prime.
The bishop's charcoal, well cut and well kept, and a pectoral on a chain with all its links matched. Then the face, which is the difference. Edward's does nothing at all. Charles's is pleasant, open at the eyes and quick at the mouth, the face of a man people tell things to without deciding to.

---

## Voice

**Notices first** · The person across from him and the thing they have not yet said. He also sees how an act will read to the road: which of three things has changed, and who will notice it first.
**Sentence length** · Medium to long and courteous, built in threes ("under your name, on your instruction, or with you in the party"), and often closed with a dry turn that lands gently.
**Contractions** · sometimes.
**Pet word** · "Forgive me."
**Never says** · Anything about the office he carries or what it costs him, and nothing about his brother to his brother. A number in his own name never comes out of him; when a count does, it is Edward's arithmetic, and he says whose it is.
**Stumbles** · Rarely at ease. Before rank his voice thins at the top, and a sentence can lose its last word ("With respect, holiness, I have every.").
**Gloss rights** · For the rites and forms of the Church, yes: he says what a rite does in plain words, the way a bishop talks to a family at the font.
**Under stress** · The sentences shorten into apology and deference ("Your holiness. Forgive me."), and then the practical facts pour out in one breath, each one a reason to move. When it is worst he says nothing at all and waits, as the Lambert house taught him to.
**In grief** · The bench's polish goes and the Hallenfeld house comes back: plain words, a younger brother's words, and at the last a sound he has never made before, with nothing written after it.
**In joy** · The pleasant open face does the work; the speech stays courteous and the joy is in the eyes and the unhurried hands.
**Sample line** · "You are going to have to say the other thing. I have been sitting here waiting for you to say the other thing and you have taken a remarkably long time about it."

### Lore · The Life Behind the Card

#### Origin

He was the second of the Lambert brothers and came up in the Church, and rose to a bishop's bench. He had been sorting and filing since he was fourteen. In thirty-eight years in the Church the only thing about himself he had ever been proud of was his handwriting. He and Edward had not spoken in nine years.

#### The Making

Ten years before the narrows, in the lower chapel at Altherion, with four men present and the door barred, Aurelian Prudentius put the Scale over his head. It is a silver amulet the size of a thumbnail, two pans on a beam, with glyphs cut eleven to the inch. Darius signed second. The office is held between two: one carries and one spends, the carrier does not know when, the spender does not know where, and neither is told the other's condition. They gave it to the emptiest man in the building, a bishop with no faculty.
He married Anyu Qilak, from far enough north that the Concord had no name for her country beyond a compass point. At the basin the Palatine Verinus VII blessed their son, and the boy's eyes went gold. Charles gave his name as Dabney Charles Lambert, and the Palatine ruled that there would be no entry and no name in the day-book. Then Verinus asked Charles to go north with the Kusharan party as his hand, because he was the only bishop on that bench who read the objection before he read the motion.

#### The Cost

At Altherion, when Kaalabad's halberd had been flicked out of his hands, the general turned on Charles. Charles slipped four deliveries with empty hands before he was kneed and thrown, while Aurelian watched from four yards away and chose to do nothing.
In the narrows at Kharven he turned the Scale. *"Sit omne quod fieri potuit. Nihil perficiatur."* It took him into the Imperceptible District, and the price was records: nineteen years of vestry minutes, the ordination roll, the marriage line in the parish book at Vaeloris, every paper that said who he had been. Then he stepped into Kujo's grip in place of the one it held, his feet left the ice, and he closed both hands on the wrist and held on.
He learned there what the office was for. *A hand holds.*

#### Where They Stand

Charles Lambert is dead. His son lay on a keel north of the Sound in a fish crate, with a wet-nurse Charles had paid, and when the unwritten bond between them broke the boy took the whole of its release. Sodoku Moto, who had never met him, saw him on his back in the ice with no file on him anywhere, and learned his name eleven days later from a woman on a keel.
Edward spent six weeks in three archives and found nothing, because the parish book no longer holds his brother's dates. He set a small stone apart from the others: *Charles Lambert. Archbishop.* Nothing else. Dabney is Edward's ward now.

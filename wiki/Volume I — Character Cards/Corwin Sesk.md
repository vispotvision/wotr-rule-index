## X · Techniques

He has no techniques in the system sense and none is invented here. His method is documented and is recorded as method:

### The work, as he does it

- **Effect:** sustained interrogation of a hanging prisoner over fourteen days. Flesh taken off both thighs "in long strips beginning at the hip and working down, six on the left and five on the right, each one lifted with a hooked blade and peeled with the patience of a man skinning a hare he intends to eat." Ten nails, one to each fingertip, driven under the nail through the pulp and seated into the bone.
- **Procedure:** "He had put in the nails the way a cooper puts in a hoop, checked his work, wiped the mallet head, and sat down."
- **Limit, self-imposed and professional:** he arrived while others were pulling molars and "put a stop to it on the grounds that a man who cannot form words is no use to anybody." Four were already out; the sockets abscessed by the fifth day.
- **The tell that gives the game away:** the prisoner is given water "twice a day and they gave him enough," and Aurelian reads it correctly — "A man being tortured for information is kept thirsty because thirst breaks people cheaply. A man being kept is kept hydrated." Somebody wanted Aurelian alive "on a timeline that extended past the end of Sesk's patience."
- **Counter (R12-3, adapted — this is a method, not a working):** the questions were never the point, which means nothing the prisoner says ends it. The only thing that ends it is the instruction above Sesk changing. He is not the party to be negotiated with, and Aurelian works this out and stops trying.
- **What nobody knows:** what he was actually instructed to produce, given that it was not answers.

## XIII · Physical Description

The source gives posture and habit rather than appearance, and the card does not fill the gap.

He is **against the wall** when he is not working, and says nothing. He speaks **to the ledger** rather than to the room: "'Five hundred and ninety-one,' Sesk said, to the ledger." Giving his report, "Sesk's jaw worked once."

Hair, face, build, clothing, distinguishing marks: **`pending Isaac`**. Table Rule 11's inventory is not in this source and will not be manufactured for a man this carefully underdescribed.

## XIV · Psychology

**The thing that defines him, in the source's own framing.** Not cruelty. The opposite, and the scene says the opposite is worse:

> "He was not cruel. That was the thing Aurelian had established by the second day and had found genuinely disturbing. Sesk had no appetite for any of it. He had put in the nails the way a cooper puts in a hoop, checked his work, wiped the mallet head, and sat down."

A cruel man would have a reason. Sesk has a task and a standard, and he meets the standard, and then he sits down.

**What he refuses.** Waste. He stops the molar extraction not because it is inhumane but because it is counterproductive — it damages the instrument he has been given. His limits are all of this kind, and the card should not soften them into mercy.

**What he stops.** On the twelfth day he stopped working, and the scene marks it by what became audible again once he did: "it had come back to him on the twelfth, when Sesk stopped working and nothing was left in the cell loud enough to stand in front of it." The reason for stopping is not given here.

**What he insists on.** The accuracy of the record, and he insists on it against his own interest. In his report:

> "On the second day I told him the arrangement stood. I said the words. Four alive and the books untouched, and he asked me to say it plainly and I said it plainly, and he thanked me." Sesk's jaw worked once. "That was true when I said it. I have since established that it was already false by about nine days and I did not know, and I want it in the record that I did not know, because a man in my tr[ade]—"

He was thanked for a promise that was already void, and he did not know, and what he wants entered is precisely the boundary of his own knowledge. This is the same man who lays flesh out in order of removal. It is one trait, not two.

**Voice.** Flat, exact, procedural. Counts aloud to a ledger. Identifies himself with his instruction attached. Speaks in short declaratives with the qualifications placed carefully — "That was true when I said it." Goes silent when there is nothing procedural to say.

## XV · Equipment

Tools of a trade, not a loadout:

- **A hooked blade** — used to lift the strips.
- **A mallet**, wiped down after use.
- **Drawing pliers** and **a wedge** for the back teeth — used by others before he stopped it, and available to him.
- **Nails**, ten of them, and the scene is specific that they are **not iron**: "Iron would have rusted and rust was a smell he knew. These drank the lamplight off their heads and gave nothing back." What they are is **`pending Isaac`**, and it matters — the blood around them ran red for about a minute and then went black.
- **A ledger**, and something to write with, and a crate to sit on.

No weapon, no armour, no proof-marks. Mass, length and balance are not given for any of it and are not invented.

## XVII · Fracture Log

Not a practitioner's fracture log. Recorded as the source has it:

- **The promise he kept that was already broken.** "Four alive and the books untouched," said plainly on the second day because he was asked to say it plainly, and thanked for it — and false by about nine days at the time he said it. He did not know. His response is to put the not-knowing in the record himself. Unconsolidated, and it is the only thing in either scene that visibly costs him anything.
- **The twelfth day.** He stopped. The source does not say why, and does not say what happened to him after. **`pending Isaac`**.
- **The nails that are not iron.** Entered here rather than in XV because he noticed what they were not, and a man who knows the smell of rust well enough to rule it out has done this a great many times.

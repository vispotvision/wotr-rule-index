#### The work, as he does it

- **Effect:** sustained interrogation of a hanging prisoner over fourteen days. Flesh taken off both thighs "in long strips beginning at the hip and working down, six on the left and five on the right, each one lifted with a hooked blade and peeled with the patience of a man skinning a hare he intends to eat." Ten nails, one to each fingertip, driven under the nail through the pulp and seated into the bone.
- **Procedure:** "He had put in the nails the way a cooper puts in a hoop, checked his work, wiped the mallet head, and sat down."
- **Limit, self-imposed and professional:** he arrived while others were pulling molars and "put a stop to it on the grounds that a man who cannot form words is no use to anybody." Four were already out; the sockets abscessed by the fifth day.
- **The tell that gives the game away:** the prisoner is given water "twice a day and they gave him enough," and Aurelian reads it correctly — "A man being tortured for information is kept thirsty because thirst breaks people cheaply. A man being kept is kept hydrated." Somebody wanted Aurelian alive "on a timeline that extended past the end of Sesk's patience."
- **Counter (R12-3, adapted — this is a method, not a working):** the questions were never the point, which means nothing the prisoner says ends it. The only thing that ends it is the instruction above Sesk changing. He is not the party to be negotiated with, and Aurelian works this out and stops trying.

### XIV · Psychology

**The thing that defines him, in the source's own framing.** Not cruelty. The opposite, and the scene says the opposite is worse:
> "He was not cruel. That was the thing Aurelian had established by the second day and had found genuinely disturbing. Sesk had no appetite for any of it. He had put in the nails the way a cooper puts in a hoop, checked his work, wiped the mallet head, and sat down."
A cruel man would have a reason. Sesk has a task and a standard, and he meets the standard, and then he sits down.
**What he refuses.** Waste. He stops the molar extraction not because it is inhumane but because it is counterproductive — it damages the instrument he has been given. His limits are all of this kind, and the card should not soften them into mercy.
**What he stops.** On the twelfth day he stopped working, and the scene marks it by what became audible again once he did: "it had come back to him on the twelfth, when Sesk stopped working and nothing was left in the cell loud enough to stand in front of it." The reason for stopping is not given here.
**What he insists on.** The accuracy of the record, and he insists on it against his own interest. In his report:
> "On the second day I told him the arrangement stood. I said the words. Four alive and the books untouched, and he asked me to say it plainly and I said it plainly, and he thanked me." Sesk's jaw worked once. "That was true when I said it. I have since established that it was already false by about nine days and I did not know, and I want it in the record that I did not know, because a man in my tr[ade]—"
He was thanked for a promise that was already void, and he did not know, and what he wants entered is precisely the boundary of his own knowledge. This is the same man who lays flesh out in order of removal. It is one trait, not two.
**Voice.** Flat, exact, procedural. Counts aloud to a ledger. Identifies himself with his instruction attached. Speaks in short declaratives with the qualifications placed carefully — "That was true when I said it." Goes silent when there is nothing procedural to say.

### Lore · The Life Behind the Card

*Written 2026-09-24 by the character-lore pass from this card, the five archived scenes it appears in, and the record of The Curia at Altherion. What those sources do not state is new here.*

#### Origin

Corwin Sesk was raised by his mother, who had no trade to hand down to him. She gave him a certain amount of sense, and he was still using it at fifty, when he stood before Kujo's Generals and kept his eyes on their table.
He went out to hired work young. The men who paid him paid by the task. He learned to finish a task to a standard, check it, and sit down.
By twenty-six he had noticed that every employer he had ever had read grovelling as weakness. He made a policy of it that year. When he bowed to a man who paid him, he bowed his head, held it two breaths and came up straight.
He had a clerk's ear before he had a trade. He heard the order in which a man said things, and he heard when that order had been arranged.

#### The Making

He came to the work at twenty-eight. Nobody ever explained it to him. It was corrected and it was priced, and he learned it one prisoner at a time, mostly by being wrong first. He worked in cellars and gaols, in one abandoned mine and in a tannery, and each told him by its smell where he was before his eyes did. He took a knee off a Dawi hammerman, who screamed until he was sick. He once broke a man in four hours by threatening a dog. Four of his contracts ended badly for other people.
He kept what he learned as method. A man softened on a curve, defiant on the first day and bargaining by the third. Sesk never lied to a prisoner about the physical work. He held that a man in his trade lived on his prisoners believing his statements of fact, and that belief was the one asset he carried into any cellar.
He wore good brown wool with the sleeves pinned above the elbow, and a leather apron he took off to eat. The ledger rode on his knee. He had a daughter in Foraye.
The Altherion contract came to him under instruction, for a cellar beneath the Grand Church, and he reported to Kujo. He arrived on the second day. Men with a wedge and drawing pliers already had four molars out of the prisoner's lower jaw. Sesk stopped them, on the grounds that a man who cannot form words is no use to anybody.
The prisoner was Aurelian Prudentius, the Word of Judger, who had walked in on his own feet for four of his Attestants and a scriptorium. That day Sesk told him the arrangement stood, and Aurelian asked him to say it plainly. Sesk said it plainly. Four alive and the books untouched. Aurelian thanked him. It had been false for about nine days, and Sesk did not know.

#### The Cost

He did the work to his standard and wrote almost none of it down. He took eleven strips from Aurelian's thighs, six on the left and five on the right, and laid them on the flags in the order of removal. He seated ten nails, one to each fingertip. Then he checked his work, wiped the mallet head and sat down on his crate. He broke the left kneecap with the same mallet. He had no appetite for any of it.
On the seventh night he left the chain slack enough that a man with one working arm could have had the ring off the hook inside a minute. He sat the night out in the passage with the door on the latch. In the morning Aurelian was hanging where he had left him, and said good morning. Nothing in twenty-two years had frightened Sesk more.
On the eleventh day he went down to the council chamber beneath Vaeloris and gave Kujo his report in ledger order. By then he had found the roster and checked it himself. The four had gone out through the low cloister in the second week of the shutdown. He had not told the prisoner. What remained to be done was eyes, tongue, hands and genitals, and he would do none of it without an instruction in a form he could produce afterward. He argued for stopping. He asked to have it entered that on the second day he had not known.
The chamber gave him nothing back. He had used that on other men for twenty-two years. He knew what it was doing to him. He kept talking. He offered four counterfeit people, any four, producible once at range in poor light. On the way down he had decided not to make that offer.
Kujo would have no counterfeit four. He ended the physical work and ordered that the prisoner be given no false assurance about the four. Sesk nodded through the lesson that came with the ruling. That night, in his room, he would enjoy it.
Then Kaalabad turned his head and looked at him. For perhaps four seconds Sesk's breath stopped. His hands stayed at his sides, and afterward that was the one thing he was proud of. He heard a gap in the halberdier's list of three houses. He called it bloodlust. He kept it with the grey powder under Kujo's table, among the things he had decided not to want to know.

#### Where They Stand

On the twelfth day he came down the stair with his apron folded over his arm and hung it on the nail by the door. He never put it on again. He fed Aurelian and watered him twice a day from a tin cup with a bent lip, held at the angle that let the water past the sockets. He took up the chain each morning and let it out each night. On the thirteenth day, unasked, he told Aurelian that the arrangement stood.
He kept one count honestly. It was the times Aurelian said his line, and on the fourteenth morning it stood at five hundred and ninety-one. "I have been correct for fourteen days," he said, "and I would give a year's fee to know what it has bought."
Then the floor lit. He swore and backed to the wall. The ledger lay where it fell. Verinus came down in the Palatine's gold, and Sesk bowed his head for two breaths and gave his name with his instruction attached. Verinus told him he was the only man in the building who had written down what he did. Then the old man signed for four people who were already gone. Sesk stood against the wall with the roster in his coat and his palm flat on the stone. He said nothing. He had never wanted a written instruction so badly. The wounds went across the room into Verinus, and the eleven strips stayed on the flags where Sesk had laid them.
He has no instruction in writing, and the roster is still in his coat. Nobody has said what he was hired to produce, since it was never answers. The ledger holds one entry he has never explained. His wrong reading of Kaalabad's list was to keep him alive eleven weeks longer.

#### Ties

- **Aurelian Prudentius Custos Clausorum · The Primate** · captive and interrogator. Sesk worked on him for fourteen days without once lying about the work, then kept the roster of the four in his coat.
- **Verinus VII · The Palatine** · witness. Sesk watched him pay, from the wall, for four people already gone, and said nothing.
- **Kaalabad** · fellow instruments. The halberdier stopped Sesk's breath with a look beneath Vaeloris, and Sesk heard the gap in his list and never mentioned it.
- **Kujo** · employer. Sesk gave the Regent his report in ledger order, volunteered the offer he had meant to keep back, and was taught in return what his own trade was.
- **His daughter in Foraye** · kin. He has not written in five weeks, and the letter must be worded carefully now.

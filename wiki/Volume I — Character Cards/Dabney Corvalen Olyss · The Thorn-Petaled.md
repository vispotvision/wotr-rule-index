### Lore · The Life Behind the Card

*Written 2026-09-24 by the character-lore pass from this card, the fourteen archived scenes it appears in, and the record of The Curia at Altherion. What those sources do not state is new here.*

#### Origin

Dabney was born in the Palatinate of the Holy Sea of Alabaster. His father was Charles Lambert, a bishop of the Curia's bench. His mother was Anyu Qilak, from far enough north that the Concord had no name for her country beyond a compass point. She had been a Lambert since the spring.
At four months he came up the aisle of the lower chapel at the Alabaster Court on his father's forearm. At the basin Charles gave his name as Dabney Charles Lambert. Verinus VII took him in both arms, and a small fist worked out of the wool and closed on the front of the Palatine's grey. The floor moved under the old man. He had never been given a reading on a person who had not opened his mouth.
He blessed the child anyway. He set two wet fingers on the boy's forehead and spoke the blessing, and Dabney's eyes went gold. The gold came up through the brown from the inner ring, held for perhaps two seconds and sank back. The child did not react. His mother took him back and sat in the third row with her cheek on his head for a quarter of an hour.
On the second step Verinus told Charles he had seen that gold in one bloodline, the crown line of the Kōkan Moto, and that neither parent explained it. Then he gave his ruling. There would be no entry, no record of the blessing and no name in the day-book. Dabney would not be written down.

#### The Making

The Palatine sent Charles north as his hand. By the fourteenth day the boy lay asleep on a keel north of the Sound, in a fish crate lined with the second petticoat of Alys Fenwick. She was a wet-nurse his father had paid, and he had told her the sum and not the reason.
That day Charles died in the narrows. The bond between father and son had never been written in any book, and when it broke the child took the whole of its release. He woke at once, with no fretting first. His cry climbed past anything a chest that size should reach. The lamp went out with its flame standing straight, and cold came into the cabin from every side. He had no training in him and nothing to slow what came out, and he did not come apart.
Alys saw threads stand out from him and from everything in the cabin, all of them leaning toward him, and later described them to the Bench as wet hair in water. He was rigid from crown to heels. He got both arms up off his chest, open and high, the way infants ask to be lifted. It was hard. They stayed up. His eyes opened gold with a ring turning inside the gold, and the boat came up level under her feet in a swell that should have put her against the bulkhead. She sat down against the crate and put her body between him and the door.
In the same hour, in the ash at Sum-gol, Gimbzo told Darius Ignatius Sanctus Sanctorum Arsenal that a child somewhere had already opened. He charged Darius to teach that child the Ways. The gate that opened behind Darius carried rigging, cold water and a child at the very top of his register.
Dabney grew up in a household that could not produce a father for him on paper. Verinus had kept him out of the day-book on purpose. The parish book at Vaeloris that had held Charles's dates no longer contained them, and nobody in four worlds could say why. He became the ward of his father's brother, Edward Lambert.
At six he sat at the end of Edward's council table in a chair too large for him, his feet short of the floor. When Lord Marmont spoke, the boy's eyes went to the man's hands. Edward asked what he thought. He opened his mouth, closed it and opened it again. "The chair," he said. "The one nobody's sitting in." Edward said the boy was right and did not look at him again. That year, when he scratched his nose, the grain of the whole Palatinate approach adjusted: the wall, the stone, the porter, the gulls, and something four streets east. He did not know he had done it.
At some point before he could keep a memory, he accepted a real cost to his own soul, in advance and in cold blood, to keep something he valued above his own structure. He did not remember it. Edward asked him about it in eleven different ways over four years and never once got an answer. He did not think the boy was refusing him. He thought it had happened before there was a person in there to keep the memory, and the rung was still bolted on.

#### The Cost

At five he asked whether there was a way to be more careful. He was told to slow down. He said no, and asked again for a way to be more careful. Nobody had an answer for him. He went on believing that care was something a person reached by trying harder.
Seven years of forms under Darius followed in the Palatinate. Darius repeated a single lesson: "Never show anyone your true power." He said it on the training floor, in the corridor and over meals, until it stopped being instruction and became the shape of how the boy sat in a room. Nothing about Dabney reached past the edge of his own chair.
He had never had a friend his own age. He asked whether the sea was different in other places or the same sea being called things. He asked whether there were children in Kharven. The Academy was the answer: a corridor with eleven hundred children in it, none of whom had heard of him. That was the argument for it, and it had nothing to do with the curriculum.

#### Where They Stand

Dabney is thirteen. The cowlick at his crown has survived seven years and Darius. His hands are larger than the rest of him, and the knuckles show their first calluses. He is still barefoot when nobody is watching. He keeps a grey and white cat, mostly ears, called Astro, named off a chart on Edward's wall; he does not know it is the name of a body in the sky. Nobody has given him a byname.
He sits at the back of Class X with his hands folded on the desk. When the Class X Open Tournament was set seven days out, he settled on entering at half. Half would look like a talented thirteen-year-old with good instruction behind him, and nobody would start the other conversation. Across the room Xanelor Rafiminar, eyes shut, felt him as one dense point, fine as a filed edge. Dabney did not look up.
In the Arena he took the second tier and watched the masked boy's feet. When what the masked boy had stored came up raw through his whole body, Dabney's hands came unfolded on his knee. He put them back, deliberately, one over the other.

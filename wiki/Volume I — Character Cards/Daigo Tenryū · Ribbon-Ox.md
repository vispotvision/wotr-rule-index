### Lore · The Life Behind the Card

*Written 2026-09-24 by the character-lore pass from this card and the record of The Tenryū and the dragon-blooded. What those sources do not state is new here.*

#### Origin

Daigo Tenryū was born to the Tenryū, whose people turn what they take in into force. His kinsman Goraku turned brew and blood-fire. Daigo turned sweets and rhythm. He was a Sky-oni and dragon-blooded, and he grew up around festival truces, where trade went on under watchful dragons.
The boy always had a skewer of dango in one hand. When another child wanted a promise from him, he had no hand free for a knot. He held the red cord in his teeth and tied it that way, and the other child kept the far end.
Hungry, he was strong. Fed, he cleared a stall at a run.
He kept his first fasting rite and went woozy and poetic before it ended. A quarrel over a pitch came to blows at the fair's edge. He was no use in it. Afterward, still fasting, he sat down between the stallholders and talked until they agreed to share the pitch.

#### The Making

He went where crowds packed a road. Tapped together, his horns boomed like shrine drums, and people who had been pushing began to walk. His cords were silk with tiny bells. They bound without bruising. He travelled with the Accord's harvest convoys to keep the crowds at the carts moving.
On the Wheat March, on the Caedor plains, cold rain lay on the harvest, and a line of the Rot's speaking mold came up through the wet sheaves toward the villages. The convoy stood at the field edge with its carts half loaded. Orya of the Wheat March walked the flank of the line with her crook, whistling cross-drafts through the stooks, and Khyr went ahead of her breathing warm gusts over the grain. The mold had already reached some mouths among the field hands. The villages began to crowd onto the road.
Daigo tapped his horns together. He walked the frightened into a marching column along the carts, and the crowd matched his beat before it knew it had decided to. Then he bit a dango and breathed out over the ones the mold had reached. The warm sugar-dust cleared their mouths. The line broke short of the villages. The convoy carried the grain out dry, and that night Khyr took a gourd off the lead wagon and sat on it.
From that season Daigo herded the people, and Orya herded the drakes.

#### The Cost

Goraku was war-leader of the Braqth Raiding Courts. Daigo was at one of their feasts on the Braqth coast when the wandering Brew-Sage, Kurokiba Ranzou, came in off the road, and the hall set Goraku against him in a drinking contest. Each man drank from his own gourd while the hall kept the pace with drums and chanting. Goraku ran hotter with every draught. Steam came off his skin. The blood-fire spilled around his seat each time he shifted, and he did not slow.
Daigo tapped his horns together, and the boom went through the basalt. The hall took his beat. The chanting slowed under it. He went between the two men, and with his teeth he tied a red cord on each man's wrist, on a quick promise: the contest would be finished on a night when he stood in the hall. Both men let him tie it. Nobody lost.
It left him two cord-ends, a night owed to him by each man, and a contest that could be finished with him in the hall and on no other night.
He tied no cord to hurt anyone. Against true malice his cords would not hold.
The skewer never left his hand, and he fed himself from it and bribed others with it. Starved, he was strong and nothing more. Overcast moods dulled him. Under a joyless commander his horns still boomed, and the ranks kept their own step.

#### Where They Stand

He is called Ribbon-Ox and the Dango Dragon: vanguard breaker, pact-binder, morale engine. His sapphire hair is worn in a festive snarl, red cord bands his ivory horns, and his sandals spark when he laughs.
Vireline of the Three Masks often taps him as champion for a cheerful, crowd-pleasing Measure. Kaede Suyori cools his over-eager pushes, and he carries them through riot heat like a breeze. Where Kaito Akagiri has led a room's breathing into fever, Daigo's horns reset its beat, and Kaito hates the laughter that breaks it. Eryndrel Vaelune stitches his routes on Daigo's ribbons and Orya's ripples, using both as moving posts.
After Lyra Drakoshi's ceasefire opened her road across the Southern Grass Sea, the watch post at her gate sent for someone to anchor a festival truce. Daigo came. He set the fair's pace with his horns, turned the crowd at the gate into columns and bit red cords into knots on the traders' quick promises. Lyra kept the gate as the watching dragon. No blade came out that season. The traders came back the next season, and the one after.
He keeps his ends of those cords, and the two from Braqth. Neither man has sat the second night.

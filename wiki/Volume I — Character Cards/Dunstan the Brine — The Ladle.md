Indeterminate age, somewhere between forty and sixty. Build of a man who was heavy once and has been reduced by ship-work to a harder, more economical version. ### Lore · The Life Behind the Card

*Written 2026-09-24 by the character-lore pass from this card, the nine archived scenes it appears in, and the record of The Kōkan expedition's hands. What those sources do not state is new here.*

#### Origin

Dunstan was born in a harbour town of the Concord, in the rooms over a cookshop on the quay. His mother kept the shop. She fed crews between voyages out of the victuallers' barrels: salt pork, barley, flour when there was flour.
He stood at her range before he could see into the pot. He learned the fire first, when to feed it and when to bank it. Then he learned the pork. It came out of the brine hard as a boot sole and had to be soaked, boiled and soaked again before a sailor would eat it. His mother never measured anything. She cooked by feel and by timing, and he learned it the same way.
Sailors on the quay named him the Brine, for the tubs he stood over and the smell he carried out of them. He answered to it. He never called himself by it.
The shop made him heavy. He ate what did not sell.

#### The Making

He went to sea late, a heavy man in a galley built for a thin one. Ship-work pared him down, voyage by voyage, to a harder frame. The steam reddened his face and never let it dry. He learned to cook with the pots lashed to the range and the deck rolling under them. Crews ate what he made without comment. Among men long at sea that was the highest compliment on offer, and he took it.
He signed on as cook, quartermaster and provisions officer for the expedition that carried the Moto brothers north. He worked the galley hatch amidships and passed up bowls of salt pork and barley, seasoned with whatever spice the stores held that was strong enough to make the pork forgettable.
At the rift camp he swore seventh, after Nella Forgeman, and walked to the oath-distance wiping his hands on his apron. He gave his name as Dunstan and left the Brine off. He told the brothers he could not fight or inscribe. "I can feed you. I can keep the galley hot when everything else is cold. I can make salt pork edible, which is the closest thing to alchemy that a man without Attunement can perform." Then he swore that no one under their command would go hungry "while I have hands and a fire." He nodded and went back to the supply cache.
Borin's forge on the ice burned rendered fat. The fat came out of the galley stores. Dunstan let it go. He entered it in his count beside the meals.
When the camp struck he banked the cooking fire, packed the galley crate and closed the supply loads with Robin Ice before the column formed. On the march he carried the galley kit and the provisions inventory. He had counted every calorie in the cache. He knew how many days it gave them, and he told nobody, because nobody asked.
On the fifth day Yoko named two Titan-class Droval. Dunstan set down the provisions sled's lead bar and picked up the largest ladle in the galley kit, two and a half pounds of cast iron on a wooden handle. It was what his hands found. "I want it entered into whatever record we are pretending to maintain," he said, "that the cook brought a ladle."

#### The Cost

When the Droval roared, Nella caught his arm. He was the nearest man there who could not fight. He held the ladle in both hands and knew it would not help. He kept holding it until the first Droval settled on the ice with Sodoku on its skull, and Nella's grip never opened the whole time. Robin looked at the ladle in Dunstan's hands. Dunstan looked at it too.
"I think," he said, "we can stop pretending the record exists."
He had sworn only what he could keep: "That is the oath I can give, and the oath I can give is the oath I will keep." The galley was the whole of his offer. It made every mouth under the brothers' command his charge.
The count was his alone. He held that a column marches better without the number of days left in its stores, until the day it has to know. So he carried that number across the shelf by himself, and the fat burned in Borin's forge had already come off it.
He kept his given name for his own use. The Brine stayed in other people's mouths.

#### Where They Stand

He came in with the column to the north gate of Kharven-Seat, his provisions sled in the line of march. Back by the sledges he argued loudly with nobody over whether the cast-iron ladle was personal property or crown property, now that crown ground lay under it. Nella told him it counted as an assault charge. Nobody has ruled on the ladle.
The expedition's galley stores have gone into the count Edward Lambert keeps at Kharven-Seat by the barrel.
His oath named no road's end. It holds while he has hands and a fire.

### Lore · The Life Behind the Card

*Written 2026-09-24 by the character-lore pass from this card, the one archived scene it appears in, and the record of The Night Register. What those sources do not state is new here.*

#### Origin

Edwyn Brack was born in the drift country of the north, where the ice had left its gravel and clay over the rock. His father kept the gauges on a pumping array there, under a supply contract, for the Logistics Division. Each morning he read the draw off the brass and entered it in the array's column. The boy stood at his elbow while he did it. Soon he read the brass himself.
The vein under the array began to fail before the boy was grown. The gauges fell and kept falling. His father entered the draw the contract called for, and the brass showed less. He did it for a season. The Logistics Division published its returns, and somebody in its offices read that column against the ones before it. The array was shut. His father was struck from the Division's roll, and the family left the gauge-house before the frost. His father never kept a gauge again.
The boy had read the brass on most of those mornings. He had known the true figure each time. He had said nothing.

#### The Making

He came to the Lattice Classification Bureau as a clerk and stayed to classify for its northern office. He wrote to the instrument. A classification went out as a line of a few words with a note beneath it. The coils the Bureau paid for read down to the Bureau's floor, and he wrote to what they read. He left out a walker's hunch. He left out a clerk's unease, and his own. What the instrument showed went into the line, and the note carried only what the measurement would bear.
His signature came to be on half the classifications out of the northern office. Closed filings went on to Cutler Row. In the experience of the officer who read them there, on the Night Register, Brack had never been wrong in a way that mattered.
Nol Tally's insert came to him from the Timberline chapter of the Night Watch Society, off the North Ferriby walk. Tally had found hinds and a stag dead where they stood in the Ferriby cut. A grey-green mass had come up through the hide along the spine and the flanks. The tissue under it had not decayed. Brack knew how a vein came up through drift. He had watched one give out under his father's gauges. He returned the insert inside two days as "Wellspring bleed, Verdantia, tertiary expression, no operator indicated." His note said the vein had probably surfaced through a fault in the drift geology and would exhaust itself by autumn. It recommended no further action and no file. Two of the Arbitration day-desk agreed before they had finished reading.

#### The Cost

He believed a return should hold the measurement and nothing past it. His father's column had shown him the other kind, and what it took from a household. No return of his was pulled for saying too much. The same rule kept out whatever no coil had read.
Tally found the last carcass short of a parish boundary marker, and he walked the clean ground on the far side of it twice. He put that in his insert's last paragraph, the one the bulletin office nearly cut for length. Brack's return did not mention the boundary. Neither did the day-desk.
His classification closed the Ferriby deaths inside two days, with no file opened on them. It was exactly the classification Malphas's method was built to produce. Neither man knew the other.

#### Where They Stand

Brack classifies for the northern office, and his signature is on half of what comes out of it. His Ferriby return went on to Cutler Row. Wystan Ashmore read it there beside Tally's insert. On a night after the fourteenth, Qiu Yinzhi broke the chapter's wax on the section she had sealed in the cut. Her sweep below the Bureau's floor found the residue signed by the hind. At the bench Wystan said that Brack was not a fool and was not corrupt, and that he had written the correct classification off a true measurement. The wedge has been a Register exhibit since that night, under wax struck with Wystan's brass.
His note still gives the vein until autumn.

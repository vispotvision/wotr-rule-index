# Garret Longshore — The Helmsman

*The Helmsman · The Man Who Trusts Nothing*
> **Full Name:** Garret Longshore
> **Age:** 48 · **Origin:** Concord (western fishing settlements)
> **Level:** 38 · **Stage:** I Ignition · **Band:** I
> **Role:** Navigator and helmsman for the Kōkan expedition
> Believes nothing. Calculates everything. Arrives anyway.

---

Broad hands cracked and salt-bleached. Forearms roped with tendon-definition from a life pulling rope against the ocean's opinion. Long, angular face, clean-shaven because beards freeze. Eyes washed-out blue from years squinting at horizons. Heavy woollen coat over canvas smock, both stained with tar and salt.
Holds the wheel with one hand and watches the compass with the eye of a man who does not trust the compass but trusts the sea less. Navigates by the tension between two distrusts.
His oath: *"I know how to hold a wheel in bad weather and how to read a compass I do not trust and how to keep a ship alive in water that wants it dead. If those things are useful, they are yours."*
When Temür walked out of the igloo in glacier-crystal armor, Garret adjusted his compass heading and said nothing. Nothing was the appropriate response.

### Lore · The Life Behind the Card

*Written 2026-09-24 by the character-lore pass from this card, the ten archived scenes it appears in, and the record of The Kōkan expedition's hands. What those sources do not state is new here.*

#### Origin

Garret Longshore was born in the western fishing settlements of the Concord, where the Guild Accord's writ ran thin and the sea ran deep. He went to the ropes young. Pulling line against the ocean roped his forearms with tendon, and the salt cracked his hands and bleached them at the knuckles.
A master on the shore gave him his first wheel. The master checked his compass every watch against the swell, the wind and the colour of the water, and he made the boy check it the same way. Garret learned to hold a wheel with one hand and read the needle with a doubting eye. He doubted the sea more. He steered by the gap between the two doubts, and he never learned another method.
Years of squinting at horizons washed the blue out of his eyes. He kept his long face shaved.
The shore gave him his byname. At the rift, years later, he explained it himself: "Called Longshore because I have been on the shore for longer than most men have been alive, and the shore has been long."

#### The Making

He worked ship after ship. On many of them the rigger was Soren, a Concord man built like a rope, and over the years the two of them made a hand language between the rigging and the helm. Watchers on deck saw the movement and could not read it.
He took the helm of the ship that carried the Kōkan expedition north, and every repair he made to her rigging left tar and salt on his coat. Off the polar shelf the wind came broadside and stayed. Garret held the wheel with one hand. The pressure was falling, and he read a storm in it by morning. At the bow the young scout Sadamu crouched with a sounding line and called depths back to him, and Soren signed from aloft. When Sodoku turned from the port rail and walked forward, Garret eased the helm to port and said nothing.
At the rift camp he measured the rift the way he measured everything, by dropping a line into it and reading what came back. What the line did turned his face very still. He wrote the soundings into a small waxed notebook and put it back inside his coat.
He swore fourth, after Matheus. He walked to the oath-distance with the rolling gait the sea had given him and stood with his cracked hands at his sides. He told the brothers he did not know how to fight, inscribe wards, forge metal or carry messages through hostile country. He knew how to hold a wheel in bad weather, read a compass he did not trust, and keep a ship alive "in water that wants it dead." If none of that was useful, he would find a way to make it useful. "I have not drowned yet." He nodded once, in the Concord form, and stepped back.
When Sodoku walked out of the igloo in glacier-crystal armor, Garret adjusted his compass heading and said nothing.

#### The Cost

North of the rift there was no deck under him. The column crossed the polar shelf on foot, and the helmsman walked with the compass in his left hand. The shelf gave him no horizon. Its white ran into the white of the sky, and that seam was the navigation problem. It was his problem, every hour of every day. For four days Soren ranged ahead and signed clear from far out on the ice, and Garret held the column's heading on those signs.
He went on shaving on the ice. Beards freeze.
On the fifth day Yoko smelled two Titan Droval. Soren came down the supply sled's mast-pole and landed beside Garret with the count. Around him Sadamu drew his knife and sheathed it, and Dunstan took up a ladle. Garret stood still with the compass in his hand and his mouth closed. He did not run, and he reached for nothing.
He believed nothing and calculated everything. In front of the Droval there was nothing left to calculate. When the first beast launched itself at Sodoku, Garret found that the sea had given him a word for every danger water makes. Storm, wave, a sea that stops carrying what floats on it. It had given him no word for a creature the size of a longhouse leaping through frozen air at a man in glacier-crystal armor. He kept watching.

#### Where They Stand

He reached Kharven on foot with the column. At supper in the Hallenfeld factor's house on the low street, wool wet from the shelf hung on the drying rail, and the food came down the table on a plank: rye cut thick, salt pork, onions fried black. When Sodoku stood up from the bench and bowed, Garret stopped with a knife halfway to a rind.
The waxed notebook is still inside his coat. It holds the final measurements of the rift, and Sonzai's first order as Commander named whatever Garret had found there. What the line did down there, he has not said.
His compass does not point away from Titan Droval.

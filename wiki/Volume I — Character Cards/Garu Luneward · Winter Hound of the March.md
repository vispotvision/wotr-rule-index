### VIII · Signature

#### Crescent Fang Rush

**Effect** · Garu condenses gathered echoes into his legs and snaps forward along a curved, non-linear line rather than a straight one; to the target he simply arrives at their flank, already inside the strike.
**Cost** · Burns the echo-charge he's spent the fight gathering; used more than once against the same emotionally loud target, it starts pulling their fear into his own nerves instead of just reading it.
**Limit** · It needs a trail to chain off. Constructs, the truly dead, and anything that leaves no emotional residue give him nothing to bend the line around, and the rush goes straight instead of curved.
**Counter** · Flood the field with false fear. An illusionist who understands his empathy-tracking can bait the curve toward an empty point and let him arrive somewhere with nothing in it.
> **What nobody knows.** Whether Sensory Drowning is a hazard of the technique, or the shape his missing Pack-Soul takes when the only feelings left to borrow are a stranger's.

### Lore · The Life Behind the Card

*Written 2026-09-24 by the character-lore pass from this card and the record of Old World roads and chains. What those sources do not state is new here.*

#### Origin

Garu Luneward was born into a Lupine pack of the Old World's north, of a strain that carries human blood beside the wolf's. He had no tail. The pack saw that at his birth. The second lack took longer to show. Most wolfkin come into the Pack-Soul past infancy, and the pack that raised him waited for it. It never came.
A wolfkin raised outside that shared life reads to his own kind as permanently and quietly wrong, and his kind read Garu that way. The others turned together on a run with no signal passing between them. He had to watch for the turn. No one pressed a cut palm to his under the full moon. What the pack knew in common, he learned alone. He learned to pick single heartbeats out of a crowd and tell them apart. He learned to smell fear in a footprint. He learned to cross snow without a sound.

#### The Making

He left the pack's country. The Hunter's Guild chartered him as a forward scout and set him on the frontier march, walking ahead of the Caravan Families' trains. He reported in to the Guild and belonged to no one there. He hunted alone, as he always had. Wolfkin kept long from their packs go quiet and fade. Garu had never been inside the Pack-Soul to begin with, and he made a discipline of the thing that had isolated him.
Then a sled convoy vanished in a whiteout. The storm lasted three days. Snow filled every track as it was made. Garu followed the fear the lost drivers had left on the wind. On that trail the first deep bond of his life formed, and it formed with the act of tracking itself. Until then a trail had been information to him. From then on he treated it as a promise someone else's fear had made him. He found the survivors in a ravine the other searchers had missed.

#### The Cost

He let nothing bond to him that deeply again. Charm and bond-workings found nothing in him to take hold of, because his isolation had calcified into a defense. The Families came to trust his tracking absolutely. They learned almost nothing else about him. Asked how he had found a thing, he gave the fewest words that would close the conversation.
For a week he hunted a Withering-touched direwolf pack alone. He laid false scent across its ground until the wolves turned on each other.
He fought the way he tracked. He came at his quarry along a curve and arrived at its flank before it knew the flank was open. Used more than once on the same frightened quarry, the curved rush began to pull that fear into his own nerves until his senses drowned in it.
Something in him leaned toward dreaming, and he kept away from it. Full dream-contact brings hallucinations. He could not afford them on watch.
At the Hollow Ford the promise held him in place. He was scouting ahead of a Families train when he caught, downwind of a village, the scent of a nest the Withering had spawned under its bridge. If he left the nest he would lose it. He stayed on it. He sent the warning back by Aren Vallestride, a courier riding probation with a company in the train. Aren ran it through the night on failing legs. He reached the village before the nest moved. When it moved, the fight came to the ford, and Aren's anchors collapsed the crossing before the creatures could swarm inland.

#### Where They Stand

Garu walks ahead of the Caravan Families' trains on the frontier march. He wears the blue cloak of the Northern Watch over a Guild field harness rigged so that nothing on him rattles. Snapped, the cloak throws a brief screen across his scent. He holds no pack and answers to no house, and the caravans are the nearest thing he has to either.
The Families' old hands tell of a Hobgoblin chain-fighter who escorted their trains long before him. The Old Chain planted, did not chase and explained nothing. They speak of him and of Garu in the same terms. The two have never met.
When a warning has to go back faster than a train can move, Garu stays on the trail and sends it by Aren. Asked how he tracks, he says that tracks can lie and the wind cannot.
He has not gone back to his kin. His isolation from them is a standing fact, and nothing about it is settled. Whether the drowning belongs to the rush, or is the shape his missing Pack-Soul takes when the only feelings left to borrow are a stranger's, nobody knows.

# Gimbzo

*The Anchor of Fate · Iron-Backed Gim · The Standing Verdict · Card 11*
> ***He Who Refused to Move Even When the World Did***
> 
> > **"You may swing again. I will still be here. That is what an anchor is for."**

---

## I · Identity and Core Profile

**Race / Lineage** Moto, Archaic Bloodline — **First-Eon Generation, contemporary of Bara himself.** *One of the earliest sons born under the Hour Before Sequence.*
**Faction** The Original Moto Conclave — **pre-chronological warrior caste**
**Temperance Stage** **XII — Zenith, anchored permanently.** *His cultivation curve was severed mid-ascent and frozen by the Plane of Fate itself, refusing decay*
> The heaviest of Bara's earliest cadre — **a slab-shouldered, bull-throated Moto whose function within the Conclave was never reconnaissance, never command, never revelation.**
> 
> ***He was Anchor.*** When the first reality bent and threatened to spill its sequence, **Gimbzo set his heel and refused the bend.**
> He held the line **through wars no later chronicler dared to transcribe** — until the day **Dougou, the Iron Bison Maximus**, met him on the rim of the unfinished world and **broke his ribcage into the dirt with a single overhead.**
>
> The Conclave mourned him. The mortal histories filed him away. ***Yet his soul did not transit.***
>
> **The Plane of Fate, recognising one of its own oldest tenants, absorbed him whole — not as ghost, not as memory, but as** ***living infrastructure*****.**
>
> *He is now a load-bearing axle of Hataraki's lower lattice, a labour of the Plane that wears Gimbzo's name and Gimbzo's posture — and the Plane itself is preparing his return.*

## II · System Metrics

**Aether Class · Fixator-Imposer** — *a Body-rooted Hataraki dialect that does not project outward but locks inward. Aether radiates as a downward weight rather than a striking arc.*

>

> **Temperance Path.** Bara taught him directly. He climbed the early Stages **through pure functional labour rather than insight** — carrying, holding, refusing, enduring.

> ***The only Moto of the First Eon to advance without ever once practising revelation.***

### Lore · The Life Behind the Card

*Written 2026-09-24 by the character-lore pass from this card, the ten archived scenes it appears in, and the record of The Curia at Altherion. What those sources do not state is new here.*

#### Origin

Gimbzo was born to the Moto in the First Eon, one of the earliest sons born under the Hour Before Sequence and a contemporary of Bara himself. The world was unfinished. Its reality still bent, and a bend threatened to spill its sequence.
He came into that world with a metallic birth-sheen along the brow-ridge and the spine, like hammered copper under the hide. He grew into the heaviest of Bara's earliest cadre. Slab-shouldered and bull-throated, he had forearms thick as cooper's mallets. Within the Original Moto Conclave, a warrior caste older than any count of years, he was Anchor. When the first reality bent, he set his heel and refused the bend.
Bara taught him directly. He climbed by labour alone, by carrying, holding, refusing and enduring. No other Moto of the First Eon advanced without once practising revelation. A fragment of the First Conclave's oral tradition sets the two of them side by side: the Founder gave the Conclave seven ways to work, and Gimbzo found seven ways to refuse.

#### The Making

He held the line through wars no later chronicler dared to transcribe. He fought with two great-axes, Tsuiji and Tsuiei, forged in the First Eon from compressed ore wrapped in a binding-rite. Their blades were broad as church doors. To anyone but Gimbzo, each weighed as much as a small mountain.
At the eastern threshold of the unfinished world he held against a tide of pre-sequence horrors for forty-one days without sleep. Bara is said to have walked the rim afterwards and pressed a palm to his shoulder.
Dougou, the Iron Bison Maximus, met him on the rim of the unfinished world. Their duel lasted three days. On the fourth dawn Dougou caught Gimbzo's left axe on the curve of his shoulder-yoke and brought a maul down through his sternum. The overhead broke his ribcage into the dirt. The Iron Bison left the body where it fell, believing the labour finished.
The Conclave mourned him. The mortal histories filed him away. Three nights after his death the corpse was gone from the salt flat, with no grave-robber's tracks and no carrion sign. His soul had not transited. The Plane of Fate had recognised one of its own oldest tenants and absorbed him whole, as living infrastructure, a load-bearing axle of Hataraki's lower lattice. Bara is said to have closed the matter with one sentence: "He is still working."

#### The Cost

He was never buried. The hafts of both axes were bandaged in linen taken from the burial wrappings he was never granted. The absorption left a bone-white crest of hair on his shaven skull that hummed under his anchor work and could not be cut. Since the salt flat he and both axes had shared one coordinate. He did nothing to the axes. The coordinate was what they were made of.
The anchoring had a price. Each planting rooted him deeper. An anchoring held too long could make him inseparable from a coordinate of fate, and then he had to be loosened by a Tengan adept's rite or risk fossilising into the lattice permanently. The Plane reclaimed most of his bleed, and waste was a refusal he did not grant himself.
One way through him ran along his own conviction. An opponent who could persuade his labour that the coordinate it gripped was unworthy of being held would collapse the Work inward. Bara is the only being known to have done it, and only as a teaching correction.
In the ash he held no writ and charged nothing. Of sixty-one arrivals at his gate he taught four, the ones who asked his standing first. He opened the gate home for eleven. Nine took it. He held it against none of them. The other two became columns on the trunk above him. Twice he watched the seventh Work learn a man, and twice he dug what was left out of the root and Kizami cut the column.
Darius Ignatius Sanctus Sanctorum, Archpaladin of Sanctum Lux, landed in the ash with four fractures in his Crystal. Gimbzo called him Weighing Man and offered him one labour, Balance. He named the seven with Ruin first, because before creation comes destruction. He called Darius's office a hinge that had bolted itself shut, and pressed until Darius said one true thing in anger. When Darius asked how long he would last, Gimbzo refused to give a number he did not know. An hour into the first lesson Darius reached for the arrest he had held open at the Bastion gate, and a fifth fracture began to ring. Gimbzo knocked him off the haft with the flat of Tsuiei. "Every man who's died in this wood died doing something that worked." Then came the arch. "An arch is two collapses leaning on each other." He told Darius his house held a man standing between two loads with his hands out, and did not say which.
Over nine months he put all seven Works into Darius, with the matrix under them. He spent four of those months configuring the Fist of God, and said one sentence about it at the end of the fourth and never another. Twice Darius asked him to file it. Twice he refused: a thing worked wholly inside a body, he said, needs no register to tell it where it lives.

#### Where They Stand

Darius was the first of his students to say the teaching back to him. Gimbzo set him one more trial before the gate and would not name it, because a man in that office does not get to prepare a position. It came at the ninth hour of the fourteenth day. Charles Lambert died, and the office the two men held between them spent Darius through a channel whose far end nobody had ever told him. Gimbzo had felt a child open about four seconds before Darius did. He sent Darius to teach that child the Ways and to keep him from the Regent. Unasked, he told Darius why the Judger had put the Ways in his hands: "a man who wants the seat teaches to make more of himself." He did not name the child.
Gimbzo sits in the gate in the wood, in the ash at Sum-gol, a plane sideways from the world. Above him Kizami cuts the columns on the trunk. The tock of the chisel comes down out of the branches. The Plane is preparing his return. When it judges that a coordinate of present-age fate requires anchoring beyond what the living Moto lines can provide, he will step out of a settled patch of ground somewhere on the continent with both axes already in hand. He will resume the labour he was never permitted to finish. His garden has not yet opened in the world, a circle of bone-pale earth among stunted iron-bark trees. Its law is "What stands, stands. What falls, stays fallen." The first thing he will look for is the bloodline that carries Dougou's residue. Until then he plants his right axe, drags the blade slow through the dust of the lattice-floor, and waits for the world above to need him.

### Lore · The Life Behind the Card

*Written 2026-09-24 by the character-lore pass from this card and the record of Gale and still point. What those sources do not state is new here.*

#### Origin

Iryen Maevith was born in Kael'Nir, a border province of forest in Eresse, in the New World. His blood was Varrisak and human. The Varrisak keep their law by mouth and write none of it down, and they hold that a rite sung as it was sung last year has stopped listening. He heard their songs first.
The temple of Kael'Nir kept a choir of wind-priests. It took him as a boy for his voice, and he learned its hymns as the brothers sang them. His hair came in silver-white. Silk cords barely held it. His eyes were light amethyst and always half-lidded, from calm and never from fatigue. People who listened to him long enough fell into his rhythm, slow, even and sure. He spoke of everything as though it had already happened, on the falling tone Eressean speech keeps for what is settled. When the brothers asked how he knew, he did not tell them.

#### The Making

He was still a wind-priest of the choir on the night Karu Velnar brought forty-three freed slaves out of the arena of Kael'Nir. Some were hurt. Others could barely keep their feet. Their road ran through the forest below the temple, and Iryen came down to it from the temple steps. He did not ask who they were. He looked at the broken shackles on Karu's wrists, went to the head of the column, and sang under his breath as he walked. The column fell into his pace. The stumbling ones kept up.
Where the trees thinned he stopped. Karu told him the story of the pit, all of it. Iryen heard him out and said he had known it already. He did not say how. He turned back up the road before it was light and was in the temple by morning.
The Verdant Ascension came after, and in it he passed beyond human resonance. From then on, when he breathed, flowers turned toward him as though he were a breeze from Verdantia's heart. The temple offered him sainthood. A saint would have been the temple's to keep. He refused, and with that he left his brothers. He chose to wander, and he took the office of Harmony Arbiter in the Guild Accord's Arbitration Division, to settle quarrels over Wellsprings with melody and never with a fight.

#### The Cost

He took for his sign a spiral of wind lines coiled around a single unfurled leaf, and for his word Peace Through Motion: movement without violence, breath without burden. His breath paid for every settlement. Each tone he sustained broke a little more of it into song. His circle of sound lasted exactly as long as the exhalation that carried it, and his exhalations grew shorter every year. What stood outside the circle was outside the peace. His wind could turn an attack aside. He never struck with it.
He recovered while he did nothing, and a long sitting never wore him down. What wore was the breath. Pressed too long, it could come apart into pure sound and take him with it. He accepted that quietly and kept singing. Ropes tied across his chest hummed in the wind, and each tone they gave off kept a fragment of his thoughts. Anyone who could read a tone could read him.
He came to the caldera belt of the Accord's chartered arc with the Accord's filing. A Wellspring there had drifted in the night from under one venture's array onto its neighbour's claim, and both ventures kept militias with Onceglass charges. Shiran Kazuren was already in the belt. No charge had been thrown while he walked between the yards. Iryen greeted him and spoke of the days before he came as if he had watched each one, and neither man said how he knew. Then Iryen stood on the drift ground and sang. The tone went out in a circle to the edges of both yards. Inside it the militias put up their charges, and the masters talked terms while he sang, breath after breath. Sairin Kazuren came down to the circle's edge. Where she stood the tone went dull. She stepped back out. The tone closed. The masters signed to share the draw for as long as the drift held. His breath came shorter afterward, and more of it was song.

#### Where They Stand

Iryen Maevith wanders as a Harmony Arbiter for the Guild Accord. Every Wellspring quarrel he has settled has two parties who remember the tone. He never hurries. His robes run from rose to ivory along the hem, and the ropes across his chest hum when the wind finds them. Flowers still turn toward him when he breathes, and each breath is a little more music than the last. He has never said how he knows what already happened. His name lingers in monastic hymns, which remember him as the one who could still a storm with laughter.

### Lore · The Life Behind the Card

*Written 2026-09-24 by the character-lore pass from this card and the record of Rot and bloom. What those sources do not state is new here.*

#### Origin

Kaien Rhuven was born in a coppice wood that fed the forges, to a charcoal-burner who cut it on rotation. His father felled the poles low and clean at the stool. The stools sent up new poles the next spring. He stacked the cordwood into a mound, turfed it over and lit it from the heart. Then the stack burned for days with almost no air. It was watched day and night. Where flame broke through the turf, his father smothered it with a spadeful of earth. A stack that caught went to ash, and the wood was lost.
The boy learned to read the smoke: thick and white while the wood gave up its water, thin and blue when the charcoal was nearly made. He cut his first poles with a short blade, low and clean. By the time he could keep watch on a stack alone, his father had the charcoal-burner's cough.

#### The Making

He went young to the Concord Citadel, to study under the Guild Accord's Aetherica. In the training sanctums of the City Below, under a fine rain that never stops, he was trained in the measurement discipline. He learned to take a soul's frequency, set it against a standard tonal profile and write the value down. He could have been trained to burn Essence for force. He did not stay for it.
He left the Citadel and its Aetheric studies for silence and soil. What he studied there, and why he left, he has only ever hinted at. He walked out of the institution that measures souls and went to spend his own on people their Wellsprings were consuming. He accepted a real cost to his soul, willingly, to preserve something he held above his own soundness. He has never said what he preserved, or what it cost him. He took stories in exchange for his cures, and no coin.

#### The Cost

Late in one year he came up the fen road into Hollowfen, in the marsh basins of Dorrosfenn, his robes worn thin. Karasaki Hozume had spent that year healing the plague's survivors there, and the healed carried a grey growth under the skin, first at the joints. It had come out of the healing. Kaien asked each household for a story. Then he knelt in the reed-bed behind the house, one hand in the roots and the other on the sick, and stayed until the growth had gone out of them into the reeds. It went through him first. He worked one house at a time to the end of the year, and Hozume went with him. At the last house Hozume told him a story of his own. Kaien took it and wrote nothing down.
Another time Chimwala N'Doro sent word for him along the lake road, in the Lake Districts of Ketsuen. One of Chimwala's younger druids, a beastkin herbalist from the lakeshore, had kept the watch at Rotwood through every day of its reclamation. When the rite closed she could not stop. Flowers opened on the backs of her hands and did not close, and the herbs drying in her satchel put out roots. Kaien asked her for a story before he touched her. He sat with her in the house by the water and set her breathing to the pace of his own, day after day. He taught her that moderation is rhythm. The flowers on her hands closed one at a time, and Chimwala stayed through all of it. Kaien left with her story and asked Chimwala for nothing.
He believes every poison once began as medicine. He carries a little of every growth he drew through himself at Hollowfen. He treats sorrow and fever as one imbalance, too much fire and too little patience. The fever he mends from the green around him, and the sorrow he pays for himself, every time. He keeps no ledger. He names no price and no working. His cures are slow. What he carries when nobody is sick is every story he was paid in.

#### Where They Stand

Kaien Rhuven tends the Verdant Hollow, a quiet enclave at the edge of Senoth's misted valleys. The Wellspring-consumed are carried to him. He wears robes ceremonial in cut and threadbare from travel, beige over dusk-purple, tied with faint red cords. The short blade at his side is for harvesting. Nothing he owns is a relic.
He hears plants in the slow rhythm of their Essence, and he listens to a patient as if for something deeper than breath. When his pulse is joined to the roots around a sickbed, his heartbeat is lent out along them, and a cut at the roots reaches him. On stone, in frost or on drawn-down ground, the roots have no voices for him.
Karasaki Hozume owes him the survivors of Hollowfen. Chimwala N'Doro owes him a student. From Hozume he took a story, and from Chimwala nothing. He rarely speaks of the Citadel.

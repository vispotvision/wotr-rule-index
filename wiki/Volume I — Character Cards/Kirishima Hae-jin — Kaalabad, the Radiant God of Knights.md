# Kirishima Hae-jin — Kaalabad, the Radiant God of Knights

**Folk name:** Kaalabad, the Radiant God of Knights
**Third names:** The Warlord God · The Chained Crown
**Classification:** Titanic-adjacent · Confirmed canon, Volume I

---

### I. Identity

**True name** — Kirishima Hae-jin (霧島 陽鎮). Kirishima — "Mist-Peak," Japanese/Ketsuen house register. Hae-jin — "Sun-Garrison," Korean/Mahuo given register.
**Folk name** — Kaalabad. What oral tradition settled on after generations of tellers who couldn't pronounce the real thing. The only name most of the Inner World has ever heard.
**Third names** — The Radiant God of Knights (pre-Fall). The Warlord God (post-Fall, folk). The Chained Crown (Accord filing, current).
**Origin culture** — Hybrid Ketsuen house / Mahuo given-name register — see Naming Guide Amendment, cross-cultural name contact. The Kirishima house predates the modern split between the two registers and predates the Sum-gol incorporation itself, a lost pre-incorporation line the current provincial arrangement was built over rather than descended from.

Eight Primary Stats, drawn from the Level 500 / Stage XIV point economy. Concentrated build — Dominion and Resilience carry the character; Gnosis and Harmonics are the deliberate gaps. A specialist's spread, not a generalist's average.

**Crystal State** — Crystallized. Self is law — his thought carries causal weight across the whole of Zarakal whether or not he is present to direct it, the mechanical description of a World Tether from the inside.

**EU Reserve** — Effectively unmetered at range — the Tether draws on Zarakal's own accumulated pressure rather than his personal reserve, which is why centuries of imprisonment have not visibly diminished the Realm's output. ### IV. Appearance

Twenty-six feet at full standing height, though he is never seen standing fully — the chains keep him at a permanent stoop that reads less like restraint than like a man leaning into a wind only he can feel. The darksteel plate has grown into him rather than onto him: centuries chained in the same suit have fused joint-seams shut, and moss-dark corrosion blooms across the pauldrons in patterns too regular to be accident, like the armor itself is being written on. Beneath the horned crest of his helm, what's visible of his face is more shadow and cavity than feature — the Radiant God's original bearing is gone, and what's left is a weight behind the eye-slits that makes men look away before they understand why.
The Halberd of Zarakal is never far from his right hand even chained; violet lightning threads its length in slow, unhurried arcs, like the weapon is still breathing. Kōgetsu no Kama, the Waning Reap, hangs low at his opposite hip, chained the same as the rest of him. Both weapons carry the same corrupted light, the color of a bruise that never finished healing.
His cloak, what remains of it, is less fabric now than accumulated dark — frayed to threads at the hem, moving in air that isn't moving, a small dead comet burning cold violet-white somewhere above his left shoulder that nobody has ever explained and nobody has been brave enough to ask about.

### V. The Man Before

Every folk telling agrees Kaalabad was, once, the kind of knight other knights measured themselves against — not gifted with cruelty, not hungry for a crown, just absolutely, unbearably certain that an oath sworn was an oath that could not be unsworn. That certainty is the single thread that survived the Fall intact. Everything else about him rotted. That didn't.
Nobody alive remembers him before the corruption, which is the point rather than a gap: a god who used to be a man is a story about how completely a fall can erase the thing that fell.

---

### VI. The Warlord

What the Halberd left him with is a knight's discipline pointed at a warlord's appetite, which is worse than either alone would be. He does not rage. He does not gloat, not really — the line attributed to him about rising again reads less like a threat than like a man reciting an oath he no longer has the context for, the way a devout believer keeps praying after they've stopped believing the god is listening. He conquers the way he once served: completely, without negotiation, and without any apparent pleasure in the act itself.
*He still thinks of it as service,* is the private read worth building toward if he ever gets scene time — not service to Azerack, whom he does not love or trust, but to whatever the original oath was actually made to. Corrupted past recognizing what that object was. Still executing the vow.

### VII. History

#### The Fall

Kirishima Hae-jin fell long before the Great Spirit War gave the Inner World a reason to notice him. Azerack found him — not in open battle, by every surviving account, but in something closer to a bargain struck with a man already cracked open by something the folk tale doesn't name — and gave him the Halberd of Zarakal. The weapon didn't corrupt so much as agree with what was already rotting. What returned from that exchange wore a knight's memories like a coat two sizes too small.

#### Before the War

By the time the Great Spirit War broke out in earnest, Kaalabad wasn't fighting it — he was old history to the things that were. His campaign through the territories stretching out from Sum-gol had already run its course by the time the war's official chronology starts counting, which is why generals on both sides of that later conflict used his name to frighten recruits rather than to describe an active front.

#### The Sum-gol Campaign

His Divine Realm manifested across Sum-gol and the surrounding Ketsuen frontier, the land taking his shape the way frost takes the shape of a cracked pipe — spreading outward from a single point of failure until the failure was the whole territory. The Tower of Zarakal rose at the center of it and has never come down. Sum-gol's Hanseon compact, the Kept Vigil, is the scar that Fall left on the province: a World-Tethered vestige of the Realm's retreat, still drawing on a debt nobody currently living incurred, which is exactly why the incorporation treaty forbids its survey. Nobody wants to find out what's still being paid.

#### The Imprisonment

However he was finally brought down — the folk tale is silent, which is its own kind of tell, since folk tales rarely stay quiet about a victory this large — he was chained rather than killed. The Tower stands in territory nobody has reclaimed. He is held within the Veil itself, bound at the threshold rather than in it, a compromise struck because nothing on the Material Plane could hold a Domain-bound Zenith warlord and nothing on the Soul Plane could hold his body. The chains have outlasted every kingdom that helped forge them.

---

### VIII. The Divine Realm — Zarakal

Zarakal doesn't remember being anything before it was this. Ask the land and it answers only in what's left: battlefields where the ground never fully healed, forges that still run hot with nobody feeding them, a spire visible from three provinces on the right kind of clear day.
**The Tower of Zarakal** — Centerpiece. Twenty-seven thousand feet of black spire, and the only structure in the realm that's never needed a single repair. Nobody has ever rebuilt it, because it has never fallen.
**The Battlefields of Eternal Conflict** — Ground where the fighting never fully stopped — whatever's left of the dead still running through the motions of a war that ended everywhere except here.
**The Darksteel Forges** — Still lit. Darksteel is native to nowhere else in the Inner World, and nobody has volunteered to go ask what's keeping the fires fed.
As a World Tether, the Realm persists independent of Kaalabad's active attention the same way Sum-gol's Hanseon compact persists independent of any living caster — it doesn't need him awake, doesn't need him fighting, doesn't need anything from him at all except that he existed long enough, and violently enough, to make the claim in the first place. That is the phenomenon underneath the Ability below.

### IX. Ability

What he refuses is yielding ground once claimed, even in chains. What he survived is total defeat without the underlying oath breaking. What he believes, at ruinous cost, is that a vow sworn cannot be unsworn — even once its object has rotted past recognition. The phenomenon that falls out of that is Realm-Claim: ground he holds with intent, blood, or sustained violence stops being neutral territory and starts being his, permanently, whether or not he is present to defend the claim.

#### *Söröl no Ken — Ruin's Blade*

**True name & gloss.** Söröl no Ken. Ruin's Blade — the folk name for the Halberd's edge, though the phenomenon runs deeper than the weapon.
**Classification.** Offensive/Supplementary · Materia primary, Vitalia secondary · Wellspring Monolithion / Materia Primordia · Method Corporis/Conjunctia · Physics Domain Biochemistry/Materials Science · Grade EX · Range territorial · Medium blade contact and sustained presence · Stage XIV
**Description.** Ground struck, bled on, or held under sustained combat pressure by Kaalabad begins accreting toward Zarakal's condition — soil darkening, native flora withdrawing, ambient temperature dropping toward the Realm's baseline. Small-scale strikes leave a scar that heals over years. Sustained occupation, or death dealt on the spot in volume, converts the site permanently.
**Cost.** Permanence is the trade for reach — he cannot claim ground he isn't willing to make Zarakal forever. Every claim narrows what he can ever again call unspoiled, including, eventually, whatever's left of himself.
**Limit.** Cannot claim through active, contested consecration — ground actively held by an opposed Domain or a standing ward-network resists conversion and must be broken first, by ordinary force, before the claim can take.
**Counter.** Break the contest before the site converts. Once territory crosses over, the claim is a World Tether and behaves like one — starved, not defeated, the way Sum-gol's Hanseon can only be starved.
**What nobody knows.** Whether the conversion requires his ongoing will at all, or whether — like Hanseon — it would keep spreading with him dead. Nobody has been willing to test it.
**Codex.** [Rt] Rot · [Anch] Anchor · [Ext] Extension — Materia primary, Vitalia secondary · Wellspring: Monolithion / Materia Primordia · Stage XIV

#### *The Unbent Knee*

**Classification.** Supplementary · Passive/Signature · Vitalia · Resilience-aligned
**Description.** Kaalabad cannot be forced into a subordinate physical posture. Chains hold weight; they do not hold stance. Even bent double under restraint that would break a lesser Stage's spine, something in his bearing reads as upright.
**Cost.** None active — this is architecture, not technique, and it never turns off.
**Limit.** Doesn't prevent capture, damage, or defeat. Only prevents the specific image of him kneeling, which several accounts suggest is the entire reason it exists.
**Counter.** N/A — not a combat-relevant working, included for characterization weight.

### X. Equipment

#### The Halberd of Zarakal

Straight spear-point atop a haft the length of a man, threaded end to end with slow violet lightning that never discharges and never stills. Godslaying by reputation rather than demonstrated feat — no confirmed account exists of it actually killing a god, only of gods being unwilling to find out.
Bonded to Kaalabad specifically; nobody else has ever been recorded lifting it.

#### Kōgetsu no Kama — the Waning Reap

Broad, curved reaping blade, older in make than the halberd and matched to it by design rather than by pairing — the two read as a single armament split into two forms. Where the halberd claims ground forward, the Waning Reap claims what's already fallen, sweeping the dead and dying into the same accreting process that converts territory.

---

### XI. Relationships

**Azerack** — The corruptor. Seated outside the Fourteen Archons proper — older, and stranger, a force that operates on individual souls rather than on Continuum-scale law, which is why the Archon roster never had a seat that fit him. Kaalabad neither loves nor trusts him, and per the read above, Azerack is largely irrelevant to Kaalabad's own sense of what he's doing and why.
**The jailers** — A joint working, Accord and Sanctum Lux both, struck at a cost neither institution has ever fully disclosed to the other. It is the reason the two bodies maintain a courtesy toward each other that neither has ever extended anywhere else.

---

### XII. Voice

He does not monologue the way the old folk-quote suggests — that line reads more like what people needed him to have said than what he actually says, which happens to dead kings and imprisoned gods alike. On the page: slow, exact, formal in a register nobody else in the Inner World still uses without irony. Threats arrive as statements of fact, not promises. He does not raise his voice, because nothing has made him raise his voice in longer than anyone alive has been counting.
> *"Bound in chains, yet my wrath endures. Fear the name Kaalabad, for I shall rise again."*
> — attributed to Kirishima Hae-jin — no living witness confirms it, and it reads truer as folk composition than reported speech

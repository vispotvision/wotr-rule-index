### Lore · The Life Behind the Card

*Written 2026-09-24 by the character-lore pass from this card, the one archived scene it appears in, and the record of Old World roads and chains. What those sources do not state is new here.*

#### Origin

Krothar Veylshroud was born Krothar Thunn-Gorr on Gorr-Keth, the tallest island of the Hobgoblin Expanse, in the family holdings on the caldera's western shelf. In later years he told no one where he was born. His father was Krothar Thunn-Gorr, a Crimson Order chain-warrior of the Kha-Urrum blood-branch, whom the Old World circuits called the Gorr-Keth Reaver. His mother was Freda Thunn-Gorr, born Vaal-Kess, of an old shelf-island line that never entered the Order. She managed the family's constituency seat in the Keth-Gorrum. She also kept the contract papers that brought a mercenary's pay home.
His father was on contract across the world. His mother raised him.
His skin came in green. The Order ran red-ochre and the Vaal-Kess brown, and his was the deep green of lichen on wet stone. The Order noticed. It always did, when something failed to match the standard carved into the Rock.
When his father was home, the boy trained in the caldera yard with his father's chains, and the first thing he learned was Binding Lash, a weighted chain thrown to wrap a target and pin it. His father taught him the weight, the arc, the snap and the bind. His mother watched. Once he asked what shape was carved into the bone-white ear-drop she wore. His parents looked at each other and changed the subject together.

#### The Making

In his seventeenth year he passed his father's ceiling in that yard, with both his parents watching. Neither spoke of it to the other, then or after. The Order had already read his skin. He left the Expanse with his father's chain technique and his mother's patience, and he named no island to anyone.
He hired out as a chain-fighter on other people's roads. Early on he rode escort for a young merchant, Torven Greis, on an iron run down the Iron-to-Salt Corridor. He gave a name and no island. At a ford in the melt the river rose while the lead wagon was in it, and the wagon began to go. He threw a weighted chain round its tongue and planted on the bank. He held it against the water. The teamsters cut the team free and dragged the load back up, and no iron went into the river. He took the pay the contract named. He sent none of it home.
What grew in him outran what his father had put in his hands. Silver chains came to him out of the Soul Plane unsummoned. Beneath them lay a black light. Its source was older than any technique he had learned. Nobody had taught him what to do with it. His father had taught him the chain, and the chain was what he had.
Then his father saw it. The father looked at his son's unmanaged output and made a decision. He had the craft to forge sigil shackles inscribed for the stone-blood, and he forged them for his son's wrists, forearms and throat. The son accepted them. He did not ask why.

#### The Cost

He went on accepting them. They had been forged for a lesser practitioner than he became, and he took them anyway. Held down, he grew denser than his father ever had, until the chains became part of how he was built. He did not resent them.
The black light took a name, Death's Lance, and it needed the chains off. He called it in the old Hobgoblin runic tongue, and his skin went black from the knuckles up. Each discharge left burns that calcified where they lay. They started in his hands. In time they ran past his wrists onto the palms and up the forearms to the elbow, almost black against the green and hot to the touch. They kept spreading.
After the Lance his hand trembled. He asked for the chains back himself, at once. He always did. The pattern ran the same way every time: the chains went on, the work came, something broke the hold, he took them off, he killed, he asked for them back. The gaps between the breaks kept shortening.
He went by Veylshroud, a name that was not his mother's. He did not write to Gorr-Keth. He did not visit. His mother kept his room, and she dusted the boyhood chains on the caldera-yard wall, which did not need it. He knew she waited.
He believed the leash was right because he had agreed to it. That belief kept the chains on him and kept him from the man who made them. His father had gone back to the same forge afterward and used the same inscriptions on his own fighting chains. His father knew how the shackles were made. The son did not contact him.
Saiya knew why he asked humans whether a beastkin with them was a slave. He had seen the other answer. It cost him something he never fully got back.

#### Where They Stand

He is in the Scourge of Hell on contracted bounty work with Saiya, a Khajiit mercenary who holds the sealing rite. She alone calls him Krothy. He has told her not to.
The archive last finds him on a ridge above the Scourge that the bounty said to clear. A wyrm came up through the earth at him. His four chains went through its hide, and its tail threw him into the ridge wall. He unclasped the suppressors one at a time and split the wyrm down the spine with Death's Lance. Then he held out his wrists to Saiya. "Get the chains back on me." She chanted the seal and the chains wound back round his wrists and throat. She flicked him between the eyes. "Boring keeps us both alive," he told her.
Sodoku Moto came over the ridge line and asked for passage. Krothar read the Moto blood in him by its feel. "I know that bloodline's type," he said. Sodoku said, "If I fought you, I'd surely die." Krothar let him through. To Saiya he said the young man's essence was not finished cooking yet, and it was best not to be standing close when it boiled over. Then he saw the beastkin woman walking with Sodoku and stepped into the road. "Is she your slave?" Sodoku put his body between them and said she was under his protection. Krothar studied him a long time. "...Good answer." He has gone back over his read of Sodoku twice since.
The chains now show hairline fractures to the naked eye, and threads of silver chain-light come at his wrists and throat uninvited. He reads intent now as well as strength, and he knows more about the people around him than he has decided what to do with. With Saiya, neither of them pretends any longer that it is purely business. When the work is finished he wants somewhere quiet, and the place has become Gorr-Keth. He has not said so. He still names no birthplace, and his mother still waits on Gorr-Keth. Nobody has yet talked about what happens when the chains stop holding. He is still deciding whether to contact his father.

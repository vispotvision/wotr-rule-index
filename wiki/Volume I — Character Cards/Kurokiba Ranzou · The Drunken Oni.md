### Lore · The Life Behind the Card

*Written 2026-09-24 by the character-lore pass from this card and the record of The Tenryū and the dragon-blooded. What those sources do not state is new here.*

#### Origin

Kurokiba Ranzou was born an oni in the Ember-Sake Highlands. He carried the torque of his forebears out of them, a necklace of black beads that ran red at the seams.
His body fermented from the start. Whatever Essence it drew, it turned slowly into drink and stored in his blood, and nobody had asked it to. He was drunk before he ever lifted a cup. He was poisoned all the time, and his frame bore it. Brewers passing through the highlands taught him the cask, and he learned it well enough to teach it back.

#### The Making

He left the highlands and never settled again. He held a gourd and no ground. He went from hall to hall among the oni clans, fighting duels and sitting drinking contests, and he lost neither kind. He fought in the Sakebrand Gauntlet. The Red Horn Gourd rode at his hip, crimson lacquer etched with flame and waves, and the brew he poured into it came out stronger. His heavy black and red hakama, braided through with cask-fibre thread, drew Essence from the air as he walked and fed his reserves back up.
He could fight for days without rest. Pain and exhaustion ran off him. When he breathed out hard, the breath was sake vapour, and it caught fire, or blinded, or turned a man round. Nobody facing him could tell where the next blow would come from.
The oni clans gave him the honorary title of Brew-Sage for his mastery of Essence fermentation. The Guild Accord began to hire him. Its records entered him as an unpredictable yet highly dangerous mercenary-for-hire.
Then he came to see his own architecture from outside, and like everyone handed that sight, he could not stop examining it. He was hard to be around. He examined his own architecture and decided to ferment it. The oni halls could never agree whether that was the least serious answer anyone had given or the only one that worked.

#### The Cost

He put himself at the top of what his body could absorb without failing, and he kept himself there on purpose all his life. He was permanently poisoned. His frame took it.
He could not stop the brew. It fermented in him day and night, a golden-red turning that gave off vapour, and the pressure built until it went. At the height of the drink he was at his strongest and fastest, and the fine work left him. He could not read a feint. The way he moved hid the next blow from the man in front of him and from Kurokiba too. He learned where his foot landed when it landed.
Early in a fight, before the brew had built, he was a very strong drunk with a gourd. Anyone who read his file knew the rest: keep the drink from him and give him no time.
Once it had built he let it all go at once. The vapour went out, the fire went after it, and the air itself was the weapon. Everyone inside it lost focus first and footing second. Every such release drew on a soul that already held more than it was built to carry. For years he lived one bad night from breaking.

#### Where They Stand

He wanders still, one bad night from breaking. Accord brokers write his contracts and know him as a "wild contract", effective and impossible to predict in the field. The oni drinking halls celebrate him as both a hero and a menace. He has never lost a duel or a drinking contest. When he falls in with brewers on the road, he leaves them a new fermentation technique as a gift, as brewers once gave the cask to him.
He came in off the road once to a domain-borne feast of the Braqth Raiding Courts. The hall set its war-leader, Goraku Tenryū, against him in a drinking contest, and drums and chanting kept the pace. Each man drank from his own gourd. Kurokiba's brew built in his blood, and the sake vapour off his breath hung over the benches. Across from him Goraku ran hotter with every draught, and steam came off his skin. Then Goraku's kinsman Daigo Tenryū tapped his horns together. The boom went through the basalt, and the chant slowed to his beat. With his teeth Daigo tied a red cord on Kurokiba's wrist, on a quick promise: the contest would be finished on a night when Daigo stood in the hall. Kurokiba let him tie it. Nobody lost.
The cord is on his wrist. It warms in his palm whenever Daigo tugs the other end. He has not sat the second night, and his record is whole. The halls tell that night both ways.

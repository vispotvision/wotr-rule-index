### Lore · The Life Behind the Card

*Written 2026-09-24 by the character-lore pass from this card and the record of The Reaper's office. What those sources do not state is new here.*

#### Origin

Lilium Verdanis was born on a lake margin in the Lake Districts of Ketsuen, where the roads were few and bad and people read the weather, the water and the fish runs. Her grandmother raised her. The old woman sang the children of the house to sleep, and Lilium knew every one of her songs by the time she could walk the shore alone.
When her grandmother lay dying, she asked for one of those songs at the end. Lilium promised to sing it. When the end came, she could not stay in the room. She sat on the step outside with her hands over her ears until the women came out. Nobody else had learned it.

#### The Making

Once she had her letters, she wrote down every lullaby the lake women would sing for her. A merchant company that bought timber on the lake margin took her as an apprentice, and its Clerk set her to taking minutes at the Court of Assistants.
At one sitting a Warden promised a cutting crew their winter pay. Lilium cupped her hands over the words. When she closed her ledger on them, a tiny white blossom stood between its leaves. After the vote the Warden asked that the promise be left out of the company's book, and the Clerk told her to copy the minutes short. She would not. She left the company without her freedom of it and took the ledger with her, blossom and all.
After that she wrote for hire. Any hearing that would pay a scribe with no company could have her, and one was held in an open yard in the rain, between two parties who would not go under one roof. The shouting buried the witnesses. She kept writing, and drew a green bracket on the page. The air inside it softened, and the people in the yard spoke like they were indoors, though the rain still fell on them. It held while she watched the page. The Star Chamber's protocol corps sent for her afterward.

#### The Cost

The Reaper's retainer began at a crowded deathbed. A glassblower lay dying with his street crowded into the room, and his last sentence was for his wife, who stood at the back. Yukizumi Yukari held his hand. Lilium cupped the sentence before the crowd could repeat it, and it sprouted in her ledger. That night she plucked it for the wife alone. The minute came back at the temperature he had spoken it. From then on she held the minute while the Reaper held the hand, and she stayed in the room to the end.
A minute taken into her custody stayed in the ledger until it reached the person it was spoken to. She would not pluck a last word before a crowd, whoever asked. The blooms she carried added up. Too many made her faint, and a tea of soot and thyme reset her. She drank it and went on.
She pinned promises where they could be seen. When she pressed her lily to a written vow, a pale petal appeared on the signer's sleeve, warm while the vow was kept and browning when the signer strayed, in front of everyone at the table. When she threaded two names with a lily fibre, a kindness to one left the other obliged. She always told the obliged one why. Chairs of some councils asked her to sit without the lily in her hair. She declined each time. Those councils seated other scribes.
The lily held only what was there. She could not root love or loyalty that nobody felt. Some vows she was asked to plant never took.

#### Where They Stand

Lilium serves in the Star Chamber's protocol corps, on retainer to the Reaper for last-words custody. She is called Lily-Quill, and her title is the Petal Scribe. Her milk-white braid is tied with the lily, there is ink on her fingers, and her ember-red eyes make liars blink.
She seats Lord Lucen Vale's councils: his stars map the room, and her petals mark who should speak. Cassiel Asterion's meridians keep her blossoms from being trampled. She witnesses Measures with Vireline. With Shiragiku Iori and Kohako she makes a trio, in which Kohako seals the minute, she plants the vow, and Iori keeps the feeling gentle enough to keep. She carries last words for Yukizumi through crowds and does not let them bruise.
Kaito Akagiri heats a room until agreement feels like the participants' own idea, and in her rooms the agreement is pinned to the sleeve of each person who gave it. He both hates and admires it. Smoke bells and Rot-mold bruise her lily, and then she sends for Kaede or Ascella to clear the air.
She hums under her breath at work. Some blossoms in her ledger are still unplucked, because the people they were spoken to have not yet come.

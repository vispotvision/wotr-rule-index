### Lore · The Life Behind the Card

*Written 2026-09-24 by the character-lore pass from this card, the one archived scene it appears in, and the record of The Night Register. What those sources do not state is new here.*

#### Origin

Lu Sanjing was born to a lineage hall and entered in its book under the surname Lu, among the names of his generation.
He grew up under the question every historian in the four quarters had failed to answer: why a Wellspring does anything at all. Sources surfaced where no survey had placed them. They wandered. They went quiet and came back east of where anybody had left them. Each of the scholarly houses held an account of the mechanism and taught it to its own. Over the houses stood the Guild Accord. Its charter granted a standing and took it away, and its Research and Archives Division kept the power to say what a thing is.
He was capped into scholarship, and he gave it to how Wellsprings behave. He learned to enter a source in ink: where it came up, which way it moved, when it went still, and where it was next found. The why of it he left to the houses.

#### The Making

The career was long. It was distinguished, and it was built on those entries.
He kept them across the slow years in which a source moves, and he read every account the houses produced against them. Where an account met his record, he wrote that it did. Where it failed, he wrote that too, in the same hand and the same ink, and sent the page to the house that had made the account.
The Accord took one account out of the houses' and enforced it. It was the one that suited the Accord's supply contracts. His entries did not move to meet it. A source that came back in the wrong place went into his record in the wrong place and stayed there, whichever account it went against.
He wrote no account of his own. When a house asked him for one, he sent it his entries.
At the end of that career, in his studio, he wrote the honest position on why Wellsprings surface, wander and return: "we do not know, and the instrument does not care that we do not know."

#### The Cost

The line named no mechanism. It gave the Accord nothing to enforce and its supply contracts nothing to cite. The account that suited them stayed in force.
He wrote it with the entries of a whole career within reach of his hand. He built nothing further on them. A scholar of his standing could have closed his work with an account under his own name, and a house would have taught it. He closed his with a sentence that left his own entries without a why. He put no mechanism under his name.
The distinction of the career had rested on what he recorded. At its end he recorded that he did not know.

#### Where They Stand

The scholarly houses have produced eleven competing accounts of the mechanism. The Accord enforces the one that suits its supply contracts. His line stands among them as the honest position, and it claims no mechanism at all.
It is still read. On Cutler Row, at the third hour, with the district main off and rain on the shutter, Wystan Ashmore of the Night Register held it against the eleven accounts and the one the Accord enforces. Beside it he set Vaeloris, which had reformed after the void took it with nobody making it happen, and which the Curia had called a miracle.
In the Research and Archives Division, Dr. Vaelion Rhest is first at the sources that fail, and he works the question the line answered. No record has the two men meet.

### Lore · The Life Behind the Card

*Written 2026-09-24 by the character-lore pass from this card and the record of The Reaper's crowd crews. What those sources do not state is new here.*

#### Origin

Maelia Cantorine was born in Altherion to a woman who sang on the rota of a choir order, and she grew up on the order's novice benches. The Accord kept no office for singing. In the order's hall a chant held up a wall, and nobody on the rota remembered a day it had not.
The novices learned to take the long vowel from the throat beside them without a gap and pass it on. Maelia had the ear early. She could hear a singer tire at the far end of the bench before he knew it. The novice-master taught the one rule the orders are built around. A vowel held false breaks the line, and a broken line drops what it holds.

#### The Making

At the solstice the order sang the Accord's dead. The rota held the wall inside with the line, and the novices sang the same line on the hall steps for the crowd in the square.
One solstice a dray horse bolted at the back of the square. The crowd surged away from it, up onto the steps. A man near the front took up the line, and the square took it from him. The push found the song's beat. People went down at the foot of the steps, and the crowd walked over them in time.
Maelia was pressed against the stair rail with the line in her mouth. She heard two things at once. One was the song, every vowel true, which the rota inside needed to scan. Under it lay the heartbeat the square had agreed on, and that was carrying the crowd. The chant was a chant. It was also a structure bearing load, and both were operative.
She kept the vowels true and kinked the beat. Across the whole square the chant stumbled on one syllable, and the push came apart into people. She gave them a plain line, a lullaby with the sweetness stripped out. They followed it back down.
Inside, the rota's line scanned, and the wall stood.

#### The Cost

The woman who sold lamp-wick at the foot of the steps did not get up.
The novice-master took Maelia off the rota. A singer who had sung against a line with a wall hanging on it, he said, could not be trusted with the vowel. He asked her to swear before the order that she never would again. She would not swear. She left the hall with her novice's copy of the office book and a voice torn on the steps. For a long while she whispered.
The Arbitration Auxilia took her into its Acoustic Ward. She wrote against the office book in its margins, in red, and put nothing there she had not first sung on pitch. In the Ward she took up the kris Canticle, made to part the join between voices and leave wrists uncut. She learned to lay it across a chant between the fourth beat and the fifth.
On the Corridor road in Stannvaard, drovers in a way-station yard sang a work-song until it became a march on the hearing inside. Sir Rhyse Calder held the doorway with Lochgate. Maelia went out by the side door, the black silk at her throat knotted twice, and cut the march into drovers. For a breath the yard had no beat, and the back began to shove. Cyrille Lacrim struck her bell once from the doorway. The yard steadied. After that, Maelia cut first and let the bell follow.
At Altherion, in another order's hall, a congregation held its dying cantor in a sworn chorus. Sumire Nekoha walked the children out. Seraphine Kharza capped the loudest voice. Maelia cut across the fifth beat and answered the stumble with a plain line. Deimos took the oath away through the cantor's confession. She drew the Silence Ring on the floorboards, and inside it the last word was heard.

#### Where They Stand

Maelia is the Countercant, on call for the Reaper whenever crowds threaten last words. She escorts Yukizumi Yukari through hot rooms. She buys him the quiet second every verdict needs. She breaks the fear choruses Niran's agents seed, keeps councils from becoming choirs, and unthreads cult liturgies so that arrests do not become massacres. She walks into the noise with the kris in one hand and The Red Margin open in the other. Her line is "Keep your words. Lose the rhythm."
Kaede Suyori cools the air, and she cuts the chorus. Kaito Akagiri gathers a room's rhythms and she unspools them. Each is fascinated by the other. Where Vespera Corvina maps night lanes, Maelia keeps songs from turning them into marches. Khasir Aluto, Kozakura Shion and Moros Pellayne wait on her when a drum-Domain breaks their work.
Whispering mobs with no shared beat blunt her first cut. If the Throat Ribbon knots a third time, she will be mute by dusk. Lord Lucen Vale dreads losing his voice at full council, where the room he holds would invert. Maelia has written down how to spend hers on purpose.

### Lore · The Life Behind the Card

*Written 2026-09-24 by the character-lore pass from this card and the record of The Withering courts. What those sources do not state is new here.*

#### Origin

Marceline Vireaux was born to old noble blood in the Rose Courts, in the Withering Era. It was an age of falling supply. The great houses bought Essence the way an army buys grain. A contract written at the old rates had become an inheritance. She grew up watching estates change owners in the weeks after a burial, while the heirs sat with the will.
The Vireaux kept a relic under their own name, a mourning hat that hides the wearer's feeling from anyone who tries to read it. It came down to Marceline. She was raised in the rooms where a family mourns. She learned who sits nearest the widow, which cousin is sent for, and whose name is spoken at the graveside. She learned poise, and nobody put a weapon in her hand. She learned to keep her voice low, and she has never raised it since.

#### The Making

She took up condolence as her trade. In her colours she came to houses in mourning and sat with the widow and heirs through the season. Her presence kept a rhythm patient enough that a room mistook it for warmth. In those weeks a family would remember a promise it had once made and let lapse, and it surfaced in her favour. The heirs wrote such things into her Ledger of Wills, where a contract holds after the paper would ordinarily stop mattering. What she arranged fed the Sable Rose Consortium.
She received in the Blackrose Parlor, a room she had conditioned, and wore the Rose-Crest Brooch there. When a rival stood in her way she spoke no word against him. Over days or weeks his standing thinned in every room that knew him, until the record itself read differently and no one could say when it had turned.
By then grief was the instrument of her trade, and she wore her colours as a costume. She stopped pretending her own mourning colours were a performance. The bereaved would lean toward her only while the mourning was real, so from then on she had to keep grieving honestly at every graveside she worked. The price came out of her own soul. She paid it willingly.

#### The Cost

The cost is the one kind of loss she is no longer permitted to feel in private. Whatever she mourns, she mourns at someone else's table, and it goes to work there. She mourns honestly the dead whose estates then move toward her networks. Nobody has ever proven she arranged a loss she mourned.
Every rival she thins costs her fine detail of unrelated days from the same season. She has stopped mentioning it to anyone. Which days are gone, she does not say.
She accuses nobody. She explains nothing. What anyone learns of her work comes from inheritance records and from what her circles have gained. Of her work she says, "I do not ruin lives. I arrange them."
Her body was never the instrument. Hostile intent softens at her side, and she has never needed to be the strongest thing in any room she has arranged a funeral in. What she cannot thin is a written record: a ledger, a sealed contract, a witnessed deed. She keeps her own arrangements in a ledger.

#### Where They Stand

Marceline Vireaux is called the Crimson Mourner, Lady Blackrose and the Widow of Quiet Deals. Her circles take in aristocratic and merchant houses across several courts, which know her as the noblewoman whose condolences cost kingdoms.
On the coal foreland of the Old World, a western kingdom's war with the Ember Crown ended in the field below its capital. Its king was carried off the last engagement and died of his wounds some days later. Marceline came to his widow within the week and stayed at her side through the season. She arranged the burial. The king went into the ground in velvet. When the terms were to be spoken in Aurelian Veymont's field, she walked beside the widow down to his pavilion. Cyranthia Valez came with them. The council's officers had ridden down hard in the face. At the table, seated beside her, they eased. The kingdom knelt. In the seasons after, the wealth of the widow's house moved toward the Consortium's networks.
In the Greaves districts the head of a tin house died in winter, and his supply book was worth more than his shafts. Marceline sat with the widow and the heirs through the weeks after the burial. In those weeks the heirs came to remember a promise the old man had made, years before, to a house of the Consortium. Before the thaw they wrote it into her Ledger of Wills. The house's creditors had been sitting in Cyranthia's Salon, and in the spring its credit failed inside a week. They took the shafts. The book was already bound, and it stayed with the Consortium. Every afternoon of those weeks, Elowen Mireille poured the tea, and she holds the afternoon the heirs spoke the promise.
Whose loss her own mourning answers to, she has never said. She still wears the colours.

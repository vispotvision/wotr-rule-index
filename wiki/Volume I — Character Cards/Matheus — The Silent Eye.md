# Matheus — The Silent Eye

*The Silent Eye · Yukari Ward-Craft*
> **Full Name:** Matheus of the Ketsuen Enclave
> **Age:** 34 · **Origin:** Yukari-trained, eastern enclaves
> **Level:** 245 · **Stage:** V Surge · **Band:** III
> **Role:** Ward-craft specialist, inscription and spatial defense for the Kokan expedition
> Looking at something twice is an admission you did not look at it correctly the first time.

---

Lean and dark-haired, angular features carrying the precision of the eastern enclaves. Cheekbones sharp enough to cast shadows. Eyes deep brown-black of lacquered wood, moving with the economy of a man trained to read rooms before entering them. Quilted coat of Yukari cut, long to the knee, belted with a woven sash carrying ward-symbols faded from washing but legible to anyone with Attunement. His hands are unmarked — no forge scars, no blade calluses. The hands of a man whose craft is inscription and whose medium is Essence.
His oath: *"I swear on my work. The inscriptions I carve will hold your wards. The arrays I build will hold your walls. The holding will outlast my hands."*
Contributed the silk-thin ward-fabric lining for the Arctic Lion gauntlets — Yukari inscription subtlety married to Dawi structural engineering. The hybrid is the only component in the Verdict Plate that carries two craftsmen's signatures.

### Lore · The Life Behind the Card

*Written 2026-09-24 by the character-lore pass from this card, the eight archived scenes it appears in, and the record of The Kōkan expedition's hands. What those sources do not state is new here.*

#### Origin

Matheus was born in the eastern enclaves, in the Uplands of the shield, where the seeps run densest and the ground gives little besides summer grazing and the high herbs and minerals that alchemists buy. His mother dug minerals out of the scree above the settlement and sold them down the valley road. His father ran stock on the summer grass. Neither of them could read a ward.
The settlement stood on ward-carved foundations, with one warded post at its gate, and a carver trained at Senri's Gate came up each summer to keep them. One winter the snow closed the road. The carver never arrived. By the thaw the ward under the storehouse had thinned past holding, and one night the storehouse came down with the winter's grain inside it. Nobody was under it. The settlement ate short until the grass came back.
The carver came up with the spring road, looked at the ruin once and named what had failed. Then she cut the foundation line for the new storehouse in a single pass and never went back over it. Matheus carried stones for that foundation. He watched her hands the whole time.

#### The Making

He passed the entrance Proving of the Ward-Carver's Academy at Senri's Gate, in the old Yukari valley behind the standing stones. An instructor there gave him the rule he kept: "Looking at something twice is an admission you did not look at it correctly the first time." He learned to read a room before he entered it and to keep the reading to himself. He never took up a blade. His hands stayed unmarked. At the autumn Ward-Lighting he cut his first permanent station on the perimeter with the rest of his year, and the town turned out to watch.
He crossed a continent to join the expedition. He came for an education. The Yukari tradition had theorised inscription at the scale of the air, and no Yukari practitioner had seen it done by a man holding a sword.
On the crossing he stood at the mainmast with his hands in his sleeves. The keel array needed re-anchoring at the third and seventh stations, because the density gradient that far north ran steeper than its first calibration. He was moving before Borin turned to find him. He fell into step beside the Dawi without speaking, and they went down the forward hatch together. From the ship onward he read the two brothers, their fields and their signatures. He judged that what they made together was necessary. By his training, nothing else was worth serving.
At the rift's edge he swore third, after Robin Ice and Borin, and stood at the oath-distance with his hands out of his sleeves and still. "I do not swear on my sword because I do not carry one. I do not swear on my blood because my blood is not the instrument." He swore on his work: the inscriptions he carved would hold their wards, the arrays he built would hold their walls, and "The holding will outlast my hands."
He inclined his head, the Yukari form. Sodoku's look down the line of oath-givers gave him one clause: "At Matheus, whose perception was the oath."
Sonzai's first order as Commander named the inscriptions among what had to be checked before the camp was struck, and the forge Borin built on the ice burned an accelerant mixed from materials Matheus provided. They were upland minerals from his own pack. When Borin built the Arctic Lion there, Matheus lined its gauntlets with silk-thin ward-fabric, Yukari inscription laid into Dawi structural work. The card records the result: "The hybrid is the only component in the Verdict Plate that carries two craftsmen's signatures." When Sodoku put the gauntlets on, the lining found his palms.

#### The Cost

Sonzai carried a stranger in off the snow and kept her in his own shelter until Nella and Matheus could say what he had found. Nella confirmed she was alive. Matheus found nothing immediately hostile. Sonzai told the woman not to interfere with Matheus's inscriptions. Later he said Matheus should examine whatever he could without treating her like a prisoner, and he gave the order: "Matheus watches her signature. Nella watches her condition."
When the column struck camp, Matheus inscribed a passive detection array on the doorframe of the last igloo and took the rear. He carried the array's parameters in memory while its link fed him the air's readings. The feed held for hours. Then he walked out of its range, and the column was alone on the ice.
On the fifth day Yoko smelled two Droval. Matheus went to the front of the column without being told. His hands came out of his sleeves. Then he told the column what he could not do.
"I cannot ward against Titan-class."
Their mass exceeded his ward-ceiling "by a factor I am not going to calculate because the calculation would be demoralizing." He had seconds before the approach became a charge. He weighed a confusion ward cut into the ice, to buy Sodoku time to decide. Against that he weighed the kind of professional arrogance that gets ward-crafts eaten. His affairs, by his own count, came to a quilted coat and a woven sash. He held to the Yukari acceptance that dying with the work intact is the only afterlife the tradition recognises.
The first Droval lowered its head to Sodoku. Sonzai claimed the second with the Chains of War.
He carried no sword onto the ice and swore on no blood. He named the ward he could not raise and left the figure uncounted. His sash had faded in the wash. Its symbols stayed legible to anyone trained to read them.

#### Where They Stand

He watched from the rear of the column as Sodoku drew Kurosetsu against the first Droval's charge. Ice grew out from the blade along the lines of its inscription, and the air itself answered. That was the thing he crossed a continent to see. He has seen it now.
He came in with the column to the north gate of Kharven-Seat. Edward Lambert counted what Sodoku brought home and set him down without a name, as "a Yukari warder".
His lining is in the gauntlets, and Sodoku wears them.
No record lifts Sonzai's order on the stranger's signature. She has told Sonzai she will say what she is only "Somewhere there are no physicians, ward-crafts, scouts, brothers, Droval, or very curious Dawi pretending not to listen." No record says what his watching has shown him.
Kharven-Seat has walls and ward-stations. His oath named both. No record yet sets his hands to them. He walks where the ward-work requires him, and he still looks once.

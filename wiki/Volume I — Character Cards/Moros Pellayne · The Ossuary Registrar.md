### Lore · The Life Behind the Card

*Written 2026-09-24 by the character-lore pass from this card and the record of The Reaper's office. What those sources do not state is new here.*

#### Origin

Moros Pellayne was born in a hill town whose burial ground was long full. When a grave's lease ran out, the bones were lifted, washed and carried up to the bone-house. His father kept the register there. Every skull went to a shelf, and every shelf had its line in the book: a name, the plot it came from, the day it came up. The book shut on brass clasps against the damp. His father read it through spectacles, and the boy read it over his shoulder.
A skull without its line belonged to nobody. Families climbed the hill to stand before their own dead, and his father answered them from the register or said nothing. Once a widow pointed at a skull on an unmarked shelf and asked him to say it was her husband's. He would not. Moros held the lamp at the foot of the ladder and watched her go down the hill with empty hands. His father wrote her visit into the margin, dated it and closed the clasps.
The shelves taught him the rest. A break that had knit in life wore a thick collar of new bone. A break at death kept its edges. A blade left a groove, and a fall left the shape of whatever the head had struck. He could read the last day of a skull off the bone while he still carried the lamp.

#### The Making

A lime-burner was found dead below his own kiln wall, and he had owed his partner money. A crowd stood at the partner's door wanting a hanging. The Arbitration Division sent an officer to hear it. Moros, keeping the register in his father's place by then, carried the dead man's skull to the hearing and set it on the table. The break at the back matched the stone ledge below the kiln, and the front of the head was whole. The partner walked home. The officer took Moros with him when he left.
In the Division he learned to ask the Codex Funebris properly, and its blank pages filled with bone-ink. Asking began where his father's book began, with a name or a token of the dead. Without one the skull-notes bickered or sulked in the margins. A record needs an index entry. He kept the doctrine of passage as the edge of his practice: the dead may leave traces, and the trace is not the soul. He cited traces and never claimed to have anyone.
The Accord made him the Reaper's adjunct for disputed deaths. Yukizumi Yukari was the last vote on such a death, and Moros held the record open while the Reaper lowered the star. He seated Measures when cause of death was the clause. One of them was in Vaultmere, at a dying child's bedside, where an instrument never sealed under Nalūn authority turned on how the child had been hurt. The child's own words would have decided it. Moros could have compelled them. Khasir Aluto held the Polar Codex open at a clean page, and the guardian withheld assent, so Khasir closed the book. Moros did not ask. The child spoke to the guardian, and nobody wrote it down. He lost the case and left the farewell unbruised.

#### The Cost

The child's page in his folio stayed blank because he did not ask, and he has never torn it out. Deimos cannot take child-borne guilt; it scalds him. Nothing stops Moros from compelling a child's last words, and he will not. He loses those cases.
The doctrine costs him wherever a family wants a voice. A skull-note enforces what the dead man intended and snaps at lies and stray blades, and it comforts nobody. He tells mourners that he holds a citation from traces and does not claim to have anyone. Some go home angrier than they came. Near his open book boasts arrive trimmed to fact, and so do eulogies. At a wake he keeps the Codex shut, and the praise runs as long as the family likes.
His footnotes bite, and he does not. A snapping marginalia clamps a spear and holds it, and the man behind the spear is someone else's work. In a drum-Domain or a chant-room his margins smear. He waits for Maelia Cantorine to split the beat.

#### Where They Stand

He serves the Forensic Cadre as the Reaper's adjunct, and now and then he consults for the Black Watch. He reconstructs murders without making more. When a crowd wants a martyr more than a verdict, he walks the Reaper through it. Khasir has kept his scenes since Vaultmere, the bones at the temperature they were found. Lilium Verdanis and Kohako secure the minute and plant the vow. He footnotes both. In Vireline's Measures and Lucen Vale's councils, his margins make the court geometry obey itself. Kaito Akagiri heats a room toward consensus. Moros cools it back to evidence.
He wears his father's spectacles. His eyes have never needed them. The coat is stiff with talisman thread and stained with ash-ink, and the folio stays chained to him.

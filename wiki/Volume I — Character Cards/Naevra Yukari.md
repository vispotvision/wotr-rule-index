### Lore · The Life Behind the Card

*Written 2026-09-24 by the character-lore pass from this card and the record of The Yukari bloodline. What those sources do not state is new here.*

#### Origin

Naevra Yukari was born in the First Eon, one of the earliest daughters of the Yukari bloodline. She came so near its beginning that the resonance in her soul ran cleaner and older than anything her descendants would inherit. Her hair was white as old parchment. Her skin had a porcelain cast, and when she worked the threads her eyes went silver-white with a faint lavender under the silver. She held herself very still. People near her took it for listening.
The line was new, and it had enemies before it had a school. Naiser Yukari had refused to be absorbed into the Seikai doctrine or the Zettari, and the name he declared stood on a split that neither house had sanctioned. Naevra was a daughter of its first generation. Her tools were few and fine. On the Ivory Threadspindle she gathered fate-lines, isolated them and drew them taut for close work. She kept the First Veil Script, a ribbon-scroll of proto-Yukari glyphs that held the earliest thread-binding doctrines and the petitions put to causality. Her Pale Oath Needles served for sealwork, for soul stitching and for the rites that keep corruption out of a line.

#### The Making

No institution taught her. She advanced through raw proximity to the bloodline's first truths. She stood in direct contact with fate at its unworked source and took psionic pressure past what a mind is built to bear. She came through it stable. That made her one of the earliest stable expressions of Yukari inheritance, and the burden of being one was hers from then on. She learned to negotiate with a thread. She found the points where its pressure gathered, and she edited them with psionic precision and a will she had ritualised.
In the Old World, among the early powers, an oath was sworn in blood to purge the new branch. An oath of that kind would have bound the descendants of everyone who swore it to the killing, one generation after the next. Naevra read the oath's thread before it was carried out. She stopped the purge. That was the White Refusal. A blood-oath that would have "crystallised an entire branch of destiny into generational slaughter" came to nothing, and the branch Naiser had split from two houses went on. Some of the early powers revered her for it. Others hated her.

#### The Cost

Her belief that fate could be negotiated with was named the Mercy Heresy. The harsher doctrinal forces of her age marked her as dangerous for holding it, and she went on saying, "Fate is only arrogant because no one taught it manners." The first quarrel over what the Yukari were for began with her, inside the line she had kept alive.
Saving others from their consequences too often could shift the burden onto her, or onto that line. She had stopped a purge to keep the branch alive, and each mercy after it could be charged to the branch. When she turned a death aside, it came out somewhere survivable, and some part of it always remained. She stitched only what had been torn. Anything a person had surrendered of their own will stayed surrendered.
After a catastrophic soul fracture, she rewove by hand what survived of the Yukari resonance, across seven nights. The needles kept fragments of the line that would otherwise have vanished. That was the Seven-Night Stitching. The work was paid for in her own soul. When she turned aside too many ordained outcomes in a short span, the rebound struck her directly. She seized. Blood ran from her eyes. The more futures she read at once, the less of her own present she kept, and she slept badly and passed through long spells of an eerie calm. The hardest choice anyone could put to her set the order of things against lives she had anchored with her own hands.

#### Where They Stand

Naevra Yukari is dormant. She lies hidden in a threshold archive tied to the earliest memory architecture of the Yukari, and nobody knows where it is. Elder Seravain of the Guild Accord's Research and Archives Division knows the legend of a Yukari ancestor asleep in an archive of the line's first memory. He wants that archive for his library, as he has offered to store some of Sandalphon Aestraen's voices there. He has not found it. Nobody has woken her.
Her waking would reopen the oldest question in the Yukari bloodline: whether fate is a thing to obey, a thing to survive, or a thing with a throat that can be made to yield.

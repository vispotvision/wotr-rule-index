### Lore · The Life Behind the Card

#### Origin

Nol Tally was born in the timber country of the north, in a parish whose people worked woods they did not own. His father hauled felled timber down to the road with a team and was paid by the load, as the yard counted it. A load counted short was a short wage. The boy walked beside the team and counted the loads himself before they left the trees. When the yard's figure came out lower, he had his own to set against it.
On every roll that held him he was entered as Tally, N.
The Night Watch Society kept a chapter in that timber, the Timberline. Its walkers went out on fixed walks, counted what the woods held and sent the counts in to the Society's bulletin office. The office printed them as inserts on the cheapest rag it could buy.

#### The Making

He took the North Ferriby walk and kept it: forty miles of timber every week, and the deer on it counted for a living. His counts went in by other hands. He never carried one to the bulletin office himself. What he brought out of the timber came measured, by count and by rule, with the place given by the nearest stone. When a find wanted sealing, the chapter's coil-carrier came out to the ground with a tin. Latterly that was Qiu Yinzhi. She learned his walk and his way of filing.
On the morning of the ninth he came down the Ferriby cut and found eleven hinds and a stag inside a quarter mile, all of them dead where they stood. He wrote that they were carrying. A forester has words for rot and for swelling, and he used neither. A pale grey-green mass had come up through the hide along the spine and the flanks, fibrous at the base and soft at the crown. He measured it. Nine inches at the tallest. He cut a section with his knife. The tissue under the mass had not decayed. Yinzhi sealed it inside the hour, standing in the cut. Four ravens sat in the birch across the cut the whole time he worked. None came down, and he counted them.
On the tenth he walked the line north to the Ferriby stones and found the ground clean, with no carcasses, no mass and nothing sick. He went back and found the last one, twenty-two paces short of the parish boundary marker. The ground on the far side of it was clean. His insert gives the rest: "I checked the far side twice."

#### The Cost

He did not walk his line after the eleventh.
On the fourteenth he carried his insert to the bulletin office himself, which he had never done. He asked the clerk what happens to a filing after the Bureau closes it. The clerk told him it goes to the Night Register. He asked whether the Night Register was a person. The clerk said it was.
The Lattice Classification Bureau's northern office returned the insert inside two days over Edwyn Brack's signature: a Wellspring bleed, no operator indicated, no further action and no file. The return said nothing of the marker. The bulletin office nearly cut his last paragraph for length, the one with the ravens and the far side in it.
His name stood at the head of the insert. It reached a room he has never seen. "A man named Tally has looked," Malphas told his cell in the Greyshaft Nine coldhouse. He knew the walk, the rule, the clerk and the far side checked twice. He called the boundary the evidence. The proof of the cell's work, he said, now had a border drawn round it in a forester's notebook, and he needed to know only that Tally wrote it down. The next work was set to make the dose walk, and Malphas meant it to leave no line anywhere for a man to stand at and check the far side of.

#### Where They Stand

Nol Tally counts deer for the Timberline chapter on the North Ferriby walk. He has not walked it since the eleventh. He carries a knife and a rule.
His insert went up to Cutler Row in a bundle of nine, and it was the only one Wystan Ashmore read twice. In its margin Wystan wrote "edges", and under it "ask Tally who set the marker". Nobody has asked him yet. The section he cut is a Register exhibit now, under wax struck with Wystan's brass. One of the hinds he counted is the forty-second minute Wystan carries.
Wystan has told Yinzhi to write to him. Tally is to keep walking his line and measure anything he finds, because Wystan wants a second dataset from the same observer. Wystan's read is that Tally has no signature on anything and is of no interest to a man who has arranged never to be within a mile of his own work. The cell knows his name. Who set the marker is still in the margin.

### Lore · The Life Behind the Card

*Written 2026-09-24 by the character-lore pass from this card and the record of the elven old-ground. What those sources do not state is new here.*

#### Origin

Onawa Ashkewe was born in the Tsohanto Reach, the daughter of its queen. The Tsohanto are a branch of the elves that grew apart from Eresse and from the Varrisak courts alike, a hidden people of forest and barrow. The Reach lies close to the Veil. It holds no standing under the Guild Accord, and its ground is grave-ground and old growth.
Her mother wore the First Ash Mask, the seat of a Tsohanto queen's sovereignty. Every queen before her had worn it lowered over the face. Through it the dead who had worn it earlier spoke again, one at a time, out of the living queen's mouth. Onawa grew up with a mother whose face and voice did not always belong to the same woman.
The Reach smelled of cold ash, crushed petals and old cedar. Later, so did she.

#### The Making

At sixteen she was sent into the barrow-ground alone, as every Tsohanto heir is, to be tested by the mask her mother wore before her. The mask tests one thing. It tests whether the wearer can carry someone else's grief without losing her own name underneath it.
In the barrow she heard, for the first time, a voice that was not her own. She stayed three days. She took the first inherited memory offered her there and did not flinch. Onawa came out wearing the mask on the crown of her head, her own face bare beneath it. No candidate before her had done it. None has since. She did not explain why, and no one asked her twice.
After the barrow her communions grew longer. The first time she sat beside a dying stranger, the dying went quickly to one of its two ends, clean healing or clean death. That season a raven came to her shoulder and bonded to her, and it was left unnamed, by design. At seventeen, in the middle of a communion, she came close to losing her own voice for good. She kept it.
Then her mother died. The mask had been her mother's, and her mother was now one of the dead it could call. Onawa held it through the whole of the funeral and spoke no borrowed word. She took the crown with the mask worn crownwise, by her own choice and against the precedent of every queen before her. The Tsohanto stopped counting her years that day.
Her sight lengthened afterward. She came to see the recently dead standing where they fell, for as long as the living had not finished grieving them, even deaths she had not witnessed. At the Long Barrow the ground first kept its stillness for her.

#### The Cost

Every gift the mask gave her was paid for. When she lowered it fully over her face, one named ancestor spoke and acted through her at the height of their skill for a short while, and afterward she kept a full day of silence. Forcing the mask up in the middle of it would harm her and the ancestor both.
With her hand pressed to bone, ash or grave soil, she could read the last memory the remains still kept. She took a fragment of each death along with it. A word given to the raven carried a curse far out of sight, and a headache sat behind her right eye until the bird came back. At the Long Barrow itself she could call the whole chorus of the Tsohanto dead into the Court at once. Every combatant inside the mile relived their own most unresolved grief as it happened. She did not remember that hour afterward. It aged her, visibly, a little, every time.
She recovered slowly, and fastest near unmarked graves, which unsettled everyone who travelled with her. On consecrated or warded ground her reach fell away sharply. The mask softened nothing that struck her body. A compulsion that forced her to speak in her own voice mid-communion could reach her.
In any room she could rank the griefs by which needed answering first. She answered them in that order, and she carried strangers' griefs past the point where they cost her. Her mourning-bead cord took a bead for every one she resolved. None was for her mother. She believed that wearing the mask over her face, as the queens before her had, would mean forgetting which griefs were hers.
A grove at the barrow's edge, inside the mile of the Long Barrow Court, was dying of blight, and Onawa was there first. In her presence the blight moved faster toward one of its two ends. Then Saetsha Bloomveil came, drawn by the mark her silent Archon left on her, and declared an Edict of Growth in that Archon's absent name. One tree took the Edict and lived. Onawa let the rest of the grove finish dying cleanly. Neither asked the other to yield, and neither did. Onawa bore the stranger's sorrow for a silent Archon past her own cost, and she added no bead to her cord for it.

#### Where They Stand

Onawa Ashkewe is Queen of the Tsohanto, She Who Wears the Old Faces, the Ash-Crowned. She is slight and still, dressed in deep indigo mourning robes trimmed in red-and-white braid and dark fur and worked with lotus and peony in a cycle of bloom, wilt and bloom. The mask rides on the crown of her head, above teal-green braids bound with bone beads and dark feathers. Her head tips slightly while others talk, as though she were listening to something behind the conversation. She usually is.
She speaks unhurriedly, naming the feeling before the thing that caused it, and she states facts about death with the evenness other rulers keep for weather. Under strain she goes quieter. The fangs of the mask are the only thing that moves. The longer she goes without calling on the mask, the more plainly she speaks, and the ceremonial register returns within a sentence of putting it back on.
For a mile around the Long Barrow the ground has the stillness of a place already grieved for, and it keeps that stillness whether she tends it or not. Enemies there find their own dead easier to think about than the fight in front of them. Allies find old fears easier to set down. The Tsohanto dead need no summoning to notice a threat to their queen. Something in the tree line always notices first.
In the days after the yard at Korvaeth, where the New World's war began, every barrow-ground east of the Vessel line took on that same stillness. It holds with nobody maintaining it. The Reach has gone quiet without going hostile and without declaring, and no Tsohanto delegation has come to a hall of either party since.
The Empress of Eresse who knelt in that yard was also called Onawa. The Reach's silence is the Tsohanto's answer to the shared name.
No sovereign has yet stood as her witness among her peers. Her mother's death is the first of her griefs, and she has not finished carrying it. She has never taken the mask off entirely. Nobody has seen who is underneath it, uninherited, and the Reach still does not know whether the crownwise mask is strength or a wound left open.

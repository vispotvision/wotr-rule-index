### Lore · The Life Behind the Card

*Written 2026-09-24 by the character-lore pass from this card and the record of Anvil and relic. What those sources do not state is new here.*

#### Origin

Orokh Sable-Forge was born Giant-kin in Braqth, in the Outer World, among the tribal confederations of the flood-basalt terraces. The stone there split by itself into six-sided posts, and the confederations quarried it by prying. His father pried posts for their standing stones. Before a post went upright, he walked its length striking it with a hammer and listened. A cracked post rang short. It went back to the terrace. The boy did the striking before he was big enough to pry.
Little wood grew anywhere in that quarter. What the confederations did not raise in stone they made from bone and hide. After storms they went out onto the pans for the glass tubes lightning fused into the crust, and they sold them by the armload to Accord factors.

#### The Making

He went to the anvil young. The first dragon he worked came to him fresh. A buyer brought him a rib from a dragon killed that season and wanted a blade quickly. The marrow was still warm. Orokh laid the rib on the anvil's face and struck it, and it answered every blow with the pain it had died in. The heat came up into his eyes. When the swelling went down, the lids had healed shut.
Eyes could be made, he was told. He did not have them made.
He went back to the anvil in the dark. He learned the work again by the heat on his face and the hammer's rebound, and then by the heartbeat in the metal. In time he could feel where a name trembled in a billet, and a lie left a ripple he could taste on his tongue. Under his taps a workpiece came up glowing with no coal in the forge. The glow kept his rhythm. He began a song on the Ash-Anvil in those years. Anyone who came to him with a carcass heard the same answer: "Bring bone that has had time to forget pain."
His eyes stayed sealed, and he did not replace them. He built an entire craft on reading heat and rhythm, and he made the wound load-bearing on purpose.

#### The Cost

His payment was always twofold: material, to teach a tool what world it came from, and story, to tell it what to do here. For a weapon he demanded a confession as well. For a lock he took a secret. For armour he took the name of something the buyer feared and would not admit.
When the Guild Accord began to contract him quietly for containment hardware, he was working a cave mouth on the outer face of the Ring above the Rimlands of Stannvaard. Gorvan Aldric Voss came up to it with the heads of the Constructs he had killed in the Temple Rift. He had waited until they had had time to forget. Orokh read them by their heat and fitted them to the Dawi's shoulders, and the fear Gorvan named for them went into the pauldrons and into no record. Garron Vuldane came after a forge collapse had taken his left arm. Orokh tapped along a rib from a dragon long dead until the marrow remembered flight, and drew it into the core of an arm. Garron paid in Aethersteel and in the story of the collapse.
When the Echo Containment Breach broke the seals in the Atlas Deep Mantle, the Accord let the new ones to him. He went down and laid his hands on the broken plates. What he read in them he gave to nobody. Kaelzar paid the lock's price with a secret, told to the smith alone in the Bastion's armory. There Orokh asked Draen Varos to let him lay a hand on the Axe of Tempered Remorse. Draen did not unsling it. At the cave mouth Orokh lifted each plate into the winter air and breathed over it in a steady count until the glow sank inward and set. Ymir Rhok came up from the Forgeworks Annex for them, and Orokh put them into the apprentice's hands himself.
Every blade, lock and piece of armour he sent out carried a confession, a secret or a fear, and he had heard each one. He repeated none of them.

#### Where They Stand

Orokh works at the cave mouth in cold wind. Sparks stop in midair around him. Filings fall in lines like handwriting. He bends bone-memory and star-iron into covenant blades, seals and tools. The Black Concord bargains with him for things no registry admits. The Bench of Attribution has no entry for what he takes, and the Accord contracts him anyway.
The Deep Mantle's seals hold on brackets Ymir made. What Kaelzar told him is in those plates, and what the broken ones told him he has kept. Draen's axe is still unread by any hand but Draen's. Orokh has offered Eryndrel Vaelune a prototype limb for four-way splits, and Eryndrel has not decided whether more power means worse choices. The ornaments along the skirt of the Ash-Anvil are pauses in the song he started a century ago. He has never finished it.

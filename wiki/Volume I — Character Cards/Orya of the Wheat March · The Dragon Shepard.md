### Lore · The Life Behind the Card

*Written 2026-09-24 by the character-lore pass from this card and the record of The Tenryū and the dragon-blooded. What those sources do not state is new here.*

#### Origin

Orya of the Wheat March was born in a village of the Wheat March, on the ash plain of Caedor in the New World's arc. That ground carried a crop and could bury a village overnight. Her mother grew barley on a strip the Village Concord of the Wheat March held by right. Her father walked the flank of the village herd.
She walked behind him before she could keep his pace. He taught her a whistle of three notes, and the drakes answered it with a twitch at the shoulder and wing-tips lifting out of the grass like sails. He showed her the barley-straw knots for good calving and gave her an iron nail to carry for storm honesty. She counted the herd at every water stop. At home she counted the mouths in the house against the barley in the bin.

#### The Making

The drought came in a year the rivers shrank to their beds. The Village Concord sent a relief run across the plain with water and seed grain slung on drakes, and Orya walked its flank. On that run a young drake with a golden hide and a mane like wheat chaff left the others and kept to her shoulder. He did not go back. That was Khyr, and he had chosen her.
After the drought she walked the harvest convoys as field partner to the Guild Accord's Logistics and Supply Division. One harvest came in under cold rain, and a line of the Rot's speaking mold came up through the wet sheaves toward the villages. The carts stood half loaded at the field edge. Orya whistled cross-drafts through the stooks. Khyr went ahead of her breathing warm gusts to dry the grain and snuff the Rot-fog, and she combed the crop behind him with her crook. The mold reached some of the field hands' mouths, and the villages crowded the road. Daigo Tenryū was travelling with the convoy. He tapped his horns together and walked the frightened into a column along the carts, then bit a dango and breathed warm sugar-dust over the ones the mold had reached until their mouths were clear. The line broke short of the villages. That night Khyr took a gourd off the lead wagon and sat on it. She let him keep it until morning.
Late in another harvest, raiders fired the grass behind the herd, and it broke toward the convoy. A convoy guard drew on the lead drake. Orya told him to lower his bow, and he lowered it. She walked into the middle of the stampede, planted the crook and drew a slow circle. The air rose in knee-high ripples. The grass parted, and every drake that ran into the ripples slowed, and none broke a leg. She held that ground on her own terms for as long as her attention stayed on it, and the herd turned around her. The farmers of the Wheat March called it the air going thick.

#### The Cost

She spent forty years learning to turn a stampede and never once learned to stop one. Her whistle for the panicked went short, short, long, and the flock wheeled into a tightening ring until the fear bled out of it. It had no other setting.
An ash storm came down on one migration. The villages on the route rang their smoke bells, and her three notes were lost under them. The herd ran into a ravine. She slowed the drakes at its mouth, and the ones behind kept coming until the flock wedged in the narrows with the juveniles at the bottom. She dug out the living with her hands. The hatchlings she had carried that spring in a sling made from her scarf were not among them.
She kept the Concord tithe: one egg per twenty heads to the Accord, none taken from grieving pairs. The count came short the next year. The convoy clerk pointed her to a clutch whose pair had lost their brood in the ravine. She sealed the ledger short, and the clerk entered the shortfall in ink.

#### Where They Stand

Orya walks the flank in red-gold travel robes stitched with pasture sigils, her fur-edged hood up and her bead-weighted scarf knotted at the throat. The Yoke of Winds sings from its ibex skull. She plots sky-pastures and water stops, negotiates grazing with the farmers and brings stray juveniles home before raiders can teach them bad habits. Her ledger satchel holds egg counts, molting times and pasture rights under wax seals from four villages.
She holds a herd, and Khyr is the one in it with a name. She is over-bonded to Khyr. Children run under his shadow when he dries a wet field, and he still steals gourds and sits on them like eggs.
Daigo works her routes beside her. He herds people while she herds drakes. Eryndrel Vaelune of the Aerial Ward uses her ripples of air and Daigo's ribbons as moving posts to stitch his routes. Smoke bells and ash storms still muddle her cues, and she keeps the herd out of enclosed ravines.

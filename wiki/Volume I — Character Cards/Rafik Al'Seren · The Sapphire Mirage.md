### VIII · Signature

#### Echo Mirage

**Effect** · He creates illusory duplicates sharing a real fraction of his own physical mass through light-Aether cohesion, enough that a blade or a pursuer cannot tell the copy from the man by sight alone.
**Cost** · **The mass is borrowed from his own reserve, not manufactured** — every duplicate that takes a hit costs him directly, in the same currency as if he had taken it himself.
**Limit** · Duplicates cap at partial mass fidelity and cannot sustain complex independent action for long before the borrowed cohesion gives out.
**Counter** · **Test mass, not sight** — weight, momentum, a grapple finds the fraction of him that is real, because the copies were never built to survive being handled rather than looked at.
> **What nobody knows.** Whether the duplicates are separate from him at all, or whether Mirage Drift Syndrome means some part of him is genuinely standing in more than one place at once.

### Lore · The Life Behind the Card

*Written 2026-09-24 by the character-lore pass from this card and the record of Sun and sand. What those sources do not state is new here.*

#### Origin

Rafik Al'Seren was born near the Mirithane Wellspring during a sandstorm bloom. Mirithane is the Wellspring of Reflection. It shows a thing as it stands and adds nothing. He drew his first breath in blown sand and brown light, a short walk from its water.
His mother sang for the caravans that crossed the dry south, and she carried him from one well to the next. He could palm a coin before he could read. One hand drew the crowd's eyes while the other did the work. He sang beside her. Near a Wellspring the note came back to him bent, and he learned to hear where. He rode the dunes down on his heels. Crowds that had not paid never caught him.

#### The Making

He grew into a performer and a poet, and he never stopped being one. Between markets he went looking for Wellsprings by song, listening for the bend in the answer and writing down what he heard. The chart became the Scroll of Sublimare, which follows the drift of the Mirage-Wellsprings. In dry air his readings held and made him famous as a scryer. When the desert turned, they fell apart.
A second Wellspring answered him: Sublimatio, the Ascending Breath, which takes a solid to vapour and skips everything between. With it he could turn sand to glass and glass to a mirror in one motion. Born near Mirithane in a sandstorm bloom, he had a Core that crystallized under illusion and truth as one harmonic. There was a toll for it. A Sublimatio ritual failed on him. It scarred his left eye. From that day he saw heat currents and soul-light, permanently, whether he wanted to or not.

#### The Cost

The scarred eye had no lid for what it saw. In a square it showed him the soul-light of every face that had paid to watch him.
His workings came out of his own body. The longer he held one, the looser his body sat on his Aether self, and long exposure bled his memory or doubled him. Every copy he raised on purpose carried a real part of his mass, and a blow on a copy landed on him. A grapple would find the real one, so he kept out of reach of hands. When a fight came to him, he hid whoever stood with him in bent light and sound and left across the sand.
He sold what the scroll showed him to whoever paid. The Wellspring under the Black Oasis was on it, and buyers paid for its coordinates before they set out. They went in by his chart with no guide. They set to work at the well-head. The Wellspring began to invert. A guide came in off the sand and led the oasis people out. A stranger went down to the water alone and stopped the inversion, and a chunk of his memory went with it. Nobody there learned the stranger's name, and it never reached Rafik. The Black Oasis stayed on his chart as what was left of it. He went on selling.

#### Where They Stand

On the Aurelian Dust Coast he walked with a Varūn caravan bound back west with copper and nitre, on nobody's commission. A volcano on the Drakhal Spine went up, and the ash came down the wind with an Aether storm inside it. The caravan stopped. Rafik read the heat currents through the ash with his scarred eye and found where the ground was cool enough to walk. Then the Ember Regent came out of the ash ahead, drew the heat around the caravan into himself, and brought it through into clear air. He asked for nothing. Rafik carried what he had seen to the Guild Accord's archivists, and on that word the Accord sent envoys up to the magma scar over Solfatara.
He works out of the Veyran Free Ports. The Accord files him as a civic-observation operative, non-hostile and under watch, and its archivists find him useful now and then. He brings pilgrims and caravans of the Varūn border through Aether storms, unofficially, and has never asked for thanks. He sells coordinates to whoever pays. The poetry half of his name has never quite covered the reputation half. He claims nothing, not even a name people trust.
He will translate his own tricks for the right audience or the right price. Nobody who has studied him agrees on which parts of Rafik are the performance. Nobody knows whether the copies he raises are apart from him at all, or whether some part of him stands in more than one place at once. Asked which of it is true, he answers, "Truth is a mirage that survives only until someone believes in it."
When a new node wakes, the Scroll of Sublimare answers. It is his warning and his income both. The Black Oasis is still on it.

### Lore · The Life Behind the Card

*Written 2026-09-24 by the character-lore pass from this card and the record of Ash and the red thread. What those sources do not state is new here.*

#### Origin

Rathien Veythar was born at Velisar, a port city of the Eastern Sun Coast, where warm sea ran in over long beaches and barrier islands lay across the harbour mouths. His mother dived for pearl on the beds off the islands, and his father worked the boat above her and kept her line. The boy went down with her as soon as he could hold his breath past the first burn in his chest. Below a certain depth the colour went out of the water. The light turned soft and grey. He learned to find the surface by where the brightness lay. She made him stay down past the first want of air, and then past the second. He saw a diver come up once with blood at his nose and in the whites of his eyes.

#### The Making

He left the boats. Men who killed for pay along the waterfront took him on and taught him to come at a man hard, to open him and make sure of him. He copied what they showed him, and it held. He was fast, and the depths had made his hands steady.
In those years blood pulled at him. When a man he struck began to bleed, he struck again, past the need. One night he killed a man in a warehouse on the quay and went on striking after the man was finished. The noise brought the watch. He went out through the loading door into the harbour and swam under the hulls until the lanterns on the quay had gone by. He came out of the water with his nose bleeding. Afterwards his fingers would not close properly on a hilt. The small vessels in them had broken under the skin, and the bruising showed blue under his nails.
While his hands mended, he worked out something nobody had shown him. He brought his visceral impulses under a rigid control, as he had once held down the want of air. He came to understand that speed and silence are more potent than destructive output. From then on he counted excessive force a flaw in method. He had built that out of his own understanding, and it held.

#### The Cost

The conclusion shut out a great deal. He worked at contact or a few steps off. He never carried anything that killed at range. He put the whole of a strike into a point smaller than a coin, and the wound went deeper than the strength behind it. He kept to three currents, the blood, the dark and the cut. On principle he reached for no more.
When he packed more into a strike than he let go, the small vessels inside him broke again. His hands lost their fineness until they mended. So he struck once.
The speed he had built on had an end. Anyone faster than he was beat him outright. He weighed every contract by the speed of the one it named, and he turned down the rest.
At Kethra a merchant was marked by two contracts in one season, and neither contractor knew of the other. Rathien held one. He watched the house for several nights and learned its lamps. On the night he chose he went in inside his grey, came within reach of the merchant and killed him. The man died with no mark anyone could find and his blood still in him. Rathien was out before the house knew.
He watched from the dark across the street as the other contract came in later that night. It was Spoil, the Butcher, on a warlord's pay. Spoil found the man already dead and nothing spilled, and the house guards gave the saber the blood it needed. Spoil cut out through them with both blades. What left the guards did not fall: it stood up as Raktabija's constructs.
In the morning the house counted its master among the Butcher's dead and put a price on Spoil's head. His kill had gone into another killer's count. The price came to him through the brokers, the way his contracts came. He declined it. Spoil was faster than he was, and he did not take a contract on anyone faster.

#### Where They Stand

Rathien Veythar is independent, and he was once of the Scarlet Accord. He takes few contracts and chooses each one. He is called the Crimson Whisper, the Black-Threaded Fang and the Visceral Cutter, and the Silent Hand of the Sanguine Tally.
He is tall and lean, fair-skinned under dark cloth that drinks the light. Close around him the light goes soft and grey. The air drags a little where he moves. His eyes go to every lamp in a room and keep going back to them.
The merchant of Kethra is still counted among the Butcher's dead, and the price is still on Spoil. Spoil has never seen him. He has taken no contract on anyone faster than himself. He puts his work in three sentences: "They won't hear me. They won't see me. They'll just fall."

### Lore · The Life Behind the Card

*Written 2026-09-24 by the character-lore pass from this card and the record of The sainted, the fallen and the vessel. What those sources do not state is new here.*

#### Origin

Rhaevan Serathiel was born in the Withering Era to a woman who drew up contracts for a living. She wrote them for the great houses of her city, and some were for the hire of a knife. He learned his letters copying her terms.
The knives those houses bought were cheap and loud. When he was a boy, a hired man came for a factor in the market at the busy hour. He cut and missed. The factor ran into the stalls, and the hired man cut his way after him through the people in between. The factor died last. The house had paid for one death. It got that one and the others with it, and a street full of people who could describe the hired man's face to anyone who asked.
The boy watched it from his mother's door. Afterward he watched the people who had seen it: who told it, who changed it in the telling, who would not speak of it at all. His mother drew up that house's next contract.

#### The Making

He went into the trade himself. Others in it studied ground, and he studied people. He learned a household before he learned its walls: who kept the keys, who slept badly, where a guarded man never looked. He came in where a man's own attention had left an opening. Anyone weaker than him saw him only when he let them. Witnesses described his face afterward, a different face each time, and none of them was his.
He read what a death would set moving before he agreed to one. He took few contracts. Once he had accepted one, events leaned toward its completion, and the people who hired him learned to count on that. Nations hired him and denied it in public, then hired him again in private. He never once found that contradictory.
He killed rarely. When he killed, he taught, and the explanation went to whoever was meant to learn from the death, never to the room.
Then he turned the study on himself. He saw his own soul from outside, and his fracture record lay open: every old break his soul had made to carry load, visible and impossible to un-see. Most practitioners flinch from that ledger. Rhaevan found nothing in his worth denying. He read it and filed it, the way he filed his contracts, and he founded a syndicate, the Obsidian Veil, on the theory that most violence is simply badly organized.

#### The Cost

The quiet he kept around himself bent light, sound and intent. The longer he stayed inside it, the faster it cut the lines of feeling that tied him to other people, faster than he has ever admitted to anyone. He bound himself to contracts. The record names no friend.
His one stroke killed, and in the same cut it thinned what everyone nearby remembered of the dead. It thinned his own memory too. Each time, he remembered the kill a little less clearly. The completed contracts went into the Ledger of Quiet Names, in ink visible to nobody but him. Open, chaotic ground took his quiet from him. Near a commander of great power whose field ran on strong feeling, the detachment the stroke depended on could give way.
Once, a house whose general the Gilded Thorn had killed, in the years Ambrose Virellith served the Violet Court, sent the Obsidian Veil an offer for the relic's death. With it came what the house knew: the executions of generals, the wings that do not stop once they open, the men who had struck the relic and gone mad with guilt. Rhaevan read it. He did not accept the contract. He put the file in front of the student he was training in person and waited while the student read it. The student gave him the reason. Rhaevan returned the offer to the house with nothing added, and he kept the file.

#### Where They Stand

Rhaevan Serathiel is founder and Sovereign of the Obsidian Veil. He carries Serathiel's Ring, which binds life-force to contract terms, and an ivory dagger called Politeness. One line is kept in his name: "Noise is for amateurs. True death arrives politely."
He still teaches from the Gilded Thorn's file. The Ledger of Quiet Names holds no entry for Ambrose, and no record says Ambrose ever learned of the offer. The student he trains in person is the one relation of his that survives contact with him. Teaching asks for the one thing his contracts cannot supply.
Around him a drawing room is forming, the Obsidian Parlor, where sound dims, heartbeats echo and the ways out seem fewer than they are. It has not finished forming.
Nobody knows why every nation that has hired him denies it afterward in the same careful wording, as though the denial were part of the contract. He shares a surname with Azure Serathiel, the Sword Saint, and no record joins the two men. Nobody knows whose name the ring carries.

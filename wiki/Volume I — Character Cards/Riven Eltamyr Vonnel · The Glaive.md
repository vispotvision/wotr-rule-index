### Lore · The Life Behind the Card

*Written 2026-09-24 by the character-lore pass from this card and the record of The courts of waking and sleeping law. What those sources do not state is new here.*

#### Origin

Riven Eltamyr Vonnel was born in the lands of a holy see and raised inside it. His father copied testimony for the see's hearings. The boy sat on the floor behind the copying desk through the long sessions, where he could see the witnesses' feet and nothing above their knees. He learned which of them shifted before they answered. By the time he could read the rolls himself, he could say which pages his father would have to copy twice.
The see kept a seminary for the arbiters and enforcers it sent out, and he entered it young. He was narrow and quick. His masters taught him to cross a hearing room in the fewest steps. Seraphion Veydrak came out of the same seminary, and both went on into the Guild Accord's Arbitration Division as enforcers.

#### The Making

The Division gave him to the Neutral Chambers, which try cases of uncertain guilt. He carried a lacquered parasol-glaive, and when he opened it over a witness, the spores it released made the witness speak truly. Lies broke into petals in front of him.
Early in his service a woman was brought before him for the death of her husband's brother. He opened the parasol over her, and she confessed. She had mixed the draught herself and carried it to the man's bed. Every word was true to her, and no petal broke. Riven chose his line. Before he stepped in, an apothecary's boy came into the sanctum with the shop's shelf-book. It showed the jar she had taken down refilled that morning by another hand. Riven lowered the glaive. He stood in the emptied sanctum with the parasol closed until the lamps burned out.
From that night he stored every testimony in petals and replayed it in a neutral sanctum before he ruled, however plain the case looked. Kingdoms began to send for him. The Chambers trusted him with singular execution rulings when Concord balance demanded one.
He asked once, and what answered was a courtroom. It was the Rosette Court. It became his Domain and the whole of his Wellspring network, self-contained, and inside it the cut he chose could not be turned.

#### The Cost

He gets one cut. The Final Petal is a single severance chosen before the strike, and he pays for it in the lungs and in the backlash that follows. An unjust verdict under it could collapse him. Each step of his footwork narrows what can happen next, and each is paid in the feet. A crowd in disorder, a masked witness, a wind across the floor: any of them keeps him from choosing his line in advance. Without a chosen line he does not strike.
At Braqth the Crimson Hunt had burned a confederation's Wellspring into a feasting ground, and Arbitration sent him to settle the loss. Varek Ka'ruun would not answer a written ruling. He called for duel court under the coming-of-age law his carvings had been cut by, and Riven accepted. They fought inside a ring of basalt posts. Hazzen Skollgrin stood at its edge under the Petal Writ, there to carry out the ruling if Varek refused it. Riven kept out of Varek's reach. His footwork narrowed the exchange toward one ending, and he closed it with the cut he had chosen before he stepped in. Varek lost and said he was honoured by the artistry of his own defeat. The Hunt left that Wellspring to the confederation. Riven walked out of the ring on strained feet, and Hazzen had nothing to enforce.
At Altherion he lost. A carrying house's master had broken a settlement sworn before Atheron Dreymarch, and Atheron had already laid a geas on his will in sleep. Before the Altherion Central Concord Tribunal, Riven argued that the geas was a sentence and that no man could be sentenced twice for one breach. Lord Maerion Lysandre answered for the injured house that a ruling given in a dream paid no carrier. Leontes Praevan read the written settlement and ruled on it alone. He gave the house its carriage. The master went home liable awake and bound asleep.
The courtroom that answered Riven signed nothing. His standing rests on law. The co-author of every ruling he gives inside the Rosette Court has put its name to no page.

#### Where They Stand

Riven Eltamyr Vonnel is Arbiter Exemplar of the Neutral Chambers. Kingdoms know him as the Parasoul Executioner and the Glaive of Quiet Rebuke. They fear him as an enforcer and use him as a diplomat's tool, and they mistrust him as an adjudicator whose mercy is structured.
He governs the waking law beside Atheron, who governs the sleeping. Maerion has beaten him in Concord trials and lost to him in others. He and Elder Seravain have debated Concord law on venom for hours, each respecting the other's elegance. Hazzen bites the Petal Writ often. It still holds him. When he focuses, soft motes of verdure bloom around him, and lies break into petals that only he can see.

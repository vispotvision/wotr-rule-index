### Lore · The Life Behind the Card

*Written 2026-09-24 by the character-lore pass from this card and the record of Alabaster and the Scale. What those sources do not state is new here.*

#### Origin

Seraphine Lurielle was born a daughter of the Holy Sea of Alabaster, to a noble house whose manor stood over a Somnalis current. The Sea wrote her birth into its archive beside the house's contracts. The current had run under those floors for longer than the house had owned them. Guests who stayed the night came down in the morning describing dreams they could not account for. The household served them tea and let them talk.
Her mother received callers in the front parlour every afternoon. She taught her daughter to pour, to keep lace clean under a glove, and to look bored while every word in the room was being kept. The Sea asked of the house what it asked of every noble house: attendance at the observances, testimony when it was called for, and loyalty spoken aloud. Seraphine gave all three. She gave them with a visible yawn.

#### The Making

The current found her while she still lived in her mother's house. It came up into her twice, in quick succession, and after the second time the household knew. She reached for cups before they tipped. During a quarrel between two guests she shifted a chair away from the window, and a stray blow went through the glass where the chair had stood. The Sea's clergy came to the manor to see what the house kept under it. They went away calling her a conduit.
The surge came later, at her own table, and it went through her right eye and took it. In its place it left a lilac lens, and through the lens came too many tomorrows at once. The clergy bound it with a ribbon of their own making. The ribbon narrowed what reached her to what she could survive.
In that same hour, with the eye already gone, she chose to keep being a person who takes tea and gossips in lace. The current went on speaking through her. She poured while it did.

#### The Cost

She wears the ribbon every waking hour and every sleeping one. Without it the visions drown her.
The gift answers in one room. She made the White Parlour its ground, and the parlour became a small, absolute country. A stubborn guest there circles back to the detail that matters. Her tea gives a guest back shallow fragments of dream that same night, the fears and plans they never spoke aloud. Away from her salon the working is not there. Outside that door she is a noblewoman with one eye and an ordinary body, and she has never once needed to hit anything. Every fight she has won, she won before it started.
When a future has to close, she takes a glowing thread in her bandaged hand and twists it. The Alabaster clergy call it a Parlour Fracture. Pulled too hard, the thread snaps the possibilities around it and forces an outcome where she meant only to deny one. Afterward she is bedridden for hours, and she can bear neither pain nor light.
She tells a guest what she saw. She has never told one how the thread-work under it runs.
Once she warned an Alabaster prince off a coronation date that would have killed him within the week. He delayed, and he lived. The people who had wanted that date remembered who had moved it. She gained enemies by it, and a quiet gratitude that has never been spoken in company. The Accord finds her useful whenever its aims preserve lives over glory.

#### Where They Stand

She is still, officially, a loyal daughter of the Sea, and publicly bored by the obligation. Guild Accord envoys visit under the pretext of tea to discuss what tomorrow might politely avoid, and those who drink from the porcelain teaset arrive and leave when it nudges them. Nobles who ask nicely have their choices read on the lace.
The Ecliptic Choir once asked for her parlour, and she lent it. The Accord's councils wanted a reading of the rupture the boy Lumen Varos had called on the Day of Seven Refractions, and the Aurum Sect would bring its initiate to an Accord reader in no house of its own. An escort brought him in and stood inside the door. Maeron Iskare came for the Obscurial Chamber. Seraphine poured. The boy drank, and Maeron left his cup where she had set it. He asked leave to take the boy into a shared sleep, with the hostess as the third, and to read the rupture from inside the sight that had seen it. She refused. She said it once. He asked his questions across the table instead, and when the teaset's hour came the escort took the boy home. She made that refusal in the hearing of the Accord she serves unofficially and of the Sea she serves by name. The Choir knows now that her parlour will say no to an Accord reader.
She does not know whether a future she cuts stops existing, or only stops being one she can find again. The ribbon stays on.

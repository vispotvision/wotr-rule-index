### Lore · The Life Behind the Card

*Written 2026-09-24 by the character-lore pass from this card and the record of Those who write mercy in ash. What those sources do not state is new here.*

#### Origin

Seraphion Veydrak was born in the lands of a holy see. His mother was a smith who made fetters for its courts, at a forge set against the wall of the see's gaol. She closed every link on a hot rivet, and a link closed that way came open again only under a chisel. The boy carried the finished lengths across the yard to the turnkeys. He brought the worn ones back to be cut.
The see kept a seminary for the arbiters and enforcers it sent out, and he entered it young. Riven Eltamyr Vonnel came out of the same seminary. High Priestess Myrall took Seraphion as her student there, and she made him hear every case to its end before he named it. He learned the forms of a vow and the forms of a sentence from the same books.

#### The Making

The see made him its High Sentinel. He kept its gates and the prisoners behind them, and he kept each sentence until it was carried out. Then he left the see's service. The Guild Accord's Arbitration Division took him and set him in its Judgment Order as an inquisitor.
In the Order he swore three vows on his own life: "No False Judgment. No Mercy for the Condemned. No Abandonment of the Chosen." A ritual spike was fused into his right forearm. With it he pierced Wellspring echoes and bound them to his own control. He learned to write left-handed.
The ring first rose behind him in a hearing room of the Order, over a man who had lied to the Order. It hung at his back, a floating circle of chained glyphs. The man could not lie inside it. When he tried to rise from the bench, his soul began to fray. Each chain held him as iron holds a wrist, and each was also the sentence passed on him. Seraphion kept both in hand and heard the case to its end. Afterward he said the chain was the shape of justice, and he meant the iron and the sentence at once.

#### The Cost

The vows bound him as they bound the men he judged. When he turned a sworn oath into a binding and closed a place of judgment around the guilty, he stood inside it with them. He had to judge truly. The first vow closed every convenient verdict to him, on pain of his life.
At Altherion, Arbitration took up the matter of the seven judges of the Broken Tribunal, and the Judgment Order sent Seraphion to hear it. Aeldoris Vanthryx came when he was called. Seraphion raised the Chain-Halo behind him before he asked anything. The clerks at the table nodded over their pages, and the scribe's pen stopped in the middle of a line. Asked whether he had struck any of the seven, the hunter said he had not lifted a hand. The halo held. Asked what had broken them, he said they had killed their own dream-selves. Seraphion found no act of the hunter's hand in the matter and entered no sentence.
At Ordas, among the Kingdoms of the Five Rivers, he condemned an elder of the Archon-Touched. The elder's house kept its elder behind its own doors. The sentence went unexecuted. Kaelan Vorn held no commission. He came to Ordas and executed the elder with one strike, and was gone. When the house laid the body on a bier in its courtyard and began a raising, Seraphion came to keep his sentence. He raised the halo over the courtyard and bound the officiants where they stood. Velastra Nirellium came up the road on foot. The chant broke off as she neared the bier, and she struck once there. The same stroke loosened the glyphs that held the officiants, and some of them got free before he raised the ring again. When the courtyard was quiet she asked him whether he wanted his three vows cut.
He said no.

#### Where They Stand

Seraphion Veydrak is the Chain-Halo, Arbiter of the Thrice-Bound Oath, an inquisitor of the Judgment Order. He sees Raivhar Drakonis, whom the Accord has declared rogue, as a heretic to be reclaimed or destroyed. He watches Ziyu "Pip" Inari with suspicion. He and High Priestess Myrall are estranged over moral disputes that no record names.
The three vows are the only bonds he keeps. They cannot all hold. If one of the Chosen is condemned, the second vow forbids him mercy and the third forbids him to abandon them, and the first forbids him to call the judgment false. Breaking any of the three forfeits his life. No record yet names the Chosen, and none shows one of them condemned.

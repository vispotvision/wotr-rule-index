### VIII · Signature

#### Temperance Synchrony

**Effect** · **Two clashing Essences in one room are brought to equilibrium.** *Judicium reads each party's spectrum; Ur on the mantle sets a boundary that equalises the opposing gradients; Coagula sinters the fractured contract between them back into one body.* Lies acquire weight and honesty loses it, and the parties fall silent under a serenity they did not choose. **At the Caedor and Babyl hearing, both sides held that silence for an hour.**
**Cost** · **A mirror that absorbs resonance in order to return it purified has, for the length of the hearing, absorbed it.** *The exhaustion beneath the tranquil exterior is the residue. It is carried out of the chamber and it is still there when nobody is disputing anything.* Coagula's porosity is the second bill: what is sintered is not fully dense, and the void sits where the next fracture will start.
**Limit** · **It equalises. It does not decide.** Both parties must be present and emitting; a hearing of forty souls is a spectral overload and the Adjudicator loses the thread. Nothing about it works at speed, and nothing about it works on a party that has already left the room.
**Counter** · **Say nothing.** *Judicium reads emission, and a soul that gives no spectrum gives nothing to weigh.* A practitioner trained in Harmonics Suppression masks the field and enters the Chambers as a blank; a Silentia working severs the room's resonance outright. **Or strike at the void: find the clause Coagula sintered around and it fractures there first.**
> **What nobody knows.** What the mirror has absorbed and never returned purified, and whether the hour of silence was the field or the Adjudicator's exhaustion reaching the room.

### Lore · The Life Behind the Card

*Written 2026-09-24 by the character-lore pass from this card and the record of Babyl and the Praetorian March. What those sources do not state is new here.*

#### Origin

Seren Valenne was born into the nobility of the kingdom Varuun Daskorr now rules, an Inner World dominion of stone and faith. The house kept its rooms in the capital, under the citadel. On feast days its halls filled with guests. The child could not stay in them. In a crowded hall every guest reached Seren at once, what each one felt and what each one kept back, and none of it arrived in order. They went to the quiet rooms and stayed there until the music stopped.
A master-at-arms came to the house to teach Seren the sword. They set the practice blade on the rack at the first lesson and left it there. They learned instead to answer a raised voice with a low one. Then they waited. The wait ran long enough for the other speaker to hear what had just been said. Most people lowered their voices after that. When the house steward lied about a debt, his words reached Seren heavy, and the honest talk around them came light.

#### The Making

They left the kingdom for the Guild Accord. The Arbitration Division took them in. Their silver-white hair was braided past the waist, the Division's mark of neutrality, and they wore grey and deep umber. Years in the Neutral Chambers tempered the bearing they had brought from the capital. They heard disputes of Concord Law, of Divine Sanction and of Temperance violations. The Oath Scale stood before them at every hearing. The miniature crystalline balance vibrated whenever someone lied. It read what was said. It passed no judgment on it.
Parties who came in shouting fell quiet in front of them. Lies grew heavy in the mouth, and the truth came easily. Seren never raised their voice. They never drew a weapon. Their peers began to call them the Grey Adjudicator, and quarrels that had once become interrealm wars ended at their table.
Then they were given the Seal of the Grey Court. It granted them immediate jurisdiction over disputes involving Archonic influence. They sat in judgment over that influence at close quarters, and the proximity did not destroy them.
After the Eighth Fracture of Faith, Caedor and the kingdom of Seren's birth contested a Wellspring boundary, and the dispute came before them. The king did not come. He decrees only in his Throne Hall, and the kingdom's case came by its advocates. Seren sat between the parties with the Oath Scale and said very little. Both sides held that silence for an hour. The hour was co-authored, and the parties felt the second author as pressure. Nobody in the chamber had commanded it. When the hearing closed, Seren set down the terms, and they went to both parties as the Accord's.

#### The Cost

A mirror keeps nothing, and that is the claim. They take in what a room feels, read it, and give it back purified. Every hearing sends them home exhausted all the same. The exhaustion leaves the chamber with them, and it is still there when nobody is disputing anything.
They bring two parties level and decide nothing between them. In a chamber of forty souls every one reports at once, as the feast halls did, and they lose the thread. A party that says nothing gives them nothing to weigh. A party that has already left the room is out of their reach. What they mend is never fully whole. A broken contract comes back together without being melted down, and the small voids left in it are where the next fracture starts.
At the Arbitration of Anguz, unofficial reports say, Seren invoked the aid of Kaelrith Dorne, a Free Lance who had deserted the Enforcement Division. The Division's record of the trial names no Free Lance. He stood through it with half a sword. Seren did not raise their voice. Neither spoke to the other throughout the trial, and both left alive, which was counted peace enough.
Seren holds that every lie leaves a gravity behind it. They have spent their working life in the rooms where it collects.

#### Where They Stand

Seren Valenne sits as a Senior Adjudicator of the Arbitration Division in the Neutral Chambers, which Atheron Dreymarch keeps for the Division. The Seal of the Grey Court gives them jurisdiction wherever Archonic influence enters a dispute.
The Caedor terms reached the Throne Hall in the kingdom of Seren's birth. Its king has not carried them out. He delays them under the Temporal Weight Clauses, as he delays the Accord's decrees. Elthiré Maen, a Pathwarden out of Caedor, was last seen near that kingdom's borders. Vaelthor Ashen-Meridian, senior counsel to the Division, counts Seren among the voting officers toward the vote that would make him its sole interpreter of Codex law.
Nobody knows what the mirror has absorbed and never returned purified. Nor does anyone know whether the hour of silence was the field or Seren's own exhaustion reaching the room.

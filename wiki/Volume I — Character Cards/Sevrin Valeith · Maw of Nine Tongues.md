### Lore · The Life Behind the Card

*Written 2026-09-24 by the character-lore pass from this card and the record of The Voice Between Wars and the Outer nobles. What those sources do not state is new here.*

#### Origin

Sevrin Valeith was born on the bone-beds of Dorrosfenn, in the Outer World, to quarrymen who worked an open bench of Marrowchalk. The rock was the bones of an ancient shallow sea, pressed into chalk and cut out by hand. Ground and spread on western fields, it fed the wheat. Prepared otherwise, it fed every necromantic practice in the quarter. The same bench cut both.
Buyers came down to the bench and said what they wanted the chalk for. Some said fields and lied. The chalk was the same either way, and nobody on the bench could have told one sack from another. Dorrosfenn's theocracy sent buyers who named no use at all. They took the chalk raw, as it came out of the ground, and the boy sacked it for them with the rest.

#### The Making

He left the benches on a chalk barge. Beneath the ossuary-city of Nerrith lies a Wellspring that teaches the soul's dismantling and rearrangement. He walked down to it and learned. He came up a binder.
The Guild Accord's Arbitration Division took him into its service. Its teams went into the field against called and bound things, and the Division needed a man who could hold what they met. Sevrin held it. He had Parunic death-script etched into both hands. With a touch he could tether a thing's Essence to a sealed relic-skull and lock it there, between life and death. The skulls floated about him wherever he went, each home to a bound echo. They scouted for him. They carried his workings, and they tormented whatever he turned them on.
The echoes spoke, and he listened to several at once. Among their voices he heard his own. His soul saw itself from outside. Looking at it changed it, and knowing that it changed it changed it again. He came out of it able to speak with up to nine spirits at once and weave their power into his own. The price was a gradual erosion of the mind.

#### The Cost

A sovereign spirit stood against the people living on its ground, and the Division took the matter as a spiritual mediation. It sent Aurevian Lysanthir to mediate with his harp. It sent Sevrin to hold the spirit if the harp failed. Aurevian played. The spirit listened, its anger came off it into the harper, and the mediation closed. Before the spirit could withdraw, Sevrin laid his etched hands on it and bound it into his own service. The Division entered the commission as concluded.
The binding came to light later, with others. The Accord moved against him for Soul Concord violations, for binding sovereign spirits into private service. He was out of the Division before it could hold him. The doctrine of passage holds that the dead may leave traces, and that the trace is not the soul. A practitioner who refuses that distinction after demonstration goes under Conviction review. Sevrin refused it. He put his answer in one line: "The dead do not lie, only the living do. That's why I keep their tongues."
He kept them. Every binding he took was drawn against a reserve that was already short. The echoes stood solid in the shape his intent gave them. A flaw in the intent stood in them too, faithfully and at scale, and he rarely saw one before it stood in front of him. He could hold nine. He came to hold nine.

#### Where They Stand

He works the Outer World's frost-barren calderas now, rogue, and trades in forbidden conjurations. The skulls float about him and whisper without stopping. Hyo Miran trades him talismans to contain the whispering, and Sevrin finds the dealer's company uncomfortably amusing.
Out of the calderas he came into Epprenean ground, and Vaelthyr Iscarn, heir of the Pale Court, received him. Sevrin offered bound spirit-messengers. Vaelthyr offered political cover: the Pale Court's ground to stand on, and his own voice at the Accord's table whenever the Accord asked after the binder. The talk ran long past the terms. Sevrin bound the first messenger in the heir's presence and handed it over. Each one after it was another binding he could not spare.
The pursuit reached the Pale Court. The Division sent Aurevian Lysanthir, the harper from the spirit's mediation, to ask the Epprenean envoy whether his court had given the rogue binder ground. Vaelthyr answered that the Pale Court kept no fugitive of the Accord's. The answer stands on the Division's record as he gave it. Sevrin was not in the room.
Karo Venrik, a colleague from his Accord years, informs for him inside the Accord, reluctantly. What Sevrin holds over him is written nowhere. Sevrin and Draven Kael Vorrick respect each other as predators. Draven despises spirit-binding, and his threat to kill Sevrin if he binds anything "with a beating heart" stands.
He is called the Pallid Binder, the Whisper-Keeper, and the Maw of Nine Tongues. The Accord still pursues him. A broken skull releases what it held, and he does not control what comes out. Nobody knows which of the nine tongues is still speaking for itself.

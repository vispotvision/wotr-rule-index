### Lore · The Life Behind the Card

*Written 2026-09-24 by the character-lore pass from this card and the record of The Reaper's office. What those sources do not state is new here.*

#### Origin

Shiragiku Iori was born in Altherion, the republic on the Ring's rim, where every people lived under one charter and argued over the same prices. Her mother laid out the dead for the Accord households of the city. She washed them and dressed them in clean linen. Within the hour, she knew whether the Echo had gone.
Those households kept their dead above ground in the house and sat with them in shifts. The sitting was the rite. Iori sat her first shift as a small child, on a chair set beside the bier. The grounding tea was poured at the start of a night shift and had gone cold by the small hours, and she drank it cold. The smell of cold tea and clean linen stayed on her into her grown life.
She began to bring flowers to the sittings with her own coin, pale chrysanthemums from the market stalls, which lasted through a sitting without browning. From the chair she learned which mourners wept for the dead and which wept for the room.

#### The Making

The procession that made her belonged to a stranger. A young man of a trading house fell from a stair in the lower market, and the crowd behind his bier grew at every corner. Somebody said the fall had been a push. By the parish ground the crowd had a name, a carter who had been on the stair that morning. They beat him against the parish wall until he stopped moving. Iori was inside the procession with a basket on her arm.
No household claimed the carter. Her mother laid him out, and Iori sat with him alone through the first night. Before dawn the parish men came and carried him to the crossroads, without the roll.
After that she walked the processions. She watched a crowd as her mother watched a body, for the hour when something went out of it. At the burial of a lodging-house keeper, the mourners began to look for a name, and the dead woman's brother led them. Iori stepped in front of him and pinned a white chrysanthemum to his coat. The flower warmed. His hands opened. The procession reached the parish ground as a procession, and nobody was beaten at the wall.
The chrysanthemum on his coat had drunk his rage and exhaled clarity. The Accord retained her to the Reaper for deathbed rites, and from then on she prepared the last rooms Yukizumi Yukari entered.

#### The Cost

In a timber house in Altherion, on a stone ground floor, a dying man's last word snagged on an old grudge his daughter held against him. The room ran hot. The Reaper brought his adjutants to the bed. Nothing green grew on the stone, and Kozakura Shion grounded his naginata at the threshold and watched. Iori laid her ring of eight blooms and pinned a chrysanthemum on the daughter. Natsume Kurogiku went for the grudge with her red shears. The hinge locked. Natsume put the shears away and hemmed the room with chalk.
Iori carried the daughter's share under the Snow-Mask for the hour, so the daughter could speak plainly. The bloom on her chest warmed. The father spoke his last word into the grudge. It landed. The daughter made her promise at the bed, and Yukizumi lowered the star. Iori had carried other shares that day. The fever came on her that night. By morning she was faint.
She carried a share for the hour and no longer. Mourners begged her to keep the whole of it, and she refused every one. At the end of the hour the veil came up. The rest was theirs to finish.
She could not fake love or sorrow, and she could not cover for anyone who did. At a wake in a merchant house the heir wept at the bier, and petals fell at Iori's feet in front of the family. She did not step off them. The house never sent for her again.

#### Where They Stand

Iori is retained to the Reaper for deathbed rites. She prepares his last rooms and catches final sentences before the crowd can bruise them. At hot councils she sits with the Star Chamber. Her living blooms mark who should speak. When riot grief turns toward a lynching, she turns it back into a funeral procession.
When wakes run hot she walks the funeral routes with Shion and Natsume. With Lilium Verdanis and Kohako she keeps last words whole. Her basket is lined with salt-felt and holds twelve live wakes without wilting, and Shirogiri hangs at her hip like a short saber. Smoke bells and Rot-mold bruise her blooms. Where Kaito Akagiri has heated a room, the mourning in it plays to the gallery, and her flowers brown.
When panic wants a martyr, she sets out white blooms, and the room remembers mercy.

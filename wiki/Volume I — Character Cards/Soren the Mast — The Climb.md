Built like a rope. All sinew and length, the specific vertical proportion of a body that has been climbing since childhood. Long arms, narrow waist, feet that grip ratlines the way hands grip tools. Hair so sun-bleached it has gone from brown to the colour of wet straw. Moves through rigging with the fluid economy of a man whose body has memorised every handhold. Communicates with the helm through a private language of hand signals developed over years with Garret.
His oath: *"The climbing is the oath. The oath is the climbing. I do not know how to separate the two and I do not intend to learn."* Then he climbed back to the roof, because the roof was the watch and the watch was the function.
He was the one who spotted the Titan Droval from the mast-pole, delivering the tactical report at rigger-speed: *"Two. Titan. Twenty-plus at the shoulder."*

### Lore · The Life Behind the Card

*Written 2026-09-24 by the character-lore pass from this card, the eight archived scenes it appears in, and the record of The Kōkan expedition's hands. What those sources do not state is new here.*

#### Origin

Soren was born in the Concord, in a harbour town where merchant hulls wintered at the quay with their masts standing. His mother sewed canvas in a sail loft above the water. He was climbing the laid-up rigging before anyone trusted him near the harbour's edge. He climbed barefoot. His soles learned to close on a ratline the way a carpenter's hand closes on a chisel, and they never unlearned it.
The quay called him the Mast before he had a trade to be named for. The name stuck. His hair was brown then, and years of sun aloft would bleach it to the colour of wet straw.

#### The Making

He went to sea as a ship's boy and stayed aloft. Early in his years on deep-water hulls a squall threw him off a topsail yard. The shrouds caught him across the chest. One bare foot hooked a ratline and held. At the next morning's watch he was back up the same mast, and he learned it hold by hold. Then he learned every mast after it. His body came to keep the map of every handhold and every tension point in a sail plan, and his hands trimmed a line in passing without his eyes going to it.
He worked ship after ship, and on many of them Garret Longshore had the helm. A shout from the masthead went downwind and was lost. Over years the rigger and the helmsman made a private language of hand signals between the rigging and the wheel. Nobody watching from the deck could read it. Soren kept his voice for what hands could not carry, and he pitched it to cross wind.
When the expedition sailed for the polar shelf, he shipped as its rigger, lookout and forward reconnaissance, with Garret at the helm. Off the shelf the wind came broadside for hours, and the rigging hummed one note while he worked the lines and signed down to the wheel. At the rift camp he took the highest igloo's roof for his watch. Sadamu kept watch from the same roof.
At the rift's edge, unsummoned, the oath-givers swore to the two brothers, and Soren went eighth. He had been on the roof since before the gathering began, and he dropped to the ice, landed light and gave his name. "I have no words for this that are better than the climbing," he said. He had climbed for them since port and would climb until Kharven, "and I will climb for you after Kharven if the climbing is needed." Then he said the rest: "The climbing is the oath. The oath is the climbing. I do not know how to separate the two and I do not intend to learn." He pressed his fist to his chest. Then he climbed back to the roof, because the roof was the watch and the watch was the function.

#### The Cost

The watch did not pause for the ceremony. When Sodoku looked along the semicircle at the people who had sworn, Soren was already back on the roof. He came down again when Sonzai ordered the camp struck. He took down the rigging that had braced the igloos against the wind.
On the shelf he left the column an hour before it moved and ranged ahead into the white. For four days his hands signed clear. Garret read the signs. Everything Soren saw reached the column through one man.
On the fifth day the ice carried a tremor. He went up the supply sled's mast-pole, and from there he was the one who spotted the Titan Droval: two of them to the northeast, their blue-white fur lost against the shelf until they moved. He came down and landed beside Garret. "Two. Titan. Twenty-plus at the shoulder." He had watched them part from one position. He called them a bonded pair, mates or parent and young, and warned that a threat to one would bring the other.
Then he went back up the pole. When the first Droval folded its legs under Sodoku, Soren made a sound from the mast-pole that was no word, somewhere between a laugh and a prayer. His reading held. After both claims the two beasts called to each other across the ice. Sonzai considered what Soren had said as the darker one turned to its companion without direction from the chain.
Before the column reached Kharven-Seat, Soren had spent nine days on a mast-pole.

#### Where They Stand

He came in with the column to the north gate of Kharven-Seat, the brothers riding the two Droval. On crown ground Dunstan argued with nobody over whether the ladle was personal or crown property. Nella told him it counted as an assault charge. Soren said the ground there smelled like a hearth somebody had died in front of. Nobody argued with him.
He is still the expedition's rigger, lookout and forward reconnaissance, and his hands still talk to Garret. The ground remains the place he visits between climbs. The last clause of his oath is open: he will climb for the brothers after Kharven if the climbing is needed.

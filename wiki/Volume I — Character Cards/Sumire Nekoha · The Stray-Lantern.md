### Lore · The Life Behind the Card

*Written 2026-09-24 by the character-lore pass from this card and the record of The Reaper's crowd crews. What those sources do not state is new here.*

#### Origin

Sumire Nekoha was born over a bakehouse in a slate town of the Old World, where the quarrymen climbed to the faces before dawn. The ovens were lit in the dark. Her mother baked. Sumire carried the loaves to the doorsteps while the town slept, with the cage-lantern from beside the oven in her hand. The bakehouse cats kept the flour free of mice and walked the rounds with her. She learned the bad stoops: a dog behind one door, a man who struck his wife behind another, a step that iced before the rest.
One winter morning she found a small boy asleep on a stoop that was not his. She took him to the quarry company's beadle. The beadle asked the boy who had lost him. He named his sister, who had been set to mind him while their mother worked the sorting sheds. By the thaw the sister had been sent into service in another town. After that Sumire walked the strays she found to their own doors and did not ask who had lost them first.

#### The Making

One pay night at the company hall the wage came short, and the quarrymen turned on the Clerk. The crowd broke out into the lanes. The children who had brought their fathers' suppers scattered through the stair-streets. Sumire had the lantern and nothing else. She hummed low in her throat, and the breathing of the children nearest her slowed toward her own. Then she tipped the lantern. A ribbon of violet moth-light unwound along the alleys and down the stairs, running where she looked, and the children followed it like a bedtime story that knows corners. It lasted as long as she watched it. When the last child reached a door she stopped, and the light went out of the lanes.
A Guild Accord factor, in town over the slate carts, had watched from the hall steps. He wrote her name down for the Safe-Route Auxiliary. In time a moth-star came to turn in the lantern's cage, and its moths chose perches instead of faces.
The Accord seconded her to the Reaper for delicate crossings through crowds. At a cantor's door in Altherion, a congregation held its dying cantor with a sworn chorus, and its children sang with the rest. Sumire went in first and walked them out along the Moth Path, down the stair and into the yard. Deimos, the Sin Eater, waited on the stair until the last of them had passed. After Maelia Cantorine cut the chorus and the oath came away, she came back up and kept the room gentle while Yukizumi lowered the star.

#### The Cost

She owned nothing made to hurt anyone. A bully who had to be stopped got a mischievous flick, and his apology arrived before the bruise. Bad luck she took on herself. She borrowed a child's misfortune into a violet bow and tied it on her apron, and when the bow unknotted, the misfortune spent on her instead. There came to be nine.
At Bridgemarch in Nalūn she laid an Arbitration escort's route through the junction crowds. A face gave way in the Cuttings. The barracks crowd came up onto the chain-bridges, and she took its children up the tiers by the Moth Path before any bell sounded. At the edge of a damaged span a bow came undone. The rail gave under her hand. She went over. Salt dust had clouded Seraphine Kharza's manifests, and her first hands would not hold. Cyrille Lacrim sweetened the water, and a nurse's cradle palm caught Sumire above the cavern floor and set her down. Only then did Cyrille strike her bell. Puddle, Cyrille's blue cat, curled on Sumire while the crowd walked. The hand that caught her had belonged to a dead nurse.

#### Where They Stand

She serves in the Accord's Safe-Route Auxiliary. After council nights she sweeps the panic routes for lost little ones, loosened pets and spillover grief. She lays hush-lanes for courier relays across festival crowds. Vespera Corvina maps the night; Sumire pads it. In last rooms she pets the air while Yukizumi lowers the star, and makes the space where Kohako saves the minute and Lilium Verdanis plants the vow. Her soft lanes lead into the wakes Shiragiku Iori and Natsume Kurogiku keep, and carry Kozakura Shion's petals farther than streets deserve. Kaito Akagiri heats rooms to consensus. She cools stoops to choice, and they tease each other without trust. Since Bridgemarch she owes a dead nurse a debt.
Cats, gutter sprites and street-lamps show her the safest stoops. When she opens her apron, the pocket smells of tea and clean bread, and a shaken witness steadies enough to speak. Her cat ears twitch when a lie edges in. Her black ribbons carry no bells; bell-drums and tin whistles rattle her focus. Rain turns the moth-light to a plain glow, and the kindness fades faster. After the ninth bow unties, she must sleep with a cat on her chest, or the bad luck lingers in her bones.

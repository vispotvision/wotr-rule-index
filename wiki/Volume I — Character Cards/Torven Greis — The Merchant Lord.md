# Torven Greis — The Merchant Lord

### The Merchant Lord · Lord of the Chainward Road

---

### I. IDENTITY

**Full Name:** Torven Greis
**Alias / Title:** The Merchant Lord
**Also Known As:** Lord of the Chainward Road · The Grey Comptoir (Nalūn trader shorthand)
**Race / Lineage:** Human — Concord (Inner World frontier-born, second generation; family arrived from the Old World's western coast during the late Long Reckoning)
**Age:** 49 · **Sex:** Male
**Realm / Faction:** Independent — Greis Trading House, headquartered at Chainward Landing, with factor-offices at Ironlink Hold (Nalūn), two Ring smelter camps, and eleven way-stations along the Iron-to-Salt Corridor
**Catalyst Event:** At seventeen, Torven was a carter's runner on a supply convoy moving structural iron south from the Ring smelters to the port. The convoy lost its lead wagon to a bridge collapse over a thaw-swollen gorge — the ward on the bridge footing had thinned past coherence and nobody had paid for the inspection. Fourteen tons of smelted iron went into the water. The convoy master, who had been running the route on credit extended by a Nalūn factor, was ruined by the loss. Torven watched a man who owned a hundred wagons become a man who owned nothing because of a three-layer ward that cost six meals to maintain, and understood two things at once: that the road was a more dangerous creditor than any bank, and that whoever owned the places where the road was *safe* owned the road itself. He bought his first way-station with borrowed salt before he was nineteen.

### II. SOUL ARCHITECTURE

**Level:** 38/500 · **Band:** I — Foundation (Levels 1–100)
**Temperance Stage:** II — Welling
**Soul Crystal Tier:** Awakened (early)
**Aether Class:** Unclassed (Class emerges at Glory)
**Attraction Force / Obsession Force:** Attraction Force. His Crystal developed around the pull toward systems, logistics, the desire to *connect* rather than to *possess*. His Wellspring attunement is minimal and entirely incidental to his actual power.
**Wellspring Harmonization(s):**
Primary — Contraxis (Contract / Agreement). Attunement acquired through prolonged exposure to Nalūn's Parun-bound instruments rather than through formal training. He cannot execute a Parun seal himself, but his Crystal resonates with them, which means he can *feel* when a contract is sound and when the terms are drifting. The Nalūn chainlords noticed this before he did.
**Coherence Band:** F (floor)
**η (Coherence Efficiency):** 0.22 — negligible. His Crystal exists. It does not perform.
**Note:** Torven Greis is not a practitioner in any meaningful combat or applied sense. His Accord certification exists because Rank is collateral, and a man who runs supply contracts across three jurisdictions cannot borrow without a token. His assayed output is so low that the Measurewright who certified him noted, in the margin, that the instrument was functioning correctly.

### VIII. TRAITS

**The Intermediary's Eye** — Torven sees supply chains the way a field commander sees terrain: choke points, redundancies, single points of failure, the place where a disruption would cascade. He cannot read a ward glyph, but he can tell you which ward on which bridge will fail first based on the traffic pattern and the last time anyone paid for maintenance. / **The Road's Memory** — He remembers every route he has ever traveled, every way-station, every ford, every stretch of track where the forest grows back fastest. Not a supernatural memory — a professional one, the kind that forms when your livelihood depends on knowing which section of road will be impassable in three weeks. / **Parun Resonance (passive)** — His Crystal's incidental attunement to Contraxis means Nalūn-sealed contracts feel *settled* to him when they are sound and feel *loose* when they are not. He has walked away from four deals in his career on nothing but this feeling, and all four counterparties defaulted within the year.

### XI. SPIRIT AXES

**Dalla Greis** (wife, deceased) — Weight: foundational, unresolved. She ran the Chainward Landing office while he ran the roads. Died of a fever the ward on her well-stone should have caught. He rebuilt the ward system for every way-station on his network within the year, at a cost that nearly broke the house. The Nalūn factors noticed and extended his credit line without being asked.
**Edsen Greis** (son, living, 23) — Weight: heavy, cautious. Edsen has no interest in the supply routes and is apprenticed to a shipwright at the Landing. Torven does not discuss this and does not discourage it.
**The Chainward Factors** — Weight: professional, mutual. The Nalūn factors at Ironlink Hold trust Greis's supply book the way they trust few non-Nalūn instruments, because he has never missed a delivery date in twenty-seven years of contracted supply.

### XIII. PHYSICAL DESCRIPTION

Tall and narrowly built, the kind of frame that looks thin until you notice the shoulders, which carry the particular breadth of a man who loaded his own wagons for the first fifteen years of his career. His hair is dark, the color of wet ironwood bark, wavy and perpetually falling across his forehead where it escapes from beneath the fur-lined papakha he wears in every season — wolf pelt, northern-trapped, practical rather than decorative, the fur gone slightly yellow at the brim from years of sweat and weather. His face is angular, almost gaunt: a prominent nose with a crook near the bridge where a wagon tongue caught him in his twenties, deep-set eyes under heavy lids that give him a permanent expression of tired calculation, a strong jaw held slightly forward in the way of men who have spent decades listening to other men's proposals and deciding, before the sentence ends, whether to say yes. His skin is weathered to the texture of oiled leather, lined at the eyes and across the forehead, carrying the particular dull flush of someone who has spent more of his life outside walls than inside them.
He dresses for the road even when he is not on it. A heavy woolen scarf wound high around his neck and jaw, thick enough to serve as a pillow, wrapped in the frontier style that keeps the wind out of the chest and the face half-hidden — a habit he acquired on the northern routes and never dropped. A layered frontier cloak, heavy across the shoulders, the outer layer oiled against rain, the inner lined with a coarser wool. Boots resoled twice a year. No rings, no chains, no visible wealth except the single linked Nalūn seal-token clipped to his belt, tarnished with use — the first Parun-bound contract he ever held, kept not for sentiment but because it still works.
His hands are large, the knuckles thickened, the nails kept short. Calloused across the palms from handling rope and cargo in his youth, now cleaner but still rough. He moves with the economy of a man who has learned that unnecessary movement costs energy and energy costs money.

### XIV. PSYCHOLOGY

**Core Wound:** The bridge collapse. Watching a man lose everything because a maintenance ward went uninspected taught Torven that the distance between wealth and ruin is exactly the width of one neglected glyph groove. He has never stopped expecting the next bridge to fail.
**Flaw:** Cannot delegate trust. He inspects his own way-stations, reviews his own supply books, walks his own routes. The Greis Trading House has forty-seven employees and one man who believes he must personally verify that every load arrives. This is sustainable at forty-nine. It will not be sustainable at sixty.
**Desire:** To build something that survives him. Not a dynasty — he has no illusions about Edsen inheriting the supply routes — but an infrastructure. Roads that stay warded. Way-stations that stay staffed. A supply network that functions whether or not Torven Greis is alive to walk it.
**Strength:** Patience that is indistinguishable from threat. Torven does not negotiate by talking. He negotiates by waiting, by knowing exactly how long his counterparty can afford to wait, and by being willing to wait longer.
**Loop:** Builds reliability, then cannot trust it. Inspects what he built. Finds a flaw. Fixes it himself rather than training someone else to find it. The network grows, and so does the distance between what it needs and what one man can verify.
**Foil:** The Nalūn chainlords, who trust their system implicitly because the Wellsprings enforce it — versus Torven, who trusts no system he cannot personally walk.
**Voice / Dialect:** Concord Human baseline, marked by trade-road idiom. Speaks slowly, with the deliberate pacing of a man who has learned that a sentence finished too quickly invites the other party to start talking. Uses occupational metaphor exclusively: roads, bridges, cargo weight, delivery schedules. Never uses court language. Never uses magical terminology. If pressed on Essence matters, redirects to cost.
**Dialogue Evolution:** Twenty years ago he talked too much, trying to fill the silence his counterparties left. Now he IS the silence his counterparties try to fill.
**Speech:** "The road eats margins" (signature). "A supply line is a noose you put around your own neck on purpose — the question is whether you hold the other end." Plain declaratives, short, final-sounding even when he is asking a question.
**Posture:** Stands with his weight slightly forward, hands loose at his sides or clasped behind his back. Sits only when the room has settled. Makes eye contact the way a man counts coins — deliberately, one at a time.
**Stress Default:** Goes still. The scarf comes up higher over his jaw. His speech, already slow, halves in pace. Anyone who has negotiated with him recognizes this as the point at which the deal has either just been won or just been lost, and they will not find out which until he speaks.

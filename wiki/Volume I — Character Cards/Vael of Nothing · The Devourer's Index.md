### VIII · Signature

#### The Final Index

**Effect** · He removes the blindfold and looks at one target directly. The Index's passive dissolution-map sharpens to surgical precision: he perceives the exact sequence of failures that would collapse the target's physical form, Soul Crystal, and Wellspring resonance at once. Perceiving the sequence begins it; no touch required.
**Cost** · 2,600,000 EU and accelerated Form Erosion onset. Used seven times; five targets did not survive; one produced a Benediction-class counter that interrupted the sequence; one he chose, mid-process, to stop. He has not explained either.
**Limit** · The target has three turns, 18 seconds, to leave his line of sight or produce a counter that disrupts the Null-Devour field entirely. He replaces the blindfold immediately after, regardless of outcome.
**Counter** · Survive three turns and disrupt the field with a Benediction- or Coagula-class working. Surviving three turns is the entire problem.
**What nobody knows.** Why the one target he stopped mid-process was the one he stopped.

### Lore · The Life Behind the Card

*Written 2026-09-24 by the character-lore pass from this card and the record of The Accord's field blades. What those sources do not state is new here.*

#### Origin

Vael of Nothing was born on the ash plain of the New World's arc, the young volcanic ground between the andesite chain and the caldera belt that the Guild Accord chartered within living memory. The plain carries a crop in three seasons and can bury a village in one night, and its farmers live with ash fall, lahar and the gas that pools in the hollows. They know that a Wellspring on ground this young does not stay where it was found.
His mother was carrying him when an Oblivara event opened on the plain. The field she was working went out of existence around her, with its fence line and the path to the next farm. Where they had been, the ground lay bare and level. She lived, and so did he.
He grew up seeing how things would fail. A roof beam, a water jar, the wall of a byre: each showed him the order in which it would come apart, whether he looked for it or not. He told a neighbour once that her roof would go under the next ash fall. It went. After that the neighbours kept their children back from him, and the ones who stopped to speak lost what they had meant to say partway through a sentence.
He could not stop the reading. As a boy he bound a strip of cloth over his eyes, and through it the reading came slowly enough to bear.

#### The Making

He grew very large, and he moved faster than a body that size should. He joined no company, no order and no Division. A sword came to him on the plain with a hexagonal inlay already set into its blade. He did not cut the inlay. Of everything he owns, the sword is what he keeps with care.
Wards weakened when he stood near them. Constructs flickered at their edges. The air he walked through thinned of what practitioners draw on.
Vael did not call on Uurgath. Uurgath answered anyway. He had spoken no rite and asked no Archon for anything. What had lived through the event on the plain inside his mother was already the kind of vessel the Devourer of Forms recognises as a co-author. The answer came unsummoned, and it needed a body willing to keep being it. Vael's has kept on.

#### The Cost

The answer is paid for in his own form. What unmakes other things works on him too, and his extremities have begun to lose definition. The ends of his fingers have lost their edges. The Accord keeps an estimate of how many years of physical coherence he has at his present rate. He has not adjusted the rate.
Blows aimed at him sometimes come partly apart on contact before they land. He chose none of it.
He takes the blindfold off for one purpose. He looks at a single target, and the sequence of failures that would bring down its body, its soul and its hold on its Wellsprings begins as soon as he sees it. Each time it wears his body down faster. He has done it seven times. Five of those he looked at did not survive. One, the enforcer at Tarvel's Crossing, raised a counter that broke the sequence. Another he chose to stop, partway through. He has explained neither. Whatever the outcome, he ties the blindfold back on at once.
Three people who stood too long near him lost resonance with their Wellsprings for good, and the Accord documented each case. None of them recovered. After the third, he began to wear the Three Medallions at his chest. They draw that reach in closer whenever he is not fighting.
He believes the answer has to go on being given, and he gives it with his own body. He travels with nobody.

#### Where They Stand

The Guild Accord keeps an open file on Vael and has issued no warrant. It has not decided what category he belongs to. The enforcer at Tarvel's Crossing asked for a posting away from combat and has not said what the turns under the Final Index were like.
With the file still open, the Enforcement Division sent Ser Caldran Veiss to the ash plain alone. Caldran found Vael there with the blindfold on and the medallions at his chest. He kept his own sword in its scabbard. He asked where Vael had been and where he was going, and Vael told him. He asked about Tarvel's Crossing and about the target Vael had stopped, and Vael gave no answer. Caldran entered in the file that Vael had told him no lie.
The Division sent Tsukihana Reimei after him. She came to learn whether anything in Vael could be cut free and kept. What she listened for thinned as she walked in. She stopped short of him and did not draw, and her entry recorded no reading.
The blindfold is on, and the file is open. Nobody knows why the one target he stopped was the one he stopped.

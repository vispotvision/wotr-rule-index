### VIII · Signature

#### Garrath-Tul Verdict

**Effect** · One catastrophic strike, channeled through the fused Titan fragment, opens a fault hundreds of meters long through an urban district and crumples fortress gates and towers in the same motion.
**Cost** · Forces the Radiant-Shell overstep past his Band-D Crystal's actual gate. Every use adds new fractures on top of the fourteen he already carries, and the working reads as a Threshold Fracture Event rather than a technique with a fixed price.
**Limit** · It requires real anger or grief to ignite — a calm, calculating Vargrath cannot call it at all, which is also why Gnosis-based tricks (feigned surrender, manufactured despair) can bait it early or deny it entirely.
**Counter** · **Deny him the target it needs.** The Verdict guides an existing fault line and does not create one; ground or a structure with no compromised point simply absorbs the overstep instead of breaking, and he pays the fracture cost for nothing.
> **What nobody knows.** How many more of these his Crystal can take before a fracture stops being a feature and starts being the end of him. He has not asked, and nobody close enough to tell him has been willing to.

### Lore · The Life Behind the Card

*Written 2026-09-24 by the character-lore pass from this card and the record of The elven old-ground. What those sources do not state is new here.*

#### Origin

Vargrath Ul-Dorr was born in the pit at Tol-Varath, to a Garrath-Ul Ironblood father and an Elshaen dream-mother. His mother was murdered.
His father's people spoke Old Vaross, and they went to war in Gravetongue. They read an elder's scars as the record of a family. They called Elves "unreal vapors," and Vargrath was half Elf.
His blood was his father's kind. It clotted into hard plates where it met the air, and it kept him on his feet through wounds that would have emptied other men.
The Ironblood carried one scar in common, the Siege of Four Suns. Their warbands had stalled the Second Shattering there by mass blood-sacrifice. It saved the Western Realms. It also marked the lineage in ways the Ironblood had never fully named.

#### The Making

His father burned alive to force the Garrath-Tul into his son's Soul Crystal. The Garrath-Tul was a shard of the Titan Valmir, and a wyrm-scale relic went in with it. His father died of the burning.
Neither thing could be taken out of him again. The relic came up across his frame as draconic scarring. It also left him a reflex, and from then on he was rarely standing at the centre of anything aimed to erase the ground under him. The shard gave him a strike that could open a fault through a city district and crumple gates and towers in one motion.
Tol-Varath exiled him.
In a gorge he found thirty-eight Elves dying. He could have walked away from them. He stayed. He made that choice ahead of what it would cost him, and he made it calm. Everything since has followed from that one refusal: the fortress-raising, the war-chant, and the debts he means the Realms to acknowledge, starting with the Siege of Four Suns.

#### The Cost

He was keeping the thirty-eight alive in a cut of rock, with the winter coming on, when Brynja Haldrís came down into the gorge. She stood in front of them, as she stood in front of anything that came for her people. The Northroot clans took them into their forge-halls for the winter, and Vargrath went in with them. In the spring he left, and the thirty-eight went with him. The clans did not follow. He came out of that winter owing a debt, in a life built on debts owed to him, to people who will never ask him to collect it.
Kaetra's dream-law fused into him later, uninvited. From then on he saw an empire of mountain-citadels that did not yet exist. He could not fully explain wanting it.
Every strike he drove through the Garrath-Tul cracked his Crystal along new lines, on top of the lines it already carried. The strike would not come to him calm. It needed real anger or grief to ignite, and an enemy who staged a surrender or a despair in front of him could draw it early or keep it from him. Ground with no weak point took the strike whole. Then he paid the cracks for nothing. Near him, cracked walls and weak tunnels gave way sooner than they should.

#### Where They Stand

Vargrath Ul-Dorr is Grand Commander of the Elven Army of the Ashen Vale Compact. The thirty-eight from the gorge were its seed. What holds the Compact together is his gravity, and both sides of that arrangement know it. The Northroot clans fight for preservation and never for conquest, and they have sent no one to his army.
Every soldier under him knows his name. He sees to that. His war-chant, Varath Gravetongue, carries through the ground under them as well as the air, and he raises fortresses out of city stone. On ground he has bled or dreamed on before, ambushes fail against him, and he knows where the earth will give way.
The citadels he sees still run ahead of the ground he holds. He is called Grand Arbiter of the Undreamed Fate, and his own line is this: "Before there is a warrior, there is a debt. I intend to collect both."
Nobody close enough to tell him how many more of those strikes his Crystal can take has been willing to. He has not asked.

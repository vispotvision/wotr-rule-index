### Lore · The Life Behind the Card

*Written 2026-09-24 by the character-lore pass from this card and the record of The Voice Between Wars and the Outer nobles. What those sources do not state is new here.*

#### Origin

Varozael Dreikthorne was born in the Thorned Court, to a lady of that court, among the predatory fey-lords of the Court of the Black Eclipse, an Outer Realm aristocracy. Its standing with the Guild Accord was already a "cordial enmity": "officially recognised, unofficially hunted."
His mother held a stretch of the court's ground. As a child he watched her rivals come up to the edge of it, stand there a while, and turn back without a blow struck. In the Thorned Court a clearing belonged to whatever the rest of the wood went quiet for. He learned that before he learned to hunt.
Then he learned to move under the wood's own noise. A thing he stalked heard leaf and wind and its own breathing, and nothing else, until he was close enough to cut it.

#### The Making

He learned the cut before the kill. A beast he had bled went on running, and he could feel where it ran. When it tired he could weaken it where it stood and draw it back toward him through the trees.
Once he followed a bled thing past the edge of the court's ground. Hunters of the Accord were in the country beyond. They closed on him in the open. His skin broke out in jagged black vine under their hands, and it tore the hands that held him. He drew blood from every one of them and got home with their cuts in him. Those who lived went back to the Accord carrying his. Long after, he could still feel them walking, somewhere past the edge of anything he held.
He never crossed that edge after prey again. He stood at it and waited, and the wounded came back across it to him.
By the time he held ground of his own, he was hunting things that had killed hunters before him. He buried the first of them under the thorn. The ground took it, and grew. From then on he hunted powerful prey and anchored their remains into his Domain's soil. The Domain grew on what he brought into it, and it began to answer. He fed the thing that answered.

#### The Cost

The Domain had to be fed to grow, and what was anchored in it stayed anchored.
Every creature he had ever wounded stayed attached to him as well: the beasts that got away, the prey anchored in his soil, the Accord's hunters who had walked home. Some of them pulled back. He could not always tell which one.
The Thorned Court's hunts ran into the ground of an Innerworld principality and drew blood there. The principality took the matter to the Guild Accord, and the Arbitration Division took it up. Enforcement wanted a writ against his court. Varozael came to the table in his own person, with the crystallised shard of the Black Eclipse Domain fused into his horns, and let the principality's envoys look at him. Aurevian Lysanthir sat between them and played.
The envoys' fear of the court came off them. His own position softened with it. He signed a treaty that set out the ground where his court's hunt might run and the ground where it might not. The Enforcement officer carrying the case for the writ reached the Council after the seal.
He had signed in a room where fear came undone. He respected the man who had done it. He did not trust him afterward.

#### Where They Stand

Varozael Dreikthorne is the Stag of the Black Eclipse, Lord of the Thorned Court and Herald of Nightglass, an Outer Realm noble who treats with the Guild Accord and is hunted by it. Lady Nytheris is his only equal in the Thorned Court, his lover and his political rival.
Kaelen Raive, the Arbitration Division's Bound Duelist, knows him for a hunter, and he knows Kaelen for one. Kaelen holds the fear he lives on to be a waste.
He resents Saruin Kye's irreverence. Their encounters often end in theft, in wreckage and in grudging laughter. No record has him drawing the monkey's blood, and he will not follow the monkey off his ground.
No record says which principality the treaty bound, what its terms held beyond the lines of the hunt, or whether it still holds. Nobody knows how many of the anchored are still aware.

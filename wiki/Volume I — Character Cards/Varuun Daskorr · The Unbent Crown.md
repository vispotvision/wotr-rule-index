### Lore · The Life Behind the Card

*Written 2026-09-24 by the character-lore pass from this card and the record of Babyl and the Praetorian March. What those sources do not state is new here.*

#### Origin

Varuun Daskorr was born an heir of the Daskorr, an ancient noble house in an Inner World kingdom of stone and faith. The house traced its descent to the original Lawbearers of Coagula. Its ancestors had served the holder of the Dawn Guard's title as oath-smiths and lords of mineral law. The kingdom was ruled from a citadel over the worked Core of Coagula. It kept the Parunic doctrine it shared with Eresse.
The line was rumoured to carry fragments of Atlazon's Strain. Each heir was said to have a faint gravity about him, enough to draw reverence from a room where nobody had spoken. Men of his house said they felt it on Varuun as a boy. He grew into a towering man.
The Robes of the Lawgivers were handwoven from scripture-thread, and each line of their golden script was a vow. His lineage renewed every line at every equinox. He stood through the renewals from childhood. He knew the vows by heart before he could read them.

#### The Making

In his turn he took the Crown of the Atlas Chain, forged from dormant Wellspring veins, and the Kneeling Sun. The sword was black steel engraved with the Oath of Stone, and he carried it as his sceptre. It was said to weigh only as much as his conscience.
He ruled from the Throne Hall. There he spoke each edict aloud, in his own voice, over ground his house had worked, and sealed it with the Kneeling Sun. Faint glyphs rose on the floor. They stayed until the edict was fulfilled. Some were gone before he left the hall, and others lay underfoot so long that his courtiers learned to walk around them. He could not decree at a distance, and he could not decree in haste. Nothing in him moved quickly.
He kept faith with the gods of his kingdom and bowed to none of them. Each decree he spoke and each judgment he bore laid another layer onto his Crystal, until it pulsed with a lawlike steadiness. There the contradiction resolved. A crown was a crown and was also a weight, and he went on ruling with both on his brow. The Domain that formed was the capital, and its law was that the air refused lies.

#### The Cost

Every edict closed a contact point between king and kingdom. Rumour held that his Crystal had begun to fuse into the Core beneath the city. Monks of the Wellspring Chambers claimed that his heartbeat echoed faintly in the harmonic core, setting the realm's rhythm to his resolve. If the rumour was true, he would one day fuse with Coagula entirely, an Anchor-King whose laws would endure after his death. He prayed for endurance. The price of it was that no part of him would remain apart from the law.
His own words for it were "Law is mercy written in weight." When he was angered his words grew heavy. Rooms dimmed, and the quills of his clerks shattered beneath their own weight.
An edict had no hold on ground it never reached, and the next fracture would start where the kingdom had not fused. Away from the Core his words kept their heaviness and lost their hold. His frontier ran against the March. At the Firefront on that frontier a Flame-aspected Wellspring imploded under corrupted pressure, and an Accord regiment was annihilated. No edict of his had ever been spoken over that ground.
After the Eighth Fracture of Faith, Caedor and his kingdom contested a Wellspring boundary before the Arbitration Division. The adjudicator was Seren Valenne, born in his kingdom, with its nobility's bearing under grey robes. Varuun did not go. He decreed only in the Throne Hall, and he did not leave it. His advocates carried the kingdom's case into the Neutral Chambers, where both parties held silent for an hour. The terms came back to the Throne Hall as the Accord's. He delayed them. He had often delayed the Accord's decrees the same way, citing the "Temporal Weight Clauses". Under his composure the record finds one fear: that progress will unmake reverence.

#### Where They Stand

Varuun reigns still, aging and unyielding. His hair is a white mane. His beard is wind-worn marble. When he rests upon the Kneeling Sun, the air hums with Wellspring tone.
He is allied with Eresse through the Parunic doctrine they share. He remains a signatory of the Guild Accord and often delays its decrees. The Caedor terms still wait on him. He accuses the March of weaponizing Wellspring fire, and the Firefront is among the fires he names. From that frontier the reports say the Wellspring is reawakening.
Scholars call him a Living Anchor. His people call him the Unbent Crown and remember him as the king who "did not bow to gods, yet never broke faith with them." Nobody knows whether the name is a description or a prophecy. He has said, "When my crown falls, so will the sky."

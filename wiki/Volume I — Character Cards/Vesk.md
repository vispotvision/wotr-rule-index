### Lore · The Life Behind the Card

*Written 2026-09-24 by the character-lore pass from this card, the one archived scene it appears in, and the record of Malphas's cell. What those sources do not state is new here.*

#### Origin

Vesk was raised in a village that put stones on its graves. A working main ran near it, and he grew up with its hum under everything, winter and summer, loud enough to sleep by. When he was a boy he asked why the graves had stones on them. He was told. He thought even then that it seemed a poor way to settle a question.
His fingers were long. He was good with small glass and small measures, and the village had no work for either. He left it young for a compounder's bench, where Wellspring stock was made up to the strength a buyer asked for and carried out by the flask. Supply was bought like grain in the Withering. A boy who could make a little stock go a long way was kept on.

#### The Making

The compounder taught him dilution: how far a stock would stretch before it stopped working, and how to put it into a living thing so that the thing carried it. Vesk made up Verdantia, the growth, for buyers who wanted living things to grow. That was what every buyer of it had always wanted. Once he asked what it would do in something dead. The compounder had no use for that and said so.
He was still asking it when he came into Malphas's cell, and at Greyshaft Nine the growth was his.
The cell proved its rot synthesis in the coldhouse there, on a capped main. The first beast the ferment finished was laid on the table. Nuvalik opened it. Foss put a coil over the residue and it read clean, and he said at the bench why. Vesk heard him out and kept his own reading. The synthesis was pure, he held, and the purity was the elegant part.
The field doses needed more Verdantia than Xu Deming could buy. Deming took it off a legitimate supply contract on the Swale line and drew it down as wastage, and his name went on the record as the buyer. The stock came to Vesk's bench. From it he made up his Verdantia formulation, third dilution, introduced live and carried on the animal's own circulation.

#### The Cost

Two grafts went onto his hands. His fingers had been longer before the second. On the two that took, the nails came in ridged and yellow and grew at a rate that embarrassed him. The hands stopped closing properly. He learned what they would and would not do, and took to carrying things out in front of him in both palms, the way a server carries wine.
He came to lose most thoughts, the way he lost most things. He kept the pleasure.
He watched Malphas eleven days for a sign of what Stage he was, or whether he was seated in anything at all. Vesk had met three people in his life who filled a room by arriving. Malphas was none of them. In a room at four degrees the man's fingers kept the colour of a living man's fingers. Vesk did not know what that meant. He did not ask.
On the night of the report he came up the rail with the flask in both palms and told Malphas the experiments had gone as he said. He called him a genius. Malphas had Foss say again why the residue read clean, and Foss called the synthesis filthy. "You know it and you do not use it," Foss told him. Vesk had been about to say that the purity was the elegant part, and he turned the flask a half-turn in his palms and said nothing.
When the growth came up he got in ahead of Foss, because it was his. Then Malphas set the next work: the dose made to walk, on the matrix the growth had built, with no anchor in it. Vesk felt the whole thing arrive at once and had to put his hands flat on the bench.

#### Where They Stand

Vesk is the formulator of Malphas's cell at the Greyshaft Nine coldhouse. The growth stands nine inches at the crown on the best of the hinds, by his own report. "It will go further. It does not know it has finished."
The walking dose is built on it. Malphas has named the work Project Zombification and ordered the name used by all of them, in writing. Vesk thought the name was doing something besides the deer. He lost the thought. What stayed was the pleasure of the dead getting up.
He still counts the drip in the far corner, twenty-two a minute. He has not asked what Stage Malphas is. The ninth hind is ordered to the table. Deming has given the Logistics Division eleven weeks to find the Swale line's bad spring, and the stock drawn off that line went into Vesk's third dilution.

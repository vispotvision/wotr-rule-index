# Wren Greymane · The Traitor's Traitor

*The Traitor's Traitor · as of the muster on the road north*
> **Full Name:** Wren Greymane
> **Race / Lineage:** Of House Greymane of the western ridge; grandson of Godfrey Greymane
> **Office:** Commander of six hundred Greymane men marching under the Tenrai. At the muster he claimed the Greymane banner and the title Shield of the North in front of them, and took two villages under it before noon.
> **Age:** Three years older than his brother Bram. He was eleven on the wall walk when Bram was eight.
> **As Of:** The morning of the muster, thirteen months into his service beside Seiji Tenrai Moto.

---

## Description

A man of the ridge. His palm is ridged across the heel from twenty years of a shield strap, and the shield is always the same one: a heater, four feet, oak core and hide, with an iron rim gone silver with wear and a boss cracked and rebound twice. He wears a grey coat. At the split he stood at the front of the Tenrai line with his arms folded, his weight on the back foot and his head tilted.
He has killed thirty men with his sword. In the left saddlebag, under the oilcloth, he carries a book.

**Sentence length** · Short, flat, unhurried and entirely certain, the voice of the ridge. One long run when he stands in the stirrups to rally his men, and then short again.

**Never says** · His reasons. He never mentions the book in his saddlebag, never explains the smile across the field, and never asks the same question twice. He counts only what he has written in the book, the dead and the months; everything else he leaves round.

**Under stress** · Flatter and shorter, down to a single word said over the work ("Bulwark." "Compact."), while he files what he sees and keeps turning the problem. When the strain is at its worst, he smiles, and cannot account for it.

### Lore · The Life Behind the Card

#### Origin

Greymane children are raised as parts of a wall. On the wall walk their father told Wren and Bram that the ridge did not move. Wren asked whether it ever had, and got no answer, and took that as the answer. The kingdom fell while they were boys, and their grandfather Godfrey held the western ridgeline for three years after it with both grandsons beside him. In the second winter Wren's lungs filled with fluid. Bram sat nine days by the cot and changed the compress every two hours, and Wren lived. Bram lied to the Tenrai assessors about where his brother was. Behind the western wall Bram planted the rye and Wren planted the turnips.
For nine years the brothers held the southern approach together, with eight hundred men and a rock shelf.

#### The Making

On the field of the Greymane split Wren stood at the front of the Tenrai line, two hundred paces off, under the banner of the line that had killed his grandfather. Bram roared that he was the one who had kept him alive. Wren smiled, raised one hand, and the Tenrai line went back over the rise. He did not turn round.
For sixteen months after that he was called traitor in every village between the ridge and the Stormfold, his brother among the voices. He chose six hundred men for their faith and spent it: they were spat at in their own villages, eleven of their wives stopped writing, and three were killed by their own kin. He entered those three in the book. In a borrowed room he forged a letter in another man's hand under a seal with no device, and told Yūgiri Yukari: *"If I have to become the villain to eventually be seen as a hero, there is no greater honour."* He had left a rat behind for Bram to find.
In summer he walked the Ashgate narrows under a false name with a hired cart of salt fish, counted two hundred and ten feet from the ditch to the drop, and drew it on the back of a psalter. Kill the middle third of a column there and the head cannot turn and the tail cannot get past.

#### The Cost

On the Ashgate road, within sight of the Kharven gates, Kaalabad drew Zarakal out of a seam in the air. Wren made the sign and six hundred men turned round in four seconds. Kaalabad looked at him for the first time in sixteen months: *"You are the traitor's traitor."* *"Aye,"* said Wren.
He held the road with the shield and the four Compacts on his belt, iron charges the size of a duck's egg whose smith died in the Stormfold without telling anyone how. His sword went about three inches into Kaalabad's throat band, and the scar there is his. Then his Bulwark failed. Zarakal's fluke pinned him through shield, arm and shoulder to the ditch bank. With the seconds he had left he pulled a Compact with his teeth and set it off in his own hand. It rolled some sixty of the men lying in the road forty feet clear of the fight, and killed none of them. He looked up at Kaalabad and did not close his eyes.
He is dead from that road on.

#### Where They Stand

His name went down the road and the battle stopped. Kwon Mu-jin closed Wren's eyes without ever learning who he was. A Reckoner entered him: *ditch bank, entered by field observation, body not recovered.*
In the eighth month a bundle came to Bram from Lambert with sixteen depositions from the men who had marched in that column. Bram wrote a dedication into the Stormfold book before his own name: *"This campaign is dedicated to Wren Greymane, who was called traitor in every village between here and the Stormfold for sixteen months, by me among others."* Any man who says the word in Bram's hearing answers to him. Wren is not on Bram's roll, and Bram has never said why. The book still lies unopened in the saddlebag of a horse Wren slapped away south.

#### Ties

- **Bram Greymane** · his younger brother. Bram kept him alive through the fever, called him traitor with the rest, and entered the dedication before his own name.
- **Renard Greymane** · his cousin, who chose Hild without hesitating when Wren went over.
- **Seiji Tenrai Moto** · the man he rode beside for sixteen months, until he turned the column on the Tenrai.
- **Kaalabad** · the general who pinned him to the ditch bank. He made Kaalabad pay for every step.
- **Kwon Mu-jin** · the stranger who closed Wren's eyes on the ditch bank.
- **Yūgiri Yukari** · the woman in the borrowed room, who had loved him first for saying the whole of a thing once.

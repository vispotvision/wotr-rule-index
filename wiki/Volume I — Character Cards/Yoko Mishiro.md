### Voice

**Notices first** · The air before the room: what happened here before she arrived, who is afraid, and the decision a person is about to make before they have made it. Her ears turn to it a second before her face admits anything.
**Sentence length** · At work, short and flat: one confirmed fact, then a full stop. At home with her husband and family she runs long and personal, following an argument clause by clause until she has said the whole of what she means.
**Contractions** · sometimes: on the road and at home they come easily; when she is reporting what her nose found, they go.
**Pet word** · "Exact."
**Never says** · A guess dressed as a finding. She tells what the scent was and stops there ("I am not telling you what it means"), and she never lets a joke about harm stand unanswered.
**Stumbles** · Never. She says what is accurate and nothing past it, and does not restart.
**Gloss rights** · Diagnostic only: she may say plainly what her nose and her Essence-sense have read, in their own terms, and explains nothing else.
**Under stress** · Speech narrows to the single confirmed fact and grows more precise, not less. The ears flatten or lock forward and report the truth whatever her face is doing.
**In grief** · The pens come back: the flat face, the offer of work, words stripped to what a captive dares to say. What she will not say in Common comes out in the fox-register of her mother, which has no Common equivalent.
**In joy** · The words stay careful and exact; the tail moves, the ears come up, and at home she lets a sentence run on because nobody is keeping count of her anymore.
**Sample line** · "I want to be exact about him, because exactness is the only thing I am useful for at a table."

### Lore · The Life Behind the Card

*Written 2026-09-24 by the character-lore pass from this card, the twenty archived scenes it appears in, and the record of The Ashen Crown: Muken's house and the Moto lines. What those sources do not state is new here.*

#### Origin

Yoko Mishiro was born fox-spirit, of the Mishiro line, a people the Accord's classification had never settled how to file. Her mother named her. The nose worked from her first day. It read a room before her eyes reached it and read what a person meant to do before they did it.
Under the Accord's Labor Provision, a beastkin who could not show free-holder papers was labour stock, and the Mishiro claim to an exemption was only ever arguable. Her mother died. Yoko came away with a folded paper that bore her mother's name in someone else's handwriting, and with the name her mother had given her. Neither proved her free.
The caravans carried her from township to township. In the pens her face learned to go flat, and her ears never learned anything at all. The men who held her watched her tail, because it told them what she was trying to hide. She stopped trying to hide it.

#### The Making

By the time a caravan brought her to Verath she was starving. Its pens stood in a row against the township's southern wall, timber to the shoulder and iron grating to the thatch. The floor was mud. She was put in the third. The manacles had come off a few days before, and the marks on both ankles still wept. She had read the pen before she saw inside it, because everyone kept there had left a layer in the air.
She called through the rain for hours, until her voice split. "Please give me something to eat," she called. When a young man stopped at the grating, she offered what the pens had taught her to offer. "I can work. I can carry. I can clean."
He struck the lock with the pommel of his blade, and the hasp snapped.
"Come out," he said.
She stayed at the bars. He pulled the gate wide and told her about an inn to the north, a room that locked, and his sister asleep in it. Then he broke the next lock, and the one after that, until the row stood open. He walked back toward the inn without looking round.
She walked through the lock on her own decision, past a man who had already stopped waiting for her. The choosing had to be hers, with no rescue owed to anyone for it.

#### The Cost

On the inn step Sodoku Moto set down bread, broth and salted pork beside her and did not look at her. She ate most of the bread and broth and kept the pork. Then she asked him why. "I am nothing but a slave," she said. "You broke a lock for nothing."
"Listen, girl," he said. "I did not save you to hear you cry about it. Eat. Shut up."
She was a tool, he told her, for a sister who needed a warmth he no longer had. She gave him her name anyway. He did not repeat it. She lay beside his sister that night with the pork under the pillow for the morning.
On the way out of Verath a woman at the well told Yoko she did not have to go with him. "He isn't," Yoko said. When the woman told her she did not need to protect him, she said, "I'm not." In her reckoning the world had two categories for beastkin, victim or animal, and neither allowed that she stood in that street because she chose to. Emira, his sister, asked her whether her brother had done what the town said. "Yes," Yoko said. "He did terrible things."
By a fire in the Scourge of Hell he called her a tool again. "I've made my peace with the word," she said. "What I haven't made peace with is the math."
She stayed. At the rift camp on the ice she learned he had a daughter he had never told her of. In the igloo afterward she was mending the left sleeve of his gambeson. "You lied to me about your past," she said, and the needle stopped. She set one condition. "You will bring her back to us." He promised. On the polar shelf a Titan Droval lowered its head to him, and she told the column, "The creature chose." At Kharven-Seat Hild planted herself in front of her in the kitchen, and Yoko answered plainly and did not crouch.
Emira asked what had become of her cheerful side. At the same supper, speaking of a priest, Yoko said, "I want to be exact about him, because exactness is the only thing I am useful for at a table." Of a courier's satchel she said, "It was on the leather. That is all of it. I am not telling you what it means."
On the day of the ninth hour the order sent her below the old cistern with Hild and Osric of Hallenfeld. Hild died in the doorway Osric had put himself in. No record says how Yoko came from that chamber to the road. At the ninth hour she came out of the ice at the road's edge at a flat sprint. A spearhead was snapped off in her left thigh. She never mentioned it. She knelt on that leg beside Sodoku and closed her hand on his arm. Her nose found no scent on him at all. She kept the hand there until he stood.

#### Where They Stand

She keeps her own record, in her own hand, in the plain form she uses for everything, and she has shown it to no one. On the night the first Accord circle was signed she wrote, "He built it for her. Every clause." She wrote as well, "I have been beside this man for nine years and this is the first thing he has done that I do not think I could have done."
Her son Rikudoku had been born in the Scourge years and was about seven the year the circle was signed. In the Thin Weeks of the fourth year the Seat's Reckoner came to enter him on the roll. The form had one slot for a bloodline, and Sodoku told the Reckoner to enter both.
That week she wrote, "My son is the heir of the oldest bloodline in the world and half of him is the thing that world puts in a pen." She wrote too, "I am afraid of the year he works out that he can use it." In the sixth year the empire's own law still filed his second inheritance under a schedule written for property, in the fourth title, eleventh article.
Her card keeps her at another hour. There she lives in a cabin on the Scourge's edge with her husband and their son, a second child coming, sworn to no one. The hair that grew back uneven after the pen is even on both sides now. Her ears still report her a second before her face does. She is still slow to claim a thing outright before someone else has seen it first. The old marks around her ankles have never stopped being marks.

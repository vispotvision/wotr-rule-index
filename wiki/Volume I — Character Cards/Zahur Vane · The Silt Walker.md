### VIII · Signature

#### Sinkhole Step

**Effect** · Zahur liquefies a radius of ground beneath a charging opponent; Petralon's compression law takes hold the instant they stop moving, and the ground re-solidifies around them like stone closing a fist.
**Cost** · Draws heavily on his own body's moisture; a day of combat run this way costs him roughly two gallons of water, and going without risks collapse from Desiccation.
**Limit** · It needs erodible ground in continuous contact: sand, loose earth, weathered stone. Frozen or paved bedrock refuses the liquefaction outright.
**Counter** · Freeze the ground first, or fight him somewhere the working can't set: open water, solid rock, or wind strong enough to scatter the dust before the trap closes.

### Lore · The Life Behind the Card

*Written 2026-09-24 by the character-lore pass from this card and the record of Sun and sand. What those sources do not state is new here.*

#### Origin

Zahur Vane was born into a nomad lineage of the Great Sand Sea. He had his people's deep brown skin and coiled dark hair, and their hardness against extreme heat and thirst. He learned young that the desert does not fight the wind. It moves with it. His craft grew out of that lesson. He read the ground for where it was going to slide, put himself there, and waited for it to go.
The Guides of the Lost Meridian, a neutral order of wayfinders, became the closest thing he had to a home. He answered to their purpose. He learned to walk so lightly that the sand kept no print of him unless he chose to leave one, and the traps of the old roads did not wake under his weight. He wore the Sun-Dials at his ears, ancient gold that carried the tremor of the earth against his skin. Through them he heard footfalls from miles off. They hummed near an active Wellspring or a disturbance of the void. His shawl was a flax weave hard to cut or burn, and when he stood still its weave blended into the ground around him.

#### The Making

He led seekers toward lost Wellsprings, and on every road he judged, without telling them, whether they deserved to arrive. Once he guided a band of ambushing bandits. He walked them into a soft patch of the desert. Nobody saw them again.
A young prince came out of Solfatara to find a cure for his father, and the road ran through the Glass Storms. The Temple-State had hired a freeblade, Kaelis Aranthor, to keep him alive. At the edge of the sand the order gave the prince Zahur. He walked the party in by the gold at his ears and halted them wherever it told him the ground was soft. When the first storm rose, Kaelis wanted to cross before it closed. Zahur put the party in the lee of a dune and made them sit. The storm took the prince from the lee. Kaelis did not find him. Zahur heard his footfalls through the gold, walked out into the storm and brought him back. After that the party moved when Zahur moved. At the end of the road he named his price, a story nobody had heard, and the prince told it to him alone.
At the Black Oasis a Wellspring began to turn over, and the gold at his ears hummed with it. The Wellspring had been on Rafik Al'Seren's chart, Rafik had sold its coordinates, and the buyers had come by the chart with no guide and set to work at the well-head. Zahur came in off the sand. He led the people of the oasis out a few at a time, along ground that would hold them, and did not tell them why he had come. While they were leaving, a stranger went down to the well-head alone and stopped the inversion, and it cost him a piece of his memory. Zahur saw what the stranger did.
Afterward a price was offered him for the location of the Hidden Oasis of the Great Sand Sea. He chose to keep guarding the Oasis. He did not sell.

#### The Cost

He pays for every working in his own body. When he turns the ground to slurry under a charging man and lets it close around him like stone the moment he stops, the water for it comes out of Zahur's flesh. If he goes without water after a day of fighting that way, he can collapse from Desiccation. The Oasis was worth more to him than staying whole.
The working needs sand, loose earth or weathered stone. Frozen ground, bare bedrock, open water and a wind that scatters the dust before the ground sets all leave him with no trap to close.
He explains nothing. He has never told the prince's story to anyone, and he has never said what he saw the stranger do at the Black Oasis. He works alone, with no network to draw on. He chose that. No living person is as close to him as the Oasis. Everyone else in his life is a guest passing through the Great Sand Sea, and the sand is the nearest thing he has to kin.

#### Where They Stand

Zahur Vane still leads seekers for the Guides of the Lost Meridian. He still guards the Hidden Oasis. Most of those he guides never learn how close they came to the Soft Trap. Through the gold at his ears he feels a Great Shift building underground. Nobody knows whether it is a Titan waking, or his own Wellspring reaching the end of what the desert still has to lend him.
The prince he brought through the Glass Storms went home to Solfatara, and Solfatara burned. The prince is the Ember Regent now, bound to the magma scar there. No record says whether Zahur knows it. He keeps the story.

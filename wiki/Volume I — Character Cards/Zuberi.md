## I · Identity

- **Name:** Zuberi. Titled **Dawn Guard**, and the scene uses the title and the name interchangeably in narration.
- **Register:** Zuberi, Asante, Ara and Kushara cluster as Zettari under R32-1-ZETTARI_REGISTER_SWAHILI_BANTU_ARABIC, which holds the Zettari to their own Swahili/Bantu/Arabic register as a standing carve-out. Kushara is the seat the Zettari forge culture is written around (R33-2). **Recorded as an observation only** — the scene never names his bloodline, so the field is **`null`**.
- **Age / origin:** not stated. Kushara is what he remembers. `null`.
- **Affiliation:** Kushara. He is called **the Sovereign** in narration and carries "the weight of a throne."
- **Status:** **killed in this scene.** "Zuberi was dead, the Material Plane knew it, the Soul Plane registered the departing absence, and Fate had already begun changing every connection that depended upon Dawn Guard remaining alive."
- **Level / Stage / Band:** `null`.
- **Catalyst Event:** not narrated. What the scene fixes is the shape of him at the end — see XIV.

## II · Soul Architecture

- He exercises **Sovereignty**, and the scene shows it as a working rather than a rank: under Kujo's attack "his Sovereignty pressed against the effect, establishing Material acknowledgment of his own form."
- What that defence assumes is the thing that kills him. "Kujo was not actually erasing that form. He was simulating the sensation of every relationship connecting Zuberi to that form being individually withdrawn, leaving authority with no reassuring proof that the person exercising it would remain coherent." Sovereignty answers an attack on the form; it has no answer to an attack on the form's relationships.
- At the end, the dissolution is described from inside: "Dawn Guard remembered Kushara, family, conquest, duty, Asante, Ara, the weight of a throne, yet the connection between those memories and the one experiencing them began dissolving."

## IV–V · Stats

The Eight Primaries and every Sub-Stat peak: **`null`**.

Qualitatively, from a losing fight recorded in unusual detail:
- **Build:** explicitly the bigger man. Kujo "used Dawn Guard's own forward momentum to throw the larger man over his shoulder."
- **Two blades**, at least one of them named — Kujo comes "inside Dawn Guard's reach before either sword could properly turn, slipping beneath the angle of Nyxion."
- **Speed of recovery is his weakness in this exchange, not his strength:** the sequence run on him is a chain of pre-emptions — palm to the inside of the elbow "to spoil the blade line," fist beneath the ribs, forearm across the back of the neck, then an elbow to the jaw, a heel behind the ankle and a shoulder driven the other way "so the Sovereign was violently twisted off his base rather than simply knocked backward."
- **He is hard to finish:** caught by the tunic and pulled upright rather than allowed to fall, then taking "three compact body shots into the same side," a rising palm under the chin, a knuckle into the sternum and a spinning elbow across the temple, and the fight continues.
- **He is still dangerous at the end:** a blade of his "missed his throat by less than an inch" before Kujo trapped the wrist between both hands.

## X · Techniques

### Sovereignty (as shown)

- **Effect:** under hostile working, it presses against the effect and establishes Material acknowledgment of his own form.
- **Cost:** `null`.
- **Limit:** it defends the form. It does not defend the relationships that make the form's holder coherent, and the scene turns entirely on that gap.
- **Counter (R12-3 mandatory):** stated in the text and executed successfully. Do not attack the form — simulate the individual withdrawal of every relationship connecting the man to it, so that his authority is left "with no reassuring proof that the person exercising it would remain coherent." Sovereignty goes on working correctly and answers the wrong question while he dies.
- **What nobody knows:** whether Sovereignty could be made to acknowledge a relationship rather than a form, and whether that would have saved him.
- **The Test (R17-2):** it does *Material acknowledgment* to *his own form*, which under *a sovereign's authority* produces *a defence that holds the body and loses the man*.

## XIV · Psychology

**What he was, underneath the offices.** The scene's best line about him is its last reading of him, and it is not about sovereignty at all:

> "Something moved through Zuberi that had nothing to do with sovereignty, war, Kushara, or whether Asante technically called him cousin. It was older and simpler than every title he carried, the reflex of a man looking at a child his family had already failed too many times."

**What he refuses.** To condemn the boy. Facing Asante "through blood, tears, and fury," his expression "did not soften, but neither did it condemn him." He does not offer comfort and he does not offer judgement.

**What he believes that costs him.** Certainty — "His answer came with the calm certainty Asante's rage lacked." It is the quality the scene admires in him and it is adjacent to the flaw that kills him: a man whose authority is built on proving his own standing does not think to defend the connections that make the standing mean anything.

**Voice:** calm, certain, unsoftened. Fuller fingerprint: **`null`** — the scene gives the description of his answer rather than enough of the answer itself.

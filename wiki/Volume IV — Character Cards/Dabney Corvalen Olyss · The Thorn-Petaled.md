# Dabney

> *He asked at five whether there was a way to be more careful and was told to slow down and said no, not slower, more careful, and nobody had an answer for him and nobody has one now.*

---

**Age** · Thirteen. **Origin** · The Palatinate, Holy Sea of Alabaster. **Affiliation** · None formal. Ward of Edward Lambert. Trained by Darius Ignatius Sanctus Sanctorum Arsenal for seven years. About to enroll at the Academy.
**Stage VI · Glory** · Level pending Isaac · Band pending Isaac.

> **Catalyst Event · Stage VI, Glory.** Willingly accepting genuine soul-cost to preserve something valued above one's own structural integrity. In advance. In cold blood. The first Catalyst on the ladder that is a decision and not a thing that happens to you.

He thinks whatever it was happened before there was a person in there to keep the memory, and the rung is still bolted on.

**η** · pending Isaac. **Coherence Band** · pending Isaac.

> **The damping interpretation.** The Stages are not achievements. A Stage is the amount of restraint a soul has successfully installed on itself. Every rung is another plate bolted onto a thing that would otherwise simply run. The reason advancement feels like gaining power is that a man who can hold a thing can finally afford to pick it up. We measure the cage and call it the animal.
>
> He is not Stage VI because he climbed six rungs. He is Stage VI because that is how much damping is currently on him, and that is a statement about how much there is to damp.

## XIII · Physical Description

**At six** (from "The Reference"): brown hair with a cowlick at the crown that no adult has successfully argued with. Bare feet, which in Alabaster in that month is a decision and not an oversight. Palatinate house wool let out at the cuffs twice and needing it again by spring, knees gone on both sides. Small, round face. Dark, steady eyes.

**At thirteen** (originated, pending ruling): the cowlick survived seven years and Darius. The face has narrowed, the jaw beginning to declare itself under the childhood roundness, the cheekbones surfacing. Taller by a head and a half, still lean, the leanness now carrying definition through the shoulders and forearms from seven years of daily forms. Hands larger than they should be for the rest of him, the way a boy's hands announce the frame that has not arrived yet. Knuckles showing the first calluses of grip work. The eyes have not changed. Dark, steady, and they hold without flinching and without challenging. Still barefoot when nobody is watching. The wool has been replaced; Darius does not tolerate training clothes that do not fit.

## XIV · Psychology

**What he refuses** · pending. He is thirteen and the refusal has not been tested.

**What he survived** · the Glory Catalyst, whatever it was, before there was a person in there to remember it.

**What he believes that costs him** · that being more careful is a thing he can achieve by trying harder. He asked Edward at five whether there was a way to be more careful, and was told to slow down, and said no, not slower, *more careful*. He is working toward something. Edward would stake his standing on it and his standing is the only thing he has ever had. Nobody knows what.

**Voice** · pending Isaac. At six his speech was direct, unperformed, and answered the question rather than performed the answering. He spoke to the cat in the voice children use for the animal and mean for whoever is listening.

> **The loneliness.** He has never had a friend his own age. Everybody in that house is twice his age at the youngest and most of them are six times it. He asked whether the sea is different in other places or the same sea being called things. He asked whether there are children in Kharven. That is a question you can only ask once the answer has stopped being obvious to you.
>
> Darius's answer: do not look for a person. Look for a corridor with eleven hundred of them, all the same age, none of whom have heard of him, where being extraordinary gets you resented by a third and adored by a third and ignored by the rest, which is the correct proportion and which he has never experienced.
>
> That is the Academy. That is the actual argument for it and it has nothing to do with the curriculum.
